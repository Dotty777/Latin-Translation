## Text

**24:1** And when Balaam saw that it pleased the Lord to bless Israel, he did not go away at all as he had gone before to seek an omen, but directing his face toward the wilderness,

**24:2** and lifting his eyes, he saw Israel dwelling in tents according to its tribes. And as the Spirit of God rushed upon him,

**24:3** he took up a parable and said, "Balaam son of Beor has spoken, the man whose eye is closed has spoken,

**24:4** the hearer of the words of God has spoken, who has gazed upon the vision of the Almighty, who falls down, and thus his eyes are opened:

**24:5** How lovely are your tabernacles, Jacob, and your tents, Israel!

**24:6** Like wooded valleys, like watered gardens beside rivers, like tabernacles that the Lord has pitched, like cedars near the waters.

**24:7** Water will flow from his bucket, and his seed will be in many waters. His king will be removed because of Agag, and his kingdom will be taken away.

**24:8** God brought him out of Egypt; his strength is like that of a rhinoceros. They will devour the nations, his enemies, and shatter their bones, and pierce them through with arrows.

**24:9** Lying down, he slept like a lion, and like a lioness whom no one will dare to rouse. Whoever blesses you will himself also be blessed; whoever curses will be counted under a curse."

**24:10** And Balak became angry with Balaam, and striking his hands together, he said, "I called you to curse my enemies, and on the contrary you have blessed them a third time.

**24:11** Return to your place. Indeed, I had resolved to honor you magnificently, but the Lord has deprived you of the appointed honor."

**24:12** Balaam answered Balak, "Did I not say to your messengers whom you sent to me,

**24:13** 'If Balak gives me his house full of silver and gold, I will not be able to go beyond the word of the Lord my God to bring forth anything either good or bad from my heart, but whatever the Lord says, this I will speak'?

**24:14** Nevertheless, going to my people, I will give counsel as to what your people will do to this people in the last time."

**24:15** Therefore, having taken up a parable, he said again, "Balaam son of Beor has spoken, the man whose eye is closed has spoken,

**24:16** the hearer of the words of God has spoken, who knows the teaching of the Most High and sees the visions of the Almighty, who, falling down, has his eyes open:

**24:17** I will see him, but not now; I will gaze upon him, but not near. A star will rise out of Jacob, and a rod will arise from Israel; it will strike the leaders of Moab and lay waste all the sons of Seth.

**24:18** And Idumea will be his possession; the inheritance of Seir will fall to its enemies, while Israel will act mightily.

**24:19** From Jacob there will be one who rules and destroys the remnant of the city."

**24:20** And when he saw Amalek, taking up a parable, he said, "Amalek is the beginning of the nations, whose end will be destroyed."

**24:21** He also saw the Kenite, and having taken up a parable, he said, "Strong indeed is your dwelling place; but if you set your nest in the rock,

**24:22** and have been chosen from the lineage of Kain, how long will you be able to remain? For Asshur will capture you."

**24:23** And having taken up a parable, he spoke again, "Alas! Who is going to live when God does these things?

**24:24** They will come in triremes from Italy; they will overcome the Assyrians and lay waste the Hebrews, and in the end they themselves also will perish."

**24:25** And Balaam rose up and returned to his place; Balak also went back by the way he had come.

## Translator Notes

- **24:3** — Vulgate cujus obturatus est oculus ("whose eye is closed") renders the debated Hebrew term as "closed," creating a deliberate contrast with "and thus his eyes are opened" in 24:4.
- **24:7** — Vulgate Tolletur propter Agag, rex ejus, et auferetur regnum illius ("His king will be removed because of Agag, and his kingdom will be taken away") differs from the Hebrew ("his king will be higher than Agag, and his kingdom will be exalted").
- **24:8** — Vulgate rhinocerotis ("rhinoceros") renders Hebrew re'em ("wild ox"; Septuagint "unicorn").
- **24:14** — Vulgate quid populus tuus populo huic faciat ("what your people will do to this people") reverses the subject and object of the Hebrew ("what this people will do to your people").
- **24:22** — Vulgate et fueris electus de stirpe Cin ("and have been chosen from the lineage of Kain") differs markedly from the Hebrew ("yet Kain will be consumed").
- **24:24** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew Kittim as Italia ("Italy"), specifies in trieribus ("in triremes"), and translates Hebrew Eber as Hebraeos ("the Hebrews").
