## Text

**21:1** When the Canaanite king of Arad, who lived toward the south, heard this—namely, that Israel had come by the way of the scouts—he fought against him, and being victorious, led away plunder from him.

**21:2** But Israel, binding himself to the Lord by a vow, said, "If You deliver this people into my hand, I will wipe out its cities."

**21:3** And the Lord heard the prayers of Israel and delivered the Canaanite, whom he killed, having overthrown his cities; and he called the name of that place Hormah, that is, anathema.

**21:4** Now they also set out from Mount Hor by the way that leads to the Red Sea, to go around the land of Edom. And the people began to grow weary of the journey and the hardship,

**21:5** and speaking against God and Moses, they said, "Why did you bring us out of Egypt so that we might die in the wilderness? Bread is lacking; there is no water; our soul is now nauseated at this utterly light food."

**21:6** For this reason the Lord sent fiery serpents among the people; and at their strikes and the deaths of very many,

**21:7** they came to Moses and said, "We have sinned, because we spoke against the Lord and you; pray that He may take the serpents away from us." And Moses prayed for the people,

**21:8** and the Lord spoke to him, "Make a bronze serpent and set it as a sign; whoever is struck and looks at it will live."

**21:9** Therefore Moses made a bronze serpent and set it as a sign; and when those who were struck looked at it, they were healed.

**21:10** And setting out, the sons of Israel encamped at Oboth.

**21:11** Departing from there, they pitched tents at Iye-abarim, in the wilderness that faces Moab toward the eastern side.

**21:12** And moving from there, they came to the torrent Zered.

**21:13** Leaving it, they encamped opposite Arnon, which is in the desert and juts out into the borders of the Amorite. For indeed Arnon is the boundary of Moab, dividing the Moabites and the Amorites.

**21:14** Therefore it is said in the Book of the Wars of the Lord, "Just as He did in the Red Sea, so He will do in the torrents of Arnon.

**21:15** The cliffs of the torrents sloped down so that they might rest in Ar and recline on the borders of the Moabites."

**21:16** From that place a well appeared, concerning which the Lord spoke to Moses, "Gather the people, and I will give them water."

**21:17** Then Israel sang this song: "Let the well rise up." They sang together:

**21:18** "The well, which the princes dug and the leaders of the multitude prepared, with the giver of the law and with their staffs." From the wilderness, Mattanah.

**21:19** From Mattanah to Nahaliel; from Nahaliel to Bamoth.

**21:20** From Bamoth there is a valley in the region of Moab, at the summit of Pisgah, which faces toward the desert.

**21:21** Then Israel sent messengers to Sihon, king of the Amorites, saying,

**21:22** "I beg that I may be permitted to pass through your land; we will not turn aside into fields and vineyards; we will not drink water from wells; we will walk along the royal road until we pass beyond your boundaries."

**21:23** But he was unwilling to grant that Israel pass through his borders; rather, having gathered an army, he went out to meet him in the desert, and came to Jahaz, and fought against him.

**21:24** By him he was struck down with the edge of the sword, and his land was taken into possession from Arnon as far as Jabbok and the sons of Ammon, because the boundaries of the Ammonites were held by a strong garrison.

**21:25** Therefore Israel took all his cities and lived in the cities of the Amorite, namely, in Heshbon and its villages.

**21:26** The city of Heshbon belonged to Sihon, king of the Amorite, who fought against the king of Moab and took all the land that had been under his dominion as far as Arnon.

**21:27** Therefore it is said in a proverb, "Come to Heshbon; let the city of Sihon be built and constructed;

**21:28** a fire went out from Heshbon, a flame from the town of Sihon, and devoured Ar of the Moabites and the inhabitants of the heights of Arnon.

**21:29** Woe to you, Moab; you have perished, people of Chemosh. He gave his sons to flight and daughters into captivity to Sihon, king of the Amorites.

**21:30** Their yoke perished completely at Heshbon as far as Dibon; weary, they arrived at Nophah, and as far as Medeba."

**21:31** Thus Israel lived in the land of the Amorite.

**21:32** And Moses sent some to scout out Jazer; they captured its villages and took possession of the inhabitants.

**21:33** And they turned and went up by the way of Bashan, and Og, king of Bashan, came to meet them with all his people to fight at Edrei.

**21:34** And the Lord said to Moses, "Do not fear him, because I have delivered him into your hand, and all his people and land; and you will do to him just as you did to Sihon, king of the Amorites, inhabitant of Heshbon."

**21:35** Therefore they struck him down as well, with his sons, and his entire people to utter destruction, and they took possession of his land.

## Translator Notes

- **21:1** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew derek ha-atarim (often taken as a place name, 'the way of Atharim') as a descriptive phrase, exploratorum viam ('the way of the scouts').
- **21:3** — The Vulgate adds the explanatory gloss id est, anathema ('that is, anathema', referring to something placed under the ban or devoted to destruction) to clarify the name Hormah, a gloss not present in the Hebrew text.
- **21:14** — Where the Hebrew text contains the obscure phrase 'Waheb in Suphah and the wadis of Arnon,' the Vulgate understands Suphah as 'the Red Sea' (mari Rubro) and supplies a complete parallel comparison.
- **21:18** — The Vulgate translates Hebrew bimchoqeq personally as in datore legis ('with the giver of the law') rather than instrumentally as 'with the scepter' or 'commander's staff.'
- **21:30** — The Vulgate reflects a distinct interpretation of this difficult Hebrew verse, rendering niram as jugum ipsorum ('their yoke') and wannashim as lassi ('weary'); the Clementine Latin text also reads ad Hesebon ('at Heshbon') rather than 'from Heshbon.'
