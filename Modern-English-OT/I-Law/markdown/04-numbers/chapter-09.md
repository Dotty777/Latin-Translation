## Text

**9:1** The Lord spoke to Moses in the desert of Sinai in the second year after they came out of the land of Egypt, in the first month, saying,

**9:2** "Let the sons of Israel keep the Passover at its time,

**9:3** on the fourteenth day of this month at evening, according to all its ceremonies and ordinances."

**9:4** And Moses commanded the sons of Israel to keep the Passover.

**9:5** And they kept it at its time, on the fourteenth day of the month at evening, on Mount Sinai. The sons of Israel did according to all that the Lord had commanded Moses.

**9:6** Now certain men, unclean on account of the soul of a person, who were not able to keep the Passover on that day, approaching Moses and Aaron,

**9:7** said to them, "We are unclean on account of the soul of a person; why are we deprived, so that we are unable to offer an offering to the Lord at its time among the sons of Israel?"

**9:8** Moses answered them, "Wait, so that I may inquire what the Lord commands concerning you."

**9:9** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying,

**9:10** "Speak to the sons of Israel: 'A person who is unclean on account of a soul, or far away on a journey in your nation, shall keep the Passover to the Lord

**9:11** in the second month, on the fourteenth day of the month at evening. With unleavened bread and wild lettuce they shall eat it;

**9:12** they shall not leave anything of it until morning, and they shall not break a bone of it; they shall observe the entire rite of the Passover.

**9:13** But if anyone is both clean and was not on a journey, and yet did not keep the Passover, that soul shall be cut off from his peoples, because he did not offer the sacrifice to the Lord at its time; he himself shall bear his sin.

**9:14** A foreigner also and a sojourner, if they are among you, shall keep the Passover to the Lord according to its ceremonies and ordinances. The same precept shall be among you both for the sojourner and for the native.'"

**9:15** So on the day on which the tabernacle was set up, the cloud covered it. And from evening over the tent there was something like the appearance of fire until morning.

**9:16** So it happened continually: by day the cloud covered it, and by night something like the appearance of fire.

**9:17** And when the cloud that sheltered the tabernacle was taken away, then the sons of Israel set out; and in the place where the cloud stopped, there they encamped.

**9:18** At the command of the Lord they set out, and at His command they pitched the tabernacle. All the days on which the cloud stood over the tabernacle, they remained in the same place;

**9:19** and if it happened that it remained over it for a long time, the sons of Israel were on watch for the Lord and did not set out

**9:20** for as many days as the cloud was over the tabernacle. At the command of the Lord they set up the tents, and at His command they took them down.

**9:21** If the cloud was there from evening until morning, and immediately at daybreak left the tabernacle, they set out; and if after a day and a night it withdrew, they dismantled the tents.

**9:22** But if for two days or one month or a longer time it was over the tabernacle, the sons of Israel remained in the same place and did not set out; but as soon as it withdrew, they moved camp.

**9:23** By the word of the Lord they pitched the tents, and by His word they set out; and they were on watch for the Lord according to His command by the hand of Moses.

## Translator Notes

- **9:5** — The Vulgate reads in monte Sinai ("on Mount Sinai"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text and Septuagint read "in the wilderness of Sinai" (as in 9:1).
- **9:6** — Latin super anima hominis ("on account of the soul of a person," also in 9:7 and 9:10) literally reproduces the Hebrew idiom (le-nefesh adam) for ritual impurity contracted through contact with a human corpse.
- **9:10** — The Vulgate reads in gente vestra ("in your nation"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "to you or to your generations."
- **9:11** — Latin lactucis agrestibus specifically identifies the Passover bitter herbs (Hebrew merorim) as "wild lettuce," consistent with the Vulgate of Exodus 12:8.
- **9:19** — The Vulgate connects verses 19 and 20 into a single sentence ("did not set out for as many days as the cloud was over the tabernacle"), whereas the Hebrew begins a new clause in verse 20; in verses 19 and 23, erant in excubiis Domini uses a Latin military phrase for keeping watch.
