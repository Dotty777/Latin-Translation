## Text

**22:1** And they set out and encamped in the plains of Moab, where Jericho is situated across the Jordan.

**22:2** Now Balak son of Zippor, seeing everything that Israel had done to the Amorite,

**22:3** and that the Moabites had become terrified of him and could not endure his assault,

**22:4** said to the elders of Midian, "This people will destroy all who dwell within our borders, just as an ox is accustomed to crop the grass down to the roots." He was king in Moab at that time.

**22:5** Therefore he sent messengers to Balaam son of Beor, a diviner, who lived beside the river of the land of the sons of Ammon, to call him and say, "Look, a people has come out of Egypt that has covered the surface of the earth, settling opposite me.

**22:6** Come, therefore, and curse this people, because it is stronger than I am, if perhaps I may be able to strike it and drive it out from my land. For I know that the one whom you bless is blessed, and the one upon whom you heap curses is cursed."

**22:7** And the elders of Moab and the elders of Midian went forth, having the fee for divination in their hands. And when they had come to Balaam and had reported to him all the words of Balak,

**22:8** he answered, "Stay here for the night, and I will answer whatever the Lord tells me." While they were staying with Balaam, God came and said to him,

**22:9** "What do these men with you want?"

**22:10** He answered, "Balak son of Zippor, king of the Moabites, sent to me,

**22:11** saying, 'Look, a people that has come out of Egypt has covered the surface of the earth; come and curse it, if perhaps by fighting I may be able to drive it away.'"

**22:12** And God said to Balaam, "Do not go with them, and do not curse the people, because it is blessed."

**22:13** And rising in the morning, he said to the princes, "Go to your land, because the Lord has forbidden me to come with you."

**22:14** Returning, the princes said to Balak, "Balaam refused to come with us."

**22:15** Again he sent many more, and more noble than those he had sent before.

**22:16** When they had come to Balaam, they said, "This is what Balak son of Zippor says: 'Do not hesitate to come to me;

**22:17** I am ready to honor you, and whatever you want I will give to you. Come and curse this people.'"

**22:18** Balaam answered, "If Balak gives me his house full of silver and gold, I will not be able to change the word of the Lord my God, so as to speak either more or less.

**22:19** I implore you to stay here tonight as well, so that I may know what the Lord will answer me again."

**22:20** Therefore God came to Balaam at night and said to him, "If these men have come to call you, rise and go with them, provided only that you do what I command you."

**22:21** Balaam rose in the morning, and having saddled his donkey, he set out with them.

**22:22** And God became angry. And the angel of the Lord stood in the road against Balaam, who was riding on the donkey and had two servants with him.

**22:23** Seeing the angel standing in the road with a drawn sword, the donkey turned aside from the way and went through the field. And when Balaam was beating her and wanted to bring her back to the path,

**22:24** the angel stood in a narrow passage between two stone walls by which vineyards were enclosed.

**22:25** Seeing him, the donkey pressed herself against the wall and crushed the rider's foot. But he beat her again.

**22:26** And nevertheless, the angel, passing on to a narrow place where she could not turn aside either to the right or to the left, stood directly in the way.

**22:27** And when the donkey had seen the angel standing, she collapsed beneath the rider's feet; and he, angry, beat her sides more fiercely with a staff.

**22:28** And the Lord opened the mouth of the donkey, and she spoke: "What have I done to you? Why are you striking me now already a third time?"

**22:29** Balaam answered, "Because you have deserved it and have mocked me! If only I had a sword, so that I might strike you!"

**22:30** The donkey said, "Am I not your animal, on which you have always been accustomed to sit up to the present day? Tell me what similar thing I have ever done to you." And he said, "Never."

**22:31** Immediately the Lord opened the eyes of Balaam, and he saw the angel standing in the road with a drawn sword, and he bowed in homage to him, prone to the ground.

**22:32** To him the angel said, "Why are you beating your donkey a third time? I came to oppose you, because your way is perverse and contrary to me.

**22:33** And if the donkey had not turned aside from the road, making room for the one standing in opposition, I would have killed you, and she would be alive."

**22:34** Balaam said, "I have sinned, not knowing that you were standing against me; and now, if it displeases you that I go, I will return."

**22:35** The angel said, "Go with these men, and be careful not to speak anything other than what I command you." Therefore he went with the princes.

**22:36** When Balak heard this, he went out to meet him in a town of the Moabites that is situated on the farthest borders of the Arnon.

**22:37** And he said to Balaam, "I sent messengers to call you; why did you not come to me immediately? Is it because I am unable to pay a reward for your coming?"

**22:38** To him he answered, "Here I am; will I be able to speak anything other than what God puts in my mouth?"

**22:39** Therefore they went on together, and came to a city that was on the farthest borders of his kingdom.

**22:40** And when Balak had slaughtered oxen and sheep, he sent gifts to Balaam and to the princes who were with him.

**22:41** Now when morning had come, he led him to the high places of Baal, and he looked upon the outermost part of the people.

## Translator Notes

- **22:5** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew place-name Pethor as a common noun, "a diviner" (ariolum), and reads "the sons of Ammon" (filiorum Ammon) where the Masoretic Text has "the sons of his people."
- **22:9** — The Vulgate asks "What do these men with you want?" (Quid sibi volunt), whereas the Hebrew text asks "Who are these men with you?"
- **22:22** — The Vulgate simply states "And God became angry" (Et iratus est Deus), omitting the Hebrew causal clause "because he was going."
- **22:39** — Where the Hebrew text gives the place-name Kiriath-huzoth, the Vulgate translates it descriptively as "a city that was on the farthest borders of his kingdom."
