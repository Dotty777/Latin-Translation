## Text

**11:1** Meanwhile a murmuring of the people, as of those grieving over hardship, arose against the Lord. When the Lord heard this, He became angry. And the fire of the Lord, kindled against them, devoured the outermost part of the camp.

**11:2** And when the people cried out to Moses, Moses prayed to the Lord, and the fire was swallowed up.

**11:3** And he called the name of that place Burning, because the fire of the Lord had been kindled against them.

**11:4** For the mixed rabble that had gone up with them burned with craving, sitting and weeping, with the sons of Israel likewise joined to them, and said, "Who will give us meat to eat?

**11:5** We remember the fish that we used to eat in Egypt at no cost; into our mind come the cucumbers, and the melons, and the leeks, and the onions, and the garlic.

**11:6** Our soul is dried up; our eyes look upon nothing else except manna."

**11:7** Now the manna was like coriander seed, of the color of bdellium.

**11:8** And the people went around, and gathering it, ground it with a millstone or pounded it in a mortar, cooking it in a pot and making from it small cakes with a taste like oiled bread.

**11:9** And when the dew descended upon the camp at night, the manna also descended along with it.

**11:10** Therefore Moses heard the people weeping by families, each one at the entrance of his tent. And the wrath of the Lord was greatly angered, and to Moses also the matter seemed unbearable,

**11:11** and he said to the Lord, "Why have You afflicted Your servant? Why do I not find favor before You? And why have You laid the weight of this entire people upon me?

**11:12** Did I conceive all this multitude, or give birth to it, that You should say to me, 'Carry them in your bosom as a nurse is accustomed to carry a little infant, and bring them to the land concerning which You swore to their fathers'?

**11:13** Where can I get meat to give to so great a multitude? They weep against me, saying, 'Give us meat so that we may eat.'

**11:14** I cannot bear all this people alone, because it is heavy for me.

**11:15** But if it seems otherwise to You, I beg that You kill me, and that I may find favor in Your eyes, so that I may not be afflicted with such great evils."

**11:16** And the Lord said to Moses, "Gather to Me seventy men from the elders of Israel, whom you know to be elders of the people and overseers; and you shall lead them to the entrance of the tabernacle of the covenant, and make them stand there with you,

**11:17** so that I may come down and speak to you; and I will take from your spirit and impart it to them, so that they may bear with you the burden of the people, and you alone may not be burdened.

**11:18** And to the people also you shall say, 'Sanctify yourselves (tomorrow you will eat meat, for I have heard you say, "Who will give us meat for food? It was well with us in Egypt"), so that the Lord may give you meat, and you may eat,

**11:19** not for one day, nor two, or five, or ten, nor even twenty,

**11:20** but up to a month of days, until it comes out through your nostrils and turns into nausea, because you have rejected the Lord, who is in the middle of you, and have wept before Him, saying, "Why did we come out of Egypt?"'"

**11:21** And Moses said, "There are six hundred thousand men on foot in this people, and You say, 'I will give them meat to eat for a whole month'?

**11:22** Will a multitude of sheep and oxen be slaughtered, so that it can suffice for food? Or will all the fish of the sea be gathered together, so that they may satisfy them?"

**11:23** The Lord answered him, "Is the hand of the Lord powerless? Right now you will see whether My word is fulfilled in deed."

**11:24** Moses therefore came and told the people the words of the Lord, gathering seventy men from the elders of Israel, whom he made to stand around the tabernacle.

**11:25** And the Lord descended through the cloud and spoke to him, taking from the spirit that was in Moses and giving it to the seventy men. And when the spirit had rested in them, they prophesied, and did not cease anymore.

**11:26** Now two men had remained in the camp, one of whom was called Eldad, and the other Medad, upon whom the spirit rested. For they too had been enrolled, and had not gone out to the tabernacle.

**11:27** And while they were prophesying in the camp, a boy ran and reported to Moses, saying, "Eldad and Medad are prophesying in the camp."

**11:28** Immediately Joshua the son of Nun, the attendant of Moses and chosen from among many, said, "My lord Moses, forbid them."

**11:29** But he said, "Why are you jealous for my sake? Who would grant that all the people might prophesy, and that the Lord would give them His Spirit?"

**11:30** And Moses and the elders of Israel returned to the camp.

**11:31** Now a wind going out from the Lord, sweeping up quail from across the sea, brought them and dropped them into the camp, as far as a journey that can be completed in one day on every side of the camp all around, and they were flying in the air at a height of two cubits above the ground.

**11:32** Therefore the people, rising up all that day, and the night, and the next day, gathered quail; the one who gathered little gathered ten kors; and they dried them all around the camp.

**11:33** The meat was still in their teeth, and food of this kind had not failed, and look, the wrath of the Lord, aroused against the people, struck them with an exceedingly great plague.

**11:34** And that place was called Graves of Craving, for there they buried the people who had craved. And having gone out from Graves of Craving, they came to Hazeroth and remained there.

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — Latin Incensio ("Burning") translates the meaning of the Hebrew place name Taberah rather than transliterating it.
- **11:15** — Vulgate Sin aliter tibi videtur ("But if it seems otherwise to You") and the coordinated clause et inveniam gratiam ("and that I may find favor") diverge from the Hebrew, which reads "If this is how you are going to treat me... if I have found favor in your sight."
- **11:25** — Vulgate nec ultra cessaverunt ("and did not cease anymore") conveys the opposite sense of the Hebrew ve-lo yasafu, normally understood as "and they did not do so again."
- **11:28** — Vulgate electus e pluribus ("chosen from among many") reflects the Hebrew root "to choose," whereas the Masoretic vocalization means "from his youth."
- **11:34** — Latin Sepulchra concupiscentiae ("Graves of Craving") translates the Hebrew place name Kibroth-hattaavah; the Clementine Vulgate also combines Masoretic Text 11:34 and 11:35 into a single verse, giving chapter 11 thirty-four verses.
