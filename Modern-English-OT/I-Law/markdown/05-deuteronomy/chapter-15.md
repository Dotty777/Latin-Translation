## Text

**15:1** In the seventh year you shall grant a remission,

**15:2** which shall be observed in this manner. Anyone to whom something is owed by his friend or neighbor and brother shall not be able to demand it back, because it is the Lord's year of remission.

**15:3** From a foreigner and a stranger you may exact it; you shall not have the authority to demand it back from a fellow citizen and relative.

**15:4** And there shall not be any needy person or beggar at all among you, so that the Lord your God may bless you in the land that He is about to hand over to you for a possession.

**15:5** If only you listen to the voice of the Lord your God, and keep all things that He has commanded and that I command you today, He will bless you, as He has promised.

**15:6** You shall lend to many nations, and you yourself shall borrow from no one. You shall rule over very many nations, and no one shall rule over you.

**15:7** If one of your brothers who dwell within the gates of your city, in the land that the Lord your God is about to give you, comes to poverty, you shall not harden your heart nor close your hand,

**15:8** but you shall open it to the poor man, and you shall lend him what you see he needs.

**15:9** Take care that a wicked thought does not steal into you, and you say in your heart, "The seventh year of remission is drawing near," and you turn your eyes away from your poor brother, unwilling to lend him what he requests, so that he does not cry out against you to the Lord, and it becomes a sin for you.

**15:10** Rather, you shall give to him, nor shall you do anything craftily in relieving his needs, so that the Lord your God may bless you at all times, and in everything to which you put your hand.

**15:11** The poor will not be lacking in the land of your dwelling; therefore I command you to open your hand to your needy and poor brother who lives with you in the land.

**15:12** When your brother, a Hebrew man or a Hebrew woman, is sold to you and has served you for six years, in the seventh year you shall send him away free;

**15:13** and the one to whom you have granted freedom you shall by no means allow to go away empty-handed;

**15:14** but you shall give provisions for the journey from your flocks, and from your threshing floor, and your winepress, with which the Lord your God has blessed you.

**15:15** Remember that you yourself also served in the land of Egypt, and that the Lord your God freed you, and therefore I now command you.

**15:16** But if he says, "I do not want to leave," because he loves you and your household, and feels that he is well off with you,

**15:17** you shall take an awl and pierce his ear at the door of your house, and he shall serve you forever. To a female servant also you shall do likewise.

**15:18** Do not turn your eyes away from them when you send them away free, because according to the wages of a hired laborer he has served you for six years, so that the Lord your God may bless you in all the works that you do.

**15:19** Of the firstborn that are born in your herds and in your sheep, whatever is of the male sex you shall consecrate to the Lord your God. You shall not work with the firstborn of the ox, and you shall not shear the firstborn of the sheep.

**15:20** In the sight of the Lord your God you shall eat them year by year in the place that the Lord chooses, you and your household.

**15:21** But if it has a blemish, or is lame, or blind, or deformed or weak in any part, it shall not be sacrificed to the Lord your God;

**15:22** but you shall eat it within the gates of your city; both the clean and the unclean alike shall eat of them, like the roe deer and the stag.

**15:23** This alone you shall observe, that you do not eat their blood, but you shall pour it out on the ground like water.

## Translator Notes

- **15:5** — The Vulgate places "He will bless you, as He has promised" at the close of verse 5 (and includes both "that He has commanded" and "that I command you today"), whereas the Hebrew places that blessing clause at the start of verse 6.
- **15:10** — Latin "nor shall you do anything craftily in relieving his needs" (nec ages quippiam callide in ejus necessitatibus sublevandis) is distinctly Vulgate; the Hebrew reads "nor shall your heart be grieved when you give to him."
- **15:14** — Latin "viaticum" literally means "provisions for the journey," giving a concrete sense to the Hebrew command to furnish the freed servant generously.
- **15:18** — Latin "according to the wages of a hired laborer" (juxta mercedem mercenarii) lacks the word "double" found in the Hebrew ("double the wages of a hired laborer"), and "Do not turn your eyes away from them" replaces the Hebrew idiom "Let it not seem hard in your eyes."
