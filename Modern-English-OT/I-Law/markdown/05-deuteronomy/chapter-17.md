## Text

**17:1** You shall not sacrifice to the Lord your God a sheep or an ox in which there is a blemish or any kind of defect, because it is an abomination to the Lord your God.

**17:2** When a man or a woman is found among you within one of your gates that the Lord your God will give you, who does evil in the sight of the Lord your God and transgresses His covenant,

**17:3** so as to go and serve foreign gods and worship them, the sun and the moon, and all the host of heaven, which I did not command,

**17:4** and this is reported to you, and hearing it, you inquire diligently and find it to be true, and an abomination has been committed in Israel,

**17:5** you shall bring out the man and the woman who committed a most wicked deed to the gates of your city, and they shall be overwhelmed with stones.

**17:6** By the mouth of two or three witnesses, the one who is to be put to death shall perish. Let no one be killed when one person gives testimony against him.

**17:7** The hand of the witnesses shall first put him to death, and the hand of the rest of the people shall be put forth last, so that you may remove the evil from your midst.

**17:8** If you perceive that a judgment before you is difficult and uncertain, between blood and blood, case and case, leprosy and leprosy, and you see that the words of the judges within your gates vary, rise and go up to the place that the Lord your God will choose.

**17:9** And you shall come to the priests of the Levitical lineage and to the judge who is there at that time; and you shall inquire of them, and they will declare to you the truth of the judgment.

**17:10** And you shall do whatever those who preside over the place that the Lord will choose say and teach you

**17:11** according to His law, and you shall follow their decision, and you shall not turn aside to the right or to the left.

**17:12** As for the one who acts proudly, unwilling to obey the command of the priest who ministers at that time to the Lord your God, and the decree of the judge, that man shall die, and you shall remove the evil from Israel;

**17:13** and all the people, hearing this, will fear, so that no one from then on may swell with pride.

**17:14** When you have entered the land that the Lord your God will give you, and have possessed it and dwelt in it, and say, "I will appoint a king over myself, just as all the nations all around have,"

**17:15** you shall appoint the one whom the Lord your God will choose from the number of your brothers. You may not make a man of another nation king, who is not your brother.

**17:16** And when he has been appointed, he shall not multiply horses for himself, nor lead the people back to Egypt, lifted up by the number of cavalry, especially since the Lord has commanded you that you should never again return by that same way.

**17:17** He shall not have very many wives, who might entice his mind, nor immense weights of silver and gold.

**17:18** Now after he has sat on the throne of his kingdom, he shall write out for himself the Deuteronomy of this law on a scroll, receiving the copy from the priests of the Levitical tribe,

**17:19** and he shall keep it with him, and he shall read it all the days of his life, so that he may learn to fear the Lord his God and to keep His words and ceremonies that have been commanded in the law.

**17:20** And let his heart not be lifted up in pride over his brothers, nor let him turn aside to the right side or the left, so that he and his sons may reign for a long time over Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **17:5** — The Vulgate reads plural virum ac mulierem ("the man and the woman"), whereas the Hebrew text maintains the singular alternative ("that man or that woman").
- **17:8** — Latin lepram et lepram ("leprosy and leprosy") narrows the broader Hebrew phrase ("stroke and stroke" or "plague and plague") to skin disease.
- **17:16** — Latin equitatus numero sublevatus ("lifted up by the number of cavalry") is a distinctive Vulgate rendering where the Hebrew reads "in order to multiply horses."
- **17:18** — The Vulgate retains the Greek loanword Deuteronomium ("second law" or "copy of the law," whence the book's English title) for Hebrew mishneh ("copy"), and explicitly supplies accipiens exemplar ("receiving the copy").
