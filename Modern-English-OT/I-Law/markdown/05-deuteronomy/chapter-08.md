## Text

**8:1** Be diligently careful to do every commandment that I command you today, so that you may be able to live and be multiplied, and, having entered, possess the land about which the Lord swore to your fathers.

**8:2** And you shall remember the entire journey along which the Lord your God led you for forty years through the desert, so that He might afflict you and test you, and that what was turning over in your mind might become known, whether you would keep His commandments or not.

**8:3** He afflicted you with scarcity and gave you manna as food, which you and your fathers did not know, so that He might show you that a human being does not live on bread alone, but on every word that comes out from the mouth of God.

**8:4** Your garment, with which you were covered, did not wear out at all with age, and your foot was not worn sore; look, it is the fortieth year,

**8:5** so that you may reflect in your heart that just as a man trains his son, so the Lord your God has trained you,

**8:6** so that you may keep the commandments of the Lord your God, and walk in His ways, and fear Him.

**8:7** For the Lord your God will bring you into a good land, a land of streams, waters, and springs, in whose plains and mountains the depths of rivers burst forth,

**8:8** a land of wheat, barley, and vineyards, in which figs, pomegranates, and olive groves grow; a land of oil and honey,

**8:9** where without any scarcity you will eat your bread and thoroughly enjoy an abundance of all things; its stones are iron, and from its mountains copper ores are dug,

**8:10** so that when you have eaten and are satisfied, you may bless the Lord your God for the excellent land that He has given you.

**8:11** Watch closely and be on guard so that you never forget the Lord your God and neglect His commandments, judgments, and ceremonies, which I command you today;

**8:12** otherwise, after you have eaten and are satisfied, and have built beautiful houses and lived in them,

**8:13** and have herds of cattle and flocks of sheep, and plenty of silver and gold and of all things,

**8:14** your heart might be lifted up, and you might fail to remember the Lord your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery,

**8:15** and was your guide in the great and terrifying wilderness, in which there was the serpent scorching with its breath, and the scorpion, and the dipsas, and no waters at all; who brought streams out of the hardest rock,

**8:16** and fed you with manna in the wilderness, which your fathers did not know. And after He afflicted and proved you, in the end He had mercy on you,

**8:17** so that you would not say in your heart, "My might and the strength of my hand have provided all these things for me,"

**8:18** but would remember the Lord your God, that He Himself has given you strength, so that He might fulfill His covenant concerning which He swore to your fathers, just as the present day shows.

**8:19** But if, forgetting the Lord your God, you follow foreign gods and worship them and adore them, look, now I foretell to you that you will completely perish.

**8:20** Just as the nations that the Lord wiped out at your entrance, so you also will perish, if you are disobedient to the voice of the Lord your God.

## Translator Notes

- **8:3** — The Vulgate reads in omni verbo quod egreditur de ore Dei ("on every word that comes out from the mouth of God"), supplying verbo ("word") and reading Dei ("of God") in agreement with the Septuagint and Matthew 4:4, whereas the Hebrew lacks "word" and uses the divine name.
- **8:4** — The Vulgate reads non est subtritus ("was not worn sore" or chafed) rather than the Hebrew "did not swell," and ends with en quadragesimus annus est ("look, it is the fortieth year") rather than "these forty years."
- **8:15** — The Vulgate renders the wilderness perils vividly as serpens flatu adurens ("the serpent scorching with its breath") and dipsas, an ancient desert viper whose bite was said to cause fatal thirst, interpreting the Hebrew word for "thirsty ground."
- **8:16** — The Vulgate reads ad extremum misertus est tui ("in the end He had mercy on you") and subordinates verse 17 directly to it as a purpose clause, whereas the Hebrew reads "to do good to you in your latter end."
- **8:18** — The Vulgate reads quod ipse vires tibi praebuerit ("that He Himself has given you strength") without the Hebrew phrase "to gain wealth," and adds indicat ("shows") in sicut praesens indicat dies.
