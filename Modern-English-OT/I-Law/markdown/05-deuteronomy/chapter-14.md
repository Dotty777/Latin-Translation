## Text

**14:1** Be sons of the Lord your God; you shall not cut yourselves, nor make baldness over a dead person,

**14:2** for you are a holy people to the Lord your God, and He has chosen you to be for Him a special people out of all the nations that are upon the earth.

**14:3** Do not eat things that are unclean.

**14:4** This is the animal that you may eat: the ox, and the sheep, and the goat,

**14:5** the stag and the roe deer, the wild ox, the goat-stag, the white-rumped antelope, the oryx, and the giraffe.

**14:6** You may eat every animal that splits the hoof into two parts and chews the cud.

**14:7** However, of those that chew the cud and do not split the hoof, you must not eat, such as the camel, the hare, and the rock badger; because these chew the cud and do not divide the hoof, they shall be unclean to you.

**14:8** The pig also, since it divides the hoof and does not chew the cud, shall be unclean. You shall not eat their flesh, and you shall not touch their carcasses.

**14:9** These you may eat from all that dwell in the waters: eat those that have fins and scales;

**14:10** do not eat those that are without fins and scales, because they are unclean.

**14:11** Eat all clean birds.

**14:12** Do not eat the unclean ones: namely, the eagle, and the griffin, and the sea eagle,

**14:13** the ixion, and the vulture, and the kite according to its kind;

**14:14** and everything of the raven kind;

**14:15** and the ostrich, and the owl, and the gull, and the hawk according to its kind;

**14:16** the heron, and the swan, and the ibis,

**14:17** and the cormorant, the purple swamphen, and the night raven,

**14:18** the pelican, and the plover, each in its own kind; also the hoopoe and the bat.

**14:19** And everything that creeps and has wings shall be unclean, and it shall not be eaten.

**14:20** Eat everything that is clean.

**14:21** However, whatever has died of itself, do not eat of it. Give it to the foreigner who is within your gates so that he may eat it, or sell it to him, because you are a holy people of the Lord your God. You shall not boil a young goat in its mother's milk.

**14:22** You shall set apart a tenth part from all your produce that grows in the land year by year,

**14:23** and you shall eat in the sight of the Lord your God, in the place that He chooses so that His name may be called upon in it, the tenth of your grain, and wine, and oil, and the firstborn of your herds and your sheep, so that you may learn to fear the Lord your God at all times.

**14:24** But when the way is too long, and the place that the Lord your God chooses is too distant, and He has blessed you, and you cannot carry all these things to it,

**14:25** you shall sell everything, and convert it into money, and carry it in your hand, and travel to the place that the Lord your God chooses;

**14:26** and with that same money you shall buy whatever pleases you, whether from the herds or from the sheep, wine also and strong drink, and everything that your soul desires; and you shall eat before the Lord your God, and you shall feast, you and your household;

**14:27** and as for the Levite who is within your gates, take care not to abandon him, because he has no other portion in your possession.

**14:28** In the third year you shall set apart another tenth from all things that grow for you at that time, and you shall store it within your doors.

**14:29** And the Levite, who has no other portion or possession with you, shall come, and the foreigner, and the orphan, and the widow, who are within your gates, and they shall eat and be filled, so that the Lord your God may bless you in all the works of your hands that you do.

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — Latin uses the imperative "estote" ("Be sons") where the Hebrew and Septuagint have a declarative statement ("You are sons").
- **14:5** — The Vulgate follows the Septuagint's Greek zoological terms here, rendering the final five clean wild animals as wild ox (bubalum), goat-stag (tragelaphum), white-rumped antelope (pygargum), oryx (orygem), and giraffe (camelopardalum).
- **14:13** — Latin "ixion" is a Vulgate-distinct bird name (likely derived from a textual form of Greek iktinos, "kite") denoting a bird of prey; likewise "gryphem" ("griffin") in 14:12 follows the Septuagint term for a large carrion vulture such as the lammergeier.
- **14:21** — The Vulgate mentions only "the foreigner who is within your gates" (peregrino) for both giving and selling, whereas the Hebrew distinguishes the resident alien (to whom it may be given) from the foreigner (to whom it may be sold).
