## Text

**24:1** If a man takes a wife and has her, and she does not find favor before his eyes because of some indecency, he shall write a document of divorce and place it in her hand, and send her away from his house.

**24:2** And when, after going out, she marries another husband,

**24:3** and he also hates her, and gives her a document of divorce, and sends her away from his house, or indeed dies,

**24:4** the former husband will not be able to take her back as a wife, because she has been defiled and has become abominable before the Lord, so that you do not cause your land to sin, which the Lord your God delivers to you to possess.

**24:5** When a man has recently taken a wife, he shall not go out to war, nor shall any public obligation be imposed on him, but he shall remain free without blame at his own home, so that for one year he may rejoice with his wife.

**24:6** You shall not take the lower and the upper millstone as a pledge, because he has pledged his own life to you.

**24:7** If a man is caught enticing away his brother from the sons of Israel, and after selling him receives the price, he shall be put to death, and you shall remove the evil from your midst.

**24:8** Watch carefully so that you do not incur the plague of leprosy, but you shall do whatever the priests of the Levitical lineage teach you, according to what I commanded them, and fulfill it diligently.

**24:9** Remember what the Lord your God did to Miriam on the way when you were coming out of Egypt.

**24:10** When you claim back from your neighbor anything that he owes you, you shall not enter his house to take away a pledge,

**24:11** but you shall stand outside, and he shall bring out to you what he has.

**24:12** But if he is poor, the pledge shall not stay overnight with you,

**24:13** but you shall immediately return it to him before sunset, so that, sleeping in his own garment, he may bless you, and you may have righteousness before the Lord your God.

**24:14** You shall not deny the wages of your needy and poor brother, or of the sojourner who resides with you in the land and is within your gates,

**24:15** but on the same day you shall pay him the price of his labor before sunset, because he is poor and sustains his life by it; otherwise he will cry out against you to the Lord, and it will be counted to you as sin.

**24:16** Fathers shall not be put to death for sons, nor sons for fathers, but each one shall die for his own sin.

**24:17** You shall not pervert the justice of the sojourner and the orphan, nor shall you take away a widow's garment as a pledge.

**24:18** Remember that you were a slave in Egypt, and the Lord your God rescued you from there. Therefore I command you to do this thing.

**24:19** When you harvest the crop in your field and, forgetting, leave a sheaf, you shall not turn back to pick it up, but you shall allow the sojourner, the orphan, and the widow to take it away, so that the Lord your God may bless you in every work of your hands.

**24:20** If you gather the fruit of olive trees, whatever remains on the trees you shall not turn back to gather, but you shall leave it for the sojourner, the orphan, and the widow.

**24:21** If you gather the vintage of your vineyard, you shall not gather the remaining clusters, but they shall go to the use of the sojourner, the orphan, and the widow.

**24:22** Remember that you also were a slave in Egypt, and therefore I command you to do this thing.

## Translator Notes

- **24:1** — The Vulgate makes verse 1 a complete conditional sentence with main future verbs ("he shall write... place... and send her away"), whereas the Hebrew treats verses 1-3 as a single subordinate condition leading to the prohibition in verse 4.
- **24:4** — The Vulgate literally reads "and has become abominable before the Lord," making the wife the subject, whereas the Hebrew refers to the act of taking her back as an abomination.
- **24:5** — Latin laetetur cum uxore sua ("may rejoice with his wife") uses an intransitive verb, whereas the Hebrew reads "make his wife glad."
- **24:8** — The Vulgate reads ne incurras plagam leprae ("so that you do not incur the plague of leprosy"), framing the caution as avoiding infection rather than strictly observing rules during an outbreak as in the Hebrew.
