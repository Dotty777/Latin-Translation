## Text

**30:1** "Therefore, when all these words have come upon you, the blessing or the curse which I have set in your sight, and you are led by repentance of your heart among all the nations into which the Lord your God has scattered you,

**30:2** and you return to Him and obey His commands, just as I command you today, with your children, with all your heart and with all your soul,

**30:3** the Lord your God will bring back your captivity, and will have mercy on you, and will gather you again from all the peoples among whom He previously scattered you.

**30:4** If you have been dispersed to the poles of heaven, from there the Lord your God will draw you back,

**30:5** and will take you, and will bring you into the land which your fathers possessed, and you will obtain it; and blessing you, He will make you greater in number than your fathers were.

**30:6** The Lord your God will circumcise your heart and the heart of your descendants, so that you may love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul, so that you may be able to live.

**30:7** And He will turn all these curses upon your enemies, and upon those who hate and persecute you.

**30:8** But you will return and listen to the voice of the Lord your God, and do all the commandments that I command you today;

**30:9** and the Lord your God will make you abound in all the works of your hands, in the offspring of your womb, and in the fruit of your livestock, in the fertility of your land, and in the bounty of all things. For the Lord will return to rejoice over you in all good things, just as He rejoiced in your fathers,

**30:10** if indeed you listen to the voice of the Lord your God, and keep His precepts and ceremonies, which are written in this law, and return to the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul.

**30:11** This commandment, which I command you today, is not above you, nor placed far away,

**30:12** nor situated in heaven, so that you might say, 'Who among us is able to ascend to heaven, so that he may bring it down to us, and we may hear it and fulfill it in deed?'

**30:13** nor placed across the sea, so that you might make excuses and say, 'Who from among us will be able to cross the sea, and bring it all the way to us, so that we may be able to hear and do what has been commanded?'

**30:14** but the word is very near to you, in your mouth and in your heart, so that you may do it.

**30:15** Consider that today I have set in your sight life and good, and on the contrary, death and evil,

**30:16** so that you may love the Lord your God, and walk in His ways, and keep His commandments, ceremonies, and judgments, and you may live, and He may multiply you and bless you in the land that you will enter to possess.

**30:17** But if your heart turns away, and you refuse to listen, and, deceived by error, you worship foreign gods and serve them,

**30:18** I declare to you today that you will perish, and will remain for a short time in the land which, once the Jordan has been crossed, you will enter to possess.

**30:19** I call heaven and earth as witnesses today that I have set before you life and death, blessing and curse. Choose life, therefore, so that both you and your descendants may live,

**30:20** and you may love the Lord your God, and obey His voice, and hold fast to Him (for He Himself is your life and the length of your days), so that you may dwell in the land concerning which the Lord swore to your fathers, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, that He would give it to them."

## Translator Notes

- **30:1** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew idiom 'bring back to your heart' explicitly as ductus poenitudine cordis tui ('led by repentance of your heart') and joins 'blessing' and 'curse' with sive ('or').
- **30:4** — The Latin ad cardines caeli literally refers to the 'hinges' or 'poles of heaven,' Jerome's poetic expression for the farthest ends of the sky.
- **30:13** — The Vulgate adds ut causeris ('so that you might make excuses') before 'and say,' and expands the end of the clause to ut possimus audire et facere quod praeceptum est ('so that we may be able to hear and do what has been commanded').
- **30:16** — Unlike the Hebrew ('in that I command you today to love...'), the Vulgate omits 'in that I command you today' and connects verse 16 directly to verse 15 as a purpose clause (ut diligas, 'so that you may love').
