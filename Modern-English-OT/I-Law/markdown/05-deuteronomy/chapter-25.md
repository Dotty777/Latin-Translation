## Text

**25:1** If there is a dispute between some people and they appeal to the judges, to the one whom they discern to be righteous they shall give the palm of righteousness; the one who is wicked they shall condemn for wickedness.

**25:2** But if they see that the one who has sinned is deserving of blows, they shall lay him down and have him beaten in their presence. According to the measure of the sin shall also be the limit of the blows,

**25:3** provided only that they do not exceed the number of forty, so that your brother does not depart shamefully torn before your eyes.

**25:4** You shall not bind the mouth of the ox treading out your produce on the threshing floor.

**25:5** When brothers live together, and one of them dies without children, the wife of the deceased shall not marry another, but his brother shall take her and raise up the seed of his brother,

**25:6** and he shall call the firstborn son from her by his name, so that his name may not be blotted out from Israel.

**25:7** But if he is unwilling to take his brother's wife, who is owed to him by law, the woman shall go to the gate of the city and appeal to the elders, and say, "My husband's brother is unwilling to raise up his brother's name in Israel, or to take me as a wife."

**25:8** And immediately they shall have him summoned and question him. If he answers, "I am unwilling to take her as a wife,"

**25:9** the woman shall approach him before the elders, and take the sandal off his foot, and spit in his face, and say, "So it shall be done to the man who does not build his brother's house."

**25:10** And his name shall be called in Israel, "The House of the Unshod."

**25:11** If two men have a quarrel between themselves, and one begins to fight against the other, and the wife of one of them, wanting to rescue her husband from the hand of the stronger one, stretches out her hand and seizes his private parts,

**25:12** you shall cut off her hand, nor shall you be moved by any mercy toward her.

**25:13** You shall not have differing weights in a bag, a larger and a smaller,

**25:14** nor shall there be in your house a larger and a smaller measure.

**25:15** You shall have a just and true weight, and you shall have an equal and true measure, so that you may live a long time upon the land that the Lord your God gives you.

**25:16** For your Lord abhors the one who does these things and recoils from all injustice.

**25:17** Remember what Amalek did to you on the way when you were coming out of Egypt,

**25:18** how he met you and cut down the hindmost of your column, who were lingering behind weary, when you were worn out by hunger and toil, and he did not fear God.

**25:19** Therefore, when the Lord your God gives you rest and subjects all the surrounding nations in the land that He has promised to you, you shall blot out his name from under heaven. Take care that you do not forget.

## Translator Notes

- **25:1** — Latin justitiae palmam dabunt ("they shall give the palm of righteousness") uses a classical Roman victory metaphor to render the Hebrew "acquit the righteous."
- **25:3** — Latin foede laceratus... abeat ("depart shamefully torn") emphasizes physical laceration, whereas the Hebrew focuses on the brother being degraded in one's eyes.
- **25:4** — The Vulgate expands on the Hebrew ("while it is treading") by adding in area fruges tuas ("your produce on the threshing floor") and literally renders the verb as ligabis os ("bind the mouth").
- **25:16** — The Clementine Vulgate reads Dominus tuus ("your Lord"), without Deus ("God") as in the Hebrew text.
