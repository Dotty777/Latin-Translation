## Text

**34:1** So Moses went up from the plains of Moab onto Mount Nebo, to the summit of Pisgah opposite Jericho; and the Lord showed him all the land of Gilead as far as Dan,

**34:2** and all Naphtali, and the land of Ephraim and Manasseh, and all the land of Judah as far as the farthest sea,

**34:3** and the southern part, and the breadth of the plain of Jericho, the city of palms, as far as Zoar.

**34:4** And the Lord said to him: "This is the land about which I swore to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, saying, 'To your offspring I will give it.' You have seen it with your eyes, and you will not cross over to it."

**34:5** And Moses, the servant of the Lord, died there in the land of Moab, at the command of the Lord;

**34:6** and He buried him in the valley of the land of Moab opposite Peor; and no man has known his tomb to the present day.

**34:7** Moses was one hundred and twenty years old when he died; his eye did not grow dim, nor were his teeth loosened.

**34:8** And the sons of Israel wept for him in the plains of Moab for thirty days; and the days of lamentation of those mourning Moses were completed.

**34:9** Now Joshua the son of Nun was filled with the spirit of wisdom, because Moses had laid his hands upon him. And the sons of Israel obeyed him and did just as the Lord had commanded Moses.

**34:10** And no prophet has arisen again in Israel like Moses, whom the Lord knew face to face,

**34:11** in all the signs and wonders which He sent through him to perform in the land of Egypt to Pharaoh, and to all his servants, and to his entire land,

**34:12** and all the mighty hand and the great marvels that Moses performed before all Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **34:2** — Latin mare novissimum ("the farthest sea") translates the Hebrew expression for the Mediterranean ("the western/hinder sea") literally; similarly, australem partem in v. 3 renders the Negev descriptively as "the southern part."
- **34:6** — The Vulgate reads contra Phogor ("opposite Peor") without the prefix "Beth-" found in the Hebrew (Beth-peor).
- **34:7** — Latin nec dentes illius moti sunt ("nor were his teeth loosened") is a distinctive Vulgate rendering of the Hebrew clause usually translated "nor had his vigor fled."
