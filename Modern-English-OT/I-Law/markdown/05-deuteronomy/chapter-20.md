## Text

**20:1** If you go out to war against your enemies, and see cavalry and chariots, and a greater multitude of the opposing army than you have, you shall not fear them, because the Lord your God is with you, who brought you out of the land of Egypt.

**20:2** Now when the battle is already drawing near, the priest shall stand before the battle line and speak to the people in this way:

**20:3** "Hear, Israel: today you are joining battle against your enemies. Let your heart not be terrified; do not be afraid, do not give way, and do not dread them,

**20:4** because the Lord your God is in your midst, and He will fight for you against the adversaries, to rescue you from danger."

**20:5** The commanders also shall proclaim through each troop, with the army listening: "Who is the man who has built a new house and has not dedicated it? Let him go and return to his house, so that he may not perhaps die in war and another dedicate it.

**20:6** Who is the man who has planted a vineyard and has not yet made it common, from which all are permitted to eat? Let him go and return to his house, so that he may not perhaps die in war and another man perform his duty.

**20:7** Who is the man who has betrothed a wife and has not taken her? Let him go and return to his house, so that he may not perhaps die in war and another man take her."

**20:8** When these things have been said, they shall add the rest and speak to the people: "Who is a fearful man and of a trembling heart? Let him go and return to his house, so that he may not make the hearts of his brothers tremble, just as he himself is terrified with fear."

**20:9** And when the commanders of the army have fallen silent and made an end of speaking, each one shall prepare his own battle formations for fighting.

**20:10** Whenever you approach to storm a city, you shall first offer it peace.

**20:11** If it accepts and opens the gates to you, all the people who are in it shall be saved and shall serve you under tribute.

**20:12** But if it is unwilling to enter into a treaty and begins war against you, you shall attack it.

**20:13** And when the Lord your God hands it over into your hand, you shall strike down everything in it that is of the male sex with the edge of the sword,

**20:14** except the women and young children, the livestock, and the rest of the things that are in the city. You shall divide all the plunder for the army, and you shall eat from the spoils of your enemies, which the Lord your God will give you.

**20:15** So you shall do to all the cities that are very far from you, and are not of these cities that you are about to receive as a possession.

**20:16** But of these cities that will be given to you, you shall permit no one at all to live;

**20:17** rather, you shall kill them with the edge of the sword, namely the Hittite and the Amorite and the Canaanite, the Perizzite and the Hivite and the Jebusite, just as the Lord your God has commanded you,

**20:18** so that they may not perhaps teach you to do all the abominations that they themselves have practiced for their gods, and so that you may not sin against the Lord your God.

**20:19** When you besiege a city for a long time and surround it with fortifications in order to storm it, you shall not cut down trees from which one can eat, nor should you lay waste the region all around with axes, for it is a tree, and not a man, nor can it increase the number of those fighting against you.

**20:20** But if any trees are not fruit-bearing, but wild and suited for other uses, cut them down and build siege engines until you capture the city that fights against you.

## Translator Notes

- **20:4** — Vulgate reads in medio vestri est ("is in your midst") and ut eruat vos de periculo ("to rescue you from danger"), where the Hebrew has "goes with you" and "to save you."
- **20:6** — Vulgate expands the Hebrew reference to releasing a vineyard's fruit for common use (cf. Lev 19:23-25) with de qua vesci omnibus liceat ("from which all are permitted to eat") and renders the final clause idiomatically as ejus fungatur officio ("perform his duty").
- **20:9** — Vulgate reads unusquisque suos ad bellandum cuneos præparabit ("each one shall prepare his own battle formations for fighting"), using the Roman military term cuneos, whereas the Hebrew states that commanders of armies shall be appointed at the head of the people.
- **20:19** — Where the Hebrew contains a famously disputed clause often read as a rhetorical question ("for is the tree of the field a man...?"), Vulgate renders it as a clear statement, quoniam lignum est, et non homo ("for it is a tree, and not a man"), and adds nec potest bellantium contra te augere numerum ("nor can it increase the number of those fighting against you").
