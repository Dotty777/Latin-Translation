## Text

**10:1** At that time the Lord said to me, "Carve for yourself two stone tablets, just as the former ones were, and come up to Me onto the mountain, and you will make a wooden ark,

**10:2** and I will write on the tablets the words that were on the ones that you shattered earlier, and you will put them in the ark."

**10:3** Therefore I made an ark of acacia wood. And when I had carved two stone tablets like the former ones, I went up onto the mountain, having them in my hands.

**10:4** And He wrote on the tablets, according to what He had written previously, the ten words that the Lord spoke to you on the mountain from the midst of the fire, when the people were gathered together; and He gave them to me.

**10:5** And turning back from the mountain, I came down, and put the tablets into the ark that I had made, and they are there until now, just as the Lord commanded me.

**10:6** Now the sons of Israel moved camp from Beeroth of the sons of Jaakan to Moserah, where Aaron died and was buried, and in his place his son Eleazar served in the priesthood.

**10:7** From there they came to Gudgodah; and setting out from that place, they camped at Jotbathah, in a land of waters and streams.

**10:8** At that time He set apart the tribe of Levi to carry the ark of the covenant of the Lord, and to stand before Him in ministry, and to bless in His name until the present day.

**10:9** For this reason Levi did not have a portion or a possession with his brothers, because the Lord Himself is his possession, just as the Lord your God promised him.

**10:10** Now I stood on the mountain, as at first, for forty days and nights; and the Lord listened to me this time as well, and was unwilling to destroy you.

**10:11** And He said to me, "Go, and go ahead of the people, so that they may enter and possess the land that I swore to their fathers to hand over to them."

**10:12** And now, Israel, what does the Lord your God ask of you, except that you fear the Lord your God, and walk in His ways, and love Him, and serve the Lord your God with your whole heart and with your whole soul,

**10:13** and keep the commandments of the Lord and His ceremonies, which I command you today, so that it may be well with you?

**10:14** Look, to the Lord your God belong heaven, and the heaven of heaven, the earth, and all things that are in it;

**10:15** and yet the Lord was closely joined to your fathers, and loved them, and chose their seed after them, that is, you, from all the nations, just as is confirmed today.

**10:16** Circumcise, therefore, the foreskin of your heart, and do not harden your neck any longer;

**10:17** for the Lord your God Himself is God of gods and Lord of those who rule, the great, mighty, and fearsome God, who shows no partiality, nor accepts gifts.

**10:18** He executes justice for the orphan and the widow; He loves the foreigner, and gives him food and clothing.

**10:19** Therefore love foreigners yourselves, because you also were strangers in the land of Egypt.

**10:20** You will fear the Lord your God, and serve Him alone; you will cling to Him, and swear in His name.

**10:21** He Himself is your praise and your God, who has done for you these great and fearsome deeds, which your eyes have seen.

**10:22** With seventy souls your fathers went down into Egypt, and look, now the Lord your God has multiplied you like the stars of heaven.

## Translator Notes

- **10:3** — Latin "de lignis setim" transliterates the Hebrew term (shittim) for acacia wood.
- **10:15** — Latin "conglutinatus est" literally means "was glued/bonded closely together with," vividly rendering the Hebrew verb for setting one's affection on someone; "as is confirmed today" (sicut hodie comprobatur) is also a distinctly Vulgate rendering of Hebrew "as at this day."
- **10:17** — Latin "Dominus dominantium" uses a participle (literally "Lord of those who rule"), corresponding to Hebrew "Lord of lords"; "shows no partiality" renders the Latin idiom "personam non accipit" (literally "does not accept a person").
- **10:20** — The Vulgate includes "alone" (ei soli, "Him alone"), agreeing with the Septuagint and the quotation in Matthew 4:10 and Luke 4:8, where the Masoretic Hebrew has simply "Him."
