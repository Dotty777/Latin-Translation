## Text

**23:1** A eunuch with crushed or amputated testicles and a severed male member shall not enter the assembly of the Lord.

**23:2** A mamzer, that is, one born of a prostitute, shall not enter into the assembly of the Lord, up to the tenth generation.

**23:3** An Ammonite and a Moabite, even after the tenth generation, shall not enter the assembly of the Lord forever,

**23:4** because they were unwilling to meet you with bread and water on the road when you came out of Egypt, and because they hired against you Balaam, the son of Beor, from Mesopotamia of Syria, so that he might curse you;

**23:5** and the Lord your God was unwilling to listen to Balaam, and He turned his curse into a blessing for you, because He loved you.

**23:6** You shall not make peace with them, nor seek good things for them all the days of your life forever.

**23:7** You shall not abhor an Edomite, for he is your brother, nor an Egyptian, for you were a resident alien in his land.

**23:8** Those who are born from them shall enter into the assembly of the Lord in the third generation.

**23:9** When you go out against your enemies into battle, you shall guard yourself from every evil thing.

**23:10** If there is a man among you who has been defiled by a nocturnal dream, he shall go outside the camp,

**23:11** and he shall not return before he washes himself with water toward evening, and after the setting of the sun he shall go back into the camp.

**23:12** You shall have a place outside the camp to which you may go out for the necessities of nature,

**23:13** carrying a stake in your belt; and when you sit down, you shall dig all around, and with the dug-up earth you shall cover

**23:14** where you uncovered yourself; for the Lord your God walks in the midst of the camp to rescue you and to hand over your enemies to you; and let your camp be holy, and let nothing foul appear in it, so that He may not abandon you.

**23:15** You shall not hand over to his master a slave who has fled to you for refuge.

**23:16** He shall dwell with you in the place that pleases him, and he shall rest in one of your cities; do not grieve him.

**23:17** There shall be no prostitute from the daughters of Israel, nor any fornicator from the sons of Israel.

**23:18** You shall not offer the wages of a prostitute nor the price of a dog in the house of the Lord your God, whatever it is that you have vowed, for both are an abomination before the Lord your God.

**23:19** You shall not lend money, nor produce, nor any other thing at interest to your brother,

**23:20** but to a foreigner. To your brother, however, you shall lend without interest what he needs, so that the Lord your God may bless you in all your work in the land that you are entering to possess.

**23:21** When you make a vow to the Lord your God, you shall not be slow to pay it, for the Lord your God will require it, and if you delay, it will be counted to you as sin.

**23:22** If you are unwilling to promise, you will be without sin.

**23:23** But what has once gone out from your lips you shall observe and do, just as you promised to the Lord your God and spoke of your own free will and with your own mouth.

**23:24** Having entered your neighbor's vineyard, eat grapes as much as pleases you, but do not carry any outside with you.

**23:25** If you enter into your friend's standing grain, you may break off the heads of grain and rub them with your hand, but you shall not reap with a sickle.

## Translator Notes

- **23:1** — The Clementine Vulgate prints intravit (literally perfect tense), an orthographic b/v variant for future intrabit ('shall enter'), and explicitly adds eunuchus ('eunuch') with attritis vel amputatis ('crushed or amputated').
- **23:2** — The Vulgate retains the Hebrew loanword mamzer and supplies the explanatory gloss hoc est, de scorto natus ('that is, one born of a prostitute'); ecclesiam ('assembly') renders the Septuagint term for the covenant congregation.
- **23:14** — The Clementine Vulgate reads quo revelatus es ('where you uncovered yourself'), a well-known variant for quo relevatus es ('that of which you were relieved,' referring to bodily waste).
- **23:25** — The Vulgate adds et manu conteres ('and rub them with your hand'), which is absent from the Hebrew text and echoes the wording of Luke 6:1.
