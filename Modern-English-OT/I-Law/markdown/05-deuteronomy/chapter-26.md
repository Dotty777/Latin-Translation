## Text

**26:1** And when you enter the land that the Lord your God is about to give you to possess, and you obtain it and dwell in it,

**26:2** you shall take the firstfruits from all your produce and place them in a basket, and go to the place that the Lord your God chooses, so that His name may be called upon there,

**26:3** and you shall approach the priest who will be in those days, and say to him, "I profess today before the Lord your God that I have entered the land concerning which He swore to our fathers that He would give it to us."

**26:4** And the priest, receiving the basket from your hand, shall place it before the altar of the Lord your God,

**26:5** and you shall speak in the sight of the Lord your God, "A Syrian was persecuting my father, who went down into Egypt and sojourned there in very small number, and grew into a great and mighty nation, and of countless multitude.

**26:6** And the Egyptians afflicted us and persecuted us, imposing very heavy burdens,

**26:7** and we cried out to the Lord, the God of our fathers, who heard us and looked upon our lowliness, toil, and distress,

**26:8** and brought us out of Egypt with a strong hand and an outstretched arm, with immense terror, with signs and wonders,

**26:9** and brought us into this place, and delivered to us a land flowing with milk and honey.

**26:10** And therefore now I offer the firstfruits of the produce of the land that the Lord has given to me." And you shall leave them in the sight of the Lord your God; and having worshiped the Lord your God,

**26:11** you shall then feast on all the good things that the Lord your God has given to you and to your house, you and the Levite and the sojourner who is with you.

**26:12** When you have completed the tithe of all your produce in the third year of tithes, you shall give it to the Levite, the sojourner, the orphan, and the widow, so that they may eat within your gates and be filled,

**26:13** and you shall speak in the sight of the Lord your God, "I have removed what has been sanctified from my house and have given it to the Levite, the sojourner, the orphan, and the widow, just as You commanded me; I have not passed over Your commandments, nor have I forgotten Your command.

**26:14** I have not eaten of them in my mourning, nor have I set them apart in any uncleanness, nor have I spent anything from them on a funeral matter. I have obeyed the voice of the Lord my God, and have done all things just as You commanded me.

**26:15** Look down from Your sanctuary and from the lofty dwelling place of the heavens, and bless Your people Israel and the land that You have given to us, just as You swore to our fathers, a land flowing with milk and honey."

**26:16** Today the Lord your God has commanded you to do these commandments and judgments, and to keep and fulfill them with all your heart and with all your soul.

**26:17** You have chosen the Lord today to be God to you, and that you may walk in His ways, and keep His ceremonies, commandments, and judgments, and obey His command.

**26:18** And the Lord has chosen you today so that you may be a special people to Him, just as He spoke to you, and that you may keep all His precepts,

**26:19** and that He may make you higher than all the nations that He has created, for His praise, name, and glory, so that you may be a holy people of the Lord your God, just as He spoke.

## Translator Notes

- **26:5** — Latin Syrus persequebatur patrem meum ("A Syrian was persecuting my father") reflects an ancient interpretive tradition referring to Laban pursuing Jacob, whereas the Hebrew is commonly rendered "A wandering (or perishing) Aramean was my father."
- **26:10** — Latin adorato Domino Deo tuo is an ablative absolute ("having worshiped the Lord your God") leading syntactically into the verb of verse 11, where the Clementine Vulgate also retains a resumptive Et ("then").
- **26:14** — Latin in re funebri ("on a funeral matter") specifies funerary expenditure where the Hebrew reads "to the dead."
- **26:17** — Both here and in verse 18, the Vulgate translates the rare Hebrew verb ("declared/avouched") as elegisti and elegit ("have chosen" / "has chosen").
- **26:19** — The Vulgate adds the possessive suam ("for His praise, name, and glory"), directing the three terms explicitly to God rather than to Israel.
