## Text

**21:1** When the corpse of a killed man is found in the land that the Lord your God is about to give you, and the one guilty of the killing is unknown,

**21:2** your elders and judges shall go out and measure from the place of the corpse the distances to each of the cities all around;

**21:3** and whichever city they determine to be nearer than the rest, the elders of that city shall take a heifer from the herd that has not pulled a yoke nor cut the earth with a plowshare,

**21:4** and they shall lead her to a rough and rocky valley that has never been plowed nor received seed, and in it they shall cut the neck of the heifer.

**21:5** And the priests, the sons of Levi, shall come forward, whom the Lord your God has chosen to minister to Him and to bless in His name, and at their word every matter and whatever is clean or unclean shall be judged.

**21:6** And the elders of that city shall come to the one who was killed, and shall wash their hands over the heifer that was struck down in the valley,

**21:7** and shall say: "Our hands have not shed this blood, nor have our eyes seen it.

**21:8** Be merciful to Your people Israel, whom You have redeemed, Lord, and do not reckon innocent blood in the midst of Your people Israel." And the guilt of blood shall be removed from them,

**21:9** and you shall be free from the blood of the innocent that was poured out, when you have done what the Lord has commanded.

**21:10** If you go out to battle against your enemies, and the Lord your God hands them over into your hand, and you lead them away as captives,

**21:11** and you see among the number of the captives a beautiful woman, and fall in love with her, and wish to have her as a wife,

**21:12** you shall bring her into your house; she shall shave her hair and trim her nails,

**21:13** and lay aside the garment in which she was captured; and sitting in your house, she shall weep for her father and her mother for one month; and afterward you shall go in to her and sleep with her, and she shall be your wife.

**21:14** But if afterward she does not please your mind, you shall send her away free; you shall not be able to sell her for money, nor oppress her by power, because you have humbled her.

**21:15** If a man has two wives, one loved and the other hated, and they have borne children by him, and the son of the hated one is the firstborn,

**21:16** and he wishes to divide his property among his sons, he shall not be able to make the son of the loved one the firstborn and prefer him to the son of the hated one;

**21:17** rather, he shall acknowledge the son of the hated one as the firstborn, and shall give him double of all the things that he possesses; for this one is the beginning of his children, and to him the rights of the firstborn are due.

**21:18** If a man fathers a stubborn and insolent son who does not listen to the command of his father or mother, and though disciplined scorns to obey,

**21:19** they shall seize him and lead him to the elders of that city and to the gate of judgment,

**21:20** and shall say to them: "This son of ours is insolent and stubborn; he scorns to listen to our warnings; he devotes himself to carousing, and to debauchery and banquets."

**21:21** The people of the city shall overwhelm him with stones, and he shall die, so that you may remove the evil from your midst, and all Israel, hearing of it, may be terrified.

**21:22** When a man commits a sin that must be punished with death, and having been sentenced to death is hung on a gibbet,

**21:23** his corpse shall not remain on the wood, but shall be buried on the same day, because the one who hangs on wood is cursed by God; and by no means shall you contaminate your land, which the Lord your God will give you as a possession.

## Translator Notes

- **21:4** — Vulgate renders Hebrew nachal eitan ("perennial wadi/stream") as vallem asperam atque saxosam ("a rough and rocky valley") and translates the ritual killing of the heifer as cædent ... cervices ("cut the neck"), rather than breaking its neck.
- **21:5** — Vulgate reads omne negotium, et quidquid mundum, vel immundum est ("every matter and whatever is clean or unclean"), interpreting Hebrew nega ("assault/plague") in its Levitical purity sense rather than as physical assault.
- **21:6** — Vulgate construes the elders as coming ad interfectum ("to the one who was killed"), whereas the Hebrew describes them as the elders of the city nearest to the dead man.
- **21:20** — Vulgate renders the two Hebrew epithets ("a glutton and a drunkard") with three classical Latin terms for dissipation: comessationibus ("carousing"), luxuriæ ("debauchery"), and conviviis ("banquets").
- **21:22** — Vulgate uses the Roman execution term in patibulo ("on a gibbet") in v. 22 and in ligno ("on the wood," corresponding to Hebrew ets / Greek xylon, "tree/wood") in v. 23, alongside the passive sepelietur ("shall be buried") and explicit maledictus a Deo ("cursed by God").
