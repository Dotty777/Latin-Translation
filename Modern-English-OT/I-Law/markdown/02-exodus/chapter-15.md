## Text

**15:1** Then Moses and the sons of Israel sang this song to the Lord, and they said, "Let us sing to the Lord, for He has been magnified gloriously; He has hurled down the horse and the rider into the sea.

**15:2** My strength and my praise is the Lord, and He has become salvation for me; this is my God, and I will glorify Him; the God of my father, and I will exalt Him.

**15:3** The Lord is like a fighting man; Almighty is His name.

**15:4** Pharaoh's chariots and his army He flung into the sea; his chosen leaders were submerged in the Red Sea.

**15:5** The depths covered them; they went down into the deep like a stone.

**15:6** Your right hand, Lord, has been magnified in strength; Your right hand, Lord, struck the enemy.

**15:7** And in the abundance of Your glory You brought down Your adversaries; You sent Your wrath, which devoured them like stubble.

**15:8** And by the breath of Your fury the waters were gathered together; the flowing wave stood still; the depths were gathered together in the middle of the sea.

**15:9** The enemy said, 'I will pursue and overtake, I will divide the spoils, my soul will be filled; I will unsheathe my sword, my hand will kill them.'

**15:10** Your breath blew, and the sea covered them; they were submerged like lead in the violent waters.

**15:11** Who is like You among the mighty, Lord? Who is like You, magnificent in holiness, fearsome and praiseworthy, doing wonders?

**15:12** You stretched out Your hand, and the earth devoured them.

**15:13** You have been a guide in Your mercy to the people whom You redeemed, and You carried them in Your strength to Your holy dwelling.

**15:14** The peoples went up and became angry; pangs took hold of the inhabitants of Philistia.

**15:15** Then the leaders of Edom were thrown into turmoil; trembling took hold of the strong men of Moab; all the inhabitants of Canaan grew rigid.

**15:16** May dread and terror rush upon them in the greatness of Your arm; may they become motionless like a stone, until Your people pass through, Lord, until this people of Yours, whom You have possessed, pass through.

**15:17** You will bring them in and plant them on the mountain of Your inheritance, Your most firm dwelling that You have made, Lord; Your sanctuary, Lord, which Your hands have made firm.

**15:18** The Lord will reign forever and beyond."

**15:19** For Pharaoh entered the sea on horseback with his chariots and his horsemen, and the Lord brought the waters of the sea back over them; the sons of Israel, however, walked across dry ground in the middle of it.

**15:20** So Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a tambourine in her hand, and all the women went out after her with tambourines and dances,

**15:21** and before them she led the song, saying, "Let us sing to the Lord, for He has been magnified gloriously; He has hurled down the horse and its rider into the sea."

**15:22** Now Moses led Israel away from the Red Sea, and they went out into the desert of Shur; and they walked for three days through the wilderness, and they did not find water.

**15:23** And they came to Marah, and they could not drink the waters from Marah because they were bitter; therefore he also assigned a fitting name to the place, calling it Marah, that is, bitterness.

**15:24** And the people grumbled against Moses, saying, "What will we drink?"

**15:25** But he cried out to the Lord, who showed him a piece of wood; and when he had cast it into the waters, they were turned into sweetness. There He established precepts and judgments for him, and there He tested him,

**15:26** saying, "If you listen to the voice of the Lord your God, and do what is right before Him, and obey His commandments, and keep all His precepts, none of the sickness that I put in Egypt will I bring upon you; for I am the Lord, your healer."

**15:27** Then the sons of Israel came to Elim, where there were twelve springs of water and seventy palm trees; and they camped beside the waters.

## Translator Notes

- **15:3** — The Vulgate reads 'quasi vir pugnator, Omnipotens nomen ejus' ("like a fighting man; Almighty is His name"), adding the comparative "like" and rendering the second occurrence of the divine name as "Almighty" rather than repeating "the Lord."
- **15:11** — Latin 'in fortibus' ("among the mighty") is a distinctive Vulgate rendering where the Hebrew reads "among the gods."
- **15:14** — The Vulgate reads 'Ascenderunt populi, et irati sunt' ("The peoples went up and became angry"), differing markedly from the Hebrew ("The peoples heard; they tremble").
- **15:19** — Latin 'eques Pharao' depicts Pharaoh entering the sea "on horseback" as a rider, whereas the Hebrew refers to "the horses of Pharaoh."
- **15:23** — The Vulgate explicitly explains the etymology of Marah with 'congruum loco nomen imposuit ... id est, amaritudinem' ("assigned a fitting name to the place ... that is, bitterness").
