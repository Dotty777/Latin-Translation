## Text

**32:1** Now the people, seeing that Moses delayed coming down from the mountain, gathered against Aaron and said, "Get up, make gods for us who will go ahead of us; for as for this man Moses, who led us out of the land of Egypt, we do not know what has happened to him."

**32:2** And Aaron said to them, "Take the gold earrings from the ears of your wives, and of your sons and daughters, and bring them to me."

**32:3** And the people did what he had ordered, bringing the earrings to Aaron.

**32:4** When he had received them, he shaped them by the work of casting and made a cast calf out of them; and they said, "These are your gods, Israel, who led you out of the land of Egypt."

**32:5** When Aaron saw this, he built an altar before it, and called out with the voice of a herald, saying, "Tomorrow is a solemn festival of the Lord."

**32:6** And rising in the morning, they offered burnt offerings and peace sacrifices; and the people sat down to eat and drink, and got up to play.

**32:7** Now the Lord spoke to Moses, saying, "Go, go down; your people, whom you led out of the land of Egypt, have sinned.

**32:8** They have quickly departed from the way that you showed them; and they have made for themselves a cast calf, and worshiped it, and sacrificing offerings to it, they said, 'These are your gods, Israel, who led you out of the land of Egypt.'

**32:10** Leave Me alone, so that My fury may rage against them and I may blot them out, and I will make you into a great nation."

**32:11** But Moses prayed to the Lord his God, saying, "Why, Lord, does Your fury rage against Your people, whom You led out of the land of Egypt with great strength and with a mighty hand?

**32:12** Please do not let the Egyptians say, 'Craftily He led them out to kill them in the mountains and blot them out from the earth.' Let Your anger subside, and be appeased over the wickedness of Your people.

**32:13** Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, Your servants, to whom You swore by Yourself, saying, 'I will multiply your offspring like the stars of heaven; and all this land about which I have spoken I will give to your offspring, and you will possess it forever.'"

**32:14** And the Lord was appeased, so that He did not do the harm that He had spoken against His people.

**32:15** And Moses returned from the mountain, carrying two tablets of the testimony in his hand, written on both sides,

**32:16** and made by the work of God; the writing of God also was engraved on the tablets.

**32:17** Now Joshua, hearing the uproar of the people shouting, said to Moses, "A howling of battle is heard in the camp."

**32:18** He answered, "It is not the clamor of those urging on to battle, nor the shouting of those driving to flight, but I hear the voice of those singing."

**32:19** And when he had approached the camp, he saw the calf and the dances; and greatly angered, he threw the tablets from his hand and shattered them at the base of the mountain.

**32:20** And seizing the calf that they had made, he burned it and ground it down to powder, which he scattered into the water, and gave a drink of it to the sons of Israel.

**32:21** And he said to Aaron, "What did this people do to you, that you should bring a very great sin upon them?"

**32:22** He answered him, "Do not let my lord be indignant; for you know this people, that they are prone to evil.

**32:23** They said to me, 'Make gods for us who will go ahead of us; for as for this Moses, who led us out of the land of Egypt, we do not know what has happened to him.'

**32:24** To them I said, 'Who among you has gold?' They took it and gave it to me, and I threw it into the fire, and this calf came out."

**32:25** Moses therefore, seeing the people, that they were stripped naked (for Aaron had despoiled them on account of the disgrace of filth, and had set them naked among their enemies),

**32:26** and standing at the gate of the camp, said, "If anyone is the Lord's, let him join me." And all the sons of Levi gathered to him.

**32:27** To them he said, "This is what the Lord God of Israel says: 'Let each man place his sword upon his thigh; go and return from gate to gate through the middle of the camp, and let each one kill his brother, and his friend, and his neighbor.'"

**32:28** And the sons of Levi did according to the word of Moses, and about twenty-three thousand men fell on that day.

**32:29** And Moses said, "You have consecrated your hands today to the Lord, each one against his son and against his brother, so that a blessing may be given to you."

**32:30** Now when the next day had come, Moses spoke to the people, "You have sinned a very great sin; I will go up to the Lord, if in any way I can plead with Him for your crime."

**32:31** And returning to the Lord, he said, "I implore You, this people has sinned a very great sin, and they have made gods of gold for themselves; either forgive them this offense,

**32:32** or if You do not do so, blot me out of Your book that You have written."

**32:33** The Lord answered him, "The one who has sinned against Me, I will blot him out of My book;

**32:34** but as for you, go and lead this people where I have spoken to you; My angel will go ahead of you. Yet on the day of vengeance I Myself will also punish this sin of theirs."

**32:35** Therefore the Lord struck the people for the guilt of the calf that Aaron had made.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**32:9** *(after `32:8`)* And once again the Lord said to Moses, "I perceive that this people is stiff-necked."

## Translator Notes

- **32:4** — The Vulgate reads "by the work of casting" (opere fusorio), referring to metal casting, whereas the Hebrew reads "with an engraving tool."
- **32:8** — The Vulgate reads "the way that you showed them" (quam ostendisti eis), referring to Moses, whereas the Hebrew reads "the way that I commanded them."
- **32:10** — Verse 32:9 ("Again the Lord said to Moses: I see that this people is stiff-necked") is absent from the Clementine Vulgate, so 32:10 follows directly upon 32:8.
- **32:25** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew "let loose" or "out of control" as "stripped naked" (nudatus) and adds the parenthetical explanation that Aaron had despoiled them "on account of the disgrace of filth" (propter ignominiam sordis) and set them naked among their enemies.
- **32:28** — The Vulgate reads "about twenty-three thousand men" (viginti tria millia), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text and Septuagint read "about three thousand men."
