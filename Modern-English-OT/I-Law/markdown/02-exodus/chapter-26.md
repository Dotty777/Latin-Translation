## Text

**26:1** Now you shall make the tabernacle in this way: you shall make ten curtains of twisted fine linen, and violet-blue, and purple, and twice-dyed scarlet, variegated with embroidered work.

**26:2** The length of one curtain shall measure twenty-eight cubits, and the width shall be four cubits. All the hangings shall be made of one measurement.

**26:3** Five curtains shall be joined to one another, and the other five shall be linked together by a similar fastening.

**26:4** You shall make violet-blue loops on the sides and edges of the curtains, so that they may be coupled to one another.

**26:5** The curtain shall have fifty loops on each side, inserted in such a way that loop comes opposite loop, and the one may be fitted to the other.

**26:6** You shall also make fifty golden rings by which the drapes of the curtains are to be joined, so that one tabernacle may be made.

**26:7** You shall also make eleven cloths of goats' hair to cover the roof of the tabernacle.

**26:8** The length of one cloth shall measure thirty cubits, and the width four; the measurement of all the cloths shall be equal.

**26:9** Of these, you shall join five separately, and six you shall couple to one another, so that you double the sixth cloth at the front of the roof.

**26:10** You shall also make fifty loops on the border of one cloth, so that it can be joined with the other, and fifty loops on the border of the other cloth, so that it may be coupled with the other.

**26:11** You shall also make fifty bronze clasps by which the loops may be joined, so that one covering may be made out of all of them.

**26:12** Now as for what remains over from the cloths that are prepared for the roof, that is, the one cloth that is extra, with half of it you shall cover the back of the tabernacle.

**26:13** And a cubit shall hang down on one side, and another on the other side, which is extra in the length of the cloths, protecting each side of the tabernacle.

**26:14** You shall also make another covering for the roof out of rams' skins dyed red, and over this again another covering of violet-colored skins.

**26:15** You shall also make upright boards of the tabernacle from acacia wood,

**26:16** which shall each have ten cubits in length, and one and a half cubits in width.

**26:17** In the sides of a board, two mortise joints shall be made, by which one board may be connected to another board; and in this manner all the boards shall be prepared.

**26:18** Of these, twenty shall be on the southern side that faces toward the south.

**26:19** For these you shall cast forty silver bases, so that two bases may be placed underneath each board at the two corners.

**26:20** Also on the second side of the tabernacle, which faces toward the north, there shall be twenty boards,

**26:21** having forty silver bases; two bases shall be set beneath each board.

**26:22** Now toward the western quarter of the tabernacle you shall make six boards,

**26:23** and again two others that shall be erected in the corners behind the back of the tabernacle.

**26:24** And they shall be joined together from the bottom to the top, and one fastening shall hold them all together. For the two boards also that are to be placed in the corners, a similar joint shall be maintained.

**26:25** And altogether there shall be eight boards, and their silver bases sixteen, with two bases counted for each board.

**26:26** You shall also make five bars of acacia wood to hold the boards together on one side of the tabernacle,

**26:27** and five others on the other side, and of the same number toward the western quarter,

**26:28** which shall be passed through the middle of the boards from one end to the other.

**26:29** You shall also gild the boards themselves, and you shall cast golden rings on them through which the bars may hold the boarding together; and you shall cover the bars with golden plates.

**26:30** And you shall erect the tabernacle according to the pattern that was shown to you on the mountain.

**26:31** You shall also make a veil of violet-blue, and purple, and twice-dyed scarlet, and twisted fine linen, woven together with embroidered work and beautiful variety,

**26:32** which you shall hang before four pillars of acacia wood, which themselves indeed shall be gilded, and shall have golden capitals, but silver bases.

**26:33** Now the veil shall be inserted through the rings, within which you shall place the ark of the testimony, by which both the sanctuary and the sanctuaries of the sanctuary shall be divided.

**26:34** You shall also place the propitiatory upon the ark of the testimony in the Holy of Holies,

**26:35** and the table outside the veil, and opposite the table the lampstand on the southern side of the tabernacle; for the table shall stand on the northern side.

**26:36** You shall also make a hanging at the entrance of the tabernacle of violet-blue, and purple, and twice-dyed scarlet, and twisted fine linen, with the work of an embroiderer.

**26:37** And you shall gild five pillars of acacia wood, before which the hanging shall be drawn, whose capitals shall be golden, and whose bases shall be bronze.

## Translator Notes

- **26:1** — The Vulgate renders the scarlet dye literally as 'twice-dyed scarlet' (cocco bis tincto) and describes the curtain design as 'variegated with embroidered work' (variatas opere plumario), without mentioning cherubim here or in 26:31 as the Hebrew and Septuagint do.
- **26:14** — Latin de janthinis pellibus ('of violet-colored skins') follows the Septuagint rendering of the obscure Hebrew term tahash, whose meaning is uncertain.
- **26:15** — Latin de lignis setim retains the transliterated Hebrew name shittim for the desert acacia tree, translated here and throughout as 'acacia wood'.
- **26:28** — The Vulgate uses the plural relative pronoun qui ('which'), referring to the bars generally passing through the middle of the boards, whereas the Hebrew specifies the single middle bar.
- **26:32** — Latin capita ('capitals' or 'heads') describes the tops of the pillars (as also in 26:37 and 27:10, 11, 17), where the Hebrew refers to 'hooks'; similarly, 'propitiatory' in 26:34 directly translates Latin propitiatorium, the golden atonement cover traditionally called the mercy seat.
