## Text

**21:1** "These are the judgments that you will set before them.

**21:2** If you buy a Hebrew slave, he will serve you for six years; in the seventh he will go out free, without charge.

**21:3** With whatever clothing he enters, with the same let him go out; if he has a wife, the wife will also go out at the same time.

**21:4** But if the master gives him a wife, and she bears sons and daughters, the woman and her children will belong to his master, while he himself will go out with his clothing.

**21:5** And if the slave says, 'I love my master and my wife and children; I will not go out free,'

**21:6** the master will present him to the gods, and he will be brought up against the door and the doorposts, and the master will pierce his ear with an awl, and he will be a slave to him forever.

**21:7** If anyone sells his daughter as a female servant, she will not go out as female slaves are accustomed to go out.

**21:8** If she displeases the eyes of her master to whom she had been delivered, he will let her go; however, he will not have authority to sell her to a foreign people, if he has spurned her.

**21:9** But if he betroths her to his son, he will deal with her according to the custom of daughters.

**21:10** And if he takes another woman for him, he will provide marriage for the girl, and clothing, and he will not deny the price of chastity.

**21:11** If he does not do these three things, she will go out without charge, without money.

**21:12** Whoever strikes a person, intending to kill, must surely die.

**21:13** But the one who did not lie in wait—rather, God delivered that one into his hands—I will appoint a place for you to which he should flee.

**21:14** If anyone deliberately kills his neighbor, and by ambush, you will tear him away from My altar, so that he may die.

**21:15** Whoever strikes his father or mother must surely die.

**21:16** Whoever steals a person and sells him, having been convicted of the offense, must surely die.

**21:17** Whoever curses his father or mother must surely die.

**21:18** If men quarrel, and one strikes his neighbor with a stone or with a fist, and that one does not die but lies in bed,

**21:19** if he gets up and walks outside on his staff, the one who struck him will be innocent, provided, however, that he makes restitution for his labor and the expenses for physicians.

**21:20** Whoever strikes his male slave or female slave with a rod, and they die in his hands, will be guilty of a crime.

**21:21** But if the slave survives one day or two, the master will not be subject to punishment, because the slave is his money.

**21:22** If men quarrel, and someone strikes a pregnant woman, and she indeed suffers a miscarriage, yet she herself lives, he will be subject to damages, as much as the woman's husband demands and the arbitrators judge.

**21:23** But if her death follows, he will pay life for life,

**21:24** eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot,

**21:25** burn for burn, wound for wound, bruise for bruise.

**21:26** If anyone strikes the eye of his male slave or female slave and makes them one-eyed, he will let them go free for the eye that he put out.

**21:27** Also, if he knocks out a tooth of his male slave or female slave, he will likewise let them go free.

**21:28** If an ox strikes a man or a woman with its horn and they die, it will be stoned to death, and its flesh will not be eaten; the owner of the ox will also be innocent.

**21:29** But if the ox has been prone to goring from yesterday and the day before, and they have warned its owner, and he has not confined it, and it kills a man or a woman, both the ox will be stoned to death and they will kill its owner.

**21:30** But if a price is imposed on him, he will give for his life whatever is demanded of him.

**21:31** Also, if it strikes a son or a daughter with its horn, he will be subject to a similar sentence.

**21:32** If it attacks a male slave or a female slave, he will give thirty shekels of silver to the master, while the ox will be crushed with stones.

**21:33** If anyone opens a cistern, and digs it, and does not cover it, and an ox or a donkey falls into it,

**21:34** the owner of the cistern will pay the price of the livestock; but what is dead will belong to him.

**21:35** If one person's ox wounds another's ox, and it dies, they will sell the live ox and divide the price, while they will apportion the carcass of the dead one between themselves.

**21:36** But if its owner knew that the ox was prone to goring from yesterday and the day before, and he did not guard it, he will pay ox for ox, and he will receive the whole carcass."

## Translator Notes

- **21:3** — The Vulgate reads "with whatever clothing" (cum quali veste, and "with his clothing" in v. 4), whereas the Hebrew text reads "by himself" (literally "with his body/back").
- **21:6** — Latin diis literally means "to the gods," rendering plural Hebrew ha'elohim, which ancient interpreters often understood as "God" or as "the judges" acting in God's name.
- **21:7** — The Vulgate specifies "female slaves" (ancillae), whereas the Hebrew text reads "male slaves." In v. 10, the Vulgate likewise reads "marriage" (nuptias) and "price of chastity" (pretium pudicitiae) where the Hebrew lists sustenance and conjugal rights.
- **21:16** — Native Vulgate Exodus 21:16-17 place kidnapping in 21:16 and cursing parents in 21:17 (reverse of Saints.Bible Septuagint-aligned order 21:17 and 21:16). The Vulgate also reads "convicted of the offense" (convictus noxae) where the Hebrew reads "found in his possession."
- **21:17** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate places cursing parents in v. 16 and kidnapping in v. 17, reversing the Hebrew Masoretic Text order. In v. 17, the Vulgate also reads "convicted of the offense" (convictus noxae) where the Hebrew reads "found in his possession."
- **21:22** — The Vulgate explicitly reads "suffers a miscarriage, yet she herself lives" (and "if her death follows" in v. 23), whereas the Hebrew reads "her children come out, yet there is no harm."
