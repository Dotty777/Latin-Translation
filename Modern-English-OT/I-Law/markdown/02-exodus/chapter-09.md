## Text

**9:1** Then the Lord said to Moses, "Go in to Pharaoh and speak to him, 'This is what the Lord God of the Hebrews says: Let My people go so that they may sacrifice to Me.

**9:2** But if you still refuse and hold them back,

**9:3** look, My hand will be upon your fields, and upon the horses, and donkeys, and camels, and oxen, and sheep, a very severe pestilence.

**9:4** And the Lord will do a marvelous thing between the possessions of Israel and the possessions of the Egyptians, so that nothing at all will perish of those things that belong to the sons of Israel.'"

**9:5** And the Lord appointed a time, saying, "Tomorrow the Lord will do this thing in the land."

**9:6** Therefore the Lord did this thing on the next day, and all the living creatures of the Egyptians died; but of the animals of the sons of Israel, nothing at all perished.

**9:7** And Pharaoh sent to see, and nothing was dead of what Israel possessed. And Pharaoh's heart was made heavy, and he did not let the people go.

**9:8** And the Lord said to Moses and Aaron, "Take handfuls of ash from the furnace, and let Moses scatter it toward heaven before Pharaoh.

**9:9** And let it be dust over all the land of Egypt; for there will be boils and swelling blisters on people and livestock in the entire land of Egypt."

**9:10** And they took ash from the furnace and stood before Pharaoh, and Moses scattered it toward heaven; and boils of swelling blisters formed on people and livestock.

**9:11** And the sorcerers could not stand before Moses because of the boils that were on them, and in all the land of Egypt.

**9:12** And the Lord hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he did not listen to them, just as the Lord spoke to Moses.

**9:13** And the Lord said to Moses, "Rise in the morning and stand before Pharaoh, and you will say to him, 'This is what the Lord God of the Hebrews says: Let My people go so that they may sacrifice to Me.

**9:14** For this time I will send all My plagues upon your heart, and upon your servants, and upon your people, so that you may know that there is no one like Me in all the earth.

**9:15** For now, stretching out My hand, I will strike you and your people with pestilence, and you will perish from the earth.

**9:16** But for this reason I have appointed you, so that I may show My strength in you, and that My name may be declared in all the earth.

**9:17** Do you still hold back My people, and are you unwilling to let them go?

**9:18** Look, tomorrow at this very hour I will rain down exceedingly heavy hail, such as has not been in Egypt from the day on which it was founded until the present time.

**9:19** Send therefore right now, and gather your livestock and all that you have in the field; for the people, and the livestock, and everything that is found outside and not gathered from the fields, and upon which the hail falls, will die.'"

**9:20** Whoever among the servants of Pharaoh feared the word of the Lord made his servants and livestock flee into the houses.

**9:21** But whoever neglected the word of the Lord left his servants and livestock in the fields.

**9:22** And the Lord said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand toward heaven, so that there may be hail in the entire land of Egypt upon people, and upon livestock, and upon every plant of the field in the land of Egypt."

**9:23** And Moses stretched out his staff toward heaven, and the Lord sent thunder and hail, and lightning running across the earth; and the Lord rained hail upon the land of Egypt.

**9:24** And hail and fire, mingled together, were swept along; and it was of such magnitude as had never before appeared in the entire land of Egypt since that nation was established.

**9:25** And the hail struck in all the land of Egypt everything that was in the fields, from human being to livestock; and the hail struck every plant of the field, and shattered every tree of the region.

**9:26** Only in the land of Goshen, where the sons of Israel were, did the hail not fall.

**9:27** And Pharaoh sent and called Moses and Aaron, saying to them, "I have sinned even now; the Lord is righteous; I and my people are wicked.

**9:28** Pray to the Lord that the thunder of God and the hail may cease, so that I may let you go, and you may no longer stay here at all."

**9:29** Moses said, "When I have gone out of the city, I will stretch out my palms to the Lord, and the thunder will cease, and there will be no hail, so that you may know that the earth is the Lord's.

**9:30** However, I know that both you and your servants do not yet fear the Lord God."

**9:31** Therefore the flax and the barley were damaged, because the barley was green, and the flax was already putting forth pods.

**9:32** But the wheat and the spelt were not damaged, because they were late-ripening.

**9:33** And Moses went out from Pharaoh, out of the city, and stretched out his hands to the Lord; and the thunder and the hail ceased, and rain no longer dripped upon the earth.

**9:34** But Pharaoh, seeing that the rain, and the hail, and the thunder had ceased, increased his sin.

**9:35** And his heart, and that of his servants, was made heavy and exceedingly hardened; and he did not let the sons of Israel go, just as the Lord had commanded through the hand of Moses.

## Translator Notes

- **9:3** — The Vulgate reads super agros tuos ("upon your fields"), whereas the Hebrew text specifies "upon your livestock that are in the field."
- **9:4** — As in 8:18 (Faciamque mirabilem ... terram Gessen, "I will make marvelous the land of Goshen"), the Vulgate reads faciet Dominus mirabile ("the Lord will do a marvelous thing") and possessiones ("possessions"), where the Hebrew emphasizes "setting apart" Israel's "livestock."
- **9:15** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate uses direct future indicatives (percutiam ... peribisque, "I will strike ... and you will perish") rather than the counterfactual past sense common in translations from the Hebrew ("I could have struck ... and you would have been cut off").
- **9:31** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew crop terms ("in the ear" and "in bud") as virens ("green") and folliculos germinaret ("putting forth pods").
- **9:35** — The Vulgate combines both hardening verbs in the passive voice in a single clause (ingravatum est ... et induratum nimis, "was made heavy and exceedingly hardened") and places the mention of Pharaoh's servants here rather than in 9:34.
