## Text

**2:1** After these things a man went out from the house of Levi and took a wife of his own lineage.

**2:2** She conceived and bore a son; and seeing him to be handsome, she hid him for three months.

**2:3** And when she could no longer conceal him, she took a basket of rushes and coated it with bitumen and pitch; and she placed the little infant inside and set him out in the sedge of the bank of the river,

**2:4** while his sister stood at a distance and observed the outcome of the matter.

**2:5** Now the daughter of Pharaoh was going down to bathe in the river, and her girls were walking along the edge of the channel. When she saw the basket in the papyrus bed, she sent one of her maidservants; and when it was brought,

**2:6** opening it and perceiving in it a little child wailing, she took pity on him and said, "This is one of the infants of the Hebrews."

**2:7** The boy's sister said to her, "Do you want me to go and call a Hebrew woman for you who can nurse the little infant?"

**2:8** She answered, "Go." The girl went and called her mother.

**2:9** Speaking to her, the daughter of Pharaoh said, "Take this boy and nurse him for me; I will give you your wages." The woman received and nursed the boy, and when he was grown, she handed him over to the daughter of Pharaoh.

**2:10** She adopted him in the place of a son and called his name Moses, saying, "Because I took him from the water."

**2:11** In those days, after Moses had grown up, he went out to his brothers and saw their affliction, and an Egyptian man striking one of his Hebrew brothers.

**2:12** And when he had looked around this way and that, and saw that no one was present, he struck the Egyptian and hid him in the sand.

**2:13** And going out on the next day, he caught sight of two Hebrews fighting; and he said to the one who was doing the wrong, "Why are you striking your neighbor?"

**2:14** He answered, "Who appointed you prince and judge over us? Do you want to kill me, as you killed the Egyptian yesterday?" Moses was afraid and said, "How has this matter become publicly known?"

**2:15** And Pharaoh heard this report and sought to kill Moses; but fleeing from his presence, he stayed in the land of Midian and sat beside a well.

**2:16** Now the priest of Midian had seven daughters, who came to draw water; and having filled the troughs, they wanted to water their father's flocks.

**2:17** Shepherds came upon them and drove them away; and Moses stood up and, having defended the girls, watered their sheep.

**2:18** When they returned to Reuel their father, he said to them, "Why have you come more quickly than usual?"

**2:19** They answered, "An Egyptian man delivered us from the hand of the shepherds; moreover, he also drew water with us and gave drink to the sheep."

**2:20** But he said, "Where is he? Why did you let the man go? Call him so that he may eat bread."

**2:21** Therefore Moses swore that he would live with him. And he took Zipporah his daughter as a wife,

**2:22** who bore him a son, whom he called Gershom, saying, "I have been a stranger in a foreign land." And she bore another, whom he called Eliezer, saying, "For the God of my father, my helper, has rescued me from the hand of Pharaoh."

**2:23** Now after much time, the king of Egypt died; and groaning, the sons of Israel cried out because of the labors, and their cry went up to God from the labors.

**2:24** And He heard their groaning, and remembered the covenant that He made with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

**2:25** And the Lord looked upon the sons of Israel and knew them.

## Translator Notes

- **2:14** — Latin "Quomodo palam factum est verbum istud?" presents Moses's reaction as a question ("How has this matter become publicly known?"), aligning with the Septuagint rather than the Hebrew Masoretic statement ("Surely the matter is known").
- **2:19** — Latin "nobiscum" ("with us") differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "for us."
- **2:21** — Latin "Juravit" ("swore") is a distinctly Vulgate reading where the Hebrew Masoretic Text means "agreed" or "was content," and "Accepitque" ("and he took") makes Moses the subject rather than Reuel giving Zipporah to him.
- **2:22** — The Clementine Vulgate (with some Septuagint and Old Latin witnesses) includes the birth and naming of Eliezer here, which is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text in this verse and appears later in Exodus 18:4.
