## Text

**37:2** of which one had twenty-eight cubits in length and four in width; there was one measurement for all the curtains.

**37:3** He also made the veil of blue and purple, crimson and twisted fine linen, with patterned weaving, variegated and adorned:

**37:5** He also made a hanging at the entrance of the tabernacle of blue, purple, crimson, and twisted fine linen, with the work of an embroiderer,

**37:6** and five pillars with their capitals, which he overlaid with gold, and he cast their bases of bronze.

**37:7** He also made the courtyard, on whose southern side there were hangings of twisted fine linen, of one hundred cubits,

**37:8** twenty bronze pillars with their bases; the capitals of the pillars and all the engraved work were of silver.

**37:9** Likewise on the northern side the hangings, pillars, bases, and capitals of the pillars were of the same measurement, workmanship, and metal.

**37:10** Now on the side that faces the west, there were hangings of fifty cubits, ten bronze pillars with their bases, and the capitals of the pillars and all the engraved work were of silver.

**37:11** Furthermore, toward the east he prepared hangings of fifty cubits:

**37:12** of which one side held fifteen cubits belonging to three pillars, with their bases;

**37:13** and on the other side (because between the two he made the entrance of the tabernacle) there were likewise hangings of fifteen cubits, and three pillars, and the same number of bases.

**37:14** All the hangings of the courtyard had been woven of twisted fine linen.

**37:15** The bases of the pillars were of bronze, but their capitals, with all their engravings, were of silver; and he even covered the pillars of the courtyard themselves with silver.

**37:16** And at its entrance he made a hanging with embroidered work of blue, purple, crimson, and twisted fine linen, which had twenty cubits in length, while the height was five cubits, according to the measurement that all the hangings of the courtyard had.

**37:17** Now the pillars at the entryway were four, with bronze bases, and their capitals and engravings were of silver.

**37:18** He also made the pegs of the tabernacle and of the courtyard all around of bronze.

**37:19** These are the furnishings of the tabernacle of the testimony, which were counted according to the command of Moses in the ceremonies of the Levites by the hand of Ithamar, the son of Aaron the priest,

**37:20** which Bezalel, the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah, had completed as the Lord commanded through Moses,

**37:21** with Oholiab, the son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan, joined to him as a partner, who was himself also an outstanding craftsman in wood, and a weaver of patterned work and an embroiderer in blue, purple, crimson, and fine linen.

## Translator Notes

- **37:3** — Latin 'varium atque distinctum' (literally "variegated and distinct/adorned") describes the patterned weave of the veil without the reference to cherubim found in the Hebrew (Exod 36:35).
- **37:8** — Both here and in v. 10, the Vulgate specifies "bronze pillars" ('columnae aeneae', where the Hebrew attributes bronze only to the bases), and renders the Hebrew hooks and bands as 'caelatura' ("engraved work" or "engravings," cf. vv. 10, 15, 17).
- **37:15** — The Vulgate states that the courtyard pillars themselves were covered with silver ('ipsas columnas atrii vestivit argento'), whereas the Hebrew refers to overlaying the tops of the pillars and making their bands of silver; similarly, v. 13 calls the courtyard entryway 'introitum tabernaculi' ("the entrance of the tabernacle").
- **37:19** — Latin 'instrumenta' ("furnishings") and 'in caeremoniis Levitarum' ("in the ceremonies of the Levites") represent what the Hebrew calls the records or inventory of the tabernacle for the service of the Levites (Exod 38:21).
