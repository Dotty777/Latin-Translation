## Text

**37:1** Now Bezalel also made the ark of acacia wood, having two and a half cubits in length and a cubit and a half in width; the height also was one cubit and a half, and he overlaid it with the purest gold inside and out.

**37:2** And he made for it a golden crown all around,

**37:3** casting four golden rings at its four corners: two rings on one side, and two on the other.

**37:4** He also made the poles of acacia wood, which he overlaid with gold,

**37:5** and which he put into the rings that were on the sides of the ark to carry it.

**37:6** He also made the mercy seat, that is, the oracle, of the purest gold, two and a half cubits in length and a cubit and a half in width.

**37:7** also two cherubim of hammered gold, which he placed on each side of the mercy seat:

**37:8** one cherub at the end of one side, and the other cherub at the end of the other side—two cherubim on the respective ends of the mercy seat,

**37:9** stretching out their wings and covering the mercy seat, looking toward one another and toward it.

**37:10** He also made the table of acacia wood, two cubits in length and one cubit in width, which had a cubit and a half in height.

**37:11** And he overlaid it with the purest gold, and made for it a golden rim all around,

**37:12** and for the rim itself a carved golden crown of four fingers, and upon the same, another golden crown.

**37:13** He also cast four golden rings, which he placed at the four corners by each of the feet of the table

**37:14** opposite the crown; and he put poles into them so that the table could be carried.

**37:15** The poles themselves he also made of acacia wood, and overlaid them with gold.

**37:16** and the vessels for the various uses of the table: dishes, bowls, cups, and censers, of pure gold, in which the drink offerings are to be offered.

**37:17** He also made the hammered lampstand of the purest gold, from whose shaft branches, cups, small spheres, and lilies extended:

**37:18** six on both sides, three branches on one side, and three on the other:

**37:19** Three cups in the shape of a nut were on each branch, and small spheres together with lilies; and three cups like a nut on the other branch, and small spheres together with lilies. The workmanship of the six branches that extended from the stem of the lampstand was uniform.

**37:20** Now on the shaft itself there were four cups in the shape of a nut, and small spheres on each one together with lilies,

**37:21** and small spheres under two branches in three places, which together make six branches proceeding from one shaft.

**37:22** Therefore both the small spheres and the branches were from it, all of them hammered work of the purest gold.

**37:23** He also made seven lamps with their snuffers, and the vessels where what has been snuffed may be extinguished, of the purest gold.

**37:24** The lampstand, along with all its vessels, weighed a talent of gold.

**37:25** He also made the altar of incense of acacia wood, measuring one cubit square and two in height, from whose corners horns extended.

**37:26** And he covered it with the purest gold, along with the grate, the walls, and the horns.

**37:27** And he made for it a small gold crown all around, and two gold rings beneath the crown on each side, so that poles could be inserted into them and the altar could be carried.

**37:28** The poles themselves, however, he made of acacia wood and overlaid with gold plates.

**37:29** He also compounded the oil for the ointment of sanctification, and the incense of the purest spices by the work of a perfumer.

## Translator Notes

- **37:1** — Latin reads 'de lignis setim' (transliterating Hebrew 'shittim', acacia wood). In this chapter arrangement, the construction of the ark, table, and lampstand (placed in Exodus 37 in the Hebrew and standard Clementine Vulgate) appears in chapter 38.
- **37:6** — The Vulgate adds the explanatory gloss 'id est, oraculum' ('that is, the oracle'—the place of divine utterance) to 'propitiatorium' ('mercy seat'); this gloss has no counterpart in the Hebrew.
- **37:19** — The Vulgate describes the lampstand cups simply as shaped 'like a nut' ('in nucis modum' / 'instar nucis') and its ornaments as 'small spheres' ('sphaerulae') and 'lilies' ('lilia'), where the Hebrew specifies almond-shaped cups, calyxes/knobs, and blossoms.
- **37:26** — The Clementine Vulgate specifically reads "grate" (craticula) for the top surface of the incense altar.
- **37:27** — Latin coronam aureolam ("small gold crown") denotes the decorative gold molding around the upper edge of the altar.
