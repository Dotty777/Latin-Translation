## Text

**11:1** And the Lord said to Moses, "With one more plague I will strike Pharaoh and Egypt, and after this he will let you go and compel you to go out.

**11:2** Therefore tell all the people that each man is to ask his friend, and each woman her neighbor, for silver and gold vessels.

**11:3** And the Lord will give favor to His people in the sight of the Egyptians." And Moses was a very great man in the land of Egypt, in the sight of Pharaoh's servants and all the people.

**11:4** And he said, "This is what the Lord says: 'At midnight I will go out into Egypt,

**11:5** and every firstborn in the land of the Egyptians will die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh, who sits on his throne, to the firstborn of the female servant who is at the mill, and all the firstborn of the livestock.

**11:6** And there will be a great outcry in the entire land of Egypt, such as has neither been before nor will be afterward.

**11:7** But among all the sons of Israel not a dog will make a sound, from man to beast, so that you may know by what a great miracle the Lord separates the Egyptians and Israel.'

**11:8** And all these servants of yours will come down to me and bow down to me, saying, 'Go out, you and all the people who are subject to you.' After this we will go out."

**11:9** And he went out from Pharaoh very angry. Then the Lord said to Moses, "Pharaoh will not listen to you, so that many signs may be done in the land of Egypt."

**11:10** Now Moses and Aaron performed before Pharaoh all the wonders that are written. And the Lord hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he did not let the sons of Israel go from his land.

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — Latin uses the future tense "Dabit" ("will give"), continuing the Lord's promise from verse 2, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text uses the narrative past ("gave").
- **11:7** — Latin "non mutiet canis" ("not a dog will make a sound") uses a classical idiom for uttering even the faintest sound or growl, rendering the Hebrew expression "will not sharpen its tongue."
- **11:8** — Latin has first-person plural "egrediemur" ("we will go out") where the Hebrew has singular "I will go out," and renders "who follow at your feet" as "qui subjectus est tibi" ("who are subject to you"). In the Vulgate verse division, Moses' angry departure opens verse 9 rather than closing verse 8.
- **11:10** — Latin "quæ scripta sunt" ("that are written") is a distinct Vulgate reading where the Hebrew Masoretic Text simply has "all these wonders."
