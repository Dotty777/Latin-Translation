## Text

**34:1** And afterward He said, "Cut out for yourself two stone tablets like the former ones, and I will write upon them the words that the tablets had which you broke.

**34:2** Be ready in the morning, so that you may go up immediately onto Mount Sinai, and you will stand with Me on the summit of the mountain.

**34:3** Let no one go up with you, nor let anyone be seen throughout the whole mountain; cattle and sheep also must not graze opposite it."

**34:4** So he cut out two stone tablets like those that had been before, and rising in the night, he went up onto Mount Sinai, just as the Lord had commanded him, carrying the tablets with him.

**34:5** And when the Lord had come down through the cloud, Moses stood with Him, calling upon the name of the Lord.

**34:6** And as He passed by before him, he said, "Ruler, Lord God, merciful and gracious, patient and of great compassion, and truthful,

**34:7** who keep mercy to thousands; who take away iniquity, and crimes, and sins, and before You no one is innocent by himself; who repay the iniquity of fathers to sons and to grandsons, to the third and fourth generation."

**34:8** And hurrying, Moses bowed prostrate to the ground, and worshiping,

**34:9** said, "If I have found favor in Your sight, Lord, I beg that You walk with us (for it is a stiff-necked people) and take away our iniquities and sins, and possess us."

**34:10** The Lord answered, "I will enter into a covenant while all look on: I will perform signs that have never been seen upon the earth, nor among any nations, so that this people, in whose midst you are, may see the fearsome work of the Lord that I am about to do.

**34:11** Observe everything that I command you today: I Myself will drive out before your face the Amorite, and the Canaanite, and the Hittite, the Perizzite also, and the Hivite, and the Jebusite.

**34:12** Be careful that you never form friendships with the inhabitants of that land, which would become a ruin to you;

**34:13** but destroy their altars, shatter the statues, and cut down the groves:

**34:14** do not worship a foreign god. The Lord: Jealous is His name; He is a jealous God.

**34:15** Do not enter into a covenant with the people of those regions, or else, when they have committed fornication with their gods and worshiped their images, someone may invite you to eat from what has been sacrificed.

**34:16** Nor shall you take a wife from their daughters for your sons, or else, after they themselves have committed fornication, they may cause your sons also to commit fornication after their gods.

**34:17** You shall not make molten gods for yourself.

**34:18** You shall keep the festival of unleavened bread. For seven days you shall eat unleavened bread, just as I commanded you, at the time of the month of new crops; for in the month of the spring season you went out from Egypt.

**34:19** Everything of the male kind that opens the womb shall be Mine. From all animals, both of cattle and of sheep, it shall be Mine.

**34:20** The firstborn of a donkey you shall redeem with a sheep; but if you do not give a price for it, it shall be put to death. The firstborn of your sons you shall redeem; and you shall not appear in My sight empty-handed.

**34:21** For six days you shall work; on the seventh day you shall cease plowing and harvesting.

**34:22** You shall celebrate for yourself the festival of weeks with the firstfruits of the produce of your wheat harvest, and the festival when, as the season of the year returns, everything is stored away.

**34:23** At three times of the year every male of yours shall appear in the sight of the Almighty Lord, the God of Israel.

**34:24** For when I have removed the nations from before your face and enlarged your borders, no one shall lie in wait against your land while you go up and appear in the sight of the Lord your God three times a year.

**34:25** You shall not sacrifice the blood of My offering upon leaven, nor shall anything remain in the morning from the sacrifice of the Passover festival.

**34:26** The firstfruits of the produce of your land you shall offer in the house of the Lord your God. You shall not cook a young goat in its mother's milk."

**34:27** And the Lord said to Moses, "Write for yourself these words, by which I have made a covenant both with you and with Israel."

**34:28** So he was there with the Lord forty days and forty nights; he did not eat bread, and he did not drink water; and he wrote on the tablets the ten words of the covenant.

**34:29** And as Moses was coming down from Mount Sinai, he was holding the two tablets of the testimony, and he did not know that his face was horned from the fellowship of the Lord's speech.

**34:30** Now Aaron and the sons of Israel, seeing the horned face of Moses, were afraid to come near.

**34:31** And called by him, both Aaron and the leaders of the assembly returned. And after he had spoken to them,

**34:32** all the sons of Israel also came to him, and he commanded them everything that he had heard from the Lord on Mount Sinai.

**34:33** And when his words were completed, he placed a veil over his face.

**34:34** When he went in to the Lord and spoke with Him, he would take it off until he went out, and then he would speak to the sons of Israel everything that had been commanded to him.

**34:35** And they would see that the face of Moses as he came out was horned, but he would cover his face again whenever he spoke to them.

## Translator Notes

- **34:6** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text, where the Lord proclaims His own name in the third person, the Vulgate renders this proclamation in the second person (v. 7: 'who keep... before You... who repay'), framing it as Moses' direct address to God ('Ruler, Lord God,' Latin Dominator Domine Deus) as the Lord passes before him.
- **34:18** — Instead of transliterating the Hebrew month name Abib, the Vulgate translates it descriptively first as 'the month of new crops' (mensis novorum) and then as 'the month of the spring season' (mense verni temporis).
- **34:29** — The Vulgate famously renders the Hebrew verb qaran ('shone, sent forth rays') as cornuta ('horned', from the related Hebrew root qeren, 'horn'), a reading repeated in vv. 30 and 35.
- **34:35** — Where the Hebrew text states that Moses replaced the veil until he went in to speak with the Lord, the Vulgate reads siquando loquebatur ad eos ('whenever he spoke to them').
