## Text

**13:1** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying:

**13:2** "Sanctify to Me every firstborn that opens the womb among the sons of Israel, both of humans and of beasts, for all are Mine."

**13:3** And Moses said to the people, "Remember this day on which you went out from Egypt and from the house of slavery, because with a strong hand the Lord brought you out from this place, so that you may not eat leavened bread.

**13:4** Today you are going out, in the month of new crops.

**13:5** And when the Lord has brought you into the land of the Canaanite, and the Hittite, and the Amorite, and the Hivite, and the Jebusite, which He swore to your fathers that He would give to you, a land flowing with milk and honey, you will celebrate this custom of sacred rites in this month.

**13:6** For seven days you will eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there will be a solemn feast of the Lord.

**13:7** You will eat unleavened bread for seven days; nothing leavened will appear with you, nor in all your borders.

**13:8** And you will tell your son on that day, saying, 'This is what the Lord did for me when I went out from Egypt.'

**13:9** And it will be like a sign on your hand, and like a memorial before your eyes, and so that the law of the Lord may always be in your mouth, for with a strong hand the Lord brought you out from Egypt.

**13:10** You will keep this kind of observance at the appointed time, from days to days.

**13:11** And when the Lord has brought you into the land of the Canaanite, just as He swore to you and to your fathers, and has given it to you,

**13:12** you will set apart everything that opens the womb to the Lord, and what is firstborn among your livestock; whatever you have of the male sex, you will consecrate to the Lord.

**13:13** You will exchange the firstborn of a donkey for a sheep, and if you do not redeem it, you will kill it. Yet every firstborn of man among your sons you will redeem with a price.

**13:14** And when your son asks you tomorrow, saying, 'What is this?' you will answer him, 'With a strong hand the Lord brought us out from the land of Egypt, from the house of slavery.

**13:15** For when Pharaoh had become hardened and was unwilling to let us go, the Lord killed every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of man up to the firstborn of beasts; for that reason I sacrifice to the Lord everything that opens the womb of the male sex, and I redeem all the firstborn of my sons.'

**13:16** Therefore it will be like a sign on your hand, and like something hung, for remembrance, between your eyes, because with a strong hand the Lord brought us out from Egypt."

**13:17** Therefore, when Pharaoh had sent the people out, God did not lead them by the way of the land of the Philistines, which is nearby, thinking that perhaps they might regret it if they saw wars rising up against them, and return to Egypt.

**13:18** But He led them around by the way of the desert, which is beside the Red Sea; and the sons of Israel went up armed from the land of Egypt.

**13:19** Moses also took the bones of Joseph with him, because he had bound the sons of Israel by an oath, saying, "God will visit you; carry my bones out from here with you."

**13:20** And setting out from Succoth, they encamped in Etham, at the farthest borders of the wilderness.

**13:21** Now the Lord was going ahead of them to show the way, by day in a pillar of cloud and by night in a pillar of fire, so that He might be the guide of the journey at both times.

**13:22** Never was the pillar of cloud absent by day, nor the pillar of fire by night, before the people.

## Translator Notes

- **13:4** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew month name Abib descriptively as mense novarum frugum ("the month of new crops").
- **13:10** — Latin a diebus in dies ("from days to days") preserves the literal Hebrew idiom for an annual recurrence ("year by year").
- **13:13** — The Vulgate generalizes the Hebrew "break its neck" to interficies ("you will kill it") and adds pretio ("with a price") for the redemption of a human firstborn.
- **13:16** — For the Hebrew term often rendered "frontlets," the Vulgate reads quasi appensum quid, ob recordationem ("like something hung, for remembrance").
- **13:21** — Where the Hebrew speaks of giving light so Israel could travel by day and night, the Vulgate reads ut dux esset itineris utroque tempore ("so that He might be the guide of the journey at both times").
