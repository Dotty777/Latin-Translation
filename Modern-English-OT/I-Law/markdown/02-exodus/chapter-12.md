## Text

**12:1** The Lord also said to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt:

**12:2** "This month shall be for you the beginning of months; it shall be the first among the months of the year.

**12:3** Speak to the entire assembly of the sons of Israel and say to them, 'On the tenth day of this month, let each one take a lamb according to their families and houses.

**12:4** But if the number is too small to suffice for eating the lamb, he shall take his neighbor who is next to his house, according to the number of souls that can suffice for the eating of the lamb.

**12:5** Now the lamb shall be without blemish, a male, one year old; according to this same rite you shall also take a young goat.

**12:6** And you shall keep it until the fourteenth day of this month, and the entire multitude of the sons of Israel shall sacrifice it at evening.

**12:7** And they shall take some of its blood and put it on both doorposts and on the lintels of the houses in which they will eat it.

**12:8** And they shall eat the meat that night, roasted with fire, and unleavened loaves with wild lettuce.

**12:9** You shall not eat anything of it raw, nor boiled in water, but only roasted with fire; you shall consume the head along with its feet and entrails.

**12:10** Nor shall anything of it remain until morning; if anything is left over, you shall burn it up with fire.

**12:11** Now you shall eat it in this way: you shall gird your waist, and have sandals on your feet, holding staffs in your hands, and you shall eat hurriedly; for it is the Passover (that is, the passing over) of the Lord.

**12:12** And I will pass through the land of Egypt on that night, and I will strike every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from man to beast; and against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments. I am the Lord.

**12:13** Now the blood will be a sign for you on the houses in which you will be; and I will see the blood, and I will pass over you, nor will there be a destroying plague among you when I strike the land of Egypt.

**12:14** Now you shall hold this day as a memorial, and you shall celebrate it as a solemn festival to the Lord throughout your generations with everlasting worship.

**12:15** For seven days you shall eat unleavened bread; on the first day there shall be no leaven in your houses. Whoever eats anything leavened, that soul shall perish from Israel, from the first day until the seventh day.

**12:16** The first day shall be holy and solemn, and the seventh day revered with the same festivity; you shall do no work on them, except for those things that pertain to eating.

**12:17** And you shall observe the unleavened bread; for on this very same day I will bring your army out of the land of Egypt, and you shall keep this day throughout your generations by a perpetual rite.

**12:18** In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month at evening, you shall eat unleavened bread until the twenty-first day of the same month at evening.

**12:19** For seven days leaven shall not be found in your houses. Whoever eats anything leavened, his soul shall perish from the assembly of Israel, whether from the strangers or from the natives of the land.

**12:20** You shall not eat anything leavened; in all your dwellings you shall eat unleavened bread.'"

**12:21** Then Moses called all the elders of the sons of Israel and said to them, "Go, taking an animal according to your families, and sacrifice the Passover.

**12:22** And dip a bundle of hyssop in the blood that is on the threshold, and sprinkle the lintel and both doorposts with it; let none of you go out of the doorway of his house until morning.

**12:23** For the Lord will pass through, striking the Egyptians; and when He sees the blood on the lintel and on both doorposts, He will pass over the doorway of the house, and He will not permit the striker to enter your houses and do harm.

**12:24** Keep this word as a statute for you and your sons forever.

**12:25** And when you enter the land that the Lord is going to give you, as He promised, you shall observe these ceremonies.

**12:26** And when your sons say to you, 'What is this religious observance?'

**12:27** you shall say to them, 'It is the sacrifice of the passing over of the Lord, when He passed over the houses of the sons of Israel in Egypt, striking the Egyptians and delivering our houses.'" And the people bowed down and worshiped.

**12:28** And the sons of Israel went out and did just as the Lord had commanded Moses and Aaron.

**12:29** Now it happened in the middle of the night that the Lord struck every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sat on his throne to the firstborn of the captive woman who was in prison, and every firstborn of the livestock.

**12:30** And Pharaoh rose up in the night, and all his servants, and all Egypt; and a great outcry arose in Egypt, for there was no house in which a dead person did not lie.

**12:31** And having called Moses and Aaron in the night, Pharaoh said, "Rise and go out from my people, you and the sons of Israel; go, sacrifice to the Lord as you say.

**12:32** Take your sheep and your herds as you had asked, and as you depart, bless me."

**12:33** And the Egyptians urged the people to go out of the land quickly, saying, "We will all die."

**12:34** Therefore the people took the kneaded flour before it was leavened, and tying it up in cloaks, they placed it on their shoulders.

**12:35** And the sons of Israel did just as Moses had commanded, and they asked of the Egyptians silver and gold vessels and very much clothing.

**12:36** Now the Lord gave favor to the people before the Egyptians, so that they lent to them; and they plundered the Egyptians.

**12:37** And the sons of Israel set out from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand men on foot, besides the little ones.

**12:38** And a countless mixed crowd also went up with them, sheep and herds and animals of various kinds, exceedingly many.

**12:39** And they baked the flour that they had previously brought kneaded out of Egypt, and they made unleavened loaves baked under the ashes; for they could not be leavened, as the Egyptians were forcing them to leave and allowing them to make no delay, nor had there been opportunity to prepare any provisions.

**12:40** Now the dwelling of the sons of Israel, during which they remained in Egypt, was four hundred thirty years.

**12:41** When these were completed, on that same day the entire army of the Lord went out of the land of Egypt.

**12:42** This night is to be observed for the Lord, when He brought them out of the land of Egypt; all the sons of Israel must observe this night throughout their generations.

**12:43** And the Lord said to Moses and Aaron, "This is the religious observance of the Passover: no foreigner shall eat of it.

**12:44** But every purchased servant shall be circumcised, and thus he shall eat.

**12:45** A stranger and a hired worker shall not eat of it.

**12:46** It shall be eaten in one house; you shall not carry any of its meat outside, nor shall you break a bone of it.

**12:47** The entire assembly of the sons of Israel shall keep it.

**12:48** And if any of the sojourners wishes to cross over into your settlement and keep the Passover of the Lord, every male belonging to him shall first be circumcised, and then he shall celebrate it according to the rite, and he shall be like a native of the land. But if anyone has not been circumcised, he shall not eat of it.

**12:49** The same law shall be for the native and for the settler who sojourns among you."

**12:50** And all the sons of Israel did just as the Lord had commanded Moses and Aaron.

**12:51** And on that same day the Lord brought the sons of Israel out of the land of Egypt by their companies.

## Translator Notes

- **12:8** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew "bitter herbs" (merorim) specifically as lactucis agrestibus ("wild lettuce").
- **12:11** — The Vulgate transliterates Hebrew Pesach as Phase and inserts the explanatory gloss (id est, transitus), "that is, the passing over," which does not appear in the Hebrew text.
- **12:22** — The Vulgate reads in limine ("on the threshold"), reflecting one meaning of the ambiguous Hebrew noun saph, which many other versions render "basin."
- **12:29** — The Vulgate specifies the feminine captivae ("the captive woman who was in prison"), whereas the Hebrew uses the masculine singular ("the captive who was in the dungeon").
- **12:48** — The Vulgate uses Roman civic terms, in vestram coloniam ("into your settlement") here and colono ("settler") in v. 49, where the Hebrew speaks simply of a stranger sojourning with Israel.
