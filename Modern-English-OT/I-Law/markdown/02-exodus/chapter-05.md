## Text

**5:1** After these things Moses and Aaron went in and said to Pharaoh, "This is what the Lord God of Israel says: Let My people go so that they may sacrifice to Me in the desert."

**5:2** But he answered, "Who is the Lord, that I should listen to His voice and let Israel go? I do not know the Lord, and I will not let Israel go."

**5:3** And they said, "The God of the Hebrews has called us, that we may go a journey of three days into the wilderness and sacrifice to the Lord our God, so that plague or sword may not by chance come upon us."

**5:4** The king of Egypt said to them, "Why, Moses and Aaron, do you distract the people from their work? Go to your burdens."

**5:5** And Pharaoh said, "The people of the land are numerous; you see that the crowd has increased; how much more if you give them rest from work?"

**5:6** Therefore on that day he commanded the overseers of the work and the taskmasters of the people, saying,

**5:7** "By no means will you give straw to the people anymore for making bricks, as before; but let them go themselves and gather stubble.

**5:8** And you will impose upon them the measure of bricks that they used to make before, nor will you reduce anything; for they are idle, and therefore they cry out, saying, 'Let us go and sacrifice to our God.'

**5:9** Let them be weighed down with work, and let them carry it out, so that they may not yield to lying words."

**5:10** Therefore the overseers of the work and the taskmasters went out to the people and said, "This is what Pharaoh says: I am not giving you straw;

**5:11** go and gather it wherever you can find it, and nothing will be reduced from your work."

**5:12** And the people were scattered throughout the whole land of Egypt to gather straw.

**5:13** The overseers of the work also pressed hard, saying, "Complete your work daily, just as you used to do before when straw was given to you."

**5:14** And those who were over the work of the sons of Israel were flogged by Pharaoh's taskmasters, who said, "Why do you not fulfill the measure of bricks as before, neither yesterday nor today?"

**5:15** And the foremen of the sons of Israel came and cried out to Pharaoh, saying, "Why do you act this way against your servants?

**5:16** Straw is not given to us, and bricks are demanded just the same; look, we your servants are beaten with whips, and injustice is done against your people."

**5:17** He said, "You are idle with leisure, and therefore you say, 'Let us go and sacrifice to the Lord.'

**5:18** Go, therefore, and work; straw will not be given to you, and you will deliver the usual number of bricks."

**5:19** And the foremen of the sons of Israel saw themselves in trouble, because it was said to them, "Nothing will be reduced from the bricks each day."

**5:20** And they met Moses and Aaron, who were standing opposite, as they came out from Pharaoh,

**5:21** and they said to them, "May the Lord look and judge, since you have made our odor stink before Pharaoh and his servants, and have offered him a sword so that he might kill us."

**5:22** And Moses returned to the Lord and said, "Lord, why have You afflicted this people? Why did You send me?

**5:23** For ever since I went in to Pharaoh to speak in Your name, he has afflicted Your people, and You have not delivered them."

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — Latin vocavit nos ("has called us") is a Vulgate-distinct rendering where the Hebrew reads "has met with us."
- **5:5** — Latin videtis quod turba succreverit: quanto magis si dederitis eis requiem ab operibus? ("you see that the crowd has increased; how much more if you give them rest from work?") is a distinctly Vulgate expansion and question compared with the shorter Hebrew clause.
- **5:12** — Latin ad colligendas paleas ("to gather straw") simplifies the Hebrew phrase "to gather stubble for straw."
- **5:16** — Latin et injuste agitur contra populum tuum ("and injustice is done against your people") is a Vulgate-distinct reading where the Hebrew literally says "and the fault is your own people's."
