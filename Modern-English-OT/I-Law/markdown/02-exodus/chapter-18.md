## Text

**18:1** And when Jethro, priest of Midian, the relative of Moses, had heard all that God had done for Moses and for Israel His people, and that the Lord had brought Israel out of Egypt,

**18:2** he took Zipporah, the wife of Moses, whom he had sent back,

**18:3** and her two sons, one of whom was called Gershom, his father saying, "I have been a sojourner in a foreign land";

**18:4** and the other Eliezer: "For the God of my father," he said, "is my helper, and He rescued me from the sword of Pharaoh."

**18:5** Therefore Jethro, the relative of Moses, and his sons and his wife came to Moses in the desert, where he had encamped beside the mountain of God.

**18:6** And he sent word to Moses, saying, "I, Jethro your relative, am coming to you, and your wife, and the two sons with her."

**18:7** And he went out to meet his relative, bowed down, and kissed him; and they greeted one another with words of peace. And when he had entered the tent,

**18:8** Moses recounted to his relative everything that the Lord had done to Pharaoh and to the Egyptians on account of Israel, and all the hardship that had happened to them on the journey, and that the Lord had delivered them.

**18:9** And Jethro rejoiced over all the good things that the Lord had done for Israel, because He had rescued them from the hand of the Egyptians.

**18:10** And he said, "Blessed be the Lord, who delivered you from the hand of the Egyptians and from the hand of Pharaoh, who rescued His people from the hand of Egypt.

**18:11** Now I know that the Lord is great above all gods, because they acted arrogantly against them."

**18:12** Therefore Jethro, the relative of Moses, offered burnt offerings and sacrifices to God; and Aaron and all the elders of Israel came to eat bread with him before God.

**18:13** Now on the next day Moses sat to judge the people, who stood before Moses from morning until evening.

**18:14** And when his relative saw this, namely everything that he was doing among the people, he said, "What is this that you are doing among the people? Why do you sit alone, and all the people wait from morning until evening?"

**18:15** Moses replied to him, "The people come to me seeking the judgment of God;

**18:16** and whenever any dispute arises among them, they come to me so that I may judge between them and show the precepts of God and His laws."

**18:17** But he said, "You are not doing a good thing.

**18:18** You will be worn out with foolish labor, both you and this people who are with you; the task is beyond your strength; you will not be able to bear it alone.

**18:19** But listen to my words and counsels, and God will be with you. Be for the people in the things that pertain to God, so that you may report to Him what is said,

**18:20** and show the people the ceremonies and the rite of worship, and the way in which they ought to walk, and the work that they ought to do.

**18:21** Moreover, select from all the people capable men who fear God, in whom there is truth, and who hate greed, and appoint from them tribunes, centurions, leaders of fifty, and leaders of ten,

**18:22** to judge the people at all times; and whatever is greater, let them bring to you, while they themselves judge only the smaller matters, so that it may be lighter for you, with the burden divided among others.

**18:23** If you do this, you will fulfill the command of God, and you will be able to uphold His precepts, and all this people will return to their own places in peace."

**18:24** Having heard these things, Moses did everything that he had suggested.

**18:25** And having chosen vigorous men from all Israel, he appointed them rulers of the people: tribunes, centurions, leaders of fifty, and leaders of ten.

**18:26** And they judged the people at all times; whatever was more serious they brought to him, judging only the easier matters.

**18:27** And he let his relative go, who returned and departed to his own land.

## Translator Notes

- **18:1** — Throughout chapter 18 the Vulgate consistently refers to Jethro as cognatus ("relative" or "kinsman") rather than socer ("father-in-law"), the term it used in Exodus 3:1 and 4:18 for the Hebrew choten.
- **18:18** — Latin Stulto labore consumeris ("You will be worn out with foolish labor") is a Vulgate-distinct reading reflecting a connection of the Hebrew verb navol ("wear out") with a homonymous root meaning "foolish."
- **18:21** — The Vulgate renders Israel's decimal judicial ranks with Roman military titles: tribunos (commanders of a thousand), centuriones (commanders of a hundred), quinquagenarios (leaders of fifty), and decanos (leaders of ten).
- **18:23** — Latin implebis imperium Dei, et praecepta ejus poteris sustentare ("you will fulfill the command of God, and you will be able to uphold His precepts") diverges from the Hebrew, which reads "and God so commands you, then you will be able to endure."
