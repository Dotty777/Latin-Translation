## Text

**27:1** You shall also make an altar of acacia wood, which shall have five cubits in length and just as many in width, that is, square, and three cubits in height.

**27:2** Now horns at the four corners shall be made from it itself, and you shall cover it with bronze.

**27:3** And for its uses you shall make pots for receiving the ashes, and tongs and forks, and receptacles for fire; all the vessels you shall fashion out of bronze.

**27:4** And you shall make a bronze grating in the manner of a net, at whose four corners there shall be four bronze rings.

**27:5** These you shall place beneath the hearth of the altar, and the grating shall reach as far as the middle of the altar.

**27:6** You shall also make two bars of acacia wood for the altar, which you shall cover with bronze plates,

**27:7** and you shall pass them through the rings, and they shall be on each side of the altar for carrying.

**27:8** You shall make it not solid, but empty and hollow on the inside, just as was shown to you on the mountain.

**27:9** You shall also make the courtyard of the tabernacle, on whose southern quarter facing the south there shall be hangings of twisted fine linen; one side shall extend one hundred cubits in length.

**27:10** And you shall make twenty pillars with just as many bronze bases, which shall have silver capitals together with their engravings.

**27:11** Likewise also along the length on the northern side there shall be hangings of one hundred cubits, twenty pillars, and bronze bases of the same number, and their capitals with their engravings of silver.

**27:12** Now along the width of the courtyard, which faces toward the west, there shall be hangings for fifty cubits, and ten pillars, and just as many bases.

**27:13** On that width of the courtyard also that faces toward the east, there shall be fifty cubits.

**27:14** Of these, hangings of fifteen cubits shall be assigned to one side, and three pillars and just as many bases;

**27:15** and on the other side there shall be hangings spanning fifteen cubits, three pillars, and just as many bases.

**27:16** Now at the entrance of the courtyard a hanging of twenty cubits shall be made of violet-blue and purple, and twice-dyed scarlet, and twisted fine linen, with the work of an embroiderer; it shall have four pillars, with just as many bases.

**27:17** All the pillars of the courtyard all around shall be clad with silver plates, with silver capitals and bronze bases.

**27:18** In length the courtyard shall occupy one hundred cubits, in width fifty; the height shall be five cubits, and it shall be made of twisted fine linen, and shall have bronze bases.

**27:19** All the vessels of the tabernacle for all uses and ceremonies, both its pegs and those of the courtyard, you shall make out of bronze.

**27:20** Command the sons of Israel to bring to you oil from olive trees, most pure and crushed with a pestle, so that the lamp may burn continually

**27:21** in the tabernacle of the testimony, outside the veil that is stretched before the testimony. And Aaron and his sons shall set it in place, so that it may shine until morning before the Lord. It shall be a perpetual observance throughout their generations by the sons of Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **27:3** — The Vulgate lists four altar utensils—pots, tongs (forcipes), forks, and receptacles for fire—whereas the Hebrew lists five, including shovels in place of tongs as well as basins.
- **27:5** — Latin quos ('these', masculine plural) refers grammatically to the bronze rings of 27:4 rather than the grating itself, and arulam (literally 'little altar' or 'hearth') corresponds to the Hebrew 'ledge'.
- **27:10** — Latin capita cum cælaturis suis ('capitals with their engravings', repeated in 27:11) corresponds to 'hooks and bands' in the Hebrew; likewise 27:17 describes the pillars as 'clad with silver plates' (vestitæ ... argenteis laminis).
- **27:21** — Latin in tabernaculo testimonii ('in the tabernacle of the testimony') follows the Septuagint reading rather than the Hebrew 'tent of meeting', and 'set it in place' (collocabunt) omits the Hebrew phrase 'from evening'.
