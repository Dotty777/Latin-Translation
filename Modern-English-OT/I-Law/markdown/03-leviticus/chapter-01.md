## Text

**1:1** Now the Lord called Moses and spoke to him from the tabernacle of the testimony, saying,

**1:2** "Speak to the sons of Israel, and you shall say to them, 'A person from among you who offers a sacrifice to the Lord from the livestock, that is, offering victims from the cattle and the sheep,

**1:3** if his offering is a burnt offering, and from the herd, he shall offer an unblemished male at the doorway of the tabernacle of the testimony, to make the Lord favorable to himself.

**1:4** And he shall place his hand on the head of the sacrifice, and it will be acceptable and effective for his expiation.

**1:5** And he shall slaughter the calf before the Lord, and the sons of Aaron, the priests, shall offer its blood, pouring it all around the altar that is before the doorway of the tabernacle.

**1:6** And when the skin of the sacrifice has been stripped off, they shall cut the limbs into pieces.

**1:7** And they shall place fire underneath on the altar, with a pile of wood arranged beforehand,

**1:8** also arranging on top the parts that have been cut, namely, the head and all that adheres to the liver,

**1:9** with the intestines and feet washed with water; and the priest shall burn them on the altar as a burnt offering and a sweet fragrance to the Lord.

**1:10** Now if the offering is from the livestock, a burnt offering from the sheep or from the goats, he shall offer a male without blemish,

**1:11** and he shall slaughter it at the side of the altar that faces toward the north, before the Lord; and the sons of Aaron shall pour its blood on the altar all around.

**1:12** And they shall divide the parts, the head, and all that adheres to the liver, and they shall place them on the wood under which fire is to be placed;

**1:13** but they shall wash the intestines and the feet with water. And the priest shall burn on the altar all that has been offered, as a burnt offering and a very sweet fragrance to the Lord.

**1:14** Now if the offering of a burnt offering to the Lord is from birds, from turtledoves or the young of a dove,

**1:15** the priest shall offer it at the altar, and with its head twisted back to the neck and the place of the wound broken open, he shall cause the blood to run down onto the ledge of the altar;

**1:16** but he shall throw the crop of the throat and the feathers near the altar toward the eastern side, in the place where the ashes are customarily poured out,

**1:17** and he shall break its wings, and shall not cut it nor divide it with iron, and he shall burn it on the altar, on the wood with fire placed underneath. It is a burnt offering and an offering of a very sweet fragrance to the Lord.'"

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Vulgate reads "tabernaculo testimonii" ("tabernacle of the testimony"), matching the Greek Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "tent of meeting."
- **1:3** — Vulgate reads "ad placandum sibi Dominum" ("to make the Lord favorable to himself"), where the Hebrew reads "for his acceptance before the Lord."
- **1:8** — Here and in 1:12, the Vulgate specifies "all that adheres to the liver" ("quae adhaerent jecori"), whereas the Hebrew reads simply "the fat" (suet).
- **1:15** — Vulgate "retorto ad collum capite, ac rupto vulneris loco" ("with its head twisted back to the neck and the place of the wound broken open") gives a detailed procedural rendering of the Hebrew verb for wringing off the bird's head; "crepidinem" denotes the projecting ledge or base of the altar.
- **1:17** — Latin "ascellas" denotes the bird's wing-joints or wings, and "neque ferro dividet eam" ("nor divide it with iron") is a Vulgate clarification of the Hebrew prohibition against severing the bird completely.
