## Text

**13:1** And the Lord spoke to Moses and Aaron, saying,

**13:2** "A person in whose skin and flesh a different color arises, or a pustule, or something as though shining, that is, a plague of leprosy, will be brought to Aaron the priest, or to any one of his sons.

**13:3** And when he sees leprosy in the skin, and the hairs changed to a white color, and the appearance of the leprosy itself lower than the skin and the rest of the flesh, it is a plague of leprosy, and at his decision he will be separated.

**13:4** But if there is a shining whiteness in the skin, and it is not lower than the rest of the flesh, and the hairs are of their former color, the priest will confine him for seven days,

**13:5** and will examine him on the seventh day; and if indeed the leprosy has grown no further, nor crossed its former limits in the skin, he will confine him again for another seven days.

**13:6** And on the seventh day he will inspect him: if the leprosy is dimmer and has not grown in the skin, he will pronounce him clean, because it is a scab; and the person will wash his garments and will be clean.

**13:7** But if, after he has been seen by the priest and restored to cleanness, the leprosy has grown again, he will be brought to him,

**13:8** and will be condemned to uncleanness.

**13:9** If a plague of leprosy is in a person, he will be brought to the priest,

**13:10** and he will see him. And when there is a white color in the skin, and it has changed the appearance of the hairs, and the living flesh itself has also appeared,

**13:11** it will be judged a very old leprosy, and ingrained in the skin. Therefore the priest will pronounce him unclean, and will not confine him, because his uncleanness is evident.

**13:12** But if the leprosy breaks out, spreading in the skin, and covers all the skin from the head all the way to the feet, whatever falls under the sight of the eyes,

**13:13** the priest will examine him, and will judge him to be held by a most clean leprosy, because all of it has turned to whiteness, and therefore the person will be clean.

**13:14** But when living flesh appears in him,

**13:15** then by the judgment of the priest he will be pronounced defiled, and will be counted among the unclean; for living flesh, if it is sprinkled with leprosy, is unclean.

**13:16** But if it has turned again to whiteness and has covered the whole person,

**13:17** the priest will examine him, and will decree him to be clean.

**13:18** Now as for flesh and skin in which an ulcer has formed and been healed,

**13:19** and in the place of the ulcer a white or reddish scar has appeared, the person will be brought to the priest.

**13:20** And when he sees the place of the leprosy lower than the rest of the flesh, and the hairs turned to whiteness, he will pronounce him unclean; for the plague of leprosy has arisen in the ulcer.

**13:21** But if the hair is of its former color, and the scar is somewhat dim, and it is not lower than the neighboring flesh, he will confine him for seven days;

**13:22** and if indeed it has grown, he will adjudge him to leprosy;

**13:23** but if it has remained in its place, it is the scar of the ulcer, and the person will be clean.

**13:24** Now as for flesh and skin that fire has burned, and that when healed has a white or red scar,

**13:25** the priest will examine it, and look, it has turned to whiteness, and its place is lower than the rest of the skin; he will pronounce him unclean, because the plague of leprosy has arisen in the scar.

**13:26** But if the color of the hairs has not been changed, nor is the plague lower than the rest of the flesh, and the appearance of the leprosy itself is somewhat dim, he will confine him for seven days,

**13:27** and on the seventh day he will inspect him: if the leprosy has grown in the skin, he will pronounce him unclean.

**13:28** But if the whiteness has remained in its place, not very bright, it is a wound of burning, and therefore he will be pronounced clean, because it is the scar of a burn.

**13:29** As for a man or a woman on whose head or beard leprosy has sprouted, the priest will see them.

**13:30** And if indeed the place is lower than the rest of the flesh, and the hair is yellow and thinner than usual, he will pronounce them unclean, because it is leprosy of the head and beard.

**13:31** But if he sees the place of the spot level with the neighboring flesh, and the hair black, he will confine him for seven days,

**13:32** and on the seventh day he will observe him. If the spot has not grown, and the hair is of its own color, and the place of the plague is level with the rest of the flesh,

**13:33** the person will be shaved, except for the place of the spot, and will be shut in for another seven days.

**13:34** If on the seventh day the plague is seen to have remained in its place, and is not lower than the rest of the flesh, he will pronounce him clean; and once his clothes have been washed, he will be clean.

**13:35** But if after cleansing the spot has grown again in the skin,

**13:36** he will inquire no further whether the hair has been changed to a yellow color, because he is plainly unclean.

**13:37** Moreover, if the spot has stood still and the hairs are black, let him recognize that the person has been healed, and let him declare him clean with confidence.

**13:38** As for a man or a woman in whose skin whiteness has appeared,

**13:39** the priest will observe them. If he detects a somewhat dim whiteness shining in the skin, let him know that it is not leprosy, but a spot of white color, and that the person is clean.

**13:40** A man from whose head the hairs fall out is bald and clean;

**13:41** and if the hairs have fallen from the forehead, he is bald at the forehead and clean.

**13:42** But if in the baldness or in the forehead baldness a white or red color has arisen,

**13:43** and the priest sees this, he will condemn him to unquestionable leprosy, which has arisen in the baldness.

**13:44** Whoever therefore has been stained with leprosy, and has been separated at the decision of the priest,

**13:45** will have unstitched garments, a bare head, his mouth covered with a garment, and will cry out that he is defiled and filthy.

**13:46** For all the time during which he is leprous and unclean, he will live alone outside the camp.

**13:47** A woolen or linen garment that has leprosy,

**13:48** in the warp and the weft, or indeed leather, or anything made of leather,

**13:49** if it has been tainted with a white or red spot, it will be counted as leprosy, and will be shown to the priest,

**13:50** who, having examined it, will confine it for seven days;

**13:51** and looking at it again on the seventh day, if he detects that it has grown, it is a persistent leprosy; he will judge the garment defiled, and everything in which it has been found,

**13:52** and therefore it will be burned up in flames.

**13:53** But if he sees that it has not grown,

**13:54** he will command, and they will wash that in which the leprosy is, and he will confine it for another seven days.

**13:55** And when he sees that the former appearance indeed has not returned, and yet that the leprosy has not grown, he will judge it unclean and will burn it up with fire, because the leprosy has been infused into the surface of the garment, or throughout the whole.

**13:56** But if the place of the leprosy is dimmer after the garment has been washed, he will tear it off and will separate it from the sound part.

**13:57** But if it appears further in those places that were previously unspotted, a flying and wandering leprosy, it must be burned up with fire.

**13:58** If it has ceased, he will wash the things that are pure with water a second time, and they will be clean.

**13:59** This is the law of the leprosy of a woolen and linen garment, of the warp and the weft, and of every leather article, how it ought to be pronounced clean or pronounced unclean."

## Translator Notes

- **13:3** — Vulgate "ad arbitrium ejus separabitur" ("at his decision he will be separated") differs from the Hebrew, which reads "pronounce him unclean." Throughout this chapter, the Vulgate also renders the depth of a lesion with "humilior" ("lower") rather than "deeper."
- **13:11** — Biblical Latin uses "contaminabit" (literally "will defile") and "mundabit" (13:6, literally "will cleanse") declaratively to mean "will pronounce unclean" and "will pronounce clean" throughout this chapter.
- **13:13** — Vulgate "teneri lepra mundissima judicabit" ("will judge him to be held by a most clean leprosy") is a distinctive Vulgate wording where the Hebrew states simply that the priest shall pronounce the affliction clean.
- **13:25** — Vulgate "versa est in alborem" ("it has turned to whiteness") takes the scar as its subject without mentioning hair, whereas the Hebrew specifies that the hair in the bright spot has turned white.
- **13:31** — Vulgate "et capillum nigrum" ("and the hair black") omits the negation found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "and there is no black hair in it."
- **13:45** — Vulgate renders this verse with distinctive wording: "vestimenta dissuta" ("unstitched garments"), "os veste contectum" ("his mouth covered with a garment," vs. Hebrew "upper lip"), and indirect speech "contaminatum ac sordidum se clamabit" ("will cry out that he is defiled and filthy," vs. Hebrew "Unclean, unclean!").
- **13:49** — Vulgate reads "alba vel rufa" ("white or red") where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "greenish or reddish."
- **13:57** — Vulgate "lepra volatilis et vaga" ("a flying and wandering leprosy") is a distinctive rendering of the Hebrew term for a spreading eruption, and "in those places that were previously unspotted" replaces the Hebrew list of garment and leather parts.
