## Text

**24:1** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying:

**24:2** "Command the sons of Israel to bring to you purest and clear oil from olives, to tend the lamps continually,

**24:3** outside the veil of the testimony in the tabernacle of the covenant. And Aaron will place them from evening until morning before the Lord, by a perpetual worship and rite in your generations.

**24:4** Upon the purest lampstand they will always be placed in the sight of the Lord.

**24:5** You will also take fine flour, and bake from it twelve loaves, which individually will contain two-tenths;

**24:6** you will set six of them on each side upon the purest table before the Lord;

**24:7** and you will place upon them the clearest frankincense, so that the bread may be for a memorial of the oblation to the Lord.

**24:8** On each sabbath they will be changed before the Lord, received from the sons of Israel by an everlasting covenant;

**24:9** and they will belong to Aaron and his sons, so that they may eat them in a holy place, because it is a holy of holies from the sacrifices of the Lord, by a perpetual right."

**24:10** Now look, the son of an Israelite woman, whom she had borne by an Egyptian man among the sons of Israel, went out and quarreled in the camp with an Israelite man.

**24:11** And when he had blasphemed the Name and cursed it, he was brought to Moses. (Now his mother was called Shelomith, daughter of Dibri, of the tribe of Dan.)

**24:12** And they put him in prison, until they knew what the Lord would command.

**24:13** And He spoke to Moses,

**24:14** saying: "Bring the blasphemer outside the camp, and let all who heard place their hands upon his head, and let the entire people stone him.

**24:15** And to the sons of Israel you will speak: 'A person who curses his God will bear his sin;

**24:16** and whoever blasphemes the name of the Lord, let him surely die; the entire multitude will overwhelm him with stones, whether he is a citizen or a foreigner. Whoever blasphemes the name of the Lord, let him surely die.

**24:17** Whoever strikes and kills a person, let him surely die.

**24:18** Whoever strikes an animal will repay a substitute, that is, life for life.

**24:19** Whoever inflicts a blemish upon any one of his citizens, just as he has done, so it will be done to him:

**24:20** fracture for fracture, eye for eye, tooth for tooth he will restore; whatever kind of blemish he has inflicted, such he will be compelled to endure.

**24:21** Whoever strikes a beast of burden will repay another. Whoever strikes a person will be punished.

**24:22** Let there be an equal judgment among you, whether a foreigner or a citizen has sinned, because I am the Lord your God.'"

**24:23** And Moses spoke to the sons of Israel; and they brought the one who had blasphemed outside the camp, and overwhelmed him with stones. And the sons of Israel did just as the Lord had commanded Moses.

## Translator Notes

- **24:2** — The Vulgate specifies "purest and clear oil" (purissimum, ac lucidum) and "to tend the lamps" (ad concinnandas lucernas), where the Hebrew reads "pure beaten olive oil" to keep a lamp burning.
- **24:8** — The Vulgate reads mutabuntur ("they will be changed"), referring to the weekly replacement of the twelve loaves, where the Hebrew verb means to arrange them.
- **24:18** — The Vulgate adds the clarifying phrase vicarium, id est ("a substitute, that is") before "life for life" (animam pro anima).
- **24:21** — The Vulgate translates the penalty for striking a person with the general term punietur ("will be punished"), whereas the Hebrew explicitly specifies capital punishment ("shall be put to death").
