## Text

**21:1** The Lord also said to Moses: "Speak to the priests, the sons of Aaron, and you shall say to them: 'Let a priest not be defiled for the deaths of his fellow citizens,

**21:2** except only for blood relatives and close kin, that is, for a father and mother, and a son and daughter, a brother also,

**21:3** and a virgin sister who is not married to a man;

**21:4** but not even for a ruler of his people shall he be defiled.

**21:5** They shall not shave the head nor the beard, nor make cuts in their flesh.

**21:6** They shall be holy to their God, and they shall not pollute His name; for they offer the incense of the Lord and the loaves of their God, and therefore they shall be holy.

**21:7** They shall not take as a wife a prostitute or a vile woman of prostitution, nor one who has been divorced by her husband, because they are consecrated to their God,

**21:8** and they offer the loaves of presentation. Therefore let them be holy, because I also am holy, the Lord who sanctifies them.

**21:9** If the daughter of a priest is caught in fornication and violates the name of her father, she shall be burned up in flames.

**21:10** The high priest, that is, the greatest priest among his brothers, upon whose head the oil of anointing has been poured, and whose hands have been consecrated in the priesthood, and who has been clothed in the holy garments, shall not uncover his head nor tear his garments,

**21:11** and he shall not go in to any dead person at all; even for his father and mother he shall not be defiled.

**21:12** Nor shall he go out from the holy places, so that he does not pollute the sanctuary of the Lord, because the oil of the holy anointing of his God is upon him. I am the Lord.

**21:13** He shall take a virgin as a wife;

**21:14** however, he shall not take a widow, or a divorced woman, or a sordid woman, or a prostitute, but a young woman from his own people,

**21:15** so that he does not mingle the stock of his lineage with the common people of his nation, because I am the Lord who sanctifies him.'"

**21:16** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying:

**21:17** "Speak to Aaron: 'A man from your seed throughout the families who has a blemish shall not offer loaves to his God,

**21:18** nor shall he approach His ministry: if he is blind, if lame, if with a small or a large or a crooked nose,

**21:19** if with a broken foot, or with a broken hand,

**21:20** if hunchbacked, if bleary-eyed, if having a white spot in the eye, if having chronic scabies, if having impetigo on the body, or if having a hernia.

**21:21** No one from the seed of Aaron the priest who has a blemish shall approach to offer sacrifices to the Lord, nor loaves to his God;

**21:22** yet he may eat of the loaves that are offered in the sanctuary,

**21:23** provided only that he does not enter within the veil, nor approach the altar, because he has a blemish and must not defile My sanctuary. I am the Lord who sanctifies them.'"

**21:24** Therefore Moses spoke to Aaron, and to his sons, and to all Israel, all the things that had been commanded to him.

## Translator Notes

- **21:4** — Latin "sed nec in principe populi sui contaminabitur" ("but not even for a ruler of his people shall he be defiled") resolves a notoriously difficult Hebrew clause (often rendered "as a husband/chief among his people, so as to profane himself") as a prohibition against defilement even for a deceased ruler.
- **21:8** — The Vulgate specifies "panes propositionis" ("the loaves of presentation") and maintains third-person plural verbs, whereas the Hebrew refers more generally to "the bread of your God" in the second person singular.
- **21:10** — Latin "Pontifex, id est, sacerdos maximus" incorporates Jerome's explanatory gloss ("that is, the greatest priest"), and "caput suum non discooperiet" ("shall not uncover his head") renders a Hebrew verb commonly understood as leaving the hair disheveled.
- **21:15** — Latin "ne commisceat stirpem generis sui vulgo gentis suae" ("so that he does not mingle the stock of his lineage with the common people of his nation") is a distinctly Vulgate rendering where the Hebrew reads "so that he does not profane his offspring among his people."
- **21:18** — The Vulgate's "si parvo vel grandi, vel torto naso" ("if with a small or a large or a crooked nose") interprets two Hebrew terms for a disfigured face/nose and an oversized limb as three nasal defects; similarly in v. 20, "lippus" ("bleary-eyed") corresponds to a Hebrew term usually taken to mean a dwarf or withered person.
