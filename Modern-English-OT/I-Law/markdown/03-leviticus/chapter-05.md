## Text

**5:1** "If a soul sins, and hears the voice of one swearing, and is a witness because he either saw it himself or is aware of it: unless he discloses it, he will bear his iniquity.

**5:2** A soul who touches anything unclean, whether what was killed by a beast, or died on its own, or any other creeping thing, and forgets his uncleanness, is guilty and has offended;

**5:3** and if he touches anything of the uncleanness of a person, according to every impurity by which one is customarily defiled, and having forgotten, comes to know it afterward, he will be liable for the offense.

**5:4** A soul who swears and utters with his lips to do either something evil or something good, and confirms that very thing with an oath and speech, and having forgotten, afterward realizes his offense,

**5:5** let him do penance for the sin,

**5:6** and let him offer from the flocks a female lamb or a female goat, and the priest shall pray for him and for his sin.

**5:7** But if instead he is not able to offer a head of livestock, let him offer two turtledoves or two young pigeons to the Lord, one for sin and the other for a burnt offering,

**5:8** and he shall give them to the priest, who, offering the first one for sin, shall twist back its head toward the little wings, so that it clings to the neck and is not completely broken off.

**5:9** And he shall sprinkle the wall of the altar with some of its blood; but whatever remains, he shall cause to drip down at its foundation, because it is for sin.

**5:10** But the other he shall burn for a burnt offering, as is customarily done; and the priest shall pray for him and for his sin, and it will be forgiven him.

**5:11** Now if his hand is not able to offer two turtledoves or two young pigeons, he shall offer for his sin a tenth part of an ephah of fine flour; he shall not put oil into it, nor place any frankincense upon it, because it is for sin.

**5:12** And he shall hand it over to the priest, who, drawing a full handful from it, shall burn it up upon the altar as a memorial of the one who offered it,

**5:13** praying for him and making expiation; but the remaining part he himself shall have as a gift."

**5:14** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying:

**5:15** "If a soul, violating the ceremonies through error, sins in the things that have been sanctified to the Lord, he shall offer for his offense an unblemished ram from the flocks, which can be bought for two shekels, according to the weight of the sanctuary;

**5:16** and he shall restore the very loss that he caused, and place a fifth part on top of it, handing it over to the priest, who shall pray for him while offering the ram, and it will be forgiven him.

**5:17** If a soul sins through ignorance, and does one of the things that are forbidden by the law of the Lord, and, guilty of sin, realizes his iniquity,

**5:18** he shall offer an unblemished ram from the flocks to the priest, according to the measure and valuation of the sin, who shall pray for him because he did it unknowingly, and it will be forgiven him,

**5:19** because through error he offended against the Lord."

**5:20** The Lord spoke to Moses, saying:

**5:21** "A soul who sins and, having despised the Lord, denies to his neighbor a deposit that had been committed to his trust, or extorts something by force, or makes a false accusation,

**5:22** or finds a lost item and, denying it, moreover swears falsely, and does any other of the many things in which people customarily sin,

**5:23** when convicted of the offense,

**5:24** he shall return in full everything that he wanted to obtain through fraud, and a fifth part in addition to the owner to whom he had caused the loss.

**5:25** But for his sin he shall offer an unblemished ram from the flock, and give it to the priest, according to the valuation and measure of the offense;

**5:26** who shall pray for him before the Lord, and it will be forgiven him for each of the things that he sinned by doing."

## Translator Notes

- **5:2** — Vulgate quod occisum a bestia est, aut per se mortuum ("what was killed by a beast, or died on its own") differs from the Hebrew list of unclean animal carcasses, and oblita fuerit ("forgets") here and in verses 3-4 renders the Hebrew "it is hidden from him."
- **5:5** — Vulgate agat poenitentiam ("let him do penance") is a distinctive Latin rendering; the Hebrew Masoretic Text and Septuagint speak of confessing the sin.
- **5:15** — Vulgate qui emi potest duobus siclis ("which can be bought for two shekels") specifies a two-shekel price reflecting ancient Jewish legal tradition, whereas the Hebrew mentions a valuation in "shekels of silver" without stating the number two.
- **5:20** — In this versification (matching the Hebrew Masoretic Text), Leviticus 5 continues through 5:26; many modern English Bibles number 5:20-26 as Leviticus 6:1-7.
