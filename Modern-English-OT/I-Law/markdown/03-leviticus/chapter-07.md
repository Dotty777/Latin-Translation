## Text

**7:1** This also is the law of the sacrifice for trespass; it is most holy.

**7:2** Therefore, where the burnt offering will be sacrificed, the victim for trespass will also be slaughtered; its blood will be poured out all around the altar.

**7:3** They will offer from it the tail and the fat that covers the internal organs,

**7:4** the two kidneys, and the fat that is beside the flanks, and the membrane of the liver with the kidneys.

**7:5** And the priest will burn them on the altar; it is a fire offering of the Lord for trespass.

**7:6** Every male of the priestly family will eat this flesh in a holy place, because it is most holy.

**7:7** Just as the sacrifice is offered for sin, so also for trespass: there will be one law for both sacrifices; it will belong to the priest who offers it.

**7:8** The priest who offers the victim of a burnt offering will have its hide.

**7:9** And every sacrifice of fine flour that is baked in an oven, and whatever is prepared on a gridiron or in a frying pan, will belong to the priest by whom it is offered;

**7:10** whether moistened with oil or dry, it will be divided among all the sons of Aaron in equal measure for each one.

**7:11** This is the law of the sacrifice of peace offerings that is offered to the Lord.

**7:12** If the offering is for thanksgiving, they will offer loaves without leaven moistened with oil, and unleavened wafers anointed with oil, and cooked fine flour, and cakes moistened with a mixture of oil,

**7:13** also leavened loaves with the sacrifice of thanksgiving, which is sacrificed for peace offerings,

**7:14** from which one will be offered as firstfruits to the Lord, and it will belong to the priest who will pour out the blood of the sacrifice,

**7:15** whose flesh will be eaten on the same day, and nothing from it will remain until morning.

**7:16** If anyone offers a sacrifice by a vow or voluntarily, it will likewise be eaten on the same day; yet even if anything remains until the next day, it is lawful to eat it;

**7:17** but whatever the third day finds, fire will consume.

**7:18** If anyone eats from the flesh of the victim of peace offerings on the third day, the offering will become void, nor will it benefit the one offering it; on the contrary, whatever soul defiles itself with such food will be guilty of transgression.

**7:19** Flesh that touches anything unclean will not be eaten, but will be burned up with fire; the one who is clean will eat from it.

**7:20** A polluted soul that eats from the flesh of the sacrifice of peace offerings that has been offered to the Lord will perish from its peoples.

**7:21** And the soul that touches the uncleanness of a person, or of a beast, or of any thing that can pollute, and eats from flesh of this kind, will perish from its peoples.

**7:22** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying:

**7:23** "Speak to the sons of Israel, 'You will not eat the fat of a sheep, or of an ox, or of a goat.

**7:24** The fat of a carcass that died of itself, and of an animal that was caught by a wild beast, you will have for various uses.

**7:25** If anyone eats the fat that ought to be offered as a fire offering of the Lord, he will perish from his people.

**7:26** Also, you will not take the blood of any animal as food, whether of birds or of livestock.

**7:27** Every soul that eats blood will perish from its peoples.'"

**7:28** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying:

**7:29** "Speak to the sons of Israel, saying, 'Let the one who offers a victim of peace offerings to the Lord offer at the same time also the sacrifice, that is, its libations.

**7:30** He will hold in his hands the fat of the sacrifice and the breast; and when he has offered and consecrated both to the Lord, he will hand them over to the priest,

**7:31** who will burn the fat on the altar, but the breast will belong to Aaron and his sons.

**7:32** The right shoulder also from the sacrifices of peace offerings will go as the firstfruits of the priest.

**7:33** The one among the sons of Aaron who offers the blood and the fat, he himself will also have the right shoulder as his portion.

**7:34** For I have taken the breast of elevation and the shoulder of separation from the sons of Israel, from their peace sacrifices, and have given them to Aaron the priest and to his sons by a perpetual law, from all the people of Israel.'"

**7:35** This is the anointing of Aaron and of his sons in the ceremonies of the Lord on the day on which Moses presented them so that they might serve in the priesthood,

**7:36** and the things that the Lord commanded to be given to them by the sons of Israel by a perpetual observance in their generations.

**7:37** This is the law of the burnt offering, and of the sacrifice for sin and trespass, and for consecration, and of the victims of peace offerings,

**7:38** which the Lord established for Moses on Mount Sinai, when He commanded the sons of Israel to offer their offerings to the Lord in the desert of Sinai.

## Translator Notes

- **7:10** — Unlike MT (which opens v. 10 as a new sentence beginning "And every grain offering"), the Vulgate links v. 10 directly to v. 9, shifting from plural "conspersa ... arida fuerint" to singular "dividetur."
- **7:20** — Vulgate renders Hebrew "be cut off" as "peribit" ("will perish"; likewise "interibit" in v. 21 and "peribit" in vv. 25, 27) and preserves the plural "de populis suis" ("from its peoples") in vv. 20, 21, and 27, contrasting with singular "de populo suo" ("from his people") in v. 25.
- **7:24** — Vulgate ends v. 24 at "habebitis in varios usus" ("you will have for various uses"), omitting MT's concluding prohibition ("but you shall by no means eat it"), and renders Hebrew "torn" as "captum" ("caught").
- **7:34** — Vulgate distinctly names the priestly portions "pectusculum elevationis" ("breast of elevation") and "armum separationis" ("shoulder of separation", using "armus" = shoulder/forequarter rather than MT "thigh", also in vv. 32-33), and in v. 29 adds the explanatory gloss "id est, libamenta ejus" ("that is, its libations").
- **7:38** — Clementine "mandabit" is a Late Latin b/v spelling for perfect "mandavit" ("commanded"), as shown by the past-sequence subjunctive "ut offerrent." In v. 37, Clementine punctuation connects "sacrificii" directly with "pro peccato atque delicto" rather than setting it off as a separate item in the list.
