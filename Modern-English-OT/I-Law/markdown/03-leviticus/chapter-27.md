## Text

**27:1** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying,

**27:2** "Speak to the sons of Israel, and you will say to them, 'A person who makes a vow and pledges his soul to God will give the price according to an assessment.

**27:3** If it is a male from the twentieth year up to the sixtieth year, he will give fifty shekels of silver by the measure of the sanctuary;

**27:4** if a woman, thirty.

**27:5** And from the fifth year up to the twentieth, a male will give twenty shekels; a female, ten.

**27:6** From one month up to the fifth year, for a male five shekels will be given; for a female, three.

**27:7** At sixty years old and older, a male will give fifteen shekels; a female, ten.

**27:8** If he is poor and is not able to pay the assessment, he will stand before the priest; and as much as the priest assesses and sees that he is able to pay, that much he will give.

**27:9** Now if anyone vows an animal that can be sacrificed to the Lord, it will be holy,

**27:10** and it cannot be exchanged, that is, neither a better one for a bad one, nor a worse one for a good one; but if he does exchange it, both the very one that has been exchanged and the one for which it has been exchanged will be consecrated to the Lord.

**27:11** If anyone vows an unclean animal that cannot be sacrificed to the Lord, it will be brought before the priest,

**27:12** who, judging whether it is good or bad, will set the price.

**27:13** And if the one who offers wishes to pay, he will add a fifth part above the assessment.

**27:14** If a person vows his house and sanctifies it to the Lord, the priest will examine it, whether it is good or bad, and according to the price that has been fixed by him, it will be sold;

**27:15** but if the one who had vowed wishes to redeem it, he will give a fifth part of the assessment in addition, and he will have the house.

**27:16** And if he vows a field of his possession and consecrates it to the Lord, the price will be assessed according to the measure of the seed: if the land is sown with thirty modii of barley, let it be sold for fifty shekels of silver.

**27:17** If he vows the field immediately from the year when the jubilee begins, it will be assessed at as much as it can be worth.

**27:18** But if it is after some time, the priest will calculate the money according to the number of the years that remain until the jubilee, and a deduction will be made from the price.

**27:19** And if the one who had vowed wishes to redeem the field, he will add a fifth part of the assessed money, and he will possess it.

**27:20** However, if he does not wish to redeem it, but it has been sold to any other person, the one who had vowed will no longer be able to redeem it.

**27:21** Because when the day of jubilee comes, it will be sanctified to the Lord, and the consecrated possession belongs by right to the priests.

**27:22** If a field that was bought, and is not from the possession of ancestors, has been sanctified to the Lord,

**27:23** the priest will calculate the price according to the number of years until the jubilee, and the one who had vowed it will pay it to the Lord.

**27:24** And in the jubilee it will return to the former owner, who had sold it and had held it in the allotment of his possession.

**27:25** Every assessment will be weighed by the shekel of the sanctuary. A shekel has twenty obols.

**27:26** No one will be able to sanctify and vow the firstborn, which belong to the Lord; whether it is an ox or a sheep, they are the Lord's.

**27:27** And if the animal is unclean, the one who offered it will redeem it according to your assessment, and will add a fifth part of the price; if he does not wish to redeem it, it will be sold to another for whatever amount it has been assessed by you.

**27:28** Everything that is consecrated to the Lord, whether it is a person, or an animal, or a field, will not be sold, nor can it be redeemed. Whatever has once been consecrated will be a holy of holies to the Lord;

**27:29** and every consecration that is offered by a person will not be redeemed, but will surely die.

**27:30** All the tithes of the land, whether from the crops or from the fruits of the trees, are the Lord's, and are sanctified to Him.

**27:31** And if anyone wishes to redeem his tithes, he will add a fifth part of them.

**27:32** Of all the tithes of ox and sheep and goat that pass under the shepherd's rod, whatever comes tenth will be sanctified to the Lord.

**27:33** Neither a good one nor a bad one will be chosen, nor will it be exchanged for another; if anyone exchanges it, both what has been exchanged and that for which it has been exchanged will be sanctified to the Lord, and will not be redeemed.'"

**27:34** These are the precepts that the Lord commanded Moses for the sons of Israel on Mount Sinai.

## Translator Notes

- **27:13** — The Vulgate reads dare voluerit (literally "wishes to give," i.e., pay the assessed price to keep the animal), whereas the Hebrew text specifies "wishes to redeem it."
- **27:16** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew homer as triginta modiis ("thirty modii," a Roman dry measure of about a peck each) and reads venundetur ("let it be sold," as in v. 14 venundabitur) where the Hebrew refers to the valuation itself.
- **27:25** — The Vulgate uses obolos ("obols," a Greco-Latin weight and coin unit following the Septuagint) corresponding to the Hebrew gerah.
- **27:29** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew term for a thing under the ban (cherem) with consecratio ("consecration"), renders it as quæ offertur ab homine ("that is offered by a person," rather than "devoted from among humans"), and uses the active emphatic idiom morte morietur (literally "will die by death") where the Hebrew has a passive verb ("be put to death").
- **27:32** — The Vulgate specifies bovis et ovis et capræ ("ox and sheep and goat") and adds pastoris ("the shepherd's" rod), where the Hebrew reads more generally "of herd and flock" and simply "under the rod."
