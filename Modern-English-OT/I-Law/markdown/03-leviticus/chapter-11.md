## Text

**11:1** And the Lord spoke to Moses and Aaron, saying,

**11:2** "Speak to the sons of Israel: 'These are the animals that you are to eat from all the living creatures of the earth:

**11:3** everything that has a divided hoof and chews the cud among the beasts, you shall eat.

**11:4** However, whatever chews the cud and has a hoof, yet does not divide it, such as the camel and the rest, you shall not eat it, and you shall count it among the unclean.

**11:5** The rock badger, which chews the cud and does not divide the hoof, is unclean.

**11:6** The hare also, for it too chews the cud, but does not divide the hoof.

**11:7** And the pig, which, although it divides the hoof, does not chew the cud.

**11:8** You shall not eat their flesh, nor shall you touch their carcasses, for they are unclean to you.

**11:9** These are the things that are bred in the waters and that are lawful to eat: everything that has fins and scales, both in the sea and in rivers and ponds, you shall eat.

**11:10** However, whatever does not have fins and scales, among those things that move and live in the waters, will be abominable to you

**11:11** and detestable: you shall not eat their flesh, and you shall avoid their dead bodies.

**11:12** All things that do not have fins and scales in the waters will be defiled.

**11:13** These are the ones among the birds that you are not to eat, and that are to be avoided by you: the eagle, and the griffin, and the sea eagle,

**11:14** and the kite and the vulture according to its kind,

**11:15** and everything of the raven kind in its likeness,

**11:16** the ostrich, and the night owl, and the gull, and the hawk according to its kind;

**11:17** the eagle owl, and the cormorant, and the ibis,

**11:18** and the swan, and the pelican, and the purple gallinule,

**11:19** the heron, and the plover according to its kind, the hoopoe also, and the bat.

**11:20** Everything among winged creatures that walks on four feet will be abominable to you.

**11:21** However, whatever walks on four feet, yet has longer hind legs with which it leaps upon the earth,

**11:22** you are to eat, such as the wingless locust in its kind, and the attacus, and the ophiomachus, and the locust, each according to its kind.

**11:23** However, whatever among winged creatures has only four feet will be detestable to you;

**11:24** and whoever touches their dead bodies will be defiled, and will be unclean until evening;

**11:25** and if it is necessary for him to carry any of these when dead, he shall wash his garments, and he will be unclean until sunset.

**11:26** Every animal that has a hoof, yet does not divide it, nor chews the cud, will be unclean; and whoever touches it will be contaminated.

**11:27** Whatever walks on paws, out of all the living creatures that go on all fours, will be unclean; whoever touches their dead bodies will be defiled until evening.

**11:28** And whoever carries carcasses of this kind shall wash his garments, and he will be unclean until evening; for all these are unclean to you.

**11:29** These also shall be counted among the defiled, of the things that move on the earth: the weasel, and the mouse, and the crocodile, each according to its kind,

**11:30** the shrew, and the chameleon, and the spotted lizard, and the lizard, and the mole.

**11:31** All these are unclean. Whoever touches their dead bodies will be unclean until evening;

**11:32** and anything upon which something from their dead bodies falls will be defiled, both a wooden vessel and a garment, as well as skins and haircloths, and anything with which work is done; they shall be dipped in water, and will be defiled until evening, and thus afterward they will be made clean.

**11:33** However, a clay vessel into which any of these has fallen inside will be defiled, and therefore it must be broken.

**11:34** All food that you eat, if water has been poured upon it, will be unclean; and every liquid that is drunk from any vessel will be unclean.

**11:35** And everything upon which anything from dead bodies of this kind falls will be unclean: whether ovens or footed pots, they shall be destroyed, and will be unclean.

**11:36** However, springs and cisterns, and every gathering of waters, will be clean. Whoever touches a dead body of theirs will be defiled.

**11:37** If it falls upon sowing seed, it will not defile it.

**11:38** But if anyone pours water over the sowing seed, and afterward it is touched by dead bodies, it will immediately be defiled.

**11:39** If an animal dies that is lawful for you to eat, whoever touches its carcass will be unclean until evening;

**11:40** and whoever eats anything from it, or carries it, shall wash his garments, and he will be unclean until evening.

**11:41** Everything that creeps upon the earth will be abominable, and shall not be taken for food.

**11:42** Whatever walks on its breast on four feet, and has many feet, or drags itself along the ground, you shall not eat, for it is abominable.

**11:43** Do not contaminate your souls, nor touch any of them, so that you may not be unclean.

**11:44** For I am the Lord your God: be holy, for I am holy. Do not defile your souls with any creeping creature that moves upon the earth.

**11:45** For I am the Lord, who brought you out of the land of Egypt so that I might be God to you. You shall be holy, for I am holy.

**11:46** This is the law of living creatures and winged creatures, and of every living soul that moves in the water and creeps on the earth,

**11:47** so that you may know the differences between the clean and the unclean, and may know what you are to eat and what to reject.'"

## Translator Notes

- **11:4** — The Vulgate adds "and the rest" (et cetera) after the camel; in 11:5 choerogryllus (from Greek choirogryllios) denotes the rock hyrax.
- **11:13** — In 11:13-19 the Vulgate follows the Septuagint's Greek bird names, yielding several renderings that differ from the Hebrew, including "griffin" (gryphem, understood in antiquity as a giant vulture or bird of prey), "ostrich" (struthionem), "ibis" (ibin), "swan" (cygnum), and "purple gallinule" (porphyrionem).
- **11:22** — The Vulgate borrows three Greek locust-type terms (bruchus, a wingless locust; attacus and ophiomachus, leaping locust species whose exact identities remain uncertain) before locusta ("locust"), in a different sequence from the Hebrew.
- **11:29** — In 11:29-30 the Vulgate again follows the Septuagint's fauna names, rendering crocodilus ("crocodile," used in antiquity for a large land lizard/monitor), mygale ("shrew"), and stellio ("spotted lizard" or gecko); in 11:35, chytropodes (literally "pot-feet") denotes footed clay cooking pots or pot-stands.
- **11:42** — The Vulgate distinctively combines "on its breast on four feet" (super pectus quadrupes graditur) and concludes with "or drags itself along the ground" (sive per humum trahitur), whereas the Hebrew lists belly-crawlers, four-footed walkers, and many-footed creatures separately; in 11:47 the Vulgate uses second-person verbs ("so that you may know..."), keeping 11:46-47 within the Lord's direct speech.
