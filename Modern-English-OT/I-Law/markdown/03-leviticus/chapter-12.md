## Text

**12:1** And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying:

**12:2** "Speak to the sons of Israel, and you will say to them: 'If a woman, after receiving seed, gives birth to a male, she shall be unclean for seven days, according to the days of menstrual separation.

**12:3** And on the eighth day the little infant shall be circumcised;

**12:4** she herself, however, shall remain for thirty-three days in the blood of her purification. She shall not touch anything holy, nor shall she enter into the sanctuary, until the days of her purification are fulfilled.

**12:5** If, however, she gives birth to a female, she shall be unclean for two weeks, according to the custom of menstrual flow, and she shall remain for sixty-six days in the blood of her purification.

**12:6** And when the days of her purification have been completed, for a son or for a daughter, she shall bring a one-year-old lamb for a burnt offering, and a young pigeon or a turtledove for sin, to the entrance of the tabernacle of the testimony, and hand them over to the priest,

**12:7** who shall offer them before the Lord and pray for her, and thus she will be cleansed from the discharge of her blood. This is the law for one who gives birth to a male or a female.

**12:8** But if her hand does not find enough, and she is not able to offer a lamb, she shall take two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one for a burnt offering and the other for sin; and the priest shall pray for her, and thus she will be cleansed.'"

## Translator Notes

- **12:2** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew verb for conceiving (literally 'produces seed') as the passive ablative absolute suscepto semine ('after receiving seed').
- **12:3** — The Vulgate reads simply 'the little infant shall be circumcised' (circumcidetur infantulus), omitting the explicit reference to 'the flesh of his foreskin' found in the Hebrew and Septuagint.
- **12:6** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate renders Hebrew 'tent of meeting' as 'the tabernacle of the testimony' (tabernaculi testimonii). Latin pro peccato literally means 'for sin', corresponding to the Hebrew term for a sin offering.
- **12:7** — Here and in verse 8, the Vulgate translates the Hebrew verb for making atonement as 'pray for her' (orabit pro ea), and renders Hebrew 'the fountain of her blood' as 'the discharge of her blood' (a profluvio sanguinis sui).
- **12:8** — Latin non invenerit manus ejus ('her hand does not find [enough]') preserves a literal Hebraism for lacking the means to provide a lamb, which the Vulgate clarifies with the following clause.
