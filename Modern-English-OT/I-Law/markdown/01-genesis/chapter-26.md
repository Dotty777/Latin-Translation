## Text

**26:1** Now when a famine arose over the land after that barrenness which had happened in the days of Abraham, Isaac went to Abimelech, king of the Philistines, in Gerar.

**26:2** And the Lord appeared to him and said, "Do not go down to Egypt, but rest in the land of which I will tell you,

**26:3** and live as a foreigner in it; and I will be with you and will bless you, for to you and to your seed I will give all these regions, fulfilling the oath that I pledged to Abraham your father.

**26:4** And I will multiply your seed like the stars of heaven, and I will give to your descendants all these regions; and in your seed all the nations of the earth will be blessed,

**26:5** because Abraham obeyed My voice, and kept My precepts and My commandments, and observed the ceremonies and laws."

**26:6** So Isaac remained in Gerar.

**26:7** When he was questioned by the men of that place about his wife, he answered, "She is my sister." For he had been afraid to admit that she was joined to him in marriage, thinking that they might perhaps kill him on account of her beauty.

**26:8** And when very many days had passed and he was staying there, Abimelech, king of the Philistines, looking out through a window, saw him playing with Rebekah his wife.

**26:9** And summoning him, he said, "It is clear that she is your wife; why did you lie that she was your sister?" He answered, "I was afraid that I might die because of her."

**26:10** And Abimelech said, "Why have you deceived us? Someone from the people could have lain with your wife, and you would have brought a great sin upon us." And he commanded all the people, saying,

**26:11** "Whoever touches this man's wife will certainly die."

**26:12** Now Isaac sowed in that land, and found a hundredfold in that same year; and the Lord blessed him.

**26:13** And the man became rich, and he kept going forward and growing until he became exceedingly great;

**26:14** he also had a possession of sheep and herds, and a very large household. Because of these things, the Philistines, envying him,

**26:15** at that time stopped up all the wells that the servants of his father Abraham had dug, filling them with earth,

**26:16** to such an extent that Abimelech himself said to Isaac, "Depart from us, because you have become far more powerful than we."

**26:17** And he, going down, came to the torrent-bed of Gerar and lived there;

**26:18** he dug again other wells, which the servants of his father Abraham had dug, and which, after his death, the Philistines had formerly stopped up; and he named them with the same names by which his father had called them before.

**26:19** They dug in the torrent-bed and found living water.

**26:20** But there also the shepherds of Gerar had a quarrel against the shepherds of Isaac, saying, "The water is ours." For this reason, from what had happened, he called the name of the well False Claim.

**26:21** They dug another one also, and over that one too they disputed, and he named it Hostility.

**26:22** Setting out from there, he dug another well, over which they did not contend; therefore he called its name Spaciousness, saying, "Now the Lord has made room for us and caused us to grow over the land."

**26:23** Now he went up from that place to Beersheba,

**26:24** where the Lord appeared to him on that very night, saying, "I am the Lord of Abraham your father; do not be afraid, because I am with you. I will bless you and multiply your seed for the sake of My servant Abraham."

**26:25** So he built an altar there, and having called upon the name of the Lord, he pitched a tent, and commanded his servants to dig a well.

**26:26** When Abimelech, and Ahuzzath his friend, and Phicol, commander of the soldiers, had come to that place from Gerar,

**26:27** Isaac spoke to them, "Why have you come to me, a man whom you hate and have driven away from you?"

**26:28** They answered, "We have seen that the Lord is with you, and for that reason we have now said, 'Let there be an oath between us, and let us enter into a covenant,

**26:29** that you will not do us any evil, just as we also have touched nothing of yours, nor have we done anything that would harm you, but we sent you away in peace, enriched by the blessing of the Lord.'"

**26:30** So he made a feast for them, and after food and drink,

**26:31** rising in the morning, they swore to one another; and Isaac sent them away peacefully to their own place.

**26:32** Now look, on that very day the servants of Isaac came, reporting to him about the well that they had dug, and saying, "We have found water."

**26:33** Because of this he named it Abundance, and the name Beersheba was given to the city, down to the present day.

**26:34** Now Esau, when he was forty years old, took as wives Judith, the daughter of Beeri the Hittite, and Basemath, the daughter of Elon of the same place,

**26:35** both of whom had offended the spirit of Isaac and Rebekah.

## Translator Notes

- **26:1** — The Vulgate refers to the Philistines as "Phalestinorum" and "Palæstini" in vv. 1, 8, and 14, and as "Philisthiim" in v. 18.
- **26:20** — In vv. 20-22 the Vulgate translates the Hebrew well names Esek, Sitnah, and Rehoboth into Latin as "Calumniam" ("False Claim"), "Inimicitias" ("Hostility"), and "Latitudo" ("Spaciousness").
- **26:24** — The Vulgate reads "Dominus Abraham patris tui" ("the Lord of Abraham your father"), whereas the Hebrew has "the God of Abraham your father."
- **26:29** — Instead of a separate final exclamation ("You are now the blessed of the Lord"), the Vulgate closes the sentence with a participial phrase: "auctum benedictione Domini" ("enriched by the blessing of the Lord").
- **26:33** — The Vulgate translates the well's name as "Abundantiam" ("Abundance"), reading the Hebrew consonants as "sava" (plenty/satiety) rather than "Shibah" (oath/seven).
