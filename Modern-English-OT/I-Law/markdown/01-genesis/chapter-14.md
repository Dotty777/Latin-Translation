## Text

**14:1** Now it happened at that time that Amraphel king of Shinar, Arioch king of Pontus, Chedorlaomer king of the Elamites, and Tidal king of the nations

**14:2** entered into war against Bera king of Sodom, and against Birsha king of Gomorrah, and against Shinab king of Admah, and against Shemeber king of Zeboiim, and against the king of Bela, which is Zoar.

**14:3** All these came together in the Woodland Valley, which is now the Sea of Salt.

**14:4** For they had served Chedorlaomer twelve years, and in the thirteenth year they had withdrawn from him.

**14:5** Therefore, in the fourteenth year, Chedorlaomer and the kings who were with him came, and they struck the Rephaim in Ashteroth-karnaim, and the Zuzim with them, and the Emim in Shaveh-kiriathaim,

**14:6** and the Horites in the mountains of Seir, as far as the Plains of Paran, which is in the wilderness.

**14:7** And they turned back and came to the spring of Mishpat, that is, Kadesh, and they struck all the region of the Amalekites, and the Amorite who lived in Hazazon-tamar.

**14:8** And the king of Sodom, the king of Gomorrah, the king of Admah, the king of Zeboiim, and also the king of Bela, which is Zoar, went out, and they drew up a battle line against them in the Woodland Valley,

**14:9** namely, against Chedorlaomer king of the Elamites, Tidal king of the nations, Amraphel king of Shinar, and Arioch king of Pontus: four kings against five.

**14:10** Now the Woodland Valley had many pits of bitumen. So the king of Sodom and the king of Gomorrah turned in flight and fell there, and those who remained fled to the mountain.

**14:11** And they took all the possessions of Sodom and Gomorrah, and everything that pertains to food, and departed,

**14:12** taking also Lot, the son of Abram's brother, who lived in Sodom, and his possessions.

**14:13** And look, one who had escaped brought word to Abram the Hebrew, who lived in the valley of Mamre the Amorite, the brother of Eshcol and the brother of Aner; for these had made a covenant with Abram.

**14:14** When Abram heard this, namely, that his brother Lot had been captured, he counted his equipped household-born servants, three hundred and eighteen, and pursued them as far as Dan.

**14:15** And having divided his companions, he fell upon them by night, and struck and pursued them as far as Hobah, which is to the left of Damascus.

**14:16** And he brought back all the possessions, and his brother Lot with his possessions, the women also, and the people.

**14:17** Now the king of Sodom went out to meet him after he had returned from the slaughter of Chedorlaomer and of the kings who were with him, in the Valley of Shaveh, which is the King's Valley.

**14:18** But Melchizedek king of Salem, bringing out bread and wine (for he was a priest of the Most High God),

**14:19** blessed him and said, "Blessed be Abram by the Exalted God, who created heaven and earth,

**14:20** and blessed be the Exalted God, under whose protection the enemies are in your hands." And he gave him a tenth of everything.

**14:21** Then the king of Sodom said to Abram, "Give me the souls; take the rest for yourself."

**14:22** He answered him, "I lift my hand to the Lord, the Exalted God, possessor of heaven and earth,

**14:23** that from a thread of the weft to a sandal strap, I will not take anything from all that is yours, so that you may not say, 'I have enriched Abram,'

**14:24** except for what the young men have eaten, and the shares of the men who came with me, Aner, Eshcol, and Mamre; these will take their shares."

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — The Vulgate identifies Arioch's realm as Pontus (Latin Ponti) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has Ellasar, and renders Hebrew Goiim descriptively as "the nations" (Latin gentium).
- **14:3** — The Vulgate translates Hebrew Siddim as "the Woodland Valley" (Latin vallem Silvestrem), understanding the name from a related term for fields or wooded country; the same rendering recurs in verses 8 and 10.
- **14:5** — The Vulgate reads "with them" (Latin cum eis, matching the Septuagint) where the Masoretic Text vocalizes the Hebrew consonants as a place name, "in Ham."
- **14:6** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew El-paran ("terebinth of Paran") as "the Plains of Paran" (Latin Campestria Pharan); similarly, in verse 13 it translates Hebrew "oaks/terebinths of Mamre" as "the valley of Mamre" (Latin convalle Mambre).
- **14:20** — The Vulgate renders this clause "under whose protection the enemies are in your hands" (Latin quo protegente, hostes in manibus tuis sunt), whereas the Hebrew states "who has delivered your enemies into your hand."
