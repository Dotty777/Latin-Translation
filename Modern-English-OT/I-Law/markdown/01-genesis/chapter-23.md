## Text

**23:1** Now Sarah lived for one hundred twenty-seven years.

**23:2** And she died in the city of Arba, which is Hebron, in the land of Canaan; and Abraham came to lament and weep for her.

**23:3** And when he had risen from the funeral rites, he spoke to the sons of Heth, saying,

**23:4** "I am a foreigner and a sojourner among you; give me the right of a burial place with you, so that I may bury my dead."

**23:5** And the sons of Heth answered,

**23:6** "Listen to us, lord: you are a prince of God among us. Bury your dead in our choicest burial places, and no one will be able to prevent you from burying your dead in his tomb."

**23:7** Abraham rose and bowed down to the people of the land, namely, the sons of Heth.

**23:8** And he said to them, "If it pleases your soul that I bury my dead, listen to me and intercede with Ephron the son of Zohar,

**23:9** so that he may give me the double cave which he has at the farthest part of his field; for a fair sum of money let him hand it over to me in your presence as a possession for a burial place."

**23:10** Now Ephron was living in the midst of the sons of Heth. And he answered Abraham in the hearing of all who entered the gate of that city, saying,

**23:11** "By no means let it be so, my lord, but rather listen closely to what I will say. I hand over the field to you, and the cave that is in it, in the presence of the sons of my people; bury your dead."

**23:12** Abraham bowed down before the people of the land.

**23:13** And he spoke to Ephron with the people standing around: "I ask that you listen to me. I will give money for the field; accept it, and so I will bury my dead in it."

**23:14** Ephron answered,

**23:15** "My lord, listen: the land that you ask for is worth four hundred shekels of silver; that is the price between me and you, but how much is this? Bury your dead."

**23:16** When Abraham had heard this, he weighed out the money that Ephron had asked for, in the hearing of the sons of Heth: four hundred shekels of silver of Heth, of approved public currency.

**23:17** And the field that was formerly Ephron's, in which was the double cave, facing Mamre—both the field itself and the cave, and all its trees within all the borders all around—was confirmed

**23:18** to Abraham as a possession, in the sight of the sons of Heth and of all who entered the gate of that city.

**23:19** And so Abraham buried Sarah his wife in the double cave of the field, which faced Mamre. This is Hebron in the land of Canaan.

**23:20** And the field, and the cavern that was in it, were confirmed to Abraham as a possession for a tomb by the sons of Heth.

## Translator Notes

- **23:1** — The Clementine Vulgate ends after Sarah's age, omitting the Hebrew closing clause ('these were the years of the life of Sarah').
- **23:3** — Latin 'ab officio funeris' literally means 'from the duty/rites of the funeral,' an idiomatic Vulgate rendering where the Hebrew has 'from before his dead.'
- **23:9** — Latin 'speluncam duplicem' ('the double cave'; also in vv. 17, 19) renders the Hebrew place name Machpelah etymologically as an adjective rather than transliterating it.
- **23:10** — Latin 'Habitabat' ('was living') takes the underlying verb in its sense of dwelling rather than sitting among the sons of Heth at the gate.
- **23:16** — This Latin text includes 'Heth' after 'argenti' ('silver of Heth') and uses the Roman commercial phrase 'probatæ monetæ publicæ' ('of approved public currency'), where the Hebrew reads 'current with the merchant.'
