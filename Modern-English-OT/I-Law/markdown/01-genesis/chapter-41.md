## Text

**41:1** After two years, Pharaoh saw a dream. He thought he was standing by the river,

**41:2** out of which seven cows were coming up, beautiful and exceedingly fat, and they were grazing in marshy places.

**41:3** Seven others also were emerging from the river, ugly and wasted with emaciation, and they were grazing on the very bank of the river in green places;

**41:4** and they devoured those whose appearance and bodily condition were remarkable. Having woken up, Pharaoh

**41:5** slept again and saw a second dream: seven heads of grain were sprouting on one stalk, full and lovely.

**41:6** Just as many other heads of grain also, thin and struck by blight, were springing up,

**41:7** devouring the beauty of all the former ones. When Pharaoh woke up after rest,

**41:8** and when morning came, terrified with dread, he sent for all the diviners of Egypt and all the wise men; and when they had been summoned, he told them the dream, and there was no one who could interpret it.

**41:9** Then at last, remembering, the master of the cupbearers said, "I confess my sin:

**41:10** when the king was angry with his servants, he ordered me and the master of the bakers to be thrust into the prison of the commander of the soldiers,

**41:11** where on one night each of us saw a dream foretelling things to come.

**41:12** There was a Hebrew youth there, a servant of that same captain of the soldiers; and when we told him the dreams,

**41:13** we heard whatever the outcome of the matter afterward proved; for I was restored to my office, and he was hanged on a cross."

**41:14** Immediately, at the king's command, they brought Joseph out of prison and shaved him; and when his clothing had been changed, they presented him to him.

**41:15** He said to him, "I have seen dreams, and there is no one who can explain them; I have heard that you interpret them most wisely."

**41:16** Joseph answered, "Apart from me, God will give a favorable answer to Pharaoh."

**41:17** Therefore Pharaoh recounted what he had seen: "I thought I was standing on the bank of the river,

**41:18** and seven cows were coming up out of the river, exceedingly beautiful and fat in flesh, which were cropping the greenery in the pasture of the marsh.

**41:19** And look, seven other cows were following these, so deformed and emaciated that I have never seen such ones in the land of Egypt;

**41:20** which, after devouring and consuming the former ones,

**41:21** gave no trace of fullness, but remained sluggish in similar emaciation and squalor. Waking up, and again weighed down by deep sleep,

**41:22** I saw a dream. Seven heads of grain were sprouting on one stalk, full and very beautiful.

**41:23** Seven others also, thin and struck by blight, were springing up from the stalk,

**41:24** which devoured the beauty of the former ones. I told the dream to the diviners, and there is no one who can explain it."

**41:25** Joseph answered, "The king's dream is one: God has shown to Pharaoh what He is about to do.

**41:26** The seven beautiful cows and the seven full heads of grain are seven years of plenty, and they contain the same meaning of the dream.

**41:27** Also the seven thin and emaciated cows that came up after them, and the seven thin heads of grain struck by a scorching wind, are seven years of coming famine.

**41:28** These will be fulfilled in this order:

**41:29** look, seven years of great fertility will come in the entire land of Egypt,

**41:30** and seven other years of such barrenness will follow them that all the former abundance will be handed over to oblivion; for the famine will consume the whole land,

**41:31** and the greatness of the scarcity will destroy the greatness of the plenty.

**41:32** Now as for the fact that you saw a dream a second time pertaining to the same matter, it is a sign of certainty, because the word of God is coming to pass and will be fulfilled very swiftly.

**41:33** Now therefore, let the king look out for a wise and industrious man and set him over the land of Egypt,

**41:34** who shall appoint overseers throughout all the regions, and a fifth part of the produce during the seven years of fertility

**41:35** that are now about to come, let him gather into granaries; and let all the grain be stored under Pharaoh's authority and kept in the cities.

**41:36** And let it be prepared for the coming seven-year famine that will afflict Egypt, and the land will not be consumed by scarcity."

**41:37** The counsel pleased Pharaoh and all his ministers;

**41:38** and he spoke to them, "Will we be able to find such a man, who is full of the spirit of God?"

**41:39** Therefore he said to Joseph, "Since God has shown you all that you have spoken, will I be able to find anyone wiser and like you?

**41:40** You will be over my house, and all the people will obey at the command of your mouth; by the throne of the kingdom alone will I precede you."

**41:41** And Pharaoh said again to Joseph, "Look, I have appointed you over the entire land of Egypt," and

**41:42** took the ring from his hand and put it on his hand; and he clothed him with a robe of fine linen and placed a gold chain around his neck.

**41:43** And he had him mount his second chariot, while a herald cried out that all should bend the knee before him and know that he was set over the entire land of Egypt.

**41:44** The king also said to Joseph, "I am Pharaoh: without your command, no one will move hand or foot in all the land of Egypt."

**41:45** And he changed his name and called him, in the Egyptian language, Savior of the world. He also gave him as wife Asenath, the daughter of Potiphera, priest of Heliopolis. So Joseph, having gone out to the land of Egypt

**41:46** (now he was thirty years old when he stood in the presence of King Pharaoh), went around all the regions of Egypt.

**41:47** And the fertility of the seven years came, and the crops, brought into sheaves, were gathered into the granaries of Egypt.

**41:48** Also all the abundance of produce was stored in each city.

**41:49** And so great was the quantity of wheat that it was equal to the sand of the sea, and the supply exceeded measure.

**41:50** Now two sons were born to Joseph before the famine came, whom Asenath, the daughter of Potiphera, priest of Heliopolis, bore to him.

**41:51** And he called the name of the firstborn Manasseh, saying, "God has made me forget all my labors and my father's house."

**41:52** He also called the name of the second Ephraim, saying, "God has made me grow in the land of my poverty."

**41:53** Therefore, when the seven years of plenty that had been in Egypt had passed,

**41:54** the seven years of scarcity that Joseph had foretold began to come; and in the entire world the famine prevailed, and in the whole land of Egypt there was bread.

**41:55** When that land grew hungry, the people cried out to Pharaoh, asking for food. He answered them, "Go to Joseph, and do whatever he tells you."

**41:56** Now the famine was growing daily in all the land, and Joseph opened all the granaries and kept selling to the Egyptians, for the famine had oppressed them as well.

**41:57** And all the provinces were coming into Egypt to buy provisions and relieve the evil of scarcity.

## Translator Notes

- **41:13** — The Vulgate renders the execution phrase as "in cruce" ("on a cross"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "on a tree."
- **41:43** — The Vulgate interprets the obscure Egyptian acclamation transliterated as "abrech" in the Hebrew text as a command "that all should bend the knee before him" ("ut omnes coram eo genu flecterent").
- **41:45** — Instead of transliterating Joseph's Egyptian name (Hebrew "Zaphenath-paneah"), the Vulgate translates it into Latin as "Salvatorem mundi" ("Savior of the world"). Like the Septuagint, the Vulgate also uses the Greek name "Heliopolis" rather than Hebrew "On" (also in 41:50).
