## Text

**40:1** After these things had thus taken place, it happened that two eunuchs, the cupbearer of the king of Egypt and the baker, sinned against their master.

**40:2** And Pharaoh, being angry against them (for one was over the cupbearers, the other over the bakers),

**40:3** sent them into the prison of the commander of the soldiers, where Joseph also was bound.

**40:4** Now the keeper of the prison handed them over to Joseph, who also served them; some time had passed, and they were held in custody.

**40:5** And both saw a dream in one night, according to an interpretation fitting for them.

**40:6** When Joseph came in to them in the morning and saw that they were sad,

**40:7** he inquired, saying, "Why is your face sadder today than usual?"

**40:8** They answered, "We have seen a dream, and there is no one to interpret it for us." And Joseph said to them, "Does not interpretation belong to God? Tell me what you have seen."

**40:9** First, the overseer of the cupbearers related his dream: "I saw before me a vine,

**40:10** on which there were three shoots, with buds gradually growing, and after the blossoms, grapes ripening,

**40:11** and Pharaoh's cup in my hand. So I took the grapes and pressed them into the cup that I was holding, and handed the cup to Pharaoh."

**40:12** Joseph answered, "This is the interpretation of the dream: the three shoots are three more days,

**40:13** after which Pharaoh will remember your office and will restore you to your former rank; and you will give him the cup according to your duty, just as you did before.

**40:14** Only remember me when it is well with you, and show mercy to me, so that you may speak to Pharaoh and he may bring me out of this prison;

**40:15** for I was carried off by theft from the land of the Hebrews, and here, innocent, I was cast into the pit."

**40:16** Seeing that he had wisely solved the dream, the master of the bakers said, "I too saw a dream: that I had three baskets of flour on my head,

**40:17** and in one basket that was uppermost, I was carrying all the foods that are made by the baker's craft, and birds were eating from it."

**40:18** Joseph answered, "This is the interpretation of the dream: the three baskets are three more days,

**40:19** after which Pharaoh will take away your head and will hang you on a cross, and the birds will tear your flesh."

**40:20** After that, the third day was Pharaoh's birthday; and making a great banquet for his servants, he remembered the master of the cupbearers and the chief of the bakers during the feast.

**40:21** And he restored the one to his place, so that he might hand the cup to the king;

**40:22** the other he hanged on a gallows, so that the truth of the dream interpreter might be proved.

**40:23** Nevertheless, with prosperity following, the overseer of the cupbearers forgot his interpreter.

## Translator Notes

- **40:13** — The Vulgate interprets the Hebrew idiom "lift up your head" as "remember your office" (recordabitur ... magisterii tui). At the end of the verse, the source Latin breaks off at the infinitive "sicut ante facere," where standard Clementine editions include "consueveras" ("as you were accustomed to do before").
- **40:16** — The Vulgate reads "baskets of flour" (canistra farinæ) for the rare Hebrew term hori ("white bread" or "wickerwork"), and states that Joseph "wisely solved" (prudenter ... dissolvisset) the dream.
- **40:19** — The Vulgate distinctively renders the place of execution as "a cross" (in crucem; cf. "a gallows," in patibulo, in 40:22) and uses "tear" (lacerabunt) rather than "eat" for the birds.
- **40:22** — The Vulgate concludes with "so that the truth of the dream interpreter might be proved" (ut conjectoris veritas probaretur), where the Hebrew reads "as Joseph had interpreted to them."
- **40:23** — The Vulgate adds "with prosperity following" (succedentibus prosperis) and refers to Joseph as "his interpreter" (interpretis sui) rather than by name.
