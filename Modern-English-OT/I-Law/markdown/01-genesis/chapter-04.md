## Text

**4:1** Now Adam knew his wife Eve, who conceived and gave birth to Cain, saying, "I have acquired a man through God."

**4:2** And again she gave birth to his brother Abel. Now Abel was a shepherd of sheep, and Cain a farmer.

**4:3** Now it happened after many days that Cain offered gifts to the Lord from the fruits of the earth.

**4:4** Abel also offered from the firstborn of his flock and from their fat; and the Lord showed regard for Abel and for his gifts.

**4:5** For Cain, however, and for his gifts He did not show regard; and Cain grew intensely angry, and his countenance fell.

**4:6** And the Lord said to him, "Why are you angry? And why has your face fallen?

**4:7** If you do well, will you not receive; yet if you do wrong, will sin not immediately be present at the door? But its desire will be under you, and you will rule over it."

**4:8** And Cain said to his brother Abel, "Let us go outside." And while they were in the field, Cain rose up against his brother Abel and killed him.

**4:9** And the Lord said to Cain, "Where is your brother Abel?" He answered, "I do not know. Am I my brother's guardian?"

**4:10** And He said to him, "What have you done? The voice of your brother's blood cries out to Me from the earth.

**4:11** Now therefore, you will be cursed upon the earth, which has opened its mouth and received your brother's blood from your hand.

**4:12** When you work it, it will not give you its fruits; you will be a wanderer and a fugitive upon the earth."

**4:13** And Cain said to the Lord, "My iniquity is too great for me to deserve pardon.

**4:14** Look, You are driving me out today from the face of the earth, and from Your face I will be hidden, and I will be a wanderer and a fugitive on the earth; therefore, everyone who finds me will kill me."

**4:15** And the Lord said to him, "By no means will it be so; rather, everyone who kills Cain will be punished sevenfold." And the Lord set a sign on Cain, so that anyone who found him would not kill him.

**4:16** And Cain went out from the face of the Lord and lived as a fugitive in the land toward the eastern region of Eden.

**4:17** Now Cain knew his wife, who conceived and gave birth to Enoch; and he built a city, and called its name after the name of his son, Enoch.

**4:18** Moreover, Enoch fathered Irad, and Irad fathered Mehujael, and Mehujael fathered Methushael, and Methushael fathered Lamech.

**4:19** He took two wives: the name of the one was Adah, and the name of the other was Zillah.

**4:20** And Adah bore Jabal, who was the father of those living in tents and of shepherds.

**4:21** And his brother's name was Jubal; he himself was the father of those playing on the lyre and the pipe.

**4:22** Zillah also bore Tubal-cain, who was a hammerer and smith in all works of bronze and iron. Now the sister of Tubal-cain was Naamah.

**4:23** And Lamech said to his wives Adah and Zillah, "Hear my voice, wives of Lamech; listen closely to my speech: for I have killed a man for my wound, and a youth for my bruise.

**4:24** Sevenfold vengeance will be given for Cain, but for Lamech seventy times seven."

**4:25** Adam also knew his wife again, and she gave birth to a son and called his name Seth, saying, "God has appointed another seed for me instead of Abel, whom Cain killed."

**4:26** And to Seth also a son was born, whom he called Enosh; this one began to call upon the name of the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **4:7** — The Vulgate renders this difficult verse distinctively: "recipies" ("will you not receive"), "statim ... aderit" ("will immediately be present" rather than "crouches"), and "sub te" ("under you" rather than "toward you").
- **4:8** — The Vulgate includes Cain's words "Egrediamur foras" ("Let us go outside"), a clause absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text (and reading "into the field" in the Septuagint).
- **4:13** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew clause as "quam ut veniam merear" ("too great for me to deserve pardon"), focusing on Cain's inability to merit forgiveness rather than his inability to bear his punishment.
- **4:16** — Rather than treating Hebrew "Nod" as a place name ("the land of Nod"), the Vulgate translates it as "profugus" ("as a fugitive"), directly echoing the curse in 4:12 and 4:14.
- **4:26** — The Vulgate reads the singular subject "iste coepit" ("this one began"), attributing the beginning of calling on the Lord's name specifically to Enosh rather than impersonally to people in general.
