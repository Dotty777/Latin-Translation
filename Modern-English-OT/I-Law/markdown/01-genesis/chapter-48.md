## Text

**48:1** After these things were thus concluded, it was reported to Joseph that his father was ill; and taking his two sons Manasseh and Ephraim, he proceeded to go.

**48:2** And it was told to the old man, "Look, your son Joseph is coming to you." And having been strengthened, he sat up on the bed.

**48:3** And when he came in to him, he said, "God Almighty appeared to me in Luz, which is in the land of Canaan, and blessed me,

**48:4** and said, 'I will increase and multiply you, and make you into companies of peoples; and I will give this land to you and to your offspring after you as an everlasting possession.'

**48:5** Therefore your two sons, who were born to you in the land of Egypt before I came here to you, will be mine; Ephraim and Manasseh, just like Reuben and Simeon, will be reckoned to me.

**48:6** But the remaining ones whom you father after them will be yours, and they will be called by the name of their brothers in their possessions.

**48:7** For to me, when I was coming from Mesopotamia, Rachel died in the land of Canaan on the journey itself, and it was springtime; and I was entering Ephrath, and I buried her beside the road of Ephrath, which by another name is called Bethlehem."

**48:8** Now seeing his sons, he said to him, "Who are these?"

**48:9** He answered, "They are my sons, whom God gave me in this place." "Bring them to me," he said, "so that I may bless them."

**48:10** For the eyes of Israel had grown dim because of extreme old age, and he could not see clearly. And when they were brought near to him, having kissed and embraced them,

**48:11** he said to his son, "I have not been deprived of the sight of you; furthermore, God has shown me your offspring."

**48:12** And when Joseph had taken them from his father's lap, he bowed down prostrate to the ground.

**48:13** And he placed Ephraim at his right hand, that is, at Israel's left, but Manasseh on his left hand, namely at his father's right, and he brought both of them near to him.

**48:14** And stretching out his right hand, he placed it upon the head of Ephraim, the younger brother, and his left upon the head of Manasseh, who was older by birth, crossing his hands.

**48:15** And he blessed his son Joseph, and said, "The God in whose presence my fathers Abraham and Isaac walked, the God who shepherds me from my youth up to the present day,

**48:16** the angel who rescued me from all evils, bless these boys; and let my name be invoked upon them, and also the names of my fathers Abraham and Isaac, and let them grow into a multitude upon the earth."

**48:17** Now Joseph, seeing that his father had placed his right hand upon the head of Ephraim, took it hard; and taking hold of his father's hand, he tried to lift it from the head of Ephraim and move it onto the head of Manasseh.

**48:18** And he said to his father, "It is not fitting this way, father; because this one is the firstborn, place your right hand upon his head."

**48:19** But he, refusing, said, "I know, my son, I know; this one too will indeed become peoples and will be multiplied, but his younger brother will be greater than he, and his offspring will grow into nations."

**48:20** And he blessed them at that very time, saying, "In you Israel will be blessed, and it will be said, 'May God do for you as for Ephraim and as for Manasseh.'" And he set Ephraim before Manasseh.

**48:21** And he said to Joseph his son, "Look, I am dying, and God will be with you and will bring you back to the land of your fathers.

**48:22** I give you one portion beyond your brothers, which I took from the hand of the Amorite with my sword and bow."

## Translator Notes

- **48:7** — The Vulgate distinctly reads "and it was springtime" (eratque vernum tempus), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to "some distance" still remaining to Ephrath; the Latin text also spells Ephrath as Eufratae in its second occurrence.
- **48:11** — The Vulgate reads "I have not been deprived of the sight of you" (Non sum fraudatus aspectui tuo, agreeing with the Septuagint), whereas the Hebrew reads "I did not expect to see your face."
- **48:15** — The Vulgate reads "from my youth" (ab adolescentia mea, agreeing with the Septuagint), whereas the Hebrew reads "all my life."
- **48:22** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew term shekhem ("ridge/shoulder" or "Shechem") as "one portion" (partem unam).
