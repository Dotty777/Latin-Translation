## Text

**45:1** Joseph could no longer restrain himself with many standing by in his presence; therefore he commanded that all go outside, and that no outsider be present at their mutual recognition.

**45:2** And he raised his voice with weeping, which the Egyptians heard, and all the house of Pharaoh.

**45:3** And he said to his brothers, "I am Joseph; is my father still alive?" And the brothers could not answer, terrified with overwhelming fear.

**45:4** To them he said kindly, "Come near to me." And when they had come near, he said, "I am Joseph, your brother, whom you sold into Egypt.

**45:5** Do not be terrified, nor let it seem hard to you that you sold me into these regions; for God sent me before you into Egypt for your preservation.

**45:6** It has been two years since famine began to be in the land, and five years still remain in which it will be possible neither to plow nor to reap.

**45:7** And God sent me ahead so that you may be preserved upon the earth, and may be able to have food to live.

**45:8** I was sent here not by your counsel, but by the will of God, who made me like a father to Pharaoh, and lord of all his house, and ruler in all the land of Egypt.

**45:9** Hurry and go up to my father, and say to him, 'Your son Joseph sends this message: God has made me lord of all the land of Egypt; come down to me, do not delay,

**45:10** and dwell in the land of Goshen; and you will be near me, you and your sons and the sons of your sons, your sheep and herds, and everything that you possess;

**45:11** and there I will feed you (for five years of famine still remain), so that you may not perish, you and your house and everything that you possess.'

**45:12** Look, your eyes, and the eyes of my brother Benjamin, see that my mouth is speaking to you.

**45:13** Report to my father all my glory, and everything that you have seen in Egypt; hurry, and bring him to me."

**45:14** And when he had embraced him and fallen upon the neck of his brother Benjamin, he wept, while he also wept likewise upon his neck.

**45:15** And Joseph kissed all his brothers and wept over each one; afterward they dared to speak to him.

**45:16** And it was heard, and spread abroad by widespread report in the king's court, "Joseph's brothers have come." And Pharaoh rejoiced, and all his household.

**45:17** And he told Joseph to command his brothers, saying, "Loading your pack animals, go into the land of Canaan,

**45:18** and take from there your father and your relatives, and come to me; and I will give you all the good things of Egypt, so that you may eat the marrow of the land.

**45:19** Command also that they take wagons from the land of Egypt for the transport of their little ones and wives, and say, 'Take your father, and hurry, coming as quickly as possible.

**45:20** Do not leave behind anything of your household goods, because all the riches of Egypt will be yours.'"

**45:21** And the sons of Israel did as had been commanded them. Joseph gave them wagons, according to Pharaoh's command, and provisions for the journey.

**45:22** And for each one he ordered two robes to be brought out; but to Benjamin he gave three hundred pieces of silver with five of the finest robes,

**45:23** sending the same amount of money and clothing to his father, adding to them ten donkeys to carry some of all the riches of Egypt, and just as many donkeys carrying wheat and bread for the journey.

**45:24** Therefore he sent his brothers away, and as they set out he said, "Do not be angry on the way."

**45:25** Going up from Egypt, they came into the land of Canaan to their father Jacob.

**45:26** And they reported to him, saying, "Joseph is alive, and he himself rules in all the land of Egypt." Having heard this, as if waking from a heavy sleep, he still did not believe them.

**45:27** They in reply recounted the whole sequence of the matter. And when he had seen the wagons and everything that he had sent, his spirit revived,

**45:28** and he said, "It is enough for me if my son Joseph is still alive; I will go and see him before I die."

## Translator Notes

- **45:1** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew "when Joseph made himself known to his brothers" with the distinctive Latin expression "at their mutual recognition" (agnitioni mutuæ).
- **45:18** — "The marrow of the land" (medullam terræ) literally renders the Vulgate idiom for the finest produce of Egypt, corresponding to Hebrew "the fat of the land."
- **45:20** — The Vulgate commands "Do not leave behind anything of your household goods" (Ne dimittatis quidquam de supellectile vestra), inverting the sense of the Hebrew ("Do not be concerned about your goods," i.e., do not mind leaving them).
- **45:23** — The Vulgate uniquely adds that Joseph sent Jacob "the same amount of money and clothing" (tantumdem pecuniæ et vestimentum) given to Benjamin, and uses masculine "donkeys" (asinos) for both groups of ten rather than specifying female donkeys for the second group as in the Hebrew.
- **45:26** — The Vulgate vividly describes Jacob's stunned reaction as "as if waking from a heavy sleep" (quasi de gravi somno evigilans), whereas the Hebrew reads "his heart grew numb."
