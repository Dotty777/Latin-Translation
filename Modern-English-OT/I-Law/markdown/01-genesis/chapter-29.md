## Text

**29:1** So Jacob set out and came to the eastern land.

**29:2** And he saw a well in a field, and three flocks of sheep lying down beside it; for from it the livestock were watered, and its mouth was closed with a large stone.

**29:3** And it was the custom that when all the sheep were gathered together, they would roll away the stone, and when the flocks were refreshed, they would place it back over the mouth of the well.

**29:4** And he said to the shepherds, "Brothers, where are you from?" And they answered, "From Haran."

**29:5** Questioning them, he said, "Do you know Laban, the son of Nahor?" They said, "We know him."

**29:6** "Is he well?" he said. "He is well," they said, "and look, his daughter Rachel is coming with her flock."

**29:7** And Jacob said, "Much of the day is still left, and it is not time for the flocks to be brought back to the sheepfolds; give drink to the sheep first, and then bring them back to pasture."

**29:8** And they answered, "We cannot until all the livestock are gathered together and we move the stone away from the mouth of the well, so that we may water the flocks."

**29:9** They were still speaking, and look, Rachel was coming with her father's sheep, for she herself was tending the flock.

**29:10** And when Jacob saw her and knew that she was his cousin, and that the sheep were those of Laban, his uncle, he moved away the stone with which the well was closed.

**29:11** And when the flock had been watered, he kissed her, and raising his voice, he wept,

**29:12** and told her that he was her father's brother and the son of Rebekah; and she hurried and reported it to her father.

**29:13** And when he heard that Jacob, his sister's son, had come, he ran to meet him, and embracing him and falling on him with kisses, he led him into his house. And having heard the reasons for the journey,

**29:14** he answered, "You are my bone and my flesh." And after the days of one month were completed,

**29:15** he said to him, "Because you are my brother, will you serve me for nothing? Tell me what wages you should receive."

**29:16** Now he had two daughters: the name of the older one was Leah, and the younger one was called Rachel.

**29:17** But Leah had bleary eyes; Rachel was beautiful in face and lovely in appearance.

**29:18** Loving her, Jacob said, "I will serve you for Rachel, your younger daughter, for seven years."

**29:19** Laban replied, "It is better for me to give her to you than to another man; remain with me."

**29:20** Therefore Jacob served seven years for Rachel, and they seemed to him a few days because of the magnitude of his love.

**29:21** And he said to Laban, "Give me my wife, because the time is already completed, so that I may go in to her."

**29:22** And having called many crowds of friends to a banquet, he held the wedding.

**29:23** And in the evening he led his daughter Leah in to him,

**29:24** giving his daughter a maidservant, Zilpah by name. And when Jacob had gone in to her as was customary, once morning arrived he saw Leah,

**29:25** and he said to his father-in-law, "What is it that you wanted to do? Did I not serve you for Rachel? Why did you deceive me?"

**29:26** Laban answered, "It is not the custom in our place for us to give the younger ones in marriage first.

**29:27** Complete the week of days of this union, and I will give you this one also for the work by which you will serve me for another seven years."

**29:28** He agreed to the terms, and when the week had passed, he took Rachel as his wife,

**29:29** to whom her father had given the servant Bilhah.

**29:30** And at last, having obtained the longed-for marriage, he placed his love for the second one ahead of the first, serving with him for another seven years.

**29:31** Now when the Lord saw that he despised Leah, He opened her womb, while her sister remained barren.

**29:32** And she conceived and bore a son, and she called his name Reuben, saying, "The Lord has seen my humiliation; now my husband will love me."

**29:33** And again she conceived and gave birth to a son, and she said, "Because the Lord has heard that I am held in contempt, He has given this one also to me." And she called his name Simeon.

**29:34** She conceived a third time and bore another, and said, "Now too my husband will be united to me, because I have given birth to three sons for him." And for that reason she gave him the name Levi.

**29:35** A fourth time she conceived and gave birth to a son, and said, "Now I will give praise to the Lord." And because of this she called him Judah, and she stopped giving birth.

## Translator Notes

- **29:1** — The Vulgate condenses the Hebrew idiom ('lifted up his feet and went to the land of the sons of the east') into 'set out and came to the eastern land' (terram orientalem).
- **29:12** — The Vulgate literally translates the broad Hebrew term for kinsman as 'brother' (frater patris eius), though v. 13 identifies Jacob more specifically as Laban's sister's son.
- **29:17** — Vulgate 'lippis erat oculis' ('had bleary eyes') gives a distinctly physical rendering of Hebrew rakkot ('soft' or 'weak').
- **29:22** — Where the Hebrew has 'gathered all the men of the place and made a feast,' the Vulgate reads 'having called many crowds of friends to a banquet, he held the wedding.'
- **29:35** — Vulgate 'confitebor Domino' (lit. 'I will confess/acknowledge the Lord') is the standard biblical Latin expression for offering praise and thanksgiving to God, reflecting the wordplay behind the name Judah.
