## Text

**37:1** Now Jacob lived in the land of Canaan, in which his father sojourned.

**37:2** And these are his generations: Joseph, when he was sixteen years old, was tending the flock with his brothers, still a boy; and he was with the sons of Bilhah and Zilpah, his father's wives, and he accused his brothers before his father of a most wicked crime.

**37:3** Now Israel loved Joseph above all his sons, because he had fathered him in old age; and he made for him a multicolored tunic.

**37:4** Now when his brothers saw that he was loved by his father more than all the sons, they hated him and could not speak anything peaceful to him.

**37:5** It also happened that he reported to his brothers a dream he had seen, and this matter was a seedbed of greater hatred.

**37:6** And he said to them, "Listen to my dream that I saw:

**37:7** I thought that we were binding sheaves in the field, and that my sheaf, as it were, rose up and stood, and that your sheaves, standing around, paid homage to my sheaf."

**37:8** His brothers answered, "Will you really be our king? Or will we be subjected to your dominion?" Therefore this matter of the dreams and words supplied fuel for envy and hatred.

**37:9** He also saw another dream, and recounting it to his brothers, he said, "I saw in a dream, as it were, the sun and the moon and eleven stars paying homage to me."

**37:10** When he had reported this to his father and brothers, his father rebuked him and said, "What does this dream that you saw mean? Will I and your mother and brothers really pay homage to you upon the earth?"

**37:11** Therefore his brothers envied him, but his father silently considered the matter.

**37:12** And when his brothers were staying in Shechem, tending their father's flocks,

**37:13** Israel said to him, "Your brothers are tending the sheep in Shechem; come, I will send you to them." When he answered,

**37:14** "I am ready," he said, "Go and see whether all things are well with your brothers and the livestock, and report back to me what is going on." Having been sent from the valley of Hebron, he came to Shechem,

**37:15** and a man found him wandering in the field and asked what he was looking for.

**37:16** And he answered, "I am looking for my brothers; tell me where they are tending the flocks."

**37:17** And the man said to him, "They have departed from this place, and I heard them saying, 'Let us go to Dothan.'" So Joseph went on after his brothers and found them in Dothan.

**37:18** When they saw him from a distance, before he came near to them, they planned to kill him,

**37:19** and they were speaking to one another, "Look, the dreamer is coming;

**37:20** come, let us kill him and throw him into an old cistern, and let us say, 'A most vicious wild animal has devoured him,' and then it will appear what his dreams profit him."

**37:21** Hearing this, Reuben tried to free him from their hands and said,

**37:22** "Do not take his life, and do not shed blood; rather, throw him into this cistern, which is in the wilderness, and keep your hands innocent." Now he said this, wishing to rescue him from their hands and return him to his father.

**37:23** Therefore, immediately when he reached his brothers, they stripped him of the ankle-length and multicolored tunic,

**37:24** and put him into the cistern, which had no water.

**37:25** And sitting down to eat bread, they saw Ishmaelite travelers coming from Gilead, and their camels carrying spices and resin and stacte into Egypt.

**37:26** Therefore Judah said to his brothers, "What does it profit us if we kill our brother and conceal his blood?

**37:27** It is better that he be sold to the Ishmaelites and that our hands not be defiled, for he is our brother and our flesh." The brothers agreed with his words.

**37:28** And as Midianite merchants were passing by, drawing him out of the cistern, they sold him to the Ishmaelites for twenty pieces of silver, who led him into Egypt.

**37:29** And Reuben, returning to the cistern, did not find the boy,

**37:30** and having torn his clothes, going to his brothers, he said, "The boy is not to be found, and where will I go?"

**37:31** Now they took his tunic and dipped it in the blood of a young goat that they had killed,

**37:32** sending some to carry it to their father and say, "We found this; see whether it is your son's tunic or not."

**37:33** When his father recognized it, he said, "It is my son's tunic; a most vicious wild animal has eaten him, a beast has devoured Joseph."

**37:34** And having torn his clothes, he put on sackcloth, mourning his son for a long time.

**37:35** Now when all his children had gathered together to soothe their father's grief, he refused to accept consolation and said, "I will go down to my son mourning into the underworld." And while he persisted in weeping,

**37:36** the Midianites sold Joseph into Egypt to Potiphar, a eunuch of Pharaoh, commander of the army.

## Translator Notes

- **37:2** — The Vulgate reads sedecim ("sixteen") where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "seventeen," and renders Joseph's report forensically as accusavit crimine pessimo ("accused of a most wicked crime").
- **37:3** — Latin tunicam polymitam literally means a tunic woven with many threads or colors; in 37:23 the Vulgate combines this with talari ("ankle-length"), reflecting both ancient interpretations of the garment.
- **37:20** — The Vulgate specifies cisternam veterem ("an old cistern") where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "one of the pits."
- **37:35** — Latin in infernum (literally "into the lower realm / underworld," traditionally rendered "into hell") translates Hebrew Sheol, the abode of the dead, rather than the place of eternal punishment.
- **37:36** — The Vulgate names the sellers Madianei ("Midianites," matching Mazianitis, a Clementine spelling for Midianite, in 37:28; cf. Hebrew Medanim), and describes Potiphar specifically as eunucho ("eunuch") and magistro militiae ("commander of the army," a Roman military title).
