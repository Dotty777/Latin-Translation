## Text

**3:1** Now the serpent also was more cunning than all the animals of the earth that the Lord God had made. And it said to the woman, "Why has God commanded you not to eat from every tree of paradise?"

**3:2** The woman answered it, "From the fruit of the trees that are in paradise we eat;

**3:3** but from the fruit of the tree that is in the midst of paradise, God commanded us not to eat and not to touch it, so that we might not die."

**3:4** Then the serpent said to the woman, "By no means will you surely die.

**3:5** For God knows that on whatever day you eat from it, your eyes will be opened, and you will be like gods, knowing good and evil."

**3:6** Therefore the woman saw that the tree was good for eating, and beautiful to the eyes, and delightful in appearance; and she took from its fruit and ate, and she gave to her husband, who ate.

**3:7** And their eyes were opened; and when they realized that they were naked, they sewed fig leaves together and made loincloths for themselves.

**3:8** And when they heard the voice of the Lord God walking about in paradise at the afternoon breeze, Adam and his wife hid themselves from the face of the Lord God in the midst of the trees of paradise.

**3:9** And the Lord God called Adam and said to him, "Where are you?"

**3:10** He said, "I heard Your voice in paradise, and I was afraid because I was naked, and I hid myself."

**3:11** He said to him, "Who then informed you that you were naked, unless you have eaten from the tree from which I had commanded you not to eat?"

**3:12** And Adam said, "The woman whom You gave as a companion to me, she gave me from the tree, and I ate."

**3:13** And the Lord God said to the woman, "Why have you done this?" She answered, "The serpent deceived me, and I ate."

**3:14** And the Lord God said to the serpent, "Because you have done this, you are cursed among all the animals and wild beasts of the earth. Upon your chest you will go, and earth you will eat all the days of your life.

**3:15** I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her seed: she will crush your head, and you will lie in wait for her heel."

**3:16** To the woman also He said, "I will multiply your hardships and your conceptions; in pain you will bear children, and you will be under your husband's authority, and he will rule over you."

**3:17** And to Adam He said, "Because you listened to the voice of your wife and ate from the tree from which I had commanded you not to eat, cursed is the earth in your work; in toil you will eat of it all the days of your life.

**3:18** Thorns and thistles it will sprout for you, and you will eat the plants of the earth.

**3:19** In the sweat of your face you will eat bread until you return to the earth from which you were taken; because you are dust, and to dust you will return."

**3:20** And Adam called the name of his wife Eve, because she was the mother of all the living.

**3:21** The Lord God also made for Adam and his wife tunics of skin, and He clothed them.

**3:22** And He said, "Look, Adam has become like one of Us, knowing good and evil; now therefore, so that he might not stretch out his hand and also take from the tree of life, and eat, and live forever"—

**3:23** the Lord God sent him out from the paradise of delight to work the earth from which he was taken.

**3:24** And He drove Adam out, and placed before the paradise of delight the cherubim and a flaming, whirling sword to guard the way of the tree of life.

## Translator Notes

- **3:4** — Latin Nequaquam morte moriemini literally negates the emphatic idiom "die by death" ("By no means will you surely die").
- **3:6** — Latin reads aspectuque delectabile ("delightful in appearance") rather than "desirable to make one wise."
- **3:8** — Latin ligni is grammatically singular ("tree") used collectively for the trees/woods of paradise.
- **3:12** — Latin explicitly adds sociam ("as a companion").
- **3:14** — Latin super pectus tuum literally reads "upon your chest" rather than "on your belly."
- **3:15** — The Vulgate famously reads feminine ipsa conteret ("she will crush") rather than masculine "he/it," and insidiaberis ("you will lie in wait for") for the serpent's action against her heel.
- **3:16** — The Vulgate reads sub viri potestate eris ("you will be under your husband's authority") where the Hebrew reads "your desire shall be for your husband."
- **3:17** — Latin in opere tuo reads "in your work" rather than "because of you."
- **3:20** — Latin spelling Heva is rendered with standard modern English "Eve."
