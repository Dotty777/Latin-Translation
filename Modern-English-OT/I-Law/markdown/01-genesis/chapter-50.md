## Text

**50:1** Seeing this, Joseph fell down on his father's face, weeping and kissing him.

**50:2** And he commanded his servants, the physicians, to embalm his father with spices.

**50:3** While they were fulfilling the orders, forty days passed, for indeed this was the custom for embalmed corpses; and Egypt wept for him seventy days.

**50:4** And when the time of mourning was completed, Joseph spoke to the household of Pharaoh, saying, "If I have found favor in your sight, speak in the ears of Pharaoh,

**50:5** because my father put me under oath, saying, 'Look, I am dying; in my tomb, which I dug for myself in the land of Canaan, you will bury me.' Therefore I will go up, and bury my father, and return."

**50:6** And Pharaoh said to him, "Go up and bury your father, just as you were put under oath."

**50:7** As he went up, there went with him all the elders of the house of Pharaoh and all the elders of the land of Egypt,

**50:8** and the house of Joseph with his brothers, except for the little ones, and the flocks and herds, which they had left behind in the land of Goshen.

**50:9** He also had chariots and horsemen in his retinue, and it became no small crowd.

**50:10** And they came to the Threshing Floor of Atad, which is situated across the Jordan, where they completed seven days, observing the funeral rites with great and intense mourning.

**50:11** When the inhabitants of the land of Canaan saw this, they said, "This is a great mourning for the Hebrews." And for that reason they called the name of that place Mourning of Egypt.

**50:12** Therefore the sons of Jacob did just as he had commanded them;

**50:13** and carrying him into the land of Canaan, they buried him in the double cave, which Abraham had bought with the field as a possession for a tomb from Ephron the Hittite, facing Mamre.

**50:14** And Joseph returned to Egypt with his brothers and the entire retinue, after his father had been buried.

**50:15** When he was dead, his brothers, being afraid and saying to one another, "Perhaps he remembers the wrong that he suffered and may repay us all the evil that we did,"

**50:16** sent word to him: "Your father commanded us before he died,

**50:17** that we should say these things to you in his words: 'I implore you to forget the crime of your brothers, and the sin and malice that they practiced against you.' We also plead that you forgive the servants of the God of your father this iniquity." Upon hearing these things, Joseph wept.

**50:18** And his brothers came to him and, prostrate on the ground, bowed down and said, "We are your servants."

**50:19** He answered them, "Do not be afraid. Can we reject the will of God?

**50:20** You planned evil against me, yet God turned it to good, so that He might exalt me, as you see at present, and save many peoples.

**50:21** Do not be afraid; I will nourish you and your little ones." And he consoled them and spoke kindly and gently.

**50:23** And he dwelt in Egypt with all his father's house, and lived one hundred and ten years. And he saw Ephraim's children to the third generation. The children of Machir, the son of Manasseh, were also born on Joseph's knees.

**50:24** After these things had passed, he spoke to his brothers, "After my death God will visit you and cause you to go up from this land to the land that He swore to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob."

**50:25** And when he had put them under oath and said, "God will visit you; carry my bones away with you from this place,"

**50:26** he died, when one hundred and ten years of his life were completed. And embalmed with spices, he was placed in a coffin in Egypt.

## Translator Notes

- **50:11** — The Latin text here reads Hebraeis ("for the Hebrews") in the Canaanites' exclamation while translating the resulting place name as Planctus Ægypti ("Mourning of Egypt," for Hebrew Abel-mizraim); the Hebrew Masoretic Text, Septuagint, and other Vulgate editions read "for the Egyptians" (Ægyptiis) in the Canaanites' remark as well.
- **50:13** — The Vulgate translates Hebrew "the cave of the field of Machpelah" as spelunca duplici ("the double cave"), following the Septuagint and the etymology of Machpelah as "double."
- **50:19** — The Vulgate distinctly reads num Dei possumus rennuere voluntatem? ("can we reject the will of God?", with rennuere for classical renuere, where other Vulgate editions read resistere, "resist"), whereas the Hebrew text asks "am I in the place of God?"
- **50:20** — The Vulgate explicitly reads ut exaltaret me ("so that He might exalt me") and plural multos populos ("many peoples"), whereas the Hebrew has "to bring about as it is this day, to keep many people alive."
- **50:23** — In this Clementine Vulgate verse numbering, the material numbered as verse 22 in Hebrew and modern English Bibles ("And he dwelt in Egypt... one hundred and ten years") is combined with verse 23.
