## Text

**43:1** Meanwhile the famine was pressing severely upon the whole land.

**43:2** And when the food that they had brought from Egypt had been consumed, Jacob said to his sons, "Return, and buy a little food for us."

**43:3** Judah answered, "That man solemnly warned us under the testimony of an oath, saying, 'You will not see my face unless you bring your youngest brother with you.'

**43:4** Therefore if you are willing to send him with us, we will go together and buy necessities for you;

**43:5** but if you are not willing, we will not go, for the man, as we have often said, solemnly warned us, saying, 'You will not see my face without your youngest brother.'"

**43:6** Israel said to them, "You have done this to my misery, by telling him that you also had another brother."

**43:7** And they answered, "The man questioned us in order about our family: whether our father was alive, whether we had a brother; and we answered him accordingly, in keeping with what he had inquired. Could we possibly know that he was going to say, 'Bring your brother with you'?"

**43:8** Judah also said to his father, "Send the boy with me, so that we may set out and be able to live, so that we and our little ones may not die.

**43:9** I myself take charge of the boy; require him from my hand. Unless I bring him back and hand him over to you, I will be guilty of a sin against you for all time.

**43:10** If delay had not intervened, we would already have come a second time."

**43:11** Therefore their father Israel said to them, "If it must be so, do what you wish: take some of the best fruits of the land in your vessels, and carry gifts down to the man: a little resin, and honey, and storax, and stacte, and terebinth, and almonds.

**43:12** And carry double the money with you, and take back that which you found in the bags, in case perhaps it was done by mistake.

**43:13** Take your brother as well, and go to the man.

**43:14** And may my Almighty God make him favorable toward you, and send back with you your brother whom he holds, and this Benjamin; as for me, I will be as one bereaved, without children."

**43:15** Therefore the men took the gifts, and double the money, and Benjamin; and they went down into Egypt and stood before Joseph.

**43:16** When he had seen them and Benjamin together, he commanded the steward of his house, saying, "Bring the men into the house, and slaughter animals, and prepare a banquet, since they are going to eat with me at noon."

**43:17** He did what had been commanded him, and brought the men into the house.

**43:18** And terrified there, they said to one another, "Because of the money that we carried back earlier in our sacks, we have been brought in, so that he may cast a false accusation down upon us and violently subject both us and our donkeys to servitude."

**43:19** For this reason, approaching the steward of the house at the very doors,

**43:20** they spoke, "We beg you, my lord, to hear us. We already came down before to buy food;

**43:21** and when it had been bought, after we had come to the lodging place, we opened our bags and found the money in the mouth of the sacks, which we are now bringing back in the same weight.

**43:22** And we have also brought other silver so that we may buy what is necessary. It is not within our knowledge who put it in our pouches."

**43:23** And he answered, "Peace be with you; do not be afraid. Your God and the God of your father has given you treasures in your bags, for I myself have the money that you gave me, verified." And he brought Simeon out to them.

**43:24** And when they had been brought into the house, he brought water, and they washed their feet, and he gave fodder to their donkeys.

**43:25** And they were preparing the gifts until Joseph should enter at noon, for they had heard that they were going to eat bread there.

**43:26** Therefore Joseph entered his house, and they presented the gifts to him, holding them in their hands, and paid homage, face down to the ground.

**43:27** And he, having kindly returned their greeting, asked them, saying, "Is your old father well, about whom you had spoken to me? Is he still alive?"

**43:28** And they answered, "Your servant our father is safe and well; he is still alive." And bowing low, they paid homage to him.

**43:29** Now Joseph, lifting up his eyes, saw Benjamin, his brother from the same womb, and said, "Is this your little brother, about whom you had spoken to me?" And again he said, "May God have mercy on you, my son."

**43:30** And he hurried, because his innermost being had been stirred over his brother, and tears were bursting forth; and entering the chamber, he wept.

**43:31** And again, having washed his face, he came out, restrained himself, and said, "Set out the loaves."

**43:32** When these had been set out, separately for Joseph, and separately for the brothers, and also separately for the Egyptians who were eating at the same time (for it is unlawful for Egyptians to eat with Hebrews, and they consider a banquet of this kind profane),

**43:33** they sat before him, the firstborn according to his birthright, and the youngest according to his age. And they marveled exceedingly,

**43:34** having taken the portions that they had received from him; and a greater portion came to Benjamin, so that it exceeded by five portions. And they drank and became drunk with him.

## Translator Notes

- **43:5** — Vulgate includes "ut saepe diximus" ("as we have often said"), an explanatory clause absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **43:11** — Vulgate lists "resinae, et mellis, et storacis, et stactis, et terebinthi, et amygdalarum" ("resin, honey, storax, stacte, terebinth, and almonds"), reflecting Jerome's identifications of several rare Hebrew plant products.
- **43:14** — Vulgate adds "meus" in "Deus autem meus omnipotens" ("my Almighty God") and renders the Hebrew expression of resignation ("if I am bereaved, I am bereaved") as "quasi orbatus absque liberis ero" ("I will be as one bereaved, without children").
- **43:23** — Vulgate "probatam ego habeo" ("I myself have [it] verified") indicates that the steward holds the brothers' payment as tested and approved, and "thesauros" ("treasures") is plural against the Hebrew singular.
- **43:24** — Latin "pauula" is an orthographic variant of "pabula" ("fodder"; compare "pabulum" in 42:27).
