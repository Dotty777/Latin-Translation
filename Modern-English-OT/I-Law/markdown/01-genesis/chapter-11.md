## Text

**11:1** Now the earth had one language and the same words.

**11:2** And as they journeyed from the east, they found a plain in the land of Shinar and lived in it.

**11:3** And one said to his neighbor, "Come, let us make bricks and bake them with fire." And they had bricks for stones, and bitumen for mortar.

**11:4** And they said, "Come, let us make for ourselves a city and a tower whose top may reach to heaven, and let us make our name renowned before we are divided across all lands."

**11:5** Now the Lord came down to see the city and the tower that the sons of Adam were building.

**11:6** And He said, "Look, there is one people, and one language for all; and they have begun to do this, nor will they desist from their plans until they complete them in deed.

**11:7** Come therefore, let Us go down, and let Us confuse their tongues there, so that no one may understand the voice of his neighbor."

**11:8** And so the Lord divided them from that place across all lands, and they stopped building the city.

**11:9** And therefore its name was called Babel, because there the language of all the earth was confused, and from there the Lord dispersed them over the face of all regions.

**11:10** These are the generations of Shem: Shem was one hundred years old when he fathered Arpachshad, two years after the flood.

**11:11** And Shem lived five hundred years after he fathered Arpachshad, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**11:12** Furthermore, Arpachshad lived thirty-five years and fathered Shelah.

**11:13** And Arpachshad lived three hundred three years after he fathered Shelah, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**11:14** Shelah also lived thirty years and fathered Eber.

**11:15** And Shelah lived four hundred three years after he fathered Eber, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**11:16** Now Eber lived thirty-four years and fathered Peleg.

**11:17** And Eber lived four hundred thirty years after he fathered Peleg, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**11:18** Peleg also lived thirty years and fathered Reu.

**11:19** And Peleg lived two hundred nine years after he fathered Reu, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**11:20** Now Reu lived thirty-two years and fathered Serug.

**11:21** And Reu lived two hundred seven years after he fathered Serug, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**11:22** And Serug lived thirty years and fathered Nahor.

**11:23** And Serug lived two hundred years after he fathered Nahor, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**11:24** Now Nahor lived twenty-nine years and fathered Terah.

**11:25** And Nahor lived one hundred nineteen years after he fathered Terah, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**11:26** And Terah lived seventy years and fathered Abram, Nahor, and Haran.

**11:27** Now these are the generations of Terah: Terah fathered Abram, Nahor, and Haran. Moreover, Haran fathered Lot.

**11:28** And Haran died before Terah his father, in the land of his birth, in Ur of the Chaldeans.

**11:29** Now Abram and Nahor took wives: the name of Abram's wife was Sarai, and the name of Nahor's wife was Milcah, the daughter of Haran, the father of Milcah and the father of Iscah.

**11:30** Now Sarai was barren and had no children.

**11:31** So Terah took Abram his son, and Lot the son of Haran, the son of his son, and Sarai his daughter-in-law, the wife of Abram his son, and led them out from Ur of the Chaldeans to go into the land of Canaan; and they came as far as Haran and lived there.

**11:32** And the days of Terah were two hundred five years, and he died in Haran.

## Translator Notes

- **11:4** — Latin celebremus nomen nostrum ("let us make our name renowned") differs in wording from the Hebrew "let us make a name for ourselves."
- **11:5** — Latin filii Adam preserves "the sons of Adam" literally, whereas the Hebrew phrase bene ha-adam is commonly rendered "the children of men" or "humankind."
- **11:6** — The Vulgate's nec desistent a cogitationibus suis, donec eas opere compleant ("nor will they desist from their plans until they complete them in deed") is a distinctly Latin rendering compared with the Hebrew "nothing that they propose to do will now be impossible for them."
- **11:13** — The Clementine Vulgate reads trecentis tribus annis (303 years) here, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 403 years.
