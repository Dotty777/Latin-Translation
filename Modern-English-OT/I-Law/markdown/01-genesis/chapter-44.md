## Text

**44:1** Now Joseph commanded the steward of his house, saying, "Fill their sacks with grain, as much as they can hold, and put each one's money at the top of the sack.

**44:2** And put my silver cup, and the price that he gave for the wheat, in the mouth of the youngest one's sack." And it was done so.

**44:3** And when morning dawned, they were sent away with their donkeys.

**44:4** And they had already gone out of the city and had gone a little way ahead; then Joseph, having summoned the steward of the house, said, "Rise, pursue the men, and when you overtake them, say, 'Why have you repaid evil for good?

**44:5** The cup that you stole is the very one from which my lord drinks, and in which he is accustomed to practice divination; you have done a most evil thing.'"

**44:6** He did as he had commanded, and when he overtook them, he spoke to them in order.

**44:7** They answered, "Why does our lord speak in this way, that your servants should have committed so great a crime?

**44:8** The money that we found at the top of the sacks we brought back to you from the land of Canaan; and how does it follow that we should have stolen gold or silver from your lord's house?

**44:9** With whichever of your servants what you seek is found, let him die, and we will be servants of our lord."

**44:10** He said, "Let it be according to your judgment: the one with whom the cup is found, let him be my servant, but you will be innocent."

**44:11** Therefore, hastily lowering their sacks to the ground, each one opened his.

**44:12** Having searched them, beginning from the oldest down to the youngest, he found the cup in Benjamin's sack.

**44:13** Then they, having torn their garments and loaded their donkeys again, returned into the town.

**44:14** And Judah first, with his brothers, went in to Joseph (for they had not yet departed from the place), and all of them fell down together on the ground before him.

**44:15** He said to them, "Why did you wish to act in this way? Or are you unaware that there is no one like me in the knowledge of divination?"

**44:16** Judah said to him, "What shall we answer my lord? Or what shall we speak, or what just thing will we be able to put forward? God has found the iniquity of your servants; look, we are all servants of my lord, both we and the one with whom the cup was found."

**44:17** Joseph answered, "Far be it from me to act in this way: the one who stole the cup, let him be my servant; but you, go away free to your father."

**44:18** Drawing nearer, Judah said confidently, "I beg you, my lord, let your servant speak a word in your ears, and do not be angry with your servant; for you are, after Pharaoh,

**44:19** my lord. You asked your servants earlier, 'Do you have a father or a brother?'

**44:20** And we answered you, my lord, 'We have an old father, and a little boy who was born in his old age, whose brother from the same mother is dead; and his mother has him alone, while his father loves him tenderly.'

**44:21** And you said to your servants, 'Bring him to me, and I will set my eyes upon him.'

**44:22** We pointed out to my lord, 'The boy cannot leave his father, for if he leaves him, he will die.'

**44:23** And you said to your servants, 'Unless your youngest brother comes with you, you will not see my face again.'

**44:24** Therefore, when we had gone up to your servant our father, we recounted to him everything that our lord spoke.

**44:25** And our father said, 'Go back and buy us a little wheat.'

**44:26** We said to him, 'We cannot go; if our youngest brother goes down with us, we will set out together; otherwise, with him absent, we do not dare to see the man's face.'

**44:27** And he answered, 'You know that my wife bore two to me.

**44:28** One went out, and you said, "A beast has devoured him," and until now he has not appeared.

**44:29** If you take this one also, and anything happens to him on the way, you will bring down my gray hairs with sorrow to the underworld.'

**44:30** Therefore, if I go in to your servant our father, and the boy is missing (since his soul depends on this one's soul),

**44:31** and he sees that he is not with us, he will die, and your servants will bring down his gray hairs with grief to the underworld.

**44:32** I personally am your servant who received this one into my trust and gave my pledge, saying, 'Unless I bring him back, I will be guilty of sin against my father for all time.'

**44:33** Therefore I, your servant, will remain in place of the boy in the service of my lord, and let the boy go up with his brothers.

**44:34** For I cannot return to my father with the boy absent, so that I may not be present as a witness of the calamity that is about to overwhelm my father."

## Translator Notes

- **44:5** — The Vulgate explicitly adds "that you stole" (quem furati estis) to the steward's accusation, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads more generally "Is not this the one from which my lord drinks?"
- **44:14** — The Vulgate uniquely specifies that Judah entered "first" (Primusque Judas) and uses the plural "they had not yet departed" (abierant), referring to Joseph and his household, where the Hebrew has the singular "he was still there."
- **44:18** — The Clementine Vulgate links "my lord" (dominus meus) at the start of 44:19 to the end of 44:18 ("after Pharaoh, my lord"), followed by second-person "You asked" (Interrogasti), whereas the Hebrew starts 44:19 with "My lord asked his servants."
- **44:20** — The Vulgate reads "his mother has him alone" (ipsum solum habet mater sua), translated strictly from the Latin rather than harmonized to the Hebrew "he alone is left of his mother."
- **44:28** — The Vulgate uses the second person plural "and you said" (et dixistis), attributing the remark about the beast directly to the brothers rather than to Jacob himself as in the Hebrew ("and I said").
