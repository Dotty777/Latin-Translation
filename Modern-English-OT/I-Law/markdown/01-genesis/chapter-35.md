## Text

**35:1** Meanwhile God spoke to Jacob: "Rise, and go up to Bethel, and live there, and make an altar to God, who appeared to you when you were fleeing from your brother Esau."

**35:2** Then Jacob, having called together all his household, said, "Cast away the foreign gods that are in your midst, and purify yourselves, and change your garments.

**35:3** Rise, and let us go up to Bethel, so that we may make an altar there to God, who heard me in the day of my distress and was the companion of my journey."

**35:4** So they gave him all the foreign gods that they had and the earrings that were in their ears; and he buried them beneath the terebinth that is behind the city of Shechem.

**35:5** And when they had set out, the terror of God fell upon everyone all around the city, and they did not dare to pursue them as they departed.

**35:6** So Jacob came to Luz, which is in the land of Canaan, called by the name Bethel, he himself and all the people with him.

**35:7** And he built an altar there, and called the name of the place House of God, for there God appeared to him when he was fleeing from his brother.

**35:8** At the same time Deborah, the nurse of Rebekah, died, and she was buried at the foot of Bethel beneath an oak; and the name of the place was called Oak of Weeping.

**35:9** Now God appeared again to Jacob after he returned from Mesopotamia of Syria, and blessed him,

**35:10** saying, "You will no longer be called Jacob, but Israel will be your name." And He called him Israel,

**35:11** and said to him, "I am God Almighty: increase and multiply; nations and peoples of nations will come from you; kings will come forth from your loins,

**35:12** and the land that I gave to Abraham and Isaac, I will give to you and to your offspring after you."

**35:13** And He departed from him.

**35:14** Then he set up a stone pillar in the place where God had spoken to him, offering libations upon it and pouring out oil,

**35:15** and calling the name of the place Bethel.

**35:16** Having gone out from there, he came in the springtime to the land that leads to Ephrath, in which, while Rachel was in labor—Having gone out from there, he pitched his tent beyond the Tower of the Flock.

**35:17** Because of the difficulty of childbirth, she began to be in danger. And the midwife said to her, "Do not be afraid, for you will have this son also."

**35:18** Now as her soul was departing because of the pain, and death was already imminent, she called the name of her son Ben-oni, that is, Son of my pain; but the father called him Benjamin, that is, Son of the right hand.

**35:19** So Rachel died and was buried on the road that leads to Ephrath; this is Bethlehem.

**35:20** And Jacob set up a pillar over her tomb; this is the pillar of Rachel's monument to the present day.

**35:22** And while he was living in that region, Reuben went and slept with Bilhah, his father's concubine; and this was not at all hidden from him. Now the sons of Jacob were twelve.

**35:23** The sons of Leah: the firstborn Reuben, and Simeon, and Levi, and Judah, and Issachar, and Zebulun.

**35:24** The sons of Rachel: Joseph and Benjamin.

**35:25** The sons of Bilhah, the maidservant of Rachel: Dan and Naphtali.

**35:26** The sons of Zilpah, the maidservant of Leah: Gad and Asher. These are the sons of Jacob who were born to him in Mesopotamia of Syria.

**35:27** He also came to his father Isaac at Mamre, the city of Arba—this is Hebron—in which Abraham and Isaac sojourned.

**35:28** And the days of Isaac were completed: one hundred eighty years.

**35:29** And worn out by age, he died, and was joined to his people, old and full of days; and his sons Esau and Jacob buried him.

## Translator Notes

- **35:5** — The Latin text reads the genitive singular 'civitatis' ('everyone all around the city,' referring to Shechem), whereas the Hebrew speaks of plural 'cities' around them.
- **35:7** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew place name El-bethel as 'House of God' (Domus Dei), and likewise translates Allon-bacuth in v. 8 as 'Oak of Weeping' (Quercus fletus) and Paddan-aram in vv. 9 and 26 as 'Mesopotamia of Syria' (Mesopotamiam Syriae).
- **35:16** — Latin 'verno tempore' ('in the springtime') is a distinctive Vulgate rendering of the Hebrew phrase for a short distance of travel. In this Latin critical text, the clause corresponding to traditional v. 21 ('Having gone out from there, he pitched his tent beyond the Tower of the Flock,' translating Migdal-eder) stands at the end of v. 16 (with v. 21 omitted), while v. 17 resumes the narrative of Rachel's labor.
- **35:18** — The Vulgate supplies explicit etymological glosses for both names—'that is, Son of my pain' (Filius doloris mei) and 'that is, Son of the right hand' (Filius dexterae)—which do not appear in the Hebrew text.
- **35:29** — Latin 'Consumptusque aetate' ('worn out by age') and 'appositus' ('joined' or 'placed beside') are distinctive Vulgate renderings of the Hebrew expressions 'breathed his last' and 'was gathered.'
