## Text

**27:1** Now Isaac grew old, and his eyes grew dim, and he could not see. And he called Esau, his older son, and said to him, "My son?" And he answered, "Here I am."

**27:2** His father said to him, "You see that I have grown old, and I do not know the day of my death.

**27:3** Take your weapons, quiver and bow, and go outside; and when you have caught something by hunting,

**27:4** make me a savory dish from it, just as you know I desire, and bring it so that I may eat, and my soul may bless you before I die."

**27:5** When Rebekah had heard this, and he had gone off into the field to fulfill his father's command,

**27:6** she said to her son Jacob, "I heard your father speaking with your brother Esau and saying to him,

**27:7** 'Bring me your game and make food so that I may eat and bless you before the Lord before I die.'

**27:8** Now therefore, my son, yield to my counsel:

**27:9** and going to the flock, bring me two of the finest young goats, so that I may make dishes from them for your father, which he gladly eats,

**27:10** and when you have brought them in and he has eaten, he may bless you before he dies."

**27:11** He answered her, "You know that my brother Esau is a hairy man, and I am smooth;

**27:12** if my father touches me and notices, I am afraid that he will think I wanted to mock him, and he will bring a curse upon me instead of a blessing."

**27:13** To him his mother said, "Let that curse be upon me, my son; only listen to my voice, and go and bring what I said."

**27:14** He went off and brought them, and gave them to his mother. She prepared the food, just as she knew his father desired.

**27:15** And she dressed him in the very fine garments of Esau, which she had with her at home,

**27:16** and she wrapped the skins of the young goats around his hands, and covered the bare parts of his neck;

**27:17** she gave him the savory dish, and handed over the loaves of bread that she had baked.

**27:18** After bringing them in, he said, "My father?" And he answered, "I am listening. Who are you, my son?"

**27:19** And Jacob said, "I am Esau, your firstborn. I have done just as you commanded me: rise, sit up, and eat from my game, so that your soul may bless me."

**27:20** Again Isaac said to his son, "How were you able to find it so quickly, my son?" And he answered, "It was the will of God that what I wanted should quickly come before me."

**27:21** And Isaac said, "Come closer here, so that I may touch you, my son, and test whether you are my son Esau or not."

**27:22** He approached his father, and after feeling him, Isaac said, "The voice is indeed Jacob's voice, yet the hands are Esau's hands."

**27:23** And he did not recognize him, because the hairy hands had reproduced the likeness of the older son. Blessing him, therefore,

**27:24** he said, "Are you my son Esau?" He answered, "I am."

**27:25** And he said, "Bring me food from your game, my son, so that my soul may bless you." When it was brought and he had eaten it, he also brought him wine. After drinking it,

**27:26** he said to him, "Come close to me, and give me a kiss, my son."

**27:27** He approached and kissed him. And as soon as he smelled the fragrance of his garments, blessing him, he said, "See, the scent of my son is like the scent of a field that the Lord has blessed.

**27:28** May the Lord give you from the dew of heaven and from the richness of the earth an abundance of grain and wine.

**27:29** And may peoples serve you, and may tribes pay homage to you; be lord over your brothers, and may the sons of your mother bow down before you. Whoever curses you, let him be cursed, and whoever blesses you, let him be filled with blessings."

**27:30** Isaac had scarcely finished his speech, and when Jacob had gone outside, Esau came,

**27:31** and brought in to his father food cooked from his game, saying, "Rise, my father, and eat from your son's game, and may your soul bless me."

**27:32** And Isaac said to him, "Who then are you?" And he answered, "I am your firstborn son, Esau."

**27:33** Isaac trembled with violent astonishment, and marveling beyond what can be believed, he said, "Who then is he who just now brought me caught game, and I ate from everything before you came, and I blessed him, and he will be blessed?"

**27:34** When Esau heard his father's words, he roared out with a great cry, and distraught, he said, "Bless me also, my father."

**27:35** And he said, "Your brother came deceitfully and took your blessing."

**27:36** And he added, "Rightly has his name been called Jacob, for look, he has supplanted me a second time: previously he took away my birthright, and now a second time he has stolen my blessing." And again to his father he said, "Have you not reserved a blessing for me too?"

**27:37** Isaac answered, "I have appointed him as your lord, and all his brothers I have subjected to his service; with grain and wine I have established him. After these things, my son, what further can I do for you?"

**27:38** To him Esau said, "Do you have only one blessing, father? I beg you to bless me as well." And as he wept with a loud wailing,

**27:39** being moved, Isaac said to him, "In the richness of the earth and in the dew of heaven from above,

**27:40** your blessing will be. You will live by the sword, and you will serve your brother; and a time will come when you will shake off and loosen his yoke from your neck."

**27:41** Therefore Esau always hated Jacob because of the blessing with which his father had blessed him; and he said in his heart, "May the days of mourning for my father come, so that I may kill my brother Jacob."

**27:42** These things were reported to Rebekah; and sending and calling her son Jacob, she said to him, "Look, your brother Esau is threatening to kill you.

**27:43** Now therefore, son, listen to my voice, and rising up, flee to my brother Laban in Haran;

**27:44** and you will dwell with him a few days, until your brother's fury subsides,

**27:45** and his indignation ceases, and he forgets the things that you did against him. Afterward I will send and bring you from there to here. Why should I be bereaved of both sons in one day?"

**27:46** Rebekah also said to Isaac, "I am weary of my life because of the daughters of Heth; if Jacob takes a wife from the stock of this land, I do not want to live."

## Translator Notes

- **27:20** — Vulgate reads 'It was the will of God that what I wanted should quickly come before me' (Voluntatis Dei fuit...), without specifying 'the Lord your God' as in the Hebrew text.
- **27:28** — Clementine Vulgate reads Dominus ('the Lord'), whereas the Hebrew text and Septuagint read 'God.'
- **27:29** — Vulgate renders the final clause as 'let him be filled with blessings' (benedictionibus repleatur), expanding on the simpler Hebrew 'be blessed.'
- **27:39** — Vulgate adds 'being moved' (motus) and reads positively 'In the richness... and in the dew... your blessing will be' (benedictio tua in v. 40), whereas the Hebrew has 'your dwelling' and is often construed privatively ('away from').
- **27:41** — Vulgate casts Esau's inward speech as a subjunctive wish ('May the days... come, so that I may kill'), whereas the Hebrew states indicatively that the days of mourning are drawing near.
