## Text

**39:1** Therefore Joseph was led into Egypt, and Potiphar, a eunuch of Pharaoh, commander of the army, an Egyptian man, bought him from the hand of the Ishmaelites by whom he had been brought.

**39:2** And the Lord was with him, and he was a man succeeding in all things; and he lived in the house of his master,

**39:3** who knew very well that the Lord was with him, and that all things that he carried out were directed by Him in his hand.

**39:4** And Joseph found favor before his master and served him; having been placed over all things by him, he governed the house entrusted to him and everything that had been handed over to him.

**39:5** And the Lord blessed the house of the Egyptian because of Joseph, and He multiplied all his possessions, both in the house and in the fields;

**39:6** nor did he know anything else except the bread that he ate. Now Joseph was handsome in face and pleasing in appearance.

**39:7** Now after many days, his mistress cast her eyes on Joseph and said, "Sleep with me."

**39:8** He, by no means consenting to the wicked deed, said to her, "Look, my master, with all things handed over to me, does not know what he has in his house,

**39:9** nor is there anything that is not in my power or that he has not handed over to me, except you, who are his wife. How then can I do this evil and sin against my God?"

**39:10** With words of this kind, day by day, the woman was troublesome to the young man, yet he refused defilement.

**39:11** Now it happened that on a certain day Joseph entered the house and was doing some work without witnesses,

**39:12** and she, having seized the hem of his garment, said, "Sleep with me." Leaving his cloak in her hand, he fled and went outside.

**39:13** And when the woman saw the garment in her hands and that she had been scorned,

**39:14** she called to herself the men of her house and said to them, "Look, he has brought in a Hebrew man to mock us; he came in to me to lie with me, and when I cried out

**39:15** and he heard my voice, he left the cloak that I was holding and fled outside."

**39:16** Therefore, as proof of credibility, she showed the cloak she had kept to her husband returning home,

**39:17** and said, "The Hebrew slave whom you brought came in to me to mock me;

**39:18** and when he heard me crying out, he left the cloak that I was holding and fled outside."

**39:19** Having heard these things, and being overly credulous of the words of his wife, the master became very angry;

**39:20** and he handed Joseph over into the prison where the prisoners of the king were guarded, and he was confined there.

**39:21** Now the Lord was with Joseph, and having had mercy on him, He gave him favor in the sight of the chief of the prison.

**39:22** He handed over into his hand all the prisoners who were held in custody, and whatever was done was under him.

**39:23** Nor did he know anything, with all things entrusted to him; for the Lord was with him, and He directed all his works.

## Translator Notes

- **39:1** — The Vulgate renders Potiphar's titles as "eunuch" (eunuchus, matching the Septuagint against Hebrew saris, "official") and "commander of the army" (princeps exercitus, where Hebrew reads "captain of the guard").
- **39:7** — The Vulgate reads "after many days" (Post multos itaque dies), whereas the Hebrew text reads "after these things."
- **39:13** — The Vulgate distinctively specifies that Potiphar's wife saw "that she had been scorned" (et se esse contemptam), where the Hebrew states that she saw Joseph had left his garment and fled outside.
- **39:19** — The Vulgate adds the explanatory detail "overly credulous of the words of his wife" (nimium credulus verbis conjugis), a distinctly Vulgate expansion absent from the Hebrew and Septuagint.
