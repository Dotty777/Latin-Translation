## Text

**1:1** The word of the Lord that came to Joel, son of Pethuel.

**1:2** Hear this, elders, and take it in with your ears, all inhabitants of the land: has this happened in your days, or in the days of your fathers?

**1:3** Tell of this to your children, and let your children tell it to their children, and their children to another generation.

**1:4** What remained from the caterpillar, the locust has eaten; and what remained from the locust, the wingless locust has eaten; and what remained from the wingless locust, the blight has eaten.

**1:5** Wake up, drunkards, and weep and wail, all you who drink wine in sweetness, because it has perished from your mouth.

**1:6** For a nation has come up against My land, mighty and innumerable; its teeth are like the teeth of a lion, and its molars like those of a lion cub.

**1:7** It has turned My vine into a wasteland and peeled the bark from My fig tree; laying it bare, it has stripped it and flung it aside; its branches have become white.

**1:8** Lament like a virgin wrapped in sackcloth over the husband of her youth.

**1:9** Sacrifice and drink offering have perished from the house of the Lord; the priests, the ministers of the Lord, have mourned.

**1:10** The countryside has been ravaged, the ground has mourned, because the wheat has been devastated, the wine has been put to shame, the oil has languished.

**1:11** The farmers have been put to shame, the vinedressers have wailed over the grain and barley, because the harvest of the field has perished.

**1:12** The vine has been put to shame, and the fig tree has languished; the pomegranate, and the palm, and the apple tree, and all the trees of the field have dried up, because joy has been put to shame away from the sons of men.

**1:13** Gird yourselves and lament, priests; wail, ministers of the altar; go in, lie down in sackcloth, ministers of my God, since sacrifice and drink offering have vanished from the house of your God.

**1:14** Sanctify a fast, call an assembly, gather the elders, all the inhabitants of the land, into the house of your God, and cry out to the Lord.

**1:15** Alas, alas, alas for the day! For the day of the Lord is near, and it will come like devastation from the Mighty One.

**1:16** Have not provisions perished before your eyes from the house of our God, gladness and exultation?

**1:17** The beasts of burden have rotted in their dung; the granaries have been demolished, the storehouses have been broken down, because the wheat has been put to shame.

**1:18** Why did the beast groan, and the herds of cattle bellow? Because there is no pasture for them; and even the flocks of sheep have utterly perished.

**1:19** To You, Lord, I will cry out, because fire has consumed the lovely places of the wilderness, and flame has set ablaze all the trees of the countryside.

**1:20** Even the wild beasts of the field, like a plot of ground thirsting for rain, have looked up to You, because the springs of water have dried up, and fire has devoured the lovely places of the wilderness.

## Translator Notes

- **1:4** — Latin lists four crop destroyers: eruca ("caterpillar"), locusta ("locust"), bruchus ("wingless locust"), and rubigo ("blight" or mildew/rust, following the Septuagint rather than a fourth insect species as in the Hebrew).
- **1:10** — Latin confusum est literally means "has been put to shame" (or "confounded"), reflecting a reading of the Hebrew verb as "be ashamed" rather than "dry up." This Vulgate motif recurs in 1:11, 1:12, and 1:17 and is reversed in 2:26-27.
- **1:11** — The Vulgate renders these verbs as declarative perfects ("have been put to shame... have wailed") rather than imperatives as in the Hebrew text.
- **1:17** — Latin Computruerunt jumenta in stercore suo ("The beasts of burden have rotted in their dung") is a distinctively Vulgate rendering of a difficult Hebrew line usually understood as seeds shriveling beneath clods.
- **1:20** — The simile quasi area sitiens imbrem ("like a plot of ground thirsting for rain") is a Vulgate expansion rendering the Hebrew verb for panting or longing; likewise speciosa deserti ("lovely places of the wilderness," also in 1:19 and 2:22) reads the Hebrew word for "pastures" from the root meaning "lovely."
