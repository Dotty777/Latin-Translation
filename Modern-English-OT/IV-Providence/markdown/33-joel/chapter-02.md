## Text

**2:1** Sound the trumpet in Zion, wail on My holy mountain; let all the inhabitants of the land be thrown into turmoil, because the day of the Lord is coming, because it is near.

**2:2** A day of darkness and gloom, a day of cloud and whirlwind: like the morning spread out over the mountains, a numerous and mighty people; there has not been one like it from the beginning, and after it there will not be, even to the years of generation and generation.

**2:3** Before its face is a devouring fire, and behind it a scorching flame. Like a garden of delight is the land before it, and behind it the desolation of a wilderness, and there is no one who escapes it.

**2:4** Like the appearance of horses is their appearance; and like horsemen, so they will run.

**2:5** Like the sound of chariots they will leap over the tops of the mountains, like the sound of a flame of fire devouring stubble, like a mighty people prepared for battle.

**2:6** Before its face the peoples will be tormented; all faces will be reduced to a pot.

**2:7** Like mighty men they will run; like fighting men they will climb the wall; the men will march in their own ways, and they will not turn aside from their paths.

**2:8** No one will crowd his brother; each will walk in his own track; and they will even fall through the windows, and they will not be destroyed.

**2:9** They will enter the city, they will run along the wall, they will climb the houses, they will come in through the windows like a thief.

**2:10** Before its face the earth has trembled, the heavens have been shaken, the sun and the moon have been darkened, and the stars have withdrawn their brightness.

**2:11** And the Lord has uttered His voice before the face of His army, because His camps are exceedingly numerous, because they are mighty and carry out His word; for the day of the Lord is great and very terrifying, and who will endure it?

**2:12** "Now therefore," says the Lord, "turn back to Me with all your heart, in fasting, and in weeping, and in lamentation."

**2:13** And tear your hearts, and not your garments, and turn back to the Lord your God, because He is kind and merciful, patient and of great mercy, and ready to relent over calamity.

**2:14** Who knows whether He may turn back and pardon, and leave a blessing behind Him, a sacrifice and drink offering for the Lord your God?

**2:15** Sound the trumpet in Zion, sanctify a fast, call an assembly.

**2:16** Gather the people, sanctify the assembly, bring together the elders, gather the little children and those nursing at the breast; let the bridegroom go out from his bedroom, and the bride from her bridal chamber.

**2:17** Between the vestibule and the altar the priests, the ministers of the Lord, will weep, and they will say, "Spare, Lord, spare Your people, and do not give Your inheritance to reproach, that the nations should rule over them. Why do they say among the peoples, 'Where is their God?'"

**2:18** The Lord has become zealous for His land, and He has spared His people.

**2:19** And the Lord answered and said to His people, "Look, I will send you grain, and wine, and oil, and you will be filled with them; and I will no longer make you a reproach among the nations.

**2:20** And the one who is from the north I will put far away from you, and I will drive him out into a pathless and deserted land, his front toward the eastern sea and his rear toward the farthest sea; and his stench will rise, and his rot will rise, because he has acted proudly."

**2:21** Do not be afraid, land; exult and be glad, because the Lord has done great things.

**2:22** Do not be afraid, animals of the countryside, because the lovely places of the wilderness have sprouted, because the tree has borne its fruit; the fig tree and the vine have given their strength.

**2:23** And, children of Zion, exult and be glad in the Lord your God, because He has given you a teacher of righteousness, and He will cause the morning and evening rain to come down to you, as in the beginning.

**2:24** And the threshing floors will be filled with grain, and the presses will overflow with wine and oil.

**2:25** And I will restore to you the years that the locust, the wingless locust, and the blight, and the caterpillar have eaten, My great force that I sent against you.

**2:26** And eating, you will eat, and you will be satisfied; and you will praise the name of the Lord your God, who has done wonders with you; and My people will not be put to shame forever.

**2:27** And you will know that I am in the midst of Israel, and I am the Lord your God, and there is no other; and My people will not be put to shame for eternity.

**2:28** And it will happen after these things: I will pour out My Spirit on all flesh, and your sons and your daughters will prophesy; your old men will dream dreams, and your young men will see visions.

**2:29** And even on My male servants and female servants in those days I will pour out My Spirit.

**2:30** And I will give portents in heaven and on earth: blood, and fire, and vapor of smoke.

**2:31** The sun will be turned into darkness, and the moon into blood, before the day of the Lord comes, great and dreadful.

**2:32** And it will happen: everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved, because on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there will be salvation, just as the Lord has said, and among those who remain whom the Lord will call.

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — Latin hortus voluptatis ("garden of delight") translates the meaning of Hebrew "garden of Eden," as voluptatis also does in Genesis 2:8 (paradisum voluptatis).
- **2:6** — Latin redigentur in ollam literally means "will be reduced to a pot," taking the rare Hebrew term parur as "cooking pot" (suggesting faces blackened like a soot-covered pot) rather than growing pale.
- **2:8** — Latin per fenestras cadent, et non demolientur ("fall through the windows, and they will not be destroyed") is a Vulgate-distinct reading; the Hebrew text speaks of plunging through weapons without breaking ranks.
- **2:13** — Latin praestabilis super malitia is a distinctive Vulgate idiom meaning "ready to relent over calamity," rendering Hebrew "relenting of disaster."
- **2:23** — Latin doctorem justitiae ("a teacher of righteousness") reads Hebrew moreh as "teacher" rather than "early rain"; imbrem matutinum et serotinum (lit. "morning and evening rain") denotes the early autumn and late spring rains.
- **2:28** — Native Clementine Vulgate Joel uses the three-chapter division (2:28-32 = Saints.Bible 3:1-5; 3:1-21 = Saints.Bible 4:1-21).
- **2:29** — Latin servos meos ("My male servants") includes the possessive "My" (agreeing with the Septuagint and Acts 2:18), whereas the Hebrew text reads simply "the male servants." Native Clementine Vulgate Joel 2:28-32 maps to Saints.Bible 3:1-5 and native 3:1-21 maps to Saints.Bible 4:1-21.
- **2:30** — Latin vaporem fumi ("vapor of smoke") follows the Septuagint rather than Hebrew "columns of smoke."
