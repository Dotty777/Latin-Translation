## Text

**2:1** I will stand on my watch, and I will set my step upon the fortification; and I will keep watch to see what is spoken to me, and what I may answer to the one reproving me.

**2:2** And the Lord answered me and said, "Write the vision, and make it plain upon tablets, so that the one who reads it may run through it.

**2:3** For the vision is still far off, and it will appear at the end, and it will not lie. If it makes a delay, wait for it; for coming it will come, and it will not be late.

**2:4** Look, as for the one who is unbelieving, his soul will not be upright within himself; but the righteous one will live in his faith.

**2:5** And just as wine deceives the one who drinks, so will the proud man be, and he will not be honored, he who has widened his soul like the underworld, and he himself is like death and is not filled; and he will gather to himself all nations, and heap up to himself all peoples.

**2:6** Will not all these take up a parable against him, and a saying of riddles about him? And it will be said, 'Woe to the one who multiplies what is not his! How long does he also load thick clay against himself?

**2:7** Will not those who bite you suddenly rise up, and those tearing you apart be roused, and you become prey for them?

**2:8** Because you have plundered many nations, all who remain of the peoples will plunder you, because of the blood of man, and the iniquity of the land, of the city, and of all who dwell in it.

**2:9** Woe to the one who gathers evil greed for his house, so that his nest may be on high, and thinks that he is delivered from the hand of evil!

**2:10** You have devised shame for your house; you have cut down many peoples, and your soul has sinned.

**2:11** For the stone will cry out from the wall, and the wood that is between the joints of the buildings will answer.

**2:12** Woe to the one who builds a city in bloodshed, and prepares a town in iniquity!

**2:13** Are not these things from the Lord of hosts? For the peoples will toil in much fire, and the nations in vain, and they will fail.

**2:14** For the earth will be filled so that they may know the glory of the Lord, like waters covering the sea.

**2:15** Woe to the one who gives drink to his friend, pouring in his gall, and making him drunk so that he may look upon his nakedness!

**2:16** You have been filled with disgrace instead of glory; drink, you too, and fall into a stupor. The cup of the right hand of the Lord will encompass you, and vomit of disgrace will be upon your glory.

**2:17** For the iniquity of Lebanon will cover you, and the devastation of animals will terrify them, because of the bloodshed of men, and the iniquity of the land, and of the city, and of all who dwell in it.

**2:18** What profit is a carved image, that its maker has carved it, a molten image and a false likeness? For its maker has trusted in his handiwork, to make mute idols.

**2:19** Woe to the one who says to wood, "Wake up!" and "Arise!" to a silent stone! Will it be able to teach? Look, it is covered with gold and silver, and there is no spirit at all in its inward parts.'

**2:20** But the Lord is in His holy temple; let all the earth be silent before His face!"

## Translator Notes

- **2:4** — Vulgate 'qui incredulus est' ("the one who is unbelieving") and 'in fide sua' ("in his faith") differ from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which describes one who is "puffed up" and the righteous living by "his faithfulness."
- **2:5** — Vulgate 'non decorabitur' ("will not be honored") translates Hebrew 'lo yinweh' ("will not stay at home" or "will not endure") via a related root denoting beauty or adornment.
- **2:6** — Vulgate 'densum lutum' ("thick clay") reads Hebrew 'avtit' ("heavy pledges" or "debt") as two words meaning "thick clay."
- **2:14** — Vulgate renders a purpose clause, 'ut cognoscant gloriam Domini' ("so that they may know the glory of the Lord"), rather than the Hebrew noun phrase "with the knowledge of the glory of the Lord."
- **2:16** — Vulgate 'consopire' ("fall into a stupor") reflects a reading meaning "stagger" or "be stunned," rather than the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "be exposed as uncircumcised."
