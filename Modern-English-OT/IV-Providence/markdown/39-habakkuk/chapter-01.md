## Text

**1:1** The burden that the prophet Habakkuk saw.

**1:2** How long, Lord, will I cry out, and You will not listen? Will I shout aloud to You, suffering violence, and You will not save?

**1:3** Why have You shown me iniquity and toil, to see plunder and injustice against me? And judgment has come about, and contradiction is more powerful.

**1:4** Because of this, the law is torn apart, and judgment does not reach all the way to the end; because the wicked one prevails against the righteous one, therefore perverted judgment goes forth.

**1:5** Look among the nations, and see; marvel, and be astounded, for a work has been done in your days that no one will believe when it is told.

**1:6** For look, I will raise up the Chaldeans, a bitter and swift nation, walking over the breadth of the earth to possess tents not its own.

**1:7** It is dreadful and terrifying; from itself its judgment and its burden will go forth.

**1:8** Its horses are lighter than leopards, and swifter than evening wolves; and its horsemen will spread out, for its horsemen will come from afar; they will fly like an eagle hurrying to eat.

**1:9** All of them will come for plunder; their face is a burning wind, and it will gather captives like sand.

**1:10** And he will triumph over kings, and tyrants will be his laughingstocks; he will laugh at every fortification, and he will pile up a mound and capture it.

**1:11** Then the spirit will be changed, and he will pass through and fall; this strength of his is his god's.

**1:12** Are You not from the beginning, Lord, my God, my Holy One, and we will not die? Lord, You have appointed him for judgment, and You have established him strong so that You might correct.

**1:13** Your eyes are pure, so that You do not see evil, and You will not be able to look upon iniquity. Why do You look upon those who do iniquity, and remain silent while the wicked devours one more righteous than himself?

**1:14** And You will make men like the fish of the sea, and like a creeping thing having no ruler.

**1:15** He lifted up the whole with a hook, dragged it in his dragnet, and gathered it into his net. Over this he will be glad and exult.

**1:16** Therefore he will offer sacrifice to his dragnet and make offerings to his net, because through them his portion has been made fat, and his food is choice.

**1:17** Because of this, then, he spreads out his dragnet, and he will not refrain from always killing nations.

## Translator Notes

- **1:3** — Vulgate 'factum est judicium, et contradictio potentior' ("judgment has come about, and contradiction is more powerful") differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which speaks of strife arising and contention lifting up.
- **1:5** — Vulgate uses the passive 'opus factum est' ("a work has been done") and third-person 'nemo credet' ("no one will believe"), where the Hebrew has first-person active ("I am doing a work") and second-person ("you will not believe").
- **1:11** — Vulgate 'corruet' ("will fall") diverges from the Hebrew ("will become guilty"), and the compressed double-genitive clause 'hæc est fortitudo ejus dei sui' ("this strength of his is his god's") attributes the conqueror's power to his god.
- **1:12** — Vulgate reads the accusative 'fortem' ("strong") in reference to the Chaldean invader, whereas the Hebrew 'tsur' ("Rock") is a vocative title addressed to God.
