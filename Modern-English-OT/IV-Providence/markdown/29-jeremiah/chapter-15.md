## Text

**15:1** And the Lord said to me, "Even if Moses and Samuel should stand before Me, My soul is not toward this people; cast them out from My presence, and let them go out.

**15:2** And if they say to you, 'Where shall we go out?' you shall say to them, 'This is what the Lord says: Whoever is for death, to death; and whoever is for the sword, to the sword; and whoever is for famine, to famine; and whoever is for captivity, to captivity.'

**15:3** And I will visit upon them four kinds," says the Lord, "the sword for slaughter, and dogs to tear apart, and the birds of the sky and the beasts of the earth to devour and to destroy.

**15:4** And I will give them over to agitation for all the kingdoms of the earth, because of Manasseh, son of Hezekiah, king of Judah, on account of all that he did in Jerusalem.

**15:5** For who will have pity on you, Jerusalem, or who will grieve for you? Or who will go to ask for your peace?

**15:6** You have abandoned Me," says the Lord; "you have gone backward. And I will stretch out My hand over you and kill you; I have grown weary of pleading.

**15:7** And I will scatter them with a winnowing fork in the gates of the land; I have killed and destroyed My people, and yet they have not turned back from their ways.

**15:8** Her widows have been multiplied to Me beyond the sand of the sea; I have brought upon them, against the mother of the young man, a destroyer at midday; I have suddenly sent terror upon the cities.

**15:9** She who bore seven has grown weak; her soul has failed; the sun went down for her while it was still day; she has been put to shame and has blushed. And her survivors I will give over to the sword in the sight of their enemies," says the Lord.

**15:10** Woe to me, my mother! Why did you give birth to me, a man of strife, a man of discord in all the earth? I have not lent at interest, nor has anyone lent at interest to me; all of them curse me.

**15:11** The Lord says, "Surely your remnant is for good; surely I have come to meet you in the time of affliction and in the time of tribulation against the enemy.

**15:12** Can iron be allied with iron from the north, and bronze?

**15:13** Your riches and your treasures I will give over to plunder without price, because of all your sins and throughout all your borders.

**15:14** And I will bring your enemies from a land that you do not know, for a fire has been kindled in My fury; it will burn upon you."

**15:15** You know, Lord; remember me and visit me, and defend me from those who persecute me. In Your patience, do not take me up; know that because of You I have endured reproach.

**15:16** Your words were found, and I ate them; and Your word became for me joy and gladness of my heart, because Your name has been invoked over me, Lord God of hosts.

**15:17** I did not sit in the assembly of those who make merry, and I gloried because of Your hand; I sat alone, because You have filled me with threatening.

**15:18** Why has my pain become perpetual, and my hopeless wound refused to be healed? It has become to me like a lie of unfaithful waters.

**15:19** Therefore, this is what the Lord says: "If you turn back, I will bring you back, and you will stand before My face; and if you separate the precious from the worthless, you will be like My mouth. They themselves will turn to you, and you will not turn to them.

**15:20** And I will make you for this people a strong wall of bronze; and they will fight against you, and they will not prevail, because I am with you to save you and to rescue you," says the Lord,

**15:21** "and I will deliver you from the hand of the most wicked, and I will redeem you from the hand of the strong."

## Translator Notes

- **15:6** — Vulgate 'laboravi rogans' literally reads "I have grown weary of pleading," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "I am weary of relenting."
- **15:11** — Latin 'Si non' ("If not...") reproduces the Hebrew elliptical oath formula ("Surely"). Vulgate 'reliquiæ tuæ' ("your remnant") and 'occurri tibi ... adversus inimicum' ("I have come to meet you ... against the enemy") differ notably from the Masoretic Text.
- **15:12** — Vulgate 'fœderabitur' ("be allied") takes the Hebrew verb in the sense of associating as companions rather than "break" or "shatter."
- **15:14** — Vulgate 'adducam inimicos tuos de terra' ("I will bring your enemies from a land") differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which speaks of making Judah serve enemies in an unknown land.
- **15:18** — Vulgate 'facta est' is third-person feminine singular, taking "my wound" ('plaga mea') as subject ("It has become to me..."), whereas the Hebrew uses second-person singular addressing God ("Will You be to me...").
