## Text

**37:1** And King Zedekiah son of Josiah reigned in place of Jeconiah son of Jehoiakim, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon appointed king in the land of Judah.

**37:2** And neither he himself, nor his servants, nor the people of the land obeyed the words of the Lord, which He spoke by the hand of Jeremiah the prophet.

**37:3** And King Zedekiah sent Jehucal son of Shelemiah, and Zephaniah son of Maaseiah the priest, to Jeremiah the prophet, saying, "Pray for us to the Lord our God."

**37:4** Now Jeremiah was walking freely in the midst of the people, for they had not put him into the custody of the prison. Then Pharaoh's army came out from Egypt, and the Chaldeans who were besieging Jerusalem, hearing such a report, withdrew from Jerusalem.

**37:5** And the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah the prophet, saying:

**37:6** "This is what the Lord God of Israel says: In this way you will say to the king of Judah, who sent you to inquire of Me, 'Look, Pharaoh's army, which came out to your aid, will return to its own land, to Egypt,

**37:7** and the Chaldeans will come back and wage war against this city, and capture it, and burn it with fire.'

**37:8** This is what the Lord says: Do not deceive your souls, saying, 'Going away, the Chaldeans will depart and withdraw from us,' for they will not depart.

**37:9** But even if you strike down the entire army of the Chaldeans who are fighting against you, and some wounded men are left from among them, each one will rise up from his tent and burn this city with fire."

**37:10** Therefore, when the army of the Chaldeans had withdrawn from Jerusalem because of Pharaoh's army,

**37:11** Jeremiah went out from Jerusalem to go into the land of Benjamin and to divide a possession there in the presence of the citizens.

**37:12** And when he had arrived at the Gate of Benjamin, a guard of the gate was there by turns, named Irijah son of Shelemiah, son of Hananiah; and he seized Jeremiah the prophet, saying, "You are deserting to the Chaldeans."

**37:13** And Jeremiah answered, "It is false; I am not fleeing to the Chaldeans." Yet he did not listen to him, but Irijah arrested Jeremiah and brought him to the officials.

**37:14** Because of this, the officials, angry against Jeremiah, beat him and put him into the prison that was in the house of Jonathan the scribe; for he himself had been set over the prison.

**37:15** So Jeremiah entered into the house of the pit and into the dungeon; and Jeremiah stayed there for many days.

**37:16** Then King Zedekiah sent and brought him, and he questioned him secretly in his house, and said, "Do you think there is a word from the Lord?" And Jeremiah said, "There is." And he said, "You will be delivered into the hands of the king of Babylon."

**37:17** And Jeremiah said to King Zedekiah, "How have I sinned against you, and against your servants, and against your people, that you have put me into the house of the prison?

**37:18** Where are your prophets who prophesied to you and said, 'The king of Babylon will not come upon you and upon this land'?

**37:19** Now therefore listen, I plead with you, my lord the king: may my plea prevail in your presence, and do not send me back into the house of Jonathan the scribe, so that I do not die there."

**37:20** Therefore King Zedekiah commanded that Jeremiah be committed to the courtyard of the prison, and that a loaf of bread be given to him daily, besides stew, until all the loaves from the city were consumed; and Jeremiah remained in the courtyard of the prison.

## Translator Notes

- **37:4** — In this critical text, verse 4 includes the clause concerning Pharaoh's army and the Chaldeans' withdrawal (often numbered separately as verse 5), and Latin libere ambulabat ("was walking freely") renders the Hebrew idiom "coming in and going out."
- **37:11** — Latin et divideret ibi possessionem in conspectu civium ("and to divide a possession there in the presence of the citizens") resolves a notoriously difficult Hebrew clause as a public division of property.
- **37:14** — Latin ipse enim praepositus erat super carcerem ("for he himself had been set over the prison") identifies Jonathan as the warden in charge, whereas the Hebrew states that his house had been made into the prison.
- **37:15** — Latin in domum laci et in ergastulum ("into the house of the pit and into the dungeon") pairs a literal rendering of "house of the cistern/pit" with the singular Roman term ergastulum ("dungeon"), where the Hebrew has plural "vaulted cells."
- **37:20** — Latin excepto pulmento ("besides stew") is a distinctive Vulgate reading for the Hebrew phrase "from the street of the bakers."
