## Text

**25:1** The word that came to Jeremiah concerning all the people of Judah, in the fourth year of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, king of Judah (that is the first year of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon),

**25:2** which Jeremiah the prophet spoke to all the people of Judah and to all the inhabitants of Jerusalem, saying:

**25:3** "From the thirteenth year of Josiah son of Amon, king of Judah, until this day—this is the twenty-third year—the word of the Lord has come to me, and I have spoken to you, rising in the night and speaking, yet you have not listened.

**25:4** And the Lord sent to you all His servants the prophets, rising at daybreak and sending them; yet you did not listen, nor did you incline your ears to hear,

**25:5** when He said, 'Turn back, each one from his evil way and from your most wicked thoughts, and you will dwell in the land that the Lord gave to you and to your fathers, from everlasting to everlasting;

**25:6** and do not go after foreign gods to serve them and worship them, and do not provoke Me to anger with the works of your hands, and I will not afflict you.'

**25:7** 'Yet you did not listen to Me,' says the Lord, 'so that you provoked Me to anger with the works of your hands, to your own harm.'

**25:8** Therefore this is what the Lord of hosts says: 'Because you have not listened to My words,

**25:9** look, I will send and take all the families of the north,' says the Lord, 'and Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, My servant, and I will bring them against this land, and against its inhabitants, and against all the nations that are around it; and I will put them to death, and I will make them an astonishment, and a hissing, and everlasting desolations.

**25:10** And I will destroy from among them the voice of joy and the voice of gladness, the voice of the bridegroom and the voice of the bride, the sound of the millstone and the light of the lamp.

**25:11** And this entire land will become a desolation and an astonishment; and all these nations will serve the king of Babylon for seventy years.

**25:12** And when the seventy years have been fulfilled, I will visit upon the king of Babylon and upon that nation,' says the Lord, 'their iniquity, and upon the land of the Chaldeans, and I will make it everlasting desolations.

**25:13** And I will bring upon that land all My words that I have spoken against it, everything that is written in this book, whatever Jeremiah has prophesied against all the nations.'"

**25:14** The word of the Lord that came to Jeremiah the prophet against Elam, in the beginning of the reign of Zedekiah king of Judah, saying:

**25:15** "This is what the Lord of hosts says: Look, I will break the bow of Elam, and their foremost strength;

**25:16** and I will bring upon Elam four winds from the four quarters of heaven, and I will scatter them into all these winds, and there will not be a nation to which the fugitives of Elam do not come.

**25:17** And I will make Elam terrified before their enemies and in the sight of those who seek their life; and I will bring calamity upon them, the anger of My fury," says the Lord, "and I will send the sword after them until I consume them.

**25:18** And I will set My throne in Elam, and I will destroy kings and princes from there," says the Lord.

**25:19** "Yet in the last days I will cause the captives of Elam to return," says the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **25:3** — Latin de nocte consurgens ("rising in the night") here and consurgens diluculo ("rising at daybreak") in 25:4 both render the Hebrew idiom for rising early and acting persistently.
- **25:14** — Following the Septuagint placement of the oracles against the nations immediately after 25:13, this Latin critical text presents the oracle against Elam at 25:14-19 (located at 49:34-39 in the Hebrew Masoretic Text and traditional Clementine Vulgate, which place the cup of wrath oracle at 25:14-38). The critical text's facum stands for factum ("came").
- **25:19** — Vulgate captivos ("the captives") renders the object personally, whereas the corresponding Hebrew idiom can mean "the fortunes" or "the captivity."
