## Text

**14:1** The word of the Lord that came to Jeremiah concerning the words of the drought.

**14:2** Judea has mourned, and her gates have collapsed and have been darkened upon the ground, and the outcry of Jerusalem has gone up.

**14:3** The nobles sent their lesser ones for water; they came to draw it. They did not find water; they brought back their vessels empty. They were put to shame and afflicted, and they covered their heads.

**14:4** Because of the devastation of the ground, since no rain came upon the land, the farmers were put to shame; they covered their heads.

**14:5** For even the doe in the field gave birth and left, because there was no grass.

**14:6** And the wild donkeys stood on the cliffs; they drew in the wind like dragons; their eyes failed, because there was no grass.

**14:7** If our iniquities answer against us, Lord, act for the sake of Your name; for our turnings away are many; against You we have sinned.

**14:8** Expectation of Israel, its Savior in the time of tribulation, why will You be like a sojourner in the land, and like a traveler turning aside to stay?

**14:9** Why will You be like a wandering man, like a mighty man who cannot save? Yet You are among us, Lord, and Your name has been invoked over us; do not abandon us.

**14:10** This is what the Lord says to this people, who have loved to move their feet and have not rested, and have not pleased the Lord: "Now He will remember their iniquities, and He will visit their sins."

**14:11** And the Lord said to me: "Do not pray for this people for good.

**14:12** When they fast, I will not heed their prayers, and if they offer burnt offerings and sacrifices, I will not accept them; for by sword, and famine, and plague I will consume them."

**14:13** And I said, "Ah, ah, ah, Lord God! The prophets say to them, 'You will not see the sword, and famine will not be among you, but He will give you true peace in this place.'"

**14:14** And the Lord says to me: "The prophets prophesy falsely in My name; I did not send them, and I did not command them, nor did I speak to them. They prophesy to you a lying vision, and divination, and fraud, and the deception of their own heart.

**14:15** Therefore this is what the Lord says concerning the prophets who prophesy in My name, whom I did not send, saying, 'Sword and famine will not be in this land': By sword and famine those prophets will be consumed.

**14:16** And the people to whom they prophesy will be cast out in the streets of Jerusalem because of famine and the sword, and there will be no one to bury them: they themselves and their wives, their sons and their daughters; and I will pour out upon them their own evil.

**14:17** And you shall speak this word to them: 'Let my eyes pour down tears through night and day, and let them not be silent, for with a great crushing the virgin daughter of my people has been crushed, with an exceedingly severe wound.

**14:18** If I go out to the fields, look, those killed by the sword! And if I enter into the city, look, those wasted away by famine! The prophet also and the priest have gone away into a land that they did not know.'"

**14:19** Have You cast aside and rejected Judah? Or has Your soul abhorred Zion? Why then have You struck us so that there is no healing? We waited for peace, and there is no good; and for a time of healing, and look, turmoil.

**14:20** We acknowledge, Lord, our acts of ungodliness, the iniquities of our fathers, for we have sinned against You.

**14:21** Do not give us over to reproach, for the sake of Your name, and do not bring upon us the dishonor of the throne of Your glory; remember, do not make void Your covenant with us.

**14:22** Are there among the carved images of the nations any who send rain? Or can the heavens give showers? Are You not the Lord our God, for whom we have waited? For You Yourself have made all these things.

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — Latin de sermonibus siccitatis ("concerning the words of the drought") literally reproduces the Hebrew idiom meaning "concerning the matters" or "events of the drought."
- **14:6** — The Vulgate renders the animal name as dracones ("dragons"), whereas the Hebrew term tannim is typically translated "jackals" in modern Hebrew-based versions.
- **14:13** — The threefold exclamation A, a, a is Jerome's characteristic Vulgate rendering of the Hebrew lament interjection, and the Vulgate reads third-person dabit ("He will give") where the Masoretic Text has first-person "I will give."
- **14:18** — The Vulgate reads abierunt in terram quam ignorabant ("have gone away into a land that they did not know"), describing exile, whereas the Masoretic Text speaks of plying their trade in the land without knowing what to do.
- **14:21** — The Vulgate reads Ne des nos in opprobrium ("Do not give us over to reproach") and includes nobis ("upon us") in neque facias nobis contumeliam, differing from the Masoretic Text's "Do not spurn ... do not disgrace the throne of Your glory."
