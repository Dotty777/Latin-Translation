## Text

**20:1** And Pashhur the son of Immer, the priest, who had been appointed ruler in the house of the Lord, heard Jeremiah prophesying these words.

**20:2** And Pashhur struck Jeremiah the prophet and put him in the stocks that were in the upper Gate of Benjamin, in the house of the Lord.

**20:3** And when it had grown light on the next day, Pashhur brought Jeremiah out of the stocks, and Jeremiah said to him, "The Lord has not called your name Pashhur, but Terror on Every Side.

**20:4** For this is what the Lord says: 'Look, I will give you over to terror, you and all your friends; and they will fall by the sword of their enemies, and your eyes will see; and I will give all Judah into the hand of the king of Babylon, and he will lead them away into Babylon and strike them with the sword.

**20:5** And I will give all the wealth of this city, and all its labor, and everything of value, and all the treasures of the kings of Judah I will give into the hand of their enemies; and they will plunder them, and take them, and bring them into Babylon.

**20:6** And you, Pashhur, and all the inhabitants of your house, will go into captivity; and you will come into Babylon, and there you will die, and there you will be buried, you and all your friends to whom you have prophesied falsehood.'"

**20:7** You have deceived me, Lord, and I was deceived; You were stronger than I, and You prevailed. I have become a mockery all day long; everyone sneers at me.

**20:8** Because for a long time now I have been speaking, shouting out iniquity and repeatedly crying out devastation; and the word of the Lord has become for me a reproach and a mockery all day long.

**20:9** And I said, "I will not remember Him, nor will I speak anymore in His name." And it became in my heart like a blazing fire, shut up in my bones; and I grew weary, unable to endure bearing it.

**20:10** For I have heard the insults of many, and terror all around, "Pursue, and let us pursue him!" from all the men who were at peace with me and watching my side: "Perhaps somehow he may be deceived, and we may prevail against him, and obtain vengeance from him."

**20:11** But the Lord is with me like a mighty warrior; therefore those who pursue me will fall and will be weak; they will be greatly put to shame, because they have not understood the everlasting reproach, which will never be blotted out.

**20:12** And You, Lord of hosts, tester of the righteous, who see the kidneys and the heart, please let me see Your vengeance on them; for to You I have revealed my cause.

**20:13** Sing to the Lord, praise the Lord, for He has delivered the soul of the poor from the hand of the wicked.

**20:14** Cursed be the day on which I was born! May the day on which my mother bore me not be blessed!

**20:15** Cursed be the man who brought news to my father, saying, "A male child has been born to you!" and made him glad as with joy!

**20:16** Let that man be like the cities that the Lord overthrew, and He did not relent; let him hear an outcry in the morning, and a wailing at midday,

**20:17** who did not kill me from the womb, so that my mother might become a tomb for me, and her womb an everlasting pregnancy!

**20:18** Why did I come out from the womb to see toil and sorrow, and for my days to be consumed in shame?

## Translator Notes

- **20:3** — Rather than transliterating the Hebrew symbolic name Magor-missabib, the Vulgate translates it directly into Latin as Pavorem undique ("Terror on Every Side").
- **20:4** — The Vulgate places te et omnes amicos tuos in the accusative ("you and all your friends"), rather than the Hebrew dative "to yourself and to all your friends."
- **20:8** — The Vulgate reads Quia jam olim loquor ("Because for a long time now I have been speaking"), whereas the Hebrew means "For whenever I speak."
- **20:10** — The Vulgate reads Persequimini, et persequamur eum ("Pursue, and let us pursue him," linking with persequuntur in 20:11) where the Hebrew has "Denounce, and let us denounce him," and renders Hebrew "watching for my stumbling" literally as custodientes latus meum ("watching my side").
- **20:11** — The Vulgate reads infirmi erunt ("will be weak," vs. Hebrew "will not prevail"), makes "the everlasting reproach" the direct object of non intellexerunt ("have not understood"), and closes with numquam delebitur ("will never be blotted out," vs. Hebrew "will never be forgotten").
