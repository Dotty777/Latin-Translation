## Text

**10:1** Hear the word that the Lord has spoken over you, house of Israel.

**10:2** This is what the Lord says: "Do not learn according to the ways of the nations, and do not be afraid of the signs of heaven, which the nations dread,

**10:3** for the laws of the peoples are empty. For someone has cut down wood from the forest, the work of the hand of a craftsman with an axe;

**10:4** with silver and gold he has adorned it; with nails and hammers he has fastened it together, so that it may not come apart;

**10:5** they are fashioned in the likeness of a palm tree, and they will not speak; carried, they will be borne, because they are not able to walk. Do not fear them therefore, because they can do neither evil nor good.

**10:9** Rolled silver is brought from Tarshish, and gold from Uphaz, the work of a craftsman and of the hand of a coppersmith. Blue and purple are their clothing; all of these are the work of craftsmen.

**10:11** This, therefore, is what you will say to them, 'May the gods who did not make the heavens and the earth perish from the earth and from these things that are under heaven!'"

**10:12** He who makes the earth by His strength, prepares the world by His wisdom, and by His understanding stretches out the heavens:

**10:13** at His voice He gives a multitude of waters in heaven, and raises up mists from the ends of the earth; He makes lightning flashes for the rain, and brings out the wind from His treasuries.

**10:14** Every man has been made foolish apart from knowledge; every craftsman has been put to shame by a carved image, since what he has cast is false, and there is no spirit in them.

**10:15** They are empty, and a work worthy of laughter; in the time of their visitation they will perish.

**10:16** The portion of Jacob is not like these; for He Himself is the One who formed all things, and Israel is the rod of His inheritance: the Lord of hosts is His name.

**10:17** Gather up your shame from the ground, you who dwell under siege,

**10:18** for this is what the Lord says: "Look, I will hurl the inhabitants of the land far away at this time, and I will distress them so that they may be found."

**10:19** Woe to me over my crushing; my wound is most severe. Yet I said, "Truly this is my infirmity, and I will bear it."

**10:20** My tent has been devastated; all my cords have been broken apart; my sons have gone out from me, and they do not remain. There is no one to stretch out my tent any longer and raise up my skins.

**10:21** Because the shepherds have acted foolishly and have not sought the Lord, therefore they have not understood, and all their flock has been scattered.

**10:22** Look, the sound of a report is coming, and a great commotion from the land of the north, to make the cities of Judah a wilderness and a dwelling place of dragons.

**10:23** I know, Lord, that a person's way is not his own, nor does it belong to a man to walk and direct his steps.

**10:24** Correct me, Lord, yet in judgment, and not in Your fury, so that You do not reduce me to nothing.

**10:25** Pour out Your indignation upon the nations that have not known You, and upon the provinces that have not called upon Your name; because they have eaten Jacob, and devoured him, and consumed him, and laid waste his splendor.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**10:7** *(after `10:5`)* Who will not fear You, King of the nations? For honor belongs to You; among all the wise of the nations, and in all their kingdoms, there is no one like You.

**10:8** *(after `10:5`)* Together they will be proven senseless and foolish; the instruction of their futility is wood.

**10:10** *(after `10:9`)* But the Lord is the true God; He is the living God and the eternal King. At His indignation the earth will tremble, and the nations will not endure His threat.

## Translator Notes

- **10:5** — The Vulgate reads in similitudinem palmae ("in the likeness of a palm tree"), where the Hebrew phrase is commonly rendered "like a scarecrow in a cucumber field."
- **10:11** — The Vulgate renders the closing phrase as de his quae sub caelo sunt ("from these things that are under heaven"), rather than "from under these heavens" as in the Aramaic text.
- **10:17** — The Vulgate reads confusionem tuam ("your shame"), linking the Hebrew word for "bundle" to the root meaning "to be humbled."
- **10:18** — The Vulgate translates literally with the passive ita ut inveniantur ("so that they may be found"), where modern translations from the Hebrew often render "so that they may feel it."
- **10:25** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew terms for "families" and "habitation" as provincias ("provinces") and decus ("splendor").
