## Text

**24:1** The Lord showed me, and look, two baskets full of figs were set before the temple of the Lord, after Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had carried away Jeconiah son of Jehoiakim, king of Judah, and his princes, and the craftsman, and the locksmith, from Jerusalem, and had brought them to Babylon.

**24:2** One basket had very good figs, such as figs of the first season usually are; and the other basket had very bad figs, which could not be eaten because they were bad.

**24:3** And the Lord said to me, "What do you see, Jeremiah?" And I said, "Figs, good figs, very good, and bad ones, very bad, which cannot be eaten because they are bad."

**24:4** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying:

**24:5** "This is what the Lord God of Israel says: Like these good figs, so I will regard the exiles of Judah, whom I sent out from this place into the land of the Chaldeans, for good.

**24:6** And I will set My eyes upon them to be appeased, and I will bring them back into this land; and I will build them up and not tear them down, and I will plant them and not uproot them.

**24:7** And I will give them a heart to know Me, that I am the Lord; and they will be My people, and I will be their God, because they will return to Me with their whole heart.

**24:8** And like the worst figs, which cannot be eaten because they are bad—this is what the Lord says—so I will give over Zedekiah king of Judah, and his princes, and the rest from Jerusalem who have remained in this city, and those who dwell in the land of Egypt.

**24:9** And I will give them over to distress and affliction among all the kingdoms of the earth, to be a reproach, and a parable, and a proverb, and a curse in all the places to which I have driven them.

**24:10** And I will send among them the sword, and famine, and plague, until they are consumed from the land that I gave to them and to their fathers."

## Translator Notes

- **24:1** — Latin uses two singular collective nouns, fabrum ("the craftsman") and inclusorem (literally "encloser," used in the Vulgate for a locksmith, metalworker, or setter of inlays).
- **24:6** — Where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "for good," the Vulgate has ad placandum ("to be appeased" or "for reconciliation").
