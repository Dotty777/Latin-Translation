## Text

**49:1** And all the commanders of the fighting men, and Johanan son of Kareah, and Jezaniah son of Hoshaiah, and the rest of the common people, from the small to the great, came near,

**49:2** and said to Jeremiah the prophet, "Let our petition fall in your sight, and pray for us to the Lord your God, for all this remnant, for we are left few out of many, just as your eyes look upon us;

**49:3** and may the Lord your God declare to us the way by which we should go, and the thing that we should do."

**49:4** Then Jeremiah the prophet said to them, "I have heard. Look, I am praying to the Lord your God according to your words; every word, whatever He answers me, I will make known to you, and I will not conceal anything from you."

**49:5** And they said to Jeremiah, "May the Lord be a witness of truth and faithfulness between us, if we do not act so according to every word with which the Lord your God sends you to us;

**49:6** whether it is good or bad, we will obey the voice of the Lord our God, to whom we are sending you, so that it may go well with us when we listen to the voice of the Lord our God."

**49:7** Now when ten days had been completed, the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah,

**49:8** and he called Johanan son of Kareah, and all the commanders of the fighting men who were with him, and all the people, from the least to the great.

**49:9** And he said to them, "This is what the Lord, the God of Israel, says, to whom you sent me to lay down your petitions in His sight:

**49:10** 'If you remain quietly in this land, I will build you up and not tear down; I will plant and not uproot; for I have already been appeased over the calamity that I have done to you.

**49:11** Do not be afraid before the king of Babylon, whom you dread in terror; do not fear him, says the Lord, for I am with you to save you and to deliver you from his hand;

**49:12** and I will grant you mercies, and I will have mercy on you, and I will make you dwell in your land.'

**49:13** But if you say, 'We will not dwell in this land, nor will we listen to the voice of the Lord our God,'

**49:14** saying, 'By no means, but we will go to the land of Egypt, where we will not see war, and we will not hear the blast of the trumpet, and we will not endure famine, and there we will dwell,'

**49:15** because of this, now hear the word of the Lord, remnant of Judah. This is what the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, says: 'If you set your face to enter Egypt, and go in to dwell there,

**49:16** the sword that you dread will overtake you there in the land of Egypt; and the famine about which you are anxious will cling to you in Egypt, and there you will die.

**49:17** And all the men who have set their face to enter Egypt, to dwell there, will die by the sword, and by famine, and by pestilence; none of them will remain or escape from before the calamity that I will bring upon them.'

**49:18** For this is what the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, says: 'Just as My fury and My indignation were kindled upon the inhabitants of Jerusalem, so My indignation will be kindled upon you when you have entered Egypt; and you will become an oath, and an astonishment, and a curse, and a reproach, and you will never again see this place.'

**49:19** The word of the Lord is upon you, remnant of Judah: 'Do not enter Egypt.' You will surely know that I have solemnly warned you today,

**49:20** because you have deceived your own souls. For you sent me to the Lord our God, saying, 'Pray for us to the Lord our God, and according to everything that the Lord our God says to you, declare it so to us, and we will do it.'

**49:21** And I have declared it to you today, and you have not listened to the voice of the Lord your God concerning all things for which He sent me to you.

**49:22** Now therefore you will surely know that by the sword, and by famine, and by pestilence you will die in the place into which you desired to enter, to dwell there."

## Translator Notes

- **49:10** — Latin jam enim placatus sum ("for I have already been appeased") renders the Hebrew verb for relenting or regretting with the language of divine wrath being pacified.
- **49:12** — Latin miserebor vestri, et habitare vos faciam ("I will have mercy on you, and I will make you dwell") uses first-person verbs with God as subject and "make dwell" rather than "bring back," differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, where the king of Babylon is the subject ("he will have compassion on you and return you").
- **49:18** — Latin conflatus est / conflabitur ("were kindled" / "will be kindled") uses the imagery of a fire fanned or smelted, where the Hebrew verb means "poured out."
- **49:19** — Latin Verbum Domini super vos ("The word of the Lord is upon you") reads a verbless noun phrase ("word") rather than the Hebrew Masoretic vocalization as a verb ("the Lord has spoken concerning you").
