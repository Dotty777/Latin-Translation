## Text

**18:1** The word that came to Jeremiah from the Lord, saying:

**18:2** "Rise, and go down to the potter's house, and there you will hear My words."

**18:3** And I went down to the potter's house, and look, he was doing work on the wheel.

**18:4** And the vessel that he was making out of clay with his hands was ruined; and turning again, he made it into another vessel, just as it had been pleasing in his eyes to make.

**18:5** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying:

**18:6** "Can I not do to you just as this potter does, house of Israel?" says the Lord. "Look, just as clay is in the hand of the potter, so are you in My hand, house of Israel.

**18:7** Suddenly I will speak against a nation and against a kingdom, to uproot, and to tear down, and to destroy it;

**18:8** if that nation repents from its evil that I have spoken against it, I also will repent over the evil that I have planned to do to it.

**18:9** And in a moment I will speak concerning a nation and concerning a kingdom, to build and to plant it.

**18:10** If it does evil in My eyes, so that it does not listen to My voice, I will repent over the good that I have spoken of doing for it.

**18:11** Now therefore speak to the men of Judah and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, saying, 'This is what the Lord says: Look, I am fashioning evil against you and devising a plan against you. Let each one turn back from his evil way, and direct your ways and your pursuits.'"

**18:12** And they said, "We have despaired; for we will go after our own plans, and each of us will practice the depravity of his evil heart."

**18:13** Therefore this is what the Lord says: "Ask the nations: Who has heard such horrible things as the virgin Israel has exceedingly done?

**18:14** Will the snow of Lebanon fail from the rock of the field? Or can the cold waters bursting forth and flowing down be torn away?

**18:15** Because My people have forgotten Me, pouring out libations in vain, and stumbling in their ways, in the ancient paths, to walk through them on an untrodden road,

**18:16** so that their land would become a desolation and an everlasting hissing; everyone who passes through it will be astonished and will shake his head.

**18:17** Like a scorching wind I will scatter them before the enemy; I will show them the back, and not the face, on the day of their destruction."

**18:18** And they said, "Come, and let us devise plans against Jeremiah; for the law will not perish from the priest, nor counsel from the wise man, nor the word from the prophet. Come, and let us strike him with the tongue, and let us pay no attention to any of his words."

**18:19** Pay attention to me, Lord, and hear the voice of my adversaries.

**18:20** Is evil repaid for good, that they have dug a pit for my soul? Remember that I stood in Your sight to speak good for them, and to turn away Your indignation from them.

**18:21** Therefore give their sons over to famine, and bring them down into the hands of the sword; let their wives become childless and widows, and let their husbands be killed by death; let their young men be pierced by the sword in battle.

**18:22** Let a cry be heard from their houses, for You will suddenly bring a robber upon them, because they have dug a pit to capture me, and have hidden snares for my feet.

**18:23** But You, Lord, know all their counsel against me for death; do not be merciful toward their iniquity, and let their sin not be blotted out from Your face; let them collapse in Your sight; in the time of Your fury, deal harshly with them.

## Translator Notes

- **18:8** — The Vulgate uses the same phrase (poenitentiam agere, "repent") for both the nation's turning from evil and God's relenting of intended judgment (and again in 18:10), whereas the Hebrew uses two different verbs.
- **18:14** — The Vulgate reads evelli ("be torn away") and erumpentes ("bursting forth") in a difficult Hebrew line; evelli corresponds to the Hebrew verb "be plucked up."
- **18:17** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew "east wind" interpretively as ventus urens ("a scorching wind").
- **18:23** — Latin abutor (abutere eis) carries the strong sense "use up, consume, or deal harshly with," stronger than the Hebrew expression "deal with them."
