## Text

**35:1** The desert and the trackless land will be glad, and the wilderness will exult, and will blossom like a lily.

**35:2** Sprouting, it will sprout, and it will exult, full of gladness and praising; the glory of Lebanon has been given to it, the beauty of Carmel and Sharon; they themselves will see the glory of the Lord and the beauty of our God.

**35:3** Strengthen the slackened hands, and make firm the weak knees.

**35:4** Say to the fainthearted, "Be strong, and do not fear: look, your God will bring the vengeance of retribution; God Himself will come and save you."

**35:5** Then the eyes of the blind will be opened, and the ears of the deaf will stand open;

**35:6** then the lame one will leap like a deer, and the tongue of the mute will be opened; because waters have broken forth in the desert, and torrents in the wilderness;

**35:7** and what was parched will become a pool, and the thirsty ground springs of water. In the lairs in which dragons previously dwelt, the greenness of reed and rush will spring up.

**35:8** And a path and a way will be there, and it will be called the Holy Way; no defiled one will pass along it, and this will be a straight way for you, so that fools will not go astray along it.

**35:9** No lion will be there, and no evil beast will go up along it, nor will one be found there; and those who have been freed will walk.

**35:10** And those redeemed by the Lord will return, and they will come to Zion with praise, and everlasting gladness upon their head; they will obtain joy and gladness, and grief and groaning will flee.

## Translator Notes

- **35:4** — Vulgate "ultionem adducet retributionis" ("will bring the vengeance of retribution") links the two nouns in a genitive phrase governed by "will bring," whereas the Hebrew text places "vengeance" and "recompense of God" in parallel clauses.
- **35:6** — Vulgate "aperta erit" ("will be opened") parallels "will be opened" in 35:5, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "will shout for joy" or "will sing."
- **35:8** — Vulgate "et hæc erit vobis directa via" ("and this will be a straight way for you") gives a clear sense to a difficult Hebrew clause (literally "and it is for them; one walking the way").
