## Text

**34:1** Draw near, nations, and hear; and peoples, pay attention: let the earth hear, and its fullness; the world, and all its offspring.

**34:2** For the indignation of the Lord is upon all the nations, and fury upon their entire host; He has killed them and given them over to slaughter.

**34:3** Those killed among them will be cast out, and from their corpses a stench will rise; the mountains will dissolve from their blood.

**34:4** And all the host of the heavens will dissolve, and the heavens will be rolled up like a scroll; and all their host will fall away, just as a leaf falls from the vine and from the fig tree.

**34:5** For My sword has become drunk in heaven; look, it will descend upon Idumea, and upon the people of My slaughter, for judgment.

**34:6** The sword of the Lord is filled with blood, it is thickened with fat, from the blood of lambs and male goats, from the blood of rams full of marrow; for there is a sacrifice of the Lord in Bozrah, and a great slaughter in the land of Edom.

**34:7** And the unicorns will descend with them, and the bulls with the mighty ones; their land will become drunk with blood, and their soil with the fat of the fattened ones.

**34:8** For it is the day of the Lord's vengeance, the year of retributions for the judgment of Zion.

**34:9** And its torrents will be turned into pitch, and its soil into sulfur; and its land will become burning pitch.

**34:10** Night and day it will not be extinguished; its smoke will rise forever; from generation to generation it will be desolate; forever and ever there will be no one passing through it.

**34:11** And the pelican and the hedgehog will possess it; the ibis and the raven will dwell in it; and a measuring line will be stretched over it so that it may be reduced to nothing, and a plumb line for desolation.

**34:12** Its nobles will not be there; rather, they will call upon a king, and all its princes will become nothing.

**34:13** And thorns and nettles will spring up in its houses, and briers in its fortresses; and it will be a lair of dragons and a pasture of ostriches.

**34:14** And demons will meet onocentaurs, and the hairy one will cry out, one to another; there the lamia has lain down and has found rest for herself.

**34:15** There the hedgehog has had a burrow, and has nourished its young, and has dug around, and has kept them warm in its shadow; there the kites have gathered together, one to another.

**34:16** Search diligently in the book of the Lord, and read: Not one of them has been missing, one has not sought another; because what proceeds from my mouth, He has commanded, and His Spirit Himself has gathered them.

**34:17** And He Himself has cast the lot for them, and His hand has divided it for them by measure: they will possess it forever; for generation after generation they will dwell in it.

## Translator Notes

- **34:5** — The Vulgate uses the Greco-Latin geographical name "Idumæa" ("Idumea") here and "Edom" in 34:6, whereas the Hebrew text uses "Edom" in both verses.
- **34:6** — Vulgate "de sanguine medullatorum arietum" ("from the blood of rams full of marrow") diverges from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "from the fat of the kidneys of rams."
- **34:7** — Vulgate "unicornes" ("unicorns"), following the Septuagint "monokerotes," renders Hebrew "re'emim" (wild oxen).
- **34:14** — Vulgate "dæmonia" ("demons"), "onocentauris" ("onocentaurs," mythical half-human, half-donkey creatures), "pilosus" ("the hairy one"), and "lamia" ("the lamia," a mythical night-monster) follow Septuagint and classical Latin equivalents for Hebrew desert-creature and night-spirit terms; similarly, "draconum" ("dragons") in 34:13 and 35:7 renders Hebrew "tannim" (jackals).
- **34:16** — Vulgate "quod ex ore meo procedit, ille mandavit" ("what proceeds from my mouth, He has commanded") distinguishes the prophet's spoken message ("my mouth") from the Lord ("He") who commanded it, smoothing a grammatical shift in the Hebrew text ("My mouth has commanded").
