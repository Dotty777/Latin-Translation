## Text

**2:1** The word that Isaiah, son of Amoz, saw regarding Judah and Jerusalem.

**2:2** And it will be in the last days: the mountain of the house of the Lord will be prepared on the summit of the mountains, and it will be lifted up above the hills, and all nations will flow to it,

**2:3** and many peoples will go and say, "Come, and let us ascend to the mountain of the Lord, and to the house of the God of Jacob; and He will teach us His ways, and we will walk in His paths, for the law will go out from Zion, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem."

**2:4** And He will judge the nations, and He will reprove many peoples; and they will forge their swords into plowshares, and their spears into sickles. Nation will not lift up a sword against nation, nor will they be trained for battle anymore.

**2:5** House of Jacob, come, and let us walk in the light of the Lord.

**2:6** For You have cast away Your people, the house of Jacob, because they are filled as in former times, and they have had diviners like the Philistines, and they have clung to foreign children.

**2:7** The land is filled with silver and gold, and there is no end to their treasures.

**2:8** And their land is filled with horses, and their chariots are innumerable. And their land is filled with idols; they have worshiped the work of their own hands, which their fingers have made.

**2:9** And mankind has bowed down, and man has been humbled; therefore do not forgive them.

**2:10** Enter into the rock, and hide yourself in the dug-out ground from the face of the fear of the Lord, and from the glory of His majesty.

**2:11** The lofty eyes of mankind have been humbled, and the haughtiness of men will be bowed down; but the Lord alone will be exalted in that day.

**2:12** For the day of the Lord of hosts is against everyone proud and high, and against everyone arrogant, and he will be humbled;

**2:13** and against all the lofty and towering cedars of Lebanon, and against all the oaks of Bashan,

**2:14** and against all the high mountains, and against all the elevated hills,

**2:15** and against every high tower, and against every fortified wall,

**2:16** and against all the ships of Tarshish, and against everything that is beautiful to see,

**2:17** and the loftiness of human beings will be bowed down, and the haughtiness of men will be humbled, and the Lord alone will be lifted up in that day;

**2:18** and the idols will be completely crushed;

**2:19** and they will enter into the caves of the rocks and into the chasms of the earth, from the face of the terror of the Lord and from the glory of His majesty, when He rises up to strike the earth.

**2:20** In that day a man will cast away the idols of his silver and the images of his gold, which he had made for himself to worship: moles and bats.

**2:21** And he goes into the clefts of the rocks and into the caverns of the crags, from the face of the terror of the Lord, and from the glory of His majesty, when He rises up to strike the earth.

**2:22** Therefore, cease from man, whose breath is in his nostrils, because he himself has been considered exalted.

## Translator Notes

- **2:4** — Vulgate "conflabunt" ("will forge" or "melt down") and "exercebuntur" ("will be trained") are distinctive Latin renderings compared to the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "beat" and "learn."
- **2:6** — Vulgate "ut olim" ("as in former times") translates Hebrew "miqqedem" temporally rather than geographically ("from the east"), and "pueris alienis adhæserunt" ("clung to foreign children") contrasts with the Hebrew sense of striking bargains with foreigners. All Clementine witnesses read augeres (orthographic variant of augures, 'diviners').
- **2:10** — Vulgate "in fossa humo" ("in the dug-out ground") is a distinctive Latin rendering compared to "in the dust" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text; likewise in 2:19 and 2:21, Vulgate "percutere terram" ("to strike the earth") contrasts with Hebrew "to terrify the earth."
- **2:20** — Vulgate "talpas et vespertiliones" ("moles and bats") stands in the accusative case, serving as the object of "adoraret" or in apposition to the idols, where the Hebrew has "to the moles and to the bats."
- **2:22** — The Latin Vulgate reads a declarative statement here (quia excelsus reputatus est ipse, "because he himself has been considered exalted"), unlike the Hebrew rhetorical question ("of what account is he?").
