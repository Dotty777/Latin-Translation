## Text

**21:1** The burden of the desert of the sea. Just as whirlwinds come from the southwest, it comes from the desert, from a terrifying land.

**21:2** A harsh vision has been announced to me: he who is unbelieving acts unfaithfully, and he who is a ravager lays waste. "Go up, Elam; lay siege, Mede; I have made all its groaning cease."

**21:3** Therefore my loins are filled with pain; anguish has taken possession of me like the anguish of a woman in labor; I collapsed when I heard; I was thrown into turmoil when I saw.

**21:4** My heart has withered; darkness has stunned me; Babylon, my beloved, has been turned into a wonder for me.

**21:5** Set the table; watch in the watchtower those who are eating and drinking: rise up, princes, seize the shield!

**21:6** For the Lord said this to me: "Go and set a watchman, and whatever he sees, let him announce it."

**21:7** And he saw a chariot of two horsemen, a rider on a donkey, and a rider on a camel; and he watched diligently with intense gaze.

**21:8** And a lion cried out: "On the watchtower of the Lord I am, standing continually by day, and on my watch I am, standing all through the nights.

**21:9** Look, this man is coming, a rider of a two-horse chariot of horsemen!" And he answered and said: "She has fallen, Babylon has fallen, and all the carved images of her gods have been crushed to the ground."

**21:10** My threshing and sons of my threshing floor, what I have heard from the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, I have announced to you.

**21:11** The burden of Dumah. One calls out to me from Seir: "Watchman, what about the night? Watchman, what about the night?"

**21:12** The watchman said: "Morning comes, and night; if you inquire, inquire; turn back, come."

**21:13** The burden in Arabia. In the forest at evening you will sleep, on the paths of the Dedanites.

**21:14** Going out to meet the thirsty one, bring water, you who inhabit the land of the south; with loaves of bread, go out to meet the one who flees.

**21:15** For they have fled from before swords, from before a threatening sword, from before a stretched bow, from before a heavy battle.

**21:16** For this is what the Lord says to me: "Within one more year, as in the year of a hired worker, all the glory of Kedar will be taken away,

**21:17** and the remainder of the number of mighty archers from the sons of Kedar will be diminished," for the Lord God of Israel has spoken.

## Translator Notes

- **21:3** — The Vulgate reads corrui cum audirem, conturbatus sum cum viderem ("I collapsed when I heard; I was thrown into turmoil when I saw"), whereas the Hebrew describes being too bowed down to hear and too dismayed to see.
- **21:4** — A striking Vulgate-distinct reading: Babylon dilecta mea posita est mihi in miraculum ("Babylon, my beloved, has been turned into a wonder for me"), where the Hebrew speaks of the longed-for twilight being turned into trembling.
- **21:8** — Following the consonantal Hebrew text (aryeh), the Vulgate reads clamavit leo ("a lion cried out"), referring figuratively to the watchman rather than emending to "the lookout."
- **21:13** — The Vulgate translates in semitis ("on the paths"), taking the Hebrew noun in the sense of "paths" rather than "caravans."
