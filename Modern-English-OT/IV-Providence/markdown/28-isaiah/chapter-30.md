## Text

**30:1** "Woe, deserting sons," says the Lord, "that you make a plan, and not from Me, and weave a web, and not through My Spirit, so that you add sin upon sin,

**30:2** who walk to go down into Egypt, and have not inquired of My mouth, hoping for help in the strength of Pharaoh, and having confidence in the shadow of Egypt!"

**30:3** And the strength of Pharaoh will become shame for you, and confidence in the shadow of Egypt, disgrace.

**30:4** For your princes were in Tanis, and your messengers reached all the way to Hanes.

**30:5** All were put to shame over a people that could not profit them; they were not for help or for any usefulness, but for shame and for reproach.

**30:6** The burden of the beasts of the south. In a land of tribulation and distress, the lioness and the lion from them, the viper and the flying basilisk; carrying their riches on the shoulders of beasts and their treasures on the hump of camels to a people that will not be able to profit them.

**30:7** For Egypt will help in vain and to no purpose. Therefore I cried out concerning this: "It is mere pride; be still."

**30:8** Now therefore go in, write for it upon boxwood, and carefully inscribe it in a book, and it will be in the last day for a testimony forever.

**30:9** For it is a people provoking to anger, and lying sons, sons unwilling to hear the law of God,

**30:10** who say to those who see, "Do not see," and to those who gaze, "Do not gaze for us at the things that are right; speak pleasing things to us; see errors for us.

**30:11** Take away the way from me; turn aside the path from me; let the Holy One of Israel cease from before our face."

**30:12** Therefore this is what the Holy One of Israel says: "Because you have rejected this word, and have hoped in slander and in tumult, and have leaned upon it,

**30:13** therefore this iniquity will be for you like a falling breach, and sought out in a high wall, because suddenly, while it is not expected, its shattering will come.

**30:14** And it will be smashed to pieces just as a potter's jar is shattered with a very powerful shattering, and among its fragments there will not be found a shard in which a little fire may be carried from a burning, or a little water drawn from a pit."

**30:15** For this is what the Lord God, the Holy One of Israel, says: "If you return and are still, you will be saved; in silence and in hope will be your strength. And you were unwilling,

**30:16** and you said, 'By no means, rather we will flee to horses'; therefore you will flee; and, 'We will mount upon swift ones'; therefore those who pursue you will be swifter.

**30:17** A thousand men before the terror of one, and before the terror of five you will flee, until you are left like the mast of a ship on the summit of a mountain, and like a signal on a hill."

**30:18** Therefore the Lord waits so that He may have mercy on you, and for this reason He will be exalted, sparing you, because the Lord is a God of judgment; blessed are all who wait for Him!

**30:19** For the people of Zion will dwell in Jerusalem; weeping, you will by no means weep; showing mercy, He will have mercy on you at the voice of your cry; as soon as He hears, He will answer you.

**30:20** And the Lord will give you scant bread and scarce water, and He will no longer cause your teacher to fly away from you, and your eyes will be seeing your instructor.

**30:21** And your ears will hear a word behind your back from one warning: "This is the way; walk in it, and do not turn aside either to the right or to the left."

**30:22** And you will defile the plates of the carved images of your silver, and the garment of the molten image of your gold, and you will scatter them like the uncleanness of a menstruating woman. "Get out!" you will say to it.

**30:23** And rain will be given to your seed wherever you sow in the earth, and the bread of the produce of the earth will be most abundant and rich; in that day the lamb will graze spaciously in your possession,

**30:24** and your bulls and the colts of donkeys that work the earth will eat mixed mash, just as it has been winnowed on the threshing floor.

**30:25** And there will be upon every high mountain and upon every uplifted hill streams of running waters, in the day of the slaughter of many, when the towers fall;

**30:26** and the light of the moon will be like the light of the sun, and the light of the sun will be sevenfold, like the light of seven days, in the day in which the Lord binds up the wound of His people and heals the bruise of its blow.

**30:27** Look, the name of the Lord comes from afar; His fury is burning and heavy to bear; His lips are filled with indignation, and His tongue is like a devouring fire.

**30:28** His breath is like an overflowing torrent up to the middle of the neck, to destroy the nations into nothing, and the bridle of error that was in the jaws of the peoples.

**30:29** You will have a song like the night of a consecrated festival, and gladness of heart like one who goes with a flute to enter into the mountain of the Lord, to the Strong One of Israel.

**30:30** And the Lord will make the glory of His voice heard, and He will show the terror of His arm in the threatening of fury and the flame of devouring fire; He will strike down in a whirlwind and in hailstones.

**30:31** For at the voice of the Lord, Assyria will be terrified, struck with the rod.

**30:32** And the passing of the rod will be established, which the Lord will make rest upon it with tambourines and harps; and in extraordinary battles He will vanquish them.

**30:33** For Topheth has been prepared since yesterday, prepared by the king, deep and widened. Its fuel is fire and much wood; the breath of the Lord is like a torrent of sulfur kindling it.

## Translator Notes

- **30:4** — Tanis renders Vulgate Tani, the Greco-Latin name (via the Septuagint) for the Egyptian city called Zoan in Hebrew.
- **30:7** — Vulgate Superbia tantum est, quiesce ("It is mere pride; be still") renders Hebrew Rahab hem shavet ("Rahab who sits still") by translating the poetic name Rahab as "pride" and taking the final word as an imperative.
- **30:13** — Vulgate et requisita ("and sought out") is a distinct reading in which Jerome translated the Hebrew verb for "bulging out" through its homonym meaning "to seek."
- **30:20** — Vulgate panem arctum, et aquam brevem denotes "scant/narrow bread and scarce/short water" (Hebrew: "bread of adversity and water of affliction"), and non faciet avolare ("will not cause to fly away") renders the Hebrew verb "hide himself" via its root meaning "wing."
- **30:33** — Vulgate a rege praeparata reads "prepared by the king" (ablative of agent), whereas the Hebrew reads "prepared for the king."
