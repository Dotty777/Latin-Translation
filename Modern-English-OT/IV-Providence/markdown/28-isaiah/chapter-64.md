## Text

**64:1** As by the burning of fire they would melt, waters would burn with fire, so that Your name would be made known to Your enemies; before Your face the nations would be thrown into turmoil.

**64:2** When You do wonders, we will not endure; You came down, and before Your face the mountains flowed down.

**64:3** From ages past they have not heard, nor perceived with their ears; no eye has seen, God, apart from You, the things You have prepared for those who wait for You.

**64:4** You have met the one who is glad and does righteousness; in Your ways they will remember You. Look, You were angry, and we have sinned; in them we have always been, and we will be saved.

**64:5** And all of us have become like someone unclean, and all our righteous deeds are like the cloth of a menstruating woman; and all of us have fallen like a leaf, and our iniquities, like the wind, have carried us away.

**64:6** There is no one who calls on Your name, who rises up and takes hold of You. You have hidden Your face from us, and You have crushed us in the hand of our iniquity.

**64:7** And now, Lord, You are our Father, while we are clay; You are our potter, and all of us are the works of Your hands.

**64:8** Do not be exceedingly angry, Lord, and do not remember our iniquity any longer; now look: all of us are Your people.

**64:9** The city of Your Holy One has become deserted; Zion has become deserted; Jerusalem is desolate.

**64:10** The house of our sanctification and our glory, where our fathers praised You, has become a burning of fire, and all our desirable things have been turned into ruins.

**64:11** Will You restrain Yourself over these things, Lord? Will You keep silent and afflict us severely?

## Translator Notes

- **64:1** — In the Vulgate chapter division, 64:1 continues the sentence begun in 63:19 about the mountains melting at God's presence; Latin also reads "burning of fire" (exustio ignis) and "would burn" (arderent), where the Hebrew refers to kindling brushwood and making water boil.
- **64:2** — Vulgate uses future tenses ("When You do ... we will not endure," Cum feceris ... non sustinebimus) rather than the Hebrew past ("when you did awesome things we did not expect"); Latin sustineo can mean either "endure" or "wait for."
- **64:4** — Vulgate reads an indicative statement of hope, "and we will be saved" (et salvabimur), where the Hebrew clause is grammatically difficult and often translated as a question ("shall we be saved?").
- **64:6** — Vulgate reads allisisti ("crushed" or "dashed down"), whereas the Hebrew reads "melted us" (or "delivered us into the hand of our iniquities").
- **64:9** — Vulgate reads singular Civitas Sancti tui ("The city of Your Holy One," which can also be taken as "of Your sanctuary"), whereas the Hebrew has plural "Your holy cities."
