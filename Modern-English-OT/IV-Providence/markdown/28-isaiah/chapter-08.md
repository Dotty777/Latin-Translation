## Text

**8:1** And the Lord said to me, "Take for yourself a large book, and write in it with a man's stylus, 'Swiftly strip the spoils, quickly plunder.'"

**8:2** And I summoned for myself faithful witnesses: Uriah the priest, and Zechariah, son of Berechiah;

**8:3** and I approached the prophetess, and she conceived and gave birth to a son. And the Lord said to me, "Call his name, 'Hasten to strip the spoils; hurry to plunder,'

**8:4** for before the boy knows how to call his father and his mother, the strength of Damascus and the spoils of Samaria will be taken away before the king of the Assyrians."

**8:5** And the Lord spoke to me yet again, saying,

**8:6** "Because this people has rejected the waters of Shiloah, which flow in silence, and has rather adopted Rezin and the son of Remaliah,

**8:7** for this reason, look, the Lord will bring upon them the strong and many waters of the river, the king of the Assyrians and all his glory; and it will rise above all its channels, and it will flow over all its banks;

**8:8** and it will go through Judah, overflowing and passing on; it will come up to the neck. And the outstretching of its wings will fill the breadth of Your land, Immanuel!"

**8:9** Gather together, peoples, and be conquered; and listen, all distant lands: be strengthened, and be conquered; gird yourselves, and be conquered.

**8:10** Enter into counsel, and it will be shattered; speak a word, and it will not happen, because God is with us.

**8:11** For this is what the Lord said to me: as with a strong hand He instructed me, so that I would not go in the way of this people, saying,

**8:12** "Do not say, 'Conspiracy,' for everything that this people speaks is a conspiracy; and do not fear its fear, nor be in dread.

**8:13** Sanctify the Lord of hosts Himself; He is your dread, and He is your terror;

**8:14** and He will be a sanctification for you, yet a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense for the two houses of Israel, a snare and a ruin for those who dwell in Jerusalem.

**8:15** And very many of them will stumble, and fall, and be crushed, and be ensnared, and be captured."

**8:16** Bind up the testimony; seal the law among my disciples.

**8:17** And I will wait for the Lord, who hides His face from the house of Jacob, and I will await Him.

**8:18** Look, I and my children, whom the Lord has given to me, are for a sign and for a portent to Israel from the Lord of hosts, who dwells on Mount Zion.

**8:19** And when they say to you, "Inquire of mediums and of diviners who chirp in their incantations," should not a people inquire of its God? On behalf of the living, from the dead?

**8:20** To the law rather, and to the testimony. But if they do not speak according to this word, there will be no morning light for them.

**8:21** And he will pass through it, collapse, and grow hungry; and when he is hungry, he will grow angry. And he will curse his king and his God, and he will look upward,

**8:22** and he will gaze at the earth; and look: tribulation and darkness, dissolution and anguish, and pursuing gloom, and he will not be able to fly away from his anguish.

**8:23** At the first time the land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali was lightened, and at the last the way of the sea beyond the Jordan, of Galilee of the nations, was weighed down.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — Both here (Velociter spolia detrahe, cito praedare) and in 8:3 (Accelera spolia detrahere; Festina praedari), the Vulgate translates the Hebrew symbolic name Maher-shalal-hash-baz directly into Latin rather than transliterating it.
- **8:6** — The Vulgate reads "has rather adopted" (assumpsit magis) Rezin and the son of Remaliah, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "and rejoicing in."
- **8:21** — This Latin text reads suscipiet sursum, an orthographic variant for suspiciet sursum ("will look upward"), parallel to "gaze at the earth" in 8:22.
- **8:22** — The Vulgate joins the opening clause of Hebrew 8:23 (English 9:1) to the end of 8:22 and derives its verb from the root "to fly," reading "and he will not be able to fly away from his anguish" (et non poterit avolare de angustia sua).
- **8:23** — Following Hebrew versification (corresponding to English 9:1), the Vulgate renders the Hebrew antithesis literally through passive verbs of weight, "was lightened" (alleviata est) versus "was weighed down" (aggravata est); the Hebrew verbs can also mean "treated with contempt" and "made glorious."
