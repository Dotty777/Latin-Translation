## Text

**31:1** Woe to those who go down into Egypt for help, hoping in horses, and having confidence in chariots, because they are many, and in horsemen, because they are exceedingly strong, and have not trusted in the Holy One of Israel, and have not sought the Lord!

**31:2** Yet He Himself, being wise, has brought calamity, and has not taken away His words; and He will rise up against the house of the most wicked, and against the help of those who work iniquity.

**31:3** Egypt is man, and not God, and their horses are flesh, and not spirit; and the Lord will lower His hand, and the helper will collapse, and the one to whom help is given will fall, and together they will all be consumed.

**31:4** For this is what the Lord says to me: "Just as if a lion, and a young lion, roars over his prey, and when a multitude of shepherds comes against him, he will not be afraid at their voice and will not be terrified at their multitude: so the Lord of hosts will come down to do battle upon Mount Zion and upon its hill.

**31:5** Like flying birds, so the Lord of hosts will protect Jerusalem, protecting and delivering, passing over and saving.

**31:6** Turn back, just as you had withdrawn into the depths, sons of Israel.

**31:7** For in that day a man will cast away the idols of his silver and the idols of his gold, which your hands made for you into sin.

**31:8** And Assyria will fall by a sword not of a man, and a sword not of a human being will devour it; and it will flee, not from before the sword, and its young men will become tributaries.

**31:9** And its strength will pass away from terror, and its princes will be terrified as they flee," said the Lord, whose fire is in Zion and His furnace in Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **31:6** — Vulgate sicut in profundum recesseratis ("just as you had withdrawn into the depths") differs from the Hebrew, which reads "to Him against whom you have deeply revolted."
- **31:8** — Vulgate non a facie gladii ("not from before the sword") reflects reading the Hebrew lo ("for himself") as the homophonous negative lo ("not").
- **31:9** — Vulgate fortitudo ejus ("its strength") renders Hebrew salo ("its rock/crag") figuratively as strength.
