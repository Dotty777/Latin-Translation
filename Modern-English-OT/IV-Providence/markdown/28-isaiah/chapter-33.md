## Text

**33:1** Woe to you who plunder! Will you not also be plundered yourself? And you who spurn, will you not also be spurned yourself? When you have finished pillaging, you will be pillaged; when, exhausted, you have ceased to scorn, you will be scorned.

**33:2** Lord, have mercy on us, for we have waited for You; be our arm in the morning, and our salvation in the time of tribulation.

**33:3** At the voice of the angel the peoples fled, and at Your exaltation the nations were scattered.

**33:4** And your spoils will be gathered just as the locust is gathered, as when ditches are full of it.

**33:5** The Lord has been magnified, because He has dwelt on high; He has filled Zion with judgment and righteousness.

**33:6** And there will be faithfulness in your times: the riches of salvation are wisdom and knowledge; the fear of the Lord itself is his treasure.

**33:7** Look, the seers will cry aloud outside; the messengers of peace will weep bitterly.

**33:8** The roads have been laid waste, the one passing along the path has ceased; the treaty has been made void, he has cast aside the cities, he has had no regard for men.

**33:9** The land has mourned and languished; Lebanon has been put to shame and has become squalid; and Sharon has become like a desert, and Bashan and Carmel have been shaken.

**33:10** "Now I will rise up," says the Lord; "now I will be exalted, now I will be lifted up.

**33:11** You will conceive heat, you will give birth to stubble; your breath like fire will devour you.

**33:12** And the peoples will be like ash from a blaze; thorns gathered together will be burned up with fire.

**33:13** Hear, you who are far away, what I have done; and know, you who are near, My might."

**33:14** The sinners in Zion have been terrified; trembling has taken hold of the hypocrites. Who among you will be able to dwell with devouring fire? Who among you will dwell with everlasting burnings?

**33:15** He who walks in righteous deeds and speaks truth, who casts away greed from extortion and shakes his hands free from every bribe, who stops up his ears so as not to hear of bloodshed and shuts his eyes so as not to see evil.

**33:16** This one will dwell in the heights; fortresses of rocks are his lofty place; bread has been given to him, his waters are reliable.

**33:17** His eyes will see the king in his beauty; they will gaze upon the land from afar.

**33:18** Your heart will meditate on fear: Where is the scholar? Where is the one weighing the words of the law? Where is the teacher of little children?

**33:19** You will not see a shameless people, a people of deep speech, so that you cannot understand the eloquence of their tongue, in whom there is no wisdom.

**33:20** Look, Zion, upon the city of our solemn feast: your eyes will see Jerusalem, a wealthy dwelling, a tent that can by no means be moved; nor will its pegs ever be taken away, and none of its ropes will be broken:

**33:21** because only there is our Lord magnificent: a place of rivers, very broad and open streams; no ship of rowers will pass through it, nor will a great trireme cross over it.

**33:22** For the Lord is our judge, the Lord is our lawgiver, the Lord is our king; He Himself will save us.

**33:23** Your ropes have been loosened, and they will not prevail; your mast will be such that you cannot spread out the banner. Then the spoils of much plunder will be divided; the lame will seize the booty.

**33:24** Nor will a neighbor say, "I have grown faint"; as for the people who dwell in it, iniquity will be taken away from them.

## Translator Notes

- **33:3** — Vulgate "A voce angeli" ("At the voice of the angel") differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("at the sound of tumult"), reflecting an interpretive tradition linking this verse to the angel who struck the Assyrian camp (cf. Isa 37:36).
- **33:7** — Vulgate "videntes" ("the seers" or "those who see") renders a rare Hebrew term usually taken as "valiant ones" or "heroes," deriving it from the verb "to see"; "angeli pacis" literally means "angels/messengers of peace."
- **33:11** — Vulgate "Concipietis ardorem" ("You will conceive heat") contrasts with the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "chaff" or "dry grass" parallel to "stubble."
- **33:14** — Vulgate "de vobis" and "ex vobis" ("among you") address the sinners in Zion directly, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "among us," placing the questions in the mouths of the terrified sinners.
- **33:18** — Vulgate "legis verba ponderans" ("weighing the words of the law") and "doctor parvulorum" ("teacher of little children") are distinctive Latin renderings of Hebrew expressions referring to one who weighs (the tribute) and one who counts the towers.
