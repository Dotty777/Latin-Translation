## Text

**55:1** All you who are thirsty, come to the waters, and you who have no silver, hurry, buy, and eat; come, buy wine and milk without silver and without any exchange.

**55:2** Why do you weigh out silver for what is not bread, and your labor for what brings no satisfaction? Listen closely to Me, and eat what is good, and your soul will delight in richness.

**55:3** Incline your ear, and come to Me; listen, and your soul will live, and I will make an everlasting covenant with you, the faithful mercies of David.

**55:4** Look, I have given him as a witness to the peoples, a leader and instructor to the nations.

**55:5** Look, you will call a nation that you did not know, and nations that have not known you will run to you, because of the Lord your God, and the Holy One of Israel, for He has glorified you.

**55:6** Seek the Lord while He can be found; call upon Him while He is near.

**55:7** Let the wicked one abandon his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts, and let him return to the Lord, and He will have mercy on him; and to our God, for He is abundant in forgiving.

**55:8** "For My thoughts are not your thoughts, nor are your ways My ways," says the Lord.

**55:9** "For just as the heavens are exalted above the earth, so are My ways exalted above your ways, and My thoughts above your thoughts.

**55:10** And just as the rain and the snow come down from heaven, and do not return there again, but saturate the earth, and water it, and make it sprout, and give seed to the one who sows, and bread to the one who eats,

**55:11** so will My word be that goes out from My mouth; it will not return to Me empty, but it will do whatever I have willed, and it will prosper in the things for which I sent it."

**55:12** For you will go out in joy, and be led on in peace; the mountains and the hills will sing praise before you, and all the trees of the region will clap their hands.

**55:13** Instead of the brier the fir tree will come up, and instead of the nettle the myrtle will grow; and the Lord will be renowned as an eternal sign that will not be taken away.

## Translator Notes

- **55:4** — Vulgate praeceptorem means 'instructor' or 'teacher,' rendering the Hebrew term usually translated 'commander.'
- **55:10** — Vulgate inebriat terram, et infundit eam ('saturate the earth, and water it') differs from the Hebrew, which reads 'water the earth and make it bring forth.'
- **55:13** — Vulgate saliunca (wild spikenard, here used for a thorny brier) renders Hebrew na'atsuts ('thornbush'); erit Dominus nominatus ('the Lord will be renowned') makes 'the Lord' the subject rather than the dative 'to the Lord for a name' in the Hebrew.
