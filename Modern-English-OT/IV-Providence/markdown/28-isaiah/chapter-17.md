## Text

**17:1** The burden of Damascus. "Look, Damascus will cease to be a city, and it will be like a heap of stones in ruin.

**17:2** The abandoned cities of Aroer will be for flocks, and they will rest there, and there will be no one to frighten them.

**17:3** And help will cease from Ephraim, and the kingdom from Damascus; and the remnant of Syria will be like the glory of the sons of Israel," says the Lord of hosts.

**17:4** "And it will be in that day: the glory of Jacob will be made thin, and the fatness of his flesh will waste away.

**17:5** And it will be like one gathering what remains in the harvest, and his arm will collect the ears of grain; and it will be like one seeking ears of grain in the valley of Rephaim.

**17:6** And there will be left in it like a cluster of grapes and like the shaking of an olive tree, two or three olives at the top of a branch, or four or five on its topmost branches, its fruit," says the Lord God of Israel.

**17:7** In that day man will bow down to his Maker, and his eyes will look to the Holy One of Israel;

**17:8** and he will not bow down to the altars that his hands made; and he will not look to what his fingers fashioned, the groves and the shrines.

**17:9** In that day the cities of its strength will be abandoned like plows and grainfields that were abandoned before the face of the sons of Israel; and you will be desolate.

**17:10** Because you have forgotten God your Savior, and have not remembered your strong Helper: therefore you will plant a faithful planting, and you will sow a foreign shoot;

**17:11** on the day of your planting there will be a wild vine, and in the morning your seed will blossom; the harvest has been taken away on the day of inheritance, and one will grieve severely.

**17:12** Woe to the multitude of many peoples, like the multitude of the roaring sea; and the tumult of crowds, like the roar of many waters.

**17:13** The peoples will roar like the roar of overflowing waters, and He will rebuke it, and it will flee far away; and it will be swept away like the dust of the mountains before the face of the wind, and like a whirlwind before a storm.

**17:14** At the time of evening, and look, turmoil; in the morning, and it will not remain. This is the portion of those who laid us waste, and the lot of those who plunder us.

## Translator Notes

- **17:3** — Latin "adjutorium" ("help") differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "fortress."
- **17:6** — Latin "fructus ejus" ("its fruit") reflects reading the Hebrew as "its fruit" rather than "a fruitful tree"; in 17:5 "quod restiterit" ("what remains") similarly differs from Hebrew "standing grain."
- **17:9** — Latin "aratra, et segetes" ("plows and grainfields") is a Vulgate-distinct rendering of a difficult Hebrew phrase understood as "wooded heights and summit" (or in the Septuagint, "the Hivites and the Amorites"); in 17:8 "lucos et delubra" ("groves and shrines") renders Hebrew Asherah poles and incense altars.
- **17:11** — Vulgate-distinct readings: "labrusca" ("a wild vine") and "in die haereditatis" ("on the day of inheritance," reading Hebrew nachalah as "inheritance" rather than "sickness/grief"), following "plantationem fidelem" ("a faithful planting") in 17:10.
- **17:13** — The Latin shifts to singular pronouns ("eum ... fugiet ... rapietur," rendered "it") for the collective multitude and translates Hebrew galgal ("whirling dust/chaff") as "turbo" ("a whirlwind"); in 17:14 "in matutino" ("in the morning") contrasts with Hebrew "before morning."
