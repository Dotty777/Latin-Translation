## Text

**50:1** This is what the Lord says: "What is this bill of divorce of your mother, by which I sent her away? Or who is My creditor, to whom I sold you? Look, for your iniquities you were sold, and for your crimes I sent your mother away.

**50:2** For I came, and there was no man; I called, and there was no one to hear. Has My hand been shortened and made small, so that I cannot redeem? Or is there no power in Me to deliver? Look, at My rebuke I will make the sea a desert, I will turn the rivers into dry ground; the fish will rot without water and die of thirst.

**50:3** I will clothe the heavens with darkness, and I will make sackcloth their covering."

**50:4** The Lord has given me a learned tongue, so that I may know how to sustain with a word the one who is weary. He rouses in the morning, in the morning He rouses my ear, so that I may listen as to a master.

**50:5** The Lord God has opened my ear, and I do not contradict Him; I have not turned back.

**50:6** I gave my body to those who struck me, and my cheeks to those who plucked them; I did not turn my face away from those who reproached me and spat on me.

**50:7** The Lord God is my helper, therefore I have not been put to shame; therefore I have set my face like a very hard rock, and I know that I will not be put to shame.

**50:8** He who justifies me is near; who will contradict me? Let us stand together; who is my adversary? Let him come near to me.

**50:9** Look, the Lord God is my helper; who is there who will condemn me? Look, all of them will be worn out like a garment; the moth will eat them.

**50:10** Who among you fears the Lord, hearing the voice of His servant? He who has walked in darkness and has no light, let him hope in the name of the Lord and lean upon his God.

**50:11** Look, all you who kindle a fire, girded with flames: walk in the light of your fire and in the flames that you have set ablaze; from My hand this has happened to you: in sorrows you will sleep.

## Translator Notes

- **50:2** — Latin "Quia" ("For") introduces the verse as a declarative explanation of v. 1 rather than a question, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text opens with "Why?"
- **50:4** — Latin "ut audiam quasi magistrum" ("so that I may listen as to a master") uses the accusative "magistrum" as the object of hearing, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "to listen like those who are taught (disciples)."
- **50:6** — Latin "Corpus meum" ("my body") broadens the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "my back," and "ab increpantibus" ("from those who reproached me") uses a personal participle where the Hebrew has an abstract noun ("insults").
- **50:11** — Latin "in doloribus dormietis" literally reads "in sorrows you will sleep," rendering the Hebrew verb ("lie down") specifically as "sleep."
