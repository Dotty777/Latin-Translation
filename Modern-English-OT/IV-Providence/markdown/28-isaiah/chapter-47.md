## Text

**47:1** Come down, sit in the dust, virgin daughter Babylon; sit on the ground; there is no throne for the daughter of the Chaldeans, for no longer will you be called soft and tender.

**47:2** Take up the millstone and grind flour; lay bare your shame; uncover your shoulder, expose your legs, cross the rivers.

**47:3** Your disgrace will be exposed, and your reproach will be seen; I will take vengeance, and no man will resist Me.

**47:4** Our Redeemer, the Lord of hosts is His name, the Holy One of Israel.

**47:5** Sit silent, and enter into darkness, daughter of the Chaldeans, for you will no longer be called mistress of kingdoms.

**47:6** I was angry with My people; I defiled My inheritance and gave them into your hand; you showed them no mercies; upon the aged you made your yoke exceedingly heavy.

**47:7** And you said, "Forever I will be mistress." You did not lay these things upon your heart, nor did you recall your end.

**47:8** And now listen to these things, you delicate one, dwelling confidently, who say in your heart, "I am, and there is no one else besides me; I will not sit as a widow, and I will not know barrenness."

**47:9** These two things will come to you suddenly in one day, barrenness and widowhood: all of them have come upon you because of the multitude of your sorceries and because of the extreme hardness of your enchanters.

**47:10** And you had confidence in your wickedness, and you said, "There is no one who sees me." Your wisdom and your knowledge, this deceived you. And you said in your heart, "I am, and besides me there is no other."

**47:11** Evil will come upon you, and you will not know its rising; and calamity will rush upon you which you will not be able to expiate; misery will come upon you suddenly which you will not know.

**47:12** Stand with your enchanters and with the multitude of your sorceries, in which you have labored from your youth, if perhaps anything may profit you, or if you may be able to become stronger.

**47:13** You have grown faint in the multitude of your counsels. Let them stand and save you, the diviners of the heavens, who observed the stars and calculated the months, so that from them they might declare things to come for you.

**47:14** Look, they have become like stubble; fire has burned them up; they will not deliver their own soul from the hand of the flame; there are no glowing coals by which they may warm themselves, nor a hearth so that they may sit by it.

**47:15** So have they become for you, whatever things you had labored in: your merchants from your youth, each one in his own way, have wandered astray; there is no one to save you.

## Translator Notes

- **47:2** — Latin turpitudinem tuam ("your shame") and humerum ("shoulder") are distinctly Vulgate renderings where the Hebrew reads "your veil" and "skirt."
- **47:3** — Latin non resistet mihi homo ("no man will resist Me") differs from the Hebrew "I will not spare a man" (or "meet a man").
- **47:8** — Latin sterilitatem ("barrenness," and sterilitas in 47:9) translates a Hebrew noun meaning "bereavement" or "loss of children."
- **47:11** — Latin ortum ejus ("its rising") reflects a reading of the Hebrew term as "dawn/rising" rather than "charming it away."
