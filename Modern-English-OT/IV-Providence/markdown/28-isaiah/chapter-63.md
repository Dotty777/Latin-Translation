## Text

**63:1** "Who is this who comes from Edom, with dyed garments from Bozrah? This splendid One in His long robe, striding in the abundance of His strength?" "It is I, who speak righteousness, and am a defender to save."

**63:2** "Why then is Your robe red, and Your garments like those of treaders in a winepress?"

**63:3** "I have trodden the winepress alone, and from the nations there is no man with Me; I trod them in My fury and trampled them in My wrath; and their blood was spattered upon My garments, and I have stained all My robes.

**63:4** For the day of vengeance is in My heart; the year of My redemption has come.

**63:5** I looked around, and there was no helper; I sought, and there was no one to assist; and My arm brought salvation to Me, and My indignation itself helped Me.

**63:6** And I trampled the peoples in My fury, and made them drunk in My indignation, and dragged down their strength to the earth."

**63:7** I will remember the compassions of the Lord, the praise of the Lord for all that the Lord has rendered to us, and for the abundance of good things to the house of Israel, which He has generously bestowed upon them according to His forbearance and according to the abundance of His mercies.

**63:8** And He said, "Surely they are My people, sons who do not deny"; and He became a savior to them.

**63:9** In all their affliction He was not afflicted, and the angel of His face saved them; in His love and in His forbearance He Himself redeemed them, and carried them, and lifted them up all the days of old.

**63:10** But they themselves provoked Him to anger and afflicted the Spirit of His Holy One; and He turned into an enemy against them, and He Himself waged war against them.

**63:11** And He remembered the days of old, of Moses and of His people. Where is He who led them out of the sea with the shepherds of His flock? Where is He who placed in its midst the Spirit of His Holy One,

**63:12** who led Moses out at the right hand by the arm of His majesty; who split the waters before them to make for Himself an everlasting name;

**63:13** who led them out through the depths, like a horse in the desert that does not stumble?

**63:14** Like an animal going down into a plain, the Spirit of the Lord was its leader. Thus You led Your people to make for Yourself a name of glory.

**63:15** Look down from heaven, and see from Your holy dwelling place, and that of Your glory. Where is Your zeal and Your strength, the abundance of Your tender affections and of Your compassions? Toward me they have held themselves back.

**63:16** For You are our Father, and Abraham did not know us, and Israel did not recognize us; You, Lord, are our Father, our Redeemer; Your name is from ages past.

**63:17** Why have You made us wander, Lord, from Your ways, and hardened our heart so that we would not fear You? Return for the sake of Your servants, the tribes of Your inheritance.

**63:18** As nothing they have possessed Your holy people; our foes have trampled Your sanctuary.

**63:19** We have become as in the beginning, when You did not rule over us, nor was Your name invoked over us.

## Translator Notes

- **63:6** — Vulgate translates the final noun as "their strength" (virtutem eorum), whereas many modern translations of the Hebrew understand a homonymous root meaning "their lifeblood."
- **63:9** — Following the Hebrew Ketiv ("not") against the Qere ("to Him"), Vulgate explicitly reads "He was not afflicted" (non est tribulatus), and renders the following title literally as "the angel of His face" (angelus faciei ejus).
- **63:10** — Both here (spiritum Sancti ejus) and in 63:11 (spiritum Sancti sui), the Clementine Vulgate uses the capitalized genitive singular "of His Holy One" rather than an adjective agreeing with "Spirit" ("His Holy Spirit").
- **63:12** — Vulgate makes Moses the direct object of "led out" ("who led Moses out at the right hand by the arm of His majesty") rather than the Hebrew "who caused His glorious arm to go at the right hand of Moses"; similarly in 63:14, Vulgate follows the Septuagint with "was its leader" (ductor ejus fuit) rather than Hebrew "gave them rest."
- **63:18** — Vulgate reads "As nothing they have possessed Your holy people" (Quasi nihilum possederunt populum sanctum tuum), making the adversaries the subject and Your holy people the object, whereas the Hebrew is commonly translated "For a little while Your holy people possessed it."
