## Text

**59:1** Look, the hand of the Lord is not shortened so that it cannot save, nor is His ear weighed down so that it cannot hear.

**59:2** But your iniquities have divided between you and your God, and your sins have hidden His face from you, so that He would not hear.

**59:3** For your hands are defiled with blood, and your fingers with iniquity; your lips have spoken falsehood, and your tongue utters iniquity.

**59:4** There is no one who calls upon righteousness, nor is there anyone who judges truthfully; instead they trust in nothing and speak empty things; they have conceived trouble and given birth to iniquity.

**59:5** They have broken open the eggs of asps and woven spider webs. Whoever eats from their eggs will die, and what is warmed will burst forth into a basilisk.

**59:6** Their webs will not become clothing, nor will they cover themselves with their works; their works are useless works, and a work of iniquity is in their hands.

**59:7** Their feet run to evil, and they hurry to shed innocent blood; their thoughts are useless thoughts; devastation and destruction are in their ways.

**59:8** They have not known the way of peace, and there is no justice in their steps; their paths have been made crooked for them; everyone who treads on them is ignorant of peace.

**59:9** Because of this, justice is far removed from us, and righteousness will not overtake us. We waited for light, and look, darkness; for brightness, and we walked in darkness.

**59:10** We groped for the wall like the blind, and as if without eyes we felt our way; we stumbled at noon as if in darkness, in gloomy places like the dead.

**59:11** We will all roar like bears, and like meditating doves we will moan; we waited for justice, and there is none; for salvation, and it is far removed from us.

**59:12** For our iniquities have been multiplied before You, and our sins have answered us, because our crimes are with us, and we know our iniquities.

**59:13** Sinning and lying against the Lord, and we have turned away so that we would not go after our God, so as to speak slander and transgression; we have conceived and spoken from the heart words of falsehood.

**59:14** And justice has been turned backward, and righteousness has stood far off, because truth has fallen in the public square, and equity could not enter.

**59:15** And truth has passed into oblivion, and the one who turned away from evil lay open to plunder. And the Lord saw, and it appeared evil in His eyes, because there is no justice.

**59:16** And He saw that there is no man, and He was perplexed because there is no one who steps forward; and His own arm brought salvation for Him, and His righteousness itself upheld Him.

**59:17** He clothed Himself with righteousness as a breastplate, and a helmet of salvation on His head; He clothed Himself with garments of vengeance, and covered Himself as with a cloak of zeal,

**59:18** as for vengeance, as for repayment of indignation to His adversaries, and requital to His enemies; to the islands He will pay back in kind.

**59:19** And those from the west will fear the name of the Lord, and those from the rising of the sun His glory, when He comes like a violent river which the Spirit of the Lord drives on,

**59:20** and a Redeemer comes to Zion, and to those who return from iniquity in Jacob, says the Lord.

**59:21** "This is My covenant with them," says the Lord: "My Spirit who is in you, and My words which I have put in your mouth, will not depart from your mouth, and from the mouth of your seed, and from the mouth of the seed of your seed," says the Lord, "from now on and forevermore."

## Translator Notes

- **59:5** — Vulgate 'quod confotum est, erumpet in regulum' ("what is warmed will burst forth into a basilisk") renders the Hebrew for a crushed egg as an incubated one and translates the serpent term with 'regulus' ("little king," i.e., basilisk).
- **59:10** — Vulgate 'in caliginosis' ("in gloomy places") interprets an obscure Hebrew word that may mean "among the vigorous" or "in desolate places."
- **59:11** — Vulgate 'quasi columbæ meditantes' ("like meditating doves") reflects the primary sense of the Hebrew verb 'hagah', which can mean either "to meditate" or "to murmur/coo."
- **59:16** — Vulgate 'aporiatus est' ("He was perplexed") borrows the Greek verb 'aporeo' ("to be at a loss") to translate the Hebrew verb for being appalled or astonished.
- **59:20** — Latin redemptor ('a redeemer') is lowercase; capitalization as 'a Redeemer' reflects traditional messianic reading and is subject to edition-wide policy.
