## Text

**15:1** The burden of Moab. Because by night Ar of Moab was laid waste, it fell silent; because by night the wall of Moab was laid waste, it fell silent.

**15:2** The house went up, and Dibon to the high places, for mourning over Nebo; and over Medeba, Moab wailed; on all its heads is baldness, and every beard will be shaved.

**15:3** In its crossroads they are girded with sackcloth; on its rooftops and in its squares all wailing goes down into weeping.

**15:4** Heshbon and Elealeh will cry out, as far as Jahaz their voice has been heard; over this the armed men of Moab will wail, its soul will wail to itself.

**15:5** My heart will cry out for Moab; its bars reach as far as Zoar, a three-year-old heifer; for by the ascent of Luhith it will go up weeping, and on the road of Horonaim they will raise a cry of destruction.

**15:6** For the waters of Nimrim will be desolate, because the grass has dried up, the shoot has failed, all greenery has perished.

**15:7** According to the greatness of the work, so also is their visitation: they will lead them to the brook of the Willows.

**15:8** For the cry has gone around the border of Moab; as far as Gallim is its wailing, and as far as the well of Elim is its cry.

**15:9** For the waters of Dibon are filled with blood; for I will set additions upon Dibon: a lion for those who flee from Moab, and for the remnant of the land.

## Translator Notes

- **15:1** — Latin "murus Moab" ("the wall of Moab") translates the Hebrew place name Kir-Moab literally; "conticuit" ("fell silent") renders a Hebrew verb that can mean either "be silenced" or "be destroyed."
- **15:3** — Latin "omnis ululatus descendit in fletum" ("all wailing goes down into weeping") takes the noun "wailing" as the subject, differing from the Hebrew ("everyone wails, going down in weeping").
- **15:5** — Latin "vectes ejus" ("its bars") translates the Hebrew as "bars/bolts" rather than "fugitives," and "vitulam conternantem" ("a three-year-old heifer") renders Hebrew Eglath-shelishiyah as a descriptive phrase rather than a proper name.
- **15:7** — Vulgate-distinct reading: "Secundum magnitudinem operis, et visitatio eorum: ad torrentem Salicum ducent eos" renders the Hebrew terms for stored wealth and carrying it away as "the greatness of the work," "their visitation," and "they will lead them."
- **15:8** — The Vulgate reads the place name "Gallim" here instead of Hebrew Eglaim, and in 15:9 reads "Dibon" instead of Hebrew Dimon.
