## Text

**46:1** Bel has been broken in pieces, Nebo has been crushed; their images have become, for beasts and pack animals, your burdens of heavy weight to the point of weariness.

**46:2** They have wasted away and have been crushed together; they could not save the one carrying them, and their soul will go into captivity.

**46:3** Listen to Me, house of Jacob, and all the remnant of the house of Israel, who are carried from My belly, who are borne from My womb.

**46:4** Even to old age I am He, and even to gray hairs I will carry; I have made, and I will bear; I will carry, and I will save.

**46:5** To whom have you likened and equated Me, and compared Me and made Me similar?

**46:6** You who contribute gold from a pouch and weigh out silver on a scale, hiring a goldsmith so that he may make a god, and they fall down and worship.

**46:7** They carry it, bearing it on their shoulders and setting it in its place, and it will stand, and from its place it will not move; indeed, even when they cry out to it, it will not hear; from distress it will not save them.

**46:8** Remember this, and be put to shame; return, transgressors, to your heart.

**46:9** Recall the former age, for I am God, and there is no other god, nor is there any like Me,

**46:10** declaring from the outset the end, and from the beginning things that have not yet been done, saying, "My counsel will stand, and all My purpose will be done."

**46:11** Calling a bird from the east, and from a distant land the man of My purpose: I have spoken, and I will bring it about; I have created, and I will do it.

**46:12** Listen to Me, you hard of heart, who are far from righteousness.

**46:13** I have brought My righteousness near; it will not be far off, and My salvation will not delay. I will give salvation in Zion, and in Israel My glory.

## Translator Notes

- **46:2** — Latin Contabuerunt ("They have wasted away") and portantem ("the one carrying them") are Vulgate-distinct renderings where the Hebrew has "they stooped" and "the burden."
- **46:3** — The Vulgate uniquely includes first-person possessives in a meo utero ("from My belly") and a mea vulva ("from My womb"), whereas the Hebrew has "from the belly ... from the womb" without the pronoun.
- **46:8** — Latin confundamini ("be put to shame") differs from the Hebrew verb usually understood as "stand firm" or "take courage"; redite ... ad cor is literally "return to the heart," meaning to reflect earnestly.
- **46:11** — Latin avem renders the Hebrew "bird of prey" simply as "bird," and creavi ("I have created") translates a Hebrew verb meaning "I have formed" or "planned."
