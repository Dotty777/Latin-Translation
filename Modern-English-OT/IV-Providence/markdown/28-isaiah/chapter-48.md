## Text

**48:1** Hear these things, house of Jacob, who are called by the name of Israel and have come out from the waters of Judah; who swear by the name of the Lord and remember the God of Israel, not in truth nor in righteousness.

**48:2** For they have been called from the holy city, and they have been firmly established upon the God of Israel: the Lord of hosts is His name.

**48:3** The former things I declared from then, and they went out from My mouth, and I made them heard; suddenly I acted, and they came to pass.

**48:4** For I knew that you are hard, and your neck is an iron sinew, and your forehead is bronze.

**48:5** I told you beforehand from then; before they came, I made them known to you, so that you might not say, "My idols have done these things, and my carved images and cast images have commanded them."

**48:6** See all that you have heard; but you, have you declared them? I have made new things heard by you from then, and things have been preserved that you do not know.

**48:7** Now they are created, and not from then; and before this day you did not hear them, so that you may not say, "Look, I knew them."

**48:8** Neither did you hear, nor did you know, nor was your ear opened from then; for I know that you will surely deal treacherously, and I have called you a transgressor from the womb.

**48:9** For the sake of My name I will keep My fury far away, and with My praise I will bridle you, so that you may not perish.

**48:10** Look, I have refined you, but not like silver; I have chosen you in the furnace of poverty.

**48:11** For My own sake, for My own sake I will act, so that I may not be blasphemed; and I will not give My glory to another.

**48:12** Hear Me, Jacob, and Israel, whom I call: I am He, I am the first, and I am the last.

**48:13** My hand also founded the earth, and My right hand measured the heavens; I will call them, and they will stand together.

**48:14** Assemble, all of you, and hear: who among them has declared these things? The Lord has loved him; He will carry out His will in Babylon, and His arm will be against the Chaldeans.

**48:15** I, I have spoken, and I have called him; I have brought him, and his way has been made straight.

**48:16** Come near to Me and hear this: from the beginning I have not spoken in secret; from the time before it came to be, I was there; and now the Lord God has sent Me, and His Spirit.

**48:17** This is what the Lord, your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel, says: "I am the Lord your God, teaching you what is beneficial, guiding you in the way in which you walk.

**48:18** If only you had heeded My commandments! Your peace would have become like a river, and your righteousness like the surges of the sea;

**48:19** and your seed would have been like the sand, and the offspring of your womb like its pebbles; its name would not have perished and would not have been worn away from before My face."

**48:20** Go out from Babylon, flee from the Chaldeans; with a voice of exultation declare it, make this heard, and carry it forth all the way to the ends of the earth. Say, "The Lord has redeemed His servant Jacob."

**48:21** They did not thirst in the desert when He led them out; He brought forth water from the rock for them, and He split the rock, and the waters flowed.

**48:22** "There is no peace for the ungodly," says the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **48:6** — Latin "num annuntiastis?" expects a negative answer in the perfect tense ("have you declared them?") and repeats "ex tunc" ("from then", used throughout vv. 3, 5, 6, 7, 8), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "will you not declare it?" and "from now" here.
- **48:9** — Latin "laude mea infrenabo te" ("with My praise I will bridle you") makes Israel the direct object of God's restraint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of God restraining His anger for Israel's sake.
- **48:10** — Latin "elegi te in camino paupertatis" literally reads "I have chosen you in the furnace of poverty," preserving the primary sense of the Hebrew verb ("choose") and translating the noun as "poverty," whereas many translations from Hebrew render "tested you in the furnace of affliction."
- **48:16** — Latin "ex tempore antequam fieret" ("from the time before it came to be") emphasizes the speaker's preexistence before events unfolded, whereas the Hebrew reads "from the time it took place"; Christian interpretation traditionally identifies the speaker sent by "the Lord God ... and His Spirit" as the Messiah.
