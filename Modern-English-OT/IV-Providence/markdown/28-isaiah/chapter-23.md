## Text

**23:1** The burden of Tyre. Wail, ships of the sea, for the house from which they used to come has been laid waste; from the land of Kittim it has been revealed to them.

**23:2** Be silent, you who dwell on the island; the merchants of Sidon, crossing over the sea, have filled you.

**23:3** On many waters was the seed of the Nile; the harvest of the river was her produce, and she became the trade of the nations.

**23:4** Be ashamed, Sidon, for the sea speaks, the stronghold of the sea, saying: "I have not been in labor, and I have not given birth, and I have not reared young men, nor have I brought virgins to maturity."

**23:5** When it is heard in Egypt, they will grieve when they hear about Tyre.

**23:6** Cross over the seas; wail, you who dwell on the island!

**23:7** Is this not yours, she who boasted from earliest days in her antiquity? Her own feet will lead her far away to sojourn.

**23:8** Who has planned this against Tyre, once crowned, whose merchants were princes, her traders the renowned of the earth?

**23:9** The Lord of hosts has planned this, to pull down the pride of all glory and to bring to disgrace all the renowned of the earth.

**23:10** Pass through your land like a river, daughter of the sea! There is no longer a belt for you.

**23:11** He stretched out His hand over the sea; He threw kingdoms into turmoil. The Lord gave a command against Canaan to crush her strong ones,

**23:12** and He said: "You will no longer continue to boast, virgin daughter of Sidon, enduring oppression; rising up, cross over to Kittim; even there, there will be no rest for you."

**23:13** Look, the land of the Chaldeans: such a people did not exist; Assyria founded it; they led away its strong men into captivity, they undermined its houses, they turned it into a ruin.

**23:14** Wail, ships of the sea, for your stronghold has been devastated.

**23:15** And it will be on that day: you will be in oblivion, Tyre, for seventy years, like the days of one king; yet after seventy years, it will be for Tyre like the song of a prostitute:

**23:16** "Take up a harp, go around the city, prostitute given over to oblivion; sing well, repeat the song often, so that there may be remembrance of you."

**23:17** And it will be after seventy years: the Lord will visit Tyre, and He will bring her back to her wages, and she will again prostitute herself with all the kingdoms of the earth upon the face of the earth;

**23:18** and her merchandise and her wages will be sanctified to the Lord; they will not be stored up nor laid away, for her merchandise will belong to those who dwell before the Lord, so that they may eat to fullness and be clothed until old age.

## Translator Notes

- **23:1** — Throughout this oracle (23:1, 6, 10, 14), the Vulgate renders Hebrew Tarshish as mare ("sea" or "seas"), and here reads domus unde venire consueverant ("the house from which they used to come") where the Hebrew says "without house or entering."
- **23:3** — The Vulgate explicitly identifies Hebrew Shihor as Nili ("the Nile").
- **23:13** — A notable Vulgate-distinct reading: Assur fundavit eam; in captivitatem traduxerunt robustos ejus ("Assyria founded it; they led away its strong men into captivity"), where the Hebrew speaks of Assyria assigning the land to desert creatures and erecting siege towers.
- **23:18** — The Vulgate translates usque ad vetustatem ("until old age"), taking the Hebrew adjective for "stately" or "fine" clothing in the temporal sense of lasting into old age.
