## Text

**57:1** The righteous one perishes, and there is no one who reflects in his heart; and men of mercy are gathered away, because there is no one who understands: for the righteous one has been gathered away from the face of evil.

**57:2** Let peace come; let him rest in his bed who has walked in his uprightness.

**57:3** But you, draw near here, sons of a female diviner, offspring of an adulterer and a prostitute.

**57:4** At whom have you mocked? At whom have you opened wide your mouth and thrust out your tongue? Are you not wicked sons, lying offspring,

**57:5** who console yourselves in gods under every leafy tree, sacrificing little children in the streambeds, beneath the overhanging rocks?

**57:6** Among the portions of the streambed is your portion; this is your lot: to them also you poured out a drink offering, you offered a sacrifice. Will I not be indignant over these things?

**57:7** On a high and lofty mountain you placed your bed, and there you went up to offer sacrifices.

**57:8** And behind the door and behind the doorpost you placed your memorial. Because beside Me you uncovered yourself, and received an adulterer, you widened your bed, and made a covenant with them; you loved their couch with an open hand.

**57:9** And you adorned yourself for the king with ointment, and multiplied your perfumes. You sent your envoys far away, and you were humbled all the way down to the underworld.

**57:10** In the great extent of your way you grew weary; you did not say, "I will rest." You found the life of your hand; therefore you did not plead.

**57:11** For whom were you anxious and afraid, that you lied, and did not remember Me, nor think of it in your heart? Because I am silent and as though not seeing, you have forgotten Me.

**57:12** I will declare your righteousness, and your works will not benefit you.

**57:13** When you cry out, let your gathered ones rescue you; and the wind will carry them all away, a breeze will lift them off. But the one who has trust in Me will inherit the land, and will possess My holy mountain.

**57:14** And I will say, "Make a way, prepare a road; turn aside from the path, remove the stumbling blocks from the way of My people."

**57:15** For this is what the High and Exalted One says, who inhabits eternity, and His name is holy, dwelling in the high and holy place and with a contrite and humble spirit, so that He may revive the spirit of the humble and revive the heart of the contrite:

**57:16** "For I will not contend forever, nor will I be angry to the end, because a spirit will go out from My presence, and I Myself will make breaths.

**57:17** Because of the iniquity of his greed I was angry, and I struck him. I hid My face from you, and I was indignant; and he went away wandering in the way of his heart.

**57:18** I have seen his ways, and I have healed him; and I have led him back, and I have restored consolations to him and to his mourners.

**57:19** I have created the fruit of the lips, peace; peace to the one who is far off and to the one who is near," said the Lord, "and I have healed him."

**57:20** But the wicked are like a churning sea, which cannot rest, and its waves overflow into trampling and mud.

**57:21** "There is no peace for the wicked," says the Lord God.

## Translator Notes

- **57:5** — Vulgate qui consolamini in diis ('who console yourselves in gods') diverges from the Hebrew, commonly translated 'who burn with lust among the oaks.'
- **57:8** — Vulgate juxta me ('beside Me'), suscepisti adulterum ('received an adulterer'), and manu aperta ('with an open hand') are distinct Vulgate readings differing from the Masoretic Text ('away from Me,' 'went up,' and 'looked on the hand/place').
- **57:10** — Vulgate Quiescam ('I will rest') and non rogasti ('you did not plead') differ from the Hebrew ('It is hopeless' and 'you did not grow faint'), while Vitam manus tuae ('the life of your hand') translates the Hebrew idiom for renewed strength literally.
- **57:16** — Vulgate egredietur ('will go out') and future faciam ('I will make') follow the Septuagint tradition rather than the Hebrew ('would grow faint' and past 'I have made').
- **57:20** — Vulgate in conculcationem ('into trampling') reflects Jerome's derivation of the Hebrew word for 'mire' (refesh) from the verb meaning 'to tread down.'
