## Text

**38:1** In those days Hezekiah fell sick to the point of death; and Isaiah, son of Amoz, the prophet, came in to him and said to him, "This is what the Lord says: 'Put your house in order, because you will die, and you will not live.'"

**38:2** And Hezekiah turned his face toward the wall and prayed to the Lord,

**38:3** and said, "I plead, Lord, remember, please, how I have walked before You in truth and with a perfect heart, and have done what is good in Your eyes." And Hezekiah wept with a great weeping.

**38:4** And the word of the Lord came to Isaiah, saying,

**38:5** "Go and say to Hezekiah, 'This is what the Lord God of your father David says: I have heard your prayer and have seen your tears; look, I will add fifteen years to your days,

**38:6** and I will rescue you and this city from the hand of the king of the Assyrians, and I will protect it.

**38:7** Now this will be the sign to you from the Lord that the Lord will perform this word that He has spoken:

**38:8** look, I will make the shadow of the lines along which it had gone down on the sundial of Ahaz in the sun return backward ten lines.'" And the sun went back ten lines along the steps that it had gone down.

**38:9** The writing of Hezekiah, king of Judah, when he had been sick and had recovered from his illness.

**38:10** I said in the middle of my days, "I will go to the gates of the underworld; I have sought the remainder of my years."

**38:11** I said, "I will not see the Lord God in the land of the living; I will look upon man no more, nor upon the inhabitant of rest."

**38:12** My generation has been taken away and rolled up from me like a tent of shepherds. My life has been cut short as by a weaver; while I was still beginning, He cut me down; from morning until evening You will bring me to an end.

**38:13** I hoped until morning; like a lion, so He crushed all my bones; from morning until evening You will bring me to an end.

**38:14** Like a young swallow, so I will cry out; I will meditate like a dove. My eyes have grown weak, looking up on high. Lord, I suffer violence; answer for me.

**38:15** What will I say, or what will He answer me, since He Himself has done it? I will recall to You all my years in the bitterness of my soul.

**38:16** Lord, if life is lived like this, and in such things is the life of my spirit, You will discipline me and give me life.

**38:17** Look, in peace my bitterness is most bitter. Yet You have rescued my soul so that it would not perish; You have thrown all my sins behind Your back.

**38:18** For the underworld will not give thanks to You, nor will death praise You; those who go down into the pit will not wait for Your truth.

**38:19** The living, the living one himself will give thanks to You, just as I also do today; a father will make Your truth known to his children.

**38:20** Lord, save me! And we will sing our psalms all the days of our life in the house of the Lord.

**38:21** And Isaiah ordered that they take a lump of figs and apply it as a poultice over the wound, so that he would be healed.

**38:22** And Hezekiah said, "What will be the sign that I will go up to the house of the Lord?"

## Translator Notes

- **38:8** — The Vulgate explicitly renders Hebrew ma'alot ("steps/degrees") as horologio ("sundial") and lineis ("lines"), before using gradus ("steps") in the final clause.
- **38:10** — Latin quaesivi ("I have sought") is a Vulgate-distinct rendering where the Masoretic Text reads "I am deprived of / consigned."
- **38:11** — Latin habitatorem quietis ("inhabitant of rest") reflects Jerome's rendering of Hebrew chadel as "rest" or "cessation."
- **38:14** — Latin meditabor ("I will meditate") translates Hebrew hagah by its primary sense of meditating/murmuring rather than "moan" or "coo."
