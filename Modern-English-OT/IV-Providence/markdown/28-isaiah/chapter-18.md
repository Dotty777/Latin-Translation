## Text

**18:1** Woe to the land, the cymbal of wings, which is beyond the rivers of Ethiopia,

**18:2** which sends envoys by sea, and in vessels of papyrus upon the waters. Go, swift messengers, to a nation uprooted and torn to pieces, to a terrifying people, after whom there is no other, to a nation waiting and trampled down, whose land the rivers have plundered.

**18:3** All you inhabitants of the world, who dwell on the earth, when a standard is lifted up on the mountains, you will see, and you will hear the blast of the trumpet.

**18:4** For this is what the Lord says to me: "I will be still and look on in My place, as the midday light is clear, and as a cloud of dew in the day of harvest."

**18:5** For before the harvest it has blossomed completely, and unripe perfection will sprout; and its shoots will be cut off with pruning hooks, and whatever has been left behind will be severed and shaken off.

**18:6** And they will be left together to the birds of the mountains and to the beasts of the earth; and in perpetual summer the birds will be upon it, and all the beasts of the earth will spend the winter upon it.

**18:7** At that time a gift will be brought to the Lord of hosts from a people pulled apart and torn to pieces, from a terrifying people, after whom there has been no other, from a nation waiting, waiting and trampled down, whose land the rivers have plundered, to the place of the name of the Lord of hosts, Mount Zion.

## Translator Notes

- **18:1** — Latin "cymbalo alarum" ("cymbal of wings") is a distinctive Vulgate rendering of a rare Hebrew phrase often translated "whirring wings" or "shadowing wings."
- **18:2** — The Vulgate renders the difficult Hebrew descriptions of the Ethiopian nation as "uprooted and torn to pieces," "after whom there is no other," and "waiting and trampled down" (repeated in 18:7), where modern translations from the Masoretic Text often read "tall and smooth," "feared near and far," and "mighty and conquering."
- **18:4** — The Vulgate reads "as the midday light is clear" and "in the day of harvest" (in die messis), whereas the Masoretic Text has "like clear heat in sunshine" and "in the heat of harvest."
- **18:5** — Latin "immatura perfectio" ("unripe perfection") literally renders a Hebrew expression for a ripening sour grape; similarly in 18:6 the Vulgate reads "aestate perpetua" ("in perpetual summer").
