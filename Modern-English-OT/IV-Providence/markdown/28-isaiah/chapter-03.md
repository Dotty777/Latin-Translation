## Text

**3:1** For look, the Sovereign, the Lord of hosts, will take away from Jerusalem and from Judah the sturdy and the mighty, every support of bread and every support of water;

**3:2** the mighty man and the fighting man, the judge and the prophet, and the diviner and the elder;

**3:3** the leader over fifty, and the one honorable in appearance, and the counselor, and the wise among master builders, and the discerning in mystical speech.

**3:4** And I will give boys as their leaders, and the effeminate will rule over them;

**3:5** and the people will fall upon one another, man against man, and each one against his neighbor; the boy will raise a tumult against the elder, and the ignoble against the noble.

**3:6** For a man will take hold of his brother, one of his father's household: "You have clothing; be our leader, and let this ruin be under your hand."

**3:7** He will answer on that day, saying, "I am not a physician, and in my house there is neither bread nor clothing; do not appoint me leader of the people."

**3:8** For Jerusalem has collapsed, and Judah has fallen, because their tongue and their doings are against the Lord, to provoke the eyes of His majesty.

**3:9** The expression of their face has answered them; and they have proclaimed their sin like Sodom, nor have they hidden it. Woe to their soul, for evils have been repaid to them!

**3:10** Say to the righteous man that it is well, for he will eat the fruit of his doings.

**3:11** Woe to the wicked! It is for evil, because the repayment of his hands will be done to him.

**3:12** Their exactors have plundered My people, and women have ruled over them. My people, those who call you blessed, they themselves deceive you and destroy the way of your steps.

**3:13** The Lord stands to judge, and stands to judge the peoples.

**3:14** The Lord will come to judgment with the elders of His people and its leaders: "For you have consumed the vineyard, and the plunder of the poor is in your house.

**3:15** Why do you crush My people and grind the faces of the poor?" says the Lord God of hosts.

**3:16** And the Lord said, "Because the daughters of Zion are lifted up and have walked with outstretched neck, and went along with winks of their eyes, and clapped, walked with their feet, and proceeded with a measured step;

**3:17** the Lord will make bald the crown of the head of the daughters of Zion, and the Lord will lay bare their hair."

**3:18** On that day the Lord will take away the ornament of footwear,

**3:19** and the crescents, and the collars, and the necklaces, and the bracelets, and the turbans,

**3:20** and the hairpins, and the anklets, and the little chains, and the scent bottles, and the earrings,

**3:21** and the rings, and the gems hanging on the forehead,

**3:22** and the changes of clothing, and the short cloaks, and the linen garments, and the pins,

**3:23** and the mirrors, and the fine linens, and the headbands, and the summer veils.

**3:24** And instead of sweet fragrance there will be a stench, and instead of a sash, a rope, and instead of curled hair, baldness, and instead of a breast-band, sackcloth.

**3:25** Your most handsome men also will fall by the sword, and your mighty men in battle.

**3:26** And her gates will grieve and mourn, and she will sit desolate on the ground.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — Latin 'validum et fortem' ('the sturdy and the mighty') renders the Hebrew wordplay for 'support and staff' concretely as strong persons, before continuing with 'every support of bread and every support of water.'
- **3:4** — Latin 'effeminati' ('the effeminate') is a distinctly Vulgate rendering of a rare Hebrew term meaning 'capricious ones' or 'babes.'
- **3:12** — Latin 'qui te beatum dicunt' ('those who call you blessed') takes the Hebrew verb in its sense 'call happy' rather than 'guide.'
- **3:17** — Latin 'crinem earum nudabit' ('will lay bare their hair') is a distinctly Vulgate rendering where the Hebrew word is variously understood as 'foreheads' or 'secret parts.'
- **3:24** — The Vulgate omits the Hebrew closing clause 'branding instead of beauty,' carrying the word for 'beauty' into 3:25 as 'Pulcherrimi' ('your most handsome men').
