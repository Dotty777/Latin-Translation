## Text

**13:1** The burden of Babylon, which Isaiah, the son of Amoz, saw.

**13:2** Upon a dark mountain lift up a banner; raise the voice, lift up the hand, and let the leaders enter the gates.

**13:3** I have commanded My sanctified ones, and I have summoned My mighty ones in My wrath, exulting in My glory.

**13:4** The sound of a multitude in the mountains, like that of thronging peoples; the sound of the roar of kings, of gathered nations. The Lord of hosts has commanded the army of war,

**13:5** those coming from a distant land, from the extremity of heaven; the Lord and the vessels of His fury, so that He may destroy the whole earth.

**13:6** Wail, because the day of the Lord is near; it will come like devastation from the Lord.

**13:7** Because of this all hands will go limp, and every human heart will waste away,

**13:8** and be crushed. Pangs and pains will take hold; they will be in pain like a woman in labor; each one will be stunned at his neighbor; their expressions will be scorched faces.

**13:9** Look, the day of the Lord will come, cruel and full of indignation, and of wrath and fury, to make the earth a desolation and to crush its sinners out of it.

**13:10** For the stars of heaven and their brightness will not spread out their light; the sun has been darkened at its rising, and the moon will not shine in its light.

**13:11** And I will punish the evils of the world, and the wicked for their iniquity; and I will make the pride of the unfaithful cease, and I will humble the arrogance of the mighty.

**13:12** A man will be more precious than gold, and a human being than pure refined gold.

**13:13** For this reason I will throw heaven into turmoil, and the earth will be moved from its place, because of the indignation of the Lord of hosts, and because of the day of the wrath of His fury.

**13:14** And it will be like a fleeing doe, and like a sheep, and there will be no one to gather it. Each one will turn back to his own people, and they will each flee to their own land.

**13:15** Everyone who is found will be killed, and everyone who comes up will fall by the sword;

**13:16** their infants will be dashed to pieces before their eyes, their houses will be plundered, and their wives will be violated.

**13:17** Look, I will rouse the Medes against them, who do not seek silver, nor desire gold;

**13:18** but with arrows they will kill the little ones, and they will have no mercy on nursing wombs, and their eye will not spare the children.

**13:19** And that Babylon, glorious among kingdoms, the renowned pride of the Chaldeans, will be just as when the Lord overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah.

**13:20** It will not be inhabited even to the end, and it will not be founded even to generation and generation; nor will the Arab pitch tents there, nor will shepherds rest there.

**13:21** Instead, wild beasts will rest there, and their houses will be filled with dragons, and ostriches will dwell there, and hairy creatures will dance there;

**13:22** and owls will answer there in its halls, and sirens in the temples of pleasure.

## Translator Notes

- **13:1** — Latin onus ("burden," also in 14:28) translates Hebrew massa ("oracle/pronouncement"); Vulgate spells Isaiah's father Amos (following the Greek) for Hebrew Amoz, distinct from the prophet Amos.
- **13:2** — Vulgate reads montem caliginosum ("dark" or "misty mountain"), connecting the Hebrew word with twilight/darkness rather than "bare mountain."
- **13:12** — Vulgate mundo obrizo ("pure refined gold") uses a technical Latin term for fire-tested gold where the Hebrew specifies "gold of Ophir."
- **13:18** — Vulgate lactantibus uteris literally reads "nursing wombs," corresponding to Hebrew "fruit of the womb."
- **13:21** — In 13:21-22, the Vulgate names the desert creatures draconibus ("dragons"), struthiones ("ostriches"), pilosi ("hairy creatures"), ululae ("owls"), and sirenes ("sirens," following the Septuagint), and renders the palaces as delubris ("temples/shrines").
