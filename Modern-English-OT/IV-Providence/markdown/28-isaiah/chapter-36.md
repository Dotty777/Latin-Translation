## Text

**36:1** And it happened in the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah that Sennacherib, king of the Assyrians, went up against all the fortified cities of Judah and captured them.

**36:2** And the king of the Assyrians sent Rabshakeh from Lachish to Jerusalem, to King Hezekiah, with a heavy force; and he stood at the aqueduct of the upper pool, on the road of the Fuller's Field.

**36:3** And Eliakim, the son of Hilkiah, who was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah, the son of Asaph, in charge of the records, went out to him.

**36:4** And Rabshakeh said to them, "Tell Hezekiah, 'This is what the great king, the king of the Assyrians, says: What is this confidence in which you trust?

**36:5** Or with what counsel or strength do you plan to rebel? In whom do you have confidence, that you have withdrawn from me?

**36:6** Look, you trust in this broken reed staff, in Egypt; if a man leans on it, it will go into his hand and pierce it. So is Pharaoh, king of Egypt, to all who trust in him.

**36:7** But if you answer me, "We trust in the Lord our God," is He not the one whose high places and altars Hezekiah took away, and said to Judah and Jerusalem, "Before this altar you shall worship"?

**36:8** And now surrender yourself to my master, the king of the Assyrians, and I will give you two thousand horses, and you will not be able on your own to supply riders for them.

**36:9** And how will you withstand the face of a judge of one place among the lesser servants of my master? But if you trust in Egypt, in chariots and in horsemen,

**36:10** and now, have I come up without the Lord against this land to destroy it? The Lord said to me, "Go up against this land and destroy it."'"

**36:11** And Eliakim, Shebna, and Joah said to Rabshakeh, "Speak to your servants in the Syrian language, for we understand; do not speak to us in Judean in the ears of the people who are on the wall."

**36:12** And Rabshakeh said to them, "Did my master send me to your master and to you to speak all these words, and not rather to the men who sit on the wall, that they may eat their own dung and drink the urine of their feet with you?"

**36:13** And Rabshakeh stood and cried out with a loud voice in Judean and said, "Hear the words of the great king, the king of the Assyrians!

**36:14** This is what the king says: Do not let Hezekiah mislead you, for he will not be able to rescue you.

**36:15** And do not let Hezekiah give you confidence in the Lord, saying, 'The Lord will certainly deliver us; this city will not be given into the hand of the king of the Assyrians.'

**36:16** Do not listen to Hezekiah, for this is what the king of the Assyrians says: Make a blessing with me and come out to me, and eat, each one his own vine and each one his own fig tree, and drink, each one the water of his own cistern,

**36:17** until I come and take you away to a land that is like your land, a land of grain and wine, a land of bread and vineyards.

**36:18** And do not let Hezekiah trouble you, saying, 'The Lord will deliver us.' Have the gods of the nations each delivered his own land from the hand of the king of the Assyrians?

**36:19** Where is the god of Hamath and Arpad? Where is the god of Sepharvaim? Have they delivered Samaria from my hand?

**36:20** Who is there among all the gods of these lands who has rescued his land from my hand, that the Lord should rescue Jerusalem from my hand?"

**36:21** And they kept silent and did not answer him a word. For the king had commanded, saying, "Do not answer him."

**36:22** And Eliakim, the son of Hilkiah, who was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah, the son of Asaph, in charge of the records, came in to Hezekiah with torn garments and reported to him the words of Rabshakeh.

## Translator Notes

- **36:5** — The Vulgate asks with what counsel or strength Hezekiah plans to rebel (quo consilio vel fortitudine rebellare disponis) and states that he has withdrawn (recessisti) from Sennacherib, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text says counsel and strength for war are mere words of the lips and asks why he has rebelled.
- **36:8** — Latin trade te ("surrender yourself") reflects the Vulgate's rendering of the Hebrew idiom for making a pledge or wager, and the closing clause (nec poteris) is framed as a negative statement rather than a conditional ("if you are able").
- **36:16** — Latin Facite mecum benedictionem ("Make a blessing with me") translates the Hebrew idiom literally; in context it means to come to terms or make peace.
- **36:19** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text (which has plural "gods"), the Vulgate reads singular deus ("god") in the first two questions before shifting to a plural verb (liberaverunt, "have they delivered").
