## Text

**37:1** And it happened, when King Hezekiah heard it, that he tore his garments and wrapped himself in sackcloth and entered the house of the Lord.

**37:2** And he sent Eliakim, who was over the household, and Shebna the scribe, and the elders of the priests, covered with sackcloth, to Isaiah, the son of Amoz, the prophet,

**37:3** and they said to him, "This is what Hezekiah says: This day is a day of distress, and of rebuke, and of blasphemy; for children have come up to birth, and there is no strength to give birth.

**37:4** Perhaps the Lord your God will hear the words of Rabshakeh, whom the king of the Assyrians, his master, sent to blaspheme the living God and to revile Him with the words that the Lord your God has heard; therefore lift up a prayer for the remnant that is found."

**37:5** And the servants of King Hezekiah came to Isaiah.

**37:6** And Isaiah said to them, "This you shall say to your master: 'This is what the Lord says: Do not be afraid because of the words that you have heard, with which the young men of the king of the Assyrians have blasphemed Me.

**37:7** Look, I will give him a spirit, and he will hear a report, and he will return to his own land, and I will make him fall by the sword in his own land.'"

**37:8** Now Rabshakeh returned and found the king of the Assyrians fighting against Libnah; for he had heard that he had departed from Lachish.

**37:9** And he heard them saying concerning Tirhakah, king of Ethiopia, "He has come out to fight against you." When he heard this, he sent messengers to Hezekiah, saying,

**37:10** "This you shall say to Hezekiah, king of Judah, speaking: 'Do not let your God, in whom you trust, deceive you, saying, "Jerusalem will not be given into the hand of the king of the Assyrians."

**37:11** Look, you have heard all that the kings of the Assyrians have done to all the lands, which they overthrew; and will you be able to be delivered?

**37:12** Did the gods of the nations rescue those whom my fathers overthrew: Gozan, and Haran, and Rezeph, and the sons of Eden who were in Telassar?

**37:13** Where is the king of Hamath, and the king of Arpad, and the king of the city of Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivvah?'"

**37:14** And Hezekiah took the letters from the hand of the messengers and read them, and went up to the house of the Lord, and Hezekiah spread them out before the Lord;

**37:15** and Hezekiah prayed to the Lord, saying,

**37:16** "Lord of hosts, God of Israel, You who sit above the cherubim, You alone are God of all the kingdoms of the earth; You made heaven and earth.

**37:17** Incline Your ear, Lord, and hear; open Your eyes, Lord, and see; and hear all the words of Sennacherib, which he sent to blaspheme the living God.

**37:18** For truly, Lord, the kings of the Assyrians have made desolate the lands and their regions,

**37:19** and have given their gods to the fire; for they were not gods, but the works of men's hands, wood and stone, and they crushed them.

**37:20** And now, Lord our God, save us from his hand, and let all the kingdoms of the earth know that You alone are the Lord."

**37:21** And Isaiah, the son of Amoz, sent to Hezekiah, saying, "This is what the Lord God of Israel says: Because of what you have asked Me concerning Sennacherib, king of the Assyrians,

**37:22** this is the word that the Lord has spoken against him: 'The virgin daughter of Zion has despised you and mocked you; the daughter of Jerusalem has shaken her head behind you.

**37:23** Whom have you reviled, and whom have you blasphemed? And against whom have you raised your voice and lifted up the haughtiness of your eyes? Against the Holy One of Israel.

**37:24** By the hand of your servants you have reviled the Lord, and you have said, "With the multitude of my chariots I have gone up the height of the mountains, the ridges of Lebanon; and I will cut down the loftiest of its cedars and its choice firs, and I will enter the height of its summit, the forest of its Carmel.

**37:25** I dug and drank water, and with the sole of my foot I dried up all the streams of the embankments."

**37:26** Have you not heard what I did for it long ago? From days of old I formed it, and now I have brought it about, and it has come to be for the uprooting of warring hills and of fortified cities.

**37:27** Their inhabitants, with shortened hand, trembled and were confounded. They became like the hay of the field, and the grass of the pasture, and the plant of the rooftops, which dried up before it ripened.

**37:28** I know your dwelling, and your going out, and your coming in, and your madness against Me.

**37:29** Because you raged against Me, your pride has come up into My ears. Therefore I will put a ring in your nostrils and a bridle in your lips, and I will lead you back into the way by which you came.'

**37:30** Now for you, this will be the sign: eat this year what grows of its own accord, and in the second year feed on fruit; and in the third year sow and reap, and plant vineyards and eat their fruit.

**37:31** And that which has been saved of the house of Judah, and that which remains, will put forth root downward and bear fruit upward;

**37:32** for from Jerusalem a remnant will go out, and salvation from Mount Zion; the zeal of the Lord of hosts will do this.

**37:33** Therefore, this is what the Lord says concerning the king of the Assyrians: He will not enter this city, and he will not shoot an arrow there, and a shield will not seize it, and he will not throw up an embankment around it.

**37:34** By the way by which he came, by it he will return, and he will not enter this city, says the Lord.

**37:35** And I will protect this city to save it, for My sake and for the sake of David, My servant."

**37:36** Then the angel of the Lord went out and struck down in the camp of the Assyrians one hundred eighty-five thousand. And they rose in the morning, and look, they were all corpses of the dead.

**37:37** And Sennacherib, king of the Assyrians, set out and went away and returned, and lived in Nineveh.

**37:38** And it happened, while he was worshiping Nisroch his god in the temple, that Adrammelech and Sharezer, his sons, struck him down with the sword and fled into the land of Ararat; and Esarhaddon, his son, reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **37:4** — In the Vulgate, exprobrandum ("to revile") is paired with ad blasphemandum ("to blaspheme") as Rabshakeh's act, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text makes the Lord the subject who will rebuke the words He has heard.
- **37:24** — The Vulgate treats the Hebrew common noun karmel ("orchard" or "lush woodland") as a proper name, Carmeli ("its Carmel").
- **37:25** — Latin aggerum ("of the embankments") reflects Jerome's reading of Hebrew masor as ramparts or siege-works rather than a poetic name for Egypt, and exsiccavi uses the past tense ("I dried up").
- **37:26** — Latin in eradicationem collium compugnantium ("for the uprooting of warring hills") is a distinctive Vulgate rendering where the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of turning fortified cities into heaps of ruins.
- **37:30** — Latin pomis vescere ("feed on fruit") interprets the rare Hebrew term for second-year volunteer growth as orchard fruit.
