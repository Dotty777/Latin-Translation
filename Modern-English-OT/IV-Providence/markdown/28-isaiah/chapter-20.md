## Text

**20:1** In the year in which Thathan entered Ashdod, when Sargon, king of the Assyrians, had sent him, and had fought against Ashdod and captured it,

**20:2** at that time the Lord spoke by the hand of Isaiah, son of Amoz, saying, "Go, and untie the sackcloth from your loins, and remove your sandals from your feet." And he did so, going naked and barefoot.

**20:3** And the Lord said, "Just as My servant Isaiah has walked naked and barefoot, he will be a sign and a wonder of three years against Egypt and against Ethiopia;

**20:4** so the king of the Assyrians will drive the captives of Egypt and the exiles of Ethiopia, young and old, naked and barefoot, with buttocks uncovered, to the disgrace of Egypt."

**20:5** And they will fear and be put to shame because of Ethiopia, their hope, and because of Egypt, their glory.

**20:6** And the inhabitant of this island will say in that day, "Look, this was our hope, to whom we fled for help, that they might deliver us from the presence of the king of the Assyrians; and how will we ourselves be able to escape?"

## Translator Notes

- **20:1** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "Thathan" (corresponding to Hebrew Tartan, an Assyrian military title meaning commander-in-chief) and "Azotum" (Azotus, the Greco-Latin name for Ashdod).
- **20:3** — The Vulgate connects "of three years" (trium annorum) to the future "sign and wonder" rather than to the duration of Isaiah's walking naked and barefoot.
- **20:6** — Latin "insulae hujus" ("of this island") is a literal rendering of a Hebrew term that can denote either an island or a coastal region ("coastland").
