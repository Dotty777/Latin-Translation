## Text

**7:1** And it happened in the days of Ahaz, son of Jotham, son of Uzziah, king of Judah, that Rezin, king of Syria, and Pekah, son of Remaliah, king of Israel, went up to Jerusalem to make war against it, and they were not able

**7:2** to conquer it. And they reported to the house of David, saying, "Syria has rested upon Ephraim." And his heart was shaken, and the heart of his people, just as the trees of the forests are moved before the face of the wind.

**7:3** And the Lord said to Isaiah, "Go out to meet Ahaz, you and he who is left behind, Jashub, your son, to the end of the aqueduct of the upper pool, on the road of the fuller's field,

**7:4** and you will say to him, 'See that you keep quiet; do not fear, and do not let your heart be terrified by these two tails of smoking firebrands, at the anger of the fury of Rezin, king of Syria, and of the son of Remaliah,

**7:5** because Syria has entered into counsel against you for evil, Ephraim, and the son of Remaliah, saying,

**7:6** "Let us go up to Judah, and let us rouse it, and let us tear it away to ourselves, and let us set the son of Tabeel as king in its midst."

**7:7** This is what the Lord God says: "It will not stand, and this will not come to be;

**7:8** rather, the head of Syria is Damascus, and the head of Damascus is Rezin; and sixty-five years more, and Ephraim will cease to be a people;

**7:9** and the head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is the son of Remaliah. If you do not believe, you will not remain."'"

**7:10** And the Lord spoke further to Ahaz, saying,

**7:11** "Ask a sign for yourself from the Lord your God, into the depth of the underworld, or into the height above."

**7:12** And Ahaz said, "I will not ask, and I will not test the Lord."

**7:13** And he said, "Hear therefore, house of David: is it too little for you to be troublesome to men, that you are troublesome to my God as well?

**7:14** For this reason the Lord Himself will give you a sign: look, a virgin will conceive and give birth to a son, and His name will be called Immanuel.

**7:15** He will eat butter and honey, so that He may know how to reject evil and choose good.

**7:16** For before the boy knows how to reject evil and choose good, the land that you detest will be abandoned before the face of its two kings.

**7:17** The Lord will bring upon you, and upon your people, and upon the house of your father, days that have not come since the days of the separation of Ephraim from Judah, with the king of the Assyrians.

**7:18** And it will be in that day: the Lord will whistle for the fly that is at the end of the rivers of Egypt, and for the bee that is in the land of Assyria;

**7:19** and they will come, and all of them will rest in the torrents of the valleys, and in the caverns of the rocks, and in all the thickets, and in all the holes.

**7:20** In that day the Lord will shave with a hired razor, by those who are beyond the river, by the king of the Assyrians, the head and the hair of the feet, and the whole beard.

**7:21** And it will be in that day: a man will raise a cow of the herd and two sheep,

**7:22** and because of the abundance of milk, he will eat butter; for everyone who is left in the midst of the land will eat butter and honey.

**7:23** And it will be in that day: every place where there were a thousand vines, for a thousand pieces of silver, will become thorns and briers.

**7:24** With arrows and a bow they will enter there, for briers and thorns will be in the whole land.

**7:25** And as for all the mountains that will be hoed with a hoe, the terror of thorns and briers will not come there; and it will be for the pastures of the ox, and for the trampling of the flock."

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The Clementine Vulgate places the break between verses 1 and 2 mid-clause, leaving "and they were not able" at the end of 7:1 and "to conquer it" at the start of 7:2.
- **7:3** — For the symbolic Hebrew name Shear-jashub ("a remnant shall return"), the Vulgate translates the first element into Latin ("he who is left behind," qui derelictus est) and retains the second as the name Jashub (Jasub).
- **7:14** — The Vulgate reads "a virgin" (virgo) and the passive "His name will be called" (vocabitur nomen ejus), aligning with the Septuagint and Matthew 1:23 rather than the Hebrew Masoretic Text's active "she will call."
- **7:17** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew particle before "the king of Assyria" as the preposition "with" (cum rege Assyriorum) rather than as a direct-object marker ("namely, the king of Assyria").
- **7:25** — The Vulgate takes "the terror of thorns and briers" as the subject of a third-person verb ("will not come there," non veniet illuc), whereas the Hebrew is commonly read in the second person ("you will not go there for fear of briers and thorns").
