## Text

**6:1** In the year in which King Uzziah died, I saw the Lord seated on a high and uplifted throne, and the things that were beneath Him were filling the temple.

**6:2** Seraphim stood above it: one had six wings, and another had six wings; with two they veiled His face, and with two they veiled His feet, and with two they flew.

**6:3** And they called out one to another and said, "Holy, holy, holy is the Lord, God of hosts; all the earth is full of His glory."

**6:4** And the lintels of the hinges were shaken by the voice of the one calling out, and the house was filled with smoke.

**6:5** And I said, "Woe to me, because I have kept silent, because I am a man with polluted lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people having polluted lips, and I have seen the King, the Lord of hosts, with my eyes."

**6:6** And one of the seraphim flew to me, and in his hand was a small stone, which he had taken from the altar with tongs,

**6:7** and he touched my mouth and said, "Look, this has touched your lips, and your iniquity will be taken away, and your sin will be cleansed."

**6:8** And I heard the voice of the Lord saying, "Whom shall I send? And who will go for Us?" And I said, "Here I am; send me."

**6:9** And He said, "Go, and you will say to this people, 'Hear, you who hear, and do not understand; and see a vision, and do not know.'

**6:10** Blind the heart of this people, and make its ears heavy, and shut its eyes, so that it may not perhaps see with its eyes, and hear with its ears, and understand with its heart, and be converted, and I heal it."

**6:11** And I said, "How long, Lord?" And He said, "Until the cities are made desolate without an inhabitant, and the houses without a man, and the land will be left deserted.

**6:12** And the Lord will remove men far away, and she who had been abandoned in the midst of the land will be multiplied.

**6:13** And still there will be a tenth in it, and it will be converted, and it will be for a display like a terebinth, and like an oak that spreads out its branches; that which stands in it will be a holy seed."

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew for the hem or train of the Lord's robe neutrally as "the things that were beneath Him" (ea quae sub ipso erant).
- **6:2** — The Vulgate reads neuter "above it" (super illud, referring to the throne) and pairs plural verbs (velabant) with singular "His" (ejus), which Jerome and the Vulgate tradition understood as veiling the Lord's face and feet, whereas the Hebrew distributive singular refers to each seraph's own face and feet.
- **6:5** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew verb (often translated "I am ruined/undone") from its homonymous root meaning "be silent" (quia tacui, "because I have kept silent").
- **6:10** — The Vulgate reads "Blind the heart" (Excaeca cor), where the Hebrew has "make fat/dull the heart."
- **6:13** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text (which speaks of burning and a felled stump), the Vulgate presents a hopeful image: the remaining tenth "will be converted" (convertetur) and stand "for a display" (in ostensionem) like an oak "that spreads out its branches" (quae expandit ramos suos).
