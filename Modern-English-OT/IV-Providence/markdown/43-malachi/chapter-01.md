## Text

**1:1** The burden of the Lord's word to Israel through the hand of Malachi.

**1:2** "I have loved you," says the Lord, "and you have said, 'In what way have You loved us?' Was Esau not Jacob's brother?" says the Lord. "Yet I have loved Jacob,

**1:3** but Esau I have hated, and I have turned his mountains into a wasteland, and his inheritance over to the dragons of the desert."

**1:4** And if Edom says, "We have been destroyed, yet returning we will rebuild what has been destroyed," this is what the Lord of hosts says: "They will build, and I will destroy; and they will be called the borders of wickedness, and the people with whom the Lord is angry forever."

**1:5** And your eyes will see, and you will say, "May the Lord be magnified beyond the border of Israel."

**1:6** "A son honors his father, and a servant his master. If then I am a father, where is My honor? And if I am the Lord, where is the fear of Me?" says the Lord of hosts. "To you, priests, who despise My name! And you have said, 'In what way have we despised Your name?'

**1:7** You offer polluted bread on My altar, and you say, 'In what way have we polluted You?' In saying, 'The table of the Lord is despised.'

**1:8** If you offer what is blind for sacrifice, is it not evil? And if you offer what is lame and sick, is it not evil? Offer it to your governor; see if it will please him, or if he will accept your face," says the Lord of hosts.

**1:9** "And now implore the face of God, that He may have mercy on you (for from your hand this has been done), if in any way He may accept your faces," says the Lord of hosts.

**1:10** "Who is there among you who would shut the doors and kindle My altar for nothing? I have no pleasure in you," says the Lord of hosts, "and I will not accept a gift from your hand.

**1:11** For from the rising of the sun even to its setting, My name is great among the nations, and in every place sacrifice is made, and a pure offering is offered to My name; for My name is great among the nations," says the Lord of hosts.

**1:12** "And you have polluted it in saying, 'The table of the Lord is defiled, and what is placed on it is contemptible, along with the fire that devours it.'

**1:13** And you have said, 'Look, out of weariness!' and you have snorted at it," says the Lord of hosts. "And you have brought in from plunder what is lame and sick, and you have brought in a gift. Will I accept it from your hand?" says the Lord.

**1:14** "Cursed is the deceiver who has a male in his flock, and making a vow sacrifices what is weak to the Lord; for I am a great king," says the Lord of hosts, "and My name is fearsome among the nations."

## Translator Notes

- **1:3** — Vulgate reads "dragons of the desert" (dracones deserti), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to "jackals of the wilderness."
- **1:8** — The Latin idiom "accept your face" (suscipere faciem, repeated in 1:9; cf. 2:9) mirrors Hebrew usage meaning to receive someone with favor or show partiality.
- **1:10** — Vulgate frames this as a rhetorical rebuke of mercenary priests ("who would shut the doors and kindle My altar for nothing," gratuito), whereas the Hebrew expresses a wish that someone would shut the doors so they would not kindle the altar in vain.
- **1:11** — Vulgate uses present-tense verbs ("is great," "sacrifice is made," "is offered") and renders the Hebrew "incense is burned" more broadly as "sacrifice is made" (sacrificatur).
- **1:12** — Vulgate reads "what is placed on it is contemptible, along with the fire that devours it," differing from the Hebrew text's "its fruit, its food."
