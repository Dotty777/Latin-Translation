## Text

**2:1** "And now this command is to you, priests.

**2:2** If you will not listen, and if you will not lay it to heart, so as to give glory to My name," says the Lord of hosts, "I will send poverty upon you, and I will curse your blessings, and I will curse them, because you have not laid it to heart.

**2:3** Look, I will cast away your arm, and I will scatter over your faces the dung of your solemn feasts, and it will take you away with it.

**2:4** And you will know that I have sent this command to you, so that My covenant might be with Levi," says the Lord of hosts.

**2:5** "My covenant with him was of life and peace, and I gave him fear, and he feared Me, and before the face of My name he trembled.

**2:6** The law of truth was in his mouth, and iniquity was not found on his lips; in peace and in equity he walked with Me, and he turned many away from iniquity.

**2:7** For the lips of a priest will guard knowledge, and they will seek the law from his mouth, for he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts.

**2:8** But you have departed from the way, and you have caused very many to stumble in the law; you have made the covenant of Levi void," says the Lord of hosts.

**2:9** "Therefore I also have made you contemptible and lowly before all the peoples, just as you have not kept My ways, and have shown partiality in the law."

**2:10** Is there not one father of us all? Has not one God created us? Why then does each one of us despise his brother, violating the covenant of our fathers?

**2:11** Judah has transgressed, and an abomination has been committed in Israel and in Jerusalem, for Judah has defiled the holiness of the Lord, which He loved, and has married the daughter of a foreign god.

**2:12** The Lord will destroy the man who does this, master and disciple, from the tents of Jacob, and him who offers a gift to the Lord of hosts.

**2:13** "And this you have done again: you were covering the altar of the Lord with tears, with weeping and groaning, so that I no longer look toward the sacrifice, nor accept anything pleasing from your hand."

**2:14** And you have said, "For what reason?" Because the Lord has borne witness between you and the wife of your youth, whom you have despised; and yet she is your partner, and the wife of your covenant.

**2:15** Did not One make them, and the remnant of the spirit is His? And what does the One seek, except the seed of God? Guard your spirit therefore, and do not despise the wife of your youth.

**2:16** "When you hate her, send her away," says the Lord God of Israel, "yet iniquity will cover his garment," says the Lord of hosts. "Guard your spirit, and do not despise her."

**2:17** You have made the Lord weary with your words, and you have said, "In what way have we made Him weary?" In saying, "Everyone who does evil is good in the sight of the Lord, and such ones please Him; or else, where is the God of judgment?"

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — Vulgate reads "poverty" (egestatem) where the Hebrew has "the curse," and translates both curses in the future tense ("I will curse... and I will curse them") rather than shifting to a completed past tense.
- **2:3** — Literally "I will cast away the arm for you" (projiciam vobis brachium); the Vulgate reads the Hebrew consonants as "arm" rather than "seed/offspring" and renders "cast away" instead of "rebuke."
- **2:12** — Vulgate renders the difficult Hebrew idiom (literally "one who wakes and one who answers") as "master and disciple" (magistrum et discipulum).
- **2:15** — The grammar is notoriously difficult; the Vulgate literally asks "Did not One make [them], and the remnant of the spirit is His?" leaving the object of "make" (fecit) unexpressed.
- **2:16** — Vulgate reads a second-person directive ("When you hate her, send her away," cf. Deut 24:1) followed by the warning that "iniquity will cover his garment"; the Hebrew opening clause can also be read as "he hates sending away."
