## Text

**42:1** And he led me out into the outer court by the way leading to the north, and he brought me into the chamber that was opposite the separated building and opposite the edifice facing toward the north.

**42:2** At the face of the length, one hundred cubits of the north door, and fifty cubits in width,

**42:3** opposite the twenty cubits of the inner court, and opposite the pavement laid with stone of the outer court, where a portico was joined to a triple portico.

**42:4** And before the chambers was a walkway ten cubits in width, facing toward the interior, a way of one cubit. And their doors were toward the north,

**42:5** where the chambers in the upper parts were lower, because they supported the porticoes that projected out from them, from the lower and from the middle parts of the building.

**42:6** For they were three-storied, and they did not have columns, as the columns of the courts were; therefore they projected from the lower and from the middle parts, fifty cubits from the ground.

**42:7** And the outer enclosure wall alongside the chambers that were on the way of the outer court before the chambers: its length was fifty cubits,

**42:8** because the length of the chambers of the outer court was fifty cubits, and the length before the face of the temple was one hundred cubits.

**42:9** And beneath these chambers was an entrance from the east for those entering into them from the outer court.

**42:10** In the width of the enclosure wall of the court that was opposite the eastern way, facing the separated building, there were also chambers before the building.

**42:11** And there was a way before their face, according to the likeness of the chambers that were on the way of the north: according to their length, so also was their width, and every entrance of theirs, and the likenesses, and their doors.

**42:12** According to the doors of the chambers that were on the way facing the south, there was a door at the head of the way, the way that was before the separated vestibule for those entering by the eastern way.

**42:13** And he said to me, "The chambers of the north and the chambers of the south, which are before the separated building, these are the holy chambers, in which the priests who draw near to the Lord into the Holy of Holies eat; there they will place the most holy things and the offering for sin and for trespass, for the place is holy.

**42:14** And when the priests have entered, they will not go out from the holy places into the outer court, and there they will lay aside their garments in which they minister, because they are holy, and they will be clothed in other garments; and thus they will proceed to the people."

**42:15** And when he had completed the measurements of the inner house, he led me out by the way of the gate that faced toward the eastern way, and he measured it on every side all around.

**42:16** Now he measured toward the east wind with the measuring reed, five hundred reeds by the measuring reed all around.

**42:17** And he measured toward the north wind five hundred reeds by the measuring reed in a circuit.

**42:18** And toward the south wind he measured five hundred reeds by the measuring reed all around.

**42:19** And toward the west wind he measured five hundred reeds by the measuring reed.

**42:20** Along the four winds he measured its wall on every side all around, a length of five hundred cubits and a width of five hundred cubits, dividing between the sanctuary and the place of the common people.

## Translator Notes

- **42:1** — Throughout chapters 41-42, Latin gazophylacium (literally 'treasury' or 'store-chamber') is Jerome's standard rendering for the temple chambers used to store sacred offerings and priestly vestments.
- **42:6** — Unlike the Hebrew, which describes the upper chambers as recessed and places the fifty-cubit measurement only in v. 7, the Vulgate states that they projected (eminebant) fifty cubits from the ground (a terra cubitis quinquaginta).
- **42:13** — Latin Sancta sanctorum appears twice: first spatially with in ('into the Holy of Holies') modifying the priests who draw near to the Lord, and second as the object of ponent ('the most holy things', i.e., sacred offerings). The Vulgate also joins the offerings as oblationem pro peccato et pro delicto ('the offering for sin and for trespass').
- **42:20** — Although vv. 16-19 measure five hundred reeds (calamos) along each wind, the Vulgate explicitly specifies five hundred cubits (cubitorum) for the length and width of the outer wall here.
