## Text

**9:1** And He cried out in my ears with a loud voice, saying, "The visitations of the city have drawn near, and each one has an instrument of slaughter in his hand."

**9:2** And look, six men were coming from the way of the upper gate, which faces toward the north, and each one's instrument of destruction was in his hand; one man also in their midst was dressed in linen, and a writer's inkhorn was at his side; and they went in and stood beside the bronze altar.

**9:3** And the glory of the Lord of Israel was lifted up from the cherub on which it was, to the threshold of the house; and He called to the man who was clothed in linen and had the writer's inkhorn at his waist,

**9:4** and the Lord said to him, "Pass through the middle of the city, in the midst of Jerusalem, and mark a tau upon the foreheads of the men sighing and grieving over all the abominations that are done in its midst."

**9:5** And to the others He said, in my hearing, "Pass through the city following him, and strike: let your eye not spare, nor have pity;

**9:6** kill old man, young man and virgin, little child and women to utter destruction; but do not kill anyone upon whom you see the tau, and begin at My sanctuary." So they began with the elder men who were before the front of the house.

**9:7** And He said to them, "Defile the house, and fill the courts with the slaughtered; go out." And they went out and were striking those who were in the city.

**9:8** And when the slaughter was completed, I remained, and I fell upon my face, and crying out I said, "Ah! Ah! Ah! Lord God, will You then destroy all the remnant of Israel, pouring out Your fury upon Jerusalem?"

**9:9** And He said to me, "The iniquity of the house of Israel and Judah is very exceedingly great, and the land is filled with bloodshed, and the city is filled with turning away; for they have said, 'The Lord has abandoned the land, and the Lord does not see.'

**9:10** Therefore My eye also will not spare, nor will I have pity; I will repay their way upon their head."

**9:11** And look, the man who was clothed in linen, who had the inkhorn on his back, replied with a word, saying, "I have done just as You commanded me."

## Translator Notes

- **9:3** — Latin Domini Israël ("the Lord of Israel") is a distinctive Vulgate reading where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "the God of Israel" (as in 8:4).
- **9:4** — Latin thau ("tau") transliterates the final letter of the Hebrew alphabet (taw), shaped like a cross or X in ancient scripts and understood here as a protective mark on the forehead.
- **9:8** — Latin repeats the cry of distress three times (Heu! heu! heu!, "Ah! Ah! Ah!") where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has a single exclamation.
- **9:11** — Latin in dorso suo ("on his back") varies from ad renes ejus ("at his side") in verse 2 and in lumbis suis ("at his waist") in verse 3, whereas the Hebrew repeats the same phrase ("at his waist") in all three verses.
