## Text

**31:1** And it happened in the eleventh year, in the third month, on the first day of the month, that the word of the Lord came to me, saying,

**31:2** "Son of man, say to Pharaoh king of Egypt and to his people, 'To whom have you become like in your greatness?

**31:3** Look, Assyria was like a cedar in Lebanon, beautiful in branches, and leafy with boughs, and lofty in height, and its top was lifted up among the dense boughs.

**31:4** Waters nourished it; the deep exalted it; its rivers flowed around its roots, and it sent out its streams to all the trees of the region.

**31:5** Therefore its height was lifted up above all the trees of the region, and its limbs were multiplied, and its branches were lifted up because of many waters.

**31:6** And when it had stretched out its shadow, in its branches all the birds of the sky made nests; and under its boughs all the beasts of the woodlands gave birth, and under its shade lived the assembly of very many nations.

**31:7** And it was most beautiful in its greatness, and in the spreading of its limbs; for its root was beside many waters.

**31:8** The cedars were not taller than it in the paradise of God; the fir trees did not equal its top, and the plane trees were not equal to its boughs; no tree of the paradise of God was likened to it and to its beauty.

**31:9** For I made it splendid, and with many dense boughs, and all the trees of delight that were in the paradise of God envied it.

**31:10** Therefore this is what the Lord God says: Because it was exalted in height, and set its top green and dense, and its heart was lifted up in its height,

**31:11** I have handed it over into the hand of the mightiest of the nations; he will surely deal with it; according to its wickedness I have cast it out.

**31:12** And foreigners, and the most cruel of the nations, will cut it down and cast it upon the mountains; and in all the valleys its branches will collapse, and its limbs will be broken on all the cliffs of the earth; and all the peoples of the earth will depart from its shade and abandon it.

**31:13** Upon its ruin all the birds of the sky lived, and on its branches were all the beasts of the region.

**31:14** For this reason none of the trees of the waters will be lifted up in their height, nor will they set their loftiness among the wooded and leafy ones, nor will any that are watered with waters stand in their loftiness; for all of them have been handed over to death, to the farthest earth, in the midst of the sons of men, to those who go down into the pit.

**31:15** This is what the Lord God says: On the day when it went down to the underworld, I brought on mourning; I covered it with the deep, and I held back its rivers, and I restrained the many waters; Lebanon was grieved over it, and all the trees of the field were shaken.

**31:16** At the sound of its ruin I shook the nations when I led it down to the underworld with those who were going down into the pit; and in the lowest earth all the trees of delight were comforted, the choice and illustrious ones in Lebanon, all that were watered with waters.

**31:17** For they also will go down with it into the underworld, to those killed by the sword; and the arm of each one will sit under its shade in the midst of the nations.

**31:18** To whom have you been likened, renowned and lofty one among the trees of delight? Look, you have been led down with the trees of delight to the farthest earth; in the midst of the uncircumcised you will sleep, with those who were killed by the sword: this is Pharaoh and all his multitude, says the Lord God.'"

## Translator Notes

- **31:6** — Vulgate opens v. 6 with "And when it had stretched out its shadow" (Cumque extendisset umbram suam), translating a phrase placed at the end of v. 5 in the Hebrew ("when it sent them out").
- **31:9** — Here and in vv. 16 and 18, the Vulgate translates Hebrew Eden etymologically as "delight" (ligna voluptatis, "trees of delight"), and renders Hebrew gan ("garden") as "paradise" (paradisus, vv. 8-9).
- **31:15** — Vulgate reads "I covered it with the deep" (operui eum abysso), whereas the Hebrew reads "I covered the deep over it"; here and in vv. 16-17, inferos/infernum ("the underworld") renders Hebrew Sheol.
- **31:17** — Vulgate reads future "will go down" (descendent) and "and the arm of each one will sit under its shade" (et brachium uniuscujusque sedebit sub umbraculo ejus), differing in tense and wording from the Hebrew ("they went down... its allies who lived under its shadow").
- **31:18** — Vulgate uses the perfect passive "you have been led down" (deductus es) and "you will sleep" (dormies), where the Hebrew has "you will be brought down" and "you will lie."
