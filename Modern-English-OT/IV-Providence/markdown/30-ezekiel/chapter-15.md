## Text

**15:1** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying,

**15:2** "Son of man, what will become of the wood of the vine, out of all the trees of the groves that are among the trees of the forests?

**15:3** Will wood really be taken from it for any work to be made, or will a peg be fashioned from it so that any vessel at all may hang on it?

**15:4** Look, it has been given to the fire for food: fire has consumed both ends of it, and its middle has been reduced to ashes. Will it really be useful for work?

**15:5** Even when it was intact, it was not fit for work; how much more, when fire has devoured it and burned it up, will no work be made from it?

**15:6** Therefore, this is what the Lord God says: Just as the wood of the vine among the trees of the forests, which I have given to the fire to be devoured, so I will hand over the inhabitants of Jerusalem.

**15:7** And I will set My face against them; they will go out from the fire, and fire will consume them; and you will know that I am the Lord when I set My face against them,

**15:8** and render the land impassable and desolate, because they have proved to be transgressors, says the Lord God."

## Translator Notes

- **15:2** — Whereas the Hebrew asks how vine wood excels any other branch among forest trees, the Vulgate asks "quid fiet de" ("what will become of") the vine wood out of all the "ligna nemorum" ("trees of the groves," literally "woods of the groves").
- **15:7** — The Vulgate renders "egredientur" in the future tense ("they will go out from the fire"), whereas the Hebrew verb is in the perfect ("they have come out").
- **15:8** — The Vulgate expands the single Hebrew noun for desolation into the pair "inviam et desolatam" ("impassable and desolate") and renders the final clause personally with "prævaricatores" ("transgressors").
