## Text

**2:1** This was the vision of the likeness of the glory of the Lord. And I saw, and I fell on my face, and I heard the voice of One speaking, and He said to me, "Son of man, stand on your feet, and I will speak with you."

**2:2** And the Spirit entered into me after He spoke to me, and set me upon my feet; and I heard Him speaking to me,

**2:3** and saying, "Son of man, I am sending you to the sons of Israel, to apostate nations that have departed from Me; they and their fathers have transgressed My covenant up to this day,

**2:4** and they are sons of a hard face and an untamable heart, to whom I am sending you. And you shall say to them, 'This is what the Lord God says,'

**2:5** whether perhaps even they will listen, and whether perhaps they will desist—for it is a provoking house—and they will know that a prophet has been in their midst.

**2:6** Therefore you, son of man, do not fear them, and do not dread their sayings, because unbelievers and subverters are with you, and you live with scorpions. Do not fear their words, and do not be terrified of their faces, because it is a provoking house.

**2:7** Therefore you shall speak My words to them, if perhaps they will listen and desist, because they are provokers.

**2:8** But you, son of man, hear whatever I speak to you, and do not be provoking, as the provoking house is; open your mouth and eat whatever I give to you."

**2:9** And I saw, and look, a hand was sent out to me, in which was a rolled-up book; and He unrolled it before me, and it was written inside and outside, and written in it were lamentations, and a song, and woe.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The Vulgate opens chapter 2 with 'Hæc visio similitudinis gloriæ Domini' ("This was the vision of the likeness of the glory of the Lord"), which the Hebrew text places at the end of 1:28.
- **2:3** — Latin 'prævaricati sunt pactum meum' ("have transgressed My covenant") is a Vulgate-distinct reading where the Hebrew text has simply "rebelled against Me."
- **2:6** — Latin 'increduli et subversores' ("unbelievers and subverters") is a Vulgate-distinct reading where the Hebrew text speaks figuratively of briers and thorns.
- **2:9** — Latin 'carmen' ("a song") renders a Hebrew word for groaning or mourning ('hegeh'), yielding the Vulgate triad "lamentations, and a song, and woe."
