## Text

**19:1** "And you, take up a lamentation over the princes of Israel,

**19:2** and you will say, 'Why did your mother, a lioness, lie down among lions? Did she nourish her cubs in the midst of young lions?

**19:3** And she brought up one of her young lions, and he became a lion; and he learned to seize prey and to eat a man.

**19:4** And the nations heard about him, and not without their own wounds they captured him, and they brought him in chains to the land of Egypt.

**19:5** And when she saw that she had grown weak, and her expectation had perished, she took one of her young lions; she made him a lion.

**19:6** And he walked among the lions, and became a lion; and he learned to seize prey and to devour men;

**19:7** he learned to make widows and to turn their cities into a desert; and the land and its fullness were made desolate at the sound of his roaring.

**19:8** And the nations gathered against him from every side, out of the provinces, and spread their net over him; in their wounds he was captured,

**19:9** and they put him in a cage; in chains they brought him to the king of Babylon, and they put him into prison, so that his voice would no longer be heard upon the mountains of Israel.

**19:10** Your mother, like a vine in your blood, was planted by the water; her fruit and her boughs grew from many waters.

**19:11** And she came to have solid rods for scepters of rulers, and her stature was exalted among the boughs, and she saw her height in the multitude of her shoots.

**19:12** And she was pulled up in wrath and cast down to the ground, and the burning wind dried up her fruit; the rods of her strength withered and dried out; fire consumed her.

**19:13** And now she is transplanted into the desert, in a pathless and thirsty land.

**19:14** And fire went out from a rod of her branches, which consumed her fruit; and there was no strong rod in her, a scepter of rulers.' It is a lamentation, and it will be for a lamentation."

## Translator Notes

- **19:2** — Vulgate "Quare" ("Why") construes the opening Hebrew interrogative as a question of reason rather than "What was your mother?"
- **19:4** — Vulgate "non absque vulneribus suis" ("not without their own wounds"), along with "in vulneribus earum" ("in their wounds") in 19:8, renders Hebrew "in their pit" by linking the Hebrew term with destruction or wounding.
- **19:7** — Vulgate "didicit viduas facere" ("he learned to make widows") reflects reading the Hebrew consonants as "widows" rather than "palaces/citadels" and taking the verb as "learned" rather than "ravaged."
- **19:10** — Vulgate "in sanguine tuo" ("in your blood") translates the difficult Hebrew phrase literally, where many modern versions emend or render "in your vineyard" or "in your likeness."
- **19:11** — Vulgate "vidit altitudinem suam" ("she saw her height") takes the verb as active rather than passive ("was seen in its height").
