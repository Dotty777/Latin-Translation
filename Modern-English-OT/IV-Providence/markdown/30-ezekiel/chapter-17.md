## Text

**17:1** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying:

**17:2** "Son of man, propose a riddle and tell a parable to the house of Israel,

**17:3** and you will say, 'This is what the Lord God says: A great eagle of large wings, with a long span of limbs, full of feathers and variegation, came to Lebanon and took the pith of the cedar.

**17:4** It tore off the tip of its boughs and carried it across to the land of Canaan; it placed it in a city of merchants.

**17:5** And it took from the seed of the land and placed it in soil for seed, so that it might establish a root by many waters; it placed it on the surface.

**17:6** And when it had sprouted, it grew into a rather broad vine, of low stature, with its branches looking toward it, and its roots were under it; therefore it became a vine, and bore fruit into shoots, and sent out offshoots.

**17:7** And there was another great eagle, with large wings and many feathers; and look, this vine, as if sending its roots toward it, stretched out its shoots toward it, so that it might water it from the small beds of its growth.

**17:8** In good soil by many waters it has been planted, so that it may produce boughs and bear fruit, so that it may become a great vine.'

**17:9** Say, 'This is what the Lord God says: Will it then prosper? Will he not pull up its roots and pluck off its fruits, and dry up all the shoots of its growth, and it will wither, and not with a great arm nor with many people, so as to pull it up by the roots?

**17:10** Look, it is planted: will it then prosper? When the burning wind touches it, will it not dry up, and wither in the beds of its growth?'"

**17:11** And the word of the Lord came to me, saying:

**17:12** "Say to the provoking house, 'Do you not know what these things signify?' Say, 'Look, the king of Babylon comes to Jerusalem, and will take the king and its princes, and will bring them to himself in Babylon.

**17:13** And he will take from the seed of the kingdom, and will strike a covenant with him, and will receive an oath from him. Moreover, he will also take the mighty ones of the land,

**17:14** so that the kingdom may be lowly and not be lifted up, but may keep his pact and observe it.

**17:15** Yet he, withdrawing from him, sent messengers to Egypt, so that it might give him horses and many people. Will the one who did these things prosper, or obtain safety? And he who broke the pact, will he escape?

**17:16** As I live, says the Lord God, surely in the place of the king who appointed him king, whose oath he made void, and he broke the pact that he had with him, in the midst of Babylon he will die.

**17:17** And neither with a great army nor with many people will Pharaoh wage battle against him, at the casting up of a mound and at the building of ramparts, to kill many souls.

**17:18** For he had despised the oath so as to break the covenant, and look, he gave his hand; and since he has done all these things, he will not escape.

**17:19** Therefore this is what the Lord God says: As I live, surely the oath that he despised, and the covenant that he transgressed, I will place upon his head.

**17:20** And I will spread My net over him, and he will be caught in My dragnet; and I will bring him to Babylon, and I will judge him there for the transgression by which he despised Me.

**17:21** And all his fugitives, with his entire host, will fall by the sword; and those who remain will be scattered to every wind; and you will know that I, the Lord, have spoken.

**17:22** This is what the Lord God says: I Myself will also take from the pith of the lofty cedar and set it; from the top of its branches I will pluck off what is tender, and I will plant it upon a high and towering mountain.

**17:23** On the lofty mountain of Israel I will plant it, and it will burst forth into growth, and produce fruit, and become a large cedar; and all birds will dwell under it, and every winged creature will nest under the shade of its boughs;

**17:24** and all the trees of the region will know that I, the Lord, have brought low the lofty tree and exalted the lowly tree, and have dried up the green tree and made the dry tree leaf out. I, the Lord, have spoken, and I have done it.'"

## Translator Notes

- **17:3** — Vulgate "longo membrorum ductu" ("with a long span of limbs") and "medullam cedri" ("the pith/marrow of the cedar," as also in 17:22) are distinctive Latin renderings where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "long of pinion" and "the crown/top of the cedar."
- **17:5** — Vulgate "in superficie" ("on the surface") reflects a distinct understanding of the obscure Hebrew word "tsaftsafah" (often rendered "like a willow" from the Masoretic Text).
- **17:10** — Vulgate "ventus urens" ("the burning wind," as also in 19:12) renders the scorching effect of the Hebrew "east wind."
- **17:12** — Following "venit" ("comes"), the Vulgate narrates the deportation in 17:12-13 with future verbs ("will take," "will bring," "will strike") before shifting to past tense in 17:15, whereas the Hebrew narrates the events in the past.
- **17:17** — Vulgate "faciet contra eum Pharao praelium" ("Pharaoh will wage battle against him") differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's sense that Pharaoh will not help Zedekiah in battle.
