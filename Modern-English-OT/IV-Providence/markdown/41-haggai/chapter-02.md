## Text

**2:1** In the seventh month, on the twenty-first of the month, the word of the Lord came through the hand of Haggai the prophet, saying:

**2:2** "Speak to Zerubbabel, son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and to Joshua, son of Jehozadak, the high priest, and to the remnant of the people, saying:

**2:3** 'Who among you is left who saw this house in its first glory? And how do you see it now? Is it not just as though it did not exist in your eyes?

**2:4** And now be strong, Zerubbabel, says the Lord; and be strong, Joshua, son of Jehozadak, high priest; and be strong, all the people of the land, says the Lord of hosts; and carry out (for I am with you, says the Lord of hosts)

**2:5** the word that I covenanted with you when you came out of the land of Egypt; and My Spirit will be in your midst; do not fear.

**2:6** For this is what the Lord of hosts says: There is still one little while, and I will shake heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the dry land.

**2:7** And I will shake all the nations, and the One desired by all nations will come; and I will fill this house with glory, says the Lord of hosts.

**2:8** Mine is the silver, and Mine is the gold, says the Lord of hosts.

**2:9** The glory of this latter house will be great, more than that of the first, says the Lord of hosts; and in this place I will give peace, says the Lord of hosts.'"

**2:10** On the twenty-fourth of the ninth month, in the second year of King Darius, the word of the Lord came to Haggai the prophet, saying:

**2:11** This is what the Lord of hosts says: "Ask the priests about the law, saying,

**2:12** 'If a man carries consecrated meat in the hem of his garment, and with its edge touches bread, or stew, or wine, or oil, or any food, will it be consecrated?'" And answering, the priests said, "No."

**2:13** And Haggai said, "If one who is defiled in soul touches any of all these things, will it be contaminated?" And the priests answered and said, "It will be contaminated."

**2:14** And Haggai answered and said, "So is this people, and so is this nation before My face," says the Lord, "and so is every work of their hands; and all the things that they have offered there will be contaminated.

**2:15** And now apply your hearts from this day and earlier, before a stone was laid upon a stone in the temple of the Lord.

**2:16** When you came to a heap of twenty measures, there came to be ten; and when you entered the winepress to press out fifty jars, there came to be twenty.

**2:17** I struck you, and all the works of your hands, with scorching wind, and mildew, and hail; and there was no one among you who returned to Me," says the Lord.

**2:18** "Apply your hearts from this day and into the future, from the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month; from the day on which the foundations of the temple of the Lord were laid, lay it upon your heart.

**2:19** Is the seed already in sprout, and have the vine, and the fig tree, and the pomegranate, and the olive tree still not blossomed? From this day I will bless."

**2:20** And the word of the Lord came a second time to Haggai on the twenty-fourth of the month, saying:

**2:21** "Speak to Zerubbabel, governor of Judah, saying, 'I will shake heaven and earth alike,

**2:22** and I will overthrow the throne of kingdoms, and I will crush the strength of the kingdom of the nations; and I will overthrow the chariot and its rider, and the horses and their riders will go down, each man by the sword of his brother.

**2:23** On that day, says the Lord of hosts, I will take you, Zerubbabel, son of Shealtiel, My servant, says the Lord, and I will set you like a seal, because I have chosen you, says the Lord of hosts.'"

## Translator Notes

- **2:4** — The Clementine Vulgate encloses quoniam ego vobiscum sum, dicit Dominus exercituum in parentheses so that et facite ("and carry out") takes verbum ("the word") in 2:5 as its direct object, whereas the Hebrew treats 2:5 as a separate clause.
- **2:7** — Latin veniet desideratus cunctis gentibus uses a singular verb and masculine singular participle ("the One desired by all nations will come"); the Hebrew has a plural verb with "the desired things/treasures of all nations."
- **2:13** — Latin pollutus in anima literally means "defiled in soul," rendering the Hebrew idiom for ritual uncleanness contracted from a corpse (Hebrew nefesh can mean either "soul" or "dead body").
- **2:16** — The Vulgate supplies dry measures (modiorum, Roman modii) and wine jars (lagenas) where the Hebrew leaves the units unstated, and uses exprimeretis ("to press out") rather than "to draw."
- **2:19** — Latin in germine ("in sprout") and floruit ("blossomed") are Vulgate-distinct readings corresponding to Hebrew "in the barn/granary" and "borne [fruit]."
