## Text

**4:1** And Jonah was afflicted with great affliction, and he became angry.

**4:2** And he prayed to the Lord and said, "I implore You, Lord, was this not my word when I was still in my land? For this reason I acted beforehand to flee to Tarshish; for I know that You are a gracious and merciful God, patient and of great compassion, and forgiving concerning evil.

**4:3** And now, Lord, please take my soul from me, because death is better for me than life."

**4:4** And the Lord said, "Do you think you are rightly angry?"

**4:5** And Jonah went out from the city and sat down opposite the east side of the city; and he made a shelter for himself there, and was sitting beneath it in the shade until he might see what would happen to the city.

**4:6** And the Lord God prepared ivy, and it came up over Jonah's head, so that it might be shade over his head and protect him (for he had suffered); and Jonah rejoiced over the ivy with great joy.

**4:7** And God prepared a worm at the rising of dawn on the next day, and it struck the ivy, and it withered.

**4:8** And when the sun had risen, the Lord commanded a hot and scorching wind; and the sun struck on Jonah's head, and he was sweltering; and he asked for his soul that he might die, and said, "It is better for me to die than to live."

**4:9** And the Lord said to Jonah, "Do you think you are rightly angry over the ivy?" And he said, "I am rightly angry, even to death."

**4:10** And the Lord said, "You grieve over the ivy on which you did not labor, nor did you make it grow, which was born in one night and perished in one night;

**4:11** and should I not spare Nineveh, the great city, in which there are more than one hundred and twenty thousand people who do not know what is between their right hand and their left, and many animals?"

## Translator Notes

- **4:2** — Vulgate scio ("I know," present tense), patiens ("patient"), and ignoscens super malitia ("forgiving concerning evil") are distinctive Latin renderings where the Hebrew reads "I knew," "slow to anger," and "relenting concerning calamity."
- **4:6** — Vulgate hederam ("ivy") is Jerome's famous distinctive rendering of Hebrew qiqayon (often taken as a castor-oil plant; Septuagint "gourd"). The parenthetical (laboraverat enim) (lit. "for he had labored/suffered") renders Hebrew "to deliver him from his discomfort" as a brief causal note on Jonah's distress.
- **4:8** — Vulgate praecepit Dominus vento calido et urenti ("the Lord commanded a hot and scorching wind") has "the Lord" for Hebrew "God," uses "commanded" rather than "appointed," and omits "east"; aestuabat ("was sweltering") stands where the Hebrew has "grew faint."
- **4:10** — Vulgate distinguishes doles ("you grieve") here from parcam ("spare") in 4:11, whereas the Hebrew uses the same verb ("have pity/spare") in both verses; sub una nocte nata est ("was born in one night") reflects the Hebrew idiom "son of a night."
