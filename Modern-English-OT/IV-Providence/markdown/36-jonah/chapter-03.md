## Text

**3:1** And the word of the Lord came to Jonah a second time, saying,

**3:2** "Rise, and go to Nineveh, the great city, and preach in it the preaching that I speak to you."

**3:3** And Jonah rose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the Lord; and Nineveh was a great city, a journey of three days.

**3:4** And Jonah began to enter the city, a journey of one day, and he cried out and said, "Forty days more, and Nineveh will be overthrown."

**3:5** And the men of Nineveh believed in God, and they proclaimed a fast and were clothed in sackcloth, from the greatest even to the least.

**3:6** And the word reached the king of Nineveh, and he rose from his throne, and cast off his garment from himself, and put on sackcloth, and sat in ashes.

**3:7** And he cried out and spoke in Nineveh from the mouth of the king and his princes, saying, "Let people and beasts and cattle and flocks not taste anything; let them not graze, and let them not drink water.

**3:8** And let people and beasts be covered with sackcloth, and let them cry out to the Lord with might; and let each man turn from his evil way and from the iniquity that is in their hands.

**3:9** Who knows whether God may turn and forgive, and turn back from the fury of His anger, and we will not perish?"

**3:10** And God saw their works, that they had turned from their evil way; and God had mercy concerning the evil that He had spoken of doing to them, and He did not do it.

## Translator Notes

- **3:3** — The Vulgate reads simply "a great city" (civitas magna), omitting the Hebrew qualifying phrase "to God" (often rendered "exceedingly great").
- **3:7** — The Vulgate reads literally "from the mouth of the king and his princes" (ex ore regis et principum ejus), whereas the Hebrew reads "by the decree of the king and his nobles."
- **3:8** — The Vulgate reads "cry out to the Lord" (ad Dominum) and "from the iniquity" (ab iniquitate), where the Hebrew reads "to God" and "from the violence."
- **3:10** — The Vulgate reads "God had mercy concerning the evil" (misertus est Deus super malitiam), echoing "forgive" (ignoscat) in 3:9, where the Hebrew uses the verb for relenting and a term encompassing disaster or harm.
