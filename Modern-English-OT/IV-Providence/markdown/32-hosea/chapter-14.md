## Text

**14:1** May Samaria perish, because she has provoked her God to bitterness! By the sword let them perish, let their little children be dashed to pieces, and let her pregnant women be torn open!

**14:2** Return, Israel, to the Lord your God, because you have fallen in your iniquity.

**14:3** Take words with you, and return to the Lord; and say to Him, "Take away all iniquity, accept what is good, and we will offer the calves of our lips.

**14:4** Assyria will not save us; we will not mount a horse, nor will we say any longer, 'Our gods are the works of our hands,' because You will have mercy on the orphan who is in You."

**14:5** I will heal their bruises; I will love them freely, because My fury has turned away from them.

**14:6** I will be like dew; Israel will sprout like a lily, and his root will burst forth like that of Lebanon.

**14:7** His branches will go forth, and his glory will be like an olive tree, and his fragrance like that of Lebanon.

**14:8** Those who sit in his shadow will return; they will live on wheat, and they will sprout like a vine; his memorial will be like the wine of Lebanon.

**14:9** Ephraim, what are idols to Me any longer? I will hear, and I Myself will direct him like a green fir tree; from Me your fruit has been found.

**14:10** Who is wise, and will understand those things? Who is discerning, and will know these things? For the ways of the Lord are straight, and the righteous will walk in them; but transgressors will fall in them.

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — Following the Hebrew versification, chapter 14 begins here (in the other common versification this verse is 13:16 and 14:2-10 are 14:1-9); Vulgate "Pereat" ("May [Samaria] perish") and "ad amaritudinem concitavit" ("provoked to bitterness") also differ from the Masoretic Text ("shall bear her guilt ... rebelled").
- **14:3** — Vulgate "vitulos labiorum nostrorum" ("the calves of our lips") follows the Hebrew wording, whereas the Septuagint reads "the fruit of our lips."
- **14:4** — Vulgate "Dii nostri opera manuum nostrarum" forms a plural nominal clause without a preposition before "works," and "ejus, qui in te est, misereberis pupilli" makes God the active subject ("You will have mercy on the orphan who is in You"), differing slightly from the Masoretic Text ("for in You the orphan finds mercy").
- **14:5** — Vulgate "contritiones" ("bruises" or "crushings," matching the Septuagint) contrasts with the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "their apostasy" or "backsliding."
- **14:9** — In the Vulgate punctuation, "Ephraim, quid mihi ultra idola?" is spoken by God to Ephraim, and the accusative "ut abietem virentem" ("like a green fir tree") modifies Ephraim ("eum") rather than God as in the Masoretic Text ("I am like a luxuriant cypress").
