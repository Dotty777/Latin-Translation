## Text

**11:1** As the morning has passed, the king of Israel has passed away. For Israel was a child, and I loved him, and from Egypt I called My son.

**11:2** They called them; thus they went away from their presence. They made offerings to the Baals, and they sacrificed to images.

**11:3** And I was like a nurturer to Ephraim; I carried them in My arms, and they did not know that I cared for them.

**11:4** With the cords of Adam I will draw them, with the bonds of love; and I will be to them like one lifting up the yoke over their jaws, and I bent down to him so that he might eat.

**11:5** He will not return to the land of Egypt, and Assyria itself will be his king, because they refused to turn back.

**11:6** The sword has begun in his cities, and it will consume his chosen ones, and will devour their heads.

**11:7** And My people will hang in suspense for My return; but a yoke will be laid upon them all together, which will not be taken away.

**11:8** How shall I give you up, Ephraim? Shall I protect you, Israel? How shall I make you like Admah, set you like Zeboiim? My heart has turned within Me; at the same time My repentance is deeply troubled.

**11:9** I will not execute the fury of My anger; I will not turn back to destroy Ephraim, for I am God, and not man, the Holy One in your midst, and I will not enter the city.

**11:10** They will walk after the Lord; He will roar like a lion, for He Himself will roar, and the sons of the sea will be afraid.

**11:11** And they will fly away like a bird from Egypt, and like a dove from the land of the Assyrians; and I will settle them in their houses, says the Lord.

**11:12** Ephraim has surrounded Me with denial, and the house of Israel with deceit; but Judah went down as a witness with God, and is faithful with the holy ones.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — In the Clementine Vulgate, the opening sentence ("As the morning has passed, the king of Israel has passed away") begins 11:1 rather than closing 10:15 as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **11:4** — The Vulgate retains Adam as a proper name for Hebrew adam ("human" or "man") and uses future tenses ("I will draw," "I will be") before shifting to the past ("I bent down").
- **11:6** — The Vulgate reads Coepit ("has begun"), electos ejus ("his chosen ones"), and capita eorum ("their heads"), where the Hebrew text speaks of the sword whirling, consuming bars or oracle-priests, and devouring because of their counsels.
- **11:7** — A distinctively Vulgate reading ("hang in suspense for My return; but a yoke will be laid upon them") where the Hebrew text speaks of the people being bent on turning away and calling to the Most High.
- **11:8** — The Vulgate translates the second question as protegam te ("Shall I protect you?"), understanding the Hebrew verb from the root for "shield" rather than "hand over."
- **11:12** — The Vulgate reads testis descendit ("went down as a witness") where the Hebrew text reads "still rules" or "is unruly." In native Clementine Vulgate numbering, 11:12 concludes chapter 11 (mapped to Saints.Bible 12:1), shifting 12:1-14 one verse relative to Saints 12:2-15.
