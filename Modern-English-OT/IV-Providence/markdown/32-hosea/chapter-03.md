## Text

**3:1** And the Lord said to me, "Go again, and love a woman who is loved by a friend and is an adulteress, just as the Lord loves the sons of Israel, though they themselves look to foreign gods and love the husks of grapes."

**3:2** And I dug her for myself for fifteen pieces of silver, and a cor of barley, and half a cor of barley.

**3:3** And I said to her, "For many days you will wait for me; you will not commit fornication, and you will not belong to a man; yet I also will wait for you."

**3:4** Because for many days the sons of Israel will sit without a king, and without a prince, and without a sacrifice, and without an altar, and without an ephod, and without teraphim.

**3:5** And after these things the sons of Israel will return, and they will seek the Lord their God, and David their king; and they will tremble before the Lord and before His goodness in the last days.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — Latin "vinacia uvarum" ("the husks of grapes," i.e., pressed grape skins and seeds) is a distinctly Vulgate rendering where the Hebrew has "raisin cakes."
- **3:2** — Latin "fodi" ("I dug") translates the Hebrew verb via its homonym "dig/pierce" (which Jerome linked to piercing a servant's ear in Exodus 21:6) rather than "buy"; "a cor ... and half a cor" renders the Hebrew homer and lethech.
- **3:3** — The Vulgate uses "exspectabis ... exspectabo" ("you will wait for ... I will wait for") symmetrically at the opening and close of Hosea's speech.
- **3:4** — Latin "altari" ("an altar") follows the Septuagint tradition where the Hebrew reads "sacred pillar."
