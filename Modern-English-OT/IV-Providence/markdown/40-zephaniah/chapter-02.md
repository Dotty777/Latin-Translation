## Text

**2:1** Assemble, gather together, unlovable nation,

**2:2** before the decree brings forth the day that passes like dust, before the wrath of the Lord's fury comes upon you, before the day of the Lord's indignation comes upon you.

**2:3** Seek the Lord, all you meek of the earth, who have carried out His judgment; seek what is just, seek what is meek, if somehow you may be hidden on the day of the Lord's fury.

**2:4** For Gaza will be destroyed, and Ashkelon will be a wasteland; they will drive out Ashdod at noon, and Ekron will be uprooted.

**2:5** Woe to you who inhabit the strip by the sea, nation of the ruined! The word of the Lord is against you, Canaan, land of the Philistines; and I will destroy you so that there is no inhabitant.

**2:6** And the strip by the sea will be a resting place for shepherds and folds for flocks;

**2:7** and it will be the strip of the one who remains from the house of Judah: there they will pasture, in the houses of Ashkelon they will rest toward evening, for the Lord their God will visit them and turn away their captivity.

**2:8** "I have heard the reproach of Moab and the blasphemies of the sons of Ammon, with which they have reproached My people and magnified themselves against their borders.

**2:9** Therefore, as I live," says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, "surely Moab will be like Sodom, and the sons of Ammon like Gomorrah: a dryness of thorns, and heaps of salt, and a wasteland forever; the remnant of My people will plunder them, and the survivors of My nation will possess them."

**2:10** This will happen to them for their pride, because they have blasphemed and magnified themselves against the people of the Lord of hosts.

**2:11** Fearsome is the Lord against them, and He will diminish all the gods of the earth; and men will worship Him, each from his own place, all the islands of the nations.

**2:12** "And you too, Ethiopians, will be killed by My sword."

**2:13** And He will stretch out His hand against the north and destroy Assyria, and He will turn the beautiful one into a desolation, and into a trackless place, and like a wasteland.

**2:14** And flocks will lie down in her midst, all the beasts of the nations; and the pelican and the hedgehog will lodge on her thresholds: the voice of one singing in the window, the raven on the lintel, for I will diminish her strength.

**2:15** This is the glorious city dwelling in confidence, which said in her heart, "I am, and besides me there is no other anymore." How has she been turned into a wasteland, a lair of a beast? Everyone who passes by her will hiss and wave his hand.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — The Vulgate construes the opening clause as "before the decree brings forth the day that passes like dust," reading pulverem ("dust") where the Hebrew has "chaff."
- **2:5** — "Strip" renders Latin funiculus (literally "cord" or "measuring line," used across 2:5-7 for an allotted tract of land), and "nation of the ruined" (gens perditorum) reflects Jerome's etymological rendering of the Hebrew proper name Cherethites.
- **2:9** — The Vulgate reads "a dryness of thorns" (siccitas spinarum), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "a possession of nettles."
- **2:13** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew city name Nineveh etymologically as speciosam ("the beautiful one").
- **2:14** — Several Vulgate-distinct readings appear here: "pelican" (onocrotalus) and "hedgehog" (ericius) lodging "on her thresholds" (in liminibus, vs. Hebrew "capitals"), "the raven on the lintel" (corvus, matching the Septuagint vs. Hebrew "desolation"), and the first-person clause "for I will diminish her strength" (vs. Hebrew "for the cedarwork has been laid bare").
