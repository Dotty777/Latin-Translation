## Text

**3:1** Woe to the defiant and redeemed city, the dove!

**3:2** She has not listened to the voice, and she has not accepted discipline; she has not trusted in the Lord, she has not drawn near to her God.

**3:3** Her princes in her midst are like roaring lions; her judges are wolves in the evening; they left nothing for the morning.

**3:4** Her prophets are mad, faithless men; her priests have polluted what is holy; they have acted unjustly against the law.

**3:5** The just Lord in her midst will not do iniquity; morning by morning He will bring His judgment to light, and it will not be hidden; but the unjust one has not known shame.

**3:6** "I have destroyed nations, and their corners have been demolished; I have made their streets deserted, with no one passing by; their cities have been made desolate, with no man remaining, nor any inhabitant.

**3:7** I said, 'Surely you will fear Me; you will accept discipline; and her dwelling will not perish, because of all the things in which I have visited her.' Yet, rising at dawn, they corrupted all their thoughts.

**3:8** Therefore wait for Me," says the Lord, "on the day of My resurrection in the future; for My judgment is to gather the nations and assemble the kingdoms, and to pour out upon them My indignation, all the wrath of My fury; for in the fire of My zeal the whole earth will be devoured.

**3:9** For then I will restore to the peoples a chosen lip, so that all of them may call upon the name of the Lord and serve Him with one shoulder.

**3:10** Beyond the rivers of Ethiopia, from there My suppliants, the sons of My scattered ones, will bring an offering to Me.

**3:11** On that day you will not be put to shame over all your contrivances by which you have transgressed against Me, for then I will remove from your midst the boasters of your pride, and you will not exalt yourself anymore on My holy mountain.

**3:12** And I will leave in your midst a poor and needy people, and they will hope in the name of the Lord.

**3:13** The remnant of Israel will not do iniquity nor speak a lie, and a deceitful tongue will not be found in their mouth; for they themselves will pasture and lie down, and there will be no one to terrify them."

**3:14** Give praise, daughter of Zion; shout for joy, Israel; be glad and exult with all your heart, daughter of Jerusalem.

**3:15** The Lord has taken away your judgment; He has turned back your enemies. The King of Israel, the Lord, is in your midst; you will fear evil no more.

**3:16** On that day it will be said to Jerusalem, "Do not fear"; to Zion, "Do not let your hands grow weak."

**3:17** The Lord your God in your midst is mighty; He Himself will save. He will rejoice over you with gladness; He will be silent in His love; He will exult over you with praise.

**3:18** "I will gather the frivolous ones who had departed from the law, because they were from you, so that you will no longer have reproach over them.

**3:19** Look, I Myself will kill all who have afflicted you at that time; and I will save the limping one, and I will gather her who had been cast out; and I will make them into praise and into a name in every land of their shame,

**3:20** at that time when I bring you in, and at the time when I gather you. For I will make you into a name and into praise among all the peoples of the earth, when I turn back your captivity before your eyes," says the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The Vulgate reads "redeemed" (redempta, vs. Hebrew "defiled") and "dove" (columba, vs. Hebrew "oppressing"), reflecting alternate root derivations of the Hebrew consonants.
- **3:8** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "on the day of My resurrection in the future" (in die resurrectionis meæ in futurum), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "for the day when I rise up to prey" (or "as a witness").
- **3:18** — The Vulgate reads "the frivolous ones who had departed from the law" (Nugas, qui a lege recesserant), a distinctive rendering of the Hebrew phrase "those grieving over the appointed feast."
- **3:20** — "Turn back your captivity" (like "turn away their captivity" in 2:7) preserves the Vulgate's literal rendering of the Hebrew idiom often translated "restore your fortunes."
