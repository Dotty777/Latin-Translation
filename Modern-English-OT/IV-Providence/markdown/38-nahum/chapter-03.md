## Text

**3:1** Woe, city of bloodshed, wholly full of the tearing apart of falsehood! Plunder will not depart from you.

**3:2** The sound of the whip, and the sound of the rushing of the wheel, and of the snorting horse, and of the surging chariot, and of the mounting horseman,

**3:3** and of the flashing sword, and of the glittering spear, and of a slaughtered multitude, and of heavy ruin; and there is no end to the corpses, and they will collapse over their bodies.

**3:4** Because of the multitude of the fornications of the prostitute, beautiful and charming and possessing sorceries, who sold nations through her fornications and families through her sorceries.

**3:5** "Look, I am against you," says the Lord of hosts, "and I will uncover your shameful parts over your face; and I will show the nations your nakedness, and the kingdoms your disgrace.

**3:6** And I will throw abominations upon you, and I will subject you to insults, and I will make you an example.

**3:7** And it will be: everyone who sees you will recoil from you and will say, 'Nineveh has been devastated.' Who will shake his head over you? From where will I seek a comforter for you?"

**3:8** Are you any better than Alexandria of the peoples, who dwells among the rivers? Waters are all around her, whose wealth is the sea; waters are her walls.

**3:9** Ethiopia was her strength, and Egypt, and there is no end; Africa and the Libyans were for your help.

**3:10** Yet even she herself was led into exile, into captivity: her little ones were dashed to pieces at the head of all the streets, and over her honored men they cast lots, and all her leading men were fastened in shackles.

**3:11** And so you also will become drunk, and you will be despised; and you will seek help from the enemy.

**3:12** All your fortresses are like fig trees with their unripe figs: if they are shaken, they will fall into the mouth of the eater.

**3:13** Look, your people are women in your midst: to your enemies the gates of your land will be thrown wide open; fire will devour your bars.

**3:14** Draw water for yourself for the siege: build up your fortresses, go into the clay and tread it; kneading it, take hold of the brick.

**3:15** There fire will consume you, you will perish by the sword, it will devour you like the young locust: gather together like the young locust, multiply like the locust.

**3:16** You have made your trading ventures more numerous than the stars of the sky; the young locust has spread out and flown away.

**3:17** Your guards are like locusts, and your little ones are like locusts of locusts, which settle in the hedges on a day of cold: the sun has risen, and they have flown away, and their place where they were is not known.

**3:18** Your shepherds have slumbered, king of Assyria, your princes will be buried; your people have hidden in the mountains, and there is no one to gather them.

**3:19** Your shattering is not hidden; your wound is most severe. All who have heard the report about you have clapped their hands together over you; for over whom has your wickedness not always passed?

## Translator Notes

- **3:5** — Where the Hebrew says 'I will lift up your skirts over your face', the Vulgate translates explicitly as 'I will uncover your shameful parts over your face' (revelabo pudenda tua in facie tua).
- **3:8** — The Vulgate distinctly identifies the city as 'Alexandria of the peoples' (Alexandria populorum), rendering the Hebrew No-Amon (Thebes in Egypt).
- **3:9** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew Put as Africa. Like the Hebrew text, the Latin shifts in the second clause from third person ('her strength') to second person ('for your help', in auxilio tuo).
- **3:17** — Where the Hebrew refers to military officials ('your marshals' or 'your scribes'), the Vulgate distinctly reads 'your little ones' (parvuli tui) and renders the Hebrew swarm idiom literally as 'locusts of locusts' (locustae locustarum).
- **3:18** — The Vulgate distinctly reads 'your princes will be buried' (sepelientur principes tui) and 'your people have hidden' (latitavit populus tuus), where the Hebrew has 'your nobles sleep' and 'your people are scattered'.
