## Text

**5:1** King Belshazzar made a great banquet for his thousand nobles, and each one was drinking according to his age.

**5:2** Therefore, being already drunk, he commanded that the gold and silver vessels be brought which Nebuchadnezzar his father had carried away from the temple that was in Jerusalem, so that the king and his nobles, and his wives and concubines, might drink from them.

**5:3** Then the gold and silver vessels were brought which he had carried away from the temple that had been in Jerusalem, and the king and his nobles, his wives and his concubines drank from them.

**5:4** They were drinking wine and praising their gods of gold and silver, of bronze, iron, wood, and stone.

**5:5** In that same hour fingers appeared, as of a man's hand writing opposite the lampstand on the surface of the wall of the royal palace; and the king watched the joints of the hand that was writing.

**5:6** Then the king's face changed, and his thoughts were disturbing him; and the joints of his loins were loosened, and his knees were knocking against one another.

**5:7** Therefore the king cried out loudly that they should bring in the magicians, the Chaldeans, and the diviners. And speaking out, the king said to the wise men of Babylon, "Whoever reads this writing and makes its interpretation clear to me will be clothed in purple, and will have a gold chain on his neck, and will be third in my kingdom."

**5:8** Then all the wise men of the king came in, and they could neither read the writing nor declare the interpretation to the king.

**5:9** Because of this, King Belshazzar was greatly disturbed, and his expression was altered; and his nobles also were troubled.

**5:10** Now the queen, on account of the matter that had happened to the king and his nobles, entered the banquet hall; and speaking out, she said, "King, live forever! Do not let your thoughts disturb you, nor let your face be altered.

**5:11** There is a man in your kingdom who has the spirit of the holy gods in him, and in the days of your father knowledge and wisdom were found in him; for King Nebuchadnezzar your father also appointed him chief of the magicians, enchanters, Chaldeans, and diviners, your father, I say, king,

**5:12** because a greater spirit, and prudence and understanding, and the interpretation of dreams, and the revealing of secrets, and the untying of bound things were found in him, that is, in Daniel, to whom the king gave the name Belteshazzar. Now therefore let Daniel be called, and he will tell the interpretation."

**5:13** Therefore Daniel was brought in before the king, and the king spoke first to him and said, "Are you Daniel, from the sons of the captivity of Judah, whom my father the king brought from Judea?

**5:16** Furthermore, I have heard about you that you are able to interpret obscure things and untie bound things. If therefore you are able to read the writing and declare its interpretation to me, you will be clothed in purple, and you will have a gold chain around your neck, and you will be the third ruler in my kingdom."

**5:17** In answer to these things, Daniel said before the king, "Let your gifts be for yourself, and give the presents of your house to another; yet I will read the writing to you, king, and I will show its interpretation to you. And this is the writing that has been set down: Mene, Tekel, Peres.

**5:23** but you have lifted yourself up against the Sovereign of heaven, and the vessels of His house were brought before you, and you and your nobles, your wives and your concubines drank wine from them. You also praised gods of silver and gold, and of bronze, iron, wood, and stone, which neither see nor hear nor perceive; yet God, who holds your breath in His hand, and all your ways, you have not glorified.

**5:26** And this is the interpretation of the word. Mene: God has numbered your kingdom and brought it to an end.

**5:27** Tekel: you have been weighed on the scales and have been found lacking.

**5:28** Peres: your kingdom has been divided and given to the Medes and Persians."

**5:29** Then, at the king's command, Daniel was clothed in purple, and a gold chain was placed around his neck; and it was proclaimed concerning him that he held authority as third in his kingdom.

**5:30** That same night Belshazzar, the Chaldean king, was killed.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**5:15** *(after `5:13`)* And now the wise men, the magicians, have come into my presence to read this writing and make its interpretation known to me; and they were unable to declare the meaning of this message.

**5:18** *(after `5:17`)* King, the Most High God gave a kingdom and majesty, glory and honor to your father Nebuchadnezzar.

**5:19** *(after `5:17`)* And because of the majesty that He had given him, all peoples, tribes, and languages trembled and feared him: those whom he wished, he put to death; and those whom he wished, he struck; and those whom he wished, he exalted; and those whom he wished, he humbled.

**5:20** *(after `5:17`)* But when his heart was lifted up, and his spirit was hardened into pride, he was deposed from the throne of his kingdom, and his glory was taken away;

**5:21** *(after `5:17`)* and he was cast out from the sons of men, and even his heart was placed with the beasts, and his dwelling was with the wild donkeys; he also ate grass like an ox, and his body was soaked with the dew of heaven, until he came to know that the Most High has authority in the kingdom of men and will raise up over it whomever He wills.

**5:22** *(after `5:17`)* You also, his son Belshazzar, have not humbled your heart, although you knew all these things;

**5:24** *(after `5:23`)* For this reason, the part of the hand that wrote what has been inscribed was sent from Him.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — Vulgate unusquisque secundum suam bibebat aetatem ("each one was drinking according to his age") is a distinctly Vulgate reading, where the Aramaic text states that the king drank wine before the thousand.
- **5:2** — Vulgate jam temulentus ("being already drunk") renders the Aramaic idiom "at the taste of the wine" more explicitly.
- **5:5** — Vulgate articulos manus scribentis literally means "the joints of the hand that was writing," rendering the Aramaic phrase for the palm or back of the hand.
- **5:17** — In this Latin text, the verse concludes with the inscription sentence (usually 5:25), which gives three words in the Vulgate—Mane, Thecel, Phares (standardized here as Mene, Tekel, Peres)—rather than the four-word Masoretic sequence (Mene, Mene, Tekel, Upharsin).
- **5:19 *(additional)*** — The Vulgate reads percutiebat ("he struck"), where the Aramaic text has "he kept alive."
- **5:21 *(additional)*** — The Vulgate literally reads "his heart was placed with the beasts" (cor ejus cum bestiis positum est).
- **5:24 *(additional)*** — Latin articulus manus literally refers to the joint or extremity of the hand.
