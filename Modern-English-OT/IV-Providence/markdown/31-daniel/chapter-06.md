## Text

**6:1** And Darius the Mede succeeded to the kingdom, being sixty-two years old.

**6:2** It pleased Darius, and he appointed one hundred and twenty satraps over the kingdom to be throughout his whole kingdom;

**6:3** and over them three princes, of whom Daniel was one, so that the satraps might render an account to them and the king might not suffer trouble.

**6:4** Therefore Daniel surpassed all the princes and satraps, because the Spirit of God was more abundant in him.

**6:5** Moreover, the king was planning to appoint him over the entire kingdom; so the princes and satraps were seeking a pretext that they might find against Daniel from the side of the king, and they could discover no grounds or suspicion, because he was faithful, and no fault or suspicion was found in him.

**6:6** Therefore those men said, "We will not find any pretext against this Daniel, unless perhaps in the law of his God."

**6:7** Then the princes and satraps approached the king stealthily and spoke to him in this way: "King Darius, live forever!

**6:8** All the princes of your kingdom, the magistrates and satraps, the senators and judges, have taken counsel that an imperial decree and an edict should go out: that everyone who asks any request from any god or man for thirty days, except from you, king, should be thrown into the den of lions.

**6:9** Now therefore, king, confirm the ruling and write the decree, so that what has been established by the Medes and Persians may not be altered, nor may anyone be permitted to transgress it."

**6:10** Accordingly, King Darius set forth the edict and established it.

**6:11** When Daniel learned this, that is, that the law had been established, he entered his house; and with the windows open in his upper room toward Jerusalem, three times a day he bent his knees, and worshiped, and gave thanks before his God, just as he had been accustomed to do before.

**6:12** Therefore those men, investigating very closely, found Daniel praying and imploring his God.

**6:13** And approaching, they spoke to the king concerning the edict: "King, did you not establish that every man who would ask anyone of the gods or men for thirty days, except you, king, should be thrown into the den of lions?" Answering them, the king said, "The word is true according to the decree of the Medes and Persians, which it is not permitted to transgress."

**6:14** Then answering, they said before the king, "Daniel, from the sons of the captivity of Judah, has paid no regard to your law and to the edict that you established, but three times a day he prays with his supplication."

**6:15** When the king heard this word, he was greatly grieved, and he set his heart on Daniel's behalf to deliver him, and until the setting of the sun he was laboring to rescue him.

**6:17** Then the king commanded, and they brought Daniel and threw him into the den of lions. And the king said to Daniel, "Your God, whom you always worship, He Himself will deliver you."

**6:18** And one stone was brought and placed over the mouth of the den, which the king sealed with his ring and with the ring of his nobles, so that nothing might be done against Daniel.

**6:19** And the king went away to his house and went to sleep without supper, and no food was brought before him; moreover, sleep also departed from him.

**6:20** Then the king, rising at first light, went in haste to the den of lions.

**6:21** And approaching the den, he called out to Daniel with a tearful voice and addressed him: "Daniel, servant of the living God, your God, whom you always serve, do you think He has been able to deliver you from the lions?"

**6:22** And Daniel, answering the king, said, "King, live forever!

**6:23** My God sent His angel and shut the mouths of the lions, and they did not harm me, because before Him righteousness was found in me; and before you as well, king, I have committed no offense."

**6:24** Then the king rejoiced exceedingly over him and commanded Daniel to be brought out of the den. And Daniel was brought out of the den, and no injury was found on him, because he believed in his God.

**6:25** And at the king's command, those men who had accused Daniel were brought and thrown into the den of lions, they themselves, and their children, and their wives; and they did not reach the floor of the den before the lions seized them and shattered all their bones.

**6:26** Then King Darius wrote to all peoples, tribes, and languages dwelling on the whole earth: "May peace be multiplied to you.

**6:27** A decree has been established by me that throughout my entire empire and kingdom people should tremble before and fear the God of Daniel; for He Himself is the living God, and eternal through the ages, and His kingdom will not be dissolved, and His authority is forever.

**6:28** He Himself is a deliverer and savior, working signs and wonders in heaven and on earth, who delivered Daniel from the den of lions."

**6:29** Moreover, Daniel persevered until the reign of Darius and the reign of Cyrus the Persian.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**6:16** *(after `6:15`)* But those men, understanding the king, said to him, "Know, king, that it is the law of the Medes and Persians that no decree which the king has established may be changed."

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — In the Vulgate, chapter 6 begins here with Darius's accession (numbered 5:31 in the Masoretic Text), shifting subsequent verse numbers in the chapter by one.
- **6:4** — Vulgate 'spiritus Dei amplior' explicitly adds 'of God,' whereas the Aramaic Masoretic Text reads simply 'an extraordinary spirit.'
- **6:7** — Vulgate 'surripuerunt regi' ('approached the king stealthily') portrays the officials creeping in craftily upon Darius, where the Aramaic verb denotes coming together in a throng or conspiracy.
- **6:19** — Vulgate 'cibi' ('food') renders an obscure Aramaic term, and 'dormivit incoenatus' literally says the king went to sleep without supper before sleep departed from him.
- **6:29** — Vulgate 'perseveravit usque ad' ('persevered until') differs from the Aramaic, which states that Daniel 'prospered during' the reign of Darius and the reign of Cyrus the Persian.
- **6:16 *(additional)*** — The Vulgate reads intelligentes regem ("understanding the king"), where the Aramaic text describes the men coming together in a throng to the king.
