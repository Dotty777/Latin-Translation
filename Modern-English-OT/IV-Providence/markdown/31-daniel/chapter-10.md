## Text

**10:1** In the third year of Cyrus, king of the Persians, a word was revealed to Daniel, surnamed Belteshazzar, both a true word and great strength; and he understood the message, for understanding is needed in a vision.

**10:2** In those days I, Daniel, was mourning for the days of three weeks.

**10:3** I did not eat desirable bread, and meat and wine did not enter my mouth, nor indeed was I anointed with ointment, until the days of the three weeks were completed.

**10:4** Now on the twenty-fourth day of the first month, I was beside the great river, which is the Tigris.

**10:5** And I lifted up my eyes and saw, and look, one man clothed in linen, and his waist was belted with refined gold.

**10:6** And his body was like chrysolite, and his face like the appearance of lightning, and his eyes like a burning torch; and his arms, and what is below down to the feet, were like the appearance of glowing bronze; and the sound of his words was like the sound of a multitude.

**10:7** Now I, Daniel, alone saw the vision; however, the men who were with me did not see it, but an overwhelming terror rushed upon them, and they fled into hiding.

**10:8** So I, left alone, saw this great vision, and no strength remained in me; and even my appearance was altered within me, and I withered away, nor did I have any vigor at all.

**10:9** And I heard the sound of his words; and as I heard them, I lay stunned on my face, and my face clung to the ground.

**10:10** And look, a hand touched me and raised me up onto my knees and onto the joints of my hands.

**10:11** And he said to me, "Daniel, man of desires, understand the words that I speak to you, and stand in your place, for now I have been sent to you." And when he had spoken this message to me, I stood trembling.

**10:12** And he said to me, "Do not fear, Daniel, for from the first day on which you set your heart to understand, so as to afflict yourself in the sight of your God, your words were heard; and I have come because of your words.

**10:13** Now the prince of the kingdom of the Persians withstood me for twenty-one days; and look, Michael, one of the chief princes, came to my aid, and I remained there beside the king of the Persians.

**10:14** Now I have come to teach you what is to come to your people in the last days, because the vision is still for days."

**10:15** And while he was speaking to me with words of this kind, I cast my face down to the ground and kept silent.

**10:16** And look, one like the likeness of a son of man touched my lips; and opening my mouth, I spoke and said to him who was standing facing me, "My lord, at the vision of you my joints have been loosened, and no vigor has remained in me.

**10:17** And how will the servant of my lord be able to speak with my lord? For no vigor has remained in me, and even my breath is cut off."

**10:18** Therefore one like the appearance of a man touched me again and strengthened me.

**10:19** And he said, "Do not be afraid, man of desires; peace to you; be strengthened and be strong." And while he was speaking with me, I regained strength and said, "Speak, my lord, for you have strengthened me."

**10:20** And he said, "Do you know why I have come to you? And now I will return to fight against the prince of the Persians. When I was going out, the prince of the Greeks appeared, coming.

**10:21** Nevertheless, I will declare to you what is set down in the writing of truth; and no one is my helper in all these things except Michael your prince."

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — Latin fortitudo magna ("great strength") and intelligentia enim est opus in visione ("for understanding is needed in a vision") are distinctive Vulgate renderings of the Hebrew clauses usually taken as "a great conflict" and "he had understanding of the vision." Latin Baltassar is the Latinate form of Daniel's Babylonian name Belteshazzar.
- **10:11** — Latin vir desideriorum ("man of desires") translates literally the Hebrew idiom for a greatly esteemed or beloved man; the same phrase recurs in 10:19.
- **10:16** — Latin dissolutae sunt compages meae ("my joints have been loosened") is a distinctly Vulgate reading corresponding to the Hebrew clause usually rendered "pains/anguish turned upon me."
- **10:20** — Latin Cum ego egrederer, apparuit princeps Graecorum veniens uses past tenses ("When I was going out, the prince of the Greeks appeared, coming"), whereas the Hebrew is normally rendered as future.
