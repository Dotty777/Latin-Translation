## Text

**7:1** Woe to me, for I have become like one who gathers clusters of the vintage in autumn! There is no bunch of grapes to eat; my soul has desired early-ripe figs.

**7:2** The holy one has perished from the earth, and there is no upright one among people; all of them lie in wait in blood; a man hunts his brother to death.

**7:3** They call the evil of their hands good: the prince demands, and the judge is engaged in repayment; and the great man has spoken the desire of his soul, and they have thrown it into confusion.

**7:4** The one who is best among them is like a brier, and the one who is upright is like a thorn from a hedge. The day of your watch, your visitation has come; now will be their devastation.

**7:5** Do not believe a friend, and do not put confidence in a leader; from her who sleeps in your bosom, guard the gates of your mouth.

**7:6** For a son brings dishonor to his father, and a daughter rises up against her mother, a daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law; and the enemies of a man are those of his household.

**7:7** As for me, however, I will look to the Lord; I will await God, my Savior; my God will hear me.

**7:8** Do not rejoice over me, my enemy, because I have fallen; I will rise up when I sit in darkness; the Lord is my light.

**7:9** I will bear the anger of the Lord, because I have sinned against Him, until He judges my case and executes my judgment. He will lead me out into the light; I will see His righteousness.

**7:10** And my enemy will look on, and she will be covered with shame, she who says to me, "Where is the Lord your God?" My eyes will look upon her; now she will be for trampling like the mud of the streets.

**7:11** A day for your walls to be built; on that day the law will be far away.

**7:12** On that day one will come even to you from Assyria, and as far as the fortified cities, and from the fortified cities as far as the river, and from sea to sea, and from mountain to mountain.

**7:13** And the earth will become a desolation because of its inhabitants, and because of the fruit of their thoughts.

**7:14** Shepherd Your people with Your rod, the flock of Your inheritance, dwelling alone in a woodland, in the midst of Carmel. They will graze in Bashan and Gilead as in ancient days.

**7:15** According to the days of your going out from the land of Egypt, I will show him marvels.

**7:16** The nations will see and be put to shame over all their might. They will put their hand over their mouth; their ears will be deaf.

**7:17** They will lick the dust like serpents; like crawling things of the earth, they will be thrown into turmoil in their houses. They will dread the Lord our God, and they will fear You.

**7:18** Who, God, is like You, who takes away iniquity and passes over the sin of the remnant of Your inheritance? He will not send forth His fury any longer, because He desires mercy.

**7:19** He will turn back and have mercy on us; He will put down our iniquities, and He will cast all our sins into the depth of the sea.

**7:20** You will give truth to Jacob, mercy to Abraham, which You swore to our fathers from ancient days.

## Translator Notes

- **7:2** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew herem as ad mortem ("to death") rather than "with a net," and uses the ablative in sanguine ("in blood") rather than "for blood."
- **7:3** — The Vulgate reads Malum manuum suarum dicunt bonum ("They call the evil of their hands good") and conturbaverunt eam ("they have thrown it into confusion"), differing from the Hebrew ("hands are on evil to do it well" and "they weave it together").
- **7:11** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew hoq as lex ("the law") rather than "boundary," giving longe fiet lex ("the law will be far away") rather than an extended border.
- **7:12** — The Vulgate translates Hebrew Matsor as civitates munitas ("the fortified cities") rather than a poetic name for Egypt, and preserves the indefinite singular verb veniet ("one will come").
- **7:18** — The Clementine Vulgate punctuates Deus as a vocative ("Who, God, is like You") and reads Non immittet ultra furorem suum ("He will not send forth His fury any longer"), where the Hebrew has "Who is a God like You" and "He does not retain His anger forever."
