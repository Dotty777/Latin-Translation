## Text

**1:1** The vision of Obadiah. This is what the Lord God says to Edom: We have heard a report from the Lord, and He has sent an envoy to the nations: "Rise up, and let us rise together against him for battle."

**1:2** "Look, I have made you small among the nations; you are utterly contemptible.

**1:3** The pride of your heart has lifted you up, you who dwell in the crevices of the rocks, raising your throne high, who say in your heart, 'Who will pull me down to the ground?'

**1:4** If you are raised high like an eagle, and if you set your nest among the stars, from there I will pull you down," says the Lord.

**1:5** "If thieves had come in to you, if robbers by night, how would you have fallen silent? Would they not have stolen enough for themselves? If grape harvesters had come in to you, would they have left at least a cluster for you?

**1:6** How they have searched Esau; they have tracked down his hidden things!

**1:7** All the way to the border they have sent you out; all the men of your covenant have tricked you. The men of your peace have prevailed against you; those who eat with you will set an ambush beneath you. There is no discernment in him.

**1:8** Will I not on that day," says the Lord, "destroy the wise from Idumea and discernment from Mount Esau?

**1:9** And your mighty ones from the south will be afraid, so that every man may perish from Mount Esau.

**1:10** Because of the slaughter and because of the iniquity against your brother Jacob, shame will cover you, and you will perish forever.

**1:11** On the day when you stood against him, when strangers were capturing his army, and foreigners were entering his gates and casting lots over Jerusalem, you too were like one of them.

**1:12** And you shall not look with scorn on the day of your brother, on the day of his exile; and you shall not rejoice over the sons of Judah on the day of their destruction; and you shall not magnify your mouth on the day of distress.

**1:13** Neither shall you enter the gate of My people on the day of their ruin, nor shall you too look with scorn on his calamities on the day of his devastation. And you shall not be sent out against his army on the day of his devastation,

**1:14** nor shall you stand at the exits to kill those who have fled, and you shall not shut in his remaining ones on the day of tribulation.

**1:15** For the day of the Lord is near upon all the nations: as you have done, it will be done to you; He will turn your retribution back upon your head.

**1:16** For as you have drunk on My holy mountain, all the nations will drink continually; and they will drink and swallow, and they will be as though they did not exist.

**1:17** And on Mount Zion there will be salvation, and it will be holy; and the house of Jacob will possess those who had possessed it.

**1:18** And the house of Jacob will be a fire, and the house of Joseph a flame, and the house of Esau stubble; and they will be kindled among them and devour them, and there will be no remnant of the house of Esau," for the Lord has spoken.

**1:19** And those who are in the south will inherit Mount Esau, and those in the plains, the Philistines; and they will possess the region of Ephraim and the region of Samaria, and Benjamin will possess Gilead.

**1:20** And the exiles of this army of the sons of Israel will possess all the places of the Canaanites as far as Zarephath, and the exiles of Jerusalem who are in Bosphorus will possess the cities of the south.

**1:21** And saviors will ascend to Mount Zion to judge Mount Esau, and the kingdom will belong to the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **1:3** — Vulgate extulit te ("has lifted you up") and exaltantem solium tuum ("raising your throne high") differ from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "has deceived you" and "whose dwelling is high."
- **1:5** — Vulgate conticuisses ("would you have fallen silent") reflects the Hebrew root meaning "be silent" rather than the homonymous root meaning "be destroyed," and numquid saltem racemum lacks a negative particle while asking about a single cluster.
- **1:8** — The Vulgate uses the Greco-Latin regional name Idumaea ("Idumea") here rather than Edom (used in 1:1).
- **1:9** — Vulgate a meridie ("from the south") translates the Hebrew name Teman as a compass direction, and the Vulgate attaches "Because of the slaughter" (Propter interfectionem) to the start of 1:10 rather than the end of 1:9.
- **1:17** — Vulgate salvatio ("salvation") and eos qui se possederant ("those who had possessed it") differ from the Masoretic Text's "those who escape" and "their possessions."
- **1:20** — Vulgate Bosphoro ("Bosphorus") identifies the Hebrew place name Sepharad with the region of the Bosporus, and exercitus ("army," as in 1:11 and 1:13) translates the Hebrew term often rendered "host" or "wealth."
