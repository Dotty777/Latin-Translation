## Text

**6:1** Woe to you who are wealthy in Zion and trust in the mountain of Samaria, you nobles, heads of the peoples, entering the house of Israel with pomp!

**6:2** Cross over to Calneh and see, and go from there to great Hamath, and go down to Gath of the Philistines, and to all the finest of these kingdoms: whether their border is wider than your border.

**6:3** You who are set apart for the evil day, and draw near to the throne of iniquity;

**6:4** who sleep on ivory beds and act wantonly on your couches; who eat a lamb from the flock and calves from the midst of the herd;

**6:5** who sing to the sound of the harp; like David they thought they had instruments of song,

**6:6** drinking wine in bowls and anointed with the finest ointment, yet they suffered nothing over the crushing of Joseph.

**6:7** Therefore now they will go into exile at the head of those going into exile, and the faction of those who act wantonly will be taken away.

**6:8** The Lord God has sworn by His soul, says the Lord God of hosts: "I detest the pride of Jacob, and I hate his houses, and I will hand over the city with its inhabitants."

**6:9** And if ten men are left in one house, they also will die.

**6:10** And his relative will take him up and burn him, to carry the bones out of the house; and he will say to the one who is in the innermost parts of the house, "Is there still anyone with you?"

**6:11** And he will answer, "It is the end." And he will say to him, "Be silent, and do not remember the name of the Lord."

**6:12** For look, the Lord will command, and will strike the larger house with ruins, and the smaller house with cracks.

**6:13** Can horses run on rocks, or can one plow with wild oxen? For you have turned judgment into bitterness, and the fruit of righteousness into wormwood.

**6:14** You who rejoice in nothing, who say, "Have we not by our own strength taken horns for ourselves?"

**6:15** "For look, I will raise up against you, house of Israel," says the Lord God of hosts, "a nation, and it will crush you from the entrance of Hamath to the torrent of the desert."

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew "at ease" as "wealthy" (opulenti) and "to whom the house of Israel comes" as "entering the house of Israel with pomp" (ingredientes pompatice domum Israel).
- **6:3** — Where the Hebrew has "who put far away the evil day," the Vulgate uses a passive verb, "who are set apart for the evil day" (separati estis in diem malum).
- **6:5** — The Vulgate reads "they thought they had instruments of song" (putaverunt se habere vasa cantici) where the Hebrew has "they invent for themselves instruments of song."
- **6:10** — In native Clementine Vulgate numbering, the relative's question closes 6:10 and the reply follows as restored verse 6:11 (both corresponding to Saints.Bible 6:10).
- **6:11** — Stage 1 restored Clementine Vulgate Amos 6:11 (corresponding to the second half of Saints.Bible 6:10). Latin non recorderis literally means 'do not remember/call to mind' (rendering the Hebrew idiom for mentioning or invoking the divine name).
- **6:12** — In native Clementine Vulgate numbering, 6:12-15 map to Saints.Bible 6:11-14 following the restoration of native 6:11.
- **6:13** — The Vulgate specifies "wild oxen" (in bubalis) rather than ordinary oxen, and uses an impersonal passive ("can one plow," arari potest).
