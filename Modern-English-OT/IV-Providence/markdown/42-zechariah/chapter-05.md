## Text

**5:1** And I turned and lifted up my eyes and saw, and look, a flying scroll.

**5:2** And he said to me, "What do you see?" And I said, "I see a flying scroll; its length is twenty cubits, and its width is ten cubits."

**5:3** And he said to me, "This is the curse that goes out over the face of all the earth; for every thief, as is written there, will be judged, and everyone who swears will likewise be judged by this.

**5:4** 'I will bring it out,' says the Lord of hosts, 'and it will come to the house of the thief, and to the house of the one who swears falsely in My name; and it will remain in the middle of his house, and will consume it, both its timbers and its stones.'"

**5:5** And the angel who was speaking in me came out and said to me, "Lift up your eyes, and see what this is that is coming out."

**5:6** And I said, "What is it?" And he said, "This is an amphora coming out." And he said, "This is their eye in the whole earth."

**5:7** And look, a talent of lead was being carried, and look, one woman sitting in the middle of the amphora.

**5:8** And he said, "This is impiety." And he threw her into the middle of the amphora, and put the mass of lead upon its mouth.

**5:9** And I lifted up my eyes and saw, and look, two women coming out, and wind was in their wings, and they had wings like the wings of a kite; and they lifted up the amphora between the earth and heaven.

**5:10** And I said to the angel who was speaking in me, "Where are these women carrying the amphora?"

**5:11** And he said to me, "So that a house may be built for it in the land of Shinar, and that it may be established and placed there upon its base."

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — The Vulgate reads "as is written there, will be judged" and "will likewise be judged by this" ("judicabitur"), rendering the Hebrew verb for being purged or cut off in terms of judicial condemnation.
- **5:6** — The Vulgate translates Hebrew "ephah" (a dry grain measure) as "amphora" (a standard Roman storage jar and measure) throughout verses 6-10, and preserves Hebrew "their eye" ("oculus eorum"), where the Septuagint reads "their iniquity".
- **5:7** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew "a round weight/cover of lead was lifted" as "a talent of lead was being carried" ("talentum plumbi portabatur").
- **5:9** — The Vulgate reads "kite" ("milvi") where the Hebrew text has "stork".
