## Text

**11:1** Open your gates, Lebanon, and let fire consume your cedars.

**11:2** Wail, fir tree, because the cedar has fallen, for the magnificent ones have been devastated; wail, oaks of Bashan, for the fortified forest has been cut down.

**11:3** The sound of the wailing of the shepherds, because their magnificence has been devastated; the sound of the roaring of lions, for the pride of the Jordan has been devastated.

**11:4** This is what the Lord my God says: "Shepherd the sheep of slaughter,

**11:5** which those who had possessed them killed and did not grieve, and they sold them, saying, 'Blessed be the Lord! We have become rich,' and their shepherds did not spare them.

**11:6** And I will no longer spare the inhabitants of the land," says the Lord. "Look, I will hand men over, each one into the hand of his neighbor and into the hand of his king; and they will crush the land, and I will not rescue them from their hand."

**11:7** And I will shepherd the sheep of slaughter because of this, poor of the flock! And I took for myself two staffs: one I called Beauty, and the other I called Cord, and I shepherded the flock.

**11:8** And I cut off three shepherds in one month, and my soul grew impatient with them, since indeed their soul also wavered against me.

**11:9** And I said, "I will not shepherd you. What is dying, let it die, and what is being cut off, let it be cut off; and let the rest each devour the flesh of his neighbor."

**11:10** And I took my staff that was called Beauty and severed it, so that I might make void my covenant which I struck with all the peoples.

**11:11** And it was made void on that day; and in this way the poor of the flock, who keep watch for me, knew that it is the word of the Lord.

**11:12** And I said to them, "If it is good in your eyes, bring my wages; and if not, refrain." And they weighed out my wages, thirty pieces of silver.

**11:13** And the Lord said to me, "Throw it to the sculptor, a splendid price at which I was appraised by them." And I took the thirty pieces of silver and threw them into the house of the Lord, to the sculptor.

**11:14** And I cut through my second staff, which was named Cord, so that I might dissolve the brotherhood between Judah and Israel.

**11:15** And the Lord said to me, "Take for yourself once more the equipment of a foolish shepherd.

**11:16** For look, I will raise up a shepherd in the land who will not visit the abandoned, will not seek the scattered, and will not heal the broken, and will not nourish what stands; and he will eat the flesh of the fat ones and break apart their hooves.

**11:17** Shepherd and idol abandoning the flock: a sword upon his arm and upon his right eye! His arm will be dried up with dryness, and his right eye, growing dark, will be obscured."

## Translator Notes

- **11:5** — The Vulgate reads qui possederant ("those who had possessed them") and non dolebant ("did not grieve"), where the Hebrew has "their buyers" and "are not held guilty."
- **11:7** — The Vulgate preserves the future tense pascam ("I will shepherd") before shifting to the past, treats o pauperes gregis as a vocative ("O poor of the flock"), and renders the second staff's name as Funiculum ("Cord") rather than Hebrew "Union."
- **11:8** — Latin contracta est anima mea literally means "my soul was contracted" (an idiom for growing impatient), and variavit ("wavered" or "changed") is a Vulgate-distinct rendering where the Hebrew means "loathed."
- **11:13** — The Vulgate translates Hebrew yotzer ("potter") as statuarium ("sculptor" or "molder of statues"), repeated twice in this verse.
- **11:17** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew "worthless shepherd" as two vocative nouns, O pastor, et idolum ("Shepherd and idol").
