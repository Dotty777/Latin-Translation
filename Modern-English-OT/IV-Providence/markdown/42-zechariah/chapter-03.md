## Text

**3:1** And the Lord showed me Joshua the high priest, standing before the angel of the Lord; and Satan was standing at his right hand to oppose him.

**3:2** And the Lord said to Satan, "May the Lord rebuke you, Satan! And may the Lord who has chosen Jerusalem rebuke you! Is this one not a burning stick snatched from the fire?"

**3:3** And Joshua was clothed in filthy garments, and he was standing before the face of the angel.

**3:4** He answered and spoke to those who were standing before him, saying, "Remove the filthy garments from him." And he said to him, "Look, I have removed your iniquity from you, and I have clothed you with fresh garments."

**3:5** And he said, "Place a clean turban on his head." And they placed a clean turban on his head and clothed him with garments; and the angel of the Lord was standing by.

**3:6** And the angel of the Lord solemnly charged Joshua, saying,

**3:7** "This is what the Lord of hosts says: If you walk in My ways and keep My charge, you also will judge My house and will keep My courts, and I will give you those who walk from among these who now stand here.

**3:8** Listen, Joshua the high priest, you and your friends who dwell before you, for they are foreshadowing men: for look, I will bring My servant, the Dawn.

**3:9** For look, the stone that I have placed before Joshua: on one stone are seven eyes; look, I will carve its engraving, declares the Lord of hosts, and I will remove the iniquity of that land in one day.

**3:10** In that day, says the Lord of hosts, each man will invite his friend under the vine and under the fig tree."

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The Vulgate renders the high priest Joshua's name as Jesus (the Greek and Latin form of Hebrew Yehoshua), a verbal correspondence frequently noted in Christian typological interpretation.
- **3:4** — Latin mutatoriis ("fresh garments," literally "changes of clothing") denotes clean ceremonial or festive robes kept for changing into.
- **3:5** — Latin reads third-person Et dixit ("And he said"), continuing the angel's command, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has first-person "And I said."
- **3:7** — Latin ambulantes ("those who walk") takes the Hebrew term as a plural participle referring to angelic attendants or companions, whereas many Hebrew-based translations read "right of access."
- **3:8** — Latin Orientem ("the Dawn" or "the Rising") follows the Septuagint Anatole as a messianic title for Hebrew Tsemach ("the Branch"); viri portendentes ("foreshadowing men") literally means men who serve as a prophetic portent.
