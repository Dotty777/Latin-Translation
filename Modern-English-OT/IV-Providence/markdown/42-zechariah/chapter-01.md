## Text

**1:1** In the eighth month, in the second year of King Darius, the word of the Lord came to Zechariah, son of Berechiah, son of Iddo, the prophet, saying:

**1:2** "The Lord was angry against your fathers with wrath.

**1:3** And you will say to them, 'This is what the Lord of hosts says: Turn back to Me, says the Lord of hosts, and I will turn back to you, says the Lord of hosts.

**1:4** Do not be like your fathers, to whom the former prophets cried out, saying, "This is what the Lord of hosts says: Turn back from your evil ways and from your most wicked thoughts." And they did not listen, nor did they pay attention to Me, says the Lord.

**1:5** Your fathers, where are they? And the prophets, will they live forever?

**1:6** Yet My words and My statutes, which I commanded My servants the prophets, did they not overtake your fathers? And they turned back and said, "Just as the Lord of hosts intended to do to us according to our ways and according to our devices, He has done to us."'"

**1:7** On the twenty-fourth day of the eleventh month, Shebat, in the second year of Darius, the word of the Lord came to Zechariah, son of Berechiah, son of Iddo, the prophet, saying:

**1:8** I saw during the night, and look, a man riding on a red horse; and he himself was standing among the myrtle groves that were in the depth, and behind him were red, dappled, and white horses.

**1:9** And I said, "What are these, my lord?" And the angel who was speaking in me said to me, "I will show you what these are."

**1:10** And the man who was standing among the myrtle groves answered and said, "These are the ones whom the Lord has sent to walk throughout the earth."

**1:11** And they answered the angel of the Lord, who was standing among the myrtle groves, and said, "We have walked throughout the earth, and look, all the earth is inhabited and is at rest."

**1:12** And the angel of the Lord answered and said, "Lord of hosts, how long will You not have mercy on Jerusalem and on the cities of Judah, with which You have been angry? This is now the seventieth year."

**1:13** And the Lord answered the angel who was speaking in me with good words, comforting words.

**1:14** And the angel who was speaking in me said to me, "Cry out, saying, 'This is what the Lord of hosts says: I have been zealous for Jerusalem and Zion with great zeal,

**1:15** and with great anger I am angry against the wealthy nations, because I was a little angry, yet they themselves helped toward evil.

**1:16** Therefore this is what the Lord says: I will return to Jerusalem with mercies, and My house will be built in it, says the Lord of hosts, and a plumb line will be stretched over Jerusalem.'

**1:17** Cry out again, saying, 'This is what the Lord of hosts says: My cities will again overflow with good things, and the Lord will again comfort Zion and will again choose Jerusalem.'"

**1:18** And I lifted up my eyes and saw, and look, four horns.

**1:19** And I said to the angel who was speaking in me, "What are these?" And he said to me, "These are the horns that scattered Judah, Israel, and Jerusalem."

**1:20** And the Lord showed me four craftsmen.

**1:21** And I said, "What are these coming to do?" And he spoke, saying, "These are the horns that scattered Judah, man by man, and none of them lifted up his head; and these have come to terrify them, so that they may cast down the horns of the nations that lifted up a horn over the land of Judah to disperse it."

## Translator Notes

- **1:4** — Latin reads "your most wicked thoughts" (cogitationibus vestris pessimis), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "your evil deeds."
- **1:8** — Latin in profundo ("in the depth") renders a Hebrew term denoting a low hollow or ravine, and varii ("dappled") follows the Septuagint where the Hebrew has "sorrel."
- **1:9** — Latin qui loquebatur in me ("who was speaking in me") literally renders the Hebrew preposition, suggesting interior prophetic communication (also in 1:13, 1:14, 2:2, and 2:7).
- **1:12** — Latin iste jam septuagesimus annus est ("This is now the seventieth year") uses a singular ordinal where the Hebrew has "these seventy years."
- **1:15** — Latin gentes opulentas ("the wealthy nations") reflects a distinctive Vulgate rendering where the Hebrew describes nations "at ease."
- **1:18** — In native Clementine Vulgate numbering (Clem1598-USFX), the vision of the four horns and four craftsmen closes chapter 1 as 1:18-21 (mapped to Saints.Bible 2:1-4), and the measuring-line vision stands at 2:1-13 (mapped to Saints.Bible 2:5-17).
- **1:21** — Latin per singulos viros ("man by man") is a literal Vulgate rendering of a Hebrew idiom usually taken as "so that no man lifted his head."
