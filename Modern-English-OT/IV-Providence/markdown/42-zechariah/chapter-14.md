## Text

**14:1** Look, the days of the Lord will come, and your spoils will be divided in your midst.

**14:2** And I will gather all the nations to Jerusalem for battle; and the city will be captured, and the houses will be ravaged, and the women will be violated; and half of the city will go out into captivity, and the rest of the people will not be removed from the city.

**14:3** And the Lord will go out and fight against those nations, as He fought on the day of combat.

**14:4** And His feet will stand in that day on the Mount of Olives, which is opposite Jerusalem toward the east; and the Mount of Olives will be split from its middle toward the east and toward the west, with a very great chasm; and half of the mountain will be separated toward the north, and half of it toward the south.

**14:5** And you will flee to the valley of their mountains, because the valley of the mountains will be joined as far as the nearest place; and you will flee just as you fled from the face of the earthquake in the days of Uzziah king of Judah; and the Lord my God will come, and all the holy ones with Him.

**14:6** And it will happen in that day: there will not be light, but cold and frost.

**14:7** And there will be one day that is known to the Lord, neither day nor night; and at evening time there will be light.

**14:8** And it will happen in that day: living waters will go out from Jerusalem, half of them to the eastern sea and half of them to the farthest sea; in summer and in winter they will be there.

**14:9** And the Lord will be king over all the earth; in that day the Lord will be one, and His name will be one.

**14:10** And all the land will return as far as the desert, from the hill of Rimmon to the south of Jerusalem; and it will be lifted up and will dwell in its place, from the Gate of Benjamin as far as the place of the former gate, and as far as the Gate of the Corners, and from the Tower of Hananel as far as the king's winepresses.

**14:11** And they will dwell in it, and there will no longer be a curse, but Jerusalem will sit secure.

**14:12** And this will be the plague with which the Lord will strike all the nations that have fought against Jerusalem: the flesh of each one standing on his feet will rot away, and his eyes will rot away in their sockets, and their tongue will rot away in their mouth.

**14:13** In that day there will be a great tumult of the Lord among them; and a man will seize the hand of his neighbor, and his hand will be locked against the hand of his neighbor.

**14:14** But Judah also will fight against Jerusalem; and the wealth of all the nations all around will be gathered, gold and silver and very many garments.

**14:15** And so will be the ruin of the horse, and of the mule, and of the camel, and of the donkey, and of all the beasts of burden that are in those camps, just like this ruin.

**14:16** And all who remain from all the nations that came against Jerusalem will go up from year to year to worship the King, the Lord of hosts, and to celebrate the Festival of Tabernacles.

**14:17** And it will happen: whoever of the families of the earth does not go up to Jerusalem to worship the King, the Lord of hosts, on them there will be no rain.

**14:18** And if the family of Egypt does not go up and does not come, neither will it be on them; but there will be the ruin with which the Lord will strike all the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Festival of Tabernacles.

**14:19** This will be the sin of Egypt, and this the sin of all the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Festival of Tabernacles.

**14:20** In that day, what is on the bridle of the horse will be holy to the Lord; and the pots in the house of the Lord will be like the bowls before the altar.

**14:21** And every pot in Jerusalem and in Judah will be sanctified to the Lord of hosts; and all who sacrifice will come and take from them and cook in them; and there will not be a merchant any longer in the house of the Lord of hosts in that day.

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — Vulgate reads the plural "venient dies Domini" ("the days of the Lord will come"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has the singular "a day is coming for the Lord."
- **14:5** — Vulgate has three distinct readings here: "montium eorum" ("their mountains," vs. Hebrew "My mountains"), "usque ad proximum" ("as far as the nearest place," rendering Hebrew "Azal" as a common noun rather than a place name), and "cum eo" ("with Him," agreeing with the Septuagint against Hebrew "with you").
- **14:6** — Vulgate "non erit lux, sed frigus et gelu" ("there will not be light, but cold and frost") agrees with the Septuagint in reading a difficult Hebrew phrase as "cold and frost."
- **14:10** — Vulgate "revertetur... usque ad desertum, de colle Remmon" ("will return as far as the desert, from the hill of Rimmon") renders Hebrew "Geba" as a common noun ("hill") and "Arabah" as "desert," contrasting with the Hebrew Masoretic Text "will be turned into a plain from Geba to Rimmon." Vulgate also has plural "portam angulorum" ("Gate of the Corners").
- **14:14** — Vulgate "adversus Jerusalem" ("against Jerusalem") interprets the Hebrew preposition in a hostile sense rather than locatively ("at/in Jerusalem").
