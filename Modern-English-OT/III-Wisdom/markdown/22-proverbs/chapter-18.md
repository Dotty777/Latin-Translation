# Proverbs 18 — Saints Modern English (Latin / Clementine Vulgate)

## Text

**18:1** Whoever wants to withdraw from a friend seeks pretexts; at all times he will be worthy of reproach.

**18:2** A fool does not accept words of prudence, unless you say the things that revolve in his heart.

**18:3** The wicked man, when he comes into the depth of sins, shows contempt; yet disgrace and reproach follow him.

**18:4** Words from a man's mouth are deep water, and the spring of wisdom is an overflowing torrent.

**18:5** Showing partiality to a wicked man is not good, so that you turn aside from the truth of judgment.

**18:6** The lips of a fool entangle themselves in disputes, and his mouth provokes quarrels.

**18:7** A fool's mouth is his destruction, and his own lips are the ruin of his soul.

**18:8** The words of a double-tongued man are as though simple, and they themselves reach all the way to the innermost parts of the belly. Fear casts down the lazy man, and the souls of the effeminate will go hungry.

**18:9** Whoever is soft and slack in his work is the brother of one who destroys his own works.

**18:10** The name of the Lord is a very strong tower; the righteous man runs to it and will be exalted.

**18:11** The wealth of a rich man is the city of his strength, and like a strong wall surrounding him.

**18:12** Before it is crushed, a man's heart is exalted, and before it is glorified, it is humbled.

**18:13** Whoever answers before he hears proves himself to be a fool and worthy of shame.

**18:14** The spirit of a man sustains his weakness, but who will be able to endure a spirit that is easily angered?

**18:15** A prudent heart will possess knowledge, and the ear of the wise seeks teaching.

**18:16** A man's gift widens his way and makes room for him before princes.

**18:17** The righteous man is the first accuser of himself; his friend comes and will search him out.

**18:18** The lot suppresses controversies and decides even between the powerful.

**18:19** A brother who is helped by a brother is like a fortified city, and judgments are like the bars of cities.

**18:20** From the fruit of a man's mouth his belly will be filled, and the yield of his own lips will satisfy him.

**18:21** Death and life are in the hand of the tongue; those who love it will eat its fruits.

**18:22** Whoever finds a good wife finds a good thing, and will draw delight from the Lord. Whoever drives out a good wife drives out a good thing, yet whoever keeps an adulteress is foolish and wicked.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**18:23** *(after `18:22`)* With pleas the poor man will speak, and the rich man will speak out harshly.

**18:24** *(after `18:22`)* A man who is amiable in companionship will be more of a friend than a brother.

**19:1** *(after `18:22`)* A poor man who walks in his sincerity is better than a rich man who twists his lips and is foolish.

**19:2** *(after `18:22`)* Where there is no knowledge of the soul, it is not good, and whoever is hasty with his feet will stumble.

## Translator Notes

- **18:1** — Vulgate-distinct reading (aligning with the Septuagint): seeking pretexts to withdraw from a friend and being "worthy of reproach," whereas the Hebrew speaks of one who isolates himself seeking his own desire and breaking out against all sound wisdom.
- **18:8** — In the first sentence, the Vulgate reads "bilinguis" ("double-tongued") and "quasi simplicia" ("as though simple") where the Hebrew has "a whisperer" and "like dainty morsels." The entire second sentence ("Fear casts down ... will go hungry") is a Vulgate addition shared with the Septuagint and absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **18:14** — Vulgate-distinct reading: "spiritum ... ad irascendum facilem" ("a spirit that is easily angered"), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "a crushed (or broken) spirit."
- **18:17** — Vulgate-distinct reading (aligning with the Septuagint): "Justus prior est accusator sui" ("The righteous man is the first accuser of himself"), where the Hebrew reads "The first to plead his case seems right."
- **18:19** — Vulgate-distinct reading (aligning with the Septuagint): "a brother who is helped by a brother" and "judgments," where the Hebrew reads "a brother offended" and "contentions."
- **18:22** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate adds "good" ("bonam"), reads "will draw delight" ("hauriet jucunditatem") where the Hebrew has "obtains favor," and includes the entire second sentence ("Whoever drives out ... foolish and wicked"), which is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
