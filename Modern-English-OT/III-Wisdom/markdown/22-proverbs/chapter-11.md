# Proverbs 11 — Saints Modern English (Latin / Clementine Vulgate)

## Text

**11:1** A deceitful scale is an abomination before the Lord, and a fair weight is His pleasure.

**11:2** Where pride is, there disgrace will also be; but where humility is, wisdom is there as well.

**11:3** The sincerity of the righteous will direct them, and the treachery of the perverse will devastate them.

**11:5** The righteousness of the sincere person will direct his way, and the wicked person will fall in his own wickedness.

**11:6** The righteousness of the upright will deliver them, and the unjust will be captured in their own ambushes.

**11:7** When a wicked person dies, there will no longer be any hope, and the expectation of the anxious will perish.

**11:8** The righteous person has been delivered from distress, and the wicked person will be handed over in his place.

**11:9** The hypocrite deceives his friend with his mouth, but the righteous will be delivered by knowledge.

**11:10** At the prosperity of the righteous the city will exult, and at the destruction of the wicked there will be praise.

**11:11** Through the blessing of the righteous the city will be exalted, and by the mouth of the wicked it will be overturned.

**11:12** Whoever despises his friend is lacking in heart, but a prudent man will keep silent.

**11:13** Whoever walks fraudulently reveals secrets, but one who is faithful in spirit conceals what a friend has entrusted.

**11:14** Where there is no helmsman, the people will fall; but there is safety where there are many counsels.

**11:15** Whoever gives a pledge for a stranger will be afflicted with harm, but whoever guards against snares will be secure.

**11:16** A gracious woman will find glory, and the mighty will possess wealth.

**11:17** A merciful man does good to his own soul, but whoever is cruel rejects even his relatives.

**11:18** The wicked person does unstable work, but for the one who sows righteousness there is a sure reward.

**11:19** Clemency prepares life, and the pursuit of evil prepares death.

**11:20** A crooked heart is abominable to the Lord, and His pleasure is in those who walk in sincerity.

**11:21** Hand in hand, an evil person will not be guiltless, but the offspring of the righteous will be saved.

**11:22** A gold ring in a pig's snout: a beautiful and foolish woman.

**11:23** The desire of the righteous is wholly good; the anticipation of the wicked is wrath.

**11:24** Some share out what is their own and become richer; others seize what is not theirs and are always in poverty.

**11:25** The soul that blesses will be made fat, and the one who satisfies with drink will himself also be satisfied.

**11:26** Whoever hides grain will be cursed among the peoples, but a blessing will be upon the head of those who sell.

**11:27** Whoever seeks good things rises well at dawn, but whoever is a searcher of evil things will be overwhelmed by them.

**11:28** Whoever trusts in his wealth will fall, but the righteous will sprout like a green leaf.

**11:29** Whoever disrupts his own house will possess the winds, and whoever is a fool will serve one who is wise.

**11:30** The fruit of the righteous person is a tree of life, and whoever receives souls is wise.

**11:31** If the righteous person receives repayment on earth, how much more the wicked person and the sinner!

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**11:4** *(after `11:3`)* Wealth will be of no benefit on the day of vengeance, yet righteousness will deliver from death.

## Translator Notes

- **11:7** — The Vulgate reads 'sollicitorum' ('the anxious' or 'those preoccupied with cares'), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text is commonly understood as the expectation of wealth, strength, or iniquity.
- **11:17** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew term for 'flesh' in its sense of 'propinquos' ('relatives' or 'near kin') and reads 'abjicit' ('rejects'), rather than 'troubles his own flesh.'
- **11:21** — Latin 'Manus in manu' ('Hand in hand') literally renders a Hebrew pledge idiom conveying either strong assurance ('be certain') or joining forces in conspiracy.
- **11:24** — The Vulgate uniquely reads 'alii rapiunt non sua' ('others seize what is not theirs'), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of withholding more than is right.
- **11:27** — The Vulgate 'Bene consurgit diluculo' ('rises well at dawn') reflects the Hebrew root link between seeking diligently and daybreak, and its second clause reads 'investigator malorum ... opprimetur ab eis' ('a searcher of evil things will be overwhelmed by them').
