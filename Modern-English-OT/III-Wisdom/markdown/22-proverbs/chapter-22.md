## Text

**22:1** A good name is better than abundant wealth; above silver and gold is good favor.

**22:2** The rich person and the poor person have met each other; the Lord is the maker of both.

**22:3** A shrewd person saw evil and hid himself; an innocent person went right on and was afflicted with loss.

**22:4** The outcome of humility is the fear of the Lord, wealth, glory, and life.

**22:5** Weapons and swords are in the way of the perverse person, yet the guardian of his own soul withdraws far from them.

**22:7** A rich man rules over the poor, and whoever takes a loan is a slave to the lender.

**22:8** Whoever sows iniquity will reap evils, and the rod of his anger will be brought to an end.

**22:9** Whoever is inclined to mercy will be blessed, for he has given from his own bread to the poor. The one who gives gifts will acquire victory and honor, yet he takes away the soul of the recipients.

**22:10** Drive out the mocker, and quarreling will go out with him, and disputes and insults will cease.

**22:11** One who loves purity of heart, because of the grace of his lips, will have the king as a friend.

**22:12** The eyes of the Lord guard knowledge, and the words of an unjust man are overturned.

**22:13** The lazy man says, "There is a lion outside; in the middle of the streets I am going to be killed."

**22:14** A deep pit is the mouth of a foreign woman; the one with whom the Lord is angry will fall into it.

**22:15** Folly is bound tightly in a child's heart, and the rod of discipline will chase it away.

**22:16** One who falsely accuses the poor in order to increase his own wealth will himself give to one who is richer, and will be in need.

**22:17** Incline your ear and listen to the words of the wise, and apply your heart to my instruction,

**22:18** which will be beautiful to you when you keep it inside your belly, and it will overflow on your lips:

**22:19** so that your confidence may be in the Lord, for which reason I have also shown it to you today.

**22:20** Look, I have written it out for you in a threefold way, in thoughts and knowledge:

**22:21** so that I might show you reliability and utterances of truth, to answer from these to those who sent you.

**22:22** Do not do violence to a poor person because he is poor, nor crush the needy in the gate:

**22:23** because the Lord will judge his case, and will pierce those who have pierced his soul.

**22:24** Do not be a friend to a hot-tempered person, and do not walk with a furious man:

**22:25** so that you might not learn his paths and take a stumbling block for your soul.

**22:26** Do not be with those who pledge their hands, and who offer themselves as guarantors for debts:

**22:27** for if you do not have the means to repay, what reason is there for him to take the covering from your bed?

**22:28** Do not cross over the ancient boundaries that your fathers placed.

**22:29** Have you seen a man quick in his work? He will stand before kings, and he will not be before obscure people.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**22:6** *(after `22:5`)* There is a proverb: a youth according to his own way; even when he has grown old, he will not depart from it.

## Translator Notes

- **22:5** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew tsinnim pachim ("thorns and snares") as Arma et gladii ("Weapons and swords"), reflecting the alternate sense of Hebrew tsinnah as a large shield.
- **22:9** — The second sentence ("The one who gives gifts will acquire victory and honor, yet he takes away the soul of the recipients") is present in the Vulgate and Septuagint but absent from the Masoretic Hebrew text.
- **22:16** — Latin calumniatur literally denotes false accusation or extortion through malicious litigation, used here in the Vulgate to render Hebrew ashaq ("oppress").
- **22:20** — Latin tripliciter ("in a threefold way") is a famous Vulgate reading—contrasting with Masoretic Hebrew "thirty [sayings]" or "excellent things"—which early Christian interpreters often connected to the threefold sense of Scripture.
- **22:23** — The Vulgate uses the repeated verb configet ... confixerunt ("will pierce ... have pierced"), whereas the Masoretic Hebrew speaks of despoiling or robbing the poor.
- **22:6 *(additional)*** — The Clementine Vulgate presents this verse as a condensed, verbless proverb ("a youth according to his own way") rather than an imperative command.
