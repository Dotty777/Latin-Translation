## Text

**9:1** Wisdom built a house for herself; she carved out seven pillars.

**9:2** She offered her sacrifices, mixed the wine, and set out her table.

**9:3** She sent her maidservants to call out at the citadel and at the walls of the city.

**9:4** "If anyone is a little one, let him come to me." And she spoke to the unwise:

**9:5** "Come, eat my bread, and drink the wine that I have mixed for you.

**9:6** Abandon childishness and live, and walk along the paths of prudence.

**9:7** Whoever instructs a mocker brings insult upon himself, and whoever rebukes a wicked person produces a blemish for himself.

**9:8** Do not rebuke a mocker, so that he will not hate you; rebuke a wise person, and he will love you.

**9:9** Give a wise person an opportunity, and wisdom will be added to him; teach a righteous person, and he will hasten to receive it.

**9:10** The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, and knowledge of the holy ones is prudence.

**9:11** For through me your days will be multiplied, and years of life will be added for you.

**9:12** If you are wise, you will be so for your own self; but if you are a scoffer, you alone will bear the evil."

**9:13** A foolish and loud woman, full of allurements and knowing nothing at all,

**9:14** sat at the doorway of her house, on a seat in a high place of the city,

**9:15** to call to those passing along the way and proceeding on their journey:

**9:16** "Whoever is a little one, let him turn aside to me." And she spoke to the senseless one:

**9:17** "Stolen waters are sweeter, and concealed bread is more delightful."

**9:18** And he was unaware that giants are there, and that her banquet guests are in the depths of the underworld.

## Translator Notes

- **9:3** — Latin "ut vocarent ad arcem et ad moenia civitatis" ("to call out at the citadel and at the walls of the city") can also be read with directional "ad" ("to invite [guests] to the citadel and walls of the city"); it is a Vulgate-distinct rendering of the Hebrew "she calls from the highest places of the town."
- **9:4** — The Vulgate uses childhood imagery—"parvulus" ("a little one," also in v. 16) and "infantia" ("childishness," v. 6)—to render the Hebrew term for the simple or naive.
- **9:9** — The Vulgate explicitly supplies "occasionem" ("an opportunity") in the first clause and reads "festinabit accipere" ("will hasten to receive it") in the second, where the Hebrew reads "will increase in learning."
- **9:10** — Latin "sanctorum" is plural, meaning "of the holy ones" (or "of holy things"), corresponding to the plural Hebrew "qedoshim."
- **9:18** — Latin "gigantes" ("giants") is the Vulgate's standard rendering of Hebrew "repha'im" (the shades or departed spirits), and "infernus" ("the underworld") denotes the realm of the dead (Hebrew Sheol).
