## Text

**6:1** My son, if you have become guarantor for your friend, you have fastened your hand with an outsider:

**6:2** you have been ensnared by the words of your mouth, and caught by your own statements.

**6:3** Therefore do what I say, my son, and free yourself, because you have fallen into the hand of your neighbor. Run about, hurry, rouse your friend.

**6:4** Do not give sleep to your eyes, nor let your eyelids doze.

**6:5** Rescue yourself like a young doe from the hand, and like a bird from the hand of the bird-catcher.

**6:6** Go to the ant, you sluggard, and examine her ways, and learn wisdom.

**6:7** Although she has no leader, nor instructor, nor ruler,

**6:8** she prepares food for herself in summer, and gathers in the harvest what she may eat.

**6:9** How long, sluggard, will you sleep? When will you rise up from your sleep?

**6:10** You will sleep a little while, you will doze a little while, you will clasp your hands a little while to sleep;

**6:11** and want will come to you like a traveler, and poverty like an armed man. Yet if you are diligent, your harvest will come like a spring, and want will flee far from you.

**6:12** An apostate person, a useless man, walks with a perverse mouth;

**6:13** he winks with his eyes, scrapes with his foot, speaks with his finger;

**6:14** with a crooked heart he devises evil, and at all times he sows quarrels.

**6:15** To this man his ruin will come immediately, and suddenly he will be crushed, and he will no longer have a remedy.

**6:16** There are six things that the Lord hates, and a seventh His soul detests:

**6:17** lofty eyes, a lying tongue, hands pouring out innocent blood,

**6:18** a heart devising the most evil thoughts, feet swift to run into evil,

**6:19** a deceitful witness bringing forth lies, and the one who sows dissensions among brothers.

**6:20** Preserve, my son, the precepts of your father, and do not abandon the law of your mother.

**6:21** Bind them continually in your heart, and wrap them around your neck.

**6:22** When you walk, let them walk with you; when you sleep, let them guard you; and on waking, speak with them.

**6:23** Because the commandment is a lamp, and the law is light, and the reproof of discipline is the way of life,

**6:24** so that they may guard you from an evil woman, and from the flattering tongue of a foreign woman.

**6:25** Do not let your heart lust after her beauty, and do not be captured by her winks;

**6:26** for the price of a prostitute is barely a single loaf of bread, yet a man's wife captures a precious soul.

**6:27** Can a man conceal fire in his bosom, so that his clothes do not burn?

**6:28** Or walk upon burning coals, so that the soles of his feet are not scorched?

**6:29** So is the one who goes in to his neighbor's wife; he will not be clean when he has touched her.

**6:30** It is not a great fault when someone steals, for he steals so that he may fill a hungry soul;

**6:31** even when caught, he will repay sevenfold, and he will hand over all the property of his house.

**6:32** Yet the one who is an adulterer, because of poverty of heart, will destroy his own soul;

**6:33** he gathers disgrace and dishonor for himself, and his reproach will not be erased,

**6:34** because the jealousy and fury of a husband will not spare on the day of vengeance,

**6:35** nor will he yield to anyone's pleas, nor will he accept very many gifts for ransom.

## Translator Notes

- **6:3** — The Vulgate renders the closing imperatives as "Run about, hurry, rouse your friend" (Discurre, festina, suscita amicum tuum), differing from the Hebrew "go, humble yourself, and importune your neighbor."
- **6:5** — The Vulgate translates the first clause literally as "from the hand" (de manu), whereas the Hebrew is commonly vocalized as "from the hunter."
- **6:11** — The second sentence ("Yet if you are diligent...") is a Vulgate reading derived from the Septuagint and absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text; in the first clause, the Vulgate reads "traveler" (viator) where the Hebrew is often rendered "robber" or "vagabond."
- **6:22** — The Vulgate takes the final verb as a second-person imperative addressed to the son ("and on waking, speak with them"), whereas the Hebrew makes the instruction the subject ("when you awake, it will talk with you").
- **6:30** — The Vulgate reads "It is not a great fault when someone steals" (Non grandis est culpa), contrasting with the Hebrew "People do not despise a thief if he steals."
