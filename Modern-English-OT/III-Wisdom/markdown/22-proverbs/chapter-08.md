## Text

**8:1** Does not wisdom call out, and prudence give her voice?

**8:2** On the highest and loftiest summits above the way, standing in the middle of the paths,

**8:3** beside the gates of the city, at the very doors she speaks, saying:

**8:4** "Men, to you I call out, and my voice is to the sons of men.

**8:5** Little ones, understand shrewdness; and foolish ones, pay attention.

**8:6** Listen, for I am about to speak of great things, and my lips will be opened so that they may proclaim what is right.

**8:7** My throat will meditate on truth, and my lips will detest the ungodly.

**8:8** All my words are just; there is nothing crooked or perverse in them;

**8:9** they are right to those who understand, and equitable to those who find knowledge.

**8:10** Receive my discipline, and not money; choose teaching rather than gold;

**8:11** for wisdom is better than all the most precious things, and no desirable thing can be compared to her.

**8:12** I, wisdom, dwell in counsel, and I am present among learned thoughts.

**8:13** The fear of the Lord hates evil; arrogance, and pride, and a crooked way, and a double-tongued mouth I detest.

**8:14** Mine are counsel and equity; mine is prudence, mine is strength.

**8:15** Through me kings reign, and lawmakers decree what is just;

**8:16** through me princes rule, and the powerful decree justice.

**8:17** I love those who love me, and those who watch early for me will find me.

**8:18** With me are riches and glory, splendid wealth and righteousness.

**8:19** For my fruit is better than gold and precious stone, and my yield than choice silver.

**8:20** I walk in the ways of righteousness, in the middle of the paths of judgment,

**8:21** so that I may enrich those who love me, and fill their treasuries.

**8:22** The Lord possessed me at the outset of His ways, before He made anything from the beginning.

**8:23** From eternity I was ordained, and from ancient times before the earth was made.

**8:24** The depths were not yet, and I had already been conceived; nor had the springs of waters yet burst forth,

**8:25** nor had the mountains yet stood firm with their heavy mass; before the hills I was being brought forth.

**8:26** He had not yet made the earth, or the rivers, or the pivots of the world.

**8:27** When He was preparing the heavens, I was there; when with a fixed law and circle He was enclosing the depths;

**8:28** when He was making the upper air firm above, and was balancing the springs of waters;

**8:29** when He was setting its boundary around the sea, and placing a law upon the waters, so that they would not cross their limits; when He was weighing out the foundations of the earth,

**8:30** I was with Him, arranging all things. And I was delighted day by day, playing before Him at all times,

**8:31** playing in the world; and my delight is to be with the sons of men.

**8:32** Now therefore, sons, listen to me: blessed are those who keep my ways.

**8:34** Blessed is the man who listens to me, and who watches at my doors daily, and keeps watch at the posts of my doorway.

**8:35** Whoever finds me will find life, and will draw salvation from the Lord.

**8:36** But whoever sins against me will harm his own soul; all who hate me love death."

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**8:33** *(after `8:32`)* Listen to discipline, and be wise, and do not cast it aside.

## Translator Notes

- **8:5** — Latin "parvuli" (literally "little ones," as also in 7:7) is the Vulgate's characteristic rendering for the naive or inexperienced.
- **8:22** — Vulgate "possedit me" ("possessed me") renders the Hebrew verb that can mean "acquired/possessed" or "created" (rendered "created" in the Greek Septuagint).
- **8:26** — Vulgate "flumina" ("rivers") and "cardines orbis terræ" ("pivots of the world") are distinctive Latin renderings where the Hebrew text refers to open fields and the first dust of the world.
- **8:30** — Vulgate "cuncta componens" ("arranging all things") resolves an ambiguous Hebrew term (debated between "master craftsman" and "little child") by portraying Wisdom as ordering creation alongside God.
