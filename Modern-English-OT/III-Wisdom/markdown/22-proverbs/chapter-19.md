## Text

**19:3** A person's folly trips up his steps, and he seethes in his spirit against God.

**19:4** Wealth adds very many friends, but from a poor man even those whom he had separate themselves.

**19:5** A false witness will not remain unpunished, and the one who speaks lies will not escape.

**19:6** Many pay court to the person of a powerful man, and they are friends of the one who gives gifts.

**19:7** The brothers of a poor man hate him; moreover, even friends have withdrawn far from him. The one who pursues only words will have nothing.

**19:8** But the one who possesses understanding loves his own soul, and the keeper of prudence will find good things.

**19:9** A false witness will not remain unpunished, and the one who speaks lies will perish.

**19:10** Luxuries are not proper for a fool, nor is it proper for a servant to rule over princes.

**19:11** A man's learning is recognized through patience, and his glory is to pass over injustices.

**19:12** Like the growling of a lion, so also is a king's anger, and like dew on the grass, so also is his cheerfulness.

**19:13** A foolish son is a father's grief, and a quarrelsome woman is a continually dripping roof.

**19:14** A house and wealth are given by parents, but a prudent wife is specifically from the Lord.

**19:15** Laziness brings on deep sleep, and a slack soul will go hungry.

**19:16** The one who keeps the commandment keeps his own soul, but the one who neglects his way will be put to death.

**19:17** The one who has mercy on a poor man lends to the Lord, and He will pay him his recompense.

**19:18** Train your son; do not despair, yet do not set your soul toward killing him.

**19:19** The one who is impatient will endure loss, and when he takes by force, he will add something else.

**19:20** Hear counsel and receive discipline, so that you may be wise in your final days.

**19:21** Many are the thoughts in a man's heart, but the will of the Lord will endure.

**19:22** A needy person is merciful, and a poor man is better than a lying man.

**19:23** The fear of the Lord leads to life, and he will dwell in fullness without a most evil visitation.

**19:24** The lazy man hides his hand under his armpit, and does not bring it to his mouth.

**19:25** When a pestilent man is flogged, the fool will become wiser; but if you rebuke a wise man, he will understand discipline.

**19:26** The one who afflicts his father and drives away his mother is disgraceful and wretched.

**19:27** Do not cease, son, to listen to instruction, nor be ignorant of the words of knowledge.

**19:28** An unjust witness mocks judgment, and the mouth of the wicked devours injustice.

**19:29** Judgments are prepared for mockers, and beating hammers for the bodies of fools.

## Translator Notes

- **19:7** — The Vulgate renders the difficult final clause as "The one who pursues only words will have nothing" (Qui tantum verba sectatur nihil habebit), contrasting it with 19:8, whereas the Hebrew is commonly taken to mean that the poor man pursues his friends with words, yet they are gone.
- **19:19** — The Vulgate translates the second half in the third person ("when he takes by force, he will add something else", cum rapuerit, aliud apponet), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text uses the second person ("if you rescue him, you will have to do it again").
- **19:22** — Following a distinct reading of the Hebrew, the Vulgate has "A needy person is merciful" (Homo indigens misericors est), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text is generally translated "What is desired in a man is steadfast love."
- **19:24** — The Vulgate reads "under his armpit" (sub ascella), a distinctly Vulgate rendering where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "in the dish."
- **19:27** — The Vulgate turns this proverb into a direct prohibition ("Do not cease, son, to listen to instruction", Non cesses, fili, audire doctrinam), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text uses an ironic imperative ("Cease, my son, to hear instruction, and you will stray from the words of knowledge").
