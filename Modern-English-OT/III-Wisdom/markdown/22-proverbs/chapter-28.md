# Proverbs 28 — Saints Modern English (Latin / Clementine Vulgate)

## Text

**28:1** A wicked man flees with no one pursuing, while a righteous man, like a confident lion, will be without terror.

**28:2** Because of the sins of a land, many are its rulers; and because of a man's wisdom, and knowledge of the things that are spoken, the life of a leader will be longer.

**28:3** A poor man oppressing the poor is like a fierce rain in which famine is prepared.

**28:4** Those who abandon the law praise the wicked man; those who keep it are inflamed against him.

**28:5** Evil men do not consider justice, but those who seek the Lord perceive all things.

**28:6** Better is a poor man walking in his integrity than a rich man on crooked paths.

**28:7** Whoever keeps the law is a wise son, but the one who feeds revelers disgraces his father.

**28:8** Whoever piles up riches by usury and interest gathers them for one who is generous toward the poor.

**28:9** Whoever turns away his ears so that he does not hear the law, his prayer will be detestable.

**28:10** Whoever deceives the righteous on an evil way will fall in his own ruin, and those of integrity will possess his goods.

**28:11** A rich man seems wise to himself, but a prudent poor man will scrutinize him.

**28:12** In the exultation of the righteous there is much glory; when the wicked reign, there are downfalls of people.

**28:13** Whoever hides his wicked deeds will not succeed, but the one who confesses and leaves them will gain mercy.

**28:14** Blessed is the person who is always fearful, whereas the one who is hard of mind will fall into evil.

**28:15** A wicked ruler over a poor people is a roaring lion and a hungry bear.

**28:16** A leader lacking prudence will oppress many through extortion, while the days of the one who hates greed will become long.

**28:17** A person who wrongs the blood of a soul, if he flees all the way to the pit, no one supports him.

**28:18** Whoever walks with integrity will be saved; whoever travels on perverse ways will collapse all at once.

**28:19** Whoever works his land will be satisfied with bread, but the one who pursues idleness will be filled with poverty.

**28:20** A faithful man will be greatly praised, but the one who hurries to grow rich will not be innocent.

**28:21** Whoever recognizes a face in judgment does not act well; that man, even for a mouthful of bread, deserts the truth.

**28:22** A man who hurries to grow rich and envies others does not know that poverty will come upon him.

**28:23** Whoever rebukes a person will afterward find favor with him, more than the one who deceives through flatteries of the tongue.

**28:24** Whoever steals something from his father and from his mother, and says that this is not a sin, is an accomplice of a murderer.

**28:25** Whoever boasts and magnifies himself stirs up quarrels, whereas the one who hopes in the Lord will be healed.

**28:26** Whoever trusts in his heart is a fool, but the one who walks wisely, he himself will be saved.

**28:27** Whoever gives to a poor man will not be in need; whoever despises one who pleads will suffer destitution.

**28:28** When the wicked rise, people will hide themselves; when they perish, the righteous will multiply.

## Translator Notes

- **28:2** — The Vulgate reads 'and knowledge of the things that are spoken, the life of a leader will be longer' (horum scientiam quæ dicuntur, vita ducis longior erit), diverging from the briefer Hebrew clause 'by a person of understanding and knowledge it endures.'
- **28:12** — The Vulgate reads 'when the wicked reign, there are downfalls of people' (regnantibus impiis, ruinæ hominum), whereas the Hebrew reads 'when the wicked rise, people hide themselves.'
- **28:17** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew passive participle actively as 'who wrongs the blood of a soul' (qui calumniatur animæ sanguinem, using calumnior in its Vulgate sense of oppressing or wrongfully injuring) and uses the indicative 'no one supports' (nemo sustinet) rather than a jussive prohibition.
- **28:25** — The Vulgate reads 'will be healed' (sanabitur) where the Hebrew reads 'will be made fat' or 'will prosper,' and renders the Hebrew idiom 'wide of soul' as 'boasts and magnifies himself.'
- **28:27** — The Vulgate reads 'whoever despises one who pleads will suffer destitution' (despicit deprecantem sustinebit penuriam), whereas the Hebrew reads 'whoever hides his eyes will have many curses.'
