## Text

**1:1** The parables of Solomon, son of David, king of Israel,

**1:2** for knowing wisdom and discipline;

**1:3** for understanding words of prudence, and for receiving the instruction of teaching, righteousness, judgment, and equity,

**1:4** so that shrewdness may be given to little ones, and knowledge and understanding to a youth.

**1:5** A wise man who listens will be wiser, and one who understands will possess guidance.

**1:6** He will perceive a parable and an interpretation, the words of the wise and their riddles.

**1:7** The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom; fools despise wisdom and teaching.

**1:8** Listen, my son, to the discipline of your father, and do not abandon the law of your mother,

**1:9** so that grace may be added to your head, and a necklace to your neck.

**1:10** My son, if sinners entice you, do not yield to them.

**1:11** If they say, "Come with us, let us lie in wait for blood; let us hide snares against an innocent man without cause;

**1:12** let us swallow him alive like the underworld, and whole like one descending into the pit;

**1:13** we will find all precious wealth; we will fill our houses with plunder;

**1:14** cast a lot with us, let there be one purse for all of us,"

**1:15** my son, do not walk with them; hold back your foot from their paths;

**1:16** for their feet run to evil, and they hurry to shed blood.

**1:17** Yet a net is cast in vain before the eyes of winged creatures.

**1:18** They themselves also lie in wait against their own blood, and plot treachery against their own lives.

**1:19** Such are the paths of every greedy person: they seize the lives of their possessors.

**1:20** Wisdom proclaims outside; in the squares she raises her voice;

**1:21** at the head of the crowds she cries out; at the entrances of the city gates she utters her words, saying,

**1:22** "How long, little ones, do you love childishness, and will fools desire the things that are harmful to themselves, and will the imprudent hate knowledge?

**1:23** Turn back to my reproof. Look, I will bring forth my spirit to you, and I will show my words to you.

**1:24** Because I called, and you refused; I stretched out my hand, and there was no one who looked;

**1:25** you despised all my counsel, and you neglected my rebukes.

**1:26** I also will laugh at your ruin, and I will mock when what you feared comes upon you.

**1:27** When sudden calamity rushes in, and ruin strikes like a storm; when affliction and distress come upon you,

**1:28** then they will call upon me, and I will not listen; they will rise early in the morning, and they will not find me,

**1:29** because they detested discipline, and did not receive the fear of the Lord,

**1:30** nor did they yield to my counsel, and they disparaged all my reproof.

**1:31** Therefore they will eat the fruits of their own way, and they will be filled with their own counsels.

**1:32** The turning away of the little ones will put them to death, and the prosperity of fools will destroy them.

**1:33** But whoever listens to me will rest without terror, and will enjoy abundance, with the fear of evils removed."

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Latin parabolae literally means "parables" or proverbial comparisons (rendering Hebrew mishle), reflected also in 1:6.
- **1:5** — Latin gubernacula literally means "helms" or "rudders," a nautical metaphor for steering, direction, and wise guidance.
- **1:7** — The Vulgate reads sapientiae ("wisdom"), agreeing with the Septuagint, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "knowledge."
- **1:22** — The Vulgate uses parvuli ("little ones") and infantia ("childishness") for terms referring to the naive in Hebrew, and reads ea quae sibi sunt noxia ("the things that are harmful to themselves") where the Hebrew speaks of scoffers delighting in scoffing.
- **1:28** — Vulgate mane consurgent ("they will rise early in the morning") reflects the Hebrew root for "dawn," where the Hebrew verb is commonly rendered "seek diligently."
