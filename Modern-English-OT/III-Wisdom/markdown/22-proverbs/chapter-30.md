## Text

**30:1** The words of the Gatherer, son of the Vomiter. The vision that the man uttered with whom God is, and who, strengthened by God dwelling with him, said:

**30:2** "I am the most foolish among men, and the wisdom of mankind is not with me.

**30:3** I have not learned wisdom, and I do not know the knowledge of the holy ones.

**30:4** Who has ascended into heaven and descended? Who has contained the wind in His hands? Who has bound the waters as in a garment? Who has raised up all the boundaries of the earth? What is His name, and what is the name of His son, if you know?

**30:5** Every word of God is tested by fire: He is a shield to those who hope in Him.

**30:6** Do not add anything to His words, so that you are not rebuked and found to be a liar.

**30:7** I have asked two things from You: do not deny them to me before I die:

**30:8** put emptiness and lying words far from me; do not give me beggary or riches; grant only what is necessary for my sustenance,

**30:9** so that, being satisfied, I may not be enticed to denial and say, 'Who is the Lord?' or, driven by want, steal and swear falsely by the name of my God.

**30:10** Do not accuse a servant to his master, or he may curse you, and you will fall.

**30:11** A generation that curses its father and that does not bless its mother;

**30:12** a generation that seems clean to itself and yet is not washed from its filth;

**30:13** a generation whose eyes are lofty and its eyelids lifted up on high;

**30:14** a generation that has swords for teeth and chews with its molars, so that it may devour the needy from the earth and the poor from among mankind.

**30:15** The leech has two daughters, saying, 'Bring, bring.' There are three insatiable things, and a fourth that never says, 'It is enough.'

**30:16** The underworld, and the opening of the womb, and the earth that is not satisfied with water; but fire never says, 'It is enough.'

**30:17** May the ravens from the streams gouge out the eye that mocks a father and that despises the childbirth of his mother, and may the young of the eagle devour it!

**30:18** Three things are difficult for me, and a fourth I do not know at all:

**30:19** the way of an eagle in the sky, the way of a serpent on a rock, the way of a ship in the middle of the sea, and the way of a man in youth.

**30:20** Such also is the way of an adulterous woman, who eats and, wiping her mouth, says, 'I have done nothing evil.'

**30:21** By three things the earth is shaken, and a fourth it cannot endure:

**30:22** by a servant when he becomes king; by a fool when he is filled with food;

**30:23** by a detested woman when she is taken in marriage; and by a maidservant when she becomes the heir of her mistress.

**30:24** Four things are the smallest on earth, and they themselves are wiser than the wise:

**30:25** the ants, a weak people, which prepares food for itself in the harvest;

**30:26** the little hare, a feeble folk, which places its lair in the rock;

**30:27** the locust has no king, and it all goes forth by its bands;

**30:28** the lizard supports itself with its hands and dwells in the palace of a king.

**30:29** There are three things that step well, and a fourth that strides smoothly:

**30:30** the lion, strongest of beasts, will dread the approach of none;

**30:31** a rooster girded about the loins; and a ram; nor is there a king who can resist him.

**30:32** There is one who has appeared foolish after he was exalted on high; for if he had understood, he would have placed his hand over his mouth.

**30:33** Now the one who squeezes the udders hard to draw out milk presses out butter; and the one who blows his nose violently draws out blood; and the one who provokes anger produces strife."

## Translator Notes

- **30:1** — Jerome renders the Hebrew proper names etymologically in the Vulgate: "the Gatherer" (Congregantis) for Agur, "the Vomiter" (Vomentis) for Jakeh, and "with whom God is, and who, strengthened by God dwelling with him" for Ithiel and Ucal.
- **30:17** — The Vulgate reads "the childbirth of his mother" (partum matris suæ), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "obedience to a mother."
- **30:19** — The Vulgate reads "the way of a man in youth" (viam viri in adolescentia), following the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "the way of a man with a young woman."
- **30:26** — The Vulgate identifies this creature as "the little hare" (lepusculus), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to the rock badger (hyrax).
- **30:31** — The Vulgate identifies the animals as a "rooster" (gallus) and a "ram" (aries) and concludes "nor is there a king who can resist him" (nec est rex, qui resistat ei), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's obscure first phrase, "male goat," and "a king whose army is with him."
