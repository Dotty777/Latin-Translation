# Proverbs 10 — Saints Modern English (Latin / Clementine Vulgate)

## Text

**10:1** A wise son gladdens a father, but a foolish son is the grief of his mother.

**10:2** Treasures of wickedness will be of no benefit, but righteousness will deliver from death.

**10:3** The Lord will not afflict the soul of the righteous with hunger, and He will overturn the plots of the wicked.

**10:4** A slack hand has produced poverty, but the hand of the strong prepares riches. Whoever relies on lies shepherds the winds, and that very same one pursues flying birds.

**10:5** Whoever gathers in the harvest is a wise son, but whoever snores in summer is a son of disgrace.

**10:6** The blessing of the Lord is on the head of the righteous, but iniquity covers the mouth of the wicked.

**10:7** The memory of the righteous is with praises, and the name of the wicked will decay.

**10:8** The wise in heart receives precepts; a fool in his lips is beaten.

**10:9** Whoever walks sincerely walks confidently, but whoever perverts his ways will be exposed.

**10:10** Whoever winks with the eye will cause pain, and a fool in his lips will be lashed.

**10:11** A wellspring of life is the mouth of the righteous, and the mouth of the wicked covers iniquity.

**10:12** Hatred stirs up quarrels, and love covers all misdeeds.

**10:13** On the lips of the wise, wisdom is found, and a rod is on the back of the one who lacks sense.

**10:14** The wise conceal knowledge, but the mouth of a fool is very close to disgrace.

**10:15** The wealth of a rich person is the city of his strength; the terror of the poor is their poverty.

**10:16** The work of the righteous leads to life, but the fruit of the wicked to sin.

**10:17** The way of life is for the one who keeps discipline, but whoever abandons reproofs goes astray.

**10:18** Lying lips conceal hatred; whoever utters insult is unwise.

**10:19** In much speaking sin will not be lacking, but whoever restrains his lips is most prudent.

**10:20** Choice silver is the tongue of the righteous, but the heart of the wicked counts for nothing.

**10:21** The lips of the righteous instruct very many, but those who are untaught will die in poverty of mind.

**10:22** The blessing of the Lord makes people rich, and affliction will not be joined to them.

**10:23** As if for a laugh, a fool commits a wicked deed, but wisdom is prudence for a man.

**10:24** What the wicked person fears will come upon him; the righteous person will be granted his desire.

**10:25** Like a passing storm, the wicked person will be no more, but the righteous person is like an everlasting foundation.

**10:26** Just as vinegar is to the teeth and smoke is to the eyes, so is a lazy person to those who sent him.

**10:27** The fear of the Lord will add days, and the years of the wicked will be shortened.

**10:28** The expectation of the righteous is gladness, but the hope of the wicked will perish.

**10:29** The strength of the sincere person is the way of the Lord, and it is terror to those who work evil.

**10:30** The righteous person will never be shaken, but the wicked will not dwell on the earth.

**10:31** The mouth of the righteous will give birth to wisdom; the tongue of the perverse will perish.

**10:32** The lips of the righteous consider pleasing things, and the mouth of the wicked, perverse things.

## Translator Notes

- **10:4** — The second sentence ("Whoever relies on lies...") is a distinctive Clementine Vulgate addition derived from the Septuagint and Old Latin traditions and is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text. In the first sentence, Latin "fortium" ("of the strong") corresponds to Hebrew "of the diligent."
- **10:6** — In v. 6 the Vulgate makes "iniquitas" nominative subject ("iniquity covers the mouth of the wicked"), whereas in v. 11 it makes "iniquitatem" accusative object ("the mouth of the wicked covers iniquity"), reflecting the two ways of parsing the identical Hebrew clause.
- **10:8** — Latin "stultus caeditur labiis" (and v. 10 "stultus labiis verberabitur") renders the Hebrew "a babbling fool will come to ruin" with verbs of beating ("is beaten" / "will be lashed"); "labiis" may also be read instrumentally ("is beaten by his lips").
- **10:23** — The Vulgate reads nominative "prudentia" ("wisdom is prudence for a man") rather than the Hebrew "wisdom [is like sport] to a man of understanding."
- **10:24** — The Latin text reads nominative singular "justus" with passive "dabitur" and retained accusative "desiderium suum" ("the righteous person will be granted his desire"), whereas some Vulgate editions read dative plural "justis" ("to the righteous").
