## Text

**21:1** Like divisions of water, so is a king's heart in the hand of the Lord; wherever He wills, He will turn it.

**21:2** Every way of a man appears right to himself, but the Lord weighs hearts.

**21:3** To practice mercy and justice pleases the Lord more than sacrifices.

**21:4** Haughtiness of the eyes is swelling of the heart; the lamp of the wicked is sin.

**21:6** One who gathers treasures by a lying tongue is empty and senseless, and will be driven into the snares of death.

**21:7** The robberies of the wicked will drag them down, because they refused to practice justice.

**21:8** A man's perverse way is foreign, while the work of one who is pure is upright.

**21:9** It is better to sit in a corner of a rooftop than with a quarrelsome woman and in a shared house.

**21:10** The soul of a wicked man desires evil; he will show no mercy to his neighbor.

**21:11** When a pestilent person is punished, a little one will grow wiser; and if he pursues a wise person, he will gain knowledge.

**21:12** A righteous man reflects carefully on the house of a wicked man, so that he may draw the wicked away from evil.

**21:13** Whoever blocks his ear to the cry of the poor will himself also cry out, and will not be heard.

**21:14** A hidden gift extinguishes fits of anger, and a present in the bosom, the greatest indignation.

**21:15** It is a joy for a righteous person to practice justice, and terror for those who work iniquity.

**21:16** A man who strays from the way of instruction will dwell in the assembly of the giants.

**21:17** Whoever loves banquets will be in poverty; whoever loves wine and rich foods will not grow rich.

**21:18** A wicked man is given in place of a righteous man, and an unjust man in place of the upright.

**21:19** It is better to dwell in a desert land than with an argumentative and hot-tempered woman.

**21:20** Desirable treasure and oil are in the dwelling of a righteous man, and an imprudent person will squander it.

**21:21** One who follows righteousness and mercy will find life, righteousness, and glory.

**21:22** A wise man scaled the city of the strong and tore down the strength of its confidence.

**21:23** The one who guards his mouth and his tongue guards his soul from distresses.

**21:24** A proud and arrogant man is called uninstructed, the one who works pride in anger.

**21:25** Desires kill the lazy man, for his hands have refused to do any work.

**21:26** All day long he craves and desires, while the one who is righteous will give and will not cease.

**21:27** The sacrifices of the wicked are abominable, because they are offered from wrongdoing.

**21:28** A lying witness will perish; an obedient man will speak victory.

**21:29** A wicked man brazenly hardens his face, while the one who is upright corrects his way.

**21:30** There is no wisdom, there is no prudence, there is no counsel against the Lord.

**21:31** A horse is made ready for the day of battle, but the Lord grants deliverance.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**21:5** *(after `21:4`)* The plans of a vigorous person are always in abundance, yet every lazy person is always in poverty.

## Translator Notes

- **21:4** — The Clementine Vulgate divides this verse into two separate statements with est ("is"), rendering dilatatio cordis literally ("swelling of the heart") and reading lucerna ("lamp", Hebrew ner) rather than "plowing" (nir).
- **21:6** — Following the Septuagint tradition, the Vulgate reads vanus et excors est, et impingetur ad laqueos mortis ("is empty and senseless, and will be driven into the snares of death"), where the Masoretic Hebrew speaks of a fleeting vapor and seekers of death.
- **21:12** — The Vulgate reads ut detrahat impios a malo ("so that he may draw the wicked away from evil"), portraying a righteous person seeking to turn the wicked from evil rather than God casting them into ruin as in the Masoretic Hebrew.
- **21:16** — Latin gigantum ("giants") renders Hebrew rephaim ("the dead" or "shades"), as also at 9:18.
- **21:28** — In a distinctive Vulgate reading, vir obediens loquetur victoriam ("an obedient man will speak victory") interprets Hebrew shomea ("listening") as "obedient" and lanetsach ("enduringly") in its later sense of "victory."
