## Text

**14:1** A wise woman builds her house, but an unwise one will tear down with her hands even one already built.

**14:2** He who walks on a straight path and fears God is despised by him who walks in a disgraceful way.

**14:3** In the mouth of a fool is a rod of pride, but the lips of the wise guard them.

**14:4** Where there are no oxen, the manger is empty; but where there are abundant crops, there the strength of the ox is evident.

**14:5** A faithful witness does not lie, but a deceitful witness utters a lie.

**14:6** A scoffer seeks wisdom and does not find it; the instruction of the prudent is easy.

**14:7** Go against a foolish man, and he does not know lips of prudence.

**14:8** The wisdom of an astute man is to understand his own way, and the imprudence of fools goes astray.

**14:9** A fool will mock sin, and among the righteous favor will dwell.

**14:10** The heart that knows the bitterness of its own soul: a stranger will not mingle in its joy.

**14:11** The house of the wicked will be wiped out, but the tents of the righteous will sprout.

**14:12** There is a way that appears just to a man, but its final ends lead down to death.

**14:13** Laughter will be mingled with sorrow, and mourning seizes the end of joy.

**14:14** A fool will be filled with his own ways, and a good man will be above him.

**14:15** An innocent man believes every word; a shrewd man considers his steps. For a deceitful son there will be nothing good, but for a wise servant his deeds will be prosperous, and his way will be directed.

**14:16** A wise man fears and turns aside from evil; a fool oversteps and is confident.

**14:17** An impatient man will work foolishness, and a crafty man is odious.

**14:18** Little ones will possess foolishness, and the shrewd will wait for knowledge.

**14:19** The evil will lie prostrate before the good, and the wicked before the gates of the righteous.

**14:20** Even to his own neighbor a poor man will be odious, but the friends of the rich are many.

**14:21** He who despises his neighbor sins, but he who shows mercy to the poor will be blessed. He who believes in the Lord loves mercy.

**14:22** Those who work evil go astray; mercy and truth prepare good things.

**14:23** In all work there will be abundance, but where words are very many, there is frequently poverty.

**14:24** The crown of the wise is their riches; the folly of fools is imprudence.

**14:25** A faithful witness delivers souls, and a two-faced man utters lies.

**14:26** In the fear of the Lord is confidence of strength, and for His children there will be hope.

**14:27** The fear of the Lord is a fountain of life, so that they may turn aside from the ruin of death.

**14:28** In a multitude of people is the dignity of a king, and in a scarcity of the populace is the disgrace of a prince.

**14:29** He who is patient is governed by great prudence, but he who is impatient exalts his own foolishness.

**14:30** The life of the flesh is health of the heart; decay of the bones is envy.

**14:31** He who slanders the needy reproaches his Maker, but he who shows mercy to the poor honors Him.

**14:32** In his malice the wicked man will be driven out, but the righteous man has hope in his death.

**14:33** In the heart of a prudent man wisdom rests, and it will instruct all the unlearned.

**14:34** Righteousness lifts up a nation, but sin makes peoples wretched.

**14:35** An understanding minister is acceptable to a king; a worthless one will endure his wrath.

## Translator Notes

- **14:7** — Latin Vade contra ("Go against") translates the Hebrew idiom for departing from someone's presence literally, and nescit ("he does not know") puts the verb in the third person rather than the second.
- **14:15** — Innocens literally means "innocent" (rather than "simple"), and the second sentence (Filio doloso ... via ejus) is an additional maxim present in the Clementine Vulgate and Septuagint but absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **14:21** — The closing sentence (Qui credit in Domino misericordiam diligit) is an additional line retained in the Clementine Vulgate (shared with the Septuagint tradition) that does not appear in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **14:32** — Vulgate sperat autem justus in morte sua ("the righteous man has hope in his death") preserves the literal reading "in his death" (matching the Hebrew consonantal text) rather than the Septuagint's "in his integrity."
- **14:33** — Vulgate et indoctos quosque erudiet ("and it will instruct all the unlearned") is a distinctly Vulgate reading, contrasting with the Hebrew ("in the midst of fools it makes itself known").
