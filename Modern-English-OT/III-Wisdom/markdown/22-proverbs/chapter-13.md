# Proverbs 13 — Saints Modern English (Latin / Clementine Vulgate)

## Text

**13:1** A wise son is a father's instruction, but he who is a mocker does not listen when he is rebuked.

**13:2** From the fruit of his own mouth a man will be satisfied with good things, but the soul of transgressors is unjust.

**13:3** He who guards his mouth guards his soul, but he who is thoughtless in speaking will experience evil.

**13:4** The lazy man wants and does not want, but the soul of those who work will be made fat.

**13:5** The righteous man will detest a lying word, but the wicked man puts to shame and will be put to shame.

**13:6** Righteousness guards the way of the innocent, but wickedness trips up the sinner.

**13:7** There is one who is as if rich, though he has nothing, and there is one who is as if poor, though he is in great riches.

**13:8** A man's own riches are the ransom of his soul, but he who is poor does not endure a rebuke.

**13:9** The light of the righteous brings gladness, but the lamp of the wicked will be extinguished.

**13:10** Among the proud there are always quarrels, but those who do all things with counsel are governed by wisdom.

**13:11** Wealth gained in haste will diminish, but what is gathered little by little by hand will be multiplied.

**13:12** Hope that is delayed afflicts the soul; a desire that arrives is a tree of life.

**13:13** He who disparages any matter binds himself for the future, but he who fears the command will live in peace. Deceitful souls go astray in sins, but the righteous are merciful and show mercy.

**13:14** The law of a wise man is a fountain of life, so that he may turn aside from the ruin of death.

**13:15** Good instruction will give favor; in the path of despisers there is a chasm.

**13:16** The shrewd man does all things with counsel, but he who is senseless exposes foolishness.

**13:17** The messenger of a wicked man will fall into evil, but a faithful envoy is health.

**13:18** Poverty and disgrace are for him who abandons discipline, but he who yields to one who rebukes will be glorified.

**13:19** A desire, if it is fulfilled, delights the soul; fools detest those who flee from evil.

**13:20** He who walks with the wise will be wise; the friend of fools will become like them.

**13:21** Evil pursues sinners, and good things will be repaid to the righteous.

**13:22** A good man leaves children and grandchildren as heirs, and the wealth of the sinner is kept for the righteous.

**13:23** Much food is in the newly tilled fields of the fathers, and it is gathered for others without justice.

**13:24** He who spares the rod hates his own son, but he who loves him instructs him persistently.

**13:25** The righteous man eats and fills his soul, but the belly of the wicked is insatiable.

## Translator Notes

- **13:1** — Latin Filius sapiens doctrina patris lacks an explicit verb, leaving doctrina either predicate nominative ("is a father's instruction") or ablative of means ("by a father's instruction"), without supplying "hears."
- **13:4** — Vulgate Vult et non vult piger ("wants and does not want") portrays the lazy person's indecision rather than the Hebrew "craves and has nothing"; impinguabitur ("will be made fat") is a literal idiom for thriving abundance.
- **13:13** — The first sentence renders the maxim distinctly in the Vulgate, and the second sentence (Animae dolosae ... miserantur) is an additional couplet present in the Clementine Vulgate and Septuagint but absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **13:20** — Vulgate similis efficietur ("will become like them") reflects a reading connecting the Hebrew verb with companionship rather than suffering harm.
- **13:23** — Vulgate in novalibus patrum ("in the newly tilled fields of the fathers") and aliis congregantur ("is gathered for others") are distinctly Vulgate readings differing from the Hebrew ("of the poor ... is swept away").
