# Proverbs 15 — Saints Modern English (Latin / Clementine Vulgate)

## Text

**15:1** A soft reply breaks anger; a harsh word arouses fury.

**15:2** The tongue of the wise adorns knowledge; the mouth of the senseless bubbles over with folly.

**15:3** In every place, the eyes of the Lord observe the good and the evil.

**15:4** A calming tongue is a tree of life, but one that is unrestrained will crush the spirit.

**15:5** A fool mocks his father's discipline, but the one who keeps rebukes will become more astute. In abundant righteousness there is the greatest strength, but the thoughts of the wicked will be uprooted.

**15:6** The house of the righteous is very great strength, and in the fruits of the wicked there is turmoil.

**15:7** The lips of the wise will spread knowledge; the heart of fools will be different.

**15:8** The sacrifices of the wicked are abominable to the Lord; the vows of the righteous are pleasing.

**15:9** An abomination to the Lord is the way of the wicked; the one who follows righteousness is loved by Him.

**15:10** Harsh instruction is for the one who abandons the way of life; the one who hates rebukes will die.

**15:11** The underworld and destruction are before the Lord; how much more the hearts of the sons of men!

**15:12** A pestilent person does not love the one who reproves him, nor does he go to the wise.

**15:13** A rejoicing heart brightens the face; in sorrow of mind the spirit is brought low.

**15:14** The heart of the wise seeks instruction, and the mouth of fools feeds on ignorance.

**15:15** All the days of the poor are bad; an untroubled mind is like a continual banquet.

**15:16** A little with the fear of the Lord is better than great and insatiable treasures.

**15:17** It is better to be invited to vegetables with love than to a fattened calf with hatred.

**15:18** A quick-tempered man provokes quarrels; the one who is patient calms those that have been aroused.

**15:19** The road of the lazy is like a hedge of thorns; the way of the righteous is without obstacle.

**15:20** A wise son gladdens a father, and a foolish man despises his own mother.

**15:21** Folly is a joy for a fool, and a prudent man directs his own steps.

**15:22** Plans are scattered where there is no counsel, but where there are many counselors, they are confirmed.

**15:23** A man rejoices in the utterance of his mouth, and a timely word is best.

**15:24** The path of life is above a learned man, so that he may turn away from the lowest underworld.

**15:25** The Lord will demolish the house of the proud, and will make firm the boundaries of the widow.

**15:26** An abomination to the Lord are evil thoughts, and pure, most beautiful speech will be established by Him.

**15:27** The one who pursues greed throws his own house into turmoil, but the one who hates bribes will live. Through mercy and faith sins are cleansed, and through the fear of the Lord everyone turns away from evil.

**15:28** The mind of the righteous meditates on obedience; the mouth of the wicked overflows with evil things. When the ways of a man please the Lord, He will turn even his enemies to peace.

**15:29** The Lord is far away from the wicked, and He will hear the prayers of the righteous. A little with righteousness is better than many fruits with unrighteousness.

**15:30** The light of the eyes gladdens the soul; a good report fattens the bones.

**15:32** The one who casts aside discipline despises his own soul, but the one who yields to rebukes is a possessor of a heart.

**15:33** The fear of the Lord is the discipline of wisdom, and humility precedes glory.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**15:31** *(after `15:30`)* The ear that listens to the rebukes of life will dwell in the midst of the wise.

**16:1** *(after `15:33`)* It belongs to a man to prepare the soul, and to the Lord to govern the tongue.

## Translator Notes

- **15:5** — The second sentence ('In abundant righteousness... will be uprooted') is a Vulgate reading derived from the Septuagint tradition and is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **15:11** — Latin 'infernus' denotes the lower realm or underworld (the abode of the dead, also in 15:24 'inferno novissimo'), corresponding to Hebrew Sheol.
- **15:16** — Vulgate reads 'insatiabiles' ('insatiable'), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'with turmoil.'
- **15:27** — The second sentences of 15:27, 15:28, and 15:29 correspond to verses 16:6, 16:7, and 16:8 in the Hebrew Masoretic Text, reflecting the distinct verse arrangement of the Clementine Vulgate.
- **15:28** — Vulgate reads 'obedientiam' ('obedience'), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'how to answer.'
