## Text

**27:1** Do not boast about tomorrow, not knowing what the coming day may produce.

**27:2** Let another person praise you, and not your mouth; a stranger, and not your lips.

**27:3** A rock is heavy and sand is burdensome, but a fool's anger is heavier than both.

**27:4** Anger has no mercy, nor does bursting fury; and who will be able to endure the onslaught of one who is stirred up?

**27:5** An open rebuke is better than concealed love.

**27:6** Better are the wounds of one who loves than the deceitful kisses of one who hates.

**27:7** A sated soul will trample a honeycomb, and a hungry soul will take even what is bitter as sweet.

**27:8** Just as a bird migrating from its nest, so is a man who abandons his place.

**27:9** The heart is delighted by ointment and various fragrances, and the soul is sweetened by a friend's good counsels.

**27:10** Do not abandon your friend and your father's friend, and do not enter your brother's house on the day of your affliction. Better is a neighbor close by than a brother far away.

**27:11** Devote yourself to wisdom, my son, and gladden my heart, so that you may be able to answer a word to one who reproaches.

**27:12** A shrewd man, seeing evil, has hidden himself; little ones, passing on, have suffered losses.

**27:13** Take the garment of the one who has become guarantor for a stranger, and for outsiders take a pledge from him.

**27:14** Whoever blesses his neighbor with a loud voice, rising during the night, will be like one who curses.

**27:15** Dripping roofs on a day of cold and a quarrelsome woman are comparable.

**27:16** The one who restrains her is like someone who would hold the wind, and he will summon the oil of his right hand.

**27:17** Iron is sharpened by iron, and a man sharpens the face of his friend.

**27:18** Whoever watches over a fig tree will eat its fruits, and the one who is the keeper of his master will be honored.

**27:19** Just as in waters the faces of those looking in shine back, so the hearts of people are evident to the prudent.

**27:20** Hell and destruction are never filled; likewise, the eyes of people are also insatiable.

**27:21** Just as silver is tested in a crucible and gold in a furnace, so a person is tested by the mouth of one who praises. The heart of an unjust person seeks evil things, but an upright heart seeks knowledge.

**27:22** If you pound a fool in a mortar like crushed barley, with the pestle striking from above, his foolishness will not be removed from him.

**27:23** Carefully know the appearance of your livestock, and examine your flocks.

**27:24** For you will not continually have power, but a crown will be bestowed from generation to generation.

**27:25** The meadows have opened, and green grasses have appeared, and hay has been gathered from the mountains.

**27:26** Lambs are for your clothing, and young goats are for the price of a field.

**27:27** Let the milk of goats be enough for you as your food, and for the necessities of your household, and for the sustenance of your female servants.

## Translator Notes

- **27:4** — The Vulgate reads 'the onslaught of one who is stirred up' (impetum concitati), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'jealousy.'
- **27:16** — The Vulgate literally reads 'he will summon the oil of his right hand' (oleum dexteræ suæ vocabit), taking the Hebrew verb in the sense of 'call' rather than 'encounter' or 'grasp.'
- **27:19** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads 'the hearts of people are evident to the prudent' (corda hominum manifesta sunt prudentibus), where the Hebrew reads 'the heart of man reflects man.'
- **27:21** — The second sentence ('The heart of an unjust person seeks evil things, but an upright heart seeks knowledge') is present in the Vulgate, agreeing with the Septuagint, and absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **27:24** — The Vulgate contrasts the two clauses affirmatively ('for you will not continually have power, but a crown will be bestowed from generation to generation'), whereas the Hebrew negates both ('riches are not forever, nor does a crown endure to all generations').
