# Proverbs 12 — Saints Modern English (Latin / Clementine Vulgate)

## Text

**12:1** One who loves discipline loves knowledge, but one who hates rebukes is unwise.

**12:2** Whoever is good will draw grace from the Lord, but whoever trusts in his own thoughts acts wickedly.

**12:3** A person will not be made strong by wickedness, and the root of the righteous will not be shaken.

**12:4** A diligent wife is a crown for her husband, and she who does things deserving of shame is decay in his bones.

**12:5** The thoughts of the righteous are judgments, and the plans of the wicked are fraudulent.

**12:6** The words of the wicked lie in ambush for blood; the mouth of the righteous will deliver them.

**12:7** Overturn the wicked, and they will not be; but the house of the righteous will endure.

**12:8** By his learning a man will be known, but whoever is empty and senseless will lie open to contempt.

**12:9** Better is someone poor and self-sufficient than someone boastful and lacking bread.

**12:10** The righteous person knows the lives of his livestock, but the mercies of the wicked are cruel.

**12:11** Whoever works his own land will be satisfied with bread, but whoever pursues idleness is utterly foolish. Whoever delights in lingering over wine leaves behind disgrace in his own strongholds.

**12:12** The desire of the wicked person is a stronghold of the most evil, but the root of the righteous will thrive.

**12:13** Because of the sins of the lips, ruin approaches the evil person, but the righteous person will escape from distress.

**12:14** From the fruit of his own mouth each one will be filled with good things, and according to the works of his hands he will be repaid.

**12:15** A fool's way is right in his eyes, but whoever is wise listens to counsels.

**12:16** A fool immediately discloses his anger, but whoever overlooks a wrong is shrewd.

**12:17** Whoever speaks what he knows is a witness to righteousness, but whoever lies is a fraudulent witness.

**12:18** There is one who promises and is pierced as if by a sword of conscience, yet the tongue of the wise is health.

**12:19** The lip of truth will be firm forever, but whoever is a hasty witness fabricates a tongue of falsehood.

**12:20** Deceit is in the heart of those who plot evil things, but joy follows those who enter into plans of peace.

**12:21** Whatever happens to the righteous person will not sadden him, but the wicked will be filled with harm.

**12:22** False lips are an abomination to the Lord, but those who act faithfully please Him.

**12:23** An experienced person conceals knowledge, and the heart of the unwise calls forth folly.

**12:24** The hand of the strong will rule, but the one that is slack will serve under tribute.

**12:25** Sorrow in a man's heart will humble him, and he will be gladdened by a good word.

**12:26** Whoever disregards loss for the sake of a friend is righteous, but the path of the wicked will deceive them.

**12:27** A fraudulent person will not find gain, and a person's property will be the value of gold.

**12:28** On the pathway of righteousness is life, but a wayward path leads to death.

## Translator Notes

- **12:11** — The second sentence ('Whoever delights in lingering over wine...') is a Clementine Vulgate addition matching the Septuagint and is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **12:18** — The Vulgate reads 'promittit, et quasi gladio pungitur conscientiae' ('promises and is pierced as if by a sword of conscience'), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of rash speech being like sword thrusts.
- **12:26** — The Vulgate renders a difficult Hebrew clause as 'Qui negligit damnum propter amicum, justus est' ('Whoever disregards loss for the sake of a friend is righteous'), whereas many modern renderings of the Hebrew read 'The righteous is a guide to his neighbor.'
- **12:27** — The Vulgate reads 'fraudulentus' ('fraudulent person') and 'auri pretium' ('the value of gold'), taking the Hebrew terms in their alternate senses of deceit and gold rather than laziness and diligence.
- **12:28** — Following the ancient versions including the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads 'iter autem devium ducit ad mortem' ('but a wayward path leads to death'), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'and in its pathway there is no death.'
