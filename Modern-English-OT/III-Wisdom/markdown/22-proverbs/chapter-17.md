# Proverbs 17 — Saints Modern English (Latin / Clementine Vulgate)

## Text

**17:1** A dry morsel with joy is better than a house full of sacrifices with quarreling.

**17:2** A wise servant will rule over foolish sons and will divide the inheritance among the brothers.

**17:3** Just as silver is tested by fire and gold in the furnace, so the Lord tests hearts.

**17:4** An evil man obeys an unjust tongue, and a deceitful man complies with lying lips.

**17:5** Whoever despises a poor man reproaches his Maker, and whoever rejoices in the ruin of another will not go unpunished.

**17:6** The crown of old men is children's children, and the glory of children is their fathers.

**17:7** Well-ordered words are not fitting for a fool, nor is a lying lip for a prince.

**17:8** The expectation of one who waits is a most pleasing gem; wherever he turns, he understands prudently.

**17:9** Whoever conceals an offense seeks friendships; whoever repeats it with a second remark separates allies.

**17:10** A rebuke accomplishes more with a prudent man than a hundred blows with a fool.

**17:11** An evil man always seeks quarrels, yet a cruel angel will be sent against him.

**17:12** It is preferable to meet a she-bear robbed of her cubs than a simpleton trusting in his folly.

**17:13** Whoever repays evil for good, evil will not withdraw from his house.

**17:14** Whoever releases water is the beginning of quarrels, and before he suffers insult, he abandons judgment.

**17:15** Whoever justifies the wicked, and whoever condemns the righteous, each of them is abominable before God.

**17:16** What does it profit a fool to have riches, since he cannot buy wisdom? Whoever makes his house high seeks ruin, and whoever avoids learning will fall into evils.

**17:17** One who is a friend loves at all times, and a brother is proven in times of distress.

**17:18** A foolish man will clap his hands when he pledges himself for his friend.

**17:19** Whoever meditates on dissensions loves disputes, and whoever raises a doorway high seeks ruin.

**17:20** Whoever has a perverse heart will not find good, and whoever twists his tongue will fall into evil.

**17:21** A fool is born to his own disgrace, and not even a father will rejoice in a simpleton.

**17:22** A joyful mind makes a flourishing life; a sorrowful spirit dries up the bones.

**17:23** A wicked man accepts gifts from the bosom so that he may pervert the paths of judgment.

**17:24** On the face of a prudent man wisdom shines; the eyes of fools are at the ends of the earth.

**17:25** A foolish son is a father's anger and the grief of the mother who gave birth to him.

**17:26** It is not good to inflict loss upon a righteous man, nor to strike a prince who judges what is right.

**17:27** Whoever moderates his words is learned and prudent, and a well-instructed man has a precious spirit.

**17:28** Even a fool, if he keeps silent, will be counted wise, and if he presses his lips shut, intelligent.

## Translator Notes

- **17:8** — Vulgate-distinct reading: "exspectatio praestolantis" ("the expectation of one who waits") and "prudenter intelligit" ("understands prudently"), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of a bribe in the eyes of its owner who prospers wherever he turns.
- **17:14** — Vulgate-distinct reading: the second clause is an indicative statement ("before he suffers insult, he abandons judgment"), whereas the Hebrew has an imperative ("abandon contention before it breaks out").
- **17:16** — The second sentence ("Whoever makes his house high ... will fall into evils") is a Vulgate reading shared with the Septuagint and absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **17:22** — Vulgate-distinct reading: "aetatem floridam facit" ("makes a flourishing life"), aligning with the Septuagint, where the Hebrew reads "is good medicine" (or "promotes healing").
