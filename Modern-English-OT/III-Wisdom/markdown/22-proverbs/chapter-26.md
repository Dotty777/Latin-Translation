# Proverbs 26 — Saints Modern English (Latin / Clementine Vulgate)

## Text

**26:1** Just as snow in summer and rain in harvest, so glory is unfitting for a fool.

**26:2** Just as a bird flying away to other places and a sparrow going wherever it wishes, so a curse uttered in vain will come upon someone.

**26:3** A whip for a horse, and a halter for a donkey, and a rod on the back of the imprudent.

**26:4** Do not answer a fool according to his folly, so that you do not become like him.

**26:5** Answer a fool according to his folly, so that he does not seem wise to himself.

**26:6** Lame in his feet and drinking iniquity: such is one who sends words through a foolish messenger.

**26:7** Just as a lame man has handsome legs in vain, so a proverb is unfitting in the mouth of fools.

**26:8** Just as one who throws a stone onto a heap of Mercury, so is one who grants honor to an unwise man.

**26:9** Just as if a thorn should sprout in the hand of a drunkard, so is a proverb in the mouth of fools.

**26:10** Judgment settles cases, and one who imposes silence on a fool calms anger.

**26:11** Just as a dog that returns to its vomit, so is an imprudent man who repeats his folly.

**26:12** Have you seen a man who seems wise to himself? An unwise man will have more hope than he.

**26:13** The lazy man says, "There is a lion on the road, and a lioness on the paths."

**26:14** Just as a door turns on its hinge, so does the lazy man on his bed.

**26:15** The lazy man hides his hand under his armpit, and he labors if he turns it to his mouth.

**26:16** The lazy man seems wiser to himself than seven men speaking maxims.

**26:17** Just as one who seizes a dog by the ears, so is one who passes by impatiently and gets mixed up in another's quarrel.

**26:18** Just as one who launches arrows and spears to bring death is harmful,

**26:19** so a man deceitfully harms his friend, and when he has been caught, he says, "I did it in play."

**26:20** When the wood runs out, the fire will be extinguished, and with the whisperer removed, disputes will quiet down.

**26:21** Just as charcoal is to glowing coals and wood to fire, so a hot-tempered man stirs up quarrels.

**26:22** The words of a whisperer are seemingly simple, and they penetrate to the depths of the belly.

**26:23** Just as if you wanted to adorn a clay vessel with impure silver, so are swelling lips allied with a very wicked heart.

**26:24** By his lips an enemy is recognized, when he has plotted deceits in his heart.

**26:25** When he lowers his voice, do not trust him, because seven evils are in his heart.

**26:26** One who covers hatred deceitfully will have his malice revealed in the council.

**26:27** One who digs a pit will fall into it, and for one who rolls a stone, it will return to him.

**26:28** A deceitful tongue does not love truth, and a slippery mouth produces ruin.

## Translator Notes

- **26:2** — The Vulgate has no negation and reads in quempiam superveniet ("will come upon someone"), agreeing with the Hebrew qere ("to him") rather than the ketiv ("not").
- **26:8** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew for a stone-heap or sling as acervum Mercurii ("a heap of Mercury"), referring to a Roman roadside cairn dedicated to Mercury onto which travelers tossed stones in tribute.
- **26:10** — Where the Hebrew text is cryptic (apparently of an archer wounding passersby or of one hiring a fool), the Vulgate has an entirely different proverb.
- **26:15** — The Vulgate reads sub ascella sua ("under his armpit") where the Masoretic Hebrew reads "in the dish."
- **26:28** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads non amat veritatem ("does not love truth") where the Masoretic Hebrew reads "hates those it crushes."
