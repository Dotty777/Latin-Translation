## Text

**24:1** Do not envy evil men, nor desire to be with them;

**24:2** for their mind plots robberies, and their lips speak frauds.

**24:3** A house will be built by wisdom, and it will be strengthened by prudence.

**24:4** Through instruction the storehouses will be filled with all valuable and finest wealth.

**24:5** A wise man is strong, and a learned man is sturdy and mighty;

**24:6** for war is undertaken with orderly planning, and there will be deliverance where counsels are many.

**24:7** Wisdom is too lofty for a fool; at the gate he will not open his mouth.

**24:8** He who plans to do evil will be called a fool.

**24:9** A fool's planning is sin, and a slanderer is an abomination to people.

**24:10** If you despair, weary in the day of distress, your strength will be diminished.

**24:11** Rescue those who are being led to death, and do not cease to free those who are being dragged to destruction.

**24:12** If you say, "Strength does not suffice," He who is the observer of the heart, He Himself understands; and nothing escapes the keeper of your soul, and He will repay a man according to his works.

**24:13** My son, eat honey, for it is good, and the honeycomb, which is sweetest to your throat.

**24:14** Such also is the instruction of wisdom for your soul; when you find it, you will have hope in the end, and your hope will not perish.

**24:15** Do not lie in wait and seek wickedness in the house of the righteous, nor lay waste his resting place.

**24:16** For a righteous man will fall seven times and will rise again, but the wicked will collapse into evil.

**24:17** When your enemy falls, do not rejoice, and in his ruin do not let your heart exult,

**24:18** or else the Lord may see it, and it may displease Him, and He may take His anger away from him.

**24:19** Do not contend with the most evil men, nor envy the wicked;

**24:20** for the evil have no hope of things to come, and the lamp of the wicked will be extinguished.

**24:21** Fear the Lord, my son, and the king, and do not mingle with slanderers;

**24:22** for their destruction will rise up suddenly, and who knows the ruin of both?

**24:23** These things also are for the wise. To favor a person in judgment is not good.

**24:24** Peoples will curse those who say to the wicked, "You are righteous," and tribes will detest them.

**24:25** Those who rebuke him will be praised, and a blessing will come upon them.

**24:26** He who answers with right words will kiss the lips.

**24:27** Prepare your work outside, and diligently cultivate your field, so that afterward you may build your house.

**24:28** Do not be a witness without cause against your neighbor, nor entice anyone with your lips.

**24:29** Do not say, "Just as he did to me, so I will do to him; I will repay each one according to his work."

**24:30** I passed through the field of a lazy man, and through the vineyard of a foolish man,

**24:31** and look, nettles had completely filled it, and thorns had covered its surface, and the wall of stones had been broken down.

**24:32** When I had seen this, I placed it in my heart, and from the example I learned discipline.

**24:33** A little, I say, you will sleep, a short while you will slumber; for a tiny moment you will fold your hands so that you may rest,

**24:34** and poverty will come to you like a runner, and beggary like an armed man.

## Translator Notes

- **24:12** — Vulgate Vires non suppetunt ("Strength does not suffice") differs from the Hebrew ("Look, we did not know this").
- **24:15** — Vulgate et quaeras impietatem in domo justi ("and seek wickedness in the house of the righteous") takes the Hebrew word for "wicked" as an abstract object ("wickedness") rather than a vocative ("O wicked one").
- **24:21** — Vulgate detractoribus ("slanderers") translates a difficult Hebrew term commonly rendered "those who change" or "rebels," matching detractor ("slanderer") in 24:9.
- **24:23** — Latin Cognoscere personam (literally "to recognize a person") is a legal idiom meaning to show favoritism or partiality in judgment.
- **24:33** — Unlike the Hebrew, which uses noun phrases ("a little sleep, a little slumber"), the Vulgate frames the verse as a direct second-person warning by the narrator with inquam ("I say") and future verbs.
