## Text

**23:1** When you sit to eat with a ruler, pay careful attention to what has been placed before your face.

**23:2** And place a knife at your throat, if indeed you have your soul in your power.

**23:3** Do not desire any of his foods, in which is bread of falsehood.

**23:4** Do not toil to become rich, but place a limit on your prudence.

**23:5** Do not lift up your eyes to riches that you cannot have, for they will make wings for themselves like an eagle's, and will fly into the sky.

**23:6** Do not eat with an envious man, and do not desire his foods,

**23:7** for in the likeness of a fortune-teller and an interpreter of omens, he calculates what he does not know. "Eat and drink," he will say to you, yet his mind is not with you.

**23:8** You will vomit up the foods that you had eaten, and you will lose your fine words.

**23:9** Do not speak in the ears of the foolish, who will despise the instruction of your discourse.

**23:10** Do not touch the boundaries of little ones, and do not enter the field of orphans;

**23:11** for their kinsman is strong, and he himself will judge their case against you.

**23:12** Let your heart enter into instruction, and your ears to the words of knowledge.

**23:13** Do not withhold discipline from a boy; for if you strike him with a rod, he will not die.

**23:14** You will strike him with a rod, and you will rescue his soul from the underworld.

**23:15** My son, if your mind is wise, my heart will rejoice with you,

**23:16** and my kidneys will exult when your lips speak what is right.

**23:17** Do not let your heart envy sinners, but remain in the fear of the Lord all day long;

**23:18** for you will have hope in the end, and your expectation will not be taken away.

**23:19** Listen, my son, and be wise, and direct your mind in the way.

**23:20** Do not be at the banquets of drinkers, nor at the revelries of those who bring meat together to eat;

**23:21** for those who devote themselves to drinking and contribute to shared feasts will be consumed, and drowsiness will be clothed in rags.

**23:22** Listen to your father who fathered you, and do not despise your mother when she has grown old.

**23:24** The father of a righteous man exults with joy; he who has fathered a wise one will be glad in him.

**23:25** Let your father and your mother rejoice, and let her who gave birth to you exult.

**23:26** Offer your heart to me, my son, and let your eyes guard my ways.

**23:27** For a prostitute is a deep pit, and a foreign woman is a narrow well.

**23:28** She lies in wait along the way like a robber, and she will kill those whom she sees unwary.

**23:29** Who has woe? Whose father has woe? Who has quarrels? Who has pits? Who has wounds without cause? Who has bloodshot eyes?

**23:30** Is it not those who linger over wine, and devote themselves to draining cups?

**23:31** Do not gaze at wine when it turns golden, when its color gleams in the glass; it goes down smoothly,

**23:32** but in the end it will bite like a serpent, and like a basilisk it will pour out venom.

**23:33** Your eyes will see foreign women, and your heart will speak perverse things.

**23:34** And you will be like one sleeping in the middle of the sea, and like a helmsman overcome with sleep, with the rudder lost.

**23:35** And you will say, "They beat me, but I felt no pain; they dragged me, and I did not sense it. When will I wake up and find wine again?"

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**23:23** *(after `23:22`)* Buy truth, and do not sell wisdom, teaching, and understanding.

## Translator Notes

- **23:2** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew idiom for a person of strong appetite ("a master of appetite") literally as "if indeed you have your soul in your power" (si tamen habes in potestate animam tuam).
- **23:7** — A distinctly Vulgate reading: Latin in similitudinem arioli et conjectoris aestimat quod ignorat ("in the likeness of a fortune-teller and an interpreter of omens, he calculates what he does not know") differs from the Hebrew ("as he reckons within himself, so is he").
- **23:10** — Vulgate parvulorum terminos ("boundaries of little ones") reflects reading the Hebrew word for "ancient" as "little children," creating a direct parallel with "orphans" in the second clause.
- **23:29** — Vulgate cujus patri vae ("whose father has woe") and cui foveae ("who has pits") are distinctive renderings of the Hebrew expressions for "sorrow" and "complaining."
