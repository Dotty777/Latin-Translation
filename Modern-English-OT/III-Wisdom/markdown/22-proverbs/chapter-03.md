## Text

**3:1** My son, do not forget my law, and let your heart guard my precepts;

**3:2** for they will add length of days, years of life, and peace to you.

**3:3** Do not let mercy and truth desert you; place them around your neck, and inscribe them on the tablets of your heart,

**3:4** and you will find grace and good discipline before God and people.

**3:5** Put your confidence in the Lord with your whole heart, and do not rely on your own discernment.

**3:6** In all your ways consider Him, and He Himself will direct your steps.

**3:7** Do not be wise in your own estimation; fear God, and depart from evil.

**3:8** For indeed it will be health to your navel, and a watering of your bones.

**3:9** Honor the Lord from your possessions, and from the firstfruits of all your crops give to Him,

**3:10** and your storehouses will be filled with plenty, and your winepresses will overflow with wine.

**3:11** My son, do not cast aside the discipline of the Lord, nor lose heart when you are corrected by Him;

**3:12** for the one whom the Lord loves He corrects, and like a father He takes pleasure in a son.

**3:13** Blessed is the person who finds wisdom, and who overflows with discernment.

**3:14** Her acquisition is better than the trade of silver, and her fruit than the finest and purest gold.

**3:15** She is more precious than all riches, and all things that are desired cannot be compared to her.

**3:16** Length of days is in her right hand, and wealth and glory are in her left hand.

**3:17** Her ways are lovely ways, and all her paths are peaceful.

**3:18** She is a tree of life for those who grasp her, and blessed is the one who holds her fast.

**3:19** The Lord founded the earth by wisdom; He established the heavens by discernment.

**3:20** By His wisdom the depths burst forth, and the clouds condense with dew.

**3:21** My son, do not let these things slip away from your eyes. Guard law and counsel,

**3:22** and it will be life for your soul, and grace for your throat.

**3:23** Then you will walk confidently on your way, and your foot will not trip.

**3:24** If you sleep, you will not be afraid; you will rest, and your sleep will be sweet.

**3:25** Do not be frightened by sudden terror, nor by the powers of the wicked rushing upon you.

**3:26** For the Lord will be at your side, and He will guard your foot so that you will not be caught.

**3:27** Do not prevent the one who is able from doing good; if you have the ability, do good yourself as well.

**3:28** Do not say to your friend, "Go and return; tomorrow I will give to you," when you can give immediately.

**3:29** Do not plot evil against your friend, while he has confidence in you.

**3:30** Do not contend against a person for no reason, when he himself has done no evil to you.

**3:31** Do not envy an unjust person, nor imitate his ways,

**3:32** because every mocker is an abomination to the Lord, and His conversation is with the straightforward.

**3:33** Poverty from the Lord is in the house of the wicked, but the dwellings of the righteous will be blessed.

**3:34** He Himself will mock the mockers, and He will give grace to the meek.

**3:35** The wise will possess glory; the exaltation of fools is dishonor.

## Translator Notes

- **3:4** — Vulgate 'disciplinam bonam' ("good discipline") contrasts with the Hebrew ("good understanding" or "good repute") and Septuagint ("take thought for noble things").
- **3:20** — Vulgate repeats 'Sapientia illius' ("By His wisdom") where the Hebrew reads "by His knowledge," and renders the second clause 'rore concrescunt' ("condense with dew") where the Hebrew has "drip down dew."
- **3:26** — Vulgate 'in latere tuo' ("at your side") reflects reading the Hebrew noun kesel as "side/flank" rather than "confidence."
- **3:27** — Vulgate 'Noli prohibere benefacere eum qui potest: si vales, et ipse benefac' ("Do not prevent the one who is able from doing good; if you have the ability, do good yourself as well") differs markedly from the Hebrew ("Do not withhold good from those to whom it is due when it is in your power to act").
- **3:33** — Vulgate 'Egestas a Domino' ("Poverty from the Lord") contrasts with the Hebrew "curse of the Lord," and the second clause uses a passive plural ("dwellings ... will be blessed").
