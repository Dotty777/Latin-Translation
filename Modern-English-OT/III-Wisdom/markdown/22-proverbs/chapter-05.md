## Text

**5:1** My son, pay attention to my wisdom, and incline your ear to my prudence,

**5:2** so that you may guard thoughts, and your lips may preserve discipline. Do not pay attention to the deceit of a woman;

**5:3** for the lips of a prostitute are a dripping honeycomb, and her throat is sleeker than oil,

**5:4** yet her end is bitter like wormwood, and sharp like a double-edged sword.

**5:5** Her feet descend into death, and her steps penetrate to the underworld.

**5:6** They do not walk along the path of life; her steps are wandering and untraceable.

**5:7** Now therefore, my son, listen to me, and do not turn away from the words of my mouth.

**5:8** Put your way far from her, and do not approach the doors of her house.

**5:9** Do not give your honor to strangers, and your years to a cruel one,

**5:10** or else outsiders may be filled with your strength, and your labors may be in another's house,

**5:11** and you may groan at the end, when you have consumed your flesh and your body, and say,

**5:12** "Why did I detest discipline, and did my heart not yield to reproofs,

**5:13** nor did I listen to the voice of those teaching me, and did I not incline my ear to teachers?

**5:14** I was nearly in all evil, in the midst of the assembly and the congregation."

**5:15** Drink water from your cistern, and the flowing waters of your well;

**5:16** let your springs be channeled abroad, and divide your waters in the streets.

**5:17** Possess them alone, and let strangers not be partners with you.

**5:18** May your wellspring be blessed, and be glad with the wife of your youth.

**5:19** A dearest doe and a most graceful fawn: may her breasts intoxicate you at all times; take delight in her love continually.

**5:20** Why are you led astray, my son, by a foreign woman, and cherished in the bosom of another?

**5:21** The Lord looks upon the ways of a man, and examines all his steps.

**5:22** His own iniquities capture the ungodly man, and he is bound tightly by the ropes of his sins.

**5:23** He himself will die, because he did not have discipline, and in the multitude of his folly he will be deceived.

## Translator Notes

- **5:2** — The second sentence ("Do not pay attention to the deceit of a woman") is a Vulgate reading shared with the Septuagint and absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text; likewise in 5:3 the Vulgate reads "prostitute" (meretricis) rather than the Hebrew "strange woman."
- **5:6** — The Vulgate uses a plural verb ("They do not walk"), continuing "her feet" and "her steps" from 5:5, and concludes "and untraceable" (investigabiles), where the Hebrew has singular "she does not ponder" and "she does not know it."
- **5:14** — Literally "in the midst of the church/assembly (ecclesiae) and the synagogue/congregation (synagogae)," preserving the Vulgate's Greek loanwords for the public gathering.
- **5:16** — Unlike many translations of the Hebrew that frame this verse as a rhetorical question, the Vulgate renders it positively as a jussive and imperative ("let your springs be channeled abroad, and divide your waters").
- **5:23** — The Vulgate reads "he will be deceived" (decipietur) rather than the Hebrew "he goes astray."
