## Text

**7:1** My son, keep my words, and store up my precepts for yourself. Son,

**7:2** guard my commandments, and you will live; and my law like the pupil of your eye.

**7:3** Bind it on your fingers; write it on the tablets of your heart.

**7:4** Say to wisdom, "You are my sister," and call prudence your friend,

**7:5** so that they may keep you from a strange woman, and from a foreign woman who makes her words sweet.

**7:6** For from the window of my house I looked out through the lattice,

**7:7** and I see little ones; I observe a senseless young man,

**7:8** who passes through the street by the corner and walks near the way to her house,

**7:9** in the dusk, as the day draws toward evening, in the darkness and gloom of night.

**7:10** And look, a woman meets him in the attire of a prostitute, prepared to capture souls; talkative and wandering,

**7:11** impatient of quiet, and unable to remain in the house with her feet;

**7:12** now outside, now in the streets, now lying in wait by the corners.

**7:13** And seizing the young man, she kisses him, and with a brazen face she flatters him, saying:

**7:14** "I vowed sacrifices for well-being; today I have paid my vows;

**7:15** therefore I have come out to meet you, longing to see you, and I have found you.

**7:16** I have woven my couch with cords; I have spread it with embroidered tapestries from Egypt.

**7:17** I have sprinkled my bed with myrrh, and aloe, and cinnamon.

**7:18** Come, let us be intoxicated with breasts, and let us enjoy desired embraces until the day dawns.

**7:19** For the man is not in his house; he has gone away on a very long journey;

**7:20** he took a bag of money with him; on the day of the full moon he is going to return to his house."

**7:21** She ensnared him with many words, and with the flatteries of her lips she drew him along.

**7:22** Immediately he follows her like an ox led to sacrifice, and like a frolicking lamb, and not knowing that he, a fool, is being dragged to bonds,

**7:23** until an arrow pierces his liver, just as if a bird hurries to a snare, and does not know that it is a matter of peril to its life.

**7:24** Now therefore, my son, listen to me, and pay attention to the words of my mouth.

**7:25** Do not let your mind be drawn away in her ways, and do not be deceived by her paths;

**7:26** for she has cast down many wounded, and all the strongest have been killed by her.

**7:27** Her house is the ways of the underworld, penetrating into the inner recesses of death.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The Clementine Vulgate repeats "Fili," ("Son,") at the end of verse 1, introducing the admonition in verse 2; this repeated vocative does not appear in the Hebrew text.
- **7:10** — Vulgate "præparata ad capiendas animas" ("prepared to capture souls") is a distinctive Latin reading where the Hebrew text has "guarded/cunning of heart."
- **7:16** — Vulgate "Intexui funibus lectulum meum" ("I have woven my couch with cords") refers to the woven rope lattice of a bed frame, whereas the Hebrew text speaks of spreading the bed with coverings.
- **7:18** — Vulgate "uberibus" ("breasts") reflects reading the unpointed Hebrew consonants as "breasts" rather than "lovemaking," paralleling the Vulgate wording of Proverbs 5:19.
- **7:22** — Vulgate "quasi agnus lasciviens" ("like a frolicking lamb") is a distinctive Latin rendering of a difficult Hebrew clause that mentions an anklet or fetter.
