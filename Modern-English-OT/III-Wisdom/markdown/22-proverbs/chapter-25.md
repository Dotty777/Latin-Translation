## Text

**25:1** These too are proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah, king of Judah, transcribed.

**25:2** The glory of God is to conceal a word, and the glory of kings is to investigate a saying.

**25:3** Heaven above, and earth below, and the heart of kings is inscrutable.

**25:4** Remove the rust from silver, and a completely pure vessel will emerge.

**25:5** Remove ungodliness from the face of the king, and his throne will be made firm by righteousness.

**25:6** Do not appear boastful before the king, and do not stand in the place of the great.

**25:7** For it is better that you be told, "Come up here," than that you be humbled before a prince.

**25:8** What your eyes have seen, do not quickly bring out in a dispute, or afterward you may not be able to correct it when you have dishonored your friend.

**25:9** Discuss your case with your friend, and do not reveal a secret to an outsider,

**25:10** or else he might insult you when he hears it and not stop reproaching you. Favor and friendship deliver; preserve them for yourself, so that you do not become an object of reproach.

**25:11** Golden apples on silver beds: such is one who speaks a word in its own time.

**25:12** A golden earring and a gleaming pearl: such is one who reproves a wise man and an obedient ear.

**25:13** Just as the coldness of snow in the day of harvest, so is a faithful envoy to the one who sent him; he causes his soul to rest.

**25:14** Clouds and wind, with rain that does not follow: such is a boastful man who does not fulfill his promises.

**25:15** Through patience a prince will be pacified, and a gentle tongue will shatter hardness.

**25:16** You have found honey: eat what is enough for you, or else, having had your fill, you might vomit it up.

**25:17** Draw back your foot from your neighbor's house, or else someday, having had his fill, he might hate you.

**25:18** A javelin, a sword, and a sharp arrow: such is a man who speaks false testimony against his neighbor.

**25:19** A rotten tooth and a weary foot: such is one who puts his hope in an unfaithful man in the day of distress,

**25:20** and loses his cloak in the day of cold. Vinegar on soda: such is one who sings songs to a wicked heart. Just as a moth does to a garment and a worm to wood, so a man's sorrow harms the heart.

**25:21** If your enemy is hungry, feed him; if he is thirsty, give him water to drink;

**25:22** for you will gather glowing coals on his head, and the Lord will repay you.

**25:23** The north wind dispels rain, and a gloomy face dispels a slandering tongue.

**25:24** It is better to sit in a corner of the rooftop than with a contentious woman and in a shared house.

**25:25** Cold water to a thirsty soul, and a good report from a distant land.

**25:26** A spring muddied by a foot and a polluted wellspring: such is a righteous man falling before an ungodly man.

**25:27** Just as it is not good for one who eats much honey, so one who is a scrutinizer of majesty will be crushed by glory.

**25:28** Just as a city lying open and without a ring of walls, so is a man who cannot restrain his spirit in speaking.

## Translator Notes

- **25:8** — The Vulgate attaches "What your eyes have seen" to v. 8 as the object of "bring out" (whereas the Hebrew attaches it to the end of v. 7) and reads "when you have dishonored your friend" instead of the Hebrew "when your neighbor puts you to shame."
- **25:10** — The second sentence ("Favor and friendship deliver; preserve them for yourself, so that you do not become an object of reproach") reflects the Septuagint via the Old Latin and is absent from the Masoretic Hebrew.
- **25:11** — The Vulgate reads in lectis argenteis ("on silver beds"), an interpretation of the Hebrew expression usually rendered "in settings of silver."
- **25:20** — The Vulgate links "and loses his cloak in the day of cold" to v. 19, translates the Hebrew for a troubled heart literally as cordi pessimo ("a wicked heart"), and includes the final sentence on the moth, worm, and sorrow from the Septuagint.
- **25:27** — Where the Hebrew text is obscure, the Vulgate reads qui scrutator est majestatis opprimetur a gloria ("one who is a scrutinizer of majesty will be crushed by glory").
