## Text

**16:2** All the ways of a man lie open to his eyes; the weigher of spirits is the Lord.

**16:5** An abomination to the Lord is every arrogant person; even if hand joins hand, he is not innocent. The beginning of a good way is doing righteousness, and it is more acceptable with God than offering sacrifices.

**16:9** The Lord has made everything for His own sake, even the wicked for the evil day.

**16:10** Divination is on the lips of a king; in judgment his mouth will not err.

**16:11** A weight and a balance are the Lord's judgments, and His works are all the stones of the pouch.

**16:12** Abominable to a king are those who act wickedly, because by righteousness a throne is established.

**16:13** Righteous lips are the pleasure of kings; the one who speaks what is right will be loved.

**16:14** A king's indignation is messengers of death, and a wise man will appease it.

**16:15** In the cheerfulness of a king's face there is life, and his clemency is like a late rain.

**16:16** Possess wisdom, because it is better than gold, and acquire prudence, because it is more precious than silver.

**16:17** The path of the righteous turns away from evils; the guardian of his own soul keeps his way.

**16:18** Pride precedes destruction, and before ruin the spirit is lifted up.

**16:19** It is better to be humbled with the gentle than to divide plunder with the proud.

**16:20** The one learned in the word will find good things, and the one who hopes in the Lord is blessed.

**16:21** The one who is wise in heart will be called prudent, and the one who is sweet in speech will receive greater things.

**16:22** Learning is a fountain of life to its possessor; the instruction of fools is senselessness.

**16:23** The heart of a wise man will teach his mouth, and to his lips it will add grace.

**16:24** Well-arranged words are a honeycomb, sweetness of the soul, health of the bones.

**16:25** There is a way that appears right to a man, and its final ends lead to death.

**16:26** The soul of the one who labors labors for itself, because his mouth has compelled him.

**16:27** A wicked man digs up evil, and on his lips a fire blazes.

**16:28** A perverse man arouses disputes, and a talkative man separates princes.

**16:29** An unrighteous man entices his friend, and leads him along a way that is not good.

**16:30** The one who with staring eyes plots crooked things, biting his own lips, accomplishes evil.

**16:31** A crown of dignity is old age, which will be found in the ways of righteousness.

**16:32** A patient person is better than a strong man, and the one who masters his own mind than a conqueror of cities.

**16:33** Lots are cast into the lap, but they are governed by the Lord.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**16:3** *(after `16:2`)* Reveal your works to the Lord, and your thoughts will be directed.

**16:6** *(after `16:5`)* Through mercy and truth iniquity is redeemed, and in the fear of the Lord one turns aside from evil.

## Translator Notes

- **16:5** — Literally 'even if hand is to hand,' an idiom expressing certainty or mutual pledging. The second sentence ('The beginning of a good way... offering sacrifices') is a Vulgate reading from the Septuagint tradition absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **16:9** — Vulgate reads 'propter semetipsum' ('for His own sake'), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text is commonly rendered 'for its own purpose.' This verse corresponds to 16:4 in the Hebrew text.
- **16:10** — Latin 'divinatio' literally means 'divination,' used here for an inspired or oracular pronouncement on a king's lips.
- **16:28** — Vulgate reads 'verbosus' ('a talkative man') and 'principes' ('princes'), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'a whisperer' and 'close friends.'
- **16:30** — Vulgate reads 'attonitis oculis' ('with staring/fixed eyes') and 'mordens labia sua' ('biting his own lips'), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'shuts his eyes' and 'compresses his lips.'
