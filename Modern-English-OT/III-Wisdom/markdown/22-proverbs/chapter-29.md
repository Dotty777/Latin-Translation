# Proverbs 29 — Saints Modern English (Latin / Clementine Vulgate)

## Text

**29:1** Upon a man who with a stiff neck despises one who corrects him, sudden destruction will come, and healing will not follow him.

**29:2** In the multiplying of the righteous, the common people will rejoice; when the wicked assume rule, the people will groan.

**29:3** A man who loves wisdom gladdens his father, but the one who supports prostitutes will lose his wealth.

**29:4** A righteous king raises up the land; a greedy man will destroy it.

**29:5** A man who speaks to his friend with smooth and deceptive words spreads a net for his steps.

**29:6** A snare will entangle an unrighteous man who sins, and the righteous one will praise and be glad.

**29:7** The righteous one knows the cause of the poor; the wicked one is ignorant of knowledge.

**29:8** Pestilent men ruin a city, but the wise turn away fury.

**29:9** If a wise man disputes with a fool, whether he grows angry or laughs, he will not find rest.

**29:10** Men of bloodshed hate the guileless one, but the righteous seek his soul.

**29:11** A fool lets out his whole spirit; a wise man holds back and reserves it for later.

**29:12** A ruler who gladly listens to words of falsehood has officials who are all wicked.

**29:13** A poor man and a creditor have met each other: the Lord is the giver of light to both of them.

**29:14** As for a king who judges the poor in truth, his throne will be made firm forever.

**29:15** The rod and correction impart wisdom, but a boy who is left to his own will disgraces his mother.

**29:16** In the multiplying of the wicked, crimes will be multiplied, and the righteous will see their ruin.

**29:17** Train your son, and he will refresh you, and he will give delights to your soul.

**29:18** When prophecy fails, the people will be scattered, but the one who keeps the law is blessed.

**29:19** A servant cannot be trained by words, because he understands what you say and disdains to answer.

**29:20** Have you seen a man quick to speak? Foolishness is more to be expected than his correction.

**29:21** Whoever raises his servant indulgently from boyhood will afterward find him defiant.

**29:22** A hot-tempered man provokes quarrels, and the one who is quick to become indignant will be more inclined to sin.

**29:23** Humiliation follows a proud man, and glory will receive the one who is humble in spirit.

**29:24** Whoever partners with a thief hates his own soul; he hears one administering an oath and does not disclose it.

**29:25** The one who fears man will quickly fall; the one who hopes in the Lord will be lifted up.

**29:26** Many seek the face of a ruler, and the judgment of each individual comes forth from the Lord.

**29:27** The righteous detest a wicked man, and the wicked detest those who are on the right way. A son who keeps the word will be beyond destruction.

## Translator Notes

- **29:10** — The Vulgate renders the second clause literally as "seek his soul" (quærunt animam ejus), meaning here that the righteous care for and seek to preserve the life of the guileless person.
- **29:13** — The Vulgate has "creditor" (creditor) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "oppressor," and "giver of light" (illuminator) without mentioning "eyes."
- **29:20** — The Vulgate reads "foolishness is more to be expected than his correction" (stultitia magis speranda est quam illius correptio), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which states that there is more hope for a fool than for him.
- **29:25** — The Vulgate reads "will quickly fall" (cito corruet) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "lays a snare."
- **29:27** — The final sentence ("A son who keeps the word will be beyond destruction," Verbum custodiens filius extra perditionem erit) is a distinctive Vulgate and Septuagint addition absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
