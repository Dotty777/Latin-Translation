## Text

**4:1** Aleph: How the gold has been darkened, the finest color changed! The stones of the sanctuary have been scattered at the head of all the streets!

**4:2** Beth: The sons of Zion, renowned and clothed in choicest gold, how they have been counted as earthenware vessels, the work of a potter's hands!

**4:3** Gimel: Yet even the lamias have bared the breast; they have nursed their young. The daughter of my people is cruel, like an ostrich in the desert.

**4:4** Daleth: The tongue of the nursing infant has clung to its palate in thirst; the small children asked for bread, and there was no one to break it for them.

**4:5** He: Those who dined luxuriously have perished in the roads; those who were raised in saffron garments have embraced dung.

**4:6** Vav: And the iniquity of the daughter of my people has become greater than the sin of Sodom, which was overthrown in a moment, and no hands laid hold in her.

**4:7** Zayin: Her Nazirites were whiter than snow, brighter than milk, ruddier than ancient ivory, more beautiful than sapphire.

**4:8** Heth: Their face has grown blacker than coals, and they are not recognized in the streets; their skin has clung to their bones; it has dried up and become like wood.

**4:9** Teth: It was better for those struck down by the sword than for those killed by famine, for these wasted away, consumed by the barrenness of the land.

**4:10** Yod: The hands of merciful women cooked their own children; they became their food in the crushing of the daughter of my people.

**4:11** Kaph: The Lord has completed His fury; He has poured out the wrath of His indignation, and He has kindled a fire in Zion, and it has devoured its foundations.

**4:12** Lamed: The kings of the earth and all the inhabitants of the world did not believe that a foe and an enemy would enter through the gates of Jerusalem.

**4:13** Mem: Because of the sins of her prophets and the iniquities of her priests, who poured out the blood of the righteous in her midst.

**4:14** Nun: They wandered blind in the streets; they were defiled with blood, and when they could not, they held their hems.

**4:15** Samekh: "Stand back, defiled ones!" they shouted to them. "Stand back, go away, do not touch!" For indeed they quarreled, and shaken, they said among the nations, "He will no longer dwell among them."

**4:16** Pe: The face of the Lord has divided them; He will no longer look upon them. They had no reverence for the faces of the priests, nor did they have mercy on the elders.

**4:17** Ayin: While we still held out, our eyes failed for our futile help, as we looked intently toward a nation that could not save.

**4:18** Tsade: Our footsteps slipped along the path of our streets; our end has drawn near; our days are completed, for our end has come.

**4:19** Qoph: Our pursuers were swifter than the eagles of the sky; over the mountains they pursued us; in the desert they lay in wait for us.

**4:20** Resh: The breath of our mouth, the anointed Lord, was captured in our sins, to whom we said, "In Your shadow we will live among the nations."

**4:21** Shin: Rejoice and be glad, daughter of Edom, you who dwell in the land of Uz! The cup will reach you as well; you will become drunk and be laid bare.

**4:22** Tav: Your iniquity is completed, daughter of Zion; He will no longer send you into exile. He has visited your iniquity, daughter of Edom; He has uncovered your sins.

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — Latin lamiae refers to legendary child-devouring monsters (or sea monsters), where the Hebrew refers to jackals; the Clementine Vulgate also preserves the Hebrew acrostic letter names at the head of each verse in chapter 4, with Pe (4:16) preceding Ayin (4:17).
- **4:7** — Vulgate "Nazirites" (Nazaraei) and "ancient ivory" (ebore antiquo) preserve distinctive renderings where the Hebrew has terms meaning "consecrated ones" (or "princes") and "corals" (or "rubies").
- **4:14** — Vulgate "when they could not, they held their hems" (cumque non possent, tenuerunt lacinias suas) is a distinctive Latin rendering of a difficult Hebrew clause usually understood as "so that no one could touch their garments."
- **4:15** — Vulgate "they quarreled" (jurgati sunt) and the singular "He will no longer dwell among them" (Non addet ultra ut habitet in eis, referring to the Lord) differ from the Hebrew, which uses plural verbs referring to the exiles ("they fled" and "they shall stay no longer").
- **4:20** — Latin christus Dominus ("the anointed Lord" or "the anointed lord") sets the two nouns in apposition, as the Septuagint does (christos kyrios), where the Hebrew reads "the anointed of the Lord"; the Vulgate also reads oris nostri ("of our mouth") and in peccatis nostris ("in our sins") where the Hebrew has "of our nostrils" and "in their pits." Editorial policy review: capitalization of "Lord" and "Your" reflects a Christological reading alongside the historical royal referent (the captured Judean king, lowercase "anointed lord" / "your").
