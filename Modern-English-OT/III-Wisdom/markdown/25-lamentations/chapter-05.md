## Text

**5:1** Remember, Lord, what has happened to us; look closely and regard our reproach.

**5:2** Our inheritance has been turned over to outsiders, our houses to foreigners.

**5:3** We have been made orphans, without a father; our mothers are like widows.

**5:4** We have drunk our water for money; we have purchased our wood at a price.

**5:5** We were driven by our necks; no rest was given to the weary.

**5:6** We gave our hand to Egypt and to the Assyrians, so that we might be filled with bread.

**5:7** Our fathers sinned and are no more, and we have carried their iniquities.

**5:8** Slaves have ruled over us; there was no one to redeem us from their hand.

**5:9** At the peril of our lives we brought bread for ourselves, because of the sword in the desert.

**5:10** Our skin has been scorched like an oven, because of the storms of famine.

**5:11** They humiliated the women in Zion, and the virgins in the cities of Judah.

**5:12** Princes were hanged by the hand; they had no reverence for the faces of the elders.

**5:13** They shamelessly abused the young men, and the boys collapsed on the wood.

**5:14** The elders are gone from the gates, the young men from the choir of musicians.

**5:15** The joy of our heart has failed; our dance has been turned into mourning.

**5:16** The crown of our head has fallen; woe to us, because we have sinned!

**5:17** Because of this our heart has grown sorrowful; for this reason our eyes have been darkened,

**5:18** on account of Mount Zion, because it has perished; foxes have walked on it.

**5:19** But You, Lord, will remain forever, Your throne from generation to generation.

**5:20** Why will You forget us forever, abandon us for the length of days?

**5:21** Turn us back, Lord, to Yourself, and we will be turned back; renew our days as from the beginning.

**5:22** But casting us away, You have rejected us; You have been fiercely angry against us.

## Translator Notes

- **5:5** — Latin minabamur is the passive of Late Latin minare ("to drive," as of livestock or captives), conveying being driven harshly by the neck, where the Hebrew reads "our pursuers are at our necks."
- **5:10** — Vulgate "storms of famine" (tempestatum famis) renders a rare Hebrew phrase usually understood as "burning heat" or "raging fever of famine."
- **5:13** — Vulgate "They shamelessly abused the young men" (Adolescentibus impudice abusi sunt) follows an ancient euphemistic interpretation of Hebrew "young men bore the millstone," and "on the wood" (in ligno) literally reflects Hebrew "under [loads of] wood."
- **5:22** — Vulgate renders the closing verse as a stark declarative contrast ("But casting us away, You have rejected us," Sed projiciens repulisti nos), whereas the Hebrew conjunction is often taken conditionally as "unless You have utterly rejected us."
