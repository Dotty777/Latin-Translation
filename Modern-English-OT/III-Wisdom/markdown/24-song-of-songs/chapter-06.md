## Text

**6:1** My beloved has gone down into his garden, to the bed of spices, to feed in the gardens and gather lilies.

**6:2** I belong to my beloved, and my beloved belongs to me, he who feeds among the lilies.

**6:3** You are beautiful, my friend, sweet and lovely as Jerusalem, terrifying as an army's battle line drawn up in order.

**6:4** Turn your eyes away from me, for they have made me fly away. Your hair is like a flock of goats that have appeared from Gilead.

**6:5** Your teeth are like a flock of sheep that have come up from the washing, all with twin offspring, and there is no barren one among them.

**6:6** Like the rind of a pomegranate, so are your cheeks, apart from what is hidden in you.

**6:7** There are sixty queens and eighty concubines, and of young women there is no number.

**6:8** My dove, my perfect one, is one alone; she is the only one of her mother, the chosen one of her who bore her. The daughters saw her and proclaimed her most blessed; the queens and the concubines, and they praised her.

**6:9** Who is this who comes forth like the rising dawn, beautiful as the moon, choice as the sun, terrifying as an army's battle line drawn up in order?

**6:10** I went down into the nut garden to see the fruits of the valleys, and to look whether the vine had blossomed and the pomegranates had budded.

**6:11** I did not know: my soul threw me into turmoil because of the chariots of Amminadab.

**6:12** Return, return, Shulammite! Return, return, so that we may gaze at you.

## Translator Notes

- **6:3** — Latin suavis ("sweet") translates the Hebrew place name Tirzah etymologically according to its sense of "pleasant" or "delightful," following the Septuagint tradition.
- **6:4** — Latin quia ipsi me avolare fecerunt ("for they have made me fly away") follows the Septuagint's vivid rendering where the Masoretic Hebrew reads "for they overwhelm me."
- **6:9** — Latin electa ut sol ("choice as the sun") follows the Septuagint (eklekte) in taking the underlying Hebrew adjective in the sense of "select" rather than "bright."
- **6:11** — Latin conturbavit me ("threw me into turmoil") and the proper name Aminadab (standard modern English "Amminadab") preserve a distinctive Vulgate reading where the obscure Hebrew may be read "set me among the chariots of my noble people." In native Vulgate numbering this verse is 6:11 (Saints 6:12); native 6:12 ('Revertere, revertere, Sulamitis!...') ends chapter 6, and Hebrew 6:13b-7:1 corresponds to native Vulgate 7:1.
- **6:12** — Native Vulgate 6:12 ends chapter 6 ('Revertere, revertere, Sulamitis!...'; parallel to Saints 7:1a / Hebrew 6:13a), while restored native 7:1 opens chapter 7 with 'Quid videbis in Sulamite...'.
