## Text

**1:1** May he kiss me with a kiss of his mouth, for your breasts are better than wine,

**1:2** fragrant with the finest ointments. Oil poured out is your name; therefore young women have loved you.

**1:3** Draw me; we will run after you toward the fragrance of your ointments. The king has brought me into his storerooms; we will exult and be glad in you, remembering your breasts more than wine. The upright love you.

**1:4** I am black, yet lovely, daughters of Jerusalem, like the tents of Kedar, like the skins of Solomon.

**1:5** Do not stare at me because I am dark, for the sun has discolored me. My mother's sons fought against me; they placed me as keeper in the vineyards; my own vineyard I did not keep.

**1:6** Tell me, you whom my soul loves, where you pasture, where you lie down at midday, so that I may not begin to wander after the flocks of your companions.

**1:7** If you do not know yourself, most beautiful among women, go out, and go after the tracks of the flocks, and pasture your young goats beside the tents of the shepherds.

**1:8** To my cavalry in the chariots of Pharaoh I have compared you, my friend.

**1:9** Your cheeks are beautiful like those of a turtledove; your neck is like necklaces.

**1:10** We will make little gold chains for you, inlaid with silver.

**1:11** While the king was at his couch, my spikenard gave off its fragrance.

**1:12** A bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me; between my breasts he will stay.

**1:13** A cluster of henna is my beloved to me in the vineyards of En-gedi.

**1:14** Look, you are beautiful, my friend! Look, you are beautiful! Your eyes are those of doves.

**1:15** Look, you are beautiful, my beloved, and charming! Our bed is in bloom.

**1:16** The beams of our houses are of cedar; our paneled ceilings are of cypress.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — The Vulgate reads ubera tua ("your breasts," here in native 1:1 and in native 1:3; Saints 1:2 and 1:4), following the Septuagint's reading of the unpointed Hebrew consonants rather than the Masoretic Text's "your love/caresses." Also, osculo ("a kiss") is singular in the Latin. Native Vulgate Song of Songs omits the title verse from numbering in ch. 1 (1:1-16 = Saints 1:2-17), places Saints 6:1 at 5:17, shifts 6:1-12 to Saints 6:2-7:1a, restores 7:1 (Saints 7:1b-2), and shifts 7:2-13 to Saints 7:3-14.
- **1:3** — The phrase in odorem unguentorum tuorum ("toward the fragrance of your ointments") follows the Septuagint and is absent from the Masoretic Text. In the closing sentence, the Vulgate renders Hebrew meysharim ("rightly/uprightly") as a plural noun, recti ("the upright").
- **1:4** — Latin pelles ("skins/hides," echoing the Septuagint) refers to leather tent-hangings, whereas the Hebrew has "curtains."
- **1:7** — The Vulgate follows the Septuagint with Si ignoras te ("If you do not know yourself"), whereas the Hebrew has an ethical dative meaning simply "If you do not know."
- **1:9** — Latin sicut turturis ("like those of a turtledove") reflects the Septuagint's reading of the Hebrew word tor as "turtledove" rather than "strings of ornaments."
