## Text

**3:1** On my bed, through the nights, I sought the one whom my soul loves; I sought him, and did not find him.

**3:2** "I will rise and go around the city; through the streets and squares I will seek the one whom my soul loves." I sought him, and did not find him.

**3:3** The watchmen who guard the city found me: "Have you seen the one whom my soul loves?"

**3:4** When I had passed just a little beyond them, I found the one whom my soul loves; I held him, and I will not let him go until I bring him into my mother's house, and into the bedroom of the one who bore me.

**3:5** I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem, by the roe deer and stags of the fields, neither to rouse nor to awaken the beloved until she herself wishes.

**3:6** Who is this who comes up through the wilderness like a slender column of smoke from the spices of myrrh and frankincense, and of all the perfumer's powder?

**3:7** Look, sixty mighty men from the mightiest of Israel surround Solomon's bed,

**3:8** all holding swords, and most skilled for battles, each one's sword upon his thigh because of the terrors of the night.

**3:9** King Solomon made a palanquin for himself from the timbers of Lebanon;

**3:10** he made its pillars of silver, its backrest of gold, its ascent of purple; its interior he lined with love, for the sake of the daughters of Jerusalem.

**3:11** Go out and see, daughters of Zion, King Solomon in the diadem with which his mother crowned him on the day of his betrothal, and on the day of the gladness of his heart.

## Translator Notes

- **3:6** — The Vulgate reads per desertum ("through the wilderness") and singular diminutive virgula fumi ("a slender column of smoke"), whereas the Hebrew has "from the wilderness" and plural "columns of smoke."
- **3:7** — Latin lectulum ("bed") repeats the same noun used in 1:16 and 3:1, whereas the Hebrew term here denotes a portable couch or litter.
- **3:10** — Latin ascensum ("ascent," following the Septuagint) corresponds to "seat" in the Hebrew, and propter filias Jerusalem ("for the sake of the daughters of Jerusalem") differs from the Hebrew "by the daughters of Jerusalem."
