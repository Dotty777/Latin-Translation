## Text

**4:1** How beautiful you are, my friend! How beautiful you are! Your eyes are those of doves, apart from what lies hidden within. Your hair is like flocks of goats that have come up from Mount Gilead.

**4:2** Your teeth are like flocks of shorn ewes that have come up from the washing, all with twin offspring, and there is no barren one among them.

**4:3** Your lips are like a scarlet ribbon, and your speech is sweet. Like a piece of a pomegranate, so are your cheeks, apart from what lies hidden within.

**4:4** Your neck is like the tower of David, which was built with battlements; a thousand shields hang from it, all the armor of the mighty.

**4:5** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a roe deer, that feed among the lilies.

**4:6** Until the day breathes and the shadows decline, I will go to the mountain of myrrh and to the hill of frankincense.

**4:7** You are wholly beautiful, my friend, and there is no blemish in you.

**4:8** Come from Lebanon, my bride; come from Lebanon, come, you will be crowned: from the summit of Amana, from the peak of Senir and Hermon, from the lairs of lions, from the mountains of leopards.

**4:9** You have wounded my heart, my sister, my bride; you have wounded my heart with one of your eyes and with one lock of hair on your neck.

**4:10** How beautiful are your breasts, my sister, my bride! More beautiful than wine are your breasts, and the fragrance of your ointments is above all spices.

**4:11** Your lips are a dripping honeycomb, my bride; honey and milk are under your tongue, and the fragrance of your garments is like the fragrance of frankincense.

**4:12** An enclosed garden is my sister, my bride; an enclosed garden, a sealed fountain.

**4:13** Your shoots are an orchard of pomegranates with the produce of fruit trees, henna with nard.

**4:14** Nard and saffron, calamus and cinnamon, with all the trees of Lebanon; myrrh and aloe, with all the finest ointments.

**4:15** A fountain of gardens, a well of living waters that flow with force from Lebanon.

**4:16** Rise up, north wind, and come, south wind; blow through my garden, and let its spices flow.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — Latin absque eo quod intrinsecus latet ("apart from what lies hidden within," repeated in 4:3; cf. absque occultis tuis in 6:7) is a distinctive Vulgate reading where the Masoretic Hebrew reads "behind your veil."
- **4:8** — Latin coronaberis ("you will be crowned") is a distinctive Vulgate reading where the Hebrew verb is commonly rendered "depart" or "look down."
- **4:9** — Latin vulnerasti ("you have wounded") and in uno crine colli tui ("with one lock of hair on your neck") preserve distinctive Vulgate readings where the Hebrew refers to captivating the heart with a single jewel of a necklace.
- **4:10** — Latin mammae and ubera ("breasts") and pulchriora ("more beautiful") follow the Septuagint tradition, whereas the Masoretic Hebrew reads "love" (or "caresses") and "better."
- **4:11** — Latin thuris ("of frankincense") differs from the Masoretic Hebrew "of Lebanon"; conversely, in 4:14 the Vulgate reads lignis Libani ("trees of Lebanon") where the Hebrew reads "trees of frankincense."
