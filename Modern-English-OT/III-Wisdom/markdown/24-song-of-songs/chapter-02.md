## Text

**2:1** I am a flower of the field, and a lily of the valleys.

**2:2** Like a lily among thorns, so is my friend among the daughters.

**2:3** Like an apple tree among the trees of the forests, so is my beloved among the sons. Beneath the shade of the one whom I had desired I sat down, and his fruit was sweet to my palate.

**2:4** He brought me into the wine cellar; he set love in order within me.

**2:5** Support me with flowers, surround me with apples, for I am faint with love.

**2:6** His left hand is beneath my head, and his right hand will embrace me.

**2:7** I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem, by the gazelles and deer of the fields, neither to rouse nor to awaken the beloved until she herself wishes.

**2:8** The voice of my beloved! Look, here he comes, leaping upon the mountains, bounding across the hills.

**2:9** My beloved is like a gazelle and a fawn of the deer. See, he himself stands behind our wall, gazing through the windows, peering through the lattices.

**2:10** Look, my beloved speaks to me: "Rise up, hurry, my friend, my dove, my lovely one, and come;

**2:11** for now winter has passed; the rain has departed and withdrawn.

**2:12** Flowers have appeared in our land; the time of pruning has arrived; the voice of the turtledove has been heard in our land;

**2:13** the fig tree has put forth its unripe figs; the blossoming vineyards have given off their fragrance. Rise up, my friend, my exquisite one, and come,

**2:14** my dove, in the crevices of the rock, in the hollow of the wall, show me your face, let your voice sound in my ears; for your voice is sweet, and your face is charming."

**2:15** Catch for us the little foxes that destroy the vineyards, for our vineyard has blossomed.

**2:16** My beloved belongs to me, and I to him, who grazes among the lilies,

**2:17** until the day breathes and the shadows decline. Return; my beloved, be like a gazelle and a fawn of the deer upon the mountains of Bether.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — Latin flos campi ("a flower of the field") follows the Septuagint in translating Hebrew sharon as a common noun ("plain/field") rather than the proper place name "Sharon."
- **2:4** — The Vulgate's ordinavit in me caritatem ("he set love in order within me") follows the Septuagint's verbal reading of the Hebrew term that the Masoretic Text reads as a noun ("his banner over me was love").
- **2:5** — Latin floribus ("with flowers") is a Vulgate rendering where the Hebrew refers to "raisin cakes."
- **2:7** — Here (and in 3:5) the Vulgate renders the feminine noun concretely as dilectam ("the beloved"), referring to the bride rather than to abstract "love" as in the Hebrew.
- **2:12** — Latin tempus putationis ("the time of pruning") follows the Septuagint in taking the ambiguous Hebrew term zamir to mean vine-pruning rather than "singing."
