## Text

**5:1** Let my beloved come into his garden and eat the fruit of his fruit trees. I have come into my garden, my sister, my bride; I have harvested my myrrh with my spices; I have eaten the honeycomb with my honey; I have drunk my wine with my milk. Eat, friends, and drink, and become drunk, dearest ones.

**5:2** I sleep, and my heart keeps watch. The voice of my beloved knocking: "Open to me, my sister, my friend, my dove, my unblemished one, for my head is full of dew, and my curls with the drops of the nights."

**5:3** I have taken off my tunic; how will I put it on? I have washed my feet; how will I soil them?

**5:4** My beloved put his hand through the opening, and my belly trembled at his touch.

**5:5** I rose to open to my beloved; my hands dripped myrrh, and my fingers were full of the purest myrrh.

**5:6** I opened the bolt of my door to my beloved, but he had turned aside and passed on. My soul melted when he spoke; I searched for him and did not find him; I called, and he did not answer me.

**5:7** The watchmen who go around the city found me; they struck me and wounded me. The watchmen of the walls took my cloak from me.

**5:8** I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem: if you find my beloved, tell him that I am faint with love.

**5:9** What is your beloved like compared to another beloved, most beautiful of women? What is your beloved like compared to another beloved, that you have adjured us like this?

**5:10** My beloved is white and ruddy, chosen from among thousands.

**5:11** His head is the finest gold; his locks are like shoots of palm trees, black as a raven.

**5:12** His eyes are like doves by brooks of water, which have been washed with milk and sit beside the fullest streams.

**5:13** His cheeks are like beds of spices planted by perfumers. His lips are lilies, dripping the finest myrrh.

**5:14** His hands are rounded, golden, full of jacinths. His belly is ivory, studded with sapphires.

**5:15** His legs are marble pillars that are founded upon golden bases. His appearance is like that of Lebanon, choice as the cedars.

**5:16** His throat is most sweet, and he is wholly desirable. Such is my beloved, and he is my friend, daughters of Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — In the Clementine Vulgate, the opening sentence ("Let my beloved come...") begins chapter 5 rather than closing 4:16 as in the Hebrew text. At the end of the verse, carissimi ("dearest ones") reads the final word as a vocative rather than "with love."
- **5:4** — Latin ad tactum ejus ("at his touch") is a distinctive Vulgate reading where the Hebrew reads simply "for him."
- **5:10** — Latin electus ex millibus ("chosen from among thousands") has "thousands" rather than the Masoretic Hebrew "ten thousand."
- **5:11** — Latin elatae palmarum ("shoots of palm trees," from Greek elatai) refers to the budding spathes or shoots of date palms, where the Hebrew describes wavy locks or palm fronds.
- **5:13** — Latin consitae a pigmentariis ("planted by perfumers") is a distinctive Vulgate reading where the Hebrew reads "yielding fragrance" or "banks of spices."
