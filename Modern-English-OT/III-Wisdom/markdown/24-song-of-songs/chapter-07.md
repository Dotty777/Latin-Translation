## Text

**7:1** Return, return, Shulammite! Return, return, so that we may gaze at you.

**7:3** Your navel is a rounded bowl, never lacking drinks. Your belly is like a mound of wheat hedged around with lilies.

**7:4** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a roe deer.

**7:5** Your neck is like an ivory tower; your eyes are like the pools in Heshbon that are at the gate of the daughter of the multitude. Your nose is like the tower of Lebanon, which looks toward Damascus.

**7:6** Your head is like Carmel, and the locks of your head are like a king's purple bound in channels.

**7:7** How beautiful you are, and how lovely, dearest, in delights!

**7:8** Your stature is likened to a palm tree, and your breasts to clusters.

**7:9** I said, "I will climb up into the palm tree and take hold of its fruits; and your breasts will be like clusters of the vine, and the fragrance of your mouth like that of apples."

**7:10** Your throat is like the finest wine, worthy for my beloved to drink, and for his lips and teeth to chew over.

**7:11** I belong to my beloved, and toward me is his turning.

**7:12** Come, my beloved, let us go out into the field; let us stay in the villages.

**7:13** Early in the morning let us rise to go to the vineyards; let us see whether the vine has blossomed, whether the blossoms are bringing forth fruits, whether the pomegranates have blossomed; there I will give you my breasts.

**7:14** The mandrakes have given forth fragrance; at our gates are all fruits: new and old, my beloved, I have saved for you.

## Translator Notes

- **7:5** — Latin "filiae multitudinis" ("daughter of the multitude") translates the Hebrew place name Bath-rabbim literally.
- **7:6** — Latin "purpura regis vincta canalibus" ("a king's purple bound in channels") is a distinct Vulgate reading depicting royal purple cloth or yarn fastened in dyeing troughs, where modern Hebrew translations generally read of a king held captive in tresses.
- **7:10** — Latin "labiisque et dentibus illius ad ruminandum" ("for his lips and teeth to chew over") is a distinct Vulgate reading; the Hebrew reads of wine gliding over the lips of sleepers.
- **7:11** — Latin "conversio" ("turning") follows the Greek Septuagint ("epistrophe") rather than the Hebrew word for "desire."
- **7:13** — Latin "ubera mea" ("my breasts") follows the Septuagint rather than the Hebrew Masoretic reading "my love."
