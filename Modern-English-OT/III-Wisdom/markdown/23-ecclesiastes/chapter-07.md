## Text

**7:1** Why is it necessary for a person to seek things greater than himself, since he does not know what is beneficial for him in his life, for the number of the days of his sojourn and the time that passes by like a shadow? Or who will be able to show him what will be after him under the sun?

**7:2** Better is a good name than costly ointments, and the day of death than the day of birth.

**7:3** It is better to go to a house of mourning than to a house of banqueting, for in that house the end of all people is brought to mind, and the one who is living considers what will be.

**7:4** Anger is better than laughter, because through sadness of face the mind of the wrongdoer is corrected.

**7:5** The heart of the wise is where sadness is, and the heart of fools is where gladness is.

**7:6** It is better to be reprimanded by a wise person than to be misled by the flattery of fools,

**7:7** for just as the sound of thorns burning under a pot, so is the laughter of a fool. Yet this also is futility.

**7:8** False accusation troubles a wise man and will destroy the strength of his heart.

**7:9** Better is the end of a speech than the beginning. Better is a patient person than an arrogant one.

**7:10** Do not be swift to become angry, because anger rests in a fool's bosom.

**7:11** Do not say, "What do you think is the reason that earlier times were better than they are now?" For a question of this sort is foolish.

**7:12** Wisdom is more useful with riches, and it is more beneficial to those who see the sun.

**7:13** For just as wisdom protects, so money protects; yet learning and wisdom have this much more, that they grant life to their possessor.

**7:14** Consider the works of God, that no one can correct the one whom He has despised.

**7:15** On a good day enjoy good things, and guard against an evil day; for just as God made this one, so also He made that one, so that man may not find just complaints against Him.

**7:16** I have also seen these things in the days of my futility: a righteous man perishes in his righteousness, and a wicked man lives for a long time in his malice.

**7:17** Do not be righteous to excess, and do not be wiser than is necessary, so that you do not become stupefied.

**7:18** Do not act wickedly to excess, and do not be foolish, so that you do not die at a time that is not your own.

**7:19** It is good for you to support the righteous man, but also do not withdraw your hand from him, because the one who fears God neglects nothing.

**7:20** Wisdom has strengthened a wise man more than ten rulers of a city,

**7:21** for there is not a righteous man on the earth who does good and does not sin.

**7:22** But also, do not direct your heart to all the words that are spoken, so that you do not happen to hear your servant cursing you,

**7:23** for your conscience knows that you also have frequently cursed others.

**7:24** I have tested all things in wisdom. I said, "I will become wise," yet it withdrew farther from me,

**7:25** much more than it was. And a deep depth, who will find it?

**7:26** I surveyed all things in my mind, so that I might know and consider, and seek wisdom and reason, and so that I might recognize the wickedness of a fool and the error of the imprudent;

**7:27** and I found more bitter than death the woman who is a snare of hunters, and her heart is a dragnet; her hands are chains. The one who pleases God will escape her, but the one who is a sinner will be captured by her.

**7:28** "Look, this I have found," said Ecclesiastes, "one thing and another, so that I might find the reason,

**7:29** which my soul still seeks, and I have not found it. One man out of a thousand I have discovered; a woman among all of them I have not found.

**7:30** Only this have I found, that God made man upright, and he himself has become entangled in endless questions." Who is like the wise man? And who has known the explanation of a word?

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The Vulgate opens with Quid necesse est homini majora se quaerere ("Why is it necessary for a person to seek things greater than himself"), expanding on the brief Hebrew question, and renders the days of vanity as peregrinationis suae ("of his sojourn"). Native Vulgate opens chapter 7 with 'Quid necesse est homini...' (7:1 = Saints/Hebrew 6:12), shifting native Vulgate 7:2-30 one verse ahead of Saints/Hebrew 7:1-29 (with native 7:30 also including the two opening questions of Hebrew 8:1). The Vulgate opens with Quid necesse est homini majora se quærere ("Why is it necessary for a person to seek things greater than himself"), expanding on the brief Hebrew question, and renders the days of vanity as peregrinationis suæ ("of his sojourn").
- **7:4** — Vulgate reads ira ("anger") and animus delinquentis ("the mind of the offender"), whereas the Hebrew refers to sorrow or vexation and simply "the heart."
- **7:14** — Vulgate reads quem ille despexerit ("the one whom He has despised"), giving a personal object where the Hebrew has "what He has made crooked."
- **7:15** — Vulgate concludes ut non inveniat homo contra eum justas querimonias ("so that man may not find just complaints against Him"), diverging from the Hebrew ("so that man will not find out anything that will be after him").
- **7:19** — Vulgate reads te sustentare justum ("for you to support the righteous man") and nihil negligit ("neglects nothing"), giving a distinctly moral sense compared to the Hebrew ("grasp one thing... comes forth with both of them").
- **7:30** — Vulgate reads infinitis miscuerit quaestionibus ("has become entangled in endless questions") rather than "many schemes," and places the two final questions at the close of chapter 7 rather than at the start of chapter 8 as in the Hebrew text.
