## Text

**9:1** I examined all these things in my heart so that I might carefully understand: the righteous and the wise, and their works, are in the hand of God, and yet a man does not know whether he is worthy of love or of hatred.

**9:2** But all things are kept uncertain for the future, because all things happen alike to the righteous and to the wicked, to the good and to the evil, to the clean and to the unclean, to the one who sacrifices victims and to the one who despises sacrifices. As the good man is, so also is the sinner; as the perjurer is, so also is the one who swears the truth.

**9:3** This is the worst among all things that are done under the sun, that the same things happen to all. Because of this, the hearts of the sons of men are also filled with malice and contempt in their life, and after these things they will be led down to the underworld.

**9:4** There is no one who lives forever and who has confidence in this matter; better is a living dog than a dead lion.

**9:5** For the living know that they are going to die, but the dead know nothing further, nor do they have a reward any longer, because their memory has been handed over to oblivion.

**9:6** Love also, and hatred, and jealousies have perished together, nor do they have a portion in this age and in the work that is carried on under the sun.

**9:7** Go therefore, and eat your bread in gladness, and drink your wine with joy, because your works please God.

**9:8** At every time let your garments be white, and let oil not be lacking from your head.

**9:9** Fully enjoy life with the wife whom you love, all the days of the life of your instability that have been given to you under the sun, through all the time of your futility; for this is the portion in life and in your labor with which you labor under the sun.

**9:10** Whatever your hand can do, perform it earnestly, because neither work, nor reason, nor wisdom, nor knowledge will be in the underworld, where you are hurrying.

**9:11** I turned to another matter and saw under the sun that the race does not belong to the swift, nor war to the strong, nor bread to the wise, nor riches to the learned, nor favor to craftsmen, but time and chance are in all things.

**9:12** A man does not know his end; rather, just as fish are caught with a hook, and just as birds are trapped in a snare, so men are caught in an evil time when it suddenly comes upon them.

**9:13** This wisdom also I saw under the sun, and I proved it to be the greatest:

**9:14** a small city, and few men in it; a great king came against it, and surrounded it, and built fortifications all around, and the siege was completed.

**9:15** And a poor and wise man was found in it, and he delivered the city through his wisdom; and afterward no one remembered that poor man.

**9:16** And I myself kept saying that wisdom is better than strength. How then has the wisdom of the poor man been despised, and his words not been heard?

**9:17** The words of the wise are heard in silence, more than the shouting of a ruler among fools.

**9:18** Wisdom is better than weapons of war, and the one who sins in one thing will destroy many good things.

## Translator Notes

- **9:1** — Vulgate reads utrum amore an odio dignus sit ("whether he is worthy of love or of hatred"), where the Hebrew states that man does not know whether love or hatred awaits him.
- **9:2** — Vulgate opens with omnia in futurum servantur incerta ("all things are kept uncertain for the future") and closes by contrasting perjurus ("the perjurer") with ille qui verum dejerat ("the one who swears the truth"), diverging from the Masoretic wording.
- **9:3** — Latin ad inferos ("to the underworld," and apud inferos in 9:10) denotes the abode of the dead, corresponding to Sheol and Hades.
- **9:4** — Vulgate opens with Nemo est qui semper vivat, et qui hujus rei habeat fiduciam ("There is no one who lives forever and who has confidence in this matter"), differing markedly from the Hebrew ("Whoever is joined to all the living has hope").
- **9:18** — Vulgate reads qui in uno peccaverit ("the one who sins in one thing") rather than the Hebrew "one sinner."
