## Text

**5:1** Do not speak anything rashly, nor let your heart be quick to utter a word before God. For God is in heaven, and you are on the earth; for that reason let your words be few.

**5:2** Dreams follow many anxieties, and folly will be found in many words.

**5:3** If you have vowed anything to God, do not delay to fulfill it; for an unfaithful and foolish promise displeases Him, but fulfill whatever you have vowed.

**5:4** And it is far better not to vow than, after a vow, not to fulfill what was promised.

**5:5** Do not give your mouth to cause your flesh to sin, nor say before the angel, "There is no providence," so that God, angered against your words, might not destroy all the works of your hands.

**5:6** Where there are many dreams, there are very many futilities and countless words; you, however, fear God.

**5:7** If you see oppressions of the needy, and violent judgments, and justice being subverted in a province, do not be astonished at this matter; because there is another higher than the high one, and above these also there are others more eminent.

**5:8** And furthermore, the king rules over the entire subject land.

**5:9** A greedy person will not be filled with money, and the one who loves riches will gain no fruit from them; therefore this too is futility.

**5:10** Where there are many goods, many also are those who eat them. And what does it benefit the owner, except that he sees the riches with his own eyes?

**5:11** Sleep is sweet for the worker, whether he eats little or much; yet the fullness of the rich person does not allow him to sleep.

**5:12** There is also another terrible sickness that I saw under the sun: riches kept to the harm of their owner.

**5:13** For they perish in a terrible affliction: he fathered a son who will be in extreme destitution.

**5:14** Just as he came out naked from his mother's womb, so he will return, and he will carry nothing away with him from his labor.

**5:15** An utterly miserable sickness: just as he came, so he will return. What, therefore, does it benefit him that he labored for the wind?

**5:16** All the days of his life he eats in darkness, and in many anxieties, and in hardship and sorrow.

**5:17** This, therefore, has seemed good to me: that a person should eat and drink, and enjoy gladness from his labor at which he himself labored under the sun, for the number of the days of his life that God has given him; and this is his portion.

**5:18** And for every person to whom God has given riches and possessions, and to whom He has granted the power to eat from them, and to enjoy his portion, and to rejoice in his labor: this is a gift of God.

**5:19** For he will not much recall the days of his life, because God occupies his heart with delights.

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — The Vulgate reads infidelis et stulta promissio ("an unfaithful and foolish promise"), whereas the Hebrew text says there is no delight in fools.
- **5:5** — The Vulgate renders the spoken excuse as Non est providentia ("There is no providence"), a distinctive Vulgate reading where the Hebrew text refers to a mistake or inadvertent error.
- **5:8** — The Vulgate reads universae terrae rex imperat servienti ("the king rules over the entire subject land"), diverging from the difficult Hebrew clause about a king served by a cultivated field.
- **5:12** — Latin infirmitas literally means "sickness" or "ailment" (repeated in 5:15), rendering the Hebrew idiom for a grievous evil.
