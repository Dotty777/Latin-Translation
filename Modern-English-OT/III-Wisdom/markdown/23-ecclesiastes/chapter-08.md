## Text

**8:1** The wisdom of a man shines in his expression, and the most powerful one will change his face.

**8:2** I observe the mouth of the king and the commands of the oath of God.

**8:3** Do not hurry to depart from his face, and do not persist in an evil work, because he will do everything that he desires.

**8:4** And his word is full of power, and no one can say to him, "Why are you acting like this?"

**8:5** The one who keeps a command will not experience any evil. The heart of a wise man understands time and response.

**8:6** For every undertaking there is a time and an opportunity, and the affliction of man is great,

**8:7** because he does not know past things, and he cannot know future things by any messenger.

**8:8** It is not in a man's power to restrain the spirit, nor does he have power on the day of death; nor is he permitted to rest when war breaks out, and wickedness will not save the wicked man.

**8:9** I have considered all these things, and I have given my heart to all the works that are done under the sun. Sometimes one man rules over another man to his own harm.

**8:10** I saw the wicked buried, who even while they were still living were in the holy place and were praised in the city as if for righteous works. Yet this also is futility.

**8:11** Indeed, because a sentence is not quickly pronounced against evil people, the sons of men commit evil deeds without any fear.

**8:12** Yet even though a sinner does evil a hundred times and is sustained through patience, I have come to know that it will be well for those who fear God, who revere His face.

**8:13** Let it not be well for the wicked man, nor let his days be prolonged, but like a shadow let those pass away who do not fear the face of the Lord.

**8:14** There is also another futility that happens on the earth: there are righteous people to whom evil things happen as though they had done the works of the wicked, and there are wicked people who are as secure as though they had the deeds of the righteous. Yet this also I judge to be most futile.

**8:15** Therefore I praised gladness, because there is no good for a man under the sun except to eat, and drink, and rejoice, and to carry away this alone with him from his labor in the days of his life that God has given him under the sun.

**8:16** And I applied my heart to know wisdom and to understand the distraction that goes on upon the earth. There is a man who by day and by night takes no sleep with his eyes.

**8:17** And I understood that a man can find no reason for all the works of God, those that are done under the sun; and the more he labors to seek, the less he finds; even if a wise man says that he knows, he will not be able to discover it.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — Vulgate reads potentissimus faciem illius commutabit ("the most powerful one will change his face"), making "the most powerful one" (often understood as God or a supreme ruler) the active subject, where the Hebrew speaks of the hardness of a man's face being changed.
- **8:7** — Vulgate uniquely reads ignorat praeterita ("does not know past things") and nullo ... nuntio ("by any messenger"), where the Hebrew asks who can tell man how what will be shall come about.
- **8:10** — Vulgate reads laudabantur ("were praised"), agreeing with the Septuagint and a Hebrew textual variant against the Masoretic "were forgotten."
- **8:17** — Vulgate renders quanto plus laboraverit ad quaerendum, tanto minus inveniat ("the more he labors to seek, the less he finds"), sharpening the Hebrew ("though man should toil to seek it out, he will not find it").
