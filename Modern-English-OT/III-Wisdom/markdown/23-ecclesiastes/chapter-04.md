## Text

**4:1** I turned to other things and saw the oppressions that are committed under the sun, and the tears of the innocent, and no one as comforter; nor could they resist their violence, deprived of everyone's aid.

**4:2** And I praised the dead more than the living.

**4:3** And I judged happier than both the one who has not yet been born and has not seen the evils that happen under the sun.

**4:4** Again I contemplated all the labors of people, and I noticed that endeavors are exposed to a neighbor's envy; therefore in this too there is futility and superfluous care.

**4:5** A fool folds his hands together and eats his own flesh, saying:

**4:6** "A handful with rest is better than both hands full with labor and affliction of mind."

**4:7** Reflecting, I discovered yet another futility under the sun.

**4:8** There is one alone, and he has no second, no son, no brother, and yet he does not stop laboring, nor are his eyes satisfied with riches; nor does he think it over, saying, "For whom am I laboring and depriving my soul of good things?" In this also there is futility and a terrible affliction.

**4:9** Therefore it is better for two to be together than one, for they have the benefit of their partnership.

**4:10** If one falls, he will be upheld by the other. Woe to the one who is alone, because when he falls, he has no one to lift him up.

**4:11** And if two sleep, they will warm each other; how will one grow warm?

**4:12** And if someone prevails against one, two resist him; a three-stranded cord is broken with difficulty.

**4:13** A poor and wise youth is better than an old and foolish king who does not know how to look ahead to the future.

**4:14** For sometimes someone comes out of prison and chains to a kingdom, and another, born in a kingdom, is consumed by poverty.

**4:15** I saw all the living who walk under the sun with the second young man, who will rise up in his place.

**4:16** The number of the people is endless, of all who were before him, and those who are to come afterward will not rejoice in him; yet this too is futility and affliction of spirit.

**4:17** Guard your foot as you enter the house of God, and draw near to listen. For obedience is much better than the sacrifices of fools, who do not know what evil they are doing.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — Latin calumnias literally denotes false accusations or wrongful extortions and is Jerome's regular Vulgate term for oppression (cf. 5:7).
- **4:4** — The Vulgate reads industrias ... patere invidiae proximi ("endeavors are exposed to a neighbor's envy"), whereas the Hebrew text speaks of toil and skill arising from a person's envy of a neighbor.
- **4:5** — The Vulgate adds dicens ("saying"), explicitly framing the proverb in 4:6 as the fool's own speech rather than the narrator's remark.
- **4:13** — The Vulgate reads qui nescit praevidere in posterum ("who does not know how to look ahead to the future"), whereas the Hebrew text speaks of a king who no longer knows how to accept a warning.
- **4:17** — This verse is 4:17 in both the Vulgate and the Hebrew Masoretic Text (5:1 in some other numberings). The Vulgate renders the clause with the explicit noun obedientia ("obedience").
