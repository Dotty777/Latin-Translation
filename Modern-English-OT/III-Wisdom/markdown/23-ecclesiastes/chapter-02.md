## Text

**2:1** I myself said in my heart, "I will go and abound in delights, and enjoy good things"; and I saw that this too was futility.

**2:2** I regarded laughter as error, and to joy I said, "Why are you deceived in vain?"

**2:3** I thought in my heart to withdraw my flesh from wine, so that I might transfer my soul to wisdom and avoid folly, until I should see what was useful for the sons of men, what needs to be done under the sun in the number of the days of their life.

**2:4** I magnified my works; I built houses for myself and planted vineyards;

**2:5** I made gardens and orchards, and planted them with trees of every kind;

**2:6** and I constructed pools of water for myself, so that I might water a forest of sprouting trees.

**2:7** I possessed male servants and maidservants, and had a large household; herds also, and great flocks of sheep, beyond all who were before me in Jerusalem;

**2:8** I amassed silver and gold for myself, and the wealth of kings and provinces; I provided for myself male singers and female singers, and the delights of the sons of men, cups and pitchers in service for pouring out wines;

**2:9** and I surpassed in wealth all who were before me in Jerusalem; wisdom also persevered with me.

**2:10** And all things that my eyes desired I did not deny them, nor did I keep my heart from enjoying every pleasure and delighting itself in the things that I had prepared; and this I considered my portion, if I made use of my labor.

**2:11** And when I had turned to all the works that my hands had made, and to the labors in which I had sweated in vain, I saw in all things futility and affliction of mind, and that nothing endures under the sun.

**2:12** I passed on to contemplate wisdom, and errors, and folly. (What, I say, is man, that he should be able to follow the King, his Maker?)

**2:13** And I saw that wisdom surpasses folly by as much as light differs from darkness.

**2:14** The eyes of the wise man are in his head; the fool walks in darkness; and I learned that there was one demise for both.

**2:15** And I said in my heart, "If the fool's end and mine will be one, what does it profit me that I gave greater effort to wisdom?" And speaking with my mind, I observed that this too was futility.

**2:16** For there will not be remembrance of the wise man forever, just as of the fool, and future times will cover all things alike with oblivion; the learned dies just like the unlearned.

**2:17** And for this reason I grew weary of my life, seeing that all things under the sun are evil, and all things are futility and affliction of spirit.

**2:18** Again I detested all my industry, with which I labored most earnestly under the sun, since I am about to have an heir after me,

**2:19** of whom I do not know whether he will be wise or foolish, and he will rule over my labors in which I sweated and was anxious; and is there anything so futile?

**2:20** Consequently I ceased, and my heart renounced laboring further under the sun.

**2:21** For though one man labors in wisdom, and learning, and anxiety, he leaves what he has acquired to an idle man; therefore this too is futility and a great evil.

**2:22** For what will it profit a man from all his labor and affliction of spirit, by which he has been tormented under the sun?

**2:23** All his days are full of pains and hardships, nor does he rest in mind through the night. And is this not futility?

**2:24** Is it not better to eat and drink, and to show one's soul good things from one's labors? And this is from the hand of God.

**2:25** Who will so devour and abound in delights as I do?

**2:26** To a man who is good in His sight, God has given wisdom, and knowledge, and gladness; but to the sinner He has given affliction and superfluous care, so that he may add, and gather, and deliver it to the one who has pleased God; yet this too is futility and an empty anxiety of mind.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — The Vulgate reads errorem ("error") and the passive question Quid frustra deciperis? ("Why are you deceived in vain?"), whereas the Hebrew has "madness" and "What does it accomplish?"
- **2:3** — Distinctively in the Vulgate, Ecclesiastes resolves to withdraw his flesh from wine and avoid folly, whereas the Hebrew is usually translated as cheering his body with wine and laying hold on folly.
- **2:8** — Latin scyphos, et urceos in ministerio ad vina fundenda ("cups and pitchers in service for pouring out wines") is Jerome's rendering of an obscure Hebrew phrase often translated "concubines" in modern versions.
- **2:12** — The Vulgate renders the parenthetical question as referring to man following God ("the King, his Maker"), taking the Hebrew verb form as "his Maker" rather than "what has already been done."
- **2:25** — The Vulgate's ut ego ("as I do") reflects the Hebrew reading mimmeni ("apart from me" or "more than I"), whereas some witnesses and modern versions read "apart from Him."
