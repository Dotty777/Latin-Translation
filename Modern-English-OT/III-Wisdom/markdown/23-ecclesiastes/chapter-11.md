## Text

**11:1** Cast your bread upon the passing waters, because after many seasons you will find it.

**11:2** Give a share to seven, and even to eight, because you do not know what evil is to come on the earth.

**11:3** If the clouds are filled, they will pour out rain on the earth. If a tree falls toward the south or toward the north, in whatever place it falls, there it will be.

**11:4** Whoever watches the wind does not sow; and whoever observes the clouds will never reap.

**11:5** Just as you do not know what the way of the spirit is, and in what manner the bones are fitted together in the womb of a pregnant woman, so you do not know the works of God, who is the Maker of all things.

**11:6** In the morning sow your seed, and in the evening do not let your hand cease, because you do not know which may rather sprout, this or that; and if both together, it will be better.

**11:7** Sweet is the light, and it is delightful to the eyes to see the sun.

**11:8** If a man lives many years and rejoices in all of them, he ought to remember the dark time and the many days; when those come, the things that have passed will be convicted of futility.

**11:9** Rejoice therefore, young man, in your adolescence, and let your heart be in what is good in the days of your youth; and walk in the ways of your heart and in the gaze of your eyes, and know that for all these things God will bring you into judgment.

**11:10** Take anger away from your heart, and remove evil from your flesh, for adolescence and pleasure are futile.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew "upon the face of the waters" as super transeuntes aquas ("upon the passing waters") and "after many days" as post tempora multa ("after many seasons").
- **11:5** — Latin spiritus can mean either "spirit" or "wind/breath"; it is rendered "spirit" here to preserve Jerome's distinction from ventum ("wind") in 11:4 and to match spiritus in 12:7.
- **11:8** — Distinctively, the Vulgate renders the closing clause vanitatis arguentur praeterita ("the things that have passed will be convicted of futility"), referring to past joyful years rather than "everything that is coming" as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **11:10** — The Vulgate translates the rare Hebrew word for the prime of youth as voluptas ("pleasure"), paired with adolescentia ("adolescence").
