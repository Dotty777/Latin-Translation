## Text

**12:1** Remember your Creator in the days of your youth, before the time of affliction comes and the years approach about which you say, "They do not please me";

**12:2** before the sun grows dark, and the light, and the moon, and the stars, and the clouds return after the rain;

**12:3** when the guardians of the house will tremble, and the strongest men will totter, and the women who grind will be idle in diminished number, and those who look through the openings will grow dark;

**12:4** and they will close the doors in the street in the lowness of the sound of the grinder, and they will rise up at the sound of a bird, and all the daughters of song will grow deaf:

**12:5** they will also fear high places, and they will be terrified on the road. The almond tree will blossom, the locust will grow fat, and the caper bush will be scattered, because a man will go into the house of his eternity, and the mourners will go about in the street.

**12:6** Before the silver cord is snapped, and the golden band runs back, and the water jar is crushed over the spring, and the wheel is shattered over the cistern,

**12:7** and the dust returns to its own earth where it was from, and the spirit goes back to God, who gave it.

**12:8** "Futility of futilities," said Ecclesiastes, "and everything is futility."

**12:9** And since Ecclesiastes was exceedingly wise, he taught the people and recounted what he had done; and while investigating, he composed many parables.

**12:10** He sought useful words, and wrote down sayings most upright and full of truth.

**12:11** The words of the wise are like goads, and like nails driven deep, which were given through the counsel of masters by one shepherd.

**12:12** Beyond these, my son, do not seek. There is no end to making many books, and frequent meditation is an affliction of the flesh.

**12:13** Let us all together hear the conclusion of speaking. Fear God and keep His commandments: for this is the whole of man,

**12:14** and God will bring everything that is done into judgment for every error, whether it is good or whether it is evil.

## Translator Notes

- **12:5** — The Vulgate distinctively renders the imagery as impinguabitur locusta, et dissipabitur capparis ("the locust will grow fat, and the caper bush will be scattered"), whereas the Hebrew depicts the grasshopper dragging itself along and desire (or the caperberry) failing.
- **12:6** — Instead of the Hebrew "golden bowl is crushed," the Vulgate reads recurrat vitta aurea ("the golden band runs back"), rendering the noun as a fillet or headband and reading the verb from the root "to run."
- **12:8** — The Vulgate retains the transliterated Greek title Ecclesiastes (literally "Assembler" or "Speaker in the assembly"), corresponding to the Hebrew Qoheleth.
- **12:11** — Where the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to "collected sayings" (or "masters of collections"), the Vulgate reads per magistrorum consilium ("through the counsel of masters").
- **12:14** — The Vulgate renders the final phrase as pro omni errato ("for every error"), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("with every hidden thing").
