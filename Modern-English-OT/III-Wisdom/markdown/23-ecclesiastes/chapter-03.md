## Text

**3:1** All things have a time, and in their own spans all things under heaven pass by.

**3:2** A time for being born, and a time for dying; a time for planting, and a time for uprooting what has been planted.

**3:3** A time for killing, and a time for healing; a time for destroying, and a time for building.

**3:4** A time for weeping, and a time for laughing; a time for mourning, and a time for dancing.

**3:5** A time for scattering stones, and a time for gathering; a time for embracing, and a time to be far from embraces.

**3:6** A time for acquiring, and a time for losing; a time for keeping, and a time for casting away.

**3:7** A time for tearing, and a time for sewing; a time for keeping silent, and a time for speaking.

**3:8** A time of love, and a time of hatred; a time of war, and a time of peace.

**3:9** What more does a man have from his labor?

**3:10** I have seen the affliction that God has given to the sons of men, so that they may be strained in it.

**3:11** He has made all things good in their own time, and He has handed the world over to their disputation, so that man may not find out the work that God has worked from the beginning even to the end.

**3:12** And I recognized that there was nothing better than to rejoice and to do good in one's life;

**3:13** for every man who eats and drinks, and sees good from his labor, this is a gift of God.

**3:14** I have learned that all the works that God has made endure forever; we cannot add anything to them, nor take anything away, and these God has made so that He may be feared.

**3:15** What has been done, that very thing endures; the things that are to be have already been, and God restores what has gone away.

**3:16** I saw under the sun, in the place of judgment, ungodliness, and in the place of justice, iniquity;

**3:17** and I said in my heart, "God will judge the just and the ungodly, and the time of every matter will be then."

**3:18** I said in my heart concerning the sons of men, that God would test them and show that they are like beasts.

**3:19** Therefore the demise of man and of animals is one, and the condition of both is equal. Just as man dies, so also do they die. All breathe alike, and man has nothing more than an animal; all things are subject to futility,

**3:20** and all proceed to one place. From the earth they were made, and into the earth they return alike.

**3:21** Who knows whether the spirit of the sons of Adam ascends upward, and whether the spirit of animals descends downward?

**3:22** And I discovered that nothing is better than for a man to rejoice in his work, and that this is his portion. For who will bring him to know the things that are to be after him?

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — Distinctively in the Vulgate, the second clause states that all things under heaven pass by in their own spans (suis spatiis transeunt), whereas the Hebrew speaks of a time for every purpose or matter under heaven.
- **3:11** — Latin mundum tradidit disputationi eorum ("handed the world over to their disputation") is a distinctive Vulgate rendering of the Hebrew clause commonly translated "He has put eternity into their heart."
- **3:17** — The Vulgate reads tunc ("then"), pointing to the time of God's judgment, where the Hebrew reads sham ("there").
- **3:21** — Unlike 1:13, 2:3, 2:8, 3:10, and 3:18 (where the Vulgate translates Hebrew bene ha-adam as filii hominum, "sons of men"), here it distinctively uses the proper name in filiorum Adam ("the sons of Adam").
