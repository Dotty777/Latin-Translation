## Text

**2:1** Son, approaching the service of God, stand in righteousness and fear, and prepare your soul for temptation.

**2:2** Humble your heart, and endure; incline your ear, and receive words of understanding; and do not hasten in the time of calamity.

**2:3** Endure the delays of God; be joined to God, and endure, so that your life may increase at the last.

**2:4** Accept everything that is laid upon you, and in sorrow endure, and in your lowliness have patience,

**2:5** for in fire gold and silver are tested, and acceptable men in the furnace of humiliation.

**2:6** Believe God, and He will restore you; and direct your way, and hope in Him; keep His fear, and grow old in it.

**2:7** You who fear the Lord, wait for His mercy, and do not turn aside from Him, so that you do not fall.

**2:8** You who fear the Lord, believe Him, and your reward will not be made void.

**2:9** You who fear the Lord, hope in Him, and mercy will come to you for delight.

**2:10** You who fear the Lord, love Him, and your hearts will be enlightened.

**2:11** Look, sons, at the nations of men, and know that no one has hoped in the Lord and been put to shame.

**2:12** For who has remained in His commandments and been abandoned? Or who has called upon Him, and He despised him?

**2:13** For God is compassionate and merciful, and will forgive sins in the day of tribulation, and is a protector to all who seek Him in truth.

**2:14** Woe to the double-hearted, and to wicked lips, and to hands that do evil, and to the sinner who walks the earth by two ways!

**2:15** Woe to those faint in heart, who do not believe God, and therefore will not be protected by Him!

**2:16** Woe to those who have lost endurance, and who have abandoned straight ways, and turned aside into crooked ways!

**2:17** And what will they do when the Lord begins to inspect?

**2:18** Those who fear the Lord will not be distrustful of His word, and those who love Him will keep His way.

**2:19** Those who fear the Lord will seek out the things that are well-pleasing to Him, and those who love Him will be filled with His law.

**2:20** Those who fear the Lord will prepare their hearts, and in His sight will sanctify their souls.

**2:21** Those who fear the Lord keep His commandments, and they will remain patient until His visitation,

**2:22** saying, "If we do not repent, we will fall into the hands of the Lord, and not into the hands of men.

**2:23** For according to His greatness, so also is His mercy with Him."

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The clause "stand in righteousness and fear" ("sta in justitia et timore") is a Vulgate addition absent from the Greek; Latin "tentationem" ("temptation") carries the broad sense of both trial/testing and temptation.
- **2:2** — The clause "incline your ear, and receive words of understanding" is a Vulgate addition; "obductionis" ("calamity," literally "a covering over" or "a bringing upon") is an Old Latin calque of Greek "epagōgē" ("calamity/affliction").
- **2:3** — The opening command "Endure the delays of God" ("Sustine sustentationes Dei," literally "endure the deferrals/sustainings of God") is a Vulgate addition not present in the Greek.
- **2:10** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 2:10 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion after Saints.Bible 2:9, without a live Saints.Bible counterpart).
- **2:11** — Vulgate "nationes hominum" ("the nations of men") reflects an Old Latin rendering of Greek "archaias geneas" ("ancient generations") and adds "sons" ("filii"); the two rhetorical questions that follow in Greek 2:10b now stand in restored native Vulgate 2:12.
- **2:12** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 2:12 (parallel to the second half of Saints/Greek 2:10; previously claimed absent in the note on old 2:10).
- **2:17** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 2:17 (parallel to Saints/Greek 2:14b).
- **2:22** — The Vulgate adds "saying, 'If we do not repent'" ("dicentes: Si pœnitentiam non egerimus"), absent from the Greek, framing the verse as a conditional statement.
- **2:23** — The Vulgate adds "with Him" ("cum ipso est") at the end.
