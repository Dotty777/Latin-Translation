## Text

**12:1** If you do good, know to whom you do it, and there will be much gratitude for your good deeds.

**12:2** Do good to a righteous man, and you will find a great reward; and if not from him, certainly from the Lord.

**12:3** For it is not well with the one who is persistent in evils and does not give alms, because the Most High also hates sinners and has shown mercy to those who repent.

**12:4** Give to a merciful man, and do not support a sinner; and to the ungodly and to sinners He will repay vengeance, keeping them for the day of vengeance.

**12:5** Do good to a humble man, and do not give to an ungodly man; forbid loaves of bread to be given to him, so that he may not become more powerful than you through them.

**12:6** For you will find double evils in all the good things that you ever do for him, because the Most High also hates sinners and will repay vengeance to the ungodly.

**12:7** Give to a good man, and do not receive a sinner.

**12:8** A friend will not be recognized in prosperity, and an enemy will not be hidden in adversity.

**12:9** In a man's prosperity, his enemies are in sorrow; and in his adversity, a friend is recognized.

**12:10** Do not ever trust your enemy, for his wickedness corrodes like bronze.

**12:11** And even if he walks humbled and bowed down, apply your mind and guard yourself from him.

**12:12** Do not place him beside you, nor let him sit at your right hand, so that he may not perhaps turn into your place and seek your seat; and in the end you will recognize my words, and be stung by my sayings.

**12:13** Who will have pity on a charmer struck by a serpent, and on all who draw near to wild beasts? And so it is with him who keeps company with an unrighteous man and is wrapped up in his sins.

**12:15** For one hour he will remain with you; but if you turn aside, he will not endure.

**12:16** With his lips an enemy speaks sweetly, and in his heart he lies in wait to overthrow you into a pit.

**12:17** And if evils come upon you, you will find him there first.

**12:18** He will shake his head and clap his hands, and whispering many things, he will change his expression.

## Translator Notes

- **12:3** — Vulgate 'eleemosynas non danti' ("does not give alms") and 'et misertus est poenitentibus' ("and has shown mercy to those who repent") are distinctly Vulgate readings; the Greek speaks of not showing gratitude and lacks the final clause.
- **12:5** — Latin 'panes' ("loaves of bread") is plural, which accounts for the plural pronoun 'in ipsis' ("through them").
- **12:9** — Vulgate 'amicus agnitus est' ("a friend is recognized") differs notably from the Greek ("even a friend will withdraw"); Latin 'malitia' here means "adversity" or "misfortune," paralleling 'in malis' in 12:8.
- **12:12** — Vulgate 'conversus in locum tuum' ("turn into your place") differs from the Greek ("having overthrown you").
