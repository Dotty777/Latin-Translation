## Text

**39:1** The wise man will seek out the wisdom of all the ancients, and will devote himself to the prophets.

**39:2** He will preserve the narrative of renowned men, and at the same time he will enter into the subtleties of parables.

**39:3** He will seek out the hidden things of proverbs, and will dwell in the secrets of parables.

**39:4** He will serve in the midst of great men, and will appear in the sight of a ruler.

**39:5** He will give his heart to keeping watch at dawn toward the Lord, who made him, and will make supplication in the sight of the Most High.

**39:6** For if the great Lord wills, He will fill him with the spirit of understanding,

**39:7** and He Himself will direct his counsel and instruction, and he will reflect on His secrets.

**39:8** He himself will openly reveal the instruction of his teaching, and will glory in the law of the covenant of the Lord.

**39:9** Many will praise his wisdom, and it will not be blotted out forever.

**39:10** The nations will recount his wisdom, and the assembly will proclaim his praise.

**39:11** If he remains, he will leave behind a name greater than a thousand, and if he rests, it will profit him.

**39:12** I will reflect further so that I may recount, for I have been filled as with frenzy.

**39:13** With a voice he says: "Listen to me, divine fruits, and bear fruit like a rose planted beside streams of waters.

**39:14** Like Lebanon, have a fragrance of sweetness.

**39:15** Give magnificence to His name, and give thanks to Him with the voice of your lips, and with songs of the lips and with harps; and thus you will say in thanksgiving:

**39:16** 'All the works of the Lord are exceedingly good.'"

**39:17** At His word water stood like a heap, and at the utterance of His mouth like reservoirs of waters,

**39:18** for at His command good pleasure comes to pass, and there is no diminishing in His salvation.

**39:19** The works of all flesh are before Him, and there is nothing hidden from His eyes.

**39:20** From eternity to eternity He looks, and nothing is marvelous in His sight.

**39:21** One must not say, "What is this, or what is that?" for all things will be sought in their own time.

**39:22** His blessing has overflowed like a river.

**39:23** Just as a deluge has drenched the dry land, so His wrath will inherit the nations that have not sought Him out.

**39:24** Just as He has turned waters into dryness, and the earth has been dried up, and His ways have been made straight for their ways, so for sinners there are stumbling blocks in His wrath.

**39:25** Good things were created for the good from the beginning; so for the most wicked, good things and evil things.

**39:26** The beginning of what is necessary for the life of men: water, fire, and iron, salt, milk, and bread of fine flour, and honey, and a cluster of grapes, and oil, and clothing.

**39:27** All these things will be turned into good for the holy, and so also into evil for the ungodly and sinners.

**39:28** There are spirits that were created for vengeance, and in their fury they have strengthened their torments.

**39:29** Fire, hail, famine, and death, all these were created for vengeance:

**39:30** the teeth of wild beasts, and scorpions, and serpents, and the sword taking vengeance on the ungodly to destruction.

**39:31** In His commands they will feast, and on the earth they will be prepared for need, and in their own times they will not transgress the word.

**39:32** Therefore from the beginning I was made firm, and I reflected, and I thought, and I left writings behind.

**39:33** All the works of the Lord are good, and He will supply every work in its own hour.

**39:34** One must not say, "This is worse than that," for all things will be approved in their own time.

**39:35** And now with whole heart and mouth praise and bless the name of the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **39:7** — The Latin pronouns (ipse, ejus, suis) are ambiguous following the Vulgate's shorter transition from 39:6; here ipse is taken as the Lord directing the wise man's counsel while consiliabitur shifts to the wise man reflecting on God's secrets, though a single subject throughout is also possible.
- **39:12** — Latin ut furore ("as with frenzy/fury") is a Vulgate-distinct reading suggesting inspired ardor, whereas the Greek has "like the full moon."
- **39:13** — Both In voce dicit ("With a voice he says") and divini fructus ("divine fruits") are Vulgate-distinct readings; the Greek lacks the introductory clause and addresses "devout children."
- **39:23** — The Vulgate makes ira ipsius ("His wrath") the singular subject that "will inherit" the nations and adds quae non exquisierunt eum ("that have not sought Him out"), whereas the Greek makes the nations the subject inheriting His wrath.
- **39:24** — Latin in siccitatem ("into dryness") and viis illorum ("for their ways") are Vulgate-distinct readings where the Greek reads "into saltiness" and "for the holy ones."
