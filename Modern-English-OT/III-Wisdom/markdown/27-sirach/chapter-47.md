## Text

**47:1** After these things Nathan arose, a prophet in the days of David.

**47:2** And like fat separated from the flesh, so was David from the sons of Israel.

**47:3** He played with lions as with lambs, and with bears he did likewise, as with lambs of the sheep, in his youth.

**47:4** Did he not kill the giant and take away reproach from the nation?

**47:5** In raising his hand, with a stone from a sling he cast down the boasting of Goliath.

**47:6** For he called upon the Lord Almighty, and He granted in his right hand to remove a man mighty in war, and to exalt the horn of his nation.

**47:7** Thus He glorified him in ten thousands, and praised him in the blessings of the Lord, in offering him a crown of glory;

**47:8** for he crushed enemies on every side, and uprooted the opposing Philistines until the present day; he crushed their horn forever.

**47:9** In every work he gave thanksgiving to the Holy One and to the Most High, in a word of glory.

**47:10** With all his heart he praised the Lord, and he loved God who made him, and He gave him power against enemies.

**47:11** And he made singers stand before the altar, and with their sound he made sweet melodies.

**47:12** And he gave splendor in the celebrations, and adorned the seasons until the completion of life, so that they might praise the holy name of the Lord and magnify the holiness of God in the morning.

**47:13** The Lord purged his sins and exalted his horn forever; and He gave him a covenant of the kingdom and a throne of glory in Israel.

**47:14** After him arose a discerning son, and because of him he cast down all the power of the enemies.

**47:15** Solomon reigned in days of peace, to whom God subjected all enemies, so that he might build a house in His name and prepare holiness forever. How you were instructed in your youth,

**47:16** and you were filled, like a river, with wisdom, and your soul uncovered the earth.

**47:17** And you filled riddles with comparisons; to the islands far away your name was spread abroad, and you were loved in your peace.

**47:18** At songs, and proverbs, and comparisons, and interpretations, the lands marveled,

**47:19** and at the name of the Lord God, whose title is the God of Israel.

**47:20** You gathered gold like brass, and like lead you piled up silver.

**47:21** And you bowed your thighs to women; you had authority over your body.

**47:22** You put a blemish on your glory, and profaned your seed, to bring wrath upon your children and for your folly to be stirred up,

**47:23** so that you made the kingdom divided in two, and a harsh kingdom to rule from Ephraim.

**47:24** But God will not abandon His mercy, and He will not ruin nor blot out His works, nor will He destroy from the root the descendants of His chosen one; and He will not ruin the seed of him who loves the Lord.

**47:25** Yet He gave a remnant to Jacob, and to David from that very stock.

**47:26** And Solomon came to his end with his fathers.

**47:27** And he left behind him from his seed the folly of the nation,

**47:28** and one lacking in prudence, Rehoboam, who turned the nation away by his counsel;

**47:29** and Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel sin and gave Ephraim a way of sinning; and their very many sins overflowed.

**47:30** They greatly turned them away from their land.

**47:31** And he sought out all wickednesses until vengeance came upon them, and He delivered them from all sins.

## Translator Notes

- **47:5** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 47:5 (parallel to Saints/Greek 47:4b).
- **47:7** — Vulgate uses singular verbs ('glorificavit', 'laudavit'), continuing the Lord from native 47:6 (Saints 47:5) as subject (or referring collectively to the nation), whereas the Greek has plural ('they glorified him... and praised him').
- **47:10** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 47:10 (parallel to Saints/Greek 47:8b).
- **47:15** — Latin 'sanctitatem' ('holiness'), here in native 47:15 (Saints 47:13) and in native 47:12 (Saints 47:10), literally renders Greek 'hagiasma' ('sanctuary').
- **47:16** — Latin 'retexit' ('uncovered' or 'revealed') stands where the Greek reads 'covered' ('ekalypsen').
- **47:20** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 47:20 (parallel to Saints/Greek 47:18b); Latin 'auricalcum' denotes brass/orichalcum (where Greek has 'tin').
- **47:21** — Vulgate reads active 'potestatem habuisti' ('you had authority') where the Greek uses the passive ('you were brought under dominion' or 'enslaved').
- **47:25** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 47:25 (parallel to Saints/Greek 47:22b).
- **47:31** — Vulgate has singular 'quaesivit' ('he sought out') where the Greek is plural, uses 'defensio' (lit. 'defense/vindication') for Greek 'vengeance,' and adds the closing clause 'et ab omnibus peccatis liberavit eos' ('and He delivered them from all sins'), which is absent from the Greek.
