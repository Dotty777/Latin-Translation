## Text

**8:1** Do not dispute with a powerful man, or else you might fall into his hands.

**8:2** Do not contend with a wealthy man, or else he might set up a lawsuit against you.

**8:3** Do not dispute with a loose-tongued man, and you will not pile wood onto his fire.

**8:4** Do not associate with an uneducated man, so that he does not speak ill of your lineage.

**8:5** Do not despise a man turning away from sin, nor reproach him; remember that we are all under correction.

**8:6** Do not scorn a man in his old age, for indeed some of us are growing old.

**8:7** Do not rejoice over your dead enemy, knowing that we all die and do not wish to become a cause of rejoicing.

**8:8** Do not despise the discourse of wise elders, and occupy yourself with their proverbs.

**8:9** Do not let the discourse of older men pass you by, for they themselves learned from their fathers.

**8:10** Do not kindle the coals of sinners by rebuking them, and do not be burned by the flame of the fire of their sins.

**8:11** Do not stand against the face of an insolent man, so that he does not sit like one lying in ambush for your mouth.

**8:12** Do not lend to a man stronger than you; and if you do lend, consider it as lost.

**8:13** Do not give a guarantee beyond your strength; and if you do give a guarantee, think as one who will repay.

**8:14** Do not judge against a judge, for he judges according to what is just.

**8:15** Do not go on the road with a reckless man, or else he might burden you with his evils; for he himself goes according to his own will, and you will perish together with his folly.

**8:16** Do not make a quarrel with a hot-tempered man, and do not go into the wilderness with a reckless man; for blood is like nothing before him, and where there is no help, he will strike you down.

**8:17** Do not hold counsel with fools, for they will not be able to love anything except what pleases them.

**8:18** Do not hold counsel in the presence of a stranger, for you do not know what he will bring forth.

**8:19** Do not reveal your heart to every man, or else he might offer you false favor and revile you.

## Translator Notes

- **8:7** — The closing clause ('and do not wish to become a cause of rejoicing,' literally 'and we do not wish to come into joy') is a Vulgate-distinct addition expressing unwillingness to become an object of others' gloating at death.
- **8:10** — The Vulgate expands the verse with 'by rebuking them' and 'of their sins,' creating a Latin wordplay on 'peccatorum' ('of sinners') and 'peccatorum' ('of sins').
- **8:14** — The Vulgate distinctly states 'for he judges according to what is just,' whereas the Greek text warns that others will judge in the judge's favor according to his standing.
- **8:17** — The Vulgate reads 'they will not be able to love anything except what pleases them,' diverging from the Greek text's statement that fools cannot keep a matter secret.
