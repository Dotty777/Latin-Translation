## Text

**10:1** A wise judge will judge his people, and the rule of a sensible man will be stable.

**10:2** As the judge of the people is, so also are his ministers; and as the ruler of the city is, such also are those who dwell in it.

**10:3** An unwise king will destroy his people, and cities will be inhabited through the good sense of the powerful.

**10:4** In the hand of God is the power of the earth, and He will raise up a useful ruler over it in due time.

**10:5** In the hand of God is a man's prosperity, and upon the face of the scribe He will place His honor.

**10:6** Do not remember any wrongdoing of a neighbor, and do nothing in works of wrongdoing.

**10:7** Pride is hateful before God and men, and all iniquity of the nations is detestable.

**10:8** A kingdom is transferred from nation to nation because of injustices, wrongs, outrages, and various deceits.

**10:9** But nothing is more wicked than a greedy man. Why are earth and ashes proud?

**10:10** Nothing is more unjust than to love money; for this man holds even his own soul for sale, because in his life he has cast away his innermost parts.

**10:11** All dominion is brief in life; a prolonged illness burdens the physician.

**10:12** A brief illness the physician cuts short; so too a king is today, and tomorrow he will die.

**10:13** For when a man dies, he will inherit serpents, beasts, and worms.

**10:14** The beginning of a man's pride is to fall away from God,

**10:15** because his heart has departed from Him who made him; for the beginning of all sin is pride. Whoever holds fast to it will be filled with curses, and it will overthrow him in the end.

**10:16** Therefore the Lord has dishonored the assemblies of evil men and has destroyed them to the end.

**10:17** God has torn down the thrones of proud leaders and made the meek sit in their place.

**10:18** God has dried up the roots of proud nations and planted the humble from those very nations.

**10:19** The Lord has overturned the lands of the nations and destroyed them down to the foundation.

**10:20** He has dried up some of them and utterly destroyed them, and has made their memory cease from the earth.

**10:21** God destroyed the memory of the proud and left the memory of those who are humble in mind.

**10:22** Pride was not created for men, nor wrath for the offspring of women.

**10:23** This seed of men will be honored, the one that fears God; but this seed will be dishonored, the one that transgresses the commandments of the Lord.

**10:24** In the midst of brothers their ruler is in honor, and those who fear the Lord will be in His eyes.

**10:25** The glory of the rich, the honored, and the poor is the fear of God.

**10:26** Do not despise a righteous poor man, and do not magnify a sinful rich man.

**10:27** The great man, the judge, and the powerful man are in honor, and no one is greater than the one who fears God.

**10:28** Free men will serve a sensible servant, and a prudent and disciplined man will not grumble when rebuked, and an ignorant man will not be honored.

**10:29** Do not lift yourself up in doing your work, and do not delay in a time of distress.

**10:30** Better is the one who works and abounds in all things than the one who boasts and lacks bread.

**10:31** Son, in gentleness preserve your soul, and give it honor according to its merit.

**10:32** Who will justify the one who sins against his own soul? And who will honor the one who dishonors his own soul?

**10:33** A poor man glories through his discipline and fear, and there is a man who is honored because of his wealth.

**10:34** But the one who glories in poverty, how much more in wealth! And let the one who glories in wealth fear poverty.

## Translator Notes

- **10:8** — The Vulgate reads "various deceits" (diversos dolos), whereas the Greek Septuagint has "wealth" (chremata).
- **10:10** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 10:10 (parallel to Saints/Greek 10:9b).
- **10:11** — The Vulgate distinctly opens this verse with "All dominion is brief in life" (Omnis potentatus brevis vita) and arranges the surrounding sayings differently from the Greek.
- **10:12** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 10:12 (parallel to Saints/Greek 10:10b).
- **10:16** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 10:16 (parallel to Saints/Greek 10:13b).
- **10:23** — Unlike the Greek Septuagint's question-and-answer format, the Vulgate renders this verse as two parallel declarative statements.
- **10:34** — The Vulgate warns that one who glories in wealth should fear poverty (et qui gloriatur in substantia, paupertatem vereatur), whereas the Greek speaks of one who is dishonored in wealth being even more dishonored in poverty.
