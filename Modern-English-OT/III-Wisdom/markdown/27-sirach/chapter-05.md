## Text

**5:1** Do not fix your attention on unjust possessions, and do not say, "I have enough for life," for it will profit nothing in the time of vengeance and calamity.

**5:2** Do not follow in your might the desire of your heart,

**5:3** and do not say, "How was I able?" or, "Who will subdue me because of my deeds?" For God will surely take vengeance.

**5:4** Do not say, "I have sinned, and what sorrowful thing has happened to me?" For the Most High is a patient repayer.

**5:5** Concerning a forgiven sin, do not be without fear, and do not add sin upon sin.

**5:6** And do not say, "The mercy of the Lord is great; He will have mercy on the multitude of my sins."

**5:7** For mercy and wrath draw near swiftly from Him, and His wrath looks upon sinners.

**5:8** Do not delay turning to the Lord, and do not put it off from day to day.

**5:9** For suddenly His wrath will come, and in the time of vengeance He will destroy you.

**5:10** Do not be anxious over unjust riches, for they will not profit you in the day of calamity and vengeance.

**5:11** Do not winnow yourself in every wind, and do not go along every way; for thus every sinner is proved by a double tongue.

**5:12** Be firm in the way of the Lord, and in the truth of your mind and knowledge; and may the word of peace and righteousness accompany you.

**5:13** Be gentle in hearing the word, so that you may understand, and with wisdom bring forth a true answer.

**5:14** If you have understanding, answer your neighbor; but if not, let your hand be upon your mouth, so that you are not caught in an undisciplined word and put to shame.

**5:15** Honor and glory are in the speech of a discerning person; but the tongue of an imprudent person is his own ruin.

**5:16** Do not be called a whisperer, and do not be caught by your tongue and put to shame;

**5:17** for upon a thief are shame and remorse, and the worst condemnation upon the double-tongued; but to the whisperer belong hatred and enmity and dishonor.

**5:18** Do justice to small and great alike.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — Latin adds "unjust" (iniquas) and the closing clause "for it will profit nothing in the time of vengeance and calamity" (in tempore vindictae et obductionis), both absent in the standard Greek text. The Latin noun obductio (literally "a bringing-upon" or "covering over," calquing Greek epagoge) denotes affliction or calamity (also in 5:8).
- **5:3** — Latin "How was I able?" (Quomodo potui?) is a Vulgate-distinct reading where the Greek has "Who will have power over me?" The closing phrase "will surely take vengeance" translates the emphatic Latin participle-and-verb idiom vindicans vindicabit (literally "avenging, will avenge").
- **5:5** — All five Sixto-Clementine Vulgate witnesses unanimously print 'De propitiatio peccato' (understood as a historic variant of 'propitiato peccato', "concerning a propitiated/forgiven sin"), differing from the Greek "concerning atonement."
- **5:7** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 5:7 (continuing 5:6 = Saints 5:6).
- **5:9** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 5:9 (continuing 5:8 = Saints 5:7).
- **5:13** — Vulgate "Be gentle in hearing" (Esto mansuetus ad audiendum) is a distinct Latin reading where the Greek has "Be swift to hear."
- **5:18** — Vulgate "Do justice to small and great alike" (Justifica pusillum et magnum similiter, literally "Justify the small and the great alike") differs from the Greek "In great and small matters do not err."
