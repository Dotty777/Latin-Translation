## Text

**48:1** And Elijah the prophet rose up like fire, and his word was burning like a torch.

**48:2** He brought a famine upon them, and those provoking him by their envy became few, for they could not endure the commands of the Lord.

**48:3** By the word of the Lord he held back heaven, and he cast down fire from heaven three times.

**48:4** Thus Elijah was magnified in his wondrous deeds. And who can boast in the same way as you?

**48:5** You who raised up a dead person from the underworld, from the lot of death, by the word of the Lord God;

**48:6** who cast down kings to destruction, and easily shattered their power, and cast down glorious men from their bed;

**48:7** who hear judgment at Sinai, and at Horeb judgments of vindication;

**48:8** who anoint kings to repentance, and make prophets successors after you;

**48:9** who were received up in a whirlwind of fire, in a chariot of fiery horses;

**48:10** who are recorded in the judgments of the times to calm the anger of the Lord, to reconcile a father's heart to his son, and to restore the tribes of Jacob.

**48:11** Blessed are those who saw you and were adorned in your friendship.

**48:12** For we live by life only; after death, however, our name will not be such.

**48:13** Elijah indeed was hidden in a whirlwind, and in Elisha his spirit was made full; in his days he did not fear any prince, and no one overcame him by power;

**48:14** nor did any word surpass him, and his dead body prophesied.

**48:15** In his life he performed marvels, and in death he worked wondrous deeds.

**48:16** In all these things the people did not repent, and they did not turn away from their sins, until they were cast out from their land and scattered into every land;

**48:17** and a very small nation was left, and a prince in the house of David.

**48:18** Some of them did what pleased God, while others committed many sins.

**48:19** Hezekiah fortified his city and brought water into the midst of it; he dug through the rock with iron and built a well for the water.

**48:20** In his days Sennacherib came up and sent Rabshakeh, and he lifted up his hand against them; he raised his hand against Zion and became proud in his power.

**48:21** Then their hearts and hands were shaken, and they suffered pain like women in labor.

**48:22** And they called upon the merciful Lord, and spreading out their hands, lifted them up to heaven; and the Holy One, the Lord God, quickly heard their voice.

**48:23** He did not remember their sins, nor did He give them over to their enemies, but He cleansed them by the hand of Isaiah the holy prophet.

**48:24** He cast down the camp of the Assyrians, and the angel of the Lord crushed them;

**48:25** for Hezekiah did what pleased God, and walked firmly in the way of his father David, which Isaiah commanded him, a great prophet and faithful in the sight of God.

**48:26** In his days the sun went backward, and he added life to the king.

**48:27** By a great spirit he saw the last things, and he comforted those mourning in Zion forever.

**48:28** He showed things to come, and hidden things before they happened.

## Translator Notes

- **48:2** — Vulgate reads 'those provoking him by their envy became few, for they could not endure the commands of the Lord,' whereas the Greek reads 'by his zeal he made them few in number' and lacks the closing clause.
- **48:6** — All five Sixto-Clementine Vulgate witnesses unanimously print the historic spelling 'pernicem' (for 'perniciem', "destruction") and include the clause "and easily shattered their power," absent from the standard Greek.
- **48:8** — Vulgate reads 'to repentance' (ad poenitentiam) and plural 'prophets' (prophetas), whereas the Greek has 'for retribution' and singular 'a prophet.'
- **48:11** — Vulgate reads 'were adorned in your friendship' (decorati sunt, reflecting the Greek variant kekosmenoi); the following clause stands in restored native Vulgate 48:12 ('For we live by life only; after death, however, our name will not be such'), contrasting with the Greek 'for we too shall surely live.'
- **48:12** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 48:12 (parallel to Saints/Greek 48:11b; previously claimed absent in the note on 48:11). Vulgate 'Nam nos vita vivimus tantum: post mortem autem non erit tale nomen nostrum' diverges sharply from Greek "for we too shall surely live."
- **48:18** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 48:18 (parallel to Saints/Greek 48:16).
- **48:23** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 48:23 (parallel to Saints/Greek 48:20b).
- **48:27** — Vulgate attaches 'forever' (usque in sempiternum) to the end of native 48:27 (Saints 48:24), whereas in the Greek the phrase opens 48:25 ('to the end of time he revealed what was to occur').
