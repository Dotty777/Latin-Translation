## Text

**40:1** A great toil was created for all men, and a heavy yoke upon the sons of Adam, from the day of coming forth from the womb of their mother until the day of burial into the mother of all.

**40:2** Their thoughts, and fears of heart, the imagining of expectation, and the day of ending,

**40:3** from the one sitting upon a glorious throne, down to the one humbled in earth and ashes:

**40:4** from him who wears violet and bears a crown, down to him who is covered with raw linen: fury, jealousy, turmoil, wavering, and fear of death, persistent anger, and strife;

**40:5** and in the time of refreshment on a bed, the sleep of night alters his knowledge.

**40:6** Little, as nothing, in rest, and from then on in dreams, as in a day of watch.

**40:7** He was troubled in the vision of his heart, like one who has escaped on the day of battle; at the time of his deliverance he rose up, even marveling at no fear,

**40:8** with all flesh, from man to beast, and upon sinners sevenfold.

**40:9** In addition to these: death, blood, strife, and sword, oppressions, famine, and ruin, and scourges;

**40:10** upon the wicked all these things were created, and because of them the deluge came to be.

**40:11** All things that are from the earth will turn back to the earth, and all waters will return to the sea.

**40:12** Every bribe and iniquity will be blotted out, and faithfulness will stand forever.

**40:13** The possessions of the unjust will be dried up like a river, and like a great thunder in rain they will resound.

**40:14** In opening his hands he will rejoice; so transgressors will waste away at the end.

**40:15** The descendants of the ungodly will not multiply branches, and unclean roots sound upon the top of a rock.

**40:16** Greenery upon every water and at the bank of a river will be plucked up before all grass.

**40:17** Grace is like a paradise in blessings, and mercy remains forever.

**40:18** The life of a self-sufficient laborer will be made sweet, and in it you will find a treasure.

**40:19** Children and the building of a city will establish a name, and above these a spotless wife will be reckoned.

**40:20** Wine and music gladden the heart, and above both, the love of wisdom.

**40:21** Flutes and psaltery make a sweet melody, and above both, a sweet tongue.

**40:22** Your eye will desire grace and beauty, and above these, green sown fields.

**40:23** A friend and a companion meeting at the right time, and above both, a wife with a husband.

**40:24** Brothers are for help in a time of tribulation, and above them mercy will deliver.

**40:25** Gold and silver are the establishing of the feet, and above both, well-pleasing counsel.

**40:26** Riches and powers exalt the heart, and above these, the fear of the Lord.

**40:27** The fear of the Lord is like a paradise of blessing, and above all glory they have covered it.

**40:28** Son, in the time of your life do not be in want, for it is better to die than to be in want.

**40:29** A man looking at another's table: his life is not in the thought of sustenance, for he nourishes his soul with another's foods.

**40:30** In the mouth of an imprudent man want will be made sweet, and in his belly a fire will burn.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**40:31** *(after `40:29`)* But a disciplined and educated man will guard himself.

## Translator Notes

- **40:6** — The Latin syntax is compressed and verbless; in die respectus ("in a day of watch/inspection") reflects the Greek image of being on lookout or guard duty.
- **40:15** — Latin sonant ("sound") is a Vulgate-distinct verb with no equivalent in the Greek, evoking dry roots rattling upon bare rock.
- **40:18** — The Vulgate merges sibi sufficientis operarii ("a self-sufficient laborer") into a single phrase and ends with et in ea invenies thesaurum ("and in it you will find a treasure"), whereas the Greek contrasts both with the one who finds a treasure.
- **40:27** — Latin operuerunt illum ("they have covered it") uses a Vulgate-distinct plural verb where the Greek has a singular verb; illum may refer either to the fear of the Lord ("it") or to the one who fears Him ("him").
- **40:29** — Latin alit ("nourishes") is a Vulgate-distinct reading where the Greek has "pollutes/defiles"; similarly, in 40:28-30 the Vulgate speaks broadly of want (indigere, inopia) rather than begging.
