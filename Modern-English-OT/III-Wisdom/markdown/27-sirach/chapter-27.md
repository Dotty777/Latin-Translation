## Text

**27:1** Because of poverty many have transgressed, and the one who seeks to become rich turns his eye away.

**27:2** Just as a peg is driven into the middle of a joint of stones, so also right between selling and buying sin will be squeezed in.

**27:3** Offense will be crushed together with the offender.

**27:4** If you do not hold yourself steadfastly in the fear of the Lord, your house will quickly be overthrown.

**27:5** Just as dust will remain in the shaking of a sieve, so will a man's perplexity in his thought.

**27:6** The furnace tests the vessels of the potter, and the testing of tribulation tests righteous people.

**27:7** Just as the cultivation of a tree shows its fruit, so too a word from the thought of a man's heart.

**27:8** Before speech, do not praise a man, for this is the testing of people.

**27:9** If you pursue righteousness, you will lay hold of her and put her on like a full-length robe of honor, and you will dwell with her, and she will protect you forever, and on the day of recognition you will find a firm support.

**27:10** Birds gather to those like themselves, and truth will return to those who practice it.

**27:11** A lion always lies in wait for prey; so do sins for those who practice iniquities.

**27:12** A holy man remains in wisdom like the sun, whereas a fool changes like the moon.

**27:13** In the midst of the senseless, reserve speech for the right time, but in the midst of the thoughtful, be constantly present.

**27:14** The talk of sinners is hateful, and their laughter is in the delights of sin.

**27:15** Speech that swears much will make the hair of the head stand on end, and its irreverence is a stopping of the ears.

**27:16** The shedding of blood is in the quarreling of the proud, and their cursing is grievous to hear.

**27:17** Whoever lays bare a friend's secrets loses trust, and he will not find a friend after his own mind.

**27:18** Love your neighbor, and unite yourself with him in trust.

**27:19** But if you lay bare his hidden things, you will not pursue after him.

**27:20** For just as a man who loses his friend, so also is the one who loses the friendship of his neighbor.

**27:21** And just as one who releases a bird from his hand, so you have abandoned your neighbor, and you will not catch him.

**27:22** Do not pursue him, because he is far away; for he has escaped like a roe deer from a snare, because his soul has been wounded.

**27:23** You will no longer be able to bind him up. And for a curse there is reconciliation.

**27:24** But to lay bare the secrets of a friend is the despair of an unhappy soul.

**27:25** The one who winks with the eye devises wicked things, and no one will cast him off.

**27:26** In the sight of your eyes he will sweeten his mouth, and he will marvel at your speeches; but in the end he will twist his mouth, and in your words he will place a stumbling block.

**27:27** I have hated many things, and I have not counted them equal to him; and the Lord will hate him.

**27:28** Whoever throws a stone high up, it will fall upon his head; and a treacherous blow of the treacherous man will deal out wounds.

**27:29** And whoever digs a pit will fall into it, and whoever places a stone for his neighbor will stumble over it, and whoever sets a snare for another will perish in it.

**27:30** As for the one who carries out a most wicked plan, it will roll down upon him, and he will not recognize from where it comes to him.

**27:31** Mockery and reproach belong to the proud, and vengeance like a lion will lie in wait for him.

**27:32** Those who delight in the fall of the righteous will perish by a snare, and anguish will consume them before they die.

**27:33** Anger and fury, both are detestable, and a sinful man will hold fast to them.

## Translator Notes

- **27:3** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 27:3 (Vulgate/LXX-II continuation of 27:2 = Saints 27:2).
- **27:5** — The Vulgate reads pulvis ("dust") and the Greek loanword aporia ("perplexity" or "distress"), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads "refuse" and "rubbish."
- **27:9** — Latin poderem denotes a foot-length priestly robe. The entire second half of the verse ("and you will dwell with her... find a firm support") is a Vulgate expansion from the Old Latin tradition absent from the shorter Greek text.
- **27:12** — The Vulgate reads Homo sanctus in sapientia manet sicut sol ("A holy man remains in wisdom like the sun"), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads "The discourse of a godly person is always wisdom."
- **27:19** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 27:19 (parallel to Saints/Greek 27:16-17).
- **27:20** — The Vulgate reads amicum suum ("his friend") and third-person qui perdit ("the one who loses"), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads "his enemy" (or "a dead man") and addresses the reader in the second person.
- **27:23** — Following the added clause at the end of native Vulgate 27:22 (Saints 27:20), the Vulgate negates the opening clause ("you will no longer be able to bind him up," vs. Greek "a wound can be bound up"); the closing clause about revealing a friend's secrets now stands in restored native Vulgate 27:24.
- **27:24** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 27:24 (parallel to Saints/Greek 27:21b; previously claimed omitted in the note on old 27:21).
