## Text

**3:1** The children of wisdom are the assembly of the righteous, and their nation is obedience and love.

**3:2** Hear the judgment of a father, children, and act in this way, so that you may be saved.

**3:3** For God honored the father in the children, and seeking out the judgment of the mother, He established it upon the children.

**3:4** He who loves God will make entreaty for sins, and will restrain himself from them, and in daily prayer he will be heard.

**3:5** And just as one who stores up treasure, so also is he who honors his mother.

**3:6** He who honors his father will rejoice in his children, and on the day of his prayer he will be heard.

**3:7** He who honors his father will live a longer life, and he who obeys his father will refresh his mother.

**3:8** He who fears the Lord honors his parents, and will serve as masters those who brought him forth.

**3:9** In deed, and speech, and all patience, honor your father.

**3:10** so that a blessing from him may come upon you, and his blessing may remain at the end.

**3:11** The blessing of a father establishes the houses of children, but the curse of a mother uproots the foundations.

**3:12** Do not glory in the dishonor of your father, for his shame is not glory to you.

**3:13** For the glory of a man is from the honor of his father, and the disgrace of a son is a father without honor.

**3:14** Son, support the old age of your father, and do not grieve him during his life;

**3:15** and if he fails in understanding, grant him pardon, and do not despise him in your strength; for almsgiving to a father will not be forgotten.

**3:16** For in place of a mother's sin, good will be restored to you;

**3:17** and in righteousness it will be built up for you, and on the day of tribulation you will be remembered, and like ice in clear weather, your sins will dissolve.

**3:18** How bad is the reputation of the one who abandons his father, and cursed by God is the one who provokes his mother!

**3:19** Son, accomplish your works in gentleness, and you will be loved above the glory of men.

**3:20** However great you are, humble yourself in all things, and before God you will find favor;

**3:21** for great is the power of God alone, and by the humble He is honored.

**3:22** Do not seek things too high for you, and do not search into things too strong for you; but the things that God has commanded you, think about those always, and do not be inquisitive about too many of His works.

**3:23** For it is not necessary for you to see with your eyes the things that are hidden.

**3:24** Do not search in many ways into unnecessary matters, and you will not be inquisitive about too many of His works.

**3:25** For very many things beyond the understanding of men have been shown to you;

**3:26** their own conjecture has also tripped up many, and has held their minds in emptiness.

**3:27** A hard heart will fare poorly in the end, and the one who loves danger will perish in it.

**3:28** A heart entering upon two ways will not have success, and the one who is crooked in heart will stumble in them.

**3:29** A wicked heart will be weighed down with sorrows, and the sinner will add to his sinning.

**3:30** For the assembly of the proud there will be no healing, for the shoot of sin will take root in them, and it will not be understood.

**3:31** The heart of a wise man is understood in wisdom, and a good ear will hear wisdom with all desire.

**3:32** A wise and discerning heart will keep itself from sins, and in deeds of righteousness it will have success.

**3:33** Water extinguishes a burning fire, and almsgiving resists sins;

**3:34** and God is the watcher over him who returns a favor: He remembers him for the future, and in the time of his fall he will find firm support.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 3:1 (Vulgate-distinct opening verse before 3:2 = Saints 3:1).
- **3:4** — Vulgate uniquely reads "He who loves God" (Qui diligit Deum) and adds the final two clauses, whereas the Greek reads "He who honors a father atones for sins." Latin exorabit pro peccatis means to appease or obtain pardon by prayer for sins, and in oratione dierum is literally "in the prayer of days" (daily prayer).
- **3:10** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 3:10 (continuing 3:9 = Saints 3:8).
- **3:16** — Vulgate distinctly reads pro peccato matris ("in place of a mother's sin"), whereas the Greek reads simply "in place of sins" without mentioning a mother.
- **3:22** — Vulgate reads operibus ejus ("His works," referring to God, repeated in native 3:24 / Saints 3:23), whereas the Greek refers to "your works" (one's own tasks).
- **3:28** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 3:28 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion between 3:27 and 3:29).
- **3:30** — Vulgate renders Greek epagoge ("calamity/affliction") as Synagogae ("assembly/synagogue") and adds the closing clause et non intelligetur ("and it will not be understood").
- **3:34** — Vulgate uniquely adds et Deus prospector est ("and God is the watcher over"), making God the subject who remembers the person who returns a favor, whereas the Greek does not mention God here.
