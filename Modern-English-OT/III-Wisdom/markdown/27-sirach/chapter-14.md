## Text

**14:1** Blessed is the man who has not slipped in word from his mouth, and has not been stung by the sorrow of transgression.

**14:2** Happy is he who has not had sorrow in his own mind, and has not fallen away from his hope.

**14:3** For a greedy and stingy man, wealth is without reason; and for an envious man, to what purpose is gold?

**14:4** Whoever heaps up unjustly from his own soul gathers for others, and in his goods another will live in luxury.

**14:5** Whoever is wicked to himself, to whom else will he be good? And he will not take delight in his own goods.

**14:6** Nothing is more wicked than he who begrudges himself, and this is the repayment of his malice.

**14:7** And even if he does good, he does it unknowingly and unwillingly; and in the end he reveals his malice.

**14:8** Wicked is the eye of an envious man, both turning away his face and despising his own soul.

**14:9** The eye of a greedy man is insatiable in a portion of iniquity; it will not be satisfied until it consumes his own soul, drying it up.

**14:10** An evil eye is directed toward evil things, and will not be filled with bread, but will be needy and in sorrow over its own table.

**14:11** Son, if you have anything, do good to yourself, and present worthy offerings to God.

**14:12** Remember that death does not delay, and remember the covenant of the underworld, because it has been shown to you; for the covenant of this world will surely die.

**14:13** Before death, do good to your friend, and according to your strength, reach out and give to a poor man.

**14:14** Do not be deprived of a good day, and do not let a small portion of a good gift pass you by.

**14:15** Will you not leave your pains and your labors to others in the division of the lot?

**14:16** Give and receive, and justify your soul.

**14:17** All flesh will grow old like grass, and like a flourishing leaf on a green tree.

**14:18** Some are brought forth, and others are cast down; so is the generation of flesh and blood: one comes to an end, and another is born.

**14:19** Every corruptible work will fail in the end, and whoever works it will go with it.

**14:20** Blessed is the man who will dwell in wisdom, and who will meditate in his righteousness, and in understanding will ponder the watchfulness of God;

**14:21** who reflects on her ways in his heart and has understanding in her secrets, going after her like a tracker and taking his stand in her ways;

**14:23** who looks through her windows and listens at her doors.

**14:25** He who rests near her house and drives a peg into her walls will set up his hut close by her hands, and good things will rest in his hut forever.

**14:26** He will place his children under her shelter, and under her branches he will dwell.

**14:27** He will be protected under her shelter from the heat, and in her glory he will rest.

## Translator Notes

- **14:4** — Latin 'ex animo suo' (literally "from his own soul/self") reflects the Greek idiom of hoarding by depriving oneself, while the Vulgate adds 'injuste' ("unjustly").
- **14:9** — Vulgate attaches 'iniquitatis' to 'in parte' ("in a portion of iniquity") and links 'consumat arefaciens' directly to 'animam suam' ("his own soul"), differing syntactically from the Greek.
- **14:12** — Vulgate omits the negative found in the Greek ("the decree of Hades has not been shown to you") and places the clause 'testamentum enim hujus mundi morte morietur' ("for the covenant of this world will surely die") here rather than in v. 17; 'testamentum' renders Greek 'diatheke' ("covenant" or "decree").
- **14:16** — Vulgate 'justifica animam tuam' ("justify your soul") differs from the Greek ("indulge/delight your soul").
- **14:20** — Vulgate 'in justitia sua' ("in his righteousness") and 'circumspectionem Dei' ("the watchfulness of God") are distinctly Vulgate readings; in vv. 21-27 the feminine pronouns ('illam', 'illius', 'ejus') refer to personified Wisdom ('sapientia').
