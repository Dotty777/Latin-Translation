## Text

**7:1** Do not do evil things, and they will not overtake you.

**7:2** Depart from what is unjust, and evil things will fall away from you.

**7:3** Son, do not sow evil things in the furrows of injustice, and you will not reap them sevenfold.

**7:4** Do not seek leadership from the Lord, nor a seat of honor from a king.

**7:5** Do not justify yourself before God, for He Himself is the knower of the heart; and in the presence of a king, do not wish to appear wise.

**7:6** Do not seek to become a judge unless you are able by strength to break through iniquities, or else you might dread the face of a powerful man and place a stumbling block in your uprightness.

**7:7** Do not sin against the multitude of the city, and do not thrust yourself into the people;

**7:8** and do not bind up double sins, for not even for one will you go unpunished.

**7:9** Do not be fainthearted in your mind;

**7:10** do not despise praying and giving alms.

**7:11** Do not say, "God will look upon the multitude of my gifts, and when I offer to God Most High, He will receive my gifts."

**7:12** Do not mock a man in bitterness of soul, for there is One who humbles and exalts: God, the all-seeing observer.

**7:13** Do not love a lie against your brother, nor do the same against a friend.

**7:14** Do not wish to tell any lie, for the continual practice of it is not good.

**7:15** Do not be wordy in the multitude of the elders, and do not repeat a word in your prayer.

**7:16** Do not hate laborious works and farming created by the Most High.

**7:17** Do not count yourself in the multitude of the undisciplined.

**7:18** Remember wrath, because it will not delay.

**7:19** Humble your spirit greatly, for the vengeance on the flesh of the ungodly is fire and the worm.

**7:20** Do not deal treacherously against a friend who delays money, nor scorn a most dear brother for gold.

**7:21** Do not depart from a sensible and good wife whom you have received as your portion in the fear of the Lord, for the grace of her modesty is above gold.

**7:22** Do not harm a servant who works in truth, nor a hired worker who gives his life.

**7:23** Let a sensible servant be dear to you like your own soul; do not defraud him of freedom, nor abandon him destitute.

**7:24** You have livestock; attend to them, and if they are useful, let them remain with you.

**7:25** Do you have sons? Instruct them, and bend them from their childhood.

**7:26** Do you have daughters? Guard their body, and do not show your cheerful face to them.

**7:27** Give away a daughter, and you will have done a great work; and give her to a sensible man.

**7:28** If you have a wife according to your soul, do not cast her away, and do not entrust yourself to a detestable one. With all your heart

**7:29** honor your father, and do not forget the groans of your mother.

**7:30** Remember that if not through them, you would not have been born, and repay them just as they also did for you.

**7:31** With all your soul fear the Lord, and sanctify His priests.

**7:32** With all your strength love Him who made you, and do not abandon His ministers.

**7:33** Honor God from all your soul, and hold the priests in honor, and purify yourself with the arms.

**7:34** Give them a share, just as it has been commanded you, of the firstfruits and of purification, and purify yourself for your negligence with a few things.

**7:35** The gift of your arms and the sacrifice of sanctification you will offer to the Lord, and the firstfruits of the holy things.

**7:36** And stretch out your hand to a poor person, so that your propitiation and blessing may be made complete.

**7:37** The grace of a gift is in the sight of every living person, and do not withhold grace from one who is dead.

**7:38** Do not be lacking in consolation for those who weep, and walk with those who mourn.

**7:39** Do not be reluctant to visit a sick person, for from these things you will be strengthened in love.

**7:40** In all your works, remember your last things, and you will never sin.

## Translator Notes

- **7:5** — The middle clause ('for He Himself is the knower of the heart') is a Vulgate-distinct expansion not found in the shorter Greek text.
- **7:11** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 7:11 (parallel to Saints/Greek 7:9, previously missing from the critical text).
- **7:13** — The Vulgate reads 'amare' ('love'), likely an early Latin transmission variant for 'arare' ('plow,' matching the Greek metaphor of devising a lie).
- **7:18** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 7:18 (parallel to Saints/Greek 7:16b).
- **7:20** — The Vulgate 'pecuniam differentem' ('who delays money,' i.e., puts off payment) is a distinct reading reflecting the Greek term 'diaphoron' ('gain' or 'money').
- **7:28** — Following the Clementine Vulgate verse division, the prepositional phrase 'With all your heart' stands at the end of native 7:28 (Saints 7:26) and governs 'honor your father' at the start of native 7:29 (Saints 7:27).
- **7:33** — The phrase 'purify yourself with the arms' ('propurga te cum brachiis') is a Vulgate-distinct reading; 'brachiis' (literally 'arms') reflects the sacrificial shoulder-portions given to the priests in the Greek text.
- **7:34** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 7:34 (continuing 7:33 = Saints 7:31).
- **7:35** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 7:35 (continuing 7:34 = Saints 7:31b); Latin 'brachiorum' literally means "arms" (reflecting the sacrificial shoulder-portions, as in 7:33).
