## Text

**7:1** Do not do evil things, and they will not overtake you.

**7:2** Depart from what is unjust, and evil things will fall away from you.

**7:3** Son, do not sow evil things in the furrows of injustice, and you will not reap them sevenfold.

**7:4** Do not seek leadership from the Lord, nor a seat of honor from a king.

**7:5** Do not justify yourself before God, for He Himself is the knower of the heart; and in the presence of a king, do not wish to appear wise.

**7:6** Do not seek to become a judge unless you are able by strength to break through iniquities, or else you might dread the face of a powerful man and place a stumbling block in your uprightness.

**7:7** Do not sin against the multitude of the city, and do not thrust yourself into the people;

**7:8** and do not bind up double sins, for not even for one will you go unpunished.

**7:10** Do not be fainthearted in your mind; do not despise praying and giving alms.

**7:11** Do not mock a man in bitterness of soul, for there is One who humbles and exalts: God, the all-seeing observer.

**7:12** Do not love a lie against your brother, nor do the same against a friend.

**7:13** Do not wish to tell any lie, for the continual practice of it is not good.

**7:14** Do not be wordy in the multitude of the elders, and do not repeat a word in your prayer.

**7:15** Do not hate laborious works and farming created by the Most High.

**7:16** Do not count yourself in the multitude of the undisciplined.

**7:17** Humble your spirit greatly, for the vengeance on the flesh of the ungodly is fire and the worm.

**7:18** Do not deal treacherously against a friend who delays money, nor scorn a most dear brother for gold.

**7:19** Do not depart from a sensible and good wife whom you have received as your portion in the fear of the Lord, for the grace of her modesty is above gold.

**7:20** Do not harm a servant who works in truth, nor a hired worker who gives his life.

**7:21** Let a sensible servant be dear to you like your own soul; do not defraud him of freedom, nor abandon him destitute.

**7:22** You have livestock; attend to them, and if they are useful, let them remain with you.

**7:23** Do you have sons? Instruct them, and bend them from their childhood.

**7:24** Do you have daughters? Guard their body, and do not show your cheerful face to them.

**7:25** Give away a daughter, and you will have done a great work; and give her to a sensible man.

**7:26** If you have a wife according to your soul, do not cast her away, and do not entrust yourself to a detestable one. With all your heart

**7:27** honor your father, and do not forget the groans of your mother.

**7:28** Remember that if not through them, you would not have been born, and repay them just as they also did for you.

**7:29** With all your soul fear the Lord, and sanctify His priests.

**7:30** With all your strength love Him who made you, and do not abandon His ministers.

**7:31** Honor God from all your soul, and hold the priests in honor, and purify yourself with the arms.

**7:32** And stretch out your hand to a poor person, so that your propitiation and blessing may be made complete.

**7:33** The grace of a gift is in the sight of every living person, and do not withhold grace from one who is dead.

**7:34** Do not be lacking in consolation for those who weep, and walk with those who mourn.

**7:35** Do not be reluctant to visit a sick person, for from these things you will be strengthened in love.

**7:36** In all your works, remember your last things, and you will never sin.

## Translator Notes

- **7:5** — The middle clause ('for He Himself is the knower of the heart') is a Vulgate-distinct expansion not found in the shorter Greek text.
- **7:12** — The Vulgate reads 'amare' ('love'), likely an early Latin transmission variant for 'arare' ('plow,' matching the Greek metaphor of devising a lie).
- **7:18** — The Vulgate 'pecuniam differentem' ('who delays money,' i.e., puts off payment) is a distinct reading reflecting the Greek term 'diaphoron' ('gain' or 'money').
- **7:26** — Following the Clementine Vulgate verse division, the prepositional phrase 'With all your heart' stands at the end of verse 26 and governs 'honor your father' at the start of verse 27.
- **7:31** — The phrase 'purify yourself with the arms' ('propurga te cum brachiis') is a Vulgate-distinct reading; 'brachiis' (literally 'arms') reflects the sacrificial shoulder-portions given to the priests in the Greek text.
