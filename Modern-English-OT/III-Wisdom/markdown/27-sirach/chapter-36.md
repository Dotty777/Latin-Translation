## Text

**36:1** Have mercy on us, God of all, and look upon us, and show us the light of Your mercies.

**36:2** And send Your fear upon the nations that have not sought You out, so that they may know that there is no god except You, and may recount Your great deeds.

**36:3** Lift up Your hand against foreign nations, so that they may see Your power.

**36:4** For just as in their sight You were sanctified in us, so in our sight You will be magnified in them,

**36:5** so that they may know You, just as we also have known that there is no god besides You, Lord.

**36:6** Renew signs, and vary wonders.

**36:7** Glorify Your hand and right arm.

**36:8** Stir up fury, and pour out wrath.

**36:9** Remove the adversary, and afflict the enemy.

**36:10** Hasten the time, and remember the end, so that they may recount Your wonders.

**36:11** In the wrath of flame let the one who escapes be devoured, and let those who mistreat Your people find destruction.

**36:12** Crush the head of the enemy rulers who say, "There is no other besides us."

**36:13** Gather all the tribes of Jacob, so that they may know that there is no god except You and recount Your great deeds, and You will inherit them just as from the beginning.

**36:14** Have mercy on Your people, upon whom Your name has been called, and on Israel, whom You have made equal to Your firstborn.

**36:15** Have mercy on the city of Your sanctification, Jerusalem, the city of Your rest.

**36:16** Fill Zion with Your inexpressible words, and Your people with Your glory.

**36:17** Give testimony to those who from the beginning are Your creatures, and raise up the proclamations which the former prophets spoke in Your name.

**36:18** Give a reward to those who wait for You, so that Your prophets may be found faithful; and hear the prayers of Your servants,

**36:19** according to the blessing of Aaron concerning Your people; and direct us into the way of righteousness, and let all who inhabit the earth know that You are God, the watcher of the ages.

**36:20** The stomach will eat every food, yet one food is better than another.

**36:21** The palate tastes the food of wild game, and a discerning heart tastes lying words.

**36:22** A perverse heart will give sorrow, and an experienced man will resist it.

**36:23** A woman will receive every male, yet one daughter is better than another.

**36:24** The beauty of a woman gladdens the face of her husband, and above every longing of a man it brings on desire.

**36:25** If there is a tongue of healing, it is also of soothing and mercy; her husband is not like the sons of men.

**36:26** He who possesses a good wife begins a possession; she is a helper like him, and a pillar as a rest.

**36:27** Where there is no hedge, the possession will be plundered; and where there is no wife, the needy one groans.

**36:28** Who trusts the one who has no nest and turns aside wherever it grows dark, like a girded robber leaping from city to city?

## Translator Notes

- **36:1** — The petition "and show us the light of Your mercies" (et ostende nobis lucem miserationum tuarum) is a Vulgate-distinct expansion absent from the standard Septuagint text.
- **36:2** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 36:2 (continuing 36:1; together native 36:1-2 correspond to Saints.Bible 36:1, while native 36:3 maps to Saints.Bible 36:2).
- **36:6** — Latin immuta literally means "change" or "alter," following the Greek rendering of a Hebrew verb that can mean either "repeat" or "make different," here conveying the sense of working fresh, varied wonders.
- **36:7** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 36:7 (parallel to Saints/Greek 36:5b).
- **36:9** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 36:9 (parallel to Saints/Greek 36:6b).
- **36:16** — "Your inexpressible words" (inenarrabilibus verbis tuis, lit. words beyond recounting) is a distinctly Vulgate reading where the Greek has "the recital of Your praises"; the Latin also reads "Your people" (populum tuum) where many Greek witnesses read "Your temple."
- **36:19** — Both "and direct us into the way of righteousness" (et dirige nos in viam justitiae) and the divine title "watcher of the ages" (conspector saeculorum) are distinctive Vulgate elements beyond the standard Greek "God of the ages."
- **36:25** — The Vulgate expands the Greek ("if on her tongue there is mercy and gentleness") into "if there is a tongue of healing, it is also of soothing and mercy" (lingua curationis, est et mitigationis et misericordiae).
