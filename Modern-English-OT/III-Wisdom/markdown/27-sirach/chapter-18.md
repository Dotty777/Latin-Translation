## Text

**18:1** He who will live forever created all things together. God alone will be justified, and He remains an unconquered king forever.

**18:2** Who is sufficient to recount His works?

**18:3** For who will search out His mighty deeds?

**18:4** And who will declare the power of His greatness? Or who will go on to recount His mercy?

**18:5** It is not possible to diminish or to add, nor is it possible to find out the mighty deeds of God.

**18:6** When a man has finished, then he will begin; and when he has rested, he will be perplexed.

**18:7** What is man, and what is his grace? And what is his good, or what is his wickedness?

**18:8** The number of the days of men, at most one hundred years, has been reckoned like a drop of water from the sea; and like a pebble of sand, so are a few years in the day of eternity.

**18:9** Because of this, God is patient toward them, and He pours out His mercy upon them.

**18:10** He saw the presumption of their heart, that it is evil; and He knew their ruin, that it is wicked.

**18:11** Therefore He filled up His propitiation in them and showed them the way of equity.

**18:12** The compassion of a man is toward his neighbor, but the mercy of God is upon all flesh.

**18:13** He who has mercy teaches and instructs, like a shepherd his flock.

**18:14** He has compassion on the one who receives the teaching of compassion, and on the one who hastens in His judgments.

**18:15** Son, in good things do not give a complaint, and in every gift do not give the sadness of an evil word.

**18:16** Will not dew cool the scorching heat? So also is a word better than a gift.

**18:17** Look, is not a word above a good gift? Yet both are with a justified man.

**18:18** A fool will harshly reproach, and the gift of an undisciplined man makes the eyes waste away.

**18:19** Before judgment prepare righteousness for yourself, and before you speak, learn.

**18:20** Before sickness apply medicine, and before judgment question yourself, and in the sight of God you will find forgiveness.

**18:21** Before sickness humble yourself, and in the time of infirmity show your way of life.

**18:22** Do not be hindered from praying always, and do not be afraid to be justified until death, for the reward of God remains forever.

**18:23** Before prayer prepare your soul, and do not be like a man who tests God.

**18:24** Remember wrath in the day of consummation, and the time of retribution in the turning of the face.

**18:25** Remember poverty in the time of abundance, and the needs of poverty in the day of riches.

**18:26** From morning until evening the time will be changed, and all these things are swift in the eyes of God.

**18:27** A wise man will fear in all things, and in the days of offenses he will be on guard against sloth.

**18:28** Every astute man recognizes wisdom, and to the one who finds her he will give praise.

**18:29** Those who are discerning in words have themselves also acted wisely, and have understood truth and righteousness, and have rained down proverbs and judgments.

**18:30** Do not go after your desires, and turn away from your will.

**18:31** If you grant your soul its desires, it will make you a joy to your enemies.

**18:32** Do not take delight in crowds, nor in small ones, for their conflict is continual.

**18:33** Do not become poor in contention from borrowing at interest when you have nothing in your purse, for you will be envious of your own life.

## Translator Notes

- **18:1** — The Clementine Vulgate reads the future tense vivet ("will live") where the Greek has a present participle ("who lives"), and adds the closing clause "and He remains an unconquered king forever."
- **18:7** — Latin gratia ("grace") is a Vulgate-distinct reading where the Greek has chresis ("use" or "worth"), likely reflecting an Old Latin rendering of the similar Greek word charis.
- **18:11** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 18:11 (parallel to Saints/Greek 18:12b).
- **18:13** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 18:13 (parallel to Saints/Greek 18:13b).
- **18:22** — Latin orare semper ("praying always") reflects an Old Latin rendering of Greek euche as "prayer" rather than "vow" (as also in v. 23), and the final clause "for the reward of God remains forever" is a Vulgate addition.
- **18:24** — All five Sixto-Clementine Vulgate witnesses read 'conversatione' here as a late-Latin variant of 'conversione' ("turning", rendering Greek apostrophe, "turning away of the face"; VulgSistina 1590 prints 'facies'), whereas in 18:21 'conversationem' carries its usual Latin sense of "way of life, conduct."
- **18:32** — Latin in turbis ... commissio ("in crowds ... conflict") is a Vulgate-distinct rendering where the Greek warns against delighting in great luxury and its expense (symbole, which can mean either "contribution" or "clash, encounter").
- **18:33** — Latin mediocris here carries the Late Latin sense "poor, of meager means" (rendering Greek ptochos), and in contentione ("in contention") reflects a Latin rendering of Greek symbolokopon ("feasting, carousing"); the final clause is a Vulgate addition.
