## Text

**34:1** Empty hope and falsehood belong to a senseless man, and dreams lift up the imprudent.

**34:2** Like one who grasps a shadow and pursues the wind, so too is the one who pays attention to false visions.

**34:3** This according to that is the vision of dreams: before the face of a person is the likeness of a person.

**34:4** From the unclean, what will be made clean? And from a liar, what truth will be spoken?

**34:5** Divination of error, and false omens, and the dreams of evildoers are emptiness.

**34:6** And like that of a woman in labor, your heart suffers fantasies. Unless a visitation has been sent out from the Most High, do not give your heart to them.

**34:7** For dreams have caused many to go astray, and those who hope in them have fallen.

**34:8** Without falsehood the word of the law will be fulfilled, and wisdom in the mouth of a faithful person will be made plain.

**34:9** The one who has not been tested—what does he know? A man experienced in many things will ponder many things, and the one who has learned many things will declare understanding.

**34:10** The one who is not experienced recognizes few things; but the one who has been in many things multiplies malice.

**34:11** The one who has not been tested, what sort of things does he know? He who has wandered will abound in wickedness.

**34:12** I have seen many things while wandering, and very many usages of words.

**34:13** Several times I was in danger even to death because of these things, and I was delivered by the grace of God.

**34:14** The spirit of those who fear God is sought, and in His regard it will be blessed.

**34:15** For their hope is in the One who saves them, and the eyes of God are upon those who love Him.

**34:16** The one who fears the Lord will tremble at nothing and will not be terrified, because He Himself is his hope.

**34:17** Blessed is the soul of the one who fears the Lord.

**34:18** To whom does he look, and who is his strength?

**34:19** The eyes of the Lord are upon those who fear Him: a mighty protector, a support of strength, a shelter from heat, and a shade at midday,

**34:20** a warding off of stumbling and a help against falling, exalting the soul and enlightening the eyes, giving health, and life, and blessing.

**34:21** The offering of one who sacrifices from what is unjust is blemished, and the mockeries of the unrighteous are not well-pleasing.

**34:22** The Lord is solely for those who wait for Him in the way of truth and righteousness.

**34:23** The Most High does not approve the gifts of the unjust, nor does He look upon the offerings of the unjust, nor by the multitude of their sacrifices will He forgive sins.

**34:24** The one who offers a sacrifice from the goods of the poor is like one who slaughters a son in the sight of his father.

**34:25** The bread of the needy is the life of the poor; the one who takes it by fraud is a man of blood.

**34:26** The one who takes away bread earned in sweat is like one who kills his neighbor.

**34:27** The one who sheds blood and the one who commits fraud against a hired worker are brothers.

**34:28** One building, and one tearing down: what does it profit them, except labor?

**34:29** One praying, and one cursing: whose voice will God hear?

**34:30** The one who is washed from a dead body and touches it again: what does his washing profit?

**34:31** So is a person who fasts in his sins and again does the same things: what does he profit by humbling himself? Who will hear his prayer?

## Translator Notes

- **34:3** — Latin "Hoc secundum hoc" (lit. "this according to that") captures the mirror-like reflection of dreams, corresponding to a person's likeness before their face.
- **34:10** — Vulgate "malitiam" literally means "malice" (or "craftiness"), translating Greek "panourgia" ("shrewdness, cleverness"), and "in multis factus est" renders a clause where Greek speaks of one who has wandered.
- **34:11** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 34:11 (parallel to Saints/Greek 34:10b); Late Latin 'implanatus' renders Greek 'peplanemenos' ("having wandered / been led astray").
- **34:12** — Vulgate "et plurimas verborum consuetudines" ("and very many usages of words") is a distinctive reading where the Greek Septuagint has "and my understanding is greater than my words."
- **34:14** — Vulgate reads "quaeritur" ("is sought") and "in respectu illius benedicetur" ("in His regard it will be blessed"), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads "will live, for their hope is in the one who saves them."
- **34:15** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 34:15 (parallel to Saints/Greek 34:13b).
- **34:18** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 34:18 (parallel to Saints/Greek 34:15b).
- **34:22** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 34:22 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion between 34:21 and 34:23).
- **34:30** — Latin "baptizatur a mortuo" refers to ritual washing after corpse-defilement (cf. Numbers 19), preserving the Greek loanword "baptizo" in its ceremonial sense.
