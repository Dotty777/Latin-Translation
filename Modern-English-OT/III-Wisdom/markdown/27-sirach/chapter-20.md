## Text

**20:1** How good it is to rebuke rather than to be angry, and not to hinder the one who confesses in prayer!

**20:2** The lust of a eunuch will deflower a young woman;

**20:3** so is the one who executes an unjust judgment by force.

**20:4** How good it is for one who has been reproved to display repentance! For in this way you will escape voluntary sin.

**20:5** There is one who keeps silent who is found to be wise, and there is a detestable person who is brash in speaking.

**20:6** There is one who keeps silent, not having understanding of speech, and there is one who keeps silent, knowing the proper time.

**20:7** A wise man will keep silent until the time, but the unrestrained and the imprudent will not observe the time.

**20:8** The one who uses many words will harm his own soul, and the one who takes authority for himself unjustly will be hated.

**20:9** There is advancement in evils for an undisciplined man, and there is a finding leading to loss.

**20:10** There is a gift that is not useful, and there is a gift whose repayment is double.

**20:11** There is diminishment because of glory, and there is one who will lift up his head from lowliness.

**20:12** There is one who buys many things at a small price and pays them back sevenfold.

**20:13** A wise man makes himself lovable in words, but the favors of fools will be poured out.

**20:14** The gift of a foolish person will not be useful to you, for his eyes are sevenfold.

**20:15** He will give little and reproach much, and the opening of his mouth is a blaze.

**20:16** Today someone lends at interest, and tomorrow he demands it back; a man of this kind is detestable.

**20:17** A fool will have no friend, and there will be no gratitude for his good things.

**20:18** For those who eat his bread are of a false tongue. How often and how many will mock him!

**20:19** For he did not distribute with right understanding what was to be held, and likewise what was not to be held.

**20:20** The slip of a false tongue is like one falling on a pavement; so the fall of evil people will come swiftly.

**20:21** An ungracious man is like an empty tale; it will be continually in the mouth of the undisciplined.

**20:22** From the mouth of a fool a proverb will be rejected, for he does not speak it in its own time.

**20:23** There is one who is prevented from sinning because of poverty, and in his rest he will be stung.

**20:24** There is one who will destroy his own soul because of shame, and because of an imprudent person he will destroy it, and by partiality toward a person he will destroy himself.

**20:25** There is one who because of shame makes a promise to a friend and has gained him as an enemy for nothing.

**20:26** A lie is a wicked reproach in a man, and it will be continually in the mouth of the undisciplined.

**20:27** Better is a thief than the persistence of a lying man, yet both will inherit destruction.

**20:28** The ways of lying men are without honor, and their shame is with them without ceasing.

**20:29** A wise man will advance himself in words, and a prudent man will please the great.

**20:30** The one who works his land will raise high a heap of crops, and the one who works righteousness will himself be exalted, and the one who pleases the great will escape iniquity.

**20:31** Presents and gifts blind the eyes of judges, and like a mute person, it turns aside their reproofs in the mouth.

**20:32** Hidden wisdom and an unseen treasure: what usefulness is in both?

**20:33** Better is the one who conceals his foolishness than a man who hides his wisdom.

## Translator Notes

- **20:2** — The Vulgate uses a finite future verb, "will deflower" (devirginabit), where the Greek has an infinitive introducing the simile ("Like a eunuch's desire to deflower a young woman, so is...").
- **20:4** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 20:4 (parallel to Saints/Greek 20:3).
- **20:16** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 20:16 (parallel to Saints/Greek 20:15b).
- **20:19** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 20:19 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion following 20:18 = Saints 20:17).
- **20:20** — The Vulgate likens "the slip of a false tongue" to falling on a pavement (Lapsus falsae linguae quasi qui in pavimento cadens), whereas the Greek contrasts them ("a slip on the pavement is better than one of the tongue").
- **20:21** — The Vulgate retains the untranslated Greek loanword acharis ("ungracious" or "graceless") and reads "an empty tale" (fabula vana) where the Greek has "an untimely tale."
- **20:23** — The Vulgate omits the negation present in the Greek ("will not be pricked by remorse"), reading affirmatively that "in his rest he will be stung" (stimulabitur).
- **20:31** — The Vulgate reads "like a mute person" (quasi mutus) with a singular verb (avertit), whereas the Greek has "like a muzzle (phimos) in the mouth, they turn aside reproofs."
