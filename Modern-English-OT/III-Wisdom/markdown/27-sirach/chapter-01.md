## Text

**1:1** The wisdom of many and great things has been shown to us through the law and the prophets and others who followed them, in which it is fitting to praise Israel for the sake of instruction and wisdom, because not only must the speakers themselves be skilled, but even outsiders can become most learned both by speaking and by writing. My grandfather Jesus, after he gave himself more fully to the diligent reading of the law and the prophets and the other books that were handed down to us by our forefathers, wished himself also to write something of these things that pertain to instruction and wisdom, so that those who desire to learn, having become skilled in them, may pay closer and closer attention in mind and be strengthened for a lawful life. Therefore I urge you to come with goodwill, and to read with more attentive zeal, and to grant indulgence in those places where we seem, while following the image of wisdom, to fall short in the phrasing of words. For Hebrew words fall short when they are translated into another language; and not only these, but even the law itself, and the prophets, and the rest of the other books have no small difference when they are spoken in themselves. For in the thirty-eighth year, in the times of King Ptolemy Euergetes, after I arrived in Egypt and had been there a long time, I found books left there of no small and no contemptible instruction. Therefore I thought it good and necessary for me myself also to add some diligence and labor in translating this book; and with much watchfulness I brought instruction over a span of time, to give this book for those things that lead to the end, and to those who wish to apply their mind and to learn how one ought to order conduct, who have resolved to live life according to the law of the Lord. All wisdom is from the Lord God, and was with Him always, and is before eternity.

**1:2** Who has counted the sand of the sea, and the drops of rain, and the days of the age? Who has measured the height of heaven, and the width of the earth, and the depth of the abyss?

**1:3** Who has searched out the wisdom of God, which precedes all things?

**1:4** Before all things wisdom was created, and understanding of prudence from eternity.

**1:5** The spring of wisdom is the word of God on high, and her approaches are eternal commandments.

**1:6** To whom has the root of wisdom been revealed? And who has recognized her subtleties?

**1:7** To whom has the instruction of wisdom been revealed and made plain? And who has understood the multiplicity of her approach?

**1:8** There is one Most High, almighty Creator, and a mighty King and exceedingly to be feared, sitting upon His throne, and a ruling God.

**1:9** He Himself created her in the Holy Spirit, and saw, and counted, and measured her,

**1:10** and poured her out upon all His works, and upon all flesh, according to His gift, and granted her to those who love Him.

**1:11** The fear of the Lord is glory, and glorying, and gladness, and a crown of exultation.

**1:12** The fear of the Lord will delight the heart, and will give gladness, and joy, and length of days.

**1:13** For the one who fears the Lord, it will be well at the end, and on the day of his death he will be blessed.

**1:14** The love of God is honorable wisdom,

**1:15** and those to whom she appears in a vision love her in the vision and in the knowledge of her great works.

**1:16** The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, and it was created together with the faithful in the womb; it walks with chosen women, and is recognized with the righteous and faithful.

**1:17** The fear of the Lord is the piety of knowledge;

**1:18** piety will guard and justify the heart; it will give delight and joy.

**1:19** For the one who fears the Lord, it will be well, and in the days of his completion he will be blessed.

**1:20** The fullness of wisdom is to fear God, and fullness is from her fruits.

**1:21** She will fill her entire house with produce, and the storerooms with her treasures.

**1:22** The crown of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, replenishing peace and the fruit of salvation.

**1:23** And He saw and counted her; moreover, both are gifts of God.

**1:24** Wisdom will share out knowledge and the understanding of prudence, and she exalts the glory of those who hold fast to her.

**1:25** The root of wisdom is to fear the Lord, and her branches are long-lived.

**1:26** In the treasuries of wisdom are understanding and the piety of knowledge, but to sinners wisdom is an abomination.

**1:27** The fear of the Lord drives out sin.

**1:28** For the one who is without fear will not be able to be justified; for the wrath of his temper is his downfall.

**1:29** For a time the patient one will endure, and afterward there is a return of delight.

**1:30** Good judgment will hide his words for a time, and the lips of many will recount his judgment.

**1:31** In the treasuries of wisdom is the indication of discipline,

**1:32** but to a sinner the worship of God is an abomination.

**1:33** Son, desiring wisdom, keep righteousness, and God will grant her to you.

**1:34** For the fear of the Lord is wisdom and discipline; and what is well-pleasing to Him

**1:35** is faith and gentleness, and He will fill his treasuries.

**1:36** Do not be distrustful of the fear of the Lord, and do not approach Him with a double heart.

**1:37** Do not be a hypocrite in the sight of men, and do not be made to stumble by your lips.

**1:38** Pay attention to them, or else you might fall, and bring dishonor upon your soul,

**1:39** and God might reveal your hidden things, and dash you down in the midst of the assembly,

**1:40** because you approached the Lord wickedly, and your heart is full of deceit and falsehood.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Stage 1 restored the Latin Translator's Prologue prefixed to Sirach 1:1 in Clem1598-USFX; Latin 'quando inter se dicuntur' (lit. "when they are spoken among/in themselves") refers to reading those scriptures in their original tongue. In the closing sentence, Vulgate "ante ævum" ("before eternity" or "before the age") is a distinct Latin reading where the Greek has "forever" ("eis ton aiōna"), and the Vulgate also adds "God" ("Deo") after "the Lord."
- **1:8** — The Vulgate expands this verse with "Most High, almighty Creator, and a mighty King" and "a ruling God," where the Greek reads simply "There is one who is wise, greatly to be feared, sitting upon His throne."
- **1:9** — The phrase "in the Holy Spirit" ("in Spiritu Sancto") is a distinct Vulgate reading not found in the Greek text.
- **1:14** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 1:14 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion without a separate Saints slot).
- **1:15** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 1:15, replacing the prior empty-alignment placeholder.
- **1:16** — Vulgate "it walks with chosen women" ("cum electis feminis graditur") is a distinct Latin reading diverging from the Greek (1:15), which speaks of Wisdom laying an eternal foundation among human beings.
- **1:17** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 1:17 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion); Latin 'religiositas' denotes religious devotion or piety (likewise in 1:18, 1:26).
- **1:18** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 1:18 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion).
- **1:19** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 1:19 (parallel doublet of 1:13, reading 'in diebus consummationis illius', "in the days of his completion/end", rather than 1:13's 'in die defunctionis suæ').
- **1:21** — Vulgate "a generationibus" ("with produce," literally "from yields/generations") reflects an Old Latin rendering of Greek "genēmata" ("produce"), and the Vulgate's wording here (native 1:21; Saints 1:17) and in native 1:20 (Saints 1:16, "fullness is from her fruits") differs from the Greek.
- **1:24** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 1:24 (parallel to Saints/Greek 1:19).
- **1:26** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 1:26 (Vulgate-distinct doublet ahead of 1:32 / Saints 1:25).
- **1:27** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 1:27 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion without a live Saints.Bible counterpart; absent from the short Greek text at 1:21).
