## Text

**1:1** All wisdom is from the Lord God, and was with Him always, and is before eternity.

**1:2** Who has counted the sand of the sea, and the drops of rain, and the days of the age? Who has measured the height of heaven, and the width of the earth, and the depth of the abyss?

**1:3** Who has searched out the wisdom of God, which precedes all things?

**1:4** Before all things wisdom was created, and understanding of prudence from eternity.

**1:6** To whom has the root of wisdom been revealed? And who has recognized her subtleties?

**1:8** There is one Most High, almighty Creator, and a mighty King and exceedingly to be feared, sitting upon His throne, and a ruling God.

**1:9** He Himself created her in the Holy Spirit, and saw, and counted, and measured her,

**1:10** and poured her out upon all His works, and upon all flesh, according to His gift, and granted her to those who love Him.

**1:11** The fear of the Lord is glory, and glorying, and gladness, and a crown of exultation.

**1:12** The fear of the Lord will delight the heart, and will give gladness, and joy, and length of days.

**1:13** For the one who fears the Lord, it will be well at the end, and on the day of his death he will be blessed.

**1:14** The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, and it was created together with the faithful in the womb; it walks with chosen women, and is recognized with the righteous and faithful.

**1:16** The fullness of wisdom is to fear God, and fullness is from her fruits.

**1:17** She will fill her entire house with produce, and the storerooms with her treasures.

**1:18** The crown of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, replenishing peace and the fruit of salvation.

**1:19** And He saw and counted her; moreover, both are gifts of God.

**1:20** The root of wisdom is to fear the Lord, and her branches are long-lived.

**1:22** For the one who is without fear will not be able to be justified; for the wrath of his temper is his downfall.

**1:23** For a time the patient one will endure, and afterward there is a return of delight.

**1:24** Good judgment will hide his words for a time, and the lips of many will recount his judgment.

**1:25** In the treasuries of wisdom is the indication of discipline, but to a sinner the worship of God is an abomination.

**1:26** Son, desiring wisdom, keep righteousness, and God will grant her to you.

**1:27** For the fear of the Lord is wisdom and discipline; and what is well-pleasing to Him is faith and gentleness, and He will fill his treasuries.

**1:28** Do not be distrustful of the fear of the Lord, and do not approach Him with a double heart.

**1:29** Do not be a hypocrite in the sight of men, and do not be made to stumble by your lips.

**1:30** Pay attention to them, or else you might fall, and bring dishonor upon your soul, and God might reveal your hidden things, and dash you down in the midst of the assembly, because you approached the Lord wickedly, and your heart is full of deceit and falsehood.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Vulgate "ante ævum" ("before eternity" or "before the age") is a distinct Latin reading where the Greek has "forever" ("eis ton aiōna"), and the Vulgate also adds "God" ("Deo") after "the Lord."
- **1:8** — The Vulgate expands this verse with "Most High, almighty Creator, and a mighty King" and "a ruling God," where the Greek reads simply "There is one who is wise, greatly to be feared, sitting upon His throne."
- **1:9** — The phrase "in the Holy Spirit" ("in Spiritu Sancto") is a distinct Vulgate reading not found in the Greek text.
- **1:14** — Vulgate "it walks with chosen women" ("cum electis feminis graditur") is a distinct Latin reading diverging from the Greek (1:15), which speaks of Wisdom laying an eternal foundation among human beings.
- **1:17** — Vulgate "a generationibus" ("with produce," literally "from yields/generations") reflects an Old Latin rendering of Greek "genēmata" ("produce"), and the Vulgate's wording here and in 1:16 ("fullness is from her fruits") differs from the Greek.
- **1:15 *(additional)*** — The Latin critical text contains no separate aligned Clementine Vulgate passage at this verse slot.
