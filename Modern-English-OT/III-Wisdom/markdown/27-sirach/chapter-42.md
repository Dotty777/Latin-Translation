## Text

**42:2** of the law of the Most High and the covenant, and of judgment to justify the ungodly,

**42:3** of the word of companions and travelers, and of the giving of the inheritance of friends,

**42:4** of the evenness of a balance and weights, of acquiring many things and few,

**42:5** of the corruption of buying and of merchants, and of much discipline of children, and of making the side of a very bad servant bleed.

**42:6** Over a wicked wife, a seal is good.

**42:7** Where there are many hands, lock up; and whatever you hand over, count and weigh; and write down everything given and received.

**42:8** Of the discipline of a senseless person and a fool, and of elderly men who are judged by young men; and you will be instructed in all things, and approved in the sight of all the living.

**42:9** A daughter is a father's hidden wakefulness, and anxiety over her takes away sleep: for fear that in her youth she may become full-grown, and when living with a husband she may become hated;

**42:10** for fear that at any time she may be defiled in her virginity and be found pregnant in her father's house; for fear that when living with a husband she may transgress, or indeed become barren.

**42:11** Over a dissolute daughter strengthen the watch, so that she may never cause you to come into disgrace before enemies, from slander in the city and the censure of the populace, and put you to shame in the multitude of the people.

**42:12** Do not look intently at any person for beauty, and do not stay in the midst of women;

**42:13** for from garments a moth comes forth, and from a woman, a man's wickedness.

**42:14** For better is a man's wickedness than a woman doing good, and a woman bringing shame into disgrace.

**42:15** Therefore I will be mindful of the works of the Lord, and what I have seen I will declare. In the words of the Lord are His works.

**42:16** The sun, giving light, has looked over all things, and His work is full of the glory of the Lord.

**42:17** Has not the Lord caused the holy ones to recount all His wonders, which the Lord Almighty has established to stand firm in His glory?

**42:18** He has searched out the abyss and the heart of people, and He has pondered their craftiness.

**42:19** For the Lord has known all knowledge, and has looked into the sign of the age, declaring what has passed and what is to come, revealing the traces of secret things.

**42:20** No thought passes Him by, and no word hides itself from Him.

**42:21** He has adorned the great works of His wisdom, He who is before the age and even forever; nor has anything been added.

**42:22** How desirable are all His works! And like a spark is what one can observe!

**42:23** All these things live and remain forever, and in every need all things obey Him.

**42:24** All things are twofold, one opposite the other, and He has made nothing lacking.

**42:25** He has established the good things of each one, and who will be satisfied seeing His glory?

## Translator Notes

- **42:2** — This verse continues the sentence begun at the end of 41:27 ('Do not be ashamed...'). The phrase 'of judgment to justify the ungodly' translates Latin 'de judicio justificare impium' literally and is debated, often understood as giving even an ungodly person a fair verdict when innocent.
- **42:8** — Vulgate 'senioribus qui judicantur ab adolescentibus' ('elderly men who are judged by young men') is a distinctively Latin reading where the Greek speaks of an aged man accused of or contending over sexual immorality.
- **42:13** — The Vulgate reads 'iniquitas viri' ('a man's wickedness'), whereas the Greek reads 'a woman's wickedness.'
- **42:17** — The Vulgate renders the Greek negative statement ('the Lord's holy ones have not been able to recount all His wonders') as a positive rhetorical question ('Nonne Dominus fecit...': 'Has not the Lord caused the holy ones to recount all His wonders...').
- **42:22** — Latin 'tamquam scintilla quæ est considerare' (lit. 'like a spark which is to consider/observe') reflects the Greek infinitive idiom 'it is possible to observe,' conveying that all one can observe of God's works is like a mere spark.
