## Text

**4:1** Son, do not defraud a poor man of his alms, and do not turn your eyes away from a poor man.

**4:2** Do not despise a hungry soul, and do not provoke a poor man in his need.

**4:3** Do not afflict the heart of a needy man, and do not delay a gift to one in distress.

**4:4** Do not reject the petition of the sorely afflicted, and do not turn your face away from a destitute man.

**4:5** Do not turn your eyes away from a needy man because of anger, and do not leave it to those who ask to curse you behind your back.

**4:6** For the supplication of one who curses you in the bitterness of his soul will be heard; and He who made him will hear him.

**4:7** Make yourself approachable to the congregation of the poor, and humble your soul before an elder, and bow your head before a great man.

**4:8** Incline your ear to a poor man without sadness, and pay your debt, and answer him with peaceful words in gentleness.

**4:9** Deliver the one who suffers wrong from the hand of the proud, and do not take it bitterly in your soul.

**4:10** In judging, be merciful to orphans like a father, and in place of a husband to their mother.

**4:11** And you will be like an obedient son of the Most High, and He will have mercy on you more than a mother.

**4:12** Wisdom breathes life into her children, and supports those who seek her, and will go ahead in the way of righteousness.

**4:13** And he who loves her loves life, and those who keep watch for her will embrace her delight.

**4:14** Those who hold fast to her will inherit life, and whatever place she enters, God will bless.

**4:15** Those who serve her will be obedient to the Holy One, and those who love her, God loves.

**4:16** He who hears her will judge the nations, and he who gazes upon her will remain confident.

**4:17** If he trusts her, he will inherit her, and his offspring will be in steadfastness,

**4:18** for in testing she walks with him, and at first she chooses him.

**4:19** She will bring fear and dread and testing upon him, and she will torment him in the tribulation of her instruction until she tests him in his thoughts and trusts his soul.

**4:20** And she will establish him, and will bring a straight path to him, and will make him glad.

**4:21** And she will lay bare her hidden things to him, and will treasure up upon him knowledge and the understanding of righteousness.

**4:22** But if he goes astray, she will abandon him, and will hand him over into the hands of his enemy.

**4:23** Son, observe the time, and turn aside from evil.

**4:24** For the sake of your soul, do not be ashamed to speak the truth.

**4:25** For there is a shame that brings sin, and there is a shame that brings glory and favor.

**4:26** Do not accept a face against your own face, nor a lie against your own soul.

**4:27** Do not defer to your neighbor in his fall.

**4:28** And do not hold back a word in a time of salvation. Do not hide your wisdom in its beauty;

**4:29** for in the tongue wisdom is recognized, and understanding, and knowledge, and instruction in the word of a man of understanding, and firmness in the works of righteousness.

**4:30** Do not contradict the word of truth in any way, and be ashamed of the lie of your ignorance.

**4:31** Do not be ashamed to confess your sins, and do not subject yourself to every man on account of sin.

**4:32** Do not resist against the face of a powerful man, and do not strive against the force of a river.

**4:33** Contend for righteousness on behalf of your soul, and fight for righteousness even to death, and God will conquer your enemies for you.

**4:34** Do not be hasty in your tongue, and useless and slack in your works.

**4:35** Do not be like a lion in your house, overturning those of your household, and oppressing those subject to you.

**4:36** Do not let your hand be stretched out for receiving, and drawn back for giving.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — Vulgate reads eleemosynam pauperis ("a poor man of his alms"), whereas the Greek reads "the livelihood of the poor."
- **4:5** — Vulgate adds propter iram ("because of anger") and renders the second clause literally as non relinquas quaerentibus tibi retro maledicere ("do not leave it to those who ask to curse you behind your back").
- **4:11** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 4:11 (continuing 4:10 = Saints 4:10).
- **4:12** — Vulgate reads vitam inspirat ("breathes life into") and adds et praeibit in via justitiae ("and will go ahead in the way of righteousness"), whereas the Greek reads "Wisdom exalts her sons."
- **4:17** — Latin creaturae illius (literally "his creatures/offspring," rendering Greek geneai, "descendants") and in confirmatione ("in steadfastness/firm establishment") reflect Late Latin usage; in native 4:18 (Saints 4:17) Vulgate reads "in testing she walks with him, and at first she chooses him," whereas the Greek speaks of walking on crooked paths.
- **4:19** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 4:19 (parallel to Saints/Greek 4:17).
- **4:21** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 4:21 (continuing 4:20 = Saints 4:18).
- **4:24** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 4:24 (parallel to Saints/Greek 4:20b).
- **4:26** — Latin accipere faciem is a literal biblical idiom for showing partiality or deference, here governing both faciem ("face") and mendacium ("lie"); Vulgate reads "against your own face" and "a lie," whereas the Greek reads "against your own soul" and "to your downfall."
- **4:27** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 4:27 (parallel to Saints/Greek 4:22b, continuing into 4:28).
