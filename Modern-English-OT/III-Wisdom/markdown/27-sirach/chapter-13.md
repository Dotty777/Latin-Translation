## Text

**13:1** Whoever touches pitch will be stained by it, and whoever associates with a proud man will put on pride.

**13:2** Whoever associates with one more honorable than himself takes a burden upon himself; and do not be a companion to one richer than yourself.

**13:3** What fellowship will a kettle have with an earthen pot? For when they strike against each other, it will be shattered.

**13:4** A rich man has acted unjustly, and he will rage; but a poor man, when wronged, will keep silent.

**13:5** If you give generously, he will take you in; and if you have nothing, he will abandon you.

**13:6** If you have anything, he will live with you and empty you out, and he himself will not grieve over you.

**13:7** If you are necessary to him, he will trip you up, and smiling, he will give hope, telling you good things, and he will say, "What do you need?"

**13:8** And he will put you to shame with his meals, until he drains you empty twice and three times; and in the end he will mock you, and afterward, seeing you, he will abandon you and shake his head at you.

**13:9** Humble yourself to God, and wait for His hands.

**13:10** Take care that you are not led astray into folly and humbled.

**13:11** Do not be humble in your wisdom, so that, being humbled, you are not led astray into folly.

**13:12** When invited by a more powerful man, withdraw; for because of this he will invite you all the more.

**13:13** Do not be intrusive, so that you may not be pushed back; and do not stay far from him, so that you may not pass into oblivion.

**13:14** Do not insist on speaking on equal terms with him, nor trust his many words; for through much talking he will test you, and smiling, he will question you about your secrets.

**13:15** His cruel mind will store up your words, and he will not hold back from malice and from chains.

**13:16** Be on your guard, and pay careful attention to your hearing, because you walk with your own overthrow.

**13:17** And hearing those things, see as in a dream, and you will be watchful.

**13:18** All your life love God, and call upon Him for your salvation.

**13:19** Every animal loves what is like itself, and so too every man loves the one nearest to him.

**13:20** All flesh will be joined to its like, and every man will become a companion to one like himself.

**13:21** If a wolf will ever associate with a lamb, so will a sinner with a righteous man.

**13:22** What association does a holy man have with a dog? Or what share does a rich man have with a poor man?

**13:23** A lion's prey is the wild donkey in the wilderness; so too the pastures of the rich are the poor.

**13:24** And just as humility is an abomination to a proud man, so also a poor man is the abhorrence of a rich man.

**13:25** A rich man, when shaken, is steadied by his friends; but a humble man, when he falls, will be driven away even by his acquaintances.

**13:26** For a rich man who is deceived, there are many rescuers; he has spoken proud things, and they have justified him.

**13:27** A humble man has been deceived, and moreover he is rebuked; he has spoken sensibly, and no place has been given to him.

**13:28** A rich man has spoken, and all have kept silent, and they will carry his word all the way to the clouds.

**13:29** A poor man has spoken, and they say, "Who is this?" And if he stumbles, they will overthrow him.

**13:30** Wealth is good in which there is no sin on the conscience, and poverty is most wicked in the mouth of an ungodly man.

**13:31** A man's heart changes his face, whether for good or for evil.

**13:32** You will find the trace of a good heart and a good face with difficulty, and with labor.

## Translator Notes

- **13:2** — Vulgate reads a positive clause ('Pondus super se tollat', literally "would/let him take a burden upon himself"), whereas the Greek has a negative command ("Do not lift a burden beyond yourself").
- **13:3** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 13:3 (parallel to Saints/Greek 13:2b).
- **13:9** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 13:9 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion before 13:10 = Saints 13:8).
- **13:11** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 13:11 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion following 13:10 = Saints 13:8).
- **13:14** — Latin 'Ne retineas' renders Greek 'me epeche' and here carries the sense "do not insist on" or "do not persist in" rather than "do not hold back from."
- **13:17** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 13:17 (continuing 13:16 = Saints 13:13).
- **13:18** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 13:18 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion at Saints 13:14).
- **13:22** — Vulgate 'sancto homini ad canem' ("a holy man with a dog") and 'pars' ("share") are distinctly Vulgate readings where the Greek has "a hyena with a dog" and "peace."
- **13:32** — Vulgate compresses the two clauses of the Greek (which link a cheerful face to a happy heart and speak of the toil of discovering proverbs) into a single statement about the difficulty of finding the trace of a good heart and a good face.
