## Text

**32:1** Have they appointed you leader? Do not exalt yourself; be among them like one of them.

**32:2** Take care of them, and then sit down; and when all your care has been completed, recline.

**32:3** Speak, you who are older, for it is fitting for you.

**32:4** Where there is no listening, do not pour out speech, and do not exalt yourself at an unsuitable time in your wisdom.

**32:5** A small carbuncle gem in an ornament of gold, so is a concert of musicians at a banquet of wine.

**32:6** Just as in gold workmanship there is a seal of emerald, so is the melody of musicians with pleasant and moderate wine.

**32:7** Young man, scarcely speak in your own cause.

**32:8** If you have been questioned twice, let your answer have a main point.

**32:9** In the midst of great men do not presume, and where elders are, do not speak much.

**32:10** Before hail, lightning will go ahead; and before modesty, favor will go ahead, and for reverence, good favor will come to you.

**32:11** And at the hour of rising do not linger; but run ahead first to your house, and there relax, and there play,

**32:12** and act on your thoughts, and not in offenses and a proud word;

**32:13** and above all these things bless the Lord, who created, and who fills you to overflowing from all His good things.

**32:14** He who fears the Lord will receive His instruction, and those who keep watch for Him will find blessing.

**32:15** He who seeks the law will be filled by it, and he who acts deceitfully will stumble in it.

**32:16** Those who fear the Lord will find righteous judgment, and they will kindle righteous deeds like a light.

**32:17** A sinful person will avoid correction, and according to his will he will find a comparison.

**32:18** A man of counsel will not destroy understanding; a stranger and a proud man will not dread fear.

**32:19** Son, do nothing without counsel, and after the deed you will not regret it.

**32:20** Do not go on the way of ruin, and you will not stumble against stones; nor entrust yourself to a toilsome way, so that you may not set a stumbling block for your soul.

**32:22** And beware of your children, and watch out for those of your household.

**32:23** In all your work, trust your soul in good faith, for this is the keeping of the commandments.

**32:24** He who believes God heeds the commandments, and he who trusts in Him will not be diminished.

## Translator Notes

- **32:8** — Latin habeat caput responsum tuum is literally "let your answer have a head," using caput in the rhetorical sense of a concise main point or summary.
- **32:13** — In this Latin text qui fecit lacks an explicit object (some Vulgate editions include te, "you"); inebriantem literally means "making drunk" or "saturating," used figuratively for filling to overflowing.
- **32:20** — Vulgate-distinct reading viae laboriosae ("a toilsome way"), where the Greek reads "an unobstructed road."
- **32:23** — Vulgate adds ex fide ("in good faith" or "from faith"); animae tuae can be read as dative ("trust your soul in good faith," matching credere with the dative in 32:20 and 32:24) or as genitive ("believe from the faith of your soul").
- **32:24** — Vulgate-distinct reading Qui credit Deo ("He who believes God"), where the Greek reads "He who trusts the law."
