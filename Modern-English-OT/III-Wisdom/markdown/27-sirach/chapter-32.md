## Text

**32:1** Have they appointed you leader? Do not exalt yourself; be among them like one of them.

**32:2** Take care of them, and then sit down; and when all your care has been completed, recline.

**32:3** so that you may rejoice on their account, and receive a crown as an ornament of grace, and obtain the esteem of the gathering.

**32:4** Speak, you who are older, for it is fitting for you.

**32:5** the first word with careful knowledge, and do not hinder the music.

**32:6** Where there is no listening, do not pour out speech, and do not exalt yourself at an unsuitable time in your wisdom.

**32:7** A small carbuncle gem in an ornament of gold, so is a concert of musicians at a banquet of wine.

**32:8** Just as in gold workmanship there is a seal of emerald, so is the melody of musicians with pleasant and moderate wine.

**32:9** Listen in silence, and for reverence good favor will come to you.

**32:10** Young man, scarcely speak in your own cause.

**32:11** If you have been questioned twice, let your answer have a main point.

**32:12** In many things be as though ignorant, and listen while keeping silent and inquiring at the same time.

**32:13** In the midst of great men do not presume, and where elders are, do not speak much.

**32:14** Before hail, lightning will go ahead; and before modesty, favor will go ahead, and for reverence, good favor will come to you.

**32:15** And at the hour of rising do not linger; but run ahead first to your house, and there relax, and there play,

**32:16** and act on your thoughts, and not in offenses and a proud word;

**32:17** and above all these things bless the Lord, who created, and who fills you to overflowing from all His good things.

**32:18** He who fears the Lord will receive His instruction, and those who keep watch for Him will find blessing.

**32:19** He who seeks the law will be filled by it, and he who acts deceitfully will stumble in it.

**32:20** Those who fear the Lord will find righteous judgment, and they will kindle righteous deeds like a light.

**32:21** A sinful person will avoid correction, and according to his will he will find a comparison.

**32:22** A man of counsel will not destroy understanding; a stranger and a proud man will not dread fear.

**32:23** even after he has acted with him without counsel, and he will be convicted by his own pursuits.

**32:24** Son, do nothing without counsel, and after the deed you will not regret it.

**32:25** Do not go on the way of ruin, and you will not stumble against stones; nor entrust yourself to a toilsome way, so that you may not set a stumbling block for your soul.

**32:26** And beware of your children, and watch out for those of your household.

**32:27** In all your work, trust your soul in good faith, for this is the keeping of the commandments.

**32:28** He who believes God heeds the commandments, and he who trusts in Him will not be diminished.

## Translator Notes

- **32:3** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 32:3 (parallel to Saints/Greek 32:2b); Late Latin 'corrogatio' denotes the invited banquet gathering.
- **32:5** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 32:5 (continuing 32:4 = Saints 32:3).
- **32:9** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 32:9 (shifted_and_changed item; Vulgate/LXX-II verse distinct from 32:14 = Saints 32:10).
- **32:11** — Latin habeat caput responsum tuum is literally "let your answer have a head," using caput in the rhetorical sense of a concise main point or summary.
- **32:12** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 32:12 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion following 32:11 = Saints 32:8).
- **32:17** — Clem1598-USFX and Clem-Saints read 'qui fecit' without an explicit object, whereas VulgHetzenauer (1906), VulgConte (2009), and VulgSistina (1590) include 'te' ("who made you"); 'inebriantem' literally means "making drunk" or "saturating," used figuratively for filling to overflowing.
- **32:23** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 32:23 (continuing 32:22 = Saints 32:18); Latin 'insectationibus' denotes pursuits, attacks, or followings.
- **32:25** — Vulgate-distinct reading viae laboriosae ("a toilsome way"), where the Greek reads "an unobstructed road."
- **32:27** — Vulgate adds ex fide ("in good faith" or "from faith"); animae tuae can be read as dative ("trust your soul in good faith," matching credere with the dative in native 32:25 and 32:28 / Saints 32:20 and 32:24) or as genitive ("believe from the faith of your soul").
- **32:28** — Vulgate-distinct reading Qui credit Deo ("He who believes God"), where the Greek reads "He who trusts the law."
