## Text

**35:1** He who keeps the law multiplies the offering.

**35:2** It is a saving sacrifice to heed the commandments and to depart from all iniquity.

**35:3** And to offer a propitiatory sacrifice over injustices, and a plea for sins, is to withdraw from injustice.

**35:4** He who offers fine flour will return thanks, and he who shows mercy offers a sacrifice.

**35:5** It is well-pleasing to the Lord to depart from iniquity, and it is a supplication for sins to depart from injustice.

**35:6** You shall not appear before the presence of the Lord empty.

**35:7** For all these things are done because of the commandment of God.

**35:8** The offering of the righteous one fattens the altar and is a fragrance of sweetness in the sight of the Most High.

**35:9** The sacrifice of the righteous one is acceptable, and the Lord will not forget its memorial.

**35:10** With a good spirit render glory to God, and do not diminish the firstfruits of your hands.

**35:11** In every gift make your face cheerful, and with exultation sanctify your tithes.

**35:12** Give to the Most High according to His gift, and with a good eye present the gain of your hands,

**35:13** for the Lord is one who repays, and He will give back to you seven times as much.

**35:14** Do not offer corrupt gifts, for He will not accept them.

**35:15** And do not look to an unjust sacrifice, for the Lord is a judge, and there is no glory of person with Him.

**35:16** The Lord will not favor a person against a poor man, and He will hear the supplication of the injured one.

**35:17** He will not despise the prayers of the orphan, nor the widow, if she pours out an utterance of groaning.

**35:18** Do the tears of the widow not descend to the cheek, and is her outcry not against the one who brings them down?

**35:19** For from the cheek they ascend even to heaven, and the Lord who hears will not take delight in them.

**35:20** He who worships God with delight will be accepted, and his supplication will draw near even to the clouds.

**35:21** The prayer of the one who humbles himself will pierce the clouds; and until it draws near, it will not be comforted, and it will not depart until the Most High looks upon it.

**35:22** And the Lord will not delay, and He will judge the righteous and execute judgment; and the Most Mighty will not have patience with them, so that He may crush their back;

**35:23** and He will render vengeance to the nations, until He takes away the fullness of the proud and crushes the scepters of the wicked;

**35:24** until He renders to people according to their deeds, and according to the works of Adam, and according to his presumption;

**35:25** until He judges the judgment of His people, and He will delight the righteous with His mercy.

**35:26** Beautiful is the mercy of God in a time of tribulation, like clouds of rain in a time of drought.

## Translator Notes

- **35:2** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 35:2 (parallel to Saints/Greek 35:1b).
- **35:3** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 35:3 (parallel to Saints/Greek 35:3); Latin 'propitiationem litare sacrificii' literally reads "to offer favorably a propitiation of sacrifice" (VulgSistina 1590 reads 'sacrificiis').
- **35:4** — The Vulgate makes "he who offers fine flour" the subject of the future verb "will return thanks" (Retribuet gratiam, also "repay a favor"), inverting the Greek sense in which returning a kindness is likened to offering fine flour.
- **35:7** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 35:7 (continuing 35:6 = Saints 35:4).
- **35:12** — "With a good eye" (in bono oculo) is a biblical idiom for generosity. The following phrase literally reads "do/make the finding of your hands" (adinventionem facito manuum tuarum), where adinventio renders Greek heurema (what one's hands have gained) and facito carries the sacrificial sense of offering or presenting.
- **35:14** — "Glory of person" (gloria personae, in native 35:15 = Saints 35:12b) is a literal rendering of the biblical idiom for outward prestige, status, or partiality (paired with "favor a person" in native 35:16 / Saints 35:13).
- **35:15** — "Glory of person" (gloria personae) is a literal rendering of the biblical idiom for outward prestige, status, or partiality (paired with "favor a person" in 35:16).
- **35:19** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 35:19 (continuing 35:18 = Saints 35:15); Latin plural 'in illis' ("in them") grammatically refers back to the widow's tears ('lacrimæ' in 35:18, expressing God's displeasure at her affliction), whereas 'deducentem' in 35:18 is singular.
- **35:22** — A distinctly Vulgate reading ("so that He may crush their back," ut contribulet dorsum ipsorum) where the Greek reads "until He crushes the loins of the merciless"; the Latin also takes "the righteous" (justos) as the direct object of "judge."
- **35:24** — A notable Vulgate-distinct reading that renders Hebrew/generic "man" as the proper name "Adam" (Adae), followed by the singular "his presumption" (praesumptionem illius), where the Greek reads "the works of men according to their intentions."
