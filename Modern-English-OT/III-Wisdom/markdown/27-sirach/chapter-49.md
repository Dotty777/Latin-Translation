## Text

**49:1** The memory of Josiah is made into a blend of fragrance, the work of a perfumer.

**49:2** He himself was divinely directed to the repentance of the nation, and he took away the abominations of impiety.

**49:3** And he guided his heart to the Lord, and in the days of sinners he strengthened piety.

**49:4** Except for David and Hezekiah and Josiah, all committed sin.

**49:5** For they gave their own kingdom to others, and their own glory to a foreign nation.

**49:6** They burned the chosen city of holiness and made its streets desolate, by the hand of Jeremiah.

**49:7** For they mistreated him who from his mother's womb was consecrated a prophet, to overthrow, and to uproot, and to destroy, and again to build and to renew.

**49:8** Ezekiel, who saw the vision of glory that He showed him on the chariot of the cherubim.

**49:9** For he made mention of the enemies in the rain, to do good to those who showed the right ways.

**49:10** And may the bones of the twelve prophets sprout from their place; for they strengthened Jacob, and they redeemed themselves in the faith of strength.

**49:11** How shall we magnify Zerubbabel? For he also is like a signet on the right hand,

**49:12** so also Jeshua, son of Jozadak, who in their days built the house and raised up a holy temple to the Lord, prepared for everlasting glory.

**49:13** And Nehemiah is in memory for a long time, who raised up for us the overthrown walls, and made the gates and bars stand, who raised up our houses.

**49:14** No one has been born on earth such as Enoch, for he himself was also received up from the earth;

**49:15** nor like Joseph, who was born a man, prince of his brothers, support of the nation, ruler of his brothers, mainstay of the people; and his bones were visited, and after death they prophesied.

**49:16** Seth and Shem attained glory among men, and above every soul in origin is Adam.

## Translator Notes

- **49:1** — The Clementine Vulgate ends verse 1 after 'the work of a perfumer,' omitting the Greek lines comparing Josiah's memory to honey and banquet music.
- **49:9** — Following the Greek (which misread Hebrew 'Job' as 'enemies'), Vulgate reads 'the enemies in the rain' and renders the closing clause 'who showed the right ways' (Greek: 'who kept straight ways').
- **49:10** — Vulgate reads reflexive 'redeemed themselves in the faith of strength' (redemerunt se in fide virtutis), whereas the Greek has 'delivered them by confident hope.'
- **49:15** — Vulgate doubles the Greek epithets for Joseph ('prince of his brothers, support of the nation, ruler of his brothers, mainstay of the people') and adds 'and after death they prophesied,' absent from the standard Greek.
- **49:16** — Vulgate reverses the Greek order ('Shem and Seth') to 'Seth and Shem' and renders Greek 'in creation' as in origine ('in origin').
