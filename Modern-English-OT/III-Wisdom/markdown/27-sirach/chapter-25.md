## Text

**25:1** In three things my spirit has found pleasure, which are approved before God and people:

**25:2** the harmony of brothers, and the love of neighbors, and a husband and wife who agree well with each other.

**25:3** My soul hates three kinds, and I am greatly burdened by their life:

**25:4** a proud poor man, a lying rich man, and an old man who is foolish and senseless.

**25:5** What you have not gathered in your youth, how will you find it in your old age?

**25:6** How beautiful is judgment for gray hair, and for elders to know counsel!

**25:7** How beautiful is wisdom for the aged, and glorious are understanding and counsel!

**25:8** The crown of old men is much experience, and their glory is the fear of God.

**25:9** I have magnified nine unsuspected things of the heart, and the tenth I will speak to people with my tongue:

**25:10** a man who delights in his children, living and seeing the downfall of his enemies.

**25:11** Blessed is he who lives with a sensible woman, and who has not slipped with his tongue, and who has not served those unworthy of him.

**25:12** Blessed is he who finds a true friend, and who declares righteousness to a listening ear.

**25:13** How great is he who finds wisdom and knowledge! Yet he is not above the one who fears the Lord.

**25:14** The fear of God has placed itself above all things.

**25:15** Blessed is the man to whom it has been granted to have the fear of God; he who holds it, to whom will he be compared?

**25:16** The fear of God is the beginning of His love, and the beginning of faith must be joined closely to it.

**25:17** Every wound is sorrow of heart, and every malice is the wickedness of a woman.

**25:18** And he will see every wound, yet not a wound of the heart:

**25:19** and every wickedness, yet not the wickedness of a woman:

**25:20** and every affliction, yet not the affliction of those who hate:

**25:21** and every vengeance, yet not the vengeance of enemies.

**25:22** There is no head more wicked than the head of a serpent,

**25:23** and there is no anger above the anger of a woman. It will be more pleasing to stay with a lion and a dragon than to live with a wicked woman.

**25:24** The wickedness of a woman changes her face, and darkens her expression like a bear, and displays it like sackcloth. In the midst of her neighbors

**25:25** her husband groaned, and hearing, he sighed a little.

**25:26** All malice is small compared to the malice of a woman; may the lot of sinners fall upon her.

**25:27** Like a sandy ascent for the feet of an aged man, so is a talkative woman to a quiet man.

**25:28** Do not gaze upon a woman's beauty, and do not desire a woman for her beauty.

**25:29** A woman's anger, and shamelessness, and great disgrace.

**25:30** If a woman holds supremacy, she is opposed to her husband.

**25:31** A downcast heart, a sad face, and a wound of the heart: a wicked woman.

**25:32** Weak hands and slack knees: a woman who does not make her husband happy.

**25:33** From a woman the beginning of sin came to be, and through her we all die.

**25:34** Do not give your water an outlet, not even a little, nor to a wicked woman permission to go out.

**25:35** If she does not walk at your hand, she will put you to shame in the sight of enemies.

**25:36** Cut her off from your flesh, so that she may not always abuse you.

## Translator Notes

- **25:2** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 25:2 (continuing 25:1 = Saints 25:1).
- **25:4** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 25:4 (continuing 25:3 = Saints 25:2).
- **25:9** — Latin insuspicabilia ("unsuspected things") and magnificavi ("I have magnified") represent a distinctive Vulgate rendering where the Greek text has "thoughts" (hyponoemata) and "I called blessed."
- **25:10** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 25:10 (continuing 25:9 = Saints 25:7).
- **25:12** — The Vulgate distinctively reads amicum verum ("a true friend"), whereas the Greek text reads "prudence" (phronesin).
- **25:15** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 25:15 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion following 25:14 = Saints 25:11).
- **25:16** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 25:16 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion without a live Saints.Bible counterpart; absent from the short Greek text at 25:12).
- **25:17** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 25:17 (Vulgate doublet ahead of 25:18 = Saints 25:13).
- **25:18** — The Latin adds the finite verb videbit ("he will see") to resolve the elliptical construction ("every wound, yet not a wound of the heart"); in native 25:20 (Saints 25:14), rare Late Latin obductum ("affliction") calques Greek epagoge.
- **25:19** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 25:19 (continuing 25:18 = Saints 25:13b).
- **25:21** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 25:21 (continuing 25:20 = Saints 25:14b).
- **25:25** — Following an alternate textual tradition, the Vulgate reads ingemuit ("groaned"), audiens ("hearing"), and modicum ("a little"), where standard Greek reads "will recline to eat," "involuntarily," and "bitterly"; in native 25:24 (Saints 25:17) it preserves a doublet ("like a bear" and "like sackcloth").
- **25:34** — The Vulgate expands with "your" and "not even a little," and reads veniam prodeundi ("permission to go out") where Greek has "freedom of speech"; similarly, native 25:35-36 (Saints 25:26) include the Vulgate additions "she will put you to shame in the sight of enemies" and "so that she may not always abuse you."
