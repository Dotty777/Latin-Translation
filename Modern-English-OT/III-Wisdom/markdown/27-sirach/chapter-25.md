## Text

**25:1** In three things my spirit has found pleasure, which are approved before God and people:

**25:2** My soul hates three kinds, and I am greatly burdened by their life:

**25:3** What you have not gathered in your youth, how will you find it in your old age?

**25:4** How beautiful is judgment for gray hair, and for elders to know counsel!

**25:5** How beautiful is wisdom for the aged, and glorious are understanding and counsel!

**25:6** The crown of old men is much experience, and their glory is the fear of God.

**25:7** I have magnified nine unsuspected things of the heart, and the tenth I will speak to people with my tongue:

**25:8** Blessed is he who lives with a sensible woman, and who has not slipped with his tongue, and who has not served those unworthy of him.

**25:9** Blessed is he who finds a true friend, and who declares righteousness to a listening ear.

**25:10** How great is he who finds wisdom and knowledge! Yet he is not above the one who fears the Lord.

**25:11** The fear of God has placed itself above all things.

**25:13** And he will see every wound, yet not a wound of the heart:

**25:14** and every affliction, yet not the affliction of those who hate:

**25:15** There is no head more wicked than the head of a serpent,

**25:16** and there is no anger above the anger of a woman. It will be more pleasing to stay with a lion and a dragon than to live with a wicked woman.

**25:17** The wickedness of a woman changes her face, and darkens her expression like a bear, and displays it like sackcloth. In the midst of her neighbors

**25:18** her husband groaned, and hearing, he sighed a little.

**25:19** All malice is small compared to the malice of a woman; may the lot of sinners fall upon her.

**25:20** Like a sandy ascent for the feet of an aged man, so is a talkative woman to a quiet man.

**25:21** Do not gaze upon a woman's beauty, and do not desire a woman for her beauty.

**25:22** A woman's anger, and shamelessness, and great disgrace. If a woman holds supremacy, she is opposed to her husband.

**25:23** A downcast heart, a sad face, and a wound of the heart: a wicked woman. Weak hands and slack knees: a woman who does not make her husband happy.

**25:24** From a woman the beginning of sin came to be, and through her we all die.

**25:25** Do not give your water an outlet, not even a little, nor to a wicked woman permission to go out.

**25:26** If she does not walk at your hand, she will put you to shame in the sight of enemies. Cut her off from your flesh, so that she may not always abuse you.

## Translator Notes

- **25:7** — Latin insuspicabilia ("unsuspected things") and magnificavi ("I have magnified") represent a distinctive Vulgate rendering where the Greek text has "thoughts" (hyponoemata) and "I called blessed."
- **25:9** — The Vulgate distinctively reads amicum verum ("a true friend"), whereas the Greek text reads "prudence" (phronesin).
- **25:13** — The Latin adds the finite verb videbit ("he will see") to resolve the elliptical construction ("every wound, yet not a wound of the heart"); in 25:14, rare Late Latin obductum ("affliction") calques Greek epagoge.
- **25:18** — Following an alternate textual tradition, the Vulgate reads ingemuit ("groaned"), audiens ("hearing"), and modicum ("a little"), where standard Greek reads "will recline to eat," "involuntarily," and "bitterly"; in 25:17 it preserves a doublet ("like a bear" and "like sackcloth").
- **25:25** — The Vulgate expands with "your" and "not even a little," and reads veniam prodeundi ("permission to go out") where Greek has "freedom of speech"; similarly, 25:26 includes the Vulgate additions "she will put you to shame in the sight of enemies" and "so that she may not always abuse you."
