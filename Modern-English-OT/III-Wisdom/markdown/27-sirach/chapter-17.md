## Text

**17:1** God created man from the earth, and according to His own image He made him.

**17:2** And He turned him back into it again, and according to Himself He clothed him with strength.

**17:3** He gave him a number of days and a time, and He gave him authority over the things that are on the earth.

**17:4** He placed the fear of him upon all flesh, and he had dominion over beasts and birds.

**17:5** He created from him a helper like himself; He gave them deliberation, and a tongue, and eyes, and ears, and a heart for thinking, and He filled them with the discipline of understanding.

**17:6** He created for them knowledge of the spirit, He filled their heart with discernment, and He showed them evil and good.

**17:7** He placed His eye upon their hearts, to show them the majesty of His works,

**17:8** so that they may praise the name of sanctification and glory in His wonders, so that they may recount the majesty of His works.

**17:9** He added discipline to them, and He gave them the law of life as an inheritance.

**17:10** He established an eternal covenant with them, and He showed them righteousness and His judgments.

**17:11** And their eye saw the majesty of His honor, and their ears heard the honor of the voice. And He said to them, "Be on guard against everything unjust."

**17:12** And He commanded them, each one concerning his neighbor.

**17:13** Their ways are always before Him; they are not hidden from His eyes.

**17:14** Over every nation He appointed a ruler.

**17:15** And Israel was made the manifest portion of God.

**17:16** And all their works are like the sun in the sight of God, and His eyes are looking upon their ways without ceasing.

**17:17** The covenants are not hidden by their iniquity, and all their iniquities are in the sight of God.

**17:18** The almsgiving of a man is like a signet with Him, and He will preserve the kindness of a man like the pupil of the eye.

**17:19** And afterward He will rise up and repay them retribution, to each one upon their own head, and He will turn them into the inner parts of the earth.

**17:20** But to those who repent He gave the way of righteousness, and He strengthened those who were failing to endure, and He appointed for them the lot of truth.

**17:21** Turn to the Lord, and abandon your sins.

**17:22** Pray before the face of the Lord, and lessen offenses.

**17:23** Return to the Lord, and turn away from your unrighteousness, and greatly hate abomination.

**17:24** And know the righteous deeds and judgments of God, and stand in the lot of the offering and of the prayer of the Most High God.

**17:25** Go into the parts of the holy age, with the living and those who give praise to God.

**17:26** Do not linger in the error of the ungodly; before death give praise; from a dead man, as from nothing, praise perishes.

**17:27** You will give praise while living; alive and whole you will give praise, and you will praise God and glory in His compassions.

**17:28** How great is the mercy of the Lord, and His forgiveness toward those who turn to Him!

**17:29** For not all things can be in men, since a son of man is not immortal, and they have taken pleasure in the vanity of malice.

**17:30** What is brighter than the sun? Yet even it will fail. Or what is more wicked than what flesh and blood has devised? And this will be reproved.

**17:31** He Himself looks upon the power of the height of heaven, and all men are earth and ashes.

## Translator Notes

- **17:2** — In the Latin this clause ("And He turned him back into it again...") precedes "He gave him a number of days" (17:2); "according to Himself" (secundum se) refers to God rather than to human strength.
- **17:5** — The opening clause "He created from him a helper like himself" is a Vulgate addition (echoing Genesis 2:18 and the expanded Greek tradition) absent from the standard Septuagint.
- **17:15** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 17:15 (continuing 17:14 = Saints 17:17a).
- **17:17** — Latin testamenta ("covenants") is a notable Vulgate-distinct reading where the Greek has "iniquities" (adikia), emphasizing that human wickedness does not conceal God's covenants.
- **17:22** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 17:22 (continuing 17:21 = Saints 17:25).
- **17:24** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 17:24 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion continuing 17:23 = Saints 17:26); Latin 'propositionis' denotes an offering/setting forth (or purpose).
- **17:25** — This verse (native 17:25 = Saints 17:27) follows the expanded Old Latin and Vulgate text ("Go into the parts of the holy age...") rather than the standard Septuagint's question about praising God in Hades; here, in native 17:26-27 (Saints 17:28), and in 18:28, Latin confessio and confiteri carry the biblical sense of giving praise and thanksgiving.
- **17:27** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 17:27 (parallel to Saints/Greek 17:28b).
- **17:31** — Latin virtutem ("power") renders Greek dynamin, referring here to the heavenly host or celestial powers in the heights of heaven.
