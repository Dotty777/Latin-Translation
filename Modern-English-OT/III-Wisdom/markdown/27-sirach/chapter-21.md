## Text

**21:1** Son, you have sinned; do not add to it again, but also plead concerning your former sins, so that they may be forgiven you.

**21:2** As from the face of a serpent, flee from sins, and if you come near to them, they will take hold of you.

**21:3** Like a two-edged broadsword is all iniquity; for its wound there is no healing.

**21:4** Berating and wrongs will bring wealth to nothing, and a house that is overly rich will be brought to nothing by pride; so the wealth of a proud person will be uprooted.

**21:5** The plea of a poor person will reach from the mouth all the way to His ears, and judgment will come swiftly to him.

**21:6** Whoever hates correction is a footprint of a sinner, and whoever fears God will turn back to his own heart.

**21:7** One who is powerful with a bold tongue is known from afar, and a sensible person knows that he slips from him.

**21:8** Whoever builds his house at the expense of others is like one who gathers his stones in winter.

**21:9** Tow gathered together is the congregation of those who sin, and their end is a flame of fire.

**21:10** The way of sinners is made smooth with stones, and at their end are the underworld, and darkness, and punishments.

**21:11** Whoever keeps righteousness will retain its understanding.

**21:12** Whoever is not wise in what is good will not be instructed.

**21:13** The knowledge of a wise person will abound like a flood, and his counsel remains like a spring of life.

**21:14** The heart of a fool is like a shattered vessel, and it will not hold any wisdom.

**21:15** Whatever wise word a knowledgeable person hears, he will praise it and add it to himself; a self-indulgent person has heard it, and it will displease him, and he will throw it behind his back.

**21:16** The discourse of a fool is like a burden on the road, whereas on the lips of a sensible person grace will be found.

**21:17** The mouth of a prudent person is sought in the assembly, and they will ponder his words in their hearts.

**21:18** Like a ruined house, so is wisdom to a fool, and the knowledge of a senseless person is inexplicable words.

**21:19** Instruction to a fool is shackles on the feet, and like bonds for the hands on the right hand.

**21:20** A fool raises his voice in laughter, but a wise man will scarcely laugh quietly.

**21:21** Instruction to a prudent person is a golden ornament, and like an armlet on the right arm.

**21:22** The foot of a fool is quick into a neighbor's house, and an experienced person will feel shame before the presence of a powerful man.

**21:23** A fool will look from a window into a house, but a well-instructed man will stand outside.

**21:24** It is the foolishness of a person to listen through a door, and a prudent person will be weighed down by disgrace.

**21:25** The lips of the imprudent will speak foolish things, but the words of the prudent will be weighed on a scale.

**21:26** In the mouth of fools is their heart, and in the heart of the wise is their mouth.

**21:27** While an ungodly person curses the devil, he himself curses his own soul.

**21:28** A whisperer will defile his own soul, and he will be hated in all things, and whoever stays with him will be detestable; a quiet and sensible person will be honored.

## Translator Notes

- **21:2** — Vulgate reads suscipient te ("they will take hold of you"), whereas the Greek reads "it will bite you"; the Greek's following line on a lion's teeth stands in the Vulgate as a separate line (Dentes leonis dentes ejus, interficientes animas hominum).
- **21:5** — Latin ejus ("His") implicitly refers to God as the hearer of the poor person's plea.
- **21:7** — Vulgate adds audaci ("bold") and reads scit labi se ab ipso ("knows that he slips from him"), whereas the Greek means "knows when the other slips."
- **21:8** — Vulgate in hieme ("in winter") reflects a Greek variant meaning "for winter" rather than the usual Greek reading "for his burial mound."
- **21:28** — Vulgate in omnibus ("in all things") differs from the Greek ("in his neighborhood"), and the final two clauses ("and whoever stays with him... will be honored") are a Vulgate-distinct addition absent from the standard Greek text.
