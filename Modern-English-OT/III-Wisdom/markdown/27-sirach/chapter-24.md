## Text

**24:1** Wisdom will praise her own soul, and in God she will be honored, and in the midst of her people she will glory,

**24:2** and in the assemblies of the Most High she will open her mouth, and in the sight of His power she will glory:

**24:3** and in the midst of her people she will be exalted, and in the holy fullness she will be admired,

**24:4** and in the multitude of the chosen she will have praise, and among the blessed she will be blessed, saying:

**24:5** "I came forth from the mouth of the Most High, firstborn before every creature.

**24:6** I caused an unfailing light to rise in the heavens, and like a mist I covered all the earth.

**24:7** I dwelt in the highest places, and my throne was in a pillar of cloud.

**24:8** I went around the circuit of heaven alone, and I penetrated the depth of the abyss; I walked in the waves of the sea.

**24:9** And in every land I stood, and among every people,

**24:10** and in every nation I held the first place,

**24:11** and with power I trod upon the hearts of all the exalted and the humble. And among all these I sought rest, and in the inheritance of the Lord I will remain.

**24:12** Then the Creator of all commanded and spoke to me, and He who created me rested in my tabernacle.

**24:13** And He said to me, 'Dwell in Jacob, and receive an inheritance in Israel, and send down roots among My chosen ones.'

**24:14** From the beginning and before the ages I was created, and even to the coming age I will not cease; and in the holy dwelling I ministered before Him.

**24:15** And so in Zion I was established, and in the sanctified city likewise I rested, and in Jerusalem is my authority.

**24:16** And I took root in an honored people, and in the portion of my God is His inheritance, and in the fullness of the holy ones is my abiding place.

**24:17** I was exalted like a cedar in Lebanon, and like a cypress on Mount Zion;

**24:18** like a palm tree I was exalted in Kadesh, and like a planting of roses in Jericho.

**24:19** Like a lovely olive tree in the plains, and like a plane tree I was exalted beside the water in the streets.

**24:20** Like cinnamon and aromatic balsam I gave forth fragrance; like choice myrrh I gave sweetness of fragrance.

**24:21** And like storax and galbanum and onycha and stacte, and like uncut frankincense I perfumed my dwelling, and my fragrance is like unmixed balsam.

**24:22** I, like a terebinth, stretched out my branches, and my branches are of honor and grace.

**24:23** I, like a vine, brought forth sweetness of fragrance, and my blossoms are fruit of honor and uprightness.

**24:24** I am the mother of beautiful love, of fear, of knowledge, and of holy hope.

**24:25** In me is all grace of the way and of truth; in me is all hope of life and of strength.

**24:26** Come over to me, all who long for me, and be filled from my produce;

**24:27** for my spirit is sweeter than honey, and my inheritance is sweeter than honey and the honeycomb.

**24:28** My memory is in the generation of the ages.

**24:29** Those who eat me will still hunger, and those who drink me will still thirst.

**24:30** The one who listens to me will not be put to shame, and those who work in me will not sin;

**24:31** those who explain me will have eternal life."

**24:32** All these things are the book of life, and the covenant of the Most High, and the knowledge of truth.

**24:33** Moses commanded the law in the precepts of righteous deeds, and an inheritance for the house of Jacob, and promises for Israel.

**24:34** He appointed for David His servant to raise up from him a most mighty king, sitting upon the throne of honor forever.

**24:35** It is he who fills wisdom like the Pishon, and like the Tigris in the days of new fruits,

**24:36** who fills understanding to the full like the Euphrates, who multiplies it like the Jordan in the time of harvest,

**24:37** who sends forth discipline like light, and stands near like the Gihon in the day of vintage.

**24:38** He first completes knowing her, and the weaker one will not trace her out.

**24:39** For her thought has abounded from the sea, and her counsel from the great abyss.

**24:40** I have poured out wisdom as rivers.

**24:41** I am like a channel of immense water from a river; I am like a canal of a river, and like an aqueduct I went out from paradise.

**24:42** I said, "I will water my garden of plantings, and I will drench the fruit of my meadow."

**24:43** And look, my channel became abundant, and my river drew near to the sea,

**24:44** for I make instruction shine like the dawn for all, and I will declare it even far away.

**24:45** I will penetrate all the lower parts of the earth, look upon all who sleep, and give light to all who hope in the Lord.

**24:46** I will still pour out instruction like prophecy, and I will leave it to those who seek wisdom, and I will not cease throughout their generations, even into the holy age.

**24:47** See that I have not labored for myself alone, but for all who seek out truth.

## Translator Notes

- **24:3** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 24:3 (continuing 24:2 = Saints 24:1-2).
- **24:4** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 24:4 (introducing Wisdom's speech beginning in 24:5 = Saints 24:3).
- **24:5** — The Vulgate reads primogenita ante omnem creaturam ("firstborn before every creature"), whereas the Greek reads "and like a mist I covered the earth."
- **24:6** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 24:6 (parallel to Saints/Greek 24:3b-4a).
- **24:10** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 24:10 (continuing 24:9 = Saints 24:6).
- **24:11** — The clause et omnium excellentium et humilium corda virtute calcavi ("and with power I trod upon the hearts of all the exalted and the humble") is unique to the Vulgate tradition, where the Greek says Wisdom held sway over every people and nation.
- **24:12** — The Vulgate reads requievit in tabernaculo meo ("rested in my tabernacle"), whereas the Greek has the Creator assign a resting place for Wisdom's tent.
- **24:13** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 24:13 (parallel to Saints/Greek 24:8b); single quotes mark speech nested within Wisdom's discourse (24:5-31).
- **24:17** — The Vulgate reads in monte Sion ("on Mount Zion") here (native 24:17 = Saints 24:13) and in Cades ("in Kadesh") in native 24:18 (Saints 24:14), whereas the Greek names Mount Hermon and En-gedi respectively.
- **24:19** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 24:19 (continuing 24:18 = Saints 24:14).
- **24:21** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 24:21 (continuing 24:20 = Saints 24:15); Latin 'ungula' (lit. "nail/claw") and 'gutta' (lit. "drop") are the traditional Vulgate names for the incense spices onycha and stacte (cf. Exod 30:34), and 'Libanus' denotes the frankincense tree/resin.
- **24:25** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 24:25 (Vulgate/LXX-II expansion following 24:24).
- **24:28** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 24:28 (Vulgate placement following 24:27 = Saints 24:20).
- **24:30** — Wisdom's first-person speech (opened in 24:5) now closes at the end of restored native Vulgate 24:31.
- **24:31** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 24:31 (completing Wisdom's speech from 24:30 = Saints 24:22; closes the double quote opened at 24:5).
- **24:33** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 24:33 (parallel to Saints/Greek 24:23b).
- **24:34** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 24:34 (Vulgate/LXX-II messianic expansion following 24:33).
- **24:38** — The Vulgate omits the negative before perficit ("completes") and reads infirmior ("the weaker one") rather than the Greek "the first man did not finish knowing her, and likewise the last man will not trace her out"; the antecedent of the masculine Qui in native 24:35-38 (Saints 24:25-28) is not stated (the Most High or liber, "book," in native 24:32 / Saints 24:23), whereas the Greek refers to the law.
- **24:40** — Native Vulgate 24:40 (formerly unplaced after 24:29). All four Sixto-Clementine witnesses (Clem1598-USFX, VulgHetzenauer 1906, VulgConte 2009, VulgSistina 1590) read accusative 'sapientiam' ("wisdom as rivers"), whereas some later critical Vulgate editions read nominative 'sapientia' ("I, wisdom, have poured out rivers").
