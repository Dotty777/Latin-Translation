## Text

**43:1** The expanse of the height is His beauty, the appearance of heaven in a vision of glory.

**43:2** The sun, when it appears, proclaiming at its going forth, is a marvelous vessel, the work of the Most High.

**43:3** At midday it scorches the earth, and before its burning heat who will be able to endure? Tending a furnace in works of burning heat:

**43:4** three times over, the sun, scorching the mountains, blowing out fiery rays, and shining brightly with its rays, blinds the eyes.

**43:5** Great is the Lord who made it, and at His words it hastened its course.

**43:6** And the moon, in all things in its time, is a showing of time and a sign of the age.

**43:7** From the moon is the sign of the feast day, a light that diminishes at completion.

**43:8** The month is according to its name, increasing wondrously in completion.

**43:9** The beauty of heaven is the glory of the stars; the Lord illuminates the world in the heights.

**43:10** At the words of the Holy One they will stand for judgment, and they will not fail in their watches.

**43:11** Look at the rainbow, and bless Him who made it; it is exceedingly beautiful in its splendor.

**43:12** It encircled heaven in the circuit of its glory; the hands of the Most High opened it.

**43:13** By His command He hastened the snow, and He hastens to send forth the lightning flashes of His judgment.

**43:14** Therefore the storehouses were opened, and the mists flew out like birds.

**43:15** In His greatness He placed the clouds, and the stones of hail were shattered.

**43:16** In His sight the mountains will be shaken, and at His will the south wind will blow.

**43:17** The voice of His thunder lashed the earth, the storm of the north wind, and the gathering of the wind:

**43:18** and like a bird coming down to settle, He sprinkles snow, and like a locust plunging down is its descent.

**43:19** He will pour out frost like salt upon the earth, and when it has frozen, it will become like the points of a thistle.

**43:20** The cold north wind blew, and ice froze from the water; upon every gathering of waters it will rest, and like a breastplate it will clothe itself with the waters:

**43:21** and it will devour the mountains, and scorch the desert, and extinguish what is green, as with fire.

**43:22** The healing of all things is in the hastening of a mist, and the dew, meeting one coming from the burning heat, will make him humble.

**43:23** At His word the wind fell silent, and by His thought He calmed the abyss, and the Lord planted islands in it.

**43:24** Let those who sail the sea recount its dangers, and hearing with our ears, we will marvel.

**43:25** There are splendid and wondrous works, various kinds of beasts and of all cattle, and the creation of monsters.

**43:26** Because of Him the end of the journey has been established, and by His word all things have been ordered.

**43:27** We will say many things, and we will fall short in words; yet the conclusion of speech is: He Himself is in all things.

**43:28** Glorying, for what will we have strength? For He Himself is almighty above all His works.

**43:29** Fearsome is the Lord, and exceedingly great, and wonderful is His power.

**43:30** Glorify the Lord as much as you are able, for He will still surpass it, and marvelous is His magnificence. Exalting Him, be filled with strength; do not grow weary, for you will not comprehend Him.

**43:31** Who will see Him and tell of Him? And who will magnify Him as He is from the beginning?

**43:32** Many hidden things are greater than these, for we have seen few of His works.

**43:33** Now the Lord has made all things, and to those who act devoutly He has given wisdom.

## Translator Notes

- **43:1** — Vulgate 'pulchritudo ejus' ('His beauty', continuing the reference to the Lord's glory in 42:25, or possibly 'its beauty', referring to the height) differs from the Greek, which reads 'the expanse of purity'.
- **43:8** — Vulgate repeats 'in consummatione' ('in completion') from v. 7, whereas the Greek reads 'in its changing' (referring to the moon's phases).
- **43:9** — Vulgate reads 'mundum illuminans in excelsis Dominus' ('the Lord illuminates the world in the heights'), taking 'mundum' as 'world' and nominative 'Dominus' as subject, whereas the Greek reads 'a shining ornament in the heights of the Lord'.
- **43:22** — Vulgate 'humilem efficiet eum' ('will make him humble') likely reflects a Latin textual shift from 'hilarem' ('cheerful'), corresponding to the Greek 'will refresh' after the scorching heat.
- **43:26** — Vulgate 'confirmatus est itineris finis' ('the end of the journey has been established') diverges from the Greek, which reads 'His messenger succeeds'.
