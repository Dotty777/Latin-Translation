## Text

**22:1** With a muddy stone a lazy person has been stoned, and everyone will speak about the scorn for him.

**22:2** With ox dung a lazy person has been stoned, and everyone who touches him will shake off his hands.

**22:3** A father's shame is over an undisciplined son, and a daughter will turn out to be a loss.

**22:4** A prudent daughter is an inheritance to her husband, whereas one who brings shame becomes a disgrace to the one who fathered her.

**22:5** A brazen woman brings shame upon father and husband, and she will not be less than the ungodly; and by both she will be dishonored.

**22:6** Music in mourning is untimely discourse; lashes and instruction are wisdom at all times.

**22:9** Whoever speaks a word to one who is not listening is like one who rouses a sleeper from deep sleep. Whoever teaches a fool is like one who glues together a potsherd.

**22:10** One who recounts wisdom to a fool speaks with a sleeper, and at the end of the discourse he says, "Who is this?"

**22:11** Weep over a dead person, for his light has failed; and weep over a fool, for understanding has failed.

**22:12** Mourning for a dead person is seven days, but for a fool and an ungodly person it is all the days of their life.

**22:13** Do not speak much with a fool, and do not go with a senseless person.

**22:14** What will be heavier than lead? And what other name is there for it than fool?

**22:15** Sand and salt and a mass of iron are easier to bear than a person who is imprudent and foolish and ungodly.

**22:16** A wooden tie-beam bound into the foundation of a building will not come apart; so also is a heart established in the thought of counsel.

**22:18** Just as stakes on high places, and rough stones laid without mortar, will not remain against the face of the wind.

**22:19** One who pricks an eye draws forth tears, and whoever pricks a heart brings forth feeling.

**22:20** One who throws a stone at birds will drive them off; so also one who reviles a friend dissolves friendship.

**22:21** Against a friend, even if you have drawn a sword, do not despair, for there is a return. Against a friend,

**22:22** if you have opened a bitter mouth, do not be afraid, for there is reconciliation, except for reviling, and reproach, and pride, and the revealing of a secret, and a treacherous blow; in all these things a friend will flee.

**22:23** Keep faith with a friend in his poverty, so that you may also rejoice in his good things. In the time of his affliction remain faithful to him, so that you may also be a co-heir in his inheritance.

**22:24** Before the fire of a furnace vapor and smoke of fire rise high; so also before bloodshed are curses, and insults, and threats.

**22:25** I will not be ashamed to greet a friend; I will not hide myself from his face, and if evils happen to me through him, I will endure.

**22:26** Everyone who hears will guard himself against him.

**22:27** Who will place a guard over my mouth, and a sure seal upon my lips, so that I may not fall because of them and my tongue may not destroy me?

## Translator Notes

- **22:1** — Vulgate reads lapidatus est ("has been stoned") here and in 22:2, whereas the Greek reads "is compared to."
- **22:5** — The clause et ab impiis non minorabitur is a Vulgate-distinct addition absent from the standard Greek; it can mean either "she will not be less [wicked] than the ungodly" or "she will not be belittled by the ungodly."
- **22:18** — Latin sine impensa uses impensa in its architectural sense of "mortar" or "binding material." As transmitted here, the Vulgate preserves only the opening half of the comparison (the concluding clause about a timid heart in the Greek text is absent).
- **22:21** — In the Clementine Vulgate verse division, the opening phrase Ad amicum ("Against a friend") of the second sentence stands at the end of verse 21 and continues into verse 22; in verse 22, Vulgate also adds triste ("bitter") to "mouth."
- **22:25** — Vulgate reads salutare ("to greet") and adds sustinebo ("I will endure"), whereas the Greek reads "to shelter" and connects "if harm happens to me through him" directly to verse 26.
