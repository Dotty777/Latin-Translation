## Text

**30:1** He who loves his son continually applies lashes to him, so that he may rejoice at his end and not grope at the doors of his neighbors.

**30:2** He who teaches his son will be praised in him, and in the midst of his household he will boast in him.

**30:3** He who teaches his son drives his enemy into envy, and in the midst of his friends he will boast in him.

**30:4** His father has died, and it is as if he has not died, for he has left behind one like himself after him.

**30:5** In his life he saw him and rejoiced in him; at his death he was not grieved, nor was he put to shame before his enemies.

**30:6** For he left behind a defender of the house against enemies, and one who repays kindness to friends.

**30:7** For the souls of his sons he will bind up his wounds, and at every cry his inward parts will be troubled.

**30:8** An untamed horse turns out stubborn, and an unrestrained son will turn out headstrong.

**30:9** Pamper a son, and he will make you fearful; play with him, and he will grieve you.

**30:10** Do not laugh along with him, so that you do not suffer pain, and in the end your teeth will be set on edge.

**30:11** Do not give him authority in his youth, and do not disregard his thoughts.

**30:12** Bend his neck in his youth, and strike his sides while he is a young child, so that he may not grow hard and disbelieve you, and it will be a pain of soul to you.

**30:13** Teach your son and work on him, so that you do not stumble into his shame.

**30:14** Better is a poor man who is healthy and strong in vigor than a rich man who is weak and scourged with evil.

**30:15** Health of the soul in the holiness of righteousness is better than all gold and silver, and a strong body than immense wealth.

**30:16** There is no wealth above the wealth of the health of the body, and there is no delight above joy of the heart.

**30:17** Better is death than a bitter life, and eternal rest than persistent sickness.

**30:18** Good things hidden in a closed mouth are like servings of banquets placed around a tomb.

**30:19** What will a libation profit an idol? For it will neither eat nor smell.

**30:20** So is the one who is driven off by the Lord, bearing the wages of iniquity,

**30:21** seeing with his eyes and groaning, like a eunuch embracing a virgin and sighing.

**30:22** Do not give sorrow to your soul, and do not afflict yourself in your own counsel.

**30:23** Gladness of heart, this is a man's life, and a treasure of holiness without failing, and the exultation of a man is long life.

**30:24** Have mercy on your soul, pleasing God, and restrain yourself; gather your heart in His holiness, and drive sorrow far away from you.

**30:25** For sorrow has killed many, and there is no usefulness in it.

**30:26** Envy and anger shorten days, and anxious thought will bring on old age before its time.

**30:27** A bright and good heart is at banquets, for its banquets are carefully prepared.

## Translator Notes

- **30:1** — The final clause "and not grope at the doors of his neighbors" (et non palpet proximorum ostia) is a distinctive Vulgate reading absent from the standard Greek text. In Sirach chapters 30-36, the Latin Vulgate preserves the original section order (contrasting with the transposed manuscript order in standard Septuagint editions where 30:25-33:13a and 33:13b-36:16a are exchanged). Native Clem1598-USFX verse counts are ch. 30 (27 vv), ch. 31 (42 vv), ch. 32 (28 vv), ch. 33 (33 vv), ch. 34 (31 vv), ch. 35 (26 vv), and ch. 36 (28 vv), including 27 newly restored verses across ch. 30-36; keep native Vulgate numbering in the Latin edition and map cleanly to Saints.Bible parallel slots.
- **30:7** — Vulgate "For the souls of his sons" (Pro animabus filiorum) reflects an Old Latin reading of the Greek participle "peripsychon" ("he who pampers") as "peri psychon" ("for the souls").
- **30:15** — Vulgate uniquely spiritualizes the opening clause as "Health of the soul in the holiness of righteousness" and adds "and silver," where the Greek speaks of bodily health and soundness better than all gold.
- **30:20** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 30:20 (corresponding to Saints.Bible 30:19b, continuing 30:19 = 30:19a and introducing 30:21 = 30:20); Latin 'effugatur' literally means "is put to flight / driven off" (rendering Greek 'ekdiokomenos', "pursued").
- **30:21** — Native Vulgate 30:21 (mapped to Saints.Bible 30:20) continues the introductory clause restored at native Vulgate 30:20 (mapped to Saints.Bible 30:19b, "So is the one who is driven off by the Lord, bearing the wages of iniquity,").
- **30:24** — Vulgate's "Have mercy on your soul, pleasing God, and restrain yourself; gather your heart in His holiness" is a distinctly spiritualized Latin reading where the Greek has "cheer your soul and comfort your heart."
- **30:25** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 30:25 (parallel to Saints/Greek 30:23b).
