## Text

**26:1** Blessed is the husband of a good woman, for the number of his years is twofold.

**26:2** A strong woman gladdens her husband, and she will complete the years of his life in peace.

**26:3** A good portion is a good woman; in the portion of those who fear God she will be given to a man for good deeds:

**26:4** moreover, the heart of the rich and of the poor is good; at all times their face is cheerful.

**26:5** Of three things my heart was afraid, and at the fourth my face feared:

**26:6** grief of heart and mourning: a jealous woman.

**26:7** Like a yoke of oxen that shifts, so also is a wicked woman: the one who holds her is like one who grasps a scorpion.

**26:8** A drunken woman is great anger and reproach, and her shame will not be covered.

**26:9** The fornication of a woman will be recognized in the lifting up of the eyes and in her eyelids.

**26:10** Over a daughter who does not turn herself away, strengthen the watch, so that when an opportunity is found, she may not indulge herself.

**26:11** Guard against all shamelessness of her eyes, and do not be surprised if she disregards you.

**26:12** Like a thirsty traveler she will open her mouth at the spring, and she will drink from every nearby water, and before every stake she will sit, and before every arrow she will open her quiver until she fails.

**26:13** The grace of a diligent woman will delight her husband, and will fatten his bones.

**26:14** Her discipline is a gift of God.

**26:15** Grace upon grace is a holy and modest woman. And no weight is worthy of a self-controlled soul.

**26:16** Like the sun rising upon the world in the highest places of God, so is the beauty of a good woman for the adornment of her house.

**26:17** A shining lamp upon a holy lampstand; so is the beauty of a face upon a steadfast age.

**26:18** Golden pillars upon silver bases; so are firm feet upon the soles of a steadfast woman.

**26:28** A warrior failing through poverty; and a sensible man despised; and the one who crosses over from righteousness to sin: God has prepared him for the sword. Over two things my heart has been grieved, and at the third, indignation has come upon me:

**26:29** Two kinds have appeared difficult and dangerous to me: with difficulty is a merchant freed from negligence, and a shopkeeper will not be justified from sins of the lips.

## Translator Notes

- **26:1** — The Vulgate reads annorum ("years") where the Greek text has "days"; similarly, 26:3 adds pro factis bonis ("for good deeds").
- **26:5** — Latin facies mea metuit ("my face feared") takes "face" as the subject and renders Greek edeethen ("I pleaded") as a verb of fearing.
- **26:10** — Latin non avertente se ("who does not turn herself away") is a literal etymological rendering of Greek adiatreptos ("shameless" or "headstrong"), and utatur se ("indulge herself") literally means "make use of herself."
- **26:17** — Latin aetatem stabilem ("a steadfast age") renders Greek helikia ("age" or "stature") specifically as "age," parallel to stabilis mulieris ("a steadfast woman") in 26:18.
- **26:28** — The Latin text places the three enumerated items before the introductory formula ("Over two things..."), and 26:29 preserves the Vulgate's opening expansion ("Two kinds have appeared difficult and dangerous to me"), the wordplay negotians ... negligentia ("merchant ... negligence," where Greek has "wrongdoing"), and "sins of the lips."
