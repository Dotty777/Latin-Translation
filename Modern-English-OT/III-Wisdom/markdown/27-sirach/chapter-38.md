## Text

**38:1** Honor the physician because of necessity, for indeed the Most High created him.

**38:2** For all healing is from God, and from the king he will receive a gift.

**38:3** The learning of the physician will lift up his head, and in the sight of great men he will be praised.

**38:4** The Most High created medicines from the earth, and a prudent man will not abhor them.

**38:5** Was not bitter water made sweet by wood?

**38:6** The power of these things is for the recognition of men, and the Most High gave knowledge to men, to be honored in His marvelous works.

**38:7** By these, in healing, he will soothe pain; and the apothecary will make compounds of sweetness, and will prepare ointments of health, and his works will not come to an end.

**38:8** For the peace of God is upon the face of the earth.

**38:9** Son, in your sickness do not neglect yourself, but pray to the Lord, and He Himself will heal you.

**38:10** Turn away from sin, and direct your hands, and cleanse your heart from every sin.

**38:11** Give a sweet fragrance and a memorial of fine flour, and make the offering rich, and give place to the physician.

**38:12** For indeed the Lord created him, and let him not depart from you, because his works are necessary.

**38:13** For there is a time when you fall into their hands.

**38:14** And they themselves will implore the Lord, that He may direct their rest and health, on account of their way of life.

**38:15** He who sins in the sight of Him who made him will fall into the hands of the physician.

**38:16** Son, bring forth tears over the dead, and as one who has suffered dreadful things, begin to weep; and according to judgment cover his body, and do not neglect his burial.

**38:17** And because of slander, bitterly bear mourning for him for one day, and be comforted because of sorrow.

**38:18** For from sorrow death hastens, and it covers over strength, and sorrow of heart bends the neck.

**38:19** In carrying away, sorrow remains, and the substance of a poor man is according to his heart.

**38:20** Do not give your heart to sorrow, but drive it away from you, and remember the last things.

**38:21** Do not forget, for there is no return; and you will be of no benefit to this one, and you will ruin yourself.

**38:22** Be mindful of my judgment, for so will yours also be: for me yesterday, and for you today.

**38:23** In the rest of the dead, cause the memory of him to rest, and comfort him at the departure of his spirit.

**38:24** The wisdom of a scribe is in a time of leisure, and he who is lessened in activity will acquire wisdom, with which wisdom he will be filled.

**38:25** He who holds the plow, and he who boasts in the javelin, drives oxen with a goad and is occupied in their works, and his discourse is about the offspring of bulls.

**38:26** He will give his heart to turning the furrows, and his wakefulness is in the fattening of cows.

**38:27** So also every craftsman and master builder, who passes the night like the day; who carves engraved seals, and his diligence varies the picture; he will give his heart to the likeness of the picture, and by his wakefulness he will complete the work.

**38:28** So also the ironsmith, sitting beside the anvil and considering the work of iron: the vapor of the fire will scorch his flesh, and in the heat of the furnace he struggles.

**38:29** So also the potter, sitting at his work, turning the wheel with his feet, who is always placed in anxious care on account of his work, and all his labor is by number.

**38:30** With his arm he will shape the clay, and before his feet he will bend his strength.

**38:31** All these have put their hope in their hands, and each one is wise in his own craft.

**38:32** Without all these a city is not built,

**38:33** and they will not dwell, nor will they walk about, and they will not pass over into the assembly.

**38:34** Upon the seat of the judge they will not sit, and they will not understand the covenant of judgment, nor will they openly declare instruction and judgment, and in parables they will not be found; but they will strengthen the creation of the age, and their prayer is in the labor of their craft, applying their soul and searching in the law of the Most High.

## Translator Notes

- **38:7** — Latin "ejus" ("his") grammatically follows the immediate subject "unguentarius" ("the apothecary"), though some readers refer "opera ejus" to God in light of vv. 6 and 8.
- **38:19** — This terse Vulgate-distinct verse renders the Greek terms for calamity or funeral carrying away ("abductio") and a poor man's life ("substantia," also "livelihood") literally, with "secundum cor ejus" ("according to his heart") where the Greek means "grievous to the heart."
- **38:24** — The Clementine Vulgate closes v. 24 with a period at "qua sapientia replebitur" ("with which wisdom he will be filled"), making both v. 24 and v. 25 declarative sentences; in the Greek, that clause introduces a question continued in v. 25 ("How can he become wise who holds the plow...?").
- **38:25** — Latin "in jaculo, stimulo" divides the Greek phrase "in the shaft of the goad" into two separate nouns ("in the javelin" and "with a goad").
- **38:34** — The Vulgate attaches the closing participial clause ("applying their soul and searching in the law of the Most High") directly to the craftsmen, lacking the contrastive transition that introduces the scribe in Greek 38:34c-39:1.
