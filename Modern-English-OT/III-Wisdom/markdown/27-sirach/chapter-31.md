## Text

**31:1** Sleeplessness over prosperity will waste away the flesh, and the thought of it will take away sleep.

**31:2** The thought of foreknowledge turns away the mind, and a severe illness makes the soul sober.

**31:3** The rich man has labored in gathering property, and in his rest he will be filled with his good things.

**31:4** The poor man has labored in a shortage of sustenance, and in the end he becomes destitute.

**31:5** He who loves gold will not be justified, and he who pursues consumption will be filled from it.

**31:6** Many have been given over to the downfalls of gold, and their destruction came about through its appearance.

**31:7** Gold is a stumbling block for those who sacrifice; woe to those who follow after it! And every unwise person will perish in it.

**31:8** Blessed is the rich man who has been found without blemish, and who has not gone after gold, nor hoped in money and treasures.

**31:9** Who is this? And we will praise him, for he has done wonders in his life.

**31:10** He who has been tested in it and made perfect will have eternal glory: he who was able to transgress and did not transgress, to do evil and did not do it.

**31:11** Therefore his good things have been established in the Lord, and the whole assembly of the saints will recount his alms.

**31:12** Have you sat down at a great table? Do not open your throat over it first.

**31:13** Remember that a wicked eye is evil.

**31:14** Do not stretch out your hand first and, defiled by envy, be put to shame.

**31:15** Understand the things of your neighbor from yourself.

**31:16** Use the things that are set before you like a frugal person, so that, when you eat much, you may not be hated.

**31:17** Stop first for the sake of discipline, and do not be excessive, so that you do not happen to give offense.

**31:18** And if you have sat down in the midst of many, do not stretch out your hand before them, nor ask to drink first.

**31:19** How sufficient a little wine is for an educated person! And in sleeping you will not suffer from it, and you will not feel pain.

**31:20** Sleeplessness, bile, and colic are for a senseless man.

**31:21** And if you have been forced into eating much, rise from the midst, vomit, and it will refresh you, and you will not bring illness upon your body.

**31:22** Listen to me, son, and do not spurn me, and in the end you will find my words.

**31:23** The lips of many will bless one who is generous with bread, and the testimony of his truth is faithful.

**31:24** The city will murmur against one who is most wicked with bread, and the testimony of his wickedness is true.

**31:25** Do not provoke those who are diligent in wine, for wine has destroyed many.

**31:26** Fire tests hard iron; so wine will convict the hearts of the proud when drunk in drunkenness.

**31:27** Wine in sobriety is equal to life for people; if you drink it moderately, you will be sober. What life is there for one who is deprived of wine? Wine was created for gladness, and not for drunkenness, from the beginning.

**31:28** Wine, when drunk moderately, is exultation of soul and heart.

**31:29** Much wine, when drunk, causes irritation, and anger, and many ruins. Much wine, when drunk, is bitterness of soul.

**31:30** The fury of drunkenness is the stumbling of an unwise person, diminishing strength and causing wounds.

**31:31** At a banquet of wine do not rebuke a neighbor, and do not despise him in his gladness. Do not speak words of reproach to him, and do not press him by demanding repayment.

## Translator Notes

- **31:1** — Latin honestatis (literally "of honor" or "dignity") is used in Late and Vulgate Latin in the sense of material prosperity or wealth.
- **31:2** — A Vulgate-distinct reading: Latin has cogitatus praescientiae ("thought of foreknowledge") and sobriam facit animam ("makes the soul sober"), where the Greek reads "anxiety of sleeplessness" and "banishes sleep."
- **31:7** — Latin lignum offensionis is literally "wood of stumbling," and the genitive aurum sacrificantium (literally "gold of those who sacrifice") has no explicit indirect object ("to it").
- **31:14** — Vulgate-distinct second clause (et invidia contaminatus erubescas), whereas the Greek warns against jostling a host in the dish.
- **31:25** — Vulgate-distinct reading Diligentes in vino noli provocare (literally "do not provoke those who are diligent in wine," also taken as "those who love wine"), where the Greek reads "do not display prowess in wine."
