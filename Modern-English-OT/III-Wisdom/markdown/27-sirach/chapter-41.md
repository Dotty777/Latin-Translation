## Text

**41:1** O death, how bitter is the memory of you for a man who has peace in his possessions!

**41:2** for a man at rest, whose ways are straight in all things, and who is still strong enough to take food!

**41:3** O death, your judgment is good for a man in need and for one who is diminished in strength.

**41:4** worn out by age, and who has anxiety about everything, and disbelieving, who loses patience!

**41:5** Do not fear the judgment of death. Remember what was before you and what is to come upon you: this is the judgment from the Lord for all flesh.

**41:6** And what will come upon you in the good pleasure of the Most High? Whether ten, or a hundred, or a thousand years.

**41:7** For in the underworld there is no accusation of life.

**41:8** The children of sinners become children of abominations, as do those who live beside the houses of the ungodly.

**41:9** The inheritance of the children of sinners will perish, and with their seed will be continual disgrace.

**41:10** Children complain about an ungodly father, for on account of him they are in disgrace.

**41:11** Woe to you, ungodly men, who have abandoned the law of the Lord Most High!

**41:12** And if you are born, you will be born in a curse; and if you die, your portion will be in a curse.

**41:13** All things that are from the earth will turn back to the earth; so too the ungodly, from a curse to destruction.

**41:14** The mourning of people is over their own body, but the name of the ungodly will be erased.

**41:15** Take care for a good name, for this will remain with you longer than a thousand precious and great treasures.

**41:16** For a good life there is a number of days, but a good name will remain forever.

**41:17** Preserve discipline in peace, children. For hidden wisdom and unseen treasure, what benefit is there in both of them?

**41:18** A man who hides his foolishness is better than a man who hides his wisdom.

**41:19** Nevertheless, have a sense of shame regarding these things that proceed from my mouth:

**41:20** for it is not good to observe every reverence, and not all things are well-pleasing to all in faithfulness.

**41:21** Be ashamed before father and mother of sexual immorality, and before a ruler and a powerful man of a lie;

**41:22** before a prince and a judge of an offense; before an assembly and the populace of wickedness;

**41:23** before a companion and a friend of injustice, and from the place in which you live,

**41:24** of theft; of the truth of God and the covenant; of reclining on the loaves, and of obscuring giving and receiving;

**41:25** before those who offer greetings, of silence; of gazing at a prostitute, and of turning away the face of a relative.

**41:26** Do not turn your face away from your neighbor, and of taking away a portion and not restoring it.

**41:27** Do not gaze at another man's wife, and do not scrutinize his female servant, nor stand by her bed.

**41:28** Before friends, of words of reproach; and when you have given, do not reproach.

## Translator Notes

- **41:2** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 41:2 (continuing 41:1 = Saints 41:1).
- **41:4** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 41:4 (continuing 41:3 = Saints 41:2).
- **41:6** — The Vulgate reads 'quid superveniet tibi' ('what will come upon you'), whereas the Greek asks 'why do you reject' the good pleasure of the Most High. The concluding clause ('for in the underworld there is no accusation of life') now stands in restored native Vulgate 41:7.
- **41:7** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 41:7 (completing 41:6 = Saints 41:4; previously claimed absent in the note on old 41:4).
- **41:12** — The Vulgate reads 'si nati fueritis' ('if you are born'), contrasting with the Greek 'if you multiply.'
- **41:20** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 41:20 (parallel to Saints/Greek 41:16b).
- **41:24** — Latin 'discubitu in panibus' ('reclining on the loaves') reflects bad table manners (cf. Greek 'leaning an elbow on the bread'), while 'obfuscatione' ('obscuring' or 'darkening') is a distinctively Vulgate rendering where the Greek refers to contemptuous rudeness in receiving and giving.
- **41:26** — The elliptical clause "and of taking away a portion and not restoring it" (native 41:26 = Saints 41:22) continues the list of shameful offenses from native 41:21-25 (Saints 41:17-21), with Vulgate "non restituendo" ("not restoring") diverging from the Greek "and a gift."
