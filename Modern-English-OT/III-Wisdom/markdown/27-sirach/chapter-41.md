## Text

**41:1** Death, how bitter is the memory of you for a man who has peace in his possessions!

**41:2** Death, your judgment is good for a man in need and for one who is diminished in strength.

**41:3** Do not fear the judgment of death. Remember what was before you and what is to come upon you: this is the judgment from the Lord for all flesh.

**41:4** And what will come upon you in the good pleasure of the Most High? Whether ten, or a hundred, or a thousand years.

**41:5** The children of sinners become children of abominations, as do those who live beside the houses of the ungodly.

**41:6** The inheritance of the children of sinners will perish, and with their offspring will be continual disgrace.

**41:7** Children complain about an ungodly father, for on account of him they are in disgrace.

**41:8** Woe to you, ungodly men, who have abandoned the law of the Lord Most High!

**41:9** And if you are born, you will be born in a curse; and if you die, your portion will be in a curse.

**41:10** All things that are from the earth will return to the earth; so too the ungodly, from a curse to destruction.

**41:11** The mourning of people is over their own body, but the name of the ungodly will be erased.

**41:12** Take care for a good name, for this will remain with you longer than a thousand precious and great treasures.

**41:13** For a good life there is a number of days, but a good name will remain forever.

**41:14** Preserve discipline in peace, children. For hidden wisdom and unseen treasure, what benefit is there in both of them?

**41:15** A man who hides his foolishness is better than a man who hides his wisdom.

**41:16** Nevertheless, have a sense of shame regarding these things that proceed from my mouth:

**41:17** Be ashamed before father and mother of sexual immorality, and before a ruler and a powerful man of a lie;

**41:18** before a prince and a judge of an offense; before an assembly and the populace of wickedness;

**41:19** before a companion and a friend of injustice, and from the place in which you live,

**41:20** of theft; of the truth of God and the covenant; of reclining on the loaves, and of obscuring giving and receiving;

**41:21** before those who offer greetings, of silence; of gazing at a prostitute, and of turning away the face of a relative.

**41:22** Do not turn your face away from your neighbor; and of taking away a portion and not restoring it. Do not gaze at another man's wife, and do not scrutinize his female servant, nor stand by her bed. Before friends, of words of reproach; and when you have given, do not reproach.

**41:23** Do not repeat a word you have heard concerning the revealing of a hidden word, and you will truly be without shame, and you will find favor in the sight of all people. Do not be ashamed of all these things, and do not show partiality so as to sin:

## Translator Notes

- **41:4** — The Vulgate reads 'quid superveniet tibi' ('what will come upon you'), whereas the Greek asks 'why do you reject' the good pleasure of the Most High. In this Latin text the verse ends after 'a thousand years' without the concluding clause found in other editions.
- **41:9** — The Vulgate reads 'si nati fueritis' ('if you are born'), contrasting with the Greek 'if you multiply.'
- **41:20** — Latin 'discubitu in panibus' ('reclining on the loaves') reflects bad table manners (cf. Greek 'leaning an elbow on the bread'), while 'obfuscatione' ('obscuring' or 'darkening') is a distinctively Vulgate rendering where the Greek refers to contemptuous rudeness in receiving and giving.
- **41:22** — The elliptical clause "and of taking away a portion and not restoring it" continues the list of shameful offenses from 41:17-21, with Vulgate "non restituendo" ("not restoring") diverging from the Greek "and a gift."
- **41:23** — Vulgate "sine confusione" ("without shame") differs from the Greek "truly modest." The closing prohibition ("Do not be ashamed..."), which the Greek numbers 42:1, continues directly into 42:2.
- **42:1 *(additional)*** — The Latin critical text contains no separate aligned Clementine Vulgate passage at this verse slot.
