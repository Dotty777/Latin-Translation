## Text

**51:1** The prayer of Jesus son of Sirach. I will give thanks to You, Lord, King, and I will extol You, God my Savior.

**51:2** I will give thanks to Your name, because You have become a helper and protector to me, and have rescued my body from destruction, from the snare of a wicked tongue, and from the lips of those who practice falsehood; and in the sight of those standing by, You have become a helper to me.

**51:4** From the pressure of the flame that surrounded me, and in the middle of the fire I was not scorched;

**51:5** from the depth of the belly of the underworld, and from a defiled tongue, and from a word of falsehood, from a wicked king, and from an unjust tongue.

**51:6** My soul will praise the Lord until death.

**51:7** They surrounded me on every side, and there was no one to help; I was looking for the help of people, and there was none.

**51:8** I remembered Your mercy, Lord, and Your work, which are from ages past.

**51:9** You exalted my dwelling above the earth, and I pleaded because of death flowing down.

**51:10** I called upon the Lord, the Father of my Lord, that He would not abandon me in the day of my distress, and in the time of the proud, without help.

**51:12** Therefore I will give thanks, and I will speak praise to You, and I will bless the name of the Lord.

**51:13** While I was still young, before I wandered about, I sought wisdom openly in my prayer.

**51:14** Before the temple I was asking for them, and even to the very end I will seek it out; and it blossomed like an early-ripening grape.

**51:15** My heart rejoiced in it; my foot walked a straight path; from my youth I kept searching for it.

**51:16** I inclined my ear a little, and received it.

**51:17** I found much wisdom in myself, and I made much progress in it.

**51:18** For I resolved to practice it. I was zealous for good, and I will not be put to shame.

**51:19** My soul wrestled in it, and in practicing it I was strengthened.

**51:20** I directed my soul toward it, and in knowledge I found it.

**51:21** My belly was in turmoil while seeking it; therefore I will possess a good possession.

**51:22** The Lord gave me a tongue as my reward, and with it I will praise Him.

**51:23** Draw near to me, you uninstructed, and gather yourselves into the house of instruction.

**51:24** Why do you still delay? And what do you say about these things? Your souls thirst intensely.

**51:25** I opened my mouth and spoke: "Buy for yourselves without silver,

**51:26** and put your neck under the yoke, and let your soul receive instruction; for it is near at hand to find it.

**51:27** See with your eyes that I labored a little, and found much rest for myself.

**51:28** Take up instruction with a great sum of silver, and possess abundant gold in it.

**51:29** Let your soul rejoice in His mercy, and you will not be put to shame in His praise.

**51:30** Do your work before the time, and He will give you your reward in His time."

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**51:11** *(after `51:10`)* I will praise Your name continually, and I will extol it in thanksgiving; and my prayer was heard.

## Translator Notes

- **51:5** — Vulgate a rege iniquo ("from a wicked king") is a Vulgate-distinct reading where the Greek text speaks of a slanderous report to a king. Syntactically, the prepositional phrases in 51:4-5 depend on the verb of deliverance in the preceding context.
- **51:9** — Vulgate pro morte defluente ("because of death flowing down") reflects a Vulgate-distinct rendering of Greek rhyseos as derived from rheo ("flow") rather than rhyomai ("deliver," as in "deliverance from death").
- **51:10** — Vulgate Dominum patrem Domini mei ("the Lord, the Father of my Lord") follows the Greek textual tradition rather than the Hebrew ("O LORD, you are my father"), a reading widely noted in Christian exegesis for its messianic resonance.
- **51:14** — Vulgate pro illis ("for them") is a Vulgate-distinct plural where the Greek has singular "for her" (wisdom), and effloruit ("it blossomed") supplies a finite verb rather than the Greek prepositional phrase "from the blossom."
- **51:20** — Vulgate in agnitione ("in knowledge") is a Vulgate-distinct reading where the Greek text reads "in purification."
