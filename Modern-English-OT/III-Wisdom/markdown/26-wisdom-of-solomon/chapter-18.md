## Text

**18:1** For Your holy ones, however, there was very great light; and indeed they heard their voice, yet they did not see their form. And because they themselves also had not suffered the same things, they magnified You;

**18:2** and those who had previously been harmed, because they were not being harmed, gave thanks, and asked for the gift that there might be a difference.

**18:3** For this reason they had a burning pillar of fire as a guide for an unknown way, and You provided a sun without harm for a good sojourn.

**18:4** They indeed deserved to be deprived of light and to suffer the prison of darkness, those who kept Your sons confined, through whom the uncorrupted light of the law was beginning to be given to the world.

**18:5** When they planned to kill the infants of the righteous, and one son was exposed and delivered, for their rebuke You took away a multitude of sons, and destroyed them together in mighty water.

**18:6** For that night was known beforehand by our fathers, so that, truly knowing which oaths they had trusted, they might be more steadfast in spirit.

**18:7** Now the safety of the righteous indeed, but the destruction of the unrighteous, was awaited by Your people.

**18:8** For just as You harmed the adversaries, so also, calling us forth, You magnified us.

**18:9** For in secret the righteous children of the good were offering sacrifice, and in harmony they established the law of righteousness, that the righteous would alike receive both good and evil, already singing the praises of the fathers.

**18:10** But the discordant voice of the enemies resounded, and a tearful lamentation for the mourned infants was heard.

**18:11** And with a similar punishment the slave was afflicted along with the master, and the common man suffered things similar to the king's.

**18:12** Therefore all alike, under one name of death, had innumerable dead; for the living were not even sufficient to bury them, since in one moment that which was their nobler generation was destroyed.

**18:13** For not believing regarding all things because of sorceries, then indeed for the first time, when the destruction of the firstborn occurred, they acknowledged them to be the people of God.

**18:14** For while tranquil silence encompassed all things, and night in its course held the middle of its journey,

**18:15** Your all-powerful word sprang forth out of heaven, from the royal thrones, a stern warrior into the middle of the land of destruction,

**18:16** a sharp sword carrying Your unfeigned command, and standing, filled all things with death, and reached all the way to heaven while standing on the earth.

**18:17** Then immediately visions of evil dreams troubled them, and unexpected fears came upon them.

**18:18** And one thrown down half-dead here, another there, declared the cause of death on account of which he was dying.

**18:19** For the visions that troubled them forewarned of these things, so that they would not perish ignorant of why they were suffering evils.

**18:20** Then, however, the trial of death touched the righteous as well, and a commotion of the multitude took place in the wilderness; yet Your wrath did not remain for long.

**18:21** For a man without reproach, speeding to intercede for the peoples, bringing forward the shield of his service, presenting prayer and intercession through incense, withstood the wrath and put an end to the distress, showing that he is Your servant.

**18:22** Yet he overcame the crowds not by strength of body nor by the power of arms, but by a word he subdued the one who was afflicting him, recalling the oaths of the forefathers and the covenant.

**18:23** For when the dead had already fallen in heaps upon one another, he stood between, and cut off the onslaught, and divided that way which led to the living.

**18:24** For on the full-length robe that he wore was the whole world, and the great deeds of the forefathers were engraved on the four rows of stones, and Your majesty was engraved on the diadem of his head.

**18:25** To these things, however, the one who was destroying yielded, and these he dreaded; for the trial of wrath alone was sufficient.

## Translator Notes

- **18:1** — The Vulgate reads magnificabant te ('they magnified You'), explicitly directing praise to God from the righteous, whereas the Greek has emakarizon ('they called them blessed'), referring to the Egyptians congratulating the Israelites.
- **18:13** — The Vulgate reads populum Dei esse ('to be the people of God'), whereas the Greek text reads 'the people to be God's son' (theou huion).
- **18:16** — In the Vulgate, gladius acutus ('a sharp sword') is nominative in apposition to the Word itself, whereas the Greek accusative makes the sword an object carried by the warrior.
- **18:21** — The Clementine Vulgate reads prosperans (lit. 'prospering/succeeding', a variant of properans, 'hastening', matching Greek speusas) and deprecari pro populis ('to intercede for the peoples') where Greek has 'fought as champion'.
- **18:22** — Vulgate turbas ('the crowds') reflects a Greek variant ochlon ('crowd/tumult') rather than cholon ('anger/wrath').
