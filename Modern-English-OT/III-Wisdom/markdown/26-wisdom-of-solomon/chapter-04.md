## Text

**4:1** Oh, how beautiful is a chaste generation with splendor! For its memory is immortal, since it is known both before God and before people.

**4:2** When it is present, they imitate it, and they long for it when it has withdrawn; and crowned forever, it triumphs, winning the prize of undefiled contests.

**4:3** But the prolific multitude of the wicked will not be useful, and illegitimate shoots will not put down deep roots, nor will they establish a stable foundation.

**4:4** Even if they sprout in branches for a time, being weakly set, they will be shaken by the wind, and by the violence of the winds they will be uprooted.

**4:5** For the unperfected branches will be broken; and their fruits are useless and sour to eat, and fit for nothing.

**4:6** For the children who are born from unlawful sleeps are witnesses of wickedness against their parents in their examination.

**4:7** But if the righteous man is overtaken early by death, he will be in refreshment;

**4:8** for honorable old age is not one of long duration, nor reckoned by the number of years; rather, gray hair is a person's understanding,

**4:9** and the age of old age is an unblemished life.

**4:10** Pleasing God, he became beloved, and while living among sinners, he was carried away.

**4:11** He was snatched away so that malice would not alter his mind, or falsehood deceive his soul.

**4:12** For the bewitchment of frivolity obscures good things, and the instability of desire overturns a mind without malice.

**4:13** Perfected in a short time, he fulfilled long periods of time;

**4:14** for his soul was pleasing to God; for this reason He hurried to bring him out from the midst of iniquities. Yet the peoples saw and did not understand, nor did they lay such things to heart,

**4:15** that the grace of God and mercy are toward His holy ones, and regard is toward His chosen ones.

**4:16** Moreover, the righteous man who has died condemns the living wicked, and youth swiftly perfected condemns the long life of the unrighteous.

**4:17** For they will see the end of the wise man, and they will not understand what God purposed concerning him, and why the Lord safeguarded him.

**4:18** They will see and despise him; but the Lord will mock them.

**4:19** And after these things they will fall down without honor, and be in disgrace among the dead forever; because He will burst them open, puffed up without a voice, and He will shake them from their foundations, and they will be laid waste to the uttermost, and they will be groaning, and their memory will perish.

**4:20** They will come fearful in the thought of their sins, and their own iniquities will expose them to their face.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — Vulgate-distinct reading: casta generatio, cum claritate ("a chaste generation with splendor") diverges sharply from the Greek Septuagint ("Better is childlessness with virtue").
- **4:6** — Literally "from unlawful sleeps" (ex iniquis somnis), an idiom referring to illicit sexual unions.
- **4:14** — Vulgate-distinct syntax: properavit educere illum makes God ("He") the subject who hurried to bring the righteous man out, whereas the Greek has his soul hurrying out of wickedness; verse 15 also adds Dei ("of God") and reverses the order of "holy ones" and "chosen ones."
- **4:19** — Vulgate-distinct readings: decidentes ("falling down") renders the Greek noun for "corpse/fall" as a participle, and inflatos sine voce ("puffed up without a voice") diverges from the Greek ("speechless, headlong").
