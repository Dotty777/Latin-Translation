## Text

**14:1** Again, another, planning to sail and beginning to make a journey through fierce waves, calls upon a piece of wood more fragile than the wood carrying him.

**14:2** For the desire for gain devised that, and the craftsman built it by his wisdom.

**14:3** Yet Your providence, Father, steers it, because You have given a way even in the sea, and a most secure path among the waves,

**14:4** showing that You are able to save from all things, even if someone goes to the sea without skill.

**14:5** Yet so that the works of Your wisdom would not be empty, for this reason people entrust their lives even to a small piece of wood, and crossing the sea by a raft, they have been delivered.

**14:6** Moreover, even from the beginning, when the proud giants were perishing, the hope of the world, fleeing for refuge to a raft, left to the age the seed of generation, which was steered by Your hand.

**14:7** For blessed is the wood through which righteousness comes about;

**14:8** but the idol that is made by hands is cursed, both itself and the one who made it, because the one indeed did the work, while the other, though it was fragile, was named a god.

**14:9** And equally hateful to God are the ungodly person and his ungodliness;

**14:10** for indeed what was made will suffer torments along with the one who made it.

**14:11** For this reason there will be no regard even for the idols of the nations, because the creatures of God have become an object of hatred, and a temptation for the souls of people, and a trap for the feet of the foolish.

**14:12** For the beginning of fornication is the devising of idols, and their invention is the corruption of life:

**14:13** for neither were they from the beginning, nor will they be forever.

**14:14** For this futility of people came into the world, and therefore a brief end for them has been found.

**14:15** For a father, grieving with bitter mourning, made an image of the son quickly snatched away from him; and the one who then had died as a man he now began to worship as a god, and he established sacred rites and sacrifices among his servants.

**14:16** Then, as time passed and the wicked custom grew strong, this error was kept as a law, and by the command of tyrants fashioned images were worshiped.

**14:17** And as for those whom people could not honor openly because they were far away, with their likeness brought from afar, they made a visible image of the king whom they wanted to honor, so that by their eagerness they might worship the one who was absent as though present.

**14:18** Moreover, the extraordinary diligence of the craftsman led even those who were ignorant to the worship of these.

**14:19** For he, wishing to please the one who engaged him, labored with his skill to shape the likeness for the better.

**14:20** And the multitude of people, carried away by the beauty of the work, now considered the one who earlier had been honored as a man to be a god.

**14:21** And this was the deception of human life, because people, serving either affection or kings, placed the incommunicable name upon stones and pieces of wood.

**14:22** And it had not been enough for them to have erred concerning the knowledge of God, but living also in a great war of ignorance, they call so many and such great evils peace.

**14:23** For either sacrificing their children, or offering secret sacrifices, or holding vigils full of madness,

**14:24** they no longer keep either life or marriage pure, but one kills another out of envy, or grieves him by committing adultery,

**14:25** and all things are mixed together: blood, murder, theft and deceit, corruption and faithlessness, turmoil and perjury, disturbance of the good,

**14:26** forgetfulness of God, defilement of souls, alteration of birth, instability of marriage, disorder of adultery and shamelessness.

**14:27** For the worship of unspeakable idols is the cause of every evil, both the beginning and the end.

**14:28** For either while rejoicing they go mad, or surely they prophesy falsehoods, or they live unrighteously, or they quickly commit perjury.

**14:29** For while they trust in idols that are without a soul, swearing wickedly, they do not expect to be harmed.

**14:30** Therefore both will deservedly come upon them, because they thought wickedly about God, giving heed to idols, and swore unrighteously, despising righteousness in deceit.

**14:31** For it is not the power of the things sworn by, but the punishment of sinners, that always pursues the transgression of the unrighteous.

## Translator Notes

- **14:2** — The Clementine Vulgate includes 'sua' ('his'), making 'artifex' ('the craftsman') the subject and 'sapientia sua' ('by his wisdom') an ablative of means; the Greek Septuagint reads 'wisdom, the artisan, built it.'
- **14:11** — A Vulgate-distinct reading: 'non erit respectus' ('there will be no regard'), whereas the Greek Septuagint has no negative and refers to a 'visitation' (judgment) upon the idols of the nations.
- **14:14** — The Vulgate uses the nominative singular 'Supervacuitas ... hæc' with singular 'advenit' ('this futility ... came'), whereas the Greek Septuagint has a dative ('by the vainglory of men they entered').
- **14:26** — A Vulgate-distinct reading: 'Dei immemoratio' ('forgetfulness of God'), where the Greek Septuagint reads 'forgetfulness of gratitude' (or 'of favors').
- **14:31** — Latin 'juratorum' renders the Greek passive participle 'of the things sworn by' (the idols), though in Latin it could also be understood as 'of those who swear.'
