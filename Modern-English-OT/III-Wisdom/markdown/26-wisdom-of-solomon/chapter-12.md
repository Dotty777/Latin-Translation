## Text

**12:1** Oh, how good and sweet is Your Spirit in all things, Lord!

**12:2** Therefore, by degrees You reprove those who go astray, and concerning the things in which they sin You warn and speak to them, so that, having abandoned wickedness, they may believe in You, Lord.

**12:3** For those ancient inhabitants of Your holy land, whom You abhorred,

**12:4** because they performed works hateful to You through sorceries and unrighteous sacrifices,

**12:5** and killers of their own children without mercy, and eaters of human entrails, and devourers of blood from the midst of Your mystery,

**12:6** and parents, authors of helpless souls, You willed to destroy by the hands of our ancestors,

**12:7** so that they might receive a worthy sojourn of the children of God, which is the land dearer to You than all.

**12:8** Yet You spared even these as human beings, and sent wasps as forerunners of Your army, so that they might gradually exterminate them.

**12:9** Not because You were powerless to subject the ungodly to the righteous in war, or to exterminate them all at once by fierce beasts or by a harsh word,

**12:10** but judging by degrees, You gave room for repentance, not unaware that their nation is evil and their wickedness is natural, and that their thinking could not be changed for all time.

**12:11** For it was a cursed seed from the beginning; and it was not out of fear of anyone that You granted pardon for their sins.

**12:12** For who will say to You, "What have You done?" Or who will stand against Your judgment? Or who will come into Your presence as an avenger of wicked people? Or who will hold it against You if the nations that You made perish?

**12:13** For there is no other god besides You, whose care is for all things, so that You should show that You do not render judgment unrighteously.

**12:14** Neither king nor tyrant will inquire in Your presence concerning those whom You have destroyed.

**12:15** Since You are righteous, then, You govern all things righteously; You consider it alien to Your power to condemn even the one who ought not to be punished.

**12:16** For Your power is the beginning of righteousness, and because You are the Lord, You make Yourself spare all.

**12:17** For You show power, You who are not believed to be complete in power, and You expose the audacity of those who do not know You.

**12:18** But You, sovereign of power, judge with tranquility and govern us with great reverence; for the ability is at Your disposal whenever You will.

**12:19** And You taught Your people through such works that one ought to be righteous and humane, and You made Your sons full of good hope, because in judging You give room for repentance in sins.

**12:20** For if You tormented with such great attentiveness those who were enemies of Your servants and due for death, giving time and place through which they might be changed from wickedness,

**12:21** with how great diligence You judged Your sons, to whose ancestors You gave oaths and covenants of good promises!

**12:22** Therefore, when You give us discipline, You scourge our enemies many times over, so that when we judge we may think on Your goodness, and when we are judged we may hope for Your mercy.

**12:23** For this reason also, to those who lived senselessly and unrighteously in their life, You gave the utmost torments through the things that they worshiped.

**12:24** For indeed, they strayed too long on the way of error, considering as gods those things that among animals are worthless, living in the manner of senseless infants.

**12:25** Because of this, as to senseless children, You gave judgment in derision.

**12:26** But those who were not corrected by mockeries and rebukes experienced a judgment worthy of God.

**12:27** For in the things in which they grew indignant while suffering through these whom they thought to be gods, seeing as they were being exterminated by those very ones, they recognized as the true God Him whom they once denied knowing; on account of which the end of their condemnation also came upon them.

## Translator Notes

- **12:1** — The Vulgate reads "good and sweet, Lord, is Your Spirit" (bonus et suavis est, Domine, spiritus tuus), whereas the Greek Septuagint has "your incorruptible spirit."
- **12:5** — The Vulgate "from the midst of Your mystery" (a medio sacramento tuo, lit. "sacrament/mystery") diverges from the Greek Septuagint, which refers to initiates from the midst of a cultic band.
- **12:6** — The Vulgate "authors" (auctores) translates Greek authentas, which in later Greek means "murderers" or "those who kill with their own hands."
- **12:7** — The Vulgate uses the plural verb "they might receive" (perciperent) and "sojourn" (peregrinationem), whereas the Greek Septuagint has a singular verb with "the land" as subject receiving a "colony."
- **12:17** — The Vulgate reads "of those who do not know You" (horum qui te nesciunt), contrasting with the Greek Septuagint ("among those who know [You]").
- **12:18** — The Vulgate "with great reverence" (cum magna reverentia) differs from the Greek Septuagint ("with great forbearance/sparing").
