## Text

**10:1** She guarded the one who was first formed by God, the Father of the world, when he was created alone,

**10:2** and brought him out of his transgression, and gave him strength to control all things.

**10:3** When an unrighteous man withdrew from her in his anger, he perished through the anger of murdering his brother.

**10:4** Because of him, when water was destroying the earth, wisdom healed it again, steering the righteous man by a paltry piece of wood.

**10:5** She also, when the nations had gathered together in an agreement of wickedness, knew the righteous man, and preserved him blameless before God, and kept him strong amid compassion for his son.

**10:6** She delivered the righteous man as he fled from the perishing ungodly, while fire came down upon Pentapolis.

**10:7** As a witness to their wickedness, a smoking deserted land still remains, and trees bearing fruit at an uncertain time, and a standing figure of salt, a memorial of an unbelieving soul.

**10:8** For in passing by wisdom, they not only fell into this, that they were ignorant of what is good, but also left mankind a memorial of their folly, so that in the things in which they sinned they could not even remain hidden.

**10:9** But wisdom delivered those who attend to her from sorrows.

**10:10** She led the righteous man fleeing his brother's anger along straight paths, and showed him the kingdom of God, and gave him knowledge of holy things; she honored him in his labors and brought his labors to completion.

**10:11** In the deceit of those defrauding him she stood by him, and made him honorable.

**10:12** She guarded him from enemies, and protected him from deceivers; and she gave him a fierce struggle so that he might prevail and know that wisdom is more powerful than all.

**10:13** She did not abandon the righteous man when he was sold, but delivered him from sinners; and she went down with him into the pit,

**10:14** and in chains she did not abandon him, until she brought him the scepter of a kingdom and power against those who oppressed him; and she showed those who sullied him to be liars, and gave him eternal glory.

**10:15** She delivered a righteous people and a blameless offspring from the nations that oppressed them.

**10:16** She entered into the soul of the servant of God, and stood against terrifying kings with wonders and signs.

**10:17** And she rendered to the righteous the reward of their labors, and led them along a wondrous way; and she became for them a covering by day, and a light of stars through the night;

**10:18** she brought them across through the Red Sea, and carried them across through vast water.

**10:19** But their enemies she sank into the sea, and from the depth of the underworld she brought them up. Therefore the righteous carried off the spoils of the ungodly,

**10:20** and they sang praises to Your holy name, Lord, and praised Your victorious hand together,

**10:21** because wisdom opened the mouth of the mute, and made the tongues of infants articulate.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — Vulgate a Deo patre orbis terrarum places patre in the ablative in apposition to Deo ("by God, the Father of the world"), whereas the Greek has the accusative "the first-formed father of the world," referring to Adam.
- **10:5** — Vulgate cum se nationes contulissent means "when the nations had gathered together," differing from the Greek "when the nations were confused." In the final clause, in filii misericordia refers to Abraham remaining steadfast amid compassion for his son Isaac.
- **10:7** — Vulgate figmentum salis literally means "a figure of salt" (something fashioned of salt, where Greek has "pillar of salt"), and incredibilis is used in its ecclesiastical Latin active sense of "unbelieving."
- **10:12** — Vulgate reads a seductoribus ("from deceivers," where Greek has "from those lying in ambush") and sapientia ("wisdom," where Greek has "godliness").
- **10:19** — Vulgate ab altitudine inferorum eduxit illos ("from the depth of the underworld she brought them up") differs from the Greek "cast them up from the depth of the abyss," allowing illos to evoke both the drowned enemies washed ashore for despoiling and Israel's rescue from the depths.
