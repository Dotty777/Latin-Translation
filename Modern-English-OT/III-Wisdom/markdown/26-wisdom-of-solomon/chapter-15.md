## Text

**15:1** But You, our God, are kind and true, patient, and ordering all things in mercy.

**15:2** For even if we sin, we are Yours, knowing Your greatness; and if we do not sin, we know that we are counted with You.

**15:3** For to know You is complete righteousness; and to know Your righteousness and power is the root of immortality.

**15:4** For the devising of people's evil craft has not led us into error, nor the shadow of a painting, a labor without fruit, a figure carved in various colors,

**15:5** the sight of which gives desire to the senseless person, and he loves the figure of a dead image without a soul.

**15:6** Lovers of evil things are worthy to have hope in such things, both those who make them, and those who love them, and those who worship them.

**15:7** Moreover, a potter, pressing soft earth, laboriously fashions each vessel for our uses; and from the same clay he fashions vessels that are clean for use, and likewise those that are contrary to these; yet the potter is the judge of what the use of these vessels is.

**15:8** And with vain labor he who a little while before had been made from the earth fashions a god from the same clay, and after a little while returns to the place from which he was taken, when the debt of the soul that he had is demanded back from him.

**15:9** Yet his concern is not that he is about to toil, nor that his life is short; rather, he competes with goldsmiths and silversmiths, and he even imitates coppersmiths, and he claims glory because he fashions useless things.

**15:10** For his heart is ashes, and his hope is useless earth, and his life is more worthless than clay,

**15:11** because he did not know Him who fashioned him, and who breathed into him a soul that works, and who blew into him a living spirit.

**15:12** Moreover, they considered that our life is a game, and the conduct of life is arranged for profit, and that one must make gain from any source, even from evil.

**15:13** For this man knows that he sins above all others, who from the material of the earth fashions fragile vessels and carved images.

**15:14** For all the enemies of Your people, and those who rule over them, are foolish, and wretched beyond measure, proud of soul,

**15:15** because they considered all the idols of the nations to be gods, which have neither the use of eyes for seeing, nor nostrils for drawing breath, nor ears for hearing, nor fingers of hands for handling, and even their feet are sluggish for walking.

**15:16** For a human being made them, and he who borrowed breath fashioned them. For no human being will be able to fashion a god like himself.

**15:17** For since he is mortal, he fashions a dead thing with wicked hands. For he himself is better than those whom he worships, because he indeed lived, though he was mortal, whereas they never did.

**15:18** Moreover, they worship even the most wretched animals; for, being senseless, compared with these, they are worse than those.

**15:19** Yet not even in appearance can anyone perceive good things in these animals; rather, they have escaped the praise of God and His blessing.

## Translator Notes

- **15:2** — A Vulgate-distinct reading: 'et si non peccaverimus, scimus' ('and if we do not sin, we know'), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads 'but we will not sin, knowing that we are reckoned as Yours.'
- **15:4** — Latin 'umbra picturæ' ('shadow of a painting') reflects a literal rendering of Greek 'skiagraphon' ('painters,' literally 'shadow-painters'), and 'sculpta' ('carved') corresponds to Greek 'stained.'
- **15:14** — A Vulgate-distinct reading: 'supra modum animæ superbi' ('beyond measure, proud of soul,' or 'proud beyond the measure of a soul'), where the Greek Septuagint reads 'more wretched than the soul of an infant.'
- **15:18** — The Vulgate 'insensata enim comparata his, illis sunt deteriora' preserves two distinct demonstratives ('these' and 'those'), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads 'for compared in stupidity, they are worse than the others.'
