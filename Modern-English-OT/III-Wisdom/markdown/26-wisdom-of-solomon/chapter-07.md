## Text

**7:1** Indeed, I too am a mortal man, like everyone else, and of the lineage of that earthly one who was made first; and in a mother's womb I was shaped as flesh;

**7:2** in the space of ten months I was congealed in blood, from the seed of a man and the accompanying delight of sleep.

**7:3** And when I was born, I received the common air, and fell upon the similarly made earth, and weeping, I uttered a first cry like everyone else.

**7:4** In swaddling cloths I was nurtured, and with great cares;

**7:5** for no one among kings had a different beginning of birth.

**7:6** Therefore there is one entrance into life for all, and a like departure.

**7:7** For this reason I wished, and understanding was given to me; and I called out, and the spirit of wisdom came into me;

**7:8** and I preferred her to kingdoms and thrones, and I reckoned riches to be nothing in comparison with her.

**7:9** Nor did I compare a precious stone to her, because all gold in comparison with her is a little sand, and silver will be counted as mud in her sight.

**7:10** Above health and beauty I loved her, and I resolved to have her in place of light, because her light is unquenchable.

**7:11** Now all good things came to me together with her, and wealth beyond number through her hands;

**7:12** and I rejoiced in them all, because this wisdom went before me, and I did not know that she is the mother of all these things.

**7:13** Without pretense I learned her, and without envy I share her, and I do not hide her wealth.

**7:14** For she is a boundless treasure for men; those who have made use of it have become sharers in the friendship of God, commended because of the gifts of instruction.

**7:15** Now God has granted me to speak with judgment, and to conceive things worthy of those things that are given to me, because He Himself is the guide of wisdom and the corrector of the wise.

**7:16** For in His hand are both we and our words, and all wisdom, and knowledge of works, and instruction.

**7:17** For He Himself gave me true knowledge of the things that exist, so that I might know the arrangement of the world and the powers of the elements,

**7:18** the beginning and the end and the middle of times, the shifts of alternations and the changes of seasons,

**7:19** the courses of the year and the arrangements of the stars,

**7:20** the natures of animals and the rages of wild beasts, the force of winds and the thoughts of men, the differences of shrubs and the powers of roots.

**7:21** And whatever things are hidden and unforeseen I learned, for wisdom, the artisan of all things, taught me.

**7:22** For there is in her a spirit of understanding, holy, unique, manifold, subtle, eloquent, mobile, undefiled, certain, sweet, loving the good, keen, which nothing hinders, doing good,

**7:23** humane, gracious, firm, certain, untroubled, possessing all power, watching over all things, and which encompasses all spirits; intelligent, pure, subtle.

**7:24** For wisdom is more mobile than all moving things, and she reaches everywhere because of her purity.

**7:25** For she is a vapor of God's power, and a certain pure outflow of the splendor of Almighty God; and therefore nothing defiled enters into her.

**7:26** For she is the radiance of eternal light, and a mirror without blemish of the majesty of God, and an image of His goodness.

**7:27** And though she is one, she can do all things; and remaining in herself, she renews all things; and through the nations she passes into holy souls; she appoints friends of God and prophets.

**7:28** For God loves no one except the one who dwells with wisdom.

**7:29** For she is more beautiful than the sun, and above every arrangement of the stars; compared with light, she is found superior.

**7:30** For night succeeds it, but malice does not overcome wisdom.

## Translator Notes

- **7:11** — Latin honestas (also in 7:13 and 8:18), ordinarily meaning honor or dignity, carries the Late Latin sense of wealth or honorable abundance here, rendering Greek ploutos.
- **7:15** — The Vulgate uses the past indicative dedit ("has granted") rather than the optative wish ("may God grant") found in the Greek Septuagint.
- **7:21** — Latin improvisa ("unforeseen") is a Vulgate-distinct reading where the Greek Septuagint has emphane ("manifest").
- **7:23** — The Vulgate renders intelligibilis, mundus, subtilis in the nominative singular describing the spirit of wisdom itself and uses capiat ("encompasses"), whereas the Greek uses genitive plurals modifying "all spirits" with a verb meaning "penetrates through."
- **7:27** — Latin per nationes ("through the nations") is a Vulgate-distinct reading where the Greek Septuagint has kata geneas ("in every generation").
