## Text

**17:1** For great are Your judgments, Lord, and indescribable are Your words; because of this, undisciplined souls went astray.

**17:2** For while the wicked were persuaded that they could rule over the holy nation, shackled with bonds of darkness and a long night, shut in under roofs, they lay as fugitives from perpetual providence.

**17:3** And while they thought they were hidden in dark sins, they were scattered under a gloomy veil of forgetfulness, horribly terrified and troubled with extreme astonishment.

**17:4** For not even the cave that held them kept them without fear, since a descending sound troubled them, and gloomy figures appearing to them brought them terror.

**17:5** And indeed no force of fire could provide them light, nor could the bright flames of the stars illuminate that horrible night.

**17:6** Yet a sudden fire appeared to them, full of fear; and struck with fear of that appearance which was not seen, they judged the things that were seen to be worse.

**17:7** And the mockeries of the magical craft were put down, and there was a rebuke with disgrace for the glory of wisdom.

**17:8** For those who promised that they would drive out fears and troubles from a languishing soul, these languished with mockery, full of fear.

**17:9** For even if nothing from the monsters troubled them, shaken by the passing of animals and the hissing of serpents, trembling they were perishing, even denying that they saw the air, which no one could in any way escape.

**17:10** For since wickedness is timid, it gives testimony of condemnation; for it always presumes harsh things, troubled by conscience.

**17:11** For fear is nothing except a betrayal of the aids of thought.

**17:12** And while expectation from within is less, it reckons greater the ignorance of that cause from which it brings torment.

**17:13** But those who were sleeping the same sleep through a truly powerless night that came upon them both from the lowest and from the highest regions of the underworld,

**17:14** were sometimes driven by fear of monsters, and sometimes fainted through the surrender of the soul; for a sudden and unexpected fear had come upon them.

**17:15** Then, if anyone of them had fallen down, he was kept shut up in a prison without iron.

**17:16** For if anyone was a farmer, or a shepherd, or a worker at the labors of the field, and had been overtaken, he endured an inescapable necessity.

**17:17** For with one chain of darkness they were all bound together. Whether a whistling wind, or a pleasant sound of birds among dense branches of trees, or the force of water rushing down fiercely,

**17:18** or the mighty sound of rocks hurled down, or the unseen running of playing animals, or the mighty voice of bellowing beasts, or an echo resounding from the highest mountains: they made them faint from fear.

**17:19** For the whole world was illuminated with bright light, and was occupied with unhindered works.

**17:20** Over them alone, however, a heavy night had been laid, an image of the darkness that was about to come upon them; therefore they themselves were heavier to themselves than the darkness.

## Translator Notes

- **17:1** — The Vulgate includes 'Domine' ("Lord") and 'verba tua' ("Your words"), whereas the Greek reads simply "Great are Your judgments and hard to explain."
- **17:3** — Vulgate 'cum admiratione nimia' ("with extreme astonishment") is a distinctly Latin reading where the Greek reads "terrified by apparitions."
- **17:7** — Latin 'appositi erant derisus' (literally "the mockeries were put/set down") corresponds to Greek 'empaigmata katekeito' ("the illusions lay prostrate/defeated").
- **17:12** — In this syntactically dense verse, Vulgate 'de qua tormentum praestat' ("from which it brings torment") corresponds to the Greek "the cause that produces the torment."
- **17:13** — Vulgate 'et ab infimis et ab altissimis inferis' ("both from the lowest and from the highest regions of the underworld") differs from the Greek, which reads "from the recesses of powerless Hades."
