## Text

**3:1** But the souls of the righteous are in God's hand, and the torment of death will not touch them.

**3:2** In the eyes of the foolish they seemed to die, and their departure was regarded as affliction,

**3:3** and their journey away from us as destruction; but they are in peace;

**3:4** even though before men they suffered torments, their hope is full of immortality.

**3:5** In a few things they were afflicted, in many things they will be well provided for, because God tested them and found them worthy of Himself.

**3:6** Like gold in the furnace He proved them, and like the sacrifice of a burnt offering He accepted them, and in time there will be regard for them.

**3:7** The righteous will shine, and like sparks in a reed bed they will dart about.

**3:8** They will judge nations and hold dominion over peoples, and their Lord will reign forever.

**3:9** Those who trust in Him will understand the truth, and those who are faithful in love will find rest in Him, because gift and peace belong to His chosen ones.

**3:10** But the ungodly will receive chastisement according to what they thought: those who disregarded the righteous man and departed from the Lord.

**3:11** For the one who casts aside wisdom and discipline is wretched; and their hope is empty, their labors without fruit, and their works useless.

**3:12** Their wives are senseless, and their children are most wicked.

**3:13** Cursed is their offspring, because the barren woman is blessed; and the undefiled woman, who has not known a bed in transgression, will have fruit in the visitation of holy souls;

**3:14** and the eunuch who has not worked iniquity through his hands, nor thought most wicked things against God; for a chosen gift of faith will be given to him, and a most acceptable lot in the temple of God.

**3:15** For glorious is the fruit of good labors, and the root of wisdom cannot fail.

**3:16** But the children of adulterers will remain incomplete, and the seed from an unlawful bed will be destroyed.

**3:17** And if indeed they have a long life, they will be counted as nothing, and their final old age will be without honor;

**3:18** and if they die sooner, they will have no hope, nor consolation in the day of recognition.

**3:19** For the endings of an unjust nation are dire.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — Vulgate tormentum mortis ("the torment of death") adds mortis ("of death"), which is absent from the standard Greek Septuagint.
- **3:6** — The Vulgate places et in tempore erit respectus illorum ("and in time there will be regard for them") as an independent clause at the end of 3:6, whereas the Greek joins "in the time of their visitation" to the opening of 3:7; respectus ("regard") renders Greek episkope ("visitation"), as in 2:20.
- **3:9** — Vulgate donum et pax ("gift and peace") differs from the standard Greek reading charis kai eleos ("grace and mercy").
- **3:18** — Latin in die agnitionis ("in the day of recognition") renders Greek diagnoseos ("decision/examination"), referring to the day of divine judgment.
