## Text

**2:1** For they said, thinking wrongly within themselves: "The time of our life is short and accompanied by weariness, and there is no relief at a man's end, and there is no one who has been known to have returned from the underworld.

**2:2** Because we were born out of nothing, and after this we will be as though we had not been. For the breath in our nostrils is smoke, and speech is a spark to stir our heart;

**2:4** when it is extinguished, our body will be ashes, and the spirit will disperse like soft air; and our life will pass away like the trace of a cloud, and will dissolve like a mist that has been driven off by the rays of the sun and weighed down by its heat. And our name will fall into oblivion over time, and no one will hold remembrance of our works.

**2:5** For our time is the passing of a shadow, and there is no return of our end; because it has been sealed, and no one returns.

**2:6** Come, therefore, and let us enjoy the good things that exist, and let us make use of creation swiftly, as in youth.

**2:7** Let us fill ourselves with precious wine and perfumes, and let the flower of the season not pass us by.

**2:8** Let us crown ourselves with roses before they wither; let there be no meadow that our revelry does not pass through;

**2:9** let none of us be without a part in our revelry. Everywhere let us leave signs of gladness, because this is our share, and this is our lot.

**2:10** Let us oppress the righteous poor man, and let us not spare the widow, nor revere the gray hairs of the old man of long years;

**2:11** rather, let our strength be the law of righteousness; for what is weak is found to be useless.

**2:12** Let us ensnare the righteous man, therefore, because he is useless to us, and is opposed to our works, and reproaches us for sins against the law, and spreads abroad against us sins against our discipline.

**2:13** He professes to have knowledge of God, and calls himself a son of God.

**2:14** He has become to us an exposure of our thoughts.

**2:15** He is burdensome to us even to look at, because his life is unlike that of others, and his ways are altered.

**2:16** We have been considered by him as triflers, and he keeps himself away from our ways as from impurities, and he prefers the final end of the righteous, and he boasts that he has God as Father.

**2:17** Let us see, therefore, whether his words are true, and let us test what is going to come to him, and we will know what his final end will be.

**2:18** For if he is a true son of God, He will uphold him and deliver him from the hands of adversaries.

**2:19** Let us examine him with insult and torment, so that we may know his reverence and test his patience.

**2:20** Let us condemn him to a most shameful death; for there will be regard for him according to his words."

**2:21** These things they thought, and they went astray; for their malice blinded them.

**2:22** And they did not know the mysteries of God, nor did they hope for the reward of righteousness, nor did they discern the honor of holy souls.

**2:23** For God created man indestructible, and made him in the image of His own likeness.

**2:24** But through the envy of the devil death entered the world, and those who are on his side imitate him.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — Vulgate ex nihilo ("out of nothing") is distinctive compared with the Greek autoschedios ("by chance"); likewise sermo ... ad commovendum cor ("speech ... to stir our heart") reflects an Old Latin rendering of Greek logos ("reason") and en kinesei kardias ("in the beating of our heart").
- **2:4** — In the Clementine Vulgate, the text of traditional verse 2:3 is combined into verse 2:4.
- **2:13** — Vulgate filium Dei ("son of God") differs from the Greek paida kyriou ("servant/child of the Lord"), a distinctive Latin wording repeated with verus filius Dei in 2:18.
- **2:23** — Vulgate ad imaginem similitudinis suae ("in the image of His own likeness") preserves an Old Latin reading reflecting the Greek variant idiotetos ("own nature") rather than the standard Septuagint aidiotetos ("eternity").
- **2:24** — Vulgate imitantur autem illum ("imitate him [the devil]") is a well-known Vulgate-distinct reading contrasting with the Greek peirazousin de auton ("experience it [death]"); this verse also combines traditional 2:24-25 in the Clementine Vulgate.
