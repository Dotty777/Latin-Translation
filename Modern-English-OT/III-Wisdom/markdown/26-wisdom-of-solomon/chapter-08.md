## Text

**8:1** Therefore she reaches from end to end strongly, and she arranges all things sweetly.

**8:2** I loved her and sought her out from my youth, and I sought to take her to myself as a bride, and I became a lover of her beauty.

**8:3** She glorifies her noble birth, having companionship with God; and indeed, the Lord of all loved her.

**8:4** For she is the teacher of the instruction of God, and the chooser of His works.

**8:5** And if riches are desired in life, what is richer than wisdom, who works all things?

**8:6** And if understanding works, who more than she is the artisan of the things that exist?

**8:7** And if anyone loves justice, her labors have great virtues: for she teaches sobriety and prudence, and justice and fortitude; nothing in life is more useful to men than these.

**8:8** And if anyone desires an abundance of knowledge, she knows past things and judges concerning future things; she knows the subtleties of words and the solutions of arguments; she knows signs and portents before they happen, and the outcomes of times and ages.

**8:9** Therefore I resolved to bring her to myself to live with me, knowing that she will share with me in good things, and will be a consolation for my thought and my weariness.

**8:10** Because of her I will have renown before the crowds, and honor among the elders as a young man;

**8:11** and I will be found keen in judgment, and in the sight of the mighty I will be admirable, and the faces of princes will marvel at me.

**8:12** When I am silent they will wait for me, and when I speak they will look to me, and when I discourse at greater length they will place their hands upon their mouth.

**8:13** Furthermore, through her I will have immortality, and I will leave an eternal memory to those who will come after me.

**8:14** I will govern peoples, and nations will be subject to me;

**8:15** terrifying kings will fear me when they hear of me. Among the multitude I will appear good, and in war strong.

**8:16** Entering into my house, I will rest with her; for her company has no bitterness, nor does shared life with her have weariness, but gladness and joy.

**8:17** Pondering these things within myself and reflecting in my heart that immortality is in kinship with wisdom,

**8:18** and in her friendship is good delight, and in the works of her hands is wealth without failing, and in the contest of her conversation is wisdom, and high renown in the sharing of her words: I went about seeking that I might take her to myself.

**8:19** Now I was a gifted boy, and I received a good soul as my lot.

**8:20** And since I was even more good, I came to an undefiled body.

**8:21** And when I knew that I could not otherwise be self-controlled unless God gives it, and that this very thing belonged to wisdom, to know whose gift this was, I approached the Lord and implored Him, and I spoke from my whole heart:

## Translator Notes

- **8:7** — Latin virtus is used here both in the plural for "virtues" generally and in the singular at the end of the list for the fourth cardinal virtue of "fortitude" (rendering Greek andreia, courage).
- **8:8** — Latin argumentorum ("arguments") is a Vulgate-distinct reading where the Greek Septuagint has ainigmaton ("riddles").
- **8:11** — The final clause et facies principum mirabuntur me ("and the faces of princes will marvel at me") is a Vulgate-distinct addition absent from standard Greek Septuagint manuscripts.
- **8:20** — Latin cum essem magis bonus literally reads "since I was more good" (or "rather good"), rendering a debated Greek clause (mallon de agathos on, "or rather, being good") often discussed regarding the goodness of the soul and its union with the body.
- **8:21** — Latin continens ("self-controlled" or "continent") reflects a distinctly Vulgate reading of Greek enkrates, which in context can also mean "gaining possession" of wisdom.
