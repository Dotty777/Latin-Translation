## Text

**11:1** She directed their works by the hands of a holy prophet.

**11:2** They journeyed through deserts that were not inhabited, and in deserted places they set up huts.

**11:3** They stood against foes, and avenged themselves on their enemies.

**11:4** They thirsted and called upon You, and water was given to them from a very high rock, and relief from thirst from hard stone.

**11:5** For through the things by which their enemies suffered punishment from the lack of their drink, and in which the sons of Israel rejoiced when they had abundance, through these same things, when they lacked them, they were treated well.

**11:6** For indeed, in place of the spring of an ever-flowing river, You gave human blood to the unrighteous.

**11:7** While they were diminished in the reproof of the killed infants, You gave them abundant water unexpectedly,

**11:8** showing through the thirst that occurred then how You exalted Your own and put their adversaries to death.

**11:9** For when they were tested, though indeed receiving discipline with mercy, they knew how the ungodly, judged with anger, suffered torments.

**11:10** These indeed You tried like a father who admonishes; but those You condemned like a harsh king who interrogates.

**11:11** For both absent and present, they were tormented alike.

**11:12** For a double weariness and groaning had seized them, along with the memory of past things.

**11:13** For when they heard that through their own torments they were being treated well, they remembered the Lord, marveling at the end of the outcome.

**11:14** For the one whom they mocked when he was cast out in wicked exposure, they admired at the end of the result, not thirsting in the same way as the righteous.

**11:15** But in return for the senseless thoughts of their iniquity, because some, going astray, worshiped mute serpents and worthless beasts, You sent upon them a multitude of mute animals for vengeance,

**11:16** so that they might know that through the things by which one sins, through these one is also tormented.

**11:17** For Your almighty hand, which created the world out of unseen matter, was not unable to send upon them a multitude of bears or bold lions,

**11:18** or unknown beasts of a new kind, full of anger, either breathing a vapor of fires, or putting forth an odor of smoke, or shooting terrifying sparks from their eyes.

**11:19** Not only could an injury from these exterminate them, but even the sight of these could kill through fear.

**11:20** Yet even without these they could have been killed by a single breath, pursued by their very own deeds and scattered by the breath of Your power; but You have set all things in order by measure, number, and weight.

**11:21** For great strength has always remained with You alone; and who will resist the power of Your arm?

**11:22** Because like the tipping of a scale, so is the world before You, and like a drop of pre-dawn dew that comes down upon the earth.

**11:23** Yet You show mercy to all, because You are able to do all things; and You overlook the sins of mankind on account of repentance.

**11:24** For You love all that exists, and You hate nothing of the things You have made; for You neither established nor made anything while hating it.

**11:25** And how could anything endure unless You had willed it? Or how could that which had not been called by You be preserved?

**11:26** Yet You spare all things, because they are Yours, Lord, You who love souls.

## Translator Notes

- **11:5** — The Vulgate includes the expanded clause a defectione potus sui, et in eis cum abundarent filii Israel laetati sunt ("from the lack of their drink, and in which the sons of Israel rejoiced when they had abundance"), which is absent from the Greek text.
- **11:6** — Vulgate specifies humanum sanguinem ("human blood"), whereas the Greek reads "foul blood."
- **11:7** — Vulgate Qui cum minuerentur ("While they were diminished") reflects a shift in pronoun reference between Egypt's punishment for the killed infants and God's unexpected gift of water to Israel; in 11:8 the Vulgate also adds tuos exaltares ("how You exalted Your own"), absent from the Greek.
- **11:13** — Vulgate bene secum agi uses the reflexive secum loosely in indirect discourse to refer to Israel's well-being (as in 11:5), and adds admirantes in finem exitus ("marveling at the end of the outcome"), which is absent from the Greek.
- **11:17** — Vulgate reads ex materia invisa ("out of unseen matter"), differing from the Greek "out of formless matter"; similarly in 11:20 the Vulgate has ab ipsis factis suis ("by their very own deeds") where the Greek has "by Justice."
