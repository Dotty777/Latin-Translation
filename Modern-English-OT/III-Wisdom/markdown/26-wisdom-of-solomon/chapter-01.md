## Text

**1:1** Love righteousness, you who judge the earth. Think of the Lord in goodness, and seek Him in simplicity of heart,

**1:2** for He is found by those who do not test Him, and He appears to those who have faith in Him.

**1:3** For crooked thoughts separate from God; and power, when tested, reproves the foolish.

**1:4** For wisdom will not enter a malevolent soul, nor live in a body subjected to sins.

**1:5** For the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee deceit, and will withdraw Himself from thoughts that are without understanding, and will be checked by approaching iniquity.

**1:6** For the Spirit of wisdom is benevolent, and will not free a blasphemer from his own lips; because God is the witness of his inward parts, and the true examiner of his heart, and the hearer of his tongue.

**1:7** For the Spirit of the Lord has filled the entire world, and that which holds all things together has knowledge of speech.

**1:8** Because of this, the one who speaks unjust things cannot remain hidden, nor will reproving judgment pass him by.

**1:9** For there will be an inquiry into the thoughts of the ungodly man, and the report of his words will come to God for the reproof of his iniquities.

**1:10** Because the ear of jealousy hears all things, and the noise of grumblings will not be concealed.

**1:11** Guard yourselves, therefore, from grumbling that profits nothing, and spare your tongue from slander; for a hidden word will not go in vain, and the mouth that lies kills the soul.

**1:12** Do not eagerly desire death in the error of your life, nor acquire destruction in the works of your hands.

**1:13** For God did not make death, nor does He rejoice in the destruction of the living.

**1:14** For He created all things so that they might exist, and He made the nations of the world healable; and there is no poison of destruction in them, nor is the kingdom of the underworld on the earth.

**1:15** For righteousness is perpetual and immortal.

**1:16** But the ungodly with hands and words summoned it, and considering it a friend, they wasted away; and they made pledges with it, because they are worthy to be on its side.

## Translator Notes

- **1:5** — Latin corripietur a superveniente iniquitate is a difficult passive rendering of Greek elenchthesetai, literally meaning "will be checked/rebuked by approaching iniquity," expressing that the Spirit withdraws or is offended when iniquity comes near.
- **1:14** — Vulgate sanabiles fecit nationes ("made the nations healable") is a distinctive Old Latin/Vulgate reading, whereas the Greek text reads "the generative creatures [or generations] of the world are wholesome."
- **1:16** — Latin feminine illam ("it") and amicam ("a friend") refer back to mortem ("death," feminine in Latin) in 1:12-13 rather than to justitia ("righteousness") in the parenthetical 1:15.
