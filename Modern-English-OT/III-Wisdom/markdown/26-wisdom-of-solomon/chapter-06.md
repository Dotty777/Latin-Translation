## Text

**6:1** Wisdom is better than strength, and a prudent man than a strong one. Listen, therefore, kings, and understand; learn, judges of the ends of the earth.

**6:2** Give ear, you who govern multitudes and are pleased with yourselves over crowds of nations.

**6:3** For power was given to you by the Lord, and might by the Most High, who will examine your works and search out your thoughts.

**6:4** Because while you were ministers of His kingdom, you did not judge rightly, nor did you keep the law of righteousness, nor did you walk according to the will of God.

**6:5** Dreadfully and swiftly He will appear to you, because the harshest judgment will come upon those who rule.

**6:6** For mercy is granted to the lowly; but the mighty will mightily suffer torments.

**6:7** For God will not exempt anyone's person, nor will He stand in awe of anyone's greatness, because He Himself made the small and the great, and He has equal care for all.

**6:8** But for the stronger, a stronger torment is at hand.

**6:9** To you, therefore, kings, are these words of mine, so that you may learn wisdom and not fall away.

**6:10** For those who have kept just things justly will be justified, and those who have learned these things will find what to answer.

**6:11** Desire my words, therefore; love them, and you will have discipline.

**6:12** Radiant is wisdom, and she never fades; and she is easily seen by those who love her, and found by those who seek her.

**6:13** She anticipates those who desire her, so that she may show herself to them first.

**6:14** Whoever wakes at daybreak for her will not toil; for he will find her sitting beside his doors.

**6:15** Therefore to think upon her is perfected understanding, and whoever keeps watch on account of her will swiftly be free from care.

**6:16** For she herself goes about seeking those worthy of her, and along the paths she shows herself cheerfully, and in every providence she meets them.

**6:17** For her beginning is the truest desire for discipline.

**6:18** Therefore care for discipline is love, and love is the keeping of her laws; and the observance of laws is the perfection of incorruption;

**6:19** and incorruption makes one near to God.

**6:20** Desire for wisdom, therefore, leads to an everlasting kingdom.

**6:21** If, therefore, you delight in thrones and scepters, kings of the people, love wisdom, so that you may reign forever; love the light of wisdom, all you who rule over peoples.

**6:22** Now what wisdom is, and how she came to be, I will declare, and I will not hide the mysteries of God from you; but I will investigate from the beginning of her birth, and I will set the knowledge of her in the light, and I will not pass by the truth.

**6:23** Neither will I journey with wasting envy, because such a person will not be a partaker of wisdom.

**6:24** Now a multitude of the wise is the health of the whole world, and a wise king is the stability of the people.

**6:25** Therefore receive discipline through my words, and it will profit you.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — Vulgate-distinct addition: the opening proverb Melior est sapientia quam vires, et vir prudens quam fortis ("Wisdom is better than strength, and a prudent man than a strong one") appears in the Clementine Vulgate but not in the standard Greek Septuagint.
- **6:7** — Vulgate-distinct readings: Latin reads Deus ("God") instead of the Greek "Master of all," renders "will not shrink from" literally as subtrahet ("will not exempt"), and contains the pleonastic double genitive ejus cujusquam ("anyone's").
- **6:8** — Vulgate-distinct reading: cruciatio ("torment") diverges from the Greek Septuagint ("scrutiny/examination").
- **6:21** — Vulgate-distinct addition: the closing exhortation diligite lumen sapientiæ, omnes qui præestis populis ("love the light of wisdom, all you who rule over peoples") is a Vulgate expansion absent from the main Greek text, as is perpetuum ("everlasting") in 6:20.
