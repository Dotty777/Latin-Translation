## Text

**136:1** A psalm of David, of Jeremiah. Beside the rivers of Babylon, there we sat and wept as we remembered Zion.

**136:2** Upon the willows in its midst we hung our instruments,

**136:3** for there those who led us captive asked us for words of songs, and those who carried us away said, "Sing to us a hymn from the songs of Zion."

**136:4** How will we sing the song of the Lord in a foreign land?

**136:5** If I forget you, Jerusalem, may my right hand be given to oblivion.

**136:6** May my tongue cling to my throat if I do not remember you, if I do not set Jerusalem at the beginning of my joy.

**136:7** Be mindful, Lord, of the sons of Edom on the day of Jerusalem, who say, "Empty it out, empty it out, all the way to the foundation in it."

**136:8** Wretched daughter of Babylon! Blessed is the one who will repay you your repayment that you repaid to us.

**136:9** Blessed is the one who will grasp and dash your little ones against the rock.

## Translator Notes

- **136:1** — Following the Septuagint tradition, the Vulgate includes the superscription "A psalm of David, of Jeremiah" (absent from the Hebrew text), associating this exilic lament with Jeremiah.
- **136:2** — Latin "organa" ("instruments") follows the Septuagint general term for musical instruments, whereas the Hebrew specifies "lyres" or "harps."
- **136:3** — Latin "qui abduxerunt nos" ("those who carried us away") and "Hymnum" ("a hymn") follow the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "our tormentors [demanded] mirth."
- **136:5** — Latin "oblivioni detur" ("be given to oblivion") reflects the passive Septuagint reading ("be forgotten"), whereas the Hebrew is commonly understood actively ("let my right hand forget [its skill]").
- **136:8** — Latin "misera" ("wretched") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "devastated" or "doomed to destruction."
