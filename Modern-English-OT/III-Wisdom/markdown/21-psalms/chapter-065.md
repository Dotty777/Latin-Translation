## Text

**65:1** For the end. A song of a psalm of the resurrection. Shout joyfully to God, all the earth;

**65:2** sing a psalm to His name; give glory to His praise.

**65:3** Say to God, "How fearsome are Your works, Lord! In the abundance of Your power Your enemies will lie to You.

**65:4** May all the earth worship You and sing praises to You; may it sing a psalm to Your name."

**65:5** Come and see the works of God: He is fearsome in counsels over the sons of men.

**65:6** He who turns the sea into dry land; they will pass through the river on foot; there we will rejoice in Him.

**65:7** He who rules in His power forever; His eyes look over the nations; let those who provoke not be exalted in themselves.

**65:8** Bless our God, nations, and make the voice of His praise heard,

**65:9** who set my soul to life and did not give my feet over to shaking.

**65:10** For You tested us, God; You tried us with fire, just as silver is tried.

**65:11** You brought us into a snare; You laid tribulations on our back;

**65:12** You placed men over our heads. We passed through fire and water, and You brought us out into refreshment.

**65:13** I will enter Your house with burnt offerings; I will pay You my vows

**65:14** which my lips pronounced and my mouth spoke in my tribulation.

**65:15** I will offer You burnt offerings full of marrow, with the incense of rams; I will offer You oxen with male goats.

**65:16** Come, listen, and I will tell, all you who fear God, what great things He has done for my soul.

**65:17** To Him I cried out with my mouth, and I exalted Him under my tongue.

**65:18** If I have looked upon iniquity in my heart, the Lord will not hear.

**65:19** Therefore God has heard, and has attended to the voice of my supplication.

**65:20** Blessed be God, who has not removed my prayer or His mercy from me.

## Translator Notes

- **65:1** — Latin "In finem" ("For the end") reflects the Septuagint ("eis to telos") rather than Hebrew "for the choirmaster," and "resurrectionis" ("of the resurrection") is a Vulgate/Septuagint addition to the superscription.
- **65:3** — Latin "mentientur tibi" ("will lie to You," reflecting Septuagint "pseusontai") idiomatically conveys feigned or cringing submission before God's power.
- **65:5** — Latin "in consiliis" ("in counsels") follows the Septuagint ("en tais boulais"), whereas the Hebrew text reads "in deeds."
- **65:9** — The Vulgate and Septuagint read singular "animam meam" and "pedes meos" ("my soul," "my feet"), whereas the Hebrew has the plural "our."
