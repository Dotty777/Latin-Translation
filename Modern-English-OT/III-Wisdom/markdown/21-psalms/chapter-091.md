## Text

**91:1** A psalm of a song, on the Sabbath day.

**91:2** It is good to give thanks to the Lord, and to sing psalms to Your name, Most High,

**91:3** to declare Your mercy in the morning, and Your truth through the night,

**91:4** on a ten-stringed instrument, the psaltery; with a song, on the lyre.

**91:5** For You have delighted me, Lord, in Your handiwork; and in the works of Your hands I will exult.

**91:6** How magnified are Your works, Lord! Your thoughts have become exceedingly deep.

**91:7** An unwise man will not know, and a fool will not understand these things.

**91:8** When sinners have sprung up like grass, and all who work iniquity have appeared, so that they may perish forever and ever,

**91:9** yet You are the Most High forever, Lord.

**91:10** For look, Your enemies, Lord, for look, Your enemies will perish; and all who work iniquity will be scattered.

**91:11** And my horn will be exalted like that of a unicorn, and my old age in rich mercy.

**91:12** And my eye has looked down on my enemies, and my ear will hear concerning the evildoers rising up against me.

**91:13** The righteous one will flourish like a palm tree; like a cedar of Lebanon he will be multiplied.

**91:14** Planted in the house of the Lord, they will flourish in the courts of the house of our God.

**91:15** They will still be multiplied in a rich old age, and they will be well-enduring,

**91:16** so that they may declare that the Lord our God is upright, and there is no iniquity in Him.

## Translator Notes

- **91:11** — Latin "unicornis" ("unicorn") and "senectus mea in misericordia uberi" ("my old age in rich mercy") follow the Septuagint, whereas the Masoretic Text reads "wild ox" and "I have been anointed with fresh oil."
- **91:13** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "will be multiplied" (matching v. 15) and in v. 14 adds "of the house" before "of our God," whereas the Masoretic Text reads "will grow tall."
- **91:15** — Latin "bene patientes erunt" ("will be well-enduring") is a literal rendering of the Septuagint "eupathountes" ("faring well" or "thriving"), whereas the Masoretic Text reads "full of sap and green."
