## Text

**59:1** To the end, for those who will be changed, as a title inscription, for David himself, for instruction,

**59:2** when he set fire to Mesopotamia of Syria and Sobal, and Joab turned back, and struck down Idumea in the Valley of the Salt Pits, twelve thousand.

**59:3** God, You have rejected us and torn us down; You have been angry, and You have had mercy on us.

**59:4** You have shaken the earth and thrown it into turmoil; heal its fractures, for it has been shaken.

**59:5** You have shown Your people harsh things; You have given us wine of compunction to drink.

**59:6** You have given a signal to those who fear You, so that they may flee from the face of the bow, so that Your beloved ones may be delivered.

**59:7** Save by Your right hand, and hear me.

**59:8** God has spoken in His sanctuary: "I will rejoice and apportion Shechem, and I will measure out the valley of tents.

**59:9** Mine is Gilead, and Mine is Manasseh; and Ephraim is the strength of My head. Judah is My king;

**59:10** Moab is the pot of My hope. Into Idumea I will stretch out My sandal; to Me foreigners have been subjected."

**59:11** Who will lead me into the fortified city? Who will lead me all the way into Idumea?

**59:12** Is it not You, God, who have rejected us? And will You not go out, God, with our forces?

**59:13** Give us help from affliction, for the deliverance of man is vain.

**59:14** In God we will show strength, and He Himself will reduce to nothing those who afflict us.

## Translator Notes

- **59:1** — The Vulgate superscription follows the Septuagint in rendering "for those who will be changed" (pro his qui immutabuntur) and "as a title inscription" (in tituli inscriptionem), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text (Psalm 60) has the musical and literary designations Shushan-eduth and Miktam.
- **59:2** — Latin succendit ("set fire to"), Mesopotamiam Syriæ et Sobal ("Mesopotamia of Syria and Sobal"), and Idumæam ("Idumea") follow the Septuagint forms corresponding to Hebrew "strove with Aram-naharaim and Aram-zobah" and "Edom."
- **59:3** — Latin misertus es nobis ("You have had mercy on us") is a past indicative following the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew text has an imperative ("restore us").
- **59:5** — Latin compunctionis ("compunction," literally a piercing sorrow or stupor, rendering Greek katanyxis) differs from the Hebrew term for "staggering" or "reeling"; similarly, in 59:6 a facie arcus ("from the face of the bow") reflects a Septuagint reading distinct from Hebrew "because of truth."
- **59:10** — Several Vulgate-distinct readings in verses 8-10 follow the Septuagint: convallem tabernaculorum ("valley of tents," translating Hebrew place-name Succoth), Juda rex meus ("Judah is My king," vs. Hebrew "scepter"), olla spei meæ ("pot of My hope," vs. Hebrew "washbowl"), alienigenæ ("foreigners," vs. Hebrew "Philistia"), and passive subditi sunt ("have been subjected").
