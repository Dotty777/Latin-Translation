## Text

**103:1** To David himself. Bless the Lord, my soul. Lord my God, You are exceedingly magnified. You have put on praise and splendor,

**103:2** wrapped in light as in a garment; stretching out heaven like a skin,

**103:3** who cover its upper chambers with waters; who set a cloud as Your ascent; who walk upon the wings of the winds;

**103:4** who make Your angels spirits, and Your ministers a burning fire.

**103:5** You who founded the earth upon its stability: it will not be tilted forever and ever.

**103:6** The deep, like a garment, is its covering; above the mountains the waters will stand.

**103:7** At Your rebuke they will flee; at the voice of Your thunder they will be terrified.

**103:8** The mountains ascend, and the plains descend, to the place that You founded for them.

**103:9** You set a boundary that they will not cross, nor will they turn back to cover the earth.

**103:10** You who send forth springs in the valleys: between the mountains the waters will pass through.

**103:11** All the beasts of the field will drink; the wild donkeys will wait in their thirst.

**103:12** Above them the birds of heaven will dwell; from the midst of the rocks they will give forth voices.

**103:13** Watering the mountains from His upper chambers; from the fruit of Your works the earth will be satisfied;

**103:14** producing grass for the livestock, and vegetation for the service of men, so that You may bring forth bread from the earth,

**103:15** and wine may gladden the heart of man, so that he may cheer the face with oil, and bread may strengthen the heart of man.

**103:16** The trees of the plain will be fully satisfied, and the cedars of Lebanon which He planted;

**103:17** there sparrows will build nests; the house of the heron is their leader.

**103:18** The high mountains are for stags; the rock is a refuge for hedgehogs.

**103:19** He made the moon for seasons; the sun knows its setting.

**103:20** You set darkness, and it became night; in it all the beasts of the forest will pass through:

**103:21** lion cubs roaring so that they may seize prey, and seek food for themselves from God.

**103:22** The sun has risen, and they have gathered together, and in their lairs they will settle down.

**103:23** Man will go out to his work, and to his labor until evening.

**103:24** How magnified are Your works, Lord! You have made all things in wisdom; the earth is filled with Your possession.

**103:25** This sea is great and spacious of hands; there are creeping things of which there is no number: living creatures, small with great.

**103:26** There ships will pass through; this dragon that You formed to play with it.

**103:27** All things wait expectantly upon You, that You may give them food in due time.

**103:28** When You give to them, they will gather; when You open Your hand, all things will be filled with goodness.

**103:29** But when You turn Your face away, they will be troubled; You will take away their spirit, and they will fail, and they will return to their dust.

**103:30** You will send forth Your Spirit, and they will be created, and You will renew the face of the earth.

**103:31** May the glory of the Lord be forever; the Lord will rejoice in His works.

**103:32** He who looks upon the earth, and makes it tremble; who touches the mountains, and they smoke.

**103:33** I will sing to the Lord in my life; I will sing psalms to my God as long as I exist.

**103:34** May my speech be pleasing to Him; and I indeed will delight in the Lord.

**103:35** May sinners cease from the earth, and the unjust, so that they are no more. Bless the Lord, my soul.

## Translator Notes

- **103:1** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate includes the superscription 'To David himself' (absent in the Masoretic Text, where this is Psalm 104). Latin confessionem (literally 'confession') here carries its ecclesiastical sense of praise and acknowledgment, where the Hebrew reads 'majesty.'
- **103:11** — Vulgate expectabunt ('will wait') follows the Septuagint, whereas the Masoretic Text reads 'quench' their thirst. In the Latin grammar, omnes bestiae is nominative plural, making the beasts the subject of potabunt ('will drink').
- **103:15** — The unexpressed third-person singular subject of ut exhilaret ('so that he may cheer') is ambiguous in Latin and may be understood either as man (from preceding hominis) or as God.
- **103:17** — Vulgate herodii domus dux est eorum ('the house of the heron is their leader') follows the Septuagint, where the Masoretic Text reads that the stork has its home in the fir trees.
- **103:25** — Latin spatiosum manibus (literally 'spacious of hands') preserves a literal biblical idiom expressing vast breadth in every direction.
