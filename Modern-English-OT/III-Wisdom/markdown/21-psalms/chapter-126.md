## Text

**126:1** A song of the steps, of Solomon. Unless the Lord builds the house, those who build it have labored in vain. Unless the Lord guards the city, the one who guards it stays awake to no avail.

**126:2** It is in vain for you to rise before daylight; rise after you have sat down, you who eat the bread of pain. When He gives sleep to His beloved,

**126:3** look, the inheritance of the Lord is sons; the reward is the fruit of the womb.

**126:4** Like arrows in the hand of a mighty man, so are the sons of those who have been shaken out.

**126:5** Blessed is the man who has filled his desire from them; he will not be put to shame when he speaks to his enemies in the gate.

## Translator Notes

- **126:1** — The Vulgate shifts tense between the two halves of the verse, using the perfect "laboraverunt" ("have labored") in the first clause and the present "vigilat" ("stays awake") in the second.
- **126:2** — Vulgate "surgite postquam sederitis" ("rise after you have sat down") is an imperative following the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "going late to rest." The final clause "Cum dederit dilectis suis somnum," ("When He gives sleep to His beloved,") introduces a temporal clause completed in v. 3, whereas the Hebrew reads "for so He gives to His beloved in sleep."
- **126:4** — Vulgate "filii excussorum" ("the sons of those who have been shaken out") follows the Septuagint, reflecting a different vocalization/root from the Hebrew Masoretic reading "sons of youth."
- **126:5** — Vulgate "desiderium suum" ("his desire") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "his quiver."
