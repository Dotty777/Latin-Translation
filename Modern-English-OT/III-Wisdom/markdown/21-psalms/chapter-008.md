## Text

**8:1** To the end, for the winepresses. A psalm of David.

**8:2** Lord, our Lord, how wondrous is Your name throughout the whole earth! For Your splendor has been lifted above the heavens.

**8:3** From the mouth of infants and nursing babies You have perfected praise on account of Your enemies, so that You may destroy the enemy and the avenger.

**8:4** For I will see Your heavens, the works of Your fingers, the moon and the stars that You have founded.

**8:5** What is man, that You remember him? Or the son of man, that You visit him?

**8:6** You have made him a little less than the angels; with glory and honor You have crowned him,

**8:7** and You have appointed him over the works of Your hands.

**8:8** You have subjected all things beneath his feet, sheep and oxen, all of them, and in addition the beasts of the field,

**8:9** the birds of the sky, and the fish of the sea that traverse the paths of the sea.

**8:10** Lord, our Lord, how wondrous is Your name throughout the whole earth!

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate renders the Hebrew musical heading ("for the choirmaster, upon the Gittith") as "To the end, for the winepresses" (In finem, pro torcularibus).
- **8:3** — Following the Septuagint (and as quoted in Matthew 21:16), the Vulgate reads "You have perfected praise" (perfecisti laudem), whereas the Hebrew reads "You have established strength."
- **8:4** — The Vulgate uses the future indicative "For I will see" (Quoniam videbo), following the Septuagint, where the Hebrew reads "When I look at."
- **8:6** — Following the Septuagint (as echoed in Hebrews 2:7), the Vulgate reads "than the angels" (ab angelis), whereas the Hebrew reads "than God" (or "heavenly beings").
