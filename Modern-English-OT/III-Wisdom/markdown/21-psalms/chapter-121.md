## Text

**121:1** A song of steps. I rejoiced in the things that were spoken to me: "We will go into the house of the Lord."

**121:2** Our feet were standing in your courts, Jerusalem.

**121:3** Jerusalem, which is built as a city, whose participation is in unity.

**121:4** For there the tribes went up, the tribes of the Lord, a testimony for Israel, to give praise to the name of the Lord.

**121:5** For there thrones were set in judgment, thrones over the house of David.

**121:6** Ask for the things that pertain to the peace of Jerusalem, and abundance for those who love you.

**121:7** May there be peace in your strength, and abundance in your towers.

**121:8** For the sake of my brothers and my neighbors, I spoke peace concerning you.

**121:9** For the sake of the house of the Lord our God, I sought good things for you.

## Translator Notes

- **121:1** — Latin "in his quæ dicta sunt mihi" ("in the things that were spoken to me") uses a neuter plural passive construction, and "ibimus" ("We will go") is future indicative following the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "when they said to me, 'Let us go.'"
- **121:3** — Latin literally reads "whose participation of it [is] in the very same" ("cujus participatio ejus in idipsum"), a calque of the Septuagint idiom meaning "together" or "in unity" with a Semitic resumptive pronoun; the Hebrew reads "that is bound firmly together." In verse 2, Latin "atriis" ("courts") also follows the Septuagint against Hebrew "gates."
- **121:5** — Latin literally reads "seats sat in judgment, seats over the house of David" ("sederunt sedes in judicio, sedes super domum David"), following the Septuagint "over the house" where the Hebrew reads "for judgment, the thrones of the house of David."
- **121:6** — Latin "abundantia" is nominative, forming a verbless blessing ("and [may there be] abundance for those who love you") that follows the Septuagint against the Hebrew verb "may they prosper."
- **121:7** — Following the Septuagint, Latin "in virtute tua" ("in your strength") and "in turribus tuis" ("in your towers") differ from the Hebrew "within your ramparts" and "within your palaces." In verses 8-9, the Vulgate also uses past tenses ("loquebar," "quæsivi") and "de te" ("concerning you") where the Hebrew has cohortatives and "within you."
