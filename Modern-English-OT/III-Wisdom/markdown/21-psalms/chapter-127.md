## Text

**127:1** A song of the steps. Blessed are all who fear the Lord, who walk in His ways.

**127:2** Because you will eat the labors of your hands, you are blessed, and it will go well for you.

**127:3** Your wife will be like an abundant vine in the sides of your house; your sons will be like new shoots of olive trees all around your table.

**127:4** Look, in this way the man who fears the Lord will be blessed.

**127:5** May the Lord bless you from Zion, and may you see the good things of Jerusalem all the days of your life.

**127:6** And may you see the sons of your sons: peace upon Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **127:1** — Vulgate "Beati omnes qui timent... qui ambulant" is plural ("Blessed are all who fear... who walk"), following the Septuagint, where the Hebrew uses the singular ("everyone who fears...").
- **127:2** — Latin "Labores manuum tuarum quia manducabis" places "quia" ("because") after the fronted object and preserves the metonymy of eating one's "labors" (meaning the produce of one's work).
- **127:3** — Vulgate "vitis abundans in lateribus domus tuae" ("an abundant vine in the sides of your house") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "a fruitful vine within the innermost parts of your house."
- **127:6** — Vulgate "pacem" ("peace") is in the accusative case, making it grammatically a further direct object of "videas" ("may you see"), whereas the Hebrew and Septuagint treat it as an independent blessing ("Peace be upon Israel").
