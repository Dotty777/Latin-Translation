## Text

**60:1** To the end. In hymns of David.

**60:2** Hear, God, my supplication; attend to my prayer.

**60:3** From the ends of the earth I cried out to You while my heart was in distress; on a rock You lifted me up. You led me,

**60:4** for You have become my hope: a tower of strength from the face of the enemy.

**60:5** I will dwell in Your tabernacle for all ages; I will be sheltered in the covering of Your wings.

**60:6** For You, my God, have heard my prayer; You have given a heritage to those who fear Your name.

**60:7** You will add days upon the days of the king, his years all the way to the day of generation and generation.

**60:8** He remains forever in the sight of God; who will seek out His mercy and truth?

**60:9** Thus I will sing a psalm to Your name forever and ever, so that I may fulfill my vows day by day.

## Translator Notes

- **60:1** — Latin In hymnis ("In hymns") follows the Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew superscription (Psalm 61), which refers to stringed instruments.
- **60:3** — Latin exaltasti me. Deduxisti me ("You lifted me up. You led me") uses past indicatives following the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew text has a petition ("lead me to the rock that is higher than I").
- **60:6** — Latin orationem meam ("my prayer") renders the Greek word euche ("prayer" or "vow"), whereas the Hebrew text specifically says "my vows"; in 60:7 usque in diem ("all the way to the day") likewise reflects the Septuagint wording.
- **60:8** — Latin misericordiam et veritatem ejus quis requiret? ("who will seek out His mercy and truth?") is a question following the Septuagint reading of Aramaic man ("who?"), whereas the Hebrew text is generally read as an imperative ("appoint mercy and truth to preserve him").
