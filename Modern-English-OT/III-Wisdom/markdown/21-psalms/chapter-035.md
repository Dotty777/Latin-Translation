## Text

**35:1** To the end. For the servant of the Lord, David himself.

**35:2** The unrighteous man has said within himself that he will transgress: there is no fear of God before his eyes.

**35:3** For he has acted deceitfully in His sight, so that his iniquity may be found hateful.

**35:4** The words of his mouth are iniquity and deceit; he has refused to understand so as to do good.

**35:5** He has meditated on iniquity on his bed; he has taken his stand on every way that is not good; yet he has not hated malice.

**35:6** Lord, in heaven is Your mercy, and Your truth reaches as far as the clouds.

**35:7** Your righteousness is like the mountains of God; Your judgments are a vast abyss. Men and beasts You will save, Lord,

**35:8** just as You have multiplied Your mercy, God. But the sons of men will hope in the shelter of Your wings.

**35:9** They will be intoxicated with the abundance of Your house, and from the torrent of Your delight You will give them to drink:

**35:10** for with You is the spring of life, and in Your light we will see light.

**35:11** Extend Your mercy to those who know You, and Your righteousness to those who are upright in heart.

**35:12** May the foot of pride not come upon me, and may the hand of the sinner not move me.

**35:13** There those who practice iniquity fell; they were driven out, and they could not stand.

## Translator Notes

- **35:1** — Latin In finem ("To the end") renders the Septuagint superscription eis to telos, corresponding to the Hebrew liturgical heading lamnatseach ("for the director").
- **35:2** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads Dixit injustus ut delinquat in semetipso ("The unrighteous man has said within himself that he will transgress"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Transgression speaks to the wicked deep in my [or his] heart."
- **35:3** — Latin in conspectu ejus ("in His sight") is traditionally understood in the Vulgate as referring to God (from v. 2), though the pronoun could alternatively refer to the sinner himself as in the Hebrew; ad odium literally means "for hatred."
- **35:8** — The Clementine Vulgate connects v. 8 to v. 7 with quemadmodum ("just as") and follows the Septuagint in reading multiplicasti ("You have multiplied") and tegmine ("shelter/covering"), where the Hebrew begins a new exclamation ("How precious is Your steadfast love") and speaks of the "shadow" of God's wings.
