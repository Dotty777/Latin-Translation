## Text

**26:1** A psalm of David, before he was anointed. The Lord is my illumination and my salvation; whom will I fear? The Lord is the protector of my life; before whom will I tremble?

**26:2** While those who do harm draw near against me to eat my flesh, my enemies who afflict me, they themselves have grown weak and fallen.

**26:3** If an encampment should take a stand against me, my heart will not fear; if battle should rise up against me, in this I will hope.

**26:4** One thing I have requested from the Lord; this I will seek: that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, so that I may see the delight of the Lord and visit His temple.

**26:5** For He hid me in His tabernacle; in the day of evils He protected me in the hidden place of His tabernacle.

**26:6** On a rock He exalted me, and now He has exalted my head above my enemies. I went around and offered in His tabernacle a sacrifice of shouting; I will sing and chant a psalm to the Lord.

**26:7** Hear my voice, Lord, with which I cried out to You; have mercy on me, and hear me.

**26:8** To You my heart has said, "My face has sought You out; Your face, Lord, I will seek."

**26:9** Do not turn Your face away from me; do not turn aside in anger from Your servant. Be my helper; do not abandon me, nor despise me, God, my Savior.

**26:10** For my father and my mother have abandoned me, but the Lord has taken me up.

**26:11** Set a law for me, Lord, in Your way, and direct me onto a straight path because of my enemies.

**26:12** Do not hand me over into the souls of those who afflict me, because unjust witnesses have risen up against me, and iniquity has lied to itself.

**26:13** I believe that I will see the good things of the Lord in the land of the living.

**26:14** Await the Lord, act courageously; and let your heart be strengthened, and wait patiently for the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **26:1** — The Vulgate superscription adds priusquam liniretur ("before he was anointed," from the Septuagint), absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text (Psalm 27 in Hebrew numbering); illuminatio literally means "illumination" or "enlightenment."
- **26:6** — Vulgate Circuivi, et immolavi ("I went around and offered") follows the Septuagint in taking the first word as a verb, whereas the Hebrew reads "around me" modifying "my enemies"; hostiam vociferationis literally means "a sacrifice of shouting" (joyful acclamation).
- **26:8** — Vulgate Exquisivit te facies mea ("My face has sought You out") follows the Septuagint and differs from the Hebrew, which quotes God's command, "Seek my face."
- **26:12** — Vulgate in animas literally means "into the souls" (an idiom for desire or will), and mentita est iniquitas sibi ("iniquity has lied to itself," following the Septuagint) differs markedly from the Hebrew "they breathe out violence."
- **26:13** — Vulgate Credo videre ("I believe that I will see," following the Septuagint) omits the conditional particle ("unless") found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
