## Text

**45:1** To the end, for the sons of Korah, for hidden things. A psalm.

**45:2** Our God is a refuge and strength, a helper in tribulations that have come upon us greatly.

**45:3** Therefore we will not fear while the earth is thrown into turmoil, and the mountains are carried into the heart of the sea.

**45:4** Their waters resounded and were thrown into turmoil; the mountains were convulsed in its might.

**45:5** The rush of the river gladdens the city of God; the Most High has sanctified His tabernacle.

**45:6** God is in her midst; she will not be shaken; God will help her early at daybreak.

**45:7** The nations were thrown into tumult, and the kingdoms swayed; He gave His voice; the earth was moved.

**45:8** The Lord of hosts is with us; our upholder is the God of Jacob.

**45:9** Come and see the works of the Lord, what wonders He has set upon the earth,

**45:10** taking away wars to the end of the earth. He will shatter the bow and break weapons in pieces, and He will burn up shields with fire.

**45:11** "Cease, and see that I am God; I will be exalted among the nations, and I will be exalted on the earth."

**45:12** The Lord of hosts is with us; our upholder is the God of Jacob.

## Translator Notes

- **45:2** — Masculine Deus noster literally means "Our God" (rather than "God is our refuge"), and quae invenerunt nos nimis ("that have come upon us greatly," literally "found us exceedingly") follows the Septuagint in making the tribulations the subject, whereas the Hebrew means "a very present help in trouble."
- **45:5** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads sanctificavit as a verb ("the Most High has sanctified His tabernacle"), whereas the Hebrew has a noun phrase ("the holy dwelling places of the Most High").
- **45:8** — Latin Dominus virtutum (literally "the Lord of powers," matching the Septuagint) corresponds to Hebrew "Lord of hosts" and is rendered "the Lord of hosts" consistently across the Psalter; susceptor ("upholder" or "protector") corresponds to Hebrew "stronghold."
- **45:10** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads arma ("weapons") and scuta ("shields"), along with prodigia ("wonders") in 45:9, where the Hebrew has "spear," "chariots," and "desolations."
- **45:11** — Latin Vacate means "cease," "be still," or "be at leisure," and the Vulgate distinctively reads videte ("see") where the Hebrew and Septuagint have "know."
