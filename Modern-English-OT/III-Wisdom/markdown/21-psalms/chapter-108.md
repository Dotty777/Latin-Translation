## Text

**108:1** To the end. A psalm of David.

**108:2** God, do not keep silent about my praise, because the mouth of a sinner and the mouth of a deceitful man have been opened against me.

**108:3** They spoke against me with a deceitful tongue, and they surrounded me with words of hatred, and attacked me without cause.

**108:4** Instead of loving me, they slandered me; but I kept praying.

**108:5** And they set evil against me for good, and hatred for my love.

**108:6** Appoint a sinner over him, and let the devil stand at his right hand.

**108:7** When he is judged, let him come out condemned, and let his prayer become sin.

**108:8** Let his days be few, and let another receive his office of oversight.

**108:9** Let his children become orphans, and his wife a widow.

**108:10** Tottering, let his children be moved from place to place and beg, and let them be driven out from their dwellings.

**108:11** Let the moneylender search through all his property, and let strangers pillage his labors.

**108:12** Let there be no helper for him, nor anyone who has mercy on his fatherless children.

**108:13** Let his offspring come to destruction; in one generation let his name be erased.

**108:14** Let the iniquity of his fathers return to memory in the sight of the Lord, and let the sin of his mother not be erased.

**108:15** Let them be before the Lord always, and let the memory of them perish from the earth,

**108:16** because he did not remember to show mercy,

**108:17** and he persecuted a needy man and a beggar, and one pierced in heart, to put him to death.

**108:18** And he loved cursing, and it will come to him; and he did not want blessing, and it will be kept far from him. And he put on cursing like a garment, and it entered like water into his inward parts and like oil into his bones.

**108:19** Let it be to him like a garment with which he is covered, and like a belt with which he is always bound.

**108:20** This is the work of those who slander me before the Lord, and who speak evil against my soul.

**108:21** And You, Lord, Lord, deal with me for the sake of Your name, because Your mercy is sweet.

**108:22** Deliver me, because I am needy and poor, and my heart is deeply troubled within me.

**108:23** Like a shadow when it declines, I have been carried away, and I have been shaken off like locusts.

**108:24** My knees have been weakened from fasting, and my flesh has been changed because of oil.

**108:25** And I have become a reproach to them; they saw me and shook their heads.

**108:26** Help me, Lord my God; save me according to Your mercy.

**108:27** And let them know that this is Your hand, and You, Lord, have done it.

**108:28** They will curse, and You will bless; let those who rise up against me be put to shame, but Your servant will rejoice.

**108:29** Let those who slander me be clothed with shame, and let them be covered with their disgrace like a double cloak.

**108:30** I will give thanks to the Lord exceedingly with my mouth, and I will praise Him in the midst of many,

**108:31** because He has stood at the right hand of the poor man, so that He might save my soul from persecutors.

## Translator Notes

- **108:2** — Vulgate 'laudem meam ne tacueris' takes 'my praise' as the direct object of 'keep silent' (following the LXX), whereas the Hebrew reads 'God of my praise, do not be silent.'
- **108:6** — Vulgate 'diabolus' ('the devil') renders Greek diabolos, corresponding to Hebrew satan ('an accuser' or 'adversary').
- **108:8** — Vulgate 'episcopatum' literally denotes an 'episcopate,' 'bishopric,' or 'office of oversight' (rendering Greek episkope), and is quoted of Judas in Acts 1:20.
- **108:16** — Native Vulgate 108:16-17 correspond to Saints 108:16 (the sentence continues directly into restored native 108:17).
- **108:17** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 108:17, continuing the causal clause of 108:16 (parallel to the second half of Saints 108:16 / Hebrew 109:16b).
- **108:18** — Native Vulgate 108:18 combines both Saints 108:17 ("And he loved cursing...") and Saints 108:18 ("And he put on cursing like a garment..."); native 108:17 is the restored second half of Saints 108:16.
- **108:24** — Vulgate 'propter oleum' ('because of oil,' following the LXX) literally renders the Hebrew expression usually understood as 'for lack of fat' or 'without oil.'
- **108:31** — Vulgate 'salvam faceret a persequentibus animam meam' follows the LXX in shifting from third-person 'the poor man' to first-person 'my soul' and reading 'persecutors' (against the Hebrew 'those who condemn his soul').
