## Text

**3:1** A psalm of David, when he was fleeing from the face of Absalom his son.

**3:2** Lord, why have those who afflict me multiplied? Many rise up against me.

**3:3** Many are saying to my soul, "He has no salvation in his God."

**3:4** Yet You, Lord, are my upholder, my glory, and the One who raises up my head.

**3:5** With my voice I cried out to the Lord, and He heard me from His holy mountain.

**3:6** I slept and fell into deep slumber; and I rose up, because the Lord upheld me.

**3:7** I will not be afraid of thousands of people surrounding me. Rise up, Lord; save me, my God.

**3:8** For You have struck all who oppose me without cause; You have crushed the teeth of sinners.

**3:9** Salvation is the Lord's, and upon Your people is Your blessing.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — In the Vulgate and Septuagint, as in the Hebrew, the superscription is numbered as verse 1.
- **3:2** — Vulgate "quid multiplicati sunt" ("why have ... multiplied?") follows the Septuagint as a question, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads as an exclamation ("how many are my foes").
- **3:4** — Vulgate "susceptor meus" ("my upholder") follows the Septuagint ("my helper/supporter"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "a shield about me."
- **3:7** — Vulgate "millia" reads "thousands," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text and Septuagint read "ten thousands" (myriads).
- **3:8** — Vulgate "sine causa" ("without cause") follows the Septuagint ("in vain"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "on the cheek."
