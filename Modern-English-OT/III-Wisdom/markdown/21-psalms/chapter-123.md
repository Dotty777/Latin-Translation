## Text

**123:1** A song of steps. Had it not been that the Lord was among us, let Israel say now,

**123:2** had it not been that the Lord was among us when people rose up against us,

**123:3** perhaps they would have swallowed us alive; when their fury raged against us,

**123:4** perhaps the water would have engulfed us;

**123:5** our soul passed through a torrent; perhaps our soul would have passed through unbearable water.

**123:6** Blessed is the Lord, who did not give us as prey to their teeth.

**123:7** Our soul has been rescued like a sparrow from the snare of the hunters; the snare has been broken, and we have been set free.

**123:8** Our aid is in the name of the Lord, who made heaven and earth.

## Translator Notes

- **123:1** — The Vulgate reads in nobis (literally "in us" or "among us"), following the Septuagint, where the Hebrew means "on our side."
- **123:3** — Here and in vv. 4-5 the Vulgate reads forte/forsitan ("perhaps"), reflecting the Septuagint particle ara, whereas the Hebrew reads "then."
- **123:5** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate makes "our soul" (anima nostra) the subject passing through "a torrent" (torrentem) and singular "unbearable water" (aquam intolerabilem), whereas the Hebrew makes the torrent and raging waters sweep over our soul.
- **123:7** — The Vulgate follows the Septuagint with passer ("sparrow"), venantium ("hunters"), and passive verbs (erepta est, liberati sumus), whereas the Hebrew has "bird," "fowlers," and "escaped."
