## Text

**128:1** A song of steps. "Often they have assailed me from my youth," let Israel now say;

**128:2** "often they have assailed me from my youth, and indeed they could not overpower me."

**128:3** Upon my back sinners have built; they have prolonged their iniquity.

**128:4** The righteous Lord has severed the necks of sinners.

**128:5** Let them be put to shame and turned backward, all who hate Zion.

**128:6** Let them become like the grass of the rooftops, which has dried up before it is pulled up,

**128:7** with which the one who reaps has not filled his hand, nor the one who gathers sheaves his bosom.

**128:8** And those who were passing by did not say, "The Lord's blessing be upon you. We have blessed you in the name of the Lord."

## Translator Notes

- **128:1** — Latin Canticum graduum ("A song of steps") renders the Septuagint title for the pilgrimage psalms (Psalms 119-133 in Vulgate numbering), traditionally associated with going up to Jerusalem or with the steps of the temple.
- **128:3** — Vulgate fabricaverunt peccatores; prolongaverunt iniquitatem suam ("sinners have built; they have prolonged their iniquity") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "plowmen plowed... they made long their furrows."
- **128:4** — Vulgate cervices peccatorum ("the necks of sinners") follows the Septuagint against the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "the cords of the wicked."
