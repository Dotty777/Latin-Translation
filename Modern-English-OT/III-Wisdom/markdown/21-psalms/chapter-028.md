## Text

**28:1** A psalm of David, at the completion of the tabernacle. Bring to the Lord, sons of God; bring to the Lord the young of rams.

**28:2** Bring to the Lord glory and honor; bring to the Lord glory for His name; worship the Lord in His holy court.

**28:3** The voice of the Lord is over the waters; the God of majesty thundered; the Lord is over many waters.

**28:4** The voice of the Lord is in strength; the voice of the Lord is in magnificence.

**28:5** The voice of the Lord shattering the cedars, and the Lord will shatter the cedars of Lebanon;

**28:6** and He will crush them into pieces like a calf of Lebanon, and the beloved like the young of unicorns.

**28:7** The voice of the Lord splitting the flame of fire;

**28:8** the voice of the Lord shaking the wilderness, and the Lord will shake the wilderness of Kadesh.

**28:9** The voice of the Lord preparing the deer, and He will uncover the dense thickets, and in His temple all will speak glory.

**28:10** The Lord causes the flood to dwell, and the Lord will sit as king forever.

**28:11** The Lord will give strength to His people; the Lord will bless His people in peace.

## Translator Notes

- **28:1** — The superscription "at the completion of the tabernacle" (Latin in consummatione tabernaculi) and the second command "bring to the Lord the young of rams" (Latin filios arietum, literally "sons of rams") follow the Septuagint and are not present in the Hebrew text.
- **28:6** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads comminuet eas ("will crush them into pieces"), dilectus ("the beloved," corresponding to Mount Sirion), and unicornium ("unicorns," for Hebrew wild oxen), whereas the Hebrew describes making Lebanon and Sirion skip like a calf and a young wild ox.
- **28:9** — Vulgate praeparantis cervos ("preparing the deer") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew speaks of making deer give birth or of twisting oaks.
- **28:10** — Vulgate diluvium inhabitare facit ("causes the flood to dwell") renders the Septuagint's transitive verb, in contrast to the Hebrew "sat enthroned over the flood."
