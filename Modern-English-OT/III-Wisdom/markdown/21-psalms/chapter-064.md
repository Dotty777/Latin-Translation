## Text

**64:1** For the end. A psalm of David, a song of Jeremiah and Ezekiel for the people of the exile, when they were beginning to go out.

**64:2** A hymn befits You, God, in Zion, and a vow will be paid to You in Jerusalem.

**64:3** Hear my prayer; to You all flesh will come.

**64:4** The words of the unjust have prevailed over us, and You Yourself will pardon our impieties.

**64:5** Blessed is the one whom You have chosen and received; he will dwell in Your courts. We will be filled with the good things of Your house; Your temple is holy,

**64:6** marvelous in equity. Hear us, God, our Savior, the hope of all the ends of the earth, and on the sea far off,

**64:7** preparing the mountains in Your power, girded with might;

**64:8** who stir up the depth of the sea, the sound of its waves. The nations will be thrown into turmoil,

**64:9** and those who inhabit the boundaries will fear at Your signs; You will delight the outgoings of morning and evening.

**64:10** You have visited the earth and drenched it; You have enriched it many times over. The river of God is filled with waters; You have prepared their food, for such is its preparation.

**64:11** Drench its streams; multiply its produce; in its raindrops it will rejoice as it sprouts.

**64:12** You will bless the crown of the year of Your kindness, and Your fields will be filled with abundance.

**64:13** The beautiful places of the desert will grow rich, and the hills will be girded with exultation.

**64:14** The rams of the sheep are clothed, and the valleys will abound with grain; they will shout; indeed, they will sing a hymn.

## Translator Notes

- **64:1** — The reference to Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and "the people of the exile" (populo transmigrationis) follows the Septuagint superscription and is absent from the Hebrew text.
- **64:3** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate uses an imperative ("Hear my prayer") where the Hebrew has a vocative participle ("You who hear prayer").
- **64:8** — Latin qui conturbas ("who stir up" or "trouble") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "who stills" the roaring of the seas.
- **64:12** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "You will bless the crown" (Benedices coronae) and "Your fields" (campi tui), where the Hebrew reads "You crown the year" and "Your wagon tracks."
- **64:14** — Latin arietes ovium ("the rams of the sheep") follows the Septuagint in rendering Hebrew karim as "rams" rather than "meadows" ("the meadows clothe themselves with flocks").
