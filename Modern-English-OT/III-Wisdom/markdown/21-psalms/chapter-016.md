## Text

**16:1** A prayer of David. Hear my righteousness, Lord; pay attention to my supplication. Receive my prayer with Your ears, not from deceitful lips.

**16:2** From Your face may my judgment go forth; may Your eyes see what is equitable.

**16:3** You have tested my heart and visited me by night; You have examined me by fire, and no iniquity has been found in me.

**16:4** So that my mouth may not speak of the deeds of men: because of the words of Your lips, I have kept hard ways.

**16:5** Perfect my steps in Your paths, so that my footsteps may not be moved.

**16:6** I have cried out, because You have heard me, God; incline Your ear to me, and hear my words.

**16:7** Make Your mercies wondrous, You who save those who hope in You.

**16:8** From those who resist Your right hand, keep me like the pupil of the eye. Under the shadow of Your wings protect me

**16:9** from the face of the wicked who have afflicted me. My enemies have surrounded my soul;

**16:10** they have closed up their fat: their mouth has spoken pride.

**16:11** Casting me out, they have now surrounded me; they have set their eyes to bend down to the earth.

**16:12** They have taken hold of me like a lion ready for prey, and like a young lion dwelling in hidden places.

**16:13** Rise up, Lord: confront him and overthrow him; rescue my soul from the wicked one; Your sword

**16:14** from the enemies of Your hand. Lord, from the few of the earth divide them in their life; from Your hidden things their belly has been filled. They have been satisfied with children, and they have left their remainder to their little ones.

**16:15** But in righteousness I will appear in Your sight; I will be satisfied when Your glory appears.

## Translator Notes

- **16:4** — The Vulgate "hard ways" (vias duras) follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "paths of the violent."
- **16:11** — The Vulgate "Casting me out" (Projicientes me) follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "our steps" ("they have now surrounded our steps").
- **16:12** — The Vulgate "They have taken hold of me" (Susceperunt me) renders the Septuagint hypelabon me, differing from the Hebrew "He is like a lion."
- **16:13** — In the Vulgate (following the Septuagint), "Your sword" (frameam tuam) is grammatically accusative as a second object of "rescue," continuing into v. 14 ("from the enemies of Your hand"), whereas the Hebrew treats "sword" and "hand" as instruments ("by Your sword ... by Your hand").
- **16:14** — The Vulgate "from the few of the earth divide them in their life" (a paucis de terra divide eos in vita eorum) follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "from men of the world, whose portion is in this life."
