## Text

**100:1** A psalm of David himself. I will sing of mercy and judgment to You, Lord; I will sing praises,

**100:2** and I will understand in an undefiled way: when will You come to me? I walked about in the innocence of my heart, in the midst of my house.

**100:3** I did not set an unjust thing before my eyes; I hated those who commit transgressions; there did not cling to me

**100:4** a perverse heart; I did not know the wicked one turning away from me.

**100:5** The one slandering his neighbor in secret, him I pursued; one with a proud eye and an insatiable heart, with him I did not eat.

**100:6** My eyes are toward the faithful of the earth, so that they may sit with me; the one walking in an undefiled way, he ministered to me.

**100:7** The one who practices pride will not dwell in the midst of my house; the one who speaks iniquitous things did not succeed in the sight of my eyes.

**100:8** In the morning I put to death all the sinners of the earth, so that I might wipe out from the city of the Lord all who work iniquity.

## Translator Notes

- **100:2** — From verse 2b through verse 6 and in verse 8, the Vulgate follows the Septuagint in using past tenses (imperfect and perfect) to recount David's habitual conduct, whereas the Hebrew uses imperfect verbs usually translated as future resolutions.
- **100:4** — The Vulgate splits the clause across verses 3-4, making cor pravum ('a perverse heart') the subject of non adhaesit mihi ('did not cling to me') and joining declinantem a me ('turning away from me') to malignum ('the wicked one'), reflecting the Septuagint rather than the Hebrew clause division.
- **100:5** — Latin persequebar ('I pursued') and cum hoc non edebam ('with him I did not eat') follow the Septuagint against the Hebrew ('I will destroy' and 'I will not endure', the latter from reading the Hebrew verb as 'eat' rather than 'be able/endure').
- **100:7** — Latin superbiam ('pride') and iniqua ('iniquitous things') follow the Septuagint against Hebrew 'deceit' and 'lies'; intransitive non direxit (literally 'did not go straight') means 'did not succeed' or 'prosper' (past tense, against Hebrew future 'will not be established').
- **100:8** — Latin In matutino ('In the morning', singular) and peccatores ('sinners') follow the Septuagint against Hebrew 'every morning' and 'wicked'.
