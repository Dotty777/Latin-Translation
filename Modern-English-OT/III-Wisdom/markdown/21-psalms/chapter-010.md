## Text

**10:1** To the end. A psalm of David.

**10:2** In the Lord I trust; how do you say to my soul, "Migrate to the mountain like a sparrow"?

**10:3** For look, sinners have drawn the bow; they have prepared their arrows in the quiver, so that in the dark they may shoot the upright in heart.

**10:4** For what You have perfected, they have destroyed; but the righteous one, what has he done?

**10:5** The Lord is in His holy temple; the Lord, His throne is in heaven. His eyes look toward the poor; His eyelids question the sons of men.

**10:6** The Lord questions the righteous and the ungodly; but the one who loves iniquity hates his own soul.

**10:7** He will rain snares upon sinners; fire and sulfur, and a wind of storms, will be the portion of their cup.

**10:8** For the Lord is righteous, and He has loved righteous deeds; His face has seen equity.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — Vulgate Psalm 10 corresponds to Psalm 11 in Hebrew numbering. "To the end" renders Latin "In finem" (from Septuagint "eis to telos"), corresponding to the Hebrew musical heading lamnatseach ("for the choirmaster").
- **10:2** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 10:2 (opening body verse of Psalm 10, parallel to the second half of Saints 10:1 / Hebrew 11:1); Latin 'Transmigra' literally means "migrate" or "move away."
- **10:3** — Native Vulgate Psalm 10:3-8 runs one verse ahead of Saints 10:2-7 because native Vulgate separates the superscription (10:1) from the opening verse (10:2).
- **10:4** — "what You have perfected" follows the Vulgate ("quae perfecisti") and Septuagint, addressing God directly, whereas the Masoretic Hebrew reads "the foundations."
- **10:5** — "toward the poor" follows the Vulgate ("in pauperem") and Septuagint and is absent from the Masoretic Hebrew. "question" renders Latin "interrogant," denoting judicial questioning or examination (likewise in native Vulgate 10:6 / Saints 10:5).
- **10:6** — Following the Vulgate ("qui autem diligit iniquitatem, odit animam suam") and Septuagint, the lover of iniquity hates his own soul; in the Masoretic Hebrew, the Lord's soul hates the lover of violence.
- **10:8** — Following the Vulgate ("aequitatem vidit vultus ejus") and Septuagint, "His face" is the subject and "equity" is the object; the Masoretic Hebrew reads "the upright will see His face."
