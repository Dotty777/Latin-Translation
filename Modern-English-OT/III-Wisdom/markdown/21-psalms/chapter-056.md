## Text

**56:1** To the end, do not destroy. Of David, for the inscription of a title, when he fled from the face of Saul into the cave.

**56:2** Have mercy on me, God, have mercy on me, for my soul trusts in You. And in the shadow of Your wings I will hope, until iniquity passes by.

**56:3** I will cry out to God Most High, God who has done good to me.

**56:4** He sent from heaven and delivered me; He gave over to reproach those who trample me. God sent His mercy and His truth,

**56:5** and rescued my soul from the midst of lion cubs. I slept troubled. The sons of men: their teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue is a sharp sword.

**56:6** Be exalted above the heavens, God, and may Your glory be throughout all the earth.

**56:7** They prepared a snare for my feet, and bowed down my soul. They dug a pit before my face, and fell into it.

**56:8** My heart is prepared, God, my heart is prepared; I will sing and recite a psalm.

**56:9** Arise, my glory; arise, psaltery and lyre; I will arise at dawn.

**56:10** I will praise You among the peoples, Lord, and I will recite a psalm to You among the nations,

**56:11** for Your mercy has been magnified even to the heavens, and Your truth even to the clouds.

**56:12** Be exalted above the heavens, God, and may Your glory be above all the earth.

## Translator Notes

- **56:1** — Vulgate 'To the end, do not destroy' (In finem, ne disperdas) and 'for the inscription of a title' (in tituli inscriptionem) literally render the Septuagint equivalents of the Hebrew musical superscriptions.
- **56:5** — Vulgate 'I slept troubled' (Dormivi conturbatus, following the Septuagint) differs from the Masoretic Hebrew 'I lie down among fiery ones'; likewise catulorum leonum specifies 'lion cubs.'
- **56:9** — Vulgate 'I will arise at dawn' (exsurgam diluculo, following the Septuagint) differs from the Masoretic Hebrew 'I will awaken the dawn.'
