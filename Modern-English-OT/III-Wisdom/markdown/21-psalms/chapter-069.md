## Text

**69:1** To the end. A psalm of David for remembrance, that the Lord saved him.

**69:2** God, attend to my help; Lord, hurry to help me.

**69:3** May those who seek my soul be put to shame and humiliated.

**69:4** May those who wish evil for me be turned backward and be ashamed; may those who say to me, "Aha, aha!" be turned away immediately, blushing with shame.

**69:5** May all who seek You exult and be glad in You; and may those who love Your salvation always say, "May the Lord be magnified."

**69:6** But I am needy and poor; God, help me. You are my helper and my deliverer; Lord, do not delay.

## Translator Notes

- **69:1** — The Vulgate superscription adds "that the Lord saved him" (quod salvum fecerit eum Dominus), reflecting a Septuagint addition not present in the Hebrew text.
- **69:3** — Latin revereantur ("be humiliated") renders the Septuagint verb for being put to shame or humiliated (entrepomai), a regular Vulgate Psalter usage also found in 70:24.
- **69:4** — Latin statim erubescentes ("immediately, blushing with shame") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "because of their shame." Latin Euge, euge! (lit. "Well done, well done!") is used sarcastically as a mocking taunt ("Aha, aha!").
