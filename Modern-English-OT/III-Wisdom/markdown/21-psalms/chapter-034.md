## Text

**34:1** For David himself. Judge, Lord, those who harm me; attack those who fight against me.

**34:2** Seize weapons and shield, and rise up to my aid.

**34:3** Pour out the sword, and block the way against those who pursue me; say to my soul, "I am your salvation."

**34:4** Let those who seek my soul be disgraced and humiliated; let those who devise evil against me be turned backward and disgraced.

**34:5** Let them become like dust before the face of the wind, and may the angel of the Lord hem them in.

**34:6** Let their way become darkness and slipperiness, and may the angel of the Lord pursue them.

**34:7** For without cause they hid for me the destruction of their snare; needlessly they reproached my soul.

**34:8** Let a snare that he does not know come upon him, and let the trap that he hid seize him, and let him fall into that very snare itself.

**34:9** But my soul will exult in the Lord and will take delight in His salvation.

**34:10** All my bones will say, "Lord, who is like You, rescuing the needy from the hand of those stronger than he, the destitute and the poor from those who plunder him?"

**34:11** Rising up, unjust witnesses questioned me about things I did not know.

**34:12** They repaid me evil for good, barrenness to my soul.

**34:13** But I, when they were troublesome to me, clothed myself with sackcloth; I humbled my soul in fasting, and my prayer will return to my bosom.

**34:14** As toward a neighbor and as toward our brother, so I sought to please; as one mourning and sorrowful, so I was humbled.

**34:15** And against me they rejoiced and came together; scourges were gathered together upon me, and I did not know.

**34:16** They were scattered and felt no remorse; they tested me, they mocked me with mockery; they gnashed at me with their teeth.

**34:17** Lord, when will You look? Restore my soul from their malice; from the lions, my only one.

**34:18** I will give thanks to You in a great assembly; among a weighty people I will praise You.

**34:19** May those who oppose me unjustly not gloat over me, those who hate me without cause and wink with their eyes.

**34:20** For to me indeed they were speaking peacefully; and speaking in the wrath of the land, they were devising deceits.

**34:21** And they opened wide their mouth against me; they said, "Aha, aha! Our eyes have seen."

**34:22** You have seen, Lord; do not be silent; Lord, do not depart from me.

**34:23** Rise up and attend to my judgment, my God; and, my Lord, to my cause.

**34:24** Judge me according to Your righteousness, Lord my God, and may they not gloat over me.

**34:25** Let them not say in their hearts, "Aha, aha, for our soul!" nor let them say, "We have devoured him."

**34:26** Let those who celebrate my misfortunes be ashamed and humiliated together; let those who speak great things against me be clothed with disgrace and humiliation.

**34:27** Let those who desire my righteousness exult and rejoice; and let those who desire the peace of His servant always say, "May the Lord be magnified."

**34:28** And my tongue will meditate on Your righteousness; all day long, Your praise.

## Translator Notes

- **34:3** — Latin effunde frameam literally means "pour out the sword" (a Septuagint-based idiom for unleashing the weapon, where Hebrew has "draw the spear"), and conclude adversus eos takes the next Hebrew term as an imperative verb ("block [the way] against them") rather than a weapon noun.
- **34:7** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads interitum laquei sui ("the destruction of their snare") and exprobraverunt ("they reproached"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of hiding a net in a pit and digging a pit.
- **34:13** — The Vulgate reads cum mihi molesti essent ("when they were troublesome to me") and uses the future tense convertetur ("will return"), whereas the Hebrew has "when they were sick."
- **34:16** — In vv. 15-16 the Vulgate preserves distinctive Septuagint readings: flagella ("scourges"), dissipati sunt, nec compuncti ("they were scattered and felt no remorse"), and tentaverunt me ("they tested me"), differing significantly from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **34:20** — The Vulgate lacks the negation of the Hebrew ("they do not speak peace"), reading instead mihi quidem pacifice loquebantur ("to me indeed they were speaking peacefully"), and renders "against the quiet ones of the land" as in iracundia terræ loquentes ("speaking in the wrath of the land").
