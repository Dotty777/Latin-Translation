## Text

**67:1** For the end. A psalm of a song for David himself.

**67:2** May God rise up, and may His enemies be scattered, and may those who hate Him flee from His face.

**67:3** As smoke vanishes, may they vanish; as wax melts before the face of fire, so may sinners perish before the face of God.

**67:4** And may the righteous feast, and exult in the sight of God, and delight in gladness.

**67:5** Sing to God; sing a psalm to His name; make a way for Him who ascends above the west. The Lord is His name; exult in His sight. They will be troubled before the face of Him,

**67:6** the father of orphans and the judge of widows; God is in His holy place.

**67:7** God, who makes those of one mind dwell in a house; who brings out prisoners in strength, likewise those who provoke, who dwell in tombs.

**67:8** God, when You went forth in the sight of Your people, when You passed through the desert,

**67:9** the earth was shaken, indeed the heavens dripped, before the face of the God of Sinai, before the face of the God of Israel.

**67:10** You will set apart a freewill rain, God, for Your inheritance; and it grew weak, yet You perfected it.

**67:11** Your living creatures will dwell in it; You prepared in Your sweetness for the poor, God.

**67:12** The Lord will give the word to those who proclaim good news, with great power.

**67:13** The King of hosts belongs to the beloved, to the beloved; and it is for the beauty of the house to divide the spoils.

**67:14** If you sleep in the midst of the lots, the wings of a dove are overlaid with silver, and the rear parts of its back are in the paleness of gold.

**67:15** While the Heavenly One distinguishes kings over it, they will be made white with snow in Zalmon.

**67:16** A mountain of God, a fat mountain; a curdled mountain, a fat mountain.

**67:17** Why do you suspect curdled mountains? This is a mountain in which God has been well pleased to dwell; for indeed the Lord will dwell to the end.

**67:18** The chariot of God is multiplied by ten thousands, thousands of those who rejoice; the Lord is among them in Sinai, in the holy place.

**67:19** You ascended on high, You captured captivity, You received gifts among men; indeed, even those who do not believe, so that the Lord God may dwell.

**67:20** Blessed be the Lord day by day; the God of our salvations will make a prosperous journey for us.

**67:21** Our God is the God of saving, and to the Lord, the Lord belong the ways out of death.

**67:22** Nevertheless, God will shatter the heads of His enemies, the crown of hair of those who walk about in their offenses.

**67:23** The Lord said, "From Bashan I will turn back, I will turn back into the depth of the sea,

**67:24** so that your foot may be dipped in blood; the tongue of your dogs from the enemies, from him."

**67:25** They have seen Your entrances, God, the entrances of my God, my King, who is in the holy place.

**67:26** Princes went ahead, joined with those who sing praises, in the midst of young women playing tambourines.

**67:27** In the assemblies bless God the Lord from the fountains of Israel.

**67:28** There is Benjamin, a youth, in ecstasy of mind; the princes of Judah, their leaders; the princes of Zebulun, the princes of Naphtali.

**67:29** Command Your power, God; confirm this, God, which You have worked in us.

**67:30** From Your temple in Jerusalem, kings will offer gifts to You.

**67:31** Rebuke the wild beasts of the reed, the gathering of bulls among the cows of the peoples, so that they may shut out those who have been tested with silver. Scatter the nations that desire wars.

**67:32** Envoys will come from Egypt; Ethiopia will hasten her hands to God.

**67:33** Kingdoms of the earth, sing to God; sing praises to the Lord; sing praises to God.

**67:34** He who ascends above the heaven of heaven, toward the east: look, He will give to His voice a voice of power.

**67:35** Give glory to God over Israel; His magnificence and His power are in the clouds.

**67:36** God is wondrous in His holy ones; the God of Israel Himself will give power and strength to His people. Blessed be God!

## Translator Notes

- **67:5** — Latin "super occasum" ("above the west," following Septuagint "epi dysmon") differs from the Hebrew, often rendered "through the deserts" or "upon the clouds"; the closing clause "Turbabuntur a facie ejus" ("They will be troubled before the face of Him") is also unique to the Vulgate/Septuagint.
- **67:7** — Latin "unius moris" ("of one manner/mind") renders Greek "monotropous" (Hebrew "the solitary"), and the closing clause "similiter eos qui exasperant, qui habitant in sepulchris" ("likewise those who provoke, who dwell in tombs") follows the Septuagint rather than Hebrew "only the rebellious dwell in a parched land."
- **67:13** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "dilecti, dilecti" ("of the beloved, of the beloved") and "speciei domus" ("for the beauty of the house"), diverging markedly from the Hebrew ("kings of armies flee, they flee, and she who remains at home divides the spoil").
- **67:16** — Latin "mons pinguis" ("a fat mountain") and "mons coagulatus" ("a curdled mountain") follow the Septuagint rather than Hebrew "mountain of Bashan" and "many-peaked mountain"; in 67:17 "montes coagulatos" is accusative as the direct object of "suspicamini" ("suspect").
- **67:19** — The closing clause "etenim non credentes inhabitare Dominum Deum" is elliptically compressed from the Septuagint and may be construed either as "even those who do not believe, so that the Lord God may dwell" or as "those who do not believe that the Lord God dwells."
- **67:31** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "in vaccis populorum" ("among the cows of the peoples") and "ut excludant eos qui probati sunt argento" ("so that they may shut out those who have been tested with silver"), differing significantly from the Hebrew text.
