## Text

**85:1** A prayer for David himself. Bend down Your ear, Lord, and hear me, for I am destitute and poor.

**85:2** Guard my soul, for I am holy; save Your servant, my God, who hopes in You.

**85:3** Have mercy on me, Lord, for I have cried out to You all day long;

**85:4** gladden the soul of Your servant, for to You, Lord, I have lifted up my soul.

**85:5** For You, Lord, are sweet and gentle, and abundant in mercy to all who call upon You.

**85:6** Receive my prayer with Your ears, Lord, and heed the voice of my supplication.

**85:7** In the day of my distress I cried out to You, because You heard me.

**85:8** There is no one like You among the gods, Lord, and there is nothing comparable to Your works.

**85:9** All the nations, as many as You have made, will come and worship before You, Lord, and will glorify Your name.

**85:10** For You are great and do wonders; You are God alone.

**85:11** Lead me, Lord, in Your way, and I will walk in Your truth; let my heart rejoice, so that it may fear Your name.

**85:12** I will give praise to You, Lord my God, with all my heart, and I will glorify Your name forever:

**85:13** because Your mercy is great upon me, and You have rescued my soul from the lower underworld.

**85:14** God, the unjust have risen up against me, and an assembly of the powerful has sought my soul, and they have not placed You in their sight.

**85:15** And You, Lord God, are compassionate and merciful, patient, and abundant in mercy, and truthful.

**85:16** Look upon me, and have mercy on me; give Your dominion to Your servant, and save the son of Your maidservant.

**85:17** Work a sign with me for good, so that those who hate me may see and be put to shame, because You, Lord, have helped me and comforted me.

## Translator Notes

- **85:2** — Latin "sanctus sum" ("I am holy") and "sperantem" ("who hopes") reflect the Septuagint ("hosios," "elpizonta"), whereas the Hebrew has "devout/godly" and "who trusts."
- **85:5** — Latin "suavis et mitis" ("sweet and gentle") follows the Septuagint ("chrestos kai epieikes"), whereas the Hebrew reads "good and ready to forgive."
- **85:11** — Latin "Deduc me" ("Lead me") and "laetetur cor meum" ("let my heart rejoice") follow the Septuagint ("hodegeson me," "euphrantheto"), whereas the Hebrew reads "Teach me" and "unite my heart."
- **85:13** — Latin "ex inferno inferiori" literally means "from the lower underworld," reflecting the Septuagint ("hadou katotatou") and corresponding to the Hebrew "depths of Sheol."
- **85:16** — Latin "imperium" ("dominion," also "authority") renders the Septuagint "kratos" ("might/rule"), where the Hebrew has "strength"; "puero" (literally "boy/child") renders Greek "paidi" in the sense of "servant."
