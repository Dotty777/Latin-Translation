## Text

**146:1** Hallelujah. Praise the Lord, for a psalm is good; let praise to our God be pleasant and fitting.

**146:2** The Lord is building Jerusalem; He will gather the scattered of Israel,

**146:3** the One who heals those crushed in heart and bandages their wounds;

**146:4** who counts the multitude of the stars and calls out names for all of them.

**146:5** Our Lord is great, and His power is great, and there is no number to His wisdom.

**146:6** The Lord upholds the meek, but He humbles sinners all the way to the ground.

**146:7** Begin singing to the Lord in thanksgiving; sing psalms to our God on the lyre,

**146:8** the One who covers the sky with clouds and prepares rain for the earth; who brings forth grass on the mountains and vegetation for the service of mankind;

**146:9** who gives to livestock their food, and to the young of ravens calling upon Him.

**146:10** He will not take delight in the strength of a horse, nor will He be well pleased with the legs of a man.

**146:11** The Lord is well pleased with those who fear Him, and with those who hope in His mercy.

## Translator Notes

- **146:1** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate divides Hebrew Psalm 147 into two psalms: Psalm 146 (Hebrew 147:1-11) and Psalm 147 (Hebrew 147:12-20). Vulgate "for a psalm is good" (quoniam bonus est psalmus) uses the noun "psalm" rather than the Hebrew verbal phrase "to sing praises."
- **146:3** — Latin pairs the cognate terms contritos ("crushed") and contritiones (lit. "crushings" or "fractures," here "wounds").
- **146:4** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "multitude" (multitudinem) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "number."
- **146:8** — The final clause "and vegetation for the service of mankind" (et herbam servituti hominum) follows the Septuagint (echoing Ps 103:14 Vulgate / 104:14 Hebrew) and is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
