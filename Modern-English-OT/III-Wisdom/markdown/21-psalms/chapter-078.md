## Text

**78:1** A psalm of Asaph. God, the nations have come into Your inheritance; they have defiled Your holy temple; they have made Jerusalem into a watch-post for fruit.

**78:2** They have made the corpses of Your servants food for the birds of the sky, the flesh of Your holy ones for the beasts of the earth.

**78:3** They have poured out their blood like water all around Jerusalem, and there was no one to bury them.

**78:4** We have become a reproach to our neighbors, a mockery and ridicule to those who are all around us.

**78:5** How long, Lord, will You be angry to the end? Will Your zeal be kindled like fire?

**78:6** Pour out Your wrath upon the nations that do not know You, and upon the kingdoms that have not called upon Your name,

**78:7** for they have devoured Jacob and have made his place desolate.

**78:8** Do not remember our former iniquities; let Your mercies quickly come to meet us, for we have become exceedingly poor.

**78:9** Help us, God our Savior, and for the sake of the glory of Your name, Lord, deliver us, and be merciful toward our sins, for the sake of Your name.

**78:10** May they never say among the nations, "Where is their God?" And let the avenging of the blood of Your servants that has been poured out become known among the peoples before our eyes.

**78:11** Let the groaning of those in shackles enter into Your presence; according to the greatness of Your arm, take possession of the sons of those put to death,

**78:12** and pay back to our neighbors sevenfold into their lap, their own reproach with which they have reproached You, Lord.

**78:13** But we, Your people and the sheep of Your pasture, will give thanks to You forever; to generation after generation we will proclaim Your praise.

## Translator Notes

- **78:1** — The Vulgate "in pomorum custodiam" ("into a watch-post for fruit") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "into heaps of ruins."
- **78:5** — The Latin "in finem" ("to the end") renders the Septuagint "eis telos" and corresponds to Hebrew "forever."
- **78:10** — The Vulgate "Ne forte dicant" ("May they never say") follows the Septuagint negative construction rather than the Hebrew question ("Why should the nations say?").
- **78:11** — The Latin "posside filios mortificatorum" ("take possession of the sons of those put to death") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "preserve those doomed to die."
