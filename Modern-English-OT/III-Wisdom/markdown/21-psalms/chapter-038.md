## Text

**38:1** To the end, for Jeduthun himself. A song of David.

**38:2** I said, "I will guard my ways, so that I may not sin with my tongue." I placed a guard over my mouth while the sinner stood against me.

**38:3** I became mute and was humbled, and I kept silent from good things; and my pain was renewed.

**38:4** My heart grew hot within me, and in my meditation a fire will blaze up.

**38:5** I spoke with my tongue, "Make known to me, Lord, my end, and what the number of my days is, so that I may know what is lacking to me.

**38:6** Look, You have made my days measurable, and my substance is as nothing before You. Indeed, every living man is complete vanity.

**38:7** Indeed, man passes through in an image; yet he is also troubled in vain: he stores up treasures, and does not know for whom he will gather them.

**38:8** And now, what is my expectation? Is it not the Lord? And my substance is with You.

**38:9** Rescue me from all my iniquities; You have made me a reproach to the fool.

**38:10** I became mute and did not open my mouth, because You did it;

**38:11** remove Your blows from me.

**38:12** From the strength of Your hand I have fainted in rebukes: because of iniquity You have disciplined man. And You have made his soul waste away like a spider; indeed, every man is troubled in vain.

**38:13** Hear my prayer, Lord, and my supplication; give ear to my tears. Do not be silent, because I am a foreigner with You and a traveler, like all my fathers.

**38:14** Relent toward me, so that I may be refreshed before I depart and will be no more."

## Translator Notes

- **38:1** — Vulgate "In finem" ("To the end") renders the Septuagint heading "eis to telos" rather than the Hebrew musical direction lamnatseach ("for the director"). "Idithun" is the Latinized form of Jeduthun, and "ipsi" reflects the Greek dative article.
- **38:3** — "Was humbled" (humiliatus sum) follows the Septuagint ("etapeinothen"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "in stillness" (or silence). In 38:4, "exardescet" ("will blaze up") is likewise future tense following the Septuagint.
- **38:5** — "What is lacking to me" (quid desit mihi) follows the Septuagint, contrasting with the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "how fleeting I am." In 38:6, "mensurabiles" ("measurable") is a distinctive Vulgate rendering where the Hebrew reads "handbreadths," and "substantia" ("substance," repeated in 38:8) translates Greek "hypostasis."
- **38:9** — "You have made me a reproach to the fool" (opprobrium insipienti dedisti me) is an indicative statement in the Vulgate and Septuagint, contrasting with the negative petition in the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("Do not make me...").
- **38:12** — "Like a spider" (sicut araneam) follows the Septuagint ("hos arachnen"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "like a moth." The Clementine punctuation also links "in increpationibus" ("in rebukes") with the preceding clause rather than with "corripuisti" ("You have disciplined").
