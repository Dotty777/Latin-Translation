## Text

**50:1** To the end. A psalm of David,

**50:2** when the prophet Nathan came to him, when he had gone in to Bathsheba.

**50:3** Have mercy on me, God, according to Your great mercy; and according to the abundance of Your compassions, erase my iniquity.

**50:4** Wash me more fully from my iniquity, and from my sin cleanse me.

**50:5** For I myself acknowledge my iniquity, and my sin is always against me.

**50:6** Against You alone I have sinned, and have done evil in Your presence, so that You may be justified in Your words and prevail when You are judged.

**50:7** For look, in iniquities I was conceived, and in sins my mother conceived me.

**50:8** For look, You have loved truth; You have made known to me the uncertain and hidden things of Your wisdom.

**50:9** You will sprinkle me with hyssop, and I will be cleansed; You will wash me, and I will become whiter than snow.

**50:10** To my hearing You will give joy and gladness, and the humbled bones will exult.

**50:11** Turn Your face away from my sins, and erase all my iniquities.

**50:12** Create a clean heart in me, God, and renew an upright spirit in my inward parts.

**50:13** Do not cast me out from Your face, and do not take away Your Holy Spirit from me.

**50:14** Restore to me the gladness of Your salvation, and strengthen me with a sovereign spirit.

**50:15** I will teach the unjust Your ways, and the ungodly will turn back to You.

**50:16** Deliver me from bloodshed, God, God of my salvation, and my tongue will exult in Your righteousness.

**50:17** Lord, You will open my lips, and my mouth will proclaim Your praise.

**50:18** For if You had desired sacrifice, I would certainly have given it; You will not take pleasure in burnt offerings.

**50:19** A sacrifice to God is a crushed spirit; a contrite and humbled heart, God, You will not despise.

**50:20** Deal kindly with Zion, Lord, in Your goodwill, so that the walls of Jerusalem may be built.

**50:21** Then You will accept a sacrifice of righteousness, offerings and burnt offerings; then they will place calves on Your altar.

## Translator Notes

- **50:1** — Latin 'In finem' ('To the end') reflects the Septuagint 'eis to telos', rendering the Hebrew superscription usually translated 'for the choirmaster' or 'for the choir director'.
- **50:5** — Latin 'contra me' literally means 'against me' (or 'facing me'), whereas the Septuagint and Hebrew read 'before me'.
- **50:6** — Latin 'cum judicaris' ('when You are judged') is passive, following the Septuagint (as also quoted in Romans 3:4), whereas the Hebrew is active ('when You judge').
- **50:8** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads 'incerta et occulta sapientiae tuae' ('the uncertain and hidden things of Your wisdom'), whereas the Hebrew speaks of truth in the inward parts and wisdom in the secret heart.
- **50:14** — Latin 'spiritu principali' literally means 'a sovereign/ruling/chief spirit' (reflecting the Septuagint 'pneumati hegemoniko'), whereas the Hebrew reads 'a willing spirit'.
