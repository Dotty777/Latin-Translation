## Text

**84:1** To the end, for the sons of Korah. A psalm.

**84:2** You blessed Your land, Lord; You turned away the captivity of Jacob.

**84:3** You pardoned the iniquity of Your people; You covered all their sins.

**84:4** You calmed all Your anger; You turned away from the anger of Your indignation.

**84:5** Turn us back, God our Savior, and turn Your anger away from us.

**84:6** Will You be angry with us forever? Or will You stretch out Your anger from generation to generation?

**84:7** God, turning back, You will give us life, and Your people will rejoice in You.

**84:8** Show us Your mercy, Lord, and give us Your salvation.

**84:9** I will hear what the Lord God speaks in me, for He will speak peace to His people, and upon His holy ones, and to those who turn back to the heart.

**84:10** Yet truly, close to those who fear Him is His salvation, so that glory may dwell in our land.

**84:11** Mercy and truth have met one another; righteousness and peace have kissed.

**84:12** Truth has sprung up from the earth, and righteousness has looked down from heaven.

**84:13** For indeed, the Lord will give goodness, and our land will give its fruit.

**84:14** Righteousness will walk before Him, and will set its steps in the way.

## Translator Notes

- **84:1** — Latin "In finem" ("To the end") follows the Septuagint ("eis to telos") rather than the Hebrew musical direction "For the choirmaster."
- **84:7** — Latin "Deus, tu conversus vivificabis nos" ("God, turning back, You will give us life") is a declarative statement of confidence with the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads as a question ("Will You not revive us again?").
- **84:9** — Latin "in me" ("in me") and "qui convertuntur ad cor" ("who turn back to the heart") are distinctive Vulgate and Septuagint readings; the Hebrew lacks "in me" and reads "let them not turn back to folly."
- **84:14** — The reflexive possessive "suos" grammatically refers to the subject "Justitia" ("its steps"), though the underlying Greek pronoun ("autou") and some interpreters refer it to the Lord ("His steps").
