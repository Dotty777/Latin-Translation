## Text

**18:1** To the end. A psalm of David.

**18:2** The heavens recount the glory of God, and the expanse announces the works of His hands.

**18:3** Day pours out a word to day, and night discloses knowledge to night.

**18:4** There are no speeches, nor sayings, whose voices are not heard.

**18:5** Into all the earth their sound has gone out, and their words to the ends of the world.

**18:6** In the sun He has set His tent, and he is like a bridegroom coming out of his bridal chamber; he has exulted like a giant to run the course;

**18:7** from the end of heaven is his going out. And his circuit extends all the way to its end, and there is no one who hides himself from his heat.

**18:8** The law of the Lord is unblemished, turning souls back; the testimony of the Lord is trustworthy, bestowing wisdom on little ones.

**18:9** The righteous decrees of the Lord are upright, gladdening hearts; the precept of the Lord is bright, illuminating the eyes.

**18:10** The fear of the Lord is holy, abiding forever and ever; the judgments of the Lord are true, justified in themselves,

**18:11** desirable beyond gold and much precious stone, and sweeter than honey and the honeycomb.

**18:12** For indeed Your servant keeps them; in keeping them there is abundant reward.

**18:13** Who discerns offenses? Cleanse me from my hidden ones;

**18:14** and spare Your servant from strangers. If they have not ruled over me, then I will be unblemished, and I will be thoroughly cleansed from the greatest offense.

**18:15** And the utterances of my mouth and the meditation of my heart will be pleasing in Your sight always, Lord, my helper and my redeemer.

## Translator Notes

- **18:1** — Latin "In finem" ("To the end," also in 19:1 and 20:1) follows the Septuagint ("eis to telos"), where the Hebrew superscription reads "For the choirmaster."
- **18:4** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate forms a double negative ("whose voices are not heard") affirming universal audibility, whereas the Hebrew reads "their voice is not heard."
- **18:5** — Latin "sonus" ("sound") follows the Septuagint ("phthongos," cited in Rom 10:18), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "measuring line."
- **18:6** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "In the sun He has set His tent" and "gigas" ("giant"), whereas the Hebrew reads "In them [the heavens] he has set a tent for the sun" and "mighty man."
- **18:10** — Latin "sanctus" ("holy") is a distinctly Vulgate reading where the Septuagint and Hebrew read "pure," and "in semetipsa" ("in themselves") corresponds to Hebrew "altogether."
- **18:14** — Latin "ab alienis" ("from strangers," which can also mean "from the offenses of others" in contrast with "meis" in v. 13) follows the Septuagint ("allotrion"), whereas the Hebrew reads "from presumptuous ones/sins."
