## Text

**62:1** A psalm of David, when he was in the desert of Idumea.

**62:2** God, my God, I keep watch for You from daybreak. My soul has thirsted for You; in how many ways my flesh has thirsted for You!

**62:3** In a desert land, both pathless and waterless, thus in the sanctuary I appeared before You, so that I might see Your power and Your glory.

**62:4** Because Your mercy is better than lives, my lips will praise You.

**62:5** So I will bless You in my life, and in Your name I will raise my hands.

**62:6** May my soul be filled as with fat and richness, and with lips of exultation my mouth will praise.

**62:7** If I have been mindful of You on my bed, in the morning hours I will meditate on You.

**62:8** For You have been my helper, and in the covering of Your wings I will exult.

**62:9** My soul has clung after You; Your right hand has upheld me.

**62:10** But they themselves have sought my soul in vain; they will enter into the lower parts of the earth;

**62:11** they will be handed over into the hands of the sword; they will be portions for foxes.

**62:12** But the king will rejoice in God; all who swear by Him will be praised, because the mouth of those who speak unjust things has been stopped up.

## Translator Notes

- **62:1** — The Vulgate (following the Septuagint) reads "in the desert of Idumea" (in deserto Idumaeae), whereas the Hebrew text reads "in the wilderness of Judah."
- **62:2** — Latin de luce vigilo (literally "from light I keep watch") follows the Septuagint "I rise early," where the Hebrew has "I earnestly seek"; quam multipliciter ("in how many ways") reflects the Septuagint reading of a Hebrew verb meaning "faints" or "longs."
- **62:3** — The Vulgate attaches "In a desert land, both pathless and waterless" to this verse rather than verse 2, and renders the Septuagint passive (apparui tibi, literally "I appeared to You") where the Hebrew has active "I have looked upon You."
- **62:7** — Latin in matutinis ("in the morning hours") follows the Septuagint ("in the early mornings"), whereas the Hebrew has "in the night watches."
- **62:10** — Latin in vanum ("in vain") follows the Septuagint (eis maten), whereas the Hebrew reads "to ruin" or "to destroy it."
