## Text

**21:1** To the end, for the morning support. A psalm of David.

**21:2** God, my God, look upon me: why have You abandoned me? Far from my salvation are the words of my offenses.

**21:3** My God, I will cry out by day, and You will not hear; and by night, and not to foolishness for me.

**21:4** Yet You dwell in the sanctuary, the praise of Israel.

**21:5** In You our fathers hoped; they hoped, and You delivered them.

**21:6** To You they cried out and were saved; in You they hoped and were not put to shame.

**21:7** But I am a worm, and not a man; a reproach of men and a castoff of the people.

**21:8** All who saw me mocked me; they spoke with their lips and shook their heads.

**21:9** "He hoped in the Lord; let Him rescue him. Let Him save him, since He desires him."

**21:10** For You are the One who drew me out from the womb, my hope from my mother's breasts.

**21:11** Upon You I was cast from the womb; from my mother's womb You are my God.

**21:12** Do not depart from me, for distress is near, for there is no one to help.

**21:13** Many calves have surrounded me; fat bulls have besieged me.

**21:14** They have opened their mouth against me, like a tearing and roaring lion.

**21:15** Like water I have been poured out, and all my bones have been scattered; my heart has become like melting wax in the midst of my belly.

**21:16** My strength has dried up like baked clay, and my tongue has clung to my throat; and into the dust of death You have brought me down.

**21:17** For many dogs have surrounded me; a council of evildoers has besieged me. They have pierced my hands and my feet.

**21:18** They have counted all my bones. Indeed, they themselves observed and stared at me.

**21:19** They divided my garments among themselves, and over my garment they cast lots.

**21:20** But You, Lord, do not keep Your help far from me; look to my defense.

**21:21** Rescue my soul from the sword, God, and my only one from the hand of the dog.

**21:22** Save me from the mouth of the lion, and my lowliness from the horns of unicorns.

**21:23** I will declare Your name to my brothers; in the midst of the assembly I will praise You.

**21:24** You who fear the Lord, praise Him; all the seed of Jacob, glorify Him.

**21:25** Let all the seed of Israel fear Him, for He has not disdained nor despised the plea of the poor, nor has He turned His face away from me; and when I cried out to Him, He heard me.

**21:26** With You is my praise in the great assembly; I will fulfill my vows in the sight of those who fear Him.

**21:27** The poor will eat and be satisfied, and those who seek Him will praise the Lord; their hearts will live forever and ever.

**21:28** All the ends of the earth will remember and turn to the Lord, and all the families of the nations will worship in His sight,

**21:29** for the kingdom belongs to the Lord, and He Himself will rule over the nations.

**21:30** All the fat ones of the earth have eaten and worshiped; in His sight all who go down into the earth will fall.

**21:31** And my soul will live for Him, and my seed will serve Him.

**21:32** The generation to come will be proclaimed to the Lord, and the heavens will proclaim His righteousness to a people that will be born, whom the Lord has made.

## Translator Notes

- **21:1** — In finem, pro susceptione matutina ("To the end, for the morning support") follows the Septuagint superscription, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "To the choirmaster: according to The Doe of the Dawn."
- **21:2** — The Vulgate includes respice in me ("look upon me"), absent from the Hebrew text, and renders verba delictorum meorum ("the words of my offenses") after the Septuagint, where the Hebrew has "the words of my groaning." Similarly in v. 3, non ad insipientiam mihi ("not to foolishness for me") follows the Septuagint against Hebrew "no rest."
- **21:17** — Foderunt ("They have pierced" or "dug through") follows the Septuagint reading rather than the Masoretic Text's "like a lion." In v. 18, dinumeraverunt ("They have counted") is likewise third-person plural rather than the Hebrew first-person singular.
- **21:22** — Unicornium ("unicorns") and humilitatem meam ("my lowliness") preserve the Vulgate and Septuagint readings, whereas the Hebrew text reads "wild oxen" and the verb "you have answered me."
- **21:32** — The Clementine Vulgate reads caeli ("the heavens") as the subject of annuntiabunt ("will proclaim") and quem fecit Dominus ("whom the Lord has made"), whereas the Hebrew has "they shall come and proclaim His righteousness... that He has done it." In v. 31, anima mea illi vivet ("my soul will live for Him") also follows the Septuagint against the Hebrew "he could not keep himself alive."
