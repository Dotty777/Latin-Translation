## Text

**107:1** A song of a psalm, for David himself.

**107:2** My heart is ready, God, my heart is ready; I will sing and make melody in my glory.

**107:3** Arise, my glory; arise, psaltery and lyre; I will arise at dawn.

**107:4** I will give thanks to You among the peoples, Lord, and I will make melody to You among the nations,

**107:5** for great above the heavens is Your mercy, and up to the clouds is Your truth.

**107:6** Be exalted above the heavens, God, and let Your glory be above all the earth,

**107:7** so that Your beloved ones may be freed. Save by Your right hand, and hear me.

**107:8** God has spoken in His sanctuary: "I will exult, and I will divide Shechem, and I will measure out the valley of tents.

**107:9** Mine is Gilead, and Mine is Manasseh, and Ephraim is the support of My head. Judah is My king;

**107:10** Moab is the basin of My hope; over Edom I will stretch out My sandal; to Me foreigners have been made friends."

**107:11** Who will lead me into a fortified city? Who will lead me all the way into Edom?

**107:12** Is it not You, God, who rejected us? And will You not go out, God, with our armies?

**107:13** Give us help from tribulation, for the salvation of man is vain.

**107:14** In God we will act with valor, and He Himself will reduce our enemies to nothing.

## Translator Notes

- **107:3** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate includes 'Arise, my glory' in this verse and reads 'I will arise at dawn' (exsurgam diluculo), whereas the Hebrew reads 'I will awaken the dawn.'
- **107:7** — Following the Clementine Vulgate punctuation, the clause 'so that Your beloved ones may be freed' completes the petition of v. 6 rather than introducing 'Save by Your right hand.'
- **107:8** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate translates the Hebrew place name Succoth literally as a common noun, 'the valley of tents' (convallem tabernaculorum).
- **107:9** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate renders Ephraim as 'the support of My head' (susceptio capitis mei) and Judah as 'My king' (rex meus), where the Hebrew reads 'helmet' and 'scepter' (or 'lawgiver').
- **107:10** — Distinctively Vulgate readings here include 'the basin of My hope' (lebes spei meae, vs. Hebrew 'washbasin') and 'to Me foreigners have been made friends' (mihi alienigenae amici facti sunt, vs. Hebrew 'over Philistia I shout in triumph'); 'Edom' renders the Greco-Latin name Idumaeam (also in v. 11).
