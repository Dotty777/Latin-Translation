## Text

**86:1** For the sons of Korah. A psalm of a song. Her foundations are in the holy mountains;

**86:2** the Lord loves the gates of Zion above all the tents of Jacob.

**86:3** Glorious things have been spoken about you, city of God!

**86:4** I will be mindful of Rahab and Babylon, of those who know Me; look, foreigners, and Tyre, and the people of the Ethiopians: these were there.

**86:5** Will Zion say, "Man and man was born in her, and the Most High Himself founded her"?

**86:6** The Lord will recount in the writings of peoples and of princes, of those who were in her.

**86:7** The dwelling in you is like that of all who rejoice.

## Translator Notes

- **86:1** — Latin "Fundamenta ejus" uses the plural "foundations," and "ejus" is gender-neutral ("Her/Its" referring to Zion, or "His" referring to the Lord).
- **86:4** — Latin "alienigenae" ("foreigners"), "populus Aethiopum" ("the people of the Ethiopians"), and "hi fuerunt illic" ("these were there") follow the Septuagint, where the Hebrew has "Philistia," "Cush," and "this one was born there."
- **86:5** — Latin "Numquid Sion dicet" ("Will Zion say") is a distinctive Vulgate reading arising from a Greek variant ("meti" for "meter," "mother"), whereas the Hebrew reads "And of Zion it shall be said"; "Homo et homo" ("Man and man") is a literal distributive idiom meaning "one person after another."
- **86:6** — Latin "et principum" ("and of princes") and "fuerunt" ("were") follow the Septuagint; the Hebrew lacks "and of princes" and reads "was born."
- **86:7** — Latin "Sicut laetantium omnium habitatio est in te" follows the Septuagint ("as of all who rejoice, the dwelling is in you"), differing notably from the Hebrew ("Singers and dancers alike say, 'All my springs are in you'").
