## Text

**111:1** Hallelujah, of the return of Haggai and Zechariah. Blessed is the man who fears the Lord; he will greatly delight in His commands.

**111:2** Mighty on the earth will be his offspring; the generation of the upright will be blessed.

**111:3** Glory and wealth are in his house, and his righteousness remains forever and ever.

**111:4** Light has dawned in darkness for the upright: merciful, and compassionate, and righteous.

**111:5** Pleasant is the man who shows mercy and lends; he will arrange his words in judgment,

**111:6** because he will never be shaken.

**111:7** The righteous man will be in eternal remembrance; he will not fear a bad report. His heart is ready to hope in the Lord,

**111:8** his heart is established; he will not be shaken until he looks down on his enemies.

**111:9** He distributed, he gave to the poor; his righteousness remains forever and ever; his horn will be lifted high in glory.

**111:10** The sinner will see and become angry; he will gnash his teeth and waste away; the desire of sinners will perish.

## Translator Notes

- **111:1** — The Vulgate superscription "of the return of Haggai and Zechariah" follows the Septuagint and is absent from the Hebrew text (which numbers this Psalm 112). Latin "volet nimis" literally reads "will exceedingly wish/desire," rendering the biblical idiom for taking great delight.
- **111:3** — Vulgate "Gloria et divitiæ" ("Glory and wealth") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "wealth and riches."
- **111:4** — The Latin places the three adjectives ("merciful, and compassionate, and righteous") without a verb, leaving open whether they describe the Lord or the upright person.
- **111:5** — Vulgate "sermones suos" specifically means "his words" (following the Septuagint), whereas the underlying Hebrew term can mean either "words" or "affairs."
- **111:10** — Vulgate "Peccator" and "peccatorum" ("sinner"/"sinners") follow the Septuagint, where the Hebrew reads "wicked."
