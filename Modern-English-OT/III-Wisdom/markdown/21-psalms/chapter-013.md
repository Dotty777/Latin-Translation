## Text

**13:1** To the end. A psalm of David. The foolish one has said in his heart, "There is no God." They have been corrupted, and they have become detestable in their pursuits; there is no one who does good, there is not even one.

**13:2** The Lord has looked down from heaven on the sons of men, to see whether there is anyone who understands, or anyone who seeks God.

**13:3** All of them have turned away; together they have become useless. There is no one who does good, there is not even one. An open tomb is their throat; with their tongues they kept acting deceitfully. The venom of asps is beneath their lips, whose mouth is full of cursing and bitterness; swift are their feet to shed blood. Ruin and wretchedness are in their ways, and they have not known the way of peace; no fear of God is before their eyes.

**13:4** Will they not come to know, all who practice iniquity, who devour My people like a meal of bread?

**13:5** They did not call on the Lord; there they trembled with fear, where there was no fear.

**13:6** For the Lord is in the righteous generation. You have put to shame the plan of the needy one, because the Lord is his hope.

**13:7** Who will give from Zion the salvation of Israel? When the Lord turns away the captivity of His people, Jacob will exult, and Israel will be glad.

## Translator Notes

- **13:1** — The superscription "To the end" (Latin "In finem," from the Septuagint) differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, usually rendered "For the choir director." The Vulgate also includes "there is not even one" ("non est usque ad unum") in verse 1 as well as verse 3.
- **13:3** — The Vulgate preserves the longer Septuagint text of verse 3 ("An open tomb is their throat ... no fear of God is before their eyes"), matching the catena quoted in Romans 3:13-18 and absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text of Psalm 14:3.
- **13:5** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate includes the closing clause "where there was no fear" ("ubi non erat timor," parallel to Psalm 52:6 / MT 53:5), which does not appear in the Hebrew Masoretic Text of Psalm 14:5.
- **13:6** — Latin "spes" ("hope," following the Septuagint) corresponds to "refuge" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
