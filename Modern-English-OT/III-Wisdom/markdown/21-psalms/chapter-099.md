## Text

**99:1** A psalm of thanksgiving.

**99:2** Shout with joy to God, all the earth; serve the Lord in gladness. Enter into His presence with exultation.

**99:3** Know that the Lord Himself is God; He Himself made us, and not we ourselves: His people, and the sheep of His pasture.

**99:4** Enter His gates with thanksgiving, His courts with hymns; give thanks to Him. Praise His name,

**99:5** for the Lord is sweet; His mercy is forever, and His truth even to generation and generation.

## Translator Notes

- **99:1** — Latin in confessione literally means 'in confession', used throughout the Vulgate Psalter for public thanksgiving and praise; the Vulgate numbers this superscription as verse 1, shifting the remaining verses by one compared to the Hebrew.
- **99:2** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads Deo ('to God') rather than the Hebrew divine name ('to the Lord') and in exsultatione ('with exultation') rather than Hebrew 'with joyful singing'.
- **99:3** — Latin et non ipsi nos ('and not we ourselves') follows the Septuagint and the Hebrew written text (Ketiv), whereas many Hebrew-based translations follow the marginal reading (Qere), 'and we are His'.
- **99:5** — Latin suavis ('sweet', also 'kind' or 'pleasant') renders Greek chrestos where the Hebrew has 'good'; likewise veritas ('truth') follows Greek aletheia against Hebrew 'faithfulness'.
