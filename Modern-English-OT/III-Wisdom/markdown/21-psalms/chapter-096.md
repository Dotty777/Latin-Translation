## Text

**96:1** For this David, when his land was restored. The Lord has reigned: let the earth exult; let many islands be glad.

**96:2** Cloud and thick darkness are all around Him; righteousness and judgment are the right ordering of His throne.

**96:3** Fire will go before Him, and will set His enemies ablaze all around.

**96:4** His lightning flashes shone upon the world; the earth saw, and was shaken.

**96:5** The mountains melted like wax before the face of the Lord; before the face of the Lord, all the earth.

**96:6** The heavens have proclaimed His righteousness, and all the peoples have seen His glory.

**96:7** Let all be put to shame who worship carved images, and who boast in their idols. Worship Him, all His angels.

**96:8** Zion heard, and was glad, and the daughters of Judah exulted because of Your judgments, Lord.

**96:9** For You are the Lord Most High over all the earth; You are exceedingly exalted above all gods.

**96:10** You who love the Lord, hate evil: the Lord guards the souls of His holy ones; He will deliver them from the hand of the sinner.

**96:11** Light has risen for the righteous one, and gladness for the upright in heart.

**96:12** Be glad, you righteous, in the Lord, and give thanks to the remembrance of His holiness.

## Translator Notes

- **96:1** — This superscription is unique to the Vulgate and Septuagint (Psalm 97 in Hebrew has none); Latin "Huic David" ("For this David") reflects the Greek dative article with a demonstrative, and "restituta est" refers to the restoration of his land.
- **96:2** — Latin "correctio" (literally "setting straight" or "right ordering," rendering Greek "katorthōsis") corresponds to "foundation" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **96:5** — The Clementine Vulgate reads nominative "omnis terra" ("all the earth," continuing the ellipsis from the preceding clause), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text and Greek Septuagint read the genitive ("the Lord of all the earth").
- **96:7** — Following the Septuagint (also quoted in Hebrews 1:6), the Vulgate reads "omnes angeli ejus" ("all His angels"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "all gods."
- **96:11** — Following the Septuagint ("aneteilen"), the Vulgate reads "orta est" ("has risen") with singular "justo" ("for the righteous one"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "is sown"; similarly in v. 10, the Vulgate has singular "peccatoris" ("of the sinner") rather than plural "of the wicked."
