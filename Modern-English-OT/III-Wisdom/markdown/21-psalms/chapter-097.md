## Text

**97:1** A psalm for David himself. Sing a new song to the Lord, for He has done marvelous deeds. His right hand has brought salvation for Himself, and His holy arm.

**97:2** The Lord has made His salvation known; in the sight of the nations He has revealed His righteousness.

**97:3** He has remembered His mercy and His truth to the house of Israel. All the bounds of the earth have seen the salvation of our God.

**97:4** Shout with joy to God, all the earth; sing, and exult, and sing psalms.

**97:5** Sing psalms to the Lord with the lyre; with the lyre and the sound of a psalm;

**97:6** with hammered trumpets, and the sound of a horn trumpet. Shout with joy in the sight of the King, the Lord:

**97:7** let the sea be moved, and its fullness; the world, and those who dwell in it.

**97:8** The rivers will clap their hands; together the mountains will exult

**97:9** before the presence of the Lord: for He is coming to judge the earth. He will judge the world in righteousness, and the peoples in equity.

## Translator Notes

- **97:1** — The Vulgate and Septuagint attribute this psalm to David ("Psalmus ipsi David"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text (Psalm 98) reads simply "A psalm." Latin "salvavit sibi" literally means "has saved for Himself," expressing salvation achieved without a stated direct object.
- **97:6** — Following the Septuagint ("elatais"), the Vulgate adds the adjective "ductilibus" ("hammered" or "beaten" metal) to "tubis" ("trumpets"), and distinguishes the shofar as "tubæ corneæ" ("a horn trumpet").
- **97:7** — Following the Septuagint ("saleuthētō"), the Vulgate reads "moveatur" ("be moved"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "let the sea roar."
- **97:8** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate uses future indicative verbs ("plaudent," "exsultabunt": "will clap," "will exult") rather than the jussive ("let ... clap/sing") of the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
