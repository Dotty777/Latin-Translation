## Text

**115:1** Hallelujah. I believed, for which reason I spoke; but I was brought exceedingly low.

**115:2** I said in my bewilderment, "Every man is a liar."

**115:3** What should I render to the Lord for all that He has rendered to me?

**115:4** I will receive the cup of salvation, and I will call upon the name of the Lord.

**115:5** I will fulfill my vows to the Lord before all His people.

**115:6** The death of His holy ones is precious in the sight of the Lord.

**115:7** Lord, for I am Your servant; I am Your servant, and the son of Your maidservant. You have broken my bonds;

**115:8** to You I will offer a sacrifice of praise, and I will call upon the name of the Lord.

**115:9** I will fulfill my vows to the Lord in the sight of all His people,

**115:10** in the courts of the house of the Lord, in the midst of you, Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **115:1** — Vulgate Psalm 115 corresponds to Hebrew Psalm 116:10-19. Latin Credidi, propter quod locutus sum ("I believed, for which reason I spoke," quoted via the Septuagint in 2 Corinthians 4:13) differs from Hebrew-based readings ("I believed even when I spoke"), and humiliatus sum ("I was brought low") repeats the exact verb used in 114:6.
- **115:2** — Latin in excessu meo (literally "in my going out [of myself]" or "in my ecstasy") translates Greek ekstasis to convey mental overwhelm or bewilderment, corresponding to "in my haste/alarm" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **115:3** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate repeats the verb retribuo in a relative clause (pro omnibus quae retribuit mihi, "for all that He has rendered to me"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text uses a noun phrase ("for all His benefits toward me").
- **115:7** — Latin quia ("for") reflects a Hebrew particle in the plea that can also be taken as emphatic ("surely"), and Dirupisti ("You have broken [apart]"), following the Septuagint, is stronger than Hebrew "You have loosed."
- **115:8** — Latin hostiam laudis ("a sacrifice of praise"), following the Septuagint, corresponds to "a sacrifice of thanksgiving" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
