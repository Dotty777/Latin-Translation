## Text

**48:1** To the end, for the sons of Korah. A psalm.

**48:2** Hear these things, all nations; take them in with your ears, all you who inhabit the world:

**48:3** both the earthborn and the sons of men, together as one, rich and poor.

**48:4** My mouth will speak wisdom, and the meditation of my heart, prudence.

**48:5** I will bend my ear to a parable; I will lay open my riddle with the psaltery.

**48:6** Why should I fear in the evil day? The iniquity of my heel will surround me.

**48:7** Those who trust in their strength also boast in the abundance of their riches.

**48:8** A brother does not redeem, a man will redeem: he will not give to God his propitiation,

**48:9** and the price of the redemption of his soul. And he will labor forever;

**48:10** and he will still live to the end.

**48:11** He will not see destruction when he sees the wise dying: together the senseless and the foolish will perish. And they will leave their riches to strangers,

**48:12** and their tombs are their houses forever; their tents through generation and generation: they called their own names on their lands.

**48:13** And man, when he was in honor, did not understand. He was compared to senseless beasts, and he became like them.

**48:14** This way of theirs is a stumbling block to themselves, and afterward they will take pleasure in their own mouth.

**48:15** Like sheep they are placed in the underworld: death will feed on them. And the righteous will have dominion over them in the morning, and their help will grow old in the underworld, away from their glory.

**48:16** Nevertheless, God will redeem my soul from the hand of the underworld when He receives me.

**48:17** Do not be afraid when a man becomes rich, and when the glory of his house is multiplied:

**48:18** for when he dies, he will not take all things, nor will his glory descend with him.

**48:19** For his soul will be blessed in his own life; he will praise You when You do good to him.

**48:20** He will enter even to the generations of his fathers, and even forever he will not see light.

**48:21** Man, when he was in honor, did not understand. He was compared to senseless beasts, and he became like them.

## Translator Notes

- **48:6** — Latin iniquitas calcanei mei ("the iniquity of my heel") follows the Septuagint literally, whereas the Hebrew is commonly understood as "the iniquity of my supplanters/foes."
- **48:8** — Latin Frater non redimit, redimet homo ("A brother does not redeem, a man will redeem") literally reflects the Septuagint rendering of an emphatic Hebrew construction ("no man can by any means redeem a brother"), taken either as a rhetorical question or, in Latin patristic tradition, as a reference to Christ; in 48:9, laborabit ("he will labor") similarly reflects the Septuagint reading.
- **48:13** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads non intellexit ("did not understand") in both 48:13 and 48:21, whereas the Hebrew has "will not remain" in 48:13 and "does not understand" in 48:21.
- **48:15** — Latin infernus (and inferi in 48:16) denotes the underworld or realm of the dead, rendering Greek Hades. The clause auxilium eorum veterascet in inferno a gloria eorum ("their help will grow old in the underworld, away from their glory") follows the Septuagint against Hebrew "their form will waste away in Sheol from their dwelling."
- **48:19** — Latin confitebitur tibi cum benefeceris ei ("he will praise You when You do good to him") follows the Septuagint in shifting Hebrew "men will praise you when you do well for yourself" into a clause traditionally read in Latin exegesis as addressing God.
