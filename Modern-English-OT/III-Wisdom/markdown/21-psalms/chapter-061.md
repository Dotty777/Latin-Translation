## Text

**61:1** To the end, for Jeduthun. A psalm of David.

**61:2** Will my soul not be subject to God? For from Him comes my salvation.

**61:3** For indeed He is my God and my Savior, my upholder; I will not be moved any longer.

**61:4** How long do you rush upon a man? All of you kill, as against a leaning wall and a pushed-down stone fence.

**61:5** Nevertheless, they planned to reject my worth; I ran in thirst; with their mouth they were blessing, and in their heart they were cursing.

**61:6** Nevertheless, be subject to God, my soul, because from Him comes my patience;

**61:7** for He Himself is my God and my Savior, my helper; I will not depart.

**61:8** My salvation and my glory are in God; He is the God of my help, and my hope is in God.

**61:9** Hope in Him, all the assembly of the people; pour out your hearts before Him: God is our helper forever.

**61:10** Nevertheless, the sons of men are vain, the sons of men are false in the scales, so that they themselves may deceive out of vanity together.

**61:11** Do not hope in iniquity, and do not covet plunder; if wealth abounds, do not set your heart on it.

**61:12** Once God has spoken; these two things I have heard: that power belongs to God,

**61:13** and to You, Lord, belongs mercy, for You will repay each one according to his works.

## Translator Notes

- **61:2** — Latin Nonne Deo subjecta erit anima mea? ("Will my soul not be subject to God?") follows the Septuagint in phrasing this as a question about subjection, whereas the Hebrew text (Psalm 62) states that the soul waits in silence for God alone.
- **61:4** — Latin interficitis ("kill") is an active verb following the Septuagint, and parieti inclinato et maceriæ depulsæ stands in the dative ("as against a leaning wall and a pushed-down stone fence"), whereas the Hebrew text is often read passively ("you shall all be battered, like a leaning wall").
- **61:5** — Latin cucurri in siti ("I ran in thirst") reflects the Septuagint reading edramon en dipsei taken as first-person singular, diverging from the Hebrew text ("they delight in falsehood"); in 61:7 non emigrabo ("I will not depart," literally "move away") also reflects a distinct Septuagint verb rather than Hebrew "be shaken."
- **61:9** — Latin omnis congregatio populi ("all the assembly of the people") follows the Septuagint reading of the Hebrew consonants as "congregation" rather than "at all times," and in 61:8 Deus auxilii mei ("God of my help") corresponds to Hebrew "rock of my strength."
- **61:10** — Latin ut decipiant ipsi de vanitate in idipsum ("so that they themselves may deceive out of vanity together") follows the Septuagint closely (with in idipsum rendering Greek epi to auto, "together"), whereas the Hebrew text speaks of ascending in the balances and being lighter than a breath; in 61:12 duo hæc ("these two things") likewise follows the Septuagint against Hebrew "twice this."
