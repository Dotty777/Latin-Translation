## Text

**150:1** Hallelujah. Praise the Lord in His holy places; praise Him in the expanse of His might.

**150:2** Praise Him for His mighty works; praise Him according to the abundance of His greatness.

**150:3** Praise Him with the sound of the trumpet; praise Him with harp and lyre.

**150:4** Praise Him with tambourine and dancing; praise Him with strings and organ.

**150:5** Praise Him with well-sounding cymbals; praise Him with cymbals of jubilation.

**150:6** May every spirit praise the Lord! Hallelujah.

## Translator Notes

- **150:1** — Vulgate Dominum ("the Lord") differs from both the Hebrew (El) and the Septuagint (theon, "God"). Latin in sanctis ejus is plural and can be parsed as neuter ("in His holy places," i.e., sanctuary) or masculine ("in His saints," a reading prominent in Latin patristic commentary).
- **150:4** — Latin choro (as in 149:3) denotes a choral dance (later also "choir"), and singular organo (from Greek organon) denotes an ancient wind instrument or pipe organ (corresponding to Hebrew ugav, a reed pipe or flute).
- **150:5** — Latin benesonantibus ("well-sounding," following Septuagint euechois) and jubilationis ("of jubilation") preserve the Vulgate-Septuagint wording rather than the Hebrew "sounding/clashing cymbals."
- **150:6** — Latin spiritus can mean either "spirit" or "breath" (translating Septuagint pnoe and Hebrew neshamah, "breath / living being"); Latin tradition widely takes Omnis spiritus as "every spirit."
