## Text

**52:1** To the end, for Mahalath; of the understanding of David. The fool has said in his heart, "There is no God."

**52:2** They are corrupt and have become abominable in iniquities; there is no one who does good.

**52:3** God has looked down from heaven on the sons of men, so that He may see whether there is one who understands, or one who seeks God.

**52:4** All have turned aside; together they have become useless; there is no one who does good, there is not even one.

**52:5** Will they not know, all who work iniquity, who devour My people like a meal of bread?

**52:6** They did not call on God; there they trembled with fear, where there was no fear. For God scattered the bones of those who please men; they were put to shame, because God spurned them.

**52:7** Who will give from Zion the salvation of Israel? When God turns back the captivity of His people, Jacob will exult, and Israel will be glad.

## Translator Notes

- **52:1** — Latin 'pro Maeleth' reflects the Hebrew musical or liturgical term 'Mahalath' via the Septuagint, and 'intelligentiae' ('of understanding') renders the Septuagint translation of Hebrew 'maskil'. In the Vulgate numbering, the superscription is joined with the opening line of the psalm.
- **52:4** — Latin 'inutiles' ('useless', following the Septuagint's 'made unprofitable') corresponds to the Hebrew 'corrupt' or 'tainted'.
- **52:6** — Latin 'eorum qui hominibus placent' ('those who please men') follows the Septuagint ('men-pleasers'), whereas the Hebrew reads 'the one who encamps against you'. Likewise, 'confusi sunt' ('they were put to shame') is passive, whereas the Hebrew is second-person active ('you put them to shame').
- **52:7** — Latin 'converterit ... captivitatem' literally speaks of turning back captivity (following the Septuagint), whereas modern translations of the Hebrew often render the idiom 'restores the fortunes'.
