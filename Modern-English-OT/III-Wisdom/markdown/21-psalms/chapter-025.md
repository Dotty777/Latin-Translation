## Text

**25:1** To the end. A psalm of David. Judge me, Lord, because I have walked in my innocence, and, hoping in the Lord, I will not grow weak.

**25:2** Examine me, Lord, and test me; burn my kidneys and my heart.

**25:3** For Your mercy is before my eyes, and I have taken delight in Your truth.

**25:4** I have not sat with a council of vanity, and I will not enter with those who carry out unjust things.

**25:5** I have hated the assembly of those who do evil, and I will not sit with the ungodly.

**25:6** I will wash my hands among the innocent, and I will go around Your altar, Lord,

**25:7** so that I may hear the voice of praise and recount all Your wonders.

**25:8** Lord, I have loved the beauty of Your house and the dwelling place of Your glory.

**25:9** Do not destroy my soul with the ungodly, God, nor my life with men of blood,

**25:10** in whose hands are iniquities; their right hand is filled with bribes.

**25:11** But I have walked in my innocence; redeem me, and have mercy on me.

**25:12** My foot has stood on straight ground; in the assemblies I will bless You, Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **25:1** — The Clementine Vulgate heading includes In finem ("To the end"), which is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text (where this is Psalm 26), and non infirmabor ("I will not grow weak") contrasts with the Hebrew "I shall not waver."
- **25:2** — Latin ure renes meos literally means "burn my kidneys," combining the imagery of testing by fire with the biblical idiom of the kidneys as the seat of inward affections and conscience.
- **25:3** — Vulgate complacui ("I have taken delight," following the Septuagint) differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "I have walked."
- **25:6** — Vulgate inter innocentes ("among the innocent") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "in innocence."
- **25:10** — Latin muneribus literally means "gifts," used here in the judicial sense of bribes.
