## Text

**6:1** To the end, in songs. A psalm of David. For the eighth.

**6:2** Lord, do not reprove me in Your fury, nor discipline me in Your anger.

**6:3** Have mercy on me, Lord, for I am weak; heal me, Lord, for my bones are deeply troubled.

**6:4** And my soul is greatly troubled; but You, Lord, how long?

**6:5** Turn back, Lord, and rescue my soul; save me because of Your mercy.

**6:6** For in death there is no one who remembers You; and in the underworld, who will give praise to You?

**6:7** I have grown weary in my groaning; every single night I will wash my bed; with my tears I will soak my bedding.

**6:8** My eye is troubled from fury; I have grown old among all my enemies.

**6:9** Depart from me, all you who practice iniquity, for the Lord has heard the voice of my weeping.

**6:10** The Lord has heard my plea; the Lord has accepted my prayer.

**6:11** Let all my enemies be put to shame and severely troubled; let them turn back and be put to shame very swiftly.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — Latin Pro octava ("For the eighth") corresponds to the Hebrew musical term sheminit, often understood as an eight-stringed instrument or a lower octave.
- **6:6** — Latin in inferno literally refers to the lower realm or underworld (translating Greek Hades and Hebrew Sheol); confitebitur in ecclesiastical Latin carries the sense of giving praise or thanks.
- **6:8** — Latin a furore ("from fury") and first-person inveteravi ("I have grown old") follow the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "from grief/vexation" and third-person "it [my eye] grows old."
- **6:10** — Latin suscepit ("has accepted") follows the Septuagint aorist, whereas the Hebrew verb is imperfect ("accepts / will accept").
