## Text

**137:1** For David himself. I will give thanks to You, Lord, with my whole heart, because You have heard the words of my mouth. In the presence of the angels I will sing psalms to You;

**137:2** I will worship toward Your holy temple, and I will give thanks to Your name for Your mercy and Your truth; because You have magnified Your holy name above everything.

**137:3** On whatever day I call upon You, hear me; You will multiply strength in my soul.

**137:4** Let all the kings of the earth give thanks to You, Lord, because they have heard all the words of Your mouth.

**137:5** And let them sing in the ways of the Lord, because the glory of the Lord is great;

**137:6** because the Lord is exalted, and He looks upon lowly things, and He knows lofty things from a distance.

**137:7** If I walk in the midst of tribulation, You will give me life; and against the wrath of my enemies You have stretched out Your hand, and Your right hand has saved me.

**137:8** The Lord will repay on my behalf. Lord, Your mercy is forever; do not despise the works of Your hands.

## Translator Notes

- **137:1** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate includes "because You have heard the words of my mouth" (absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text) and reads "angels" (angelorum) where the Hebrew has elohim ("gods").
- **137:2** — Vulgate magnificasti super omne, nomen sanctum tuum ("magnified Your holy name above everything") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "magnified Your word above all Your name" (or "Your name and Your word above all").
- **137:3** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate uses the imperative exaudi me ("hear me") and future multiplicabis ("You will multiply"), whereas the Hebrew uses past-tense verbs.
- **137:8** — Vulgate retribuet pro me ("will repay on my behalf") and ne despicias ("do not despise") reflect the Septuagint, where the Hebrew has "will accomplish for me" and "do not forsake."
