## Text

**32:1** A psalm of David. Exult, you righteous, in the Lord; praise is fitting for the upright.

**32:2** Give thanks to the Lord with the lyre; make melody to Him on a harp of ten strings.

**32:3** Sing to Him a new song; make melody well to Him with a loud shout.

**32:4** For the word of the Lord is upright, and all His works are in faithfulness.

**32:5** He loves mercy and judgment; the earth is full of the Lord's mercy.

**32:6** By the word of the Lord the heavens were established, and by the breath of His mouth all their power.

**32:7** He gathers the waters of the sea as in a waterskin; He places the deeps in storehouses.

**32:8** Let all the earth fear the Lord, and let all who inhabit the world be shaken before Him.

**32:9** For He Himself spoke, and they were made; He Himself commanded, and they were created.

**32:10** The Lord frustrates the plans of the nations; He rejects the thoughts of the peoples, and He rejects the plans of princes.

**32:11** But the plan of the Lord remains forever, the thoughts of His heart from generation to generation.

**32:12** Blessed is the nation whose God is the Lord, the people whom He chose as an inheritance for Himself.

**32:13** From heaven the Lord looked down; He saw all the sons of men.

**32:14** From His prepared dwelling place He looked out upon all who inhabit the earth,

**32:15** He who fashioned their hearts one by one, who understands all their works.

**32:16** A king is not saved by great power, and a giant will not be saved by the greatness of his power.

**32:17** A horse is deceptive for safety, yet by the abundance of its power it will not be saved.

**32:18** Look, the eyes of the Lord are upon those who fear Him, and upon those who hope in His mercy,

**32:19** so that He may rescue their souls from death and nourish them in famine.

**32:20** Our soul waits for the Lord, because He is our helper and protector.

**32:21** For in Him our heart will be glad, and in His holy name we have hoped.

**32:22** May Your mercy, Lord, be upon us, just as we have hoped in You.

## Translator Notes

- **32:1** — The superscription Psalmus David ("A psalm of David") follows the Septuagint and is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text; Vulgate Psalm 32 corresponds to Psalm 33 in Hebrew numbering.
- **32:6** — Latin spiritu means both "breath" and "spirit," and virtus ("power," rendering Greek dynamis) corresponds to Hebrew "host" (the heavenly host); the Vulgate also reads firmati sunt ("were established") where the Hebrew has "were made."
- **32:7** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads in utre ("in a waterskin"), reflecting a different vocalization of the Hebrew consonants that the Masoretic Text reads as "as a heap."
- **32:10** — The third clause, et reprobat consilia principum ("and He rejects the plans of princes"), appears in the Vulgate and Septuagint but is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **32:15** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads sigillatim ("one by one"), whereas the Hebrew reads "altogether" (or "all of them").
