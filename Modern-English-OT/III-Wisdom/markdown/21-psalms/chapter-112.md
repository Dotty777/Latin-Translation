## Text

**112:1** Hallelujah. Praise the Lord, servants; praise the name of the Lord.

**112:2** May the name of the Lord be blessed from this time on and forever.

**112:3** From the rising of the sun to its setting, the name of the Lord is praiseworthy.

**112:4** High above all nations is the Lord, and above the heavens is His glory.

**112:5** Who is like the Lord our God, who dwells in the heights,

**112:6** and looks upon lowly things in heaven and on earth?

**112:7** Raising the needy man from the earth, and lifting the poor man from the dung,

**112:8** so that He may seat him with princes, with the princes of His people.

**112:9** He who makes the barren woman dwell in a house, a rejoicing mother of children.

## Translator Notes

- **112:1** — Latin "pueri" (rendering Greek "paides") can mean either "servants" or "children"; unlike the Hebrew text (which numbers this Psalm 113), the Vulgate does not add "of the Lord" after "pueri."
- **112:6** — Vulgate "humilia respicit" ("looks upon lowly things") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "who humbles Himself to look."
- **112:7** — Vulgate "a terra" ("from the earth") follows the Septuagint where the Hebrew reads "from the dust," and "de stercore" literally means "from the dung."
