## Text

**138:1** To the end, a psalm of David. Lord, You have tested me, and You have known me;

**138:2** You have known my sitting down and my rising up.

**138:3** You have understood my thoughts from a distance; You have traced out my path and my cord:

**138:4** and You have foreseen all my ways, because there is no word on my tongue.

**138:5** Look, Lord, You have known all things, the last and the ancient. You have formed me, and You have placed Your hand upon me.

**138:6** Your knowledge has become wonderful from me; it has been made strong, and I will not be able to reach it.

**138:7** Where will I go from Your Spirit? And where will I flee from Your face?

**138:8** If I ascend into heaven, You are there; if I descend into hell, You are present.

**138:9** If I take my wings at dawn, and dwell in the farthest parts of the sea,

**138:10** for indeed Your hand will lead me to that place, and Your right hand will hold me.

**138:11** And I said, "Perhaps darkness will trample me, and the night will be my illumination in my delights."

**138:12** Because darkness will not be obscured from You, and the night will be illuminated like the day: as its darkness is, so also is its light.

**138:13** Because You have possessed my kidneys; You have taken me up from my mother's womb.

**138:14** I will give thanks to You, because You have been fearfully magnified; Your works are wonderful, and my soul knows it exceedingly well.

**138:15** My bone has not been hidden from You, which You made in secret, and my substance in the lower parts of the earth.

**138:16** Your eyes saw my unfinished state, and in Your book all will be written. Days will be formed, and no one will be in them.

**138:17** But to me Your friends have been exceedingly honored, God; their rule has been made exceedingly strong.

**138:18** I will count them, and they will be multiplied beyond the sand. I have risen up, and I am still with You.

**138:19** If You kill sinners, God, men of blood, turn aside from me:

**138:20** because you say in thought, "They will take Your cities in vain."

**138:21** Did I not hate those who hate You, Lord, and waste away over Your enemies?

**138:22** With a complete hatred I hated them, and they have become enemies to me.

**138:23** Test me, God, and know my heart; question me, and know my paths.

**138:24** And see if a way of iniquity is in me, and lead me in the eternal way.

## Translator Notes

- **138:1** — The superscription In finem ("To the end," also in 139:1) follows the Septuagint (eis to telos), corresponding to the Hebrew musical heading often rendered "For the choir director."
- **138:3** — Vulgate funiculum meum ("my cord" or "measuring line") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "my lying down."
- **138:11** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads nox illuminatio mea in deliciis meis ("the night will be my illumination in my delights"), where the Hebrew has "the light around me will be night."
- **138:16** — Vulgate Imperfectum meum denotes an unformed or unfinished state; the closing clause Dies formabuntur, et nemo in eis ("Days will be formed, and no one will be in them") follows the Septuagint and the written Hebrew (Ketiv), whereas many translations follow the spoken Hebrew (Qere) "when as yet there was not one of them."
- **138:17** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads amici tui ("Your friends") and principatus eorum ("their rule"), whereas the Hebrew reads "Your thoughts" and "their sum."
- **138:20** — Vulgate dicitis in cogitatione: Accipient in vanitate civitates tuas ("you say in thought, 'They will take Your cities in vain'") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew refers to enemies taking God's name in vain.
