## Text

**87:1** A song of a psalm, for the sons of Korah, to the end, for Mahalath, to respond. Understanding of Heman the Ezrahite.

**87:2** Lord, God of my salvation, by day I have cried out, and by night before You.

**87:3** Let my prayer enter into Your sight; incline Your ear to my plea.

**87:4** For my soul has been filled with evils, and my life has drawn near to the underworld.

**87:5** I have been counted with those going down into the pit; I have become like a man without help,

**87:6** free among the dead; like the wounded sleeping in tombs, of whom You are no longer mindful, and they themselves have been cast away from Your hand.

**87:7** They have placed me in the lower pit, in dark places, and in the shadow of death.

**87:8** Upon me Your fury has been firmly set, and You have brought all Your waves upon me.

**87:9** You have put my acquaintances far from me; they have made me an abomination to themselves. I was handed over, and I did not go out;

**87:10** my eyes have grown faint from want. I have cried out to You, Lord, all day long; I have stretched out my hands to You.

**87:11** Will You perform wonders for the dead? Or will physicians raise them up, and will they praise You?

**87:12** Will anyone recount Your mercy in the tomb, and Your truth in destruction?

**87:13** Will Your wonders be known in the darkness, and Your righteousness in the land of forgetfulness?

**87:14** And I have cried out to You, Lord, and in the morning my prayer will come before You.

**87:15** Why, Lord, do You reject my prayer and turn Your face away from me?

**87:16** I am poor, and in labors from my youth; yet having been exalted, I have been humbled and deeply troubled.

**87:17** Over me Your wraths have passed, and Your terrors have deeply troubled me;

**87:18** they have surrounded me like water all day long; they have surrounded me together.

**87:19** You have removed friend and neighbor far from me, and my acquaintances because of misery.

## Translator Notes

- **87:1** — Latin 'in finem' ('to the end') and 'Intellectus' ('Understanding') are distinctive Vulgate superscriptions following the Septuagint for the Hebrew headings ('for the choir director' and 'Maskil').
- **87:6** — Vulgate 'liber' ('free') and 'vulnerati' ('the wounded') follow the Septuagint; the Hebrew has chofshi ('free,' that is, cut loose among the dead) and 'the slain.'
- **87:11** — Vulgate 'medici' ('physicians') follows the Septuagint in reading the Hebrew consonants for 'shades of the dead' as 'healers'.
- **87:15** — Vulgate reads 'orationem meam' ('my prayer'), a distinctive reading where the Hebrew and Septuagint read 'my soul'.
- **87:19** — Vulgate 'a miseria' ('because of misery') follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew text reads 'darkness'.
