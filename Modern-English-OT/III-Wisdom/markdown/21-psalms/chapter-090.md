## Text

**90:1** Praise of a song of David. He who dwells in the help of the Most High will remain in the protection of the God of heaven.

**90:2** He will say to the Lord, "You are my upholder and my refuge; my God, I will hope in Him."

**90:3** For He Himself has delivered me from the snare of the hunters, and from a harsh word.

**90:4** With His shoulders He will overshadow you, and under His wings you will hope.

**90:5** His truth will surround you with a shield; you will not be afraid of terror by night,

**90:6** of the arrow flying by day, of the matter walking about in darkness, of assault and the noonday demon.

**90:7** A thousand will fall at your side, and ten thousand at your right hand; yet it will not draw near to you.

**90:8** Nevertheless, with your eyes you will observe, and you will see the retribution of sinners.

**90:9** For You, Lord, are my hope; you have set the Most High as your refuge.

**90:10** No evil will approach you, and no scourge will draw near to your tent.

**90:11** For He has commanded His angels regarding you, that they may guard you in all your ways.

**90:12** In their hands they will carry you, so that you do not by chance strike your foot against a stone.

**90:13** Upon the asp and the basilisk you will walk, and you will trample the lion and the dragon.

**90:14** "Because he has hoped in Me, I will deliver him; I will protect him, because he has known My name.

**90:15** He will cry out to Me, and I will hear him; I am with him in tribulation; I will rescue him, and I will glorify him.

**90:16** With length of days I will fill him, and I will show him My salvation."

## Translator Notes

- **90:1** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "in the help of the Most High" and "in the protection of the God of heaven," whereas the Masoretic Text reads "in the shelter of the Most High" and "in the shadow of the Almighty."
- **90:3** — Latin "liberavit me ... a verbo aspero" ("He has delivered me ... from a harsh word") follows the Septuagint tradition reading Hebrew "davar" ("word"), whereas the Masoretic Text reads "He will deliver you ... from deadly pestilence" ("dever").
- **90:6** — Latin "negotio" ("matter" or "business") and "incursu, et dæmonio meridiano" ("assault and the noonday demon") follow the Septuagint, whereas the Masoretic Text reads "pestilence" and "destruction that wastes at noonday."
- **90:13** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate names "the asp and the basilisk," whereas the Masoretic Text reads "the lion and the cobra."
