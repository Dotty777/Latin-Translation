## Text

**55:1** To the end, for the people who were removed far from the holy things. Of David, for a title inscription, when the foreigners held him in Gath.

**55:2** Be merciful to me, God, for man has trampled on me; attacking all day long, he has afflicted me.

**55:3** My enemies have trampled on me all day long, for many are waging war against me.

**55:4** From the height of the day I will fear, but I will hope in You.

**55:5** In God I will praise my words; in God I have hoped; I will not fear what flesh may do to me.

**55:6** All day long they detested my words; all their thoughts are against me for evil.

**55:7** They will dwell and hide; they themselves will watch my heel. Just as they have waited for my soul,

**55:8** for nothing You will save them; in anger You will shatter the peoples.

**55:9** God, I have declared my life to You; You have set my tears in Your sight, just as also in Your promise;

**55:10** then my enemies will be turned back. On whatever day I call upon You, look, I know that You are my God.

**55:11** In God I will praise the word; in the Lord I will praise the saying. In God I have hoped; I will not fear what man may do to me.

**55:13** In me, God, are Your vows, which I will pay, praises to You;

**55:14** for You have rescued my soul from death and my feet from slipping, so that I may be pleasing before God in the light of the living.

## Translator Notes

- **55:1** — Psalm 55 in the Vulgate corresponds to Psalm 56 in Hebrew numbering; Latin "a sanctis" can mean either "from the holy things/sanctuary" or "from the holy ones/saints." Latin "Allophyli" (from Greek "allophyloi," literally "foreigners") is the Septuagint and Gallican Vulgate designation here for the Philistines (cf. 1 Sam 21:10-15).
- **55:4** — Latin "Ab altitudine diei" ("From the height of the day") follows the Septuagint in joining Hebrew "marom" ("on high," the end of verse 3 in the Masoretic Text) with "day" at the start of verse 4.
- **55:5** — Latin "sermones meos" ("my words") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "His word."
- **55:8** — Latin "pro nihilo salvos facies illos" ("for nothing You will save them") literally renders the Septuagint (often understood rhetorically as "on no account will You save them"), whereas the Hebrew reads "for iniquity, cast them out" or "will they escape?"
- **55:9** — Latin "vitam meam" ("my life"), "in conspectu tuo" ("in Your sight"), and "in promissione tua" ("in Your promise") follow the Septuagint, where the Hebrew reads "my wanderings," "in Your bottle," and "in Your book."
