## Text

**92:1** Praise of a song for David himself, on the day before the Sabbath, when the earth was founded. The Lord has reigned; He has clothed Himself with splendor. The Lord has clothed Himself with strength and girded Himself. For indeed He has made the world firm, which will not be shaken.

**92:2** Your throne is prepared from then; You are from eternity.

**92:3** The rivers have lifted up, Lord, the rivers have lifted up their voice; the rivers have lifted up their waves,

**92:4** from the voices of many waters. Marvelous are the swellings of the sea; marvelous is the Lord in the heights.

**92:5** Your testimonies have become exceedingly trustworthy; holiness is fitting for Your house, Lord, for length of days.

## Translator Notes

- **92:1** — The Vulgate superscription (absent in the Hebrew Masoretic Text) follows the Septuagint in assigning this psalm to the day before the Sabbath (Friday), while rendering the Greek 'was inhabited' as 'was founded' (fundata est).
- **92:2** — Latin 'ex tunc' ('from then') literally mirrors the Septuagint.
- **92:4** — Latin 'a vocibus aquarum multarum' ('from the voices of many waters') continues the sentence of verse 3, whereas the Hebrew reads it as a comparative ('more than the roar of many waters').
