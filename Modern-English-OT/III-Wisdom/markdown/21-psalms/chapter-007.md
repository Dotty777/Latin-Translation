## Text

**7:1** A psalm of David, which he sang to the Lord concerning the words of Cushi, son of Jemini.

**7:2** Lord, my God, in You I have hoped; save me from all who pursue me, and deliver me,

**7:3** so that he may never seize my soul like a lion, while there is no one to redeem, nor anyone to save.

**7:4** Lord, my God, if I have done this, if there is iniquity in my hands,

**7:5** if I have paid back those who return evil to me, may I deservedly fall away empty from my enemies.

**7:6** Let the enemy pursue my soul and overtake it; and let him trample my life on the ground, and bring my glory down into the dust.

**7:7** Rise up, Lord, in Your anger, and be exalted in the borders of my enemies; and rise up, Lord, my God, in the decree that You have commanded,

**7:8** and an assembly of peoples will surround You; and on account of it, return on high.

**7:9** The Lord judges the peoples. Judge me, Lord, according to my righteousness, and according to my innocence upon me.

**7:10** The wickedness of sinners will be consumed, and You will direct the righteous, searching hearts and kidneys, God.

**7:11** My just help is from the Lord, who saves those upright in heart.

**7:12** God is a righteous judge, strong and patient; does He grow angry every single day?

**7:13** Unless you turn back, He will brandish His sword; He has bent His bow and prepared it.

**7:14** And in it He has prepared implements of death; He has fashioned His arrows for those who burn.

**7:15** Look, he has been in labor with injustice; he has conceived pain and given birth to iniquity.

**7:16** He opened a pit and dug it out, and he fell into the pitfall that he made.

**7:17** His pain will turn back upon his own head, and upon the crown of his own head his iniquity will descend.

**7:18** I will give thanks to the Lord according to His righteousness, and I will sing psalms to the name of the Lord Most High.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate superscription reads "Cushi, son of Jemini" (Chusi, filii Jemini), whereas the Hebrew reads "Cush, a Benjamite."
- **7:5** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate renders this verse as an oath against retaliating against enemies who return evil ("deservedly fall away empty"), whereas the Hebrew reads "if I have repaid evil to one at peace with me—indeed I rescued my adversary without cause."
- **7:10** — Latin "renes" (literally "kidneys") reflects the biblical idiom for the innermost seat of emotions and desires.
- **7:12** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate adds "strong and patient" and frames the second clause as a rhetorical question ("does He grow angry every single day?"), whereas the Hebrew reads "and a God who feels indignation every day."
- **7:14** — Following the Septuagint dative plural, the Vulgate reads "for those who burn" (ardentibus), whereas the Hebrew refers to "fiery arrows."
