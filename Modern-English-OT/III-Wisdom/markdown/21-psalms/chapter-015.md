## Text

**15:1** An inscription of a title, for David himself. Preserve me, Lord, for I have hoped in You.

**15:2** I said to the Lord, "You are my God, because You have no need of my good things."

**15:3** For the holy ones who are in His land, He has made all my desires marvelous in them.

**15:4** Their weaknesses were multiplied; afterward they hurried. I will not assemble their gatherings of blood, nor will I recall their names through my lips.

**15:5** The Lord is the share of my inheritance and of my cup; You are the One who will restore my inheritance to me.

**15:6** The cords have fallen to me in splendid places; for indeed my inheritance is splendid to me.

**15:7** I will bless the Lord, who has granted me understanding; moreover, even until night my kidneys have reproved me.

**15:8** I kept seeing the Lord before my sight always, because He is at my right hand, so that I will not be shaken.

**15:9** For this reason my heart was glad, and my tongue exulted; moreover, even my flesh will rest in hope.

**15:10** For You will not abandon my soul in the underworld, nor will You permit Your Holy One to see corruption.

**15:11** You have made the ways of life known to me; You will fill me with gladness with Your countenance; delights are in Your right hand even to the end.

## Translator Notes

- **15:1** — The superscription "An inscription of a title" (Latin "Tituli inscriptio," from Septuagint "stelographia," an inscription on a monument) renders the obscure Hebrew term "Miktam."
- **15:2** — Latin "quoniam bonorum meorum non eges" ("because You have no need of my good things," following the Septuagint) differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, typically rendered "I have no good apart from You."
- **15:4** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "Their weaknesses were multiplied; afterward they hurried. I will not assemble their gatherings of blood," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to the sorrows of those who hasten after another god and refuses to pour out their drink offerings of blood.
- **15:7** — Latin "renes mei" literally means "my kidneys," viewed in biblical idiom as the seat of conscience and inner reflection, and "increpuerunt" means "reproved" or "rebuked" (where the Hebrew Masoretic Text says "instruct").
- **15:10** — Latin "in inferno" ("in the underworld," traditionally "in hell") denotes the abode of the dead. Following the Septuagint (and cited in Acts 2:27), the Vulgate reads "corruption" ("corruptionem") rather than the Hebrew Masoretic "pit."
- **15:11** — In the Latin text this verse is included in verse 10. The Vulgate reads "even to the end" ("usque in finem") rather than "forever."
