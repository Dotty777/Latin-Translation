## Text

**101:1** A prayer of a poor man, when he is distressed and pours out his petition in the sight of the Lord.

**101:2** Lord, hear my prayer, and let my cry come to You.

**101:3** Do not turn Your face away from me: on whatever day I am afflicted, incline Your ear to me; on whatever day I call upon You, hear me quickly.

**101:4** For my days have vanished like smoke, and my bones have dried up like kindling.

**101:5** I have been struck like grass, and my heart has dried up, because I have forgotten to eat my bread.

**101:6** From the sound of my groaning, my bone has clung to my flesh.

**101:7** I have become like a pelican of the wilderness; I have become like a night owl in a dwelling.

**101:8** I have stayed awake, and I have become like a solitary sparrow on a roof.

**101:9** All day long my enemies reproached me, and those who praised me swore against me:

**101:10** for I ate ashes like bread and mixed my drink with weeping,

**101:11** because of Your wrath and indignation: for, lifting me up, You have dashed me down.

**101:12** My days have declined like a shadow, and I have dried up like grass.

**101:13** But You, Lord, remain forever, and Your remembrance from generation to generation.

**101:14** Rising up, You will have mercy on Zion, for it is time to have mercy on her, for the time has come:

**101:15** since her stones have pleased Your servants, and they will have pity on her earth.

**101:16** And the nations will fear Your name, Lord, and all the kings of the earth Your glory:

**101:17** for the Lord has built Zion, and He will be seen in His glory.

**101:18** He has looked upon the prayer of the humble and has not despised their petition.

**101:19** Let these things be written for another generation, and a people that will be created will praise the Lord.

**101:20** For He has looked forth from His holy height; the Lord has looked from heaven onto the earth:

**101:21** to hear the groans of the shackled; to release the children of those put to death;

**101:22** so that they may proclaim the name of the Lord in Zion, and His praise in Jerusalem,

**101:23** when peoples gather together as one, and kings, to serve the Lord.

**101:24** He answered Him in the way of his strength: "Tell me the fewness of my days;

**101:25** do not call me back in the middle of my days; Your years are from generation to generation.

**101:26** In the beginning You, Lord, founded the earth, and the works of Your hands are the heavens.

**101:27** They will perish, but You remain; and all of them will grow old like a garment. And like a cloak You will change them, and they will be changed;

**101:28** but You are the very same, and Your years will not fail.

**101:29** The children of Your servants will dwell, and their seed will be directed forever."

## Translator Notes

- **101:4** — Latin cremium means dry firewood or kindling (following the Septuagint phrygion), whereas the Hebrew reads "like a hearth."
- **101:7** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate names a "pelican" (pellicano) and a "night-raven" (nycticorax, a nocturnal owl), and reads in domicilio ("in a dwelling") where the Hebrew has "among the ruins."
- **101:9** — The Vulgate (following the Septuagint) reads qui laudabant me ("those who praised me"), whereas the Hebrew reads "those who rage against me" (or "deride me"), reflecting two senses of the Hebrew root halal.
- **101:21** — Literally "the children of those put to death" (filios interemptorum, following the Septuagint's perfect passive participle), where the Hebrew idiom means "those doomed to die."
- **101:24** — A notable Vulgate reading following the Septuagint ("He answered Him in the way of his strength: 'Tell me the fewness of my days'"), whereas the Hebrew reads "He has broken my strength in the way; He has shortened my days." In 101:29 the Vulgate also reads in saeculum dirigetur ("will be directed forever"), where the Hebrew has "will be established before You."
