## Text

**41:1** To the end. Understanding for the sons of Korah.

**41:2** Just as a deer longs for the springs of water, so my soul longs for You, God.

**41:3** My soul has thirsted for the strong, living God; when will I come and appear before the face of God?

**41:4** My tears have been bread for me day and night, while it is said to me daily, "Where is your God?"

**41:5** I remembered these things and poured out my soul within me, for I will pass over into the place of the wonderful tabernacle, all the way to the house of God, with a voice of exultation and thanksgiving, the sound of one feasting.

**41:6** Why are you sorrowful, my soul? And why do you trouble me? Hope in God, for I will still give thanks to Him, the salvation of my face,

**41:7** and my God. Within myself my soul has been troubled; therefore I will be mindful of You from the land of the Jordan and Hermonim, from the small mountain.

**41:8** Abyss calls upon abyss at the voice of Your waterfalls; all Your heights and Your waves have passed over me.

**41:9** By day the Lord has commanded His mercy, and by night His song; with me is a prayer to the God of my life.

**41:10** I will say to God, "You are my protector; why have You forgotten me? And why do I walk about saddened, while the enemy afflicts me?"

**41:11** While my bones are being broken, my enemies who distress me have reproached me, while they say to me day by day, "Where is your God?"

**41:12** Why are you sorrowful, my soul? And why do you trouble me? Hope in God, for I will still give thanks to Him, the salvation of my face, and my God.

## Translator Notes

- **41:1** — Vulgate "To the end. Understanding" (In finem. Intellectus) follows the Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew superscription ("For the choir director. A Maskil").
- **41:5** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate uses the future tense and reads "I will pass over into the place of the wonderful tabernacle" (transibo in locum tabernaculi admirabilis) where the Hebrew has "I would go along with the throng." Latin confessio ("thanksgiving") and confiteor ("give thanks") in the Psalms express open acknowledgment and grateful praise.
- **41:7** — In the Clementine Vulgate, "and my God" opens verse 7 rather than closing verse 6. Hermoniim retains the plural form of Hermon (its peaks or region), and "from the small mountain" (a monte modico) renders Hebrew har Mitz'ar ("Mount Mizar") as a descriptive phrase.
- **41:8** — Latin "all Your heights" (omnia excelsa tua) literally translates Greek meteorismoi ("high swells"), corresponding to Hebrew "breakers."
- **41:9** — The Vulgate uses the perfect tense "has commanded" (mandavit), whereas the Hebrew and Septuagint use the future ("will command").
