## Text

**94:1** Praise of a song for David himself. Come, let us exult to the Lord; let us shout for joy to God our Savior;

**94:2** let us come before His face with thanksgiving, and in psalms let us shout for joy to Him,

**94:3** because the Lord is a great God, and a great King over all gods.

**94:4** For all the ends of the earth are in His hand, and the heights of the mountains belong to Him;

**94:5** since the sea belongs to Him, and He Himself made it, and His hands shaped the dry land.

**94:6** Come, let us worship and fall down, and let us weep before the Lord who made us,

**94:7** for He Himself is the Lord our God, and we are the people of His pasture and the sheep of His hand.

**94:8** Today, if you listen to His voice, do not harden your hearts,

**94:9** as in the provocation, according to the day of testing in the desert, where your fathers tested Me; they put Me to the proof and saw My works.

**94:10** For forty years I was offended by that generation, and I said, "These always go astray in heart."

**94:11** And they have not known My ways; as I swore in My wrath, "They will not enter into My rest."

## Translator Notes

- **94:1** — The superscription derives from the Septuagint (absent in the Hebrew), and 'Deo salutari nostro' ('God our Savior') follows the Septuagint where the Hebrew has 'the rock of our salvation.'
- **94:4** — Latin 'omnes fines terrae' ('all the ends of the earth') follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads 'the depths of the earth.'
- **94:6** — Latin 'ploremus' ('let us weep') follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads 'let us kneel.'
- **94:9** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate renders the Hebrew place names Meribah ('Provocation') and Massah ('Testing') as common nouns ('in irritatione' and 'diem tentationis').
- **94:11** — Latin 'Si introibunt in requiem meam' (lit. 'If they will enter into My rest') preserves the biblical Hebraic oath idiom expressing emphatic negation ('They will not enter into My rest').
