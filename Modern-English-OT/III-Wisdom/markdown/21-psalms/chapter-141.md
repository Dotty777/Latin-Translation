## Text

**141:1** Understanding of David, when he was in the cave; a prayer.

**141:2** With my voice I cried out to the Lord; with my voice I offered supplication to the Lord.

**141:3** I pour out my prayer in His sight, and I declare my affliction before Him,

**141:4** as my spirit fails from me; and You have known my paths. Along this way on which I was walking, they have hidden a snare for me.

**141:5** I was looking closely to the right and watching, and there was no one who recognized me; flight has perished from me, and there is no one who inquires after my soul.

**141:6** I cried out to You, Lord; I said, "You are my hope, my portion in the land of the living."

**141:7** Give attention to my supplication, for I have been brought exceedingly low. Deliver me from those who pursue me, for they have grown strong over me.

**141:8** Lead my soul out of prison to give thanks to Your name; the righteous are waiting for me until You reward me.

## Translator Notes

- **141:1** — Latin 'Intellectus' ("Understanding") renders the Hebrew superscription 'maskil' via the Septuagint.
- **141:4** — Latin 'in deficiendo ... et' preserves the Septuagint and Hebraic construction connecting the failing of the psalmist's spirit with the preceding declaration or with God's knowledge of his paths.
- **141:5** — Latin 'Considerabam ... et videbam' ("I was looking closely ... and watching") uses first-person imperfect verbs following the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew has imperatives ("Look ... and see").
- **141:8** — Latin 'me exspectant ... donec retribuas' ("are waiting for me until You reward me") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "will surround me, for You will deal bountifully with me."
