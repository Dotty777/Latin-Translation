## Text

**76:1** To the end, for Jeduthun. A psalm of Asaph.

**76:2** With my voice I cried out to the Lord; with my voice to God, and He paid attention to me.

**76:3** In the day of my tribulation I sought out God, with my hands by night before Him, and I was not deceived. My soul refused to be comforted;

**76:4** I was mindful of God, and I was delighted, and I was exercised, and my spirit failed.

**76:5** My eyes anticipated the watches; I was troubled, and I did not speak.

**76:6** I thought of the ancient days, and I had the eternal years in mind.

**76:7** And I meditated by night with my heart, and I was exercised, and I swept my spirit.

**76:8** Will God cast off forever? Or will He not go on to be more favorable still?

**76:9** Or will He cut off His mercy to the end, from generation to generation?

**76:10** Or will God forget to have mercy? Or will He hold back His mercies in His wrath?

**76:11** And I said, "Now I have begun; this is the change of the right hand of the Most High."

**76:12** I was mindful of the works of the Lord, for I will be mindful of Your wonders from the beginning;

**76:13** and I will meditate on all Your works, and I will be exercised in Your designs.

**76:14** God, Your way is in the holy place; what god is as great as our God?

**76:15** You are the God who performs wonders; You made Your power known among the peoples.

**76:16** You redeemed Your people with Your arm, the sons of Jacob and Joseph.

**76:17** The waters saw You, God; the waters saw You and feared, and the depths were troubled.

**76:18** There was a multitude of the sound of waters; the clouds gave voice. Indeed, Your arrows pass through;

**76:19** the voice of Your thunder is in the wheel. Your lightning flashes lit up the world; the earth was shaken and trembled.

**76:20** In the sea is Your way, and Your paths are in many waters, and Your footprints will not be known.

**76:21** You led Your people like sheep, by the hand of Moses and Aaron.

## Translator Notes

- **76:3** — Vulgate "with my hands by night before Him, and I was not deceived" follows the Septuagint (with "contra" rendering Greek "enantion," meaning "before/facing"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of the psalmist's hand stretched out at night without growing weary.
- **76:4** — Vulgate "and I was delighted" differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("and I groan"); "I was exercised" (here and in verses 7 and 13) literally renders Latin "exercitor/exerceor" for intense inward reflection or spiritual preoccupation.
- **76:7** — Vulgate "I swept my spirit" literally renders Latin "scopebam" ("swept clean"), a vivid Vulgate image corresponding to the Septuagint's "dug/probed" and the Hebrew's "searched."
- **76:11** — Vulgate "Now I have begun; this is the change of the right hand of the Most High" follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text is commonly read as referring to the psalmist's grief or the years of the right hand of the Most High.
- **76:19** — Vulgate "in the wheel" follows the Septuagint in rendering Hebrew "galgal" literally as "wheel" rather than "whirlwind."
