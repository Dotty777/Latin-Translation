## Text

**51:1** To the end. Understanding of David,

**51:2** when Doeg the Edomite came and reported to Saul, "David has come into the house of Ahimelech."

**51:3** Why do you boast in malice, you who are mighty in iniquity?

**51:4** All day long your tongue has devised unrighteousness; like a sharp razor, you have practiced deceit.

**51:5** You have loved malice above kindness; iniquity rather than speaking uprightness.

**51:6** You have loved all words of downfall, deceitful tongue.

**51:7** Therefore God will destroy you to the end; He will tear you out and remove you from your tent, and your root from the land of the living.

**51:8** The righteous will see and fear; and they will laugh over him and say:

**51:9** "Here is the man who did not make God his helper, but hoped in the abundance of his riches and grew strong in his vanity."

**51:10** But I am like a fruitful olive tree in the house of God; I have hoped in the mercy of God forever, and forever and ever.

**51:11** I will give thanks to You forever, because You have acted; and I will wait for Your name, for it is good in the sight of Your holy ones.

## Translator Notes

- **51:3** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads 'you who are mighty in iniquity' and places 'all day long' at the start of verse 4, omitting the Hebrew clause 'the steadfast love of God endures all the day'.
- **51:6** — Latin 'verba praecipitationis' literally means 'words of headlong downfall' or 'ruin' (reflecting the Septuagint's 'of plunging down/submersion'), whereas the Hebrew has 'devouring words'.
- **51:7** — Latin 'in finem' ('to the end') here carries the sense 'utterly' or 'forever', using the same phrase found in the psalm superscriptions.
- **51:9** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads 'adjutorem' ('helper') and 'in vanitate sua' ('in his vanity'), where the Hebrew has 'his refuge/stronghold' and 'in his destruction/desire'.
