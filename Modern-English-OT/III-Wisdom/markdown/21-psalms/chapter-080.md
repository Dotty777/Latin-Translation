## Text

**80:1** To the end, for the winepresses. A psalm for Asaph himself.

**80:2** Exult in God our helper; shout joyfully to the God of Jacob.

**80:3** Take up a psalm, and bring the tambourine, the pleasant harp with the lyre.

**80:4** Blow the trumpet at the new moon, on the notable day of your solemn feast,

**80:5** for it is a precept in Israel and a judgment for the God of Jacob.

**80:6** He set it as a testimony in Joseph when he went out from the land of Egypt; he heard a language that he did not know.

**80:7** He turned his back away from the burdens; his hands served in the basket.

**80:8** In affliction you called upon Me, and I delivered you. I heard you in the hidden place of the storm; I tested you at the water of contention.

**80:9** Listen, My people, and I will admonish you. Israel, if you will listen to Me,

**80:10** there will be no new god among you, nor will you worship a foreign god.

**80:11** For I am the Lord your God, who led you out of the land of Egypt. Open your mouth wide, and I will fill it.

**80:12** And My people did not listen to My voice, and Israel did not pay attention to Me.

**80:13** And I let them go according to the desires of their heart; they will walk in their own schemes.

**80:14** If My people had listened to Me, if Israel had walked in My ways,

**80:15** perhaps for nothing I would have humbled their enemies, and I would have laid My hand upon those who afflict them.

**80:16** The enemies of the Lord have lied to Him, and their time will be forever.

**80:17** And He fed them from the fat of wheat, and from the rock He satisfied them with honey.

## Translator Notes

- **80:1** — The superscription "pro torcularibus" ("for the winepresses") follows the Septuagint rendering of the Hebrew musical term "Gittith."
- **80:4** — The Latin "in insigni die" ("on the notable day") translates the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "at the full moon."
- **80:7** — The Vulgate "manus ejus in cophino servierunt" ("his hands served in the basket") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "his hands were freed from the basket."
- **80:8** — The Vulgate "aquam contradictionis" ("the water of contention") translates the place name Meribah (compare Exodus 17:7; Numbers 20:13).
- **80:15** — The Latin "pro nihilo forsitan" ("perhaps for nothing") literally renders the Septuagint "en to medeni an" in the sense of "effortlessly" or "in no time," corresponding to Hebrew "soon."
