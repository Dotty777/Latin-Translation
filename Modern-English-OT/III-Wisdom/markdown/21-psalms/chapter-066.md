## Text

**66:1** To the end, in hymns. A psalm of a song of David.

**66:2** May God have mercy on us and bless us; may He make His face shine upon us and have mercy on us,

**66:3** so that we may know Your way on the earth, Your salvation among all nations.

**66:4** May the peoples give thanks to You, God; may all the peoples give thanks to You.

**66:5** May the nations rejoice and exult, for You judge the peoples in equity and direct the nations on the earth.

**66:6** May the peoples give thanks to You, God; may all the peoples give thanks to You.

**66:7** The earth has given its fruit: may God, our God, bless us!

**66:8** May God bless us, and may all the ends of the earth fear Him.

## Translator Notes

- **66:1** — Latin "in hymnis" ("in hymns") follows the Septuagint ("en hymnois") rather than Hebrew "with stringed instruments," and the attribution to David is specific to the Vulgate/Septuagint superscription.
- **66:2** — The repeated clause "et misereatur nostri" ("and have mercy on us") is a Vulgate/Septuagint addition absent from the Hebrew text.
- **66:4** — Latin "confiteantur tibi" (lit. "confess to You") is the standard liturgical Vulgate idiom for publicly praising or giving thanks to God.
