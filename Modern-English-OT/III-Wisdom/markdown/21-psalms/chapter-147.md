## Text

**147:1** Hallelujah. Praise the Lord, Jerusalem; praise your God, Zion.

**147:2** For He has fortified the bars of your gates; He has blessed your children within you.

**147:3** He has made your borders peace and satisfies you with the choicest wheat.

**147:4** He sends out His utterance to the earth; swiftly His word runs.

**147:5** He gives snow like wool; He scatters mist like ash.

**147:6** He casts His ice like morsels; before the face of His cold, who will endure?

**147:7** He will send out His word and melt them; His wind will blow, and the waters will flow.

**147:8** He proclaims His word to Jacob, His ordinances and judgments to Israel.

**147:9** He has not done so for any nation, and He has not revealed His judgments to them. Hallelujah.

## Translator Notes

- **147:1** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate treats Hebrew Psalm 147:12-20 as a separate psalm (Psalm 147) and opens it with "Hallelujah" (Alleluja), which is absent here in the Hebrew text.
- **147:3** — Latin fines tuos pacem uses a double accusative (lit. "your borders peace"), and adipe frumenti is literally "the fat of wheat," a biblical idiom for the richest, finest wheat.
- **147:5** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "mist" (nebulam) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "frost."
- **147:7** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate casts all four verbs in the future tense and makes spiritus ("wind," which can also mean "breath" or "Spirit") the subject of the intransitive verb "will blow" (flabit), rather than the Hebrew causative "He makes His wind blow."
- **147:9** — Latin omni nationi is literally "for every nation," a biblical idiom with non meaning "for any nation." Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "He has not revealed His judgments to them" (judicia sua non manifestavit eis) with God as subject, whereas the Hebrew reads "they have not known them."
