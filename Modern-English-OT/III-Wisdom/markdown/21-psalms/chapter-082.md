## Text

**82:1** A song of a psalm of Asaph.

**82:2** God, who will be like You? Do not keep silent, nor be restrained, God,

**82:3** for look, Your enemies have roared, and those who hate You have lifted their heads.

**82:4** Against Your people they have wickedly devised a plan, and they have plotted against Your holy ones.

**82:5** They said, "Come, and let us destroy them from being a nation, and let the name of Israel not be remembered any longer."

**82:6** For they have plotted with one mind; together they have formed a covenant against You:

**82:7** the tents of the Edomites and the Ishmaelites, Moab and the Hagrites,

**82:8** Gebal, Ammon, and Amalek; the foreigners with those who dwell in Tyre.

**82:9** For indeed, Assyria has come with them; they have become an aid to the sons of Lot.

**82:10** Do to them as to Midian and Sisera, as to Jabin at the Kishon torrent.

**82:11** They perished in Endor; they became like manure for the earth.

**82:12** Make their princes like Oreb, Zeeb, Zebah, and Zalmunna: all their princes,

**82:13** who said, "Let us possess the sanctuary of God by inheritance."

**82:14** My God, make them like a wheel, and like stubble before the face of the wind.

**82:15** Like a fire that burns up a forest, and like a flame burning up mountains,

**82:16** so You will pursue them in Your tempest, and in Your anger You will throw them into turmoil.

**82:17** Fill their faces with disgrace, and they will seek Your name, Lord.

**82:18** May they be ashamed and thrown into turmoil forever and ever, and may they be humiliated and perish.

**82:19** And may they know that Your name is the Lord: You alone are the Most High in all the earth.

## Translator Notes

- **82:2** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "God, who will be like You?" (quis similis erit tibi), whereas the Hebrew reads "O God, do not remain silent."
- **82:4** — The Vulgate reads "Your holy ones" (sanctos tuos), following the Septuagint, where the Hebrew has "Your hidden/treasured ones."
- **82:8** — The Vulgate renders the Septuagint's standard designation for the Philistines literally as "the foreigners" (alienigenae).
- **82:13** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "the sanctuary of God" (sanctuarium Dei), whereas the Hebrew reads "the pastures" or "habitations of God."
- **82:14** — Latin rotam literally means "a wheel," following the Septuagint rendering of Hebrew galgal, which can also denote whirling dust or tumbleweed.
