## Text

**95:1** A song for David himself, when the house was being built after the captivity. Sing a new song to the Lord; sing to the Lord, all the earth.

**95:2** Sing to the Lord, and bless His name; proclaim His salvation from day to day.

**95:3** Proclaim His glory among the nations; among all the peoples, His marvelous deeds.

**95:4** For the Lord is great, and exceedingly praiseworthy: He is fearsome above all gods.

**95:5** For all the gods of the nations are demons; but the Lord made the heavens.

**95:6** Thanksgiving and beauty are in His sight; holiness and magnificence are in His sanctuary.

**95:7** Bring to the Lord, families of the nations, bring to the Lord glory and honor;

**95:8** bring to the Lord glory for His name. Take up sacrifices, and enter into His courts;

**95:9** worship the Lord in His holy court. Let the whole earth be shaken before His face;

**95:10** say among the nations that the Lord has reigned. For indeed He has set right the world, which will not be shaken; He will judge the peoples in equity.

**95:11** Let the heavens be glad, and let the earth exult; let the sea and its fullness be shaken;

**95:12** the fields will rejoice, and all things that are in them. Then all the trees of the forests will exult

**95:13** before the face of the Lord, because He is coming, for He is coming to judge the earth. He will judge the world in equity, and the peoples in His truth.

## Translator Notes

- **95:1** — The Vulgate and Septuagint include this superscription referring to the post-exilic rebuilding of the Temple ("the house"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text (Psalm 96) has no superscription.
- **95:5** — Following the Septuagint ("daimonia"), the Vulgate reads "dæmonia" ("demons"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "idols" or "worthless things."
- **95:6** — The Vulgate follows the Septuagint in reading "confessio et pulchritudo" ("thanksgiving/praise and beauty") and "sanctimonia et magnificentia in sanctificatione ejus" ("holiness and magnificence in His sanctuary"), where the Hebrew has "splendor and majesty" and "strength and beauty in His sanctuary."
- **95:9** — Following the Septuagint ("en aule hagia autou"), the Vulgate reads "in atrio sancto ejus" ("in His holy court"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "in holy splendor" (or "holy attire").
- **95:10** — Latin "correxit" (literally "set straight" or "set right," rendering Greek "katōrthōsen") corresponds to "is established" in Hebrew; similarly in v. 11, "commoveatur" ("be shaken") reflects the Septuagint verb rather than the Hebrew "roar."
