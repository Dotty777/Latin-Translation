## Text

**24:1** For the end. A psalm of David. To You, Lord, I have lifted up my soul.

**24:2** My God, in You I trust; may I not be ashamed.

**24:3** Nor let my enemies deride me; for indeed, all who wait for You will not be put to shame.

**24:4** Let all who act wickedly without cause be put to shame. Show me Your ways, Lord, and instruct me in Your paths.

**24:5** Guide me in Your truth and teach me, for You are God my Savior, and for You I have waited all day long.

**24:6** Remember Your acts of compassion, Lord, and Your mercies that are from eternity.

**24:7** Do not remember the offenses of my youth and my acts of ignorance. According to Your mercy, You Yourself remember me, on account of Your goodness, Lord.

**24:8** Sweet and upright is the Lord; for this reason He will give a law to those who offend in the way.

**24:9** He will guide the gentle in judgment; He will teach the meek His ways.

**24:10** All the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth for those who seek His covenant and His testimonies.

**24:11** On account of Your name, Lord, You will pardon my sin, for it is great.

**24:12** Who is the man who fears the Lord? He has established a law for him in the way that he has chosen.

**24:13** His soul will dwell in good things, and his offspring will inherit the earth.

**24:14** The Lord is a firm support for those who fear Him, and His covenant is to be revealed to them.

**24:15** My eyes are always toward the Lord, for He Himself will pull my feet out of the snare.

**24:16** Look upon me and have mercy on me, for I am solitary and poor.

**24:17** The distresses of my heart have multiplied; rescue me from my hardships.

**24:18** See my lowliness and my toil, and forgive all my offenses.

**24:19** Look upon my enemies, for they have multiplied, and they hate me with an unjust hatred.

**24:20** Guard my soul and rescue me; may I not be ashamed, for I have hoped in You.

**24:21** The innocent and the upright have clung to me, because I have waited for You.

**24:22** Deliver Israel, God, from all its distresses.

## Translator Notes

- **24:7** — Ignorantias meas ("my acts of ignorance") follows the Septuagint reading, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "my transgressions."
- **24:8** — Dulcis ("Sweet") is a distinctive Vulgate rendering of Greek chrestos (Hebrew "good"), and legem dabit ("will give a law," cf. v. 12 legem statuit) follows the Septuagint where the Hebrew means "instructs."
- **24:14** — Firmamentum ("a firm support" or "stronghold") translates the Septuagint krataioma, whereas the Hebrew text reads "secret counsel" or "friendship."
- **24:21** — Innocentes et recti adhaeserunt mihi ("The innocent and the upright have clung to me") translates personal plurals following the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew has abstract nouns: "May integrity and uprightness preserve me."
