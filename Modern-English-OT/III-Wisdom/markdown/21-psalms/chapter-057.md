## Text

**57:1** To the end, do not destroy. Of David, for the inscription of a title.

**57:2** If indeed you truly speak justice, judge what is right, sons of men.

**57:3** For indeed in your heart you work iniquities; on the earth your hands contrive injustices.

**57:4** Sinners have been estranged from the womb; they have gone astray from the belly; they have spoken falsehoods.

**57:5** Their fury is according to the likeness of a serpent, like that of a deaf asp stopping up its ears,

**57:6** which will not hear the voice of charmers and of the sorcerer charming wisely.

**57:7** God will crush their teeth in their own mouth; the Lord will shatter the grinding teeth of the lions.

**57:8** They will come to nothing like water flowing away; He bends His bow until they are weakened.

**57:9** Like wax that melts, they will be taken away; fire has fallen upon them, and they have not seen the sun.

**57:10** Before your thorns could understand the bramble, as though alive, so in wrath He swallows them up.

**57:11** The righteous one will rejoice when he sees vengeance; he will wash his hands in the blood of the sinner.

**57:12** And man will say, "If indeed there is fruit for the righteous one, indeed there is a God judging them on the earth."

## Translator Notes

- **57:2** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate casts this verse as a conditional clause with an imperative ('If indeed you truly speak justice, judge what is right'), whereas the Masoretic Hebrew asks a question.
- **57:5** — Vulgate Furor ('fury') follows the Septuagint rendering of a Hebrew term that can mean either 'wrath/heat' or 'venom.'
- **57:9** — Vulgate 'Like wax that melts... fire has fallen upon them' (Sicut cera quæ fluit... supercecidit ignis) follows the Septuagint, whereas the Masoretic Hebrew refers to a dissolving snail and a woman's miscarriage.
- **57:10** — In this difficult verse, the Vulgate follows the Septuagint in reading 'Before your thorns could understand the bramble' (spinæ vestræ rhamnum, vs. Hebrew 'pots' and 'thorns') and concludes with the present tense 'as though alive, so in wrath He swallows them up' (sicut viventes sic in ira absorbet eos).
- **57:11** — Vulgate manus suas ('his hands', following the Septuagint) differs from the Masoretic Hebrew 'his feet.'
