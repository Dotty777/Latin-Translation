## Text

**27:1** A psalm for David himself. To You, Lord, I will cry out; my God, do not be silent toward me; otherwise, if You ever keep quiet toward me, I will become like those descending into the pit.

**27:2** Hear the voice of my supplication, Lord, while I pray to You, while I lift up my hands toward Your holy temple.

**27:3** Do not drag me along with sinners, and do not destroy me with those who work iniquity, who speak peace with their neighbor, yet evil things are in their hearts.

**27:4** Give to them according to their works and according to the wickedness of their schemes. According to the works of their hands render to them; pay back their retribution to them.

**27:5** Because they have not understood the works of the Lord and the works of His hands, You will destroy them, and You will not build them up.

**27:6** Blessed is the Lord, because He has heard the voice of my supplication.

**27:7** The Lord is my helper and my protector; in Him my heart has hoped, and I have been helped; and my flesh has bloomed again, and of my own will I will give thanks to Him.

**27:8** The Lord is the strength of His people and the protector of the salvations of His anointed one.

**27:9** Save Your people, Lord, and bless Your inheritance; and rule them, and lift them up forever.

## Translator Notes

- **27:1** — Vulgate Deus meus ("my God") and the doubled silence idiom ne sileas ... nequando taceas follow the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "my rock, do not be deaf to me, lest you be silent" (Psalm 28 in Hebrew numbering).
- **27:5** — Latin et in opera manuum ejus retains the preposition in from the Greek idiom for "understand/consider," and destrues ... non aedificabis addresses the Lord in the second person ("You will destroy ... will not build up"), unlike the Hebrew third person.
- **27:7** — Vulgate refloruit caro mea ("my flesh has bloomed again") and ex voluntate mea ("of my own will") follow the Septuagint against the Hebrew ("my heart exults, and with my song"); confitebor with the dative means to praise or give thanks.
- **27:8** — Vulgate plebis suae ("of His people") follows the Septuagint rather than the Hebrew "to them"; salvationum is plural ("salvations"), and christi sui literally means "of His anointed one."
- **27:9** — Vulgate rege literally means "rule" or "govern," whereas the Hebrew and Septuagint use the verb "shepherd."
