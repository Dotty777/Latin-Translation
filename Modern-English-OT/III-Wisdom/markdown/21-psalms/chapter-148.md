## Text

**148:1** Hallelujah. Praise the Lord from the heavens; praise Him in the heights.

**148:2** Praise Him, all His angels; praise Him, all His powers.

**148:3** Praise Him, sun and moon; praise Him, all stars and light.

**148:4** Praise Him, heavens of heavens; and all the waters that are above the heavens,

**148:5** let them praise the name of the Lord. For He Himself spoke, and they came into being; He Himself commanded, and they were created.

**148:6** He established them forever, and forever and ever; He set a decree, and it will not pass away.

**148:7** Praise the Lord from the earth, dragons and all abysses;

**148:8** fire, hail, snow, ice, wind of storms, which carry out His word;

**148:9** mountains and all hills; fruit-bearing trees and all cedars;

**148:10** wild beasts and all livestock; reptiles and winged birds;

**148:11** kings of the earth and all peoples; rulers and all judges of the earth;

**148:12** young men and virgins; the elderly with the younger, let them praise the name of the Lord,

**148:13** for His name alone has been exalted.

**148:14** Thanksgiving to Him is above heaven and earth; and He has exalted the horn of His people. A hymn for all His holy ones, the sons of Israel, a people drawing near to Him. Hallelujah.

## Translator Notes

- **148:2** — Latin virtutes ("powers," rendering Septuagint dynameis for Hebrew "hosts") denotes the heavenly hosts or angelic powers.
- **148:3** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads coordinate "all stars and light" (omnes stellae et lumen) rather than the Hebrew construct "all stars of light" (shining stars).
- **148:5** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate includes "For He Himself spoke, and they came into being" (Quia ipse dixit, et facta sunt; cf. Ps 32:9 Vulgate / 33:9 Hebrew), a clause absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **148:8** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "ice" (glacies) where the Hebrew has "mist" or "smoke," and uses neuter plural quae faciunt ("which carry out"), referring to all the preceding elements rather than only the wind.
- **148:14** — Vulgate Confessio ejus (lit. "confession/thanksgiving of Him," following Septuagint exomologesis) corresponds to Hebrew "His majesty/splendor," and begins verse 14 here rather than concluding verse 13 as in the Hebrew numbering.
