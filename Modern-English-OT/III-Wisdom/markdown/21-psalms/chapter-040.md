## Text

**40:1** For the end. A psalm for David himself.

**40:2** Blessed is the one who has understanding for the needy and the poor: in the evil day the Lord will deliver him.

**40:3** May the Lord preserve him, and give him life, and make him blessed on the earth, and not hand him over to the soul of his enemies.

**40:4** May the Lord bring help to him on his bed of pain; You have turned all his bedding in his sickness.

**40:5** I said, "Lord, have mercy on me; heal my soul, because I have sinned against You."

**40:6** My enemies spoke evil against me, "When will he die, and his name perish?"

**40:7** And if he came in to see, he spoke empty things; his heart gathered iniquity to itself. He went outside and spoke.

**40:8** Together, all my enemies were whispering against me; against me they were devising evil for me.

**40:9** They decreed an unjust word against me: "Will the one who sleeps rise up no more?"

**40:10** For even the man of my peace, in whom I trusted, who ate my bread, has magnified treachery against me.

**40:11** But You, Lord, have mercy on me, and raise me up; and I will repay them.

**40:12** By this I know that You have favored me, because my enemy will not rejoice over me.

**40:13** But You have upheld me because of innocence, and You have established me in Your sight forever.

**40:14** Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, from eternity and even to eternity. Let it be so, let it be so.

## Translator Notes

- **40:3** — "To the soul" (in animam) follows the Hebrew ("benephesh," meaning desire/will) rather than the standard Septuagint reading "into the hands," while the four verbs here (and "ferat" in 40:4) are jussive subjunctives. In 40:2, "egenum et pauperem" ("the needy and the poor") uses a double expression matching the Septuagint.
- **40:9** — "An unjust word" (Verbum iniquum) and the rhetorical question "Will the one who sleeps rise up no more?" (Numquid qui dormit non adjiciet ut resurgat?) follow the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "a deadly thing is poured out on him; now that he lies down, he will not rise again."
- **40:10** — "Has magnified treachery against me" translates "magnificavit super me supplantationem," where "supplantatio" literally means "tripping by the heel" or "supplanting" (rendering Greek "pternismon," cf. Hebrew "has lifted up his heel against me").
- **40:14** — "Let it be so, let it be so" translates Latin "Fiat, fiat," which renders the Septuagint's "genoito, genoito" rather than transliterating the Hebrew "Amen, amen."
