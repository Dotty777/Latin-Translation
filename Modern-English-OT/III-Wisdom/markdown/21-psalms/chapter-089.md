## Text

**89:1** A prayer of Moses, the man of God. Lord, You have become a refuge for us from generation to generation.

**89:2** Before the mountains came into being, or the earth and the world were formed, from eternity even to eternity You are, God.

**89:3** Do not turn man away into lowliness; and You said, "Turn back, sons of men."

**89:4** For a thousand years before Your eyes are like yesterday that has passed by, and a watch in the night;

**89:5** things that are counted as nothing will be their years.

**89:6** In the morning he may pass away like grass; in the morning he may flourish and pass away; in the evening he may fall, grow hard, and dry up.

**89:7** Because we have faded away in Your wrath, and in Your fury we have been troubled.

**89:8** You have set our iniquities in Your sight, our age in the illumination of Your face.

**89:9** For all our days have faded away, and in Your wrath we have faded away. Our years will meditate like a spider;

**89:10** the days of our years in them are seventy years. But if in strength, eighty years, and the greater part of them is toil and pain; for gentleness has arrived, and we will be corrected.

**89:11** Who knows the power of Your wrath, and for fear of You

**89:12** how to count Your wrath? Make Your right hand known in this way, and those instructed in heart in wisdom.

**89:13** Turn back, Lord; how long? And be moved by prayer toward Your servants.

**89:14** We have been filled in the morning with Your mercy, and we have exulted and been delighted all our days.

**89:15** We have been glad for the days in which You humbled us, the years in which we saw evils.

**89:16** Look upon Your servants and upon Your works, and direct their sons.

**89:17** And may the splendor of the Lord our God be upon us, and direct the works of our hands upon us, and direct the work of our hands.

## Translator Notes

- **89:5** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate reads "things that are counted as nothing will be their years," whereas the Masoretic Text reads "You sweep them away as with a flood; they are like a dream."
- **89:8** — Latin "sæculum nostrum" ("our age" or "our lifetime") follows the Septuagint reading of the Hebrew consonants, whereas the Masoretic Text reads "our secret sins."
- **89:9** — Latin "sicut aranea meditabuntur" ("will meditate like a spider") follows the Septuagint's rendering of the Hebrew root for "murmur/meditate" (MT: "we finish our years like a sigh"), evoking the fragile labor of a spider.
- **89:10** — Latin "supervenit mansuetudo, et corripiemur" ("gentleness has arrived, and we will be corrected") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Masoretic Text reads "it soon passes, and we fly away."
- **89:12** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate attaches "dinumerare" ("to count") to the question of v. 11 and reads "Dexteram tuam" ("Your right hand"), whereas the Masoretic Text reads "teach us to number our days."
