## Text

**142:1** A psalm of David, when his son Absalom was pursuing him. Lord, hear my prayer; give ear to my earnest plea in Your truth; hear me in Your righteousness.

**142:2** And do not enter into judgment with Your servant, because no one living will be justified in Your sight.

**142:3** For the enemy has pursued my soul; he has brought my life low to the ground; he has placed me in dark places, like the dead of old.

**142:4** And my spirit has been filled with anguish over me; within me my heart has been troubled.

**142:5** I was mindful of the days of old; I meditated on all Your works; I kept meditating on the deeds of Your hands.

**142:6** I stretched out my hands to You; my soul is like land without water for You.

**142:7** Swiftly hear me, Lord; my spirit has failed. Do not turn Your face away from me, or I will be like those who descend into the pit.

**142:8** Make Your mercy heard to me in the morning, for I have hoped in You. Make known to me the way in which I should walk, for to You I have lifted up my soul.

**142:9** Rescue me from my enemies, Lord; to You I have fled for refuge.

**142:10** Teach me to do Your will, because You are my God. Your good Spirit will lead me into a level land.

**142:11** For the sake of Your name, Lord, You will give me life; in Your equity You will lead my soul out of affliction,

**142:12** and in Your mercy You will wipe out my enemies, and You will destroy all who afflict my soul, because I am Your servant.

## Translator Notes

- **142:1** — The historical notice "when his son Absalom was pursuing him" is present in the Vulgate and Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads only "A Psalm of David."
- **142:2** — Latin 'justificabitur' ("will be justified") follows the Septuagint future passive verb, whereas the Hebrew reads "is righteous."
- **142:9** — Latin 'ad te confugi' ("to You I have fled for refuge") follows the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "to You I have hidden [myself]."
- **142:10** — Latin 'deducet' ("will lead") is future indicative (as are the verbs in verses 11-12), following the Septuagint rather than Hebrew jussives or imperatives; 'terram rectam' ("a level land") also carries the moral sense "upright."
