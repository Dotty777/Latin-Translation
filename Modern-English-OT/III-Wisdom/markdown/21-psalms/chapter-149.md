## Text

**149:1** Hallelujah. Sing to the Lord a new song; His praise is in the assembly of the holy ones.

**149:2** Let Israel be glad in Him who made him, and let the sons of Zion exult in their King.

**149:3** Let them praise His name with dancing; with tambourine and harp let them sing praise to Him.

**149:4** For the Lord finds pleasure in His people, and He will exalt the meek to salvation.

**149:5** The holy ones will exult in glory; they will be glad on their beds.

**149:6** The exaltations of God are in their throats, and double-edged swords are in their hands:

**149:7** to carry out vengeance on the nations, rebukes on the peoples;

**149:8** to bind their kings in fetters, and their nobles in iron manacles;

**149:9** so that they may carry out on them the written judgment: this is glory for all His holy ones. Hallelujah.

## Translator Notes

- **149:1** — Latin ecclesia sanctorum literally means "assembly of the holy ones," where Vulgate sancti renders Greek hosioi (Hebrew chasidim, "godly/faithful ones").
- **149:4** — Vulgate exaltabit ... in salutem ("will exalt ... to salvation") follows the Septuagint verb ("exalt") with an accusative of goal ("to salvation"), whereas the Hebrew reads "adorns the afflicted with salvation."
- **149:5** — Vulgate Exsultabunt ... laetabuntur uses future indicative verbs ("will exult ... will be glad"), following the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew uses jussives ("let them exult ...").
- **149:7** — Latin increpationes literally means "rebukes" or "reproofs" (following Septuagint elegmous), where the Hebrew word can also mean "punishments" or "chastisements."
