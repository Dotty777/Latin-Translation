# Psalms 110 — Saints Modern English (Latin / Clementine Vulgate)

## Text

**110:1** Hallelujah. I will give thanks to You, Lord, with my whole heart, in the council of the righteous and the congregation.

**110:2** Great are the works of the Lord, sought out for all His purposes.

**110:3** Praise and magnificence are His work, and His righteousness remains forever and ever.

**110:4** He made a memorial of His wonders; the Lord is merciful and compassionate.

**110:5** He gave food to those who fear Him; He will be mindful of His covenant forever.

**110:6** He will declare the power of His works to His people,

**110:7** so that He may give them the inheritance of the nations. The works of His hands are truth and judgment.

**110:8** Faithful are all His commandments, established forever and ever, done in truth and equity.

**110:9** He sent redemption to His people; He commanded His covenant for eternity. Holy and awe-inspiring is His name.

**110:10** The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord; good understanding belongs to all who practice it; His praise remains forever and ever.

## Translator Notes

- **110:2** — Vulgate 'exquisita in omnes voluntates ejus' ('sought out for all His purposes,' literally 'wills,' following the LXX) differs from the Hebrew ('studied by all who delight in them').
- **110:3** — Vulgate 'Confessio' renders Greek exomologesis ('praise,' 'thanksgiving,' or 'confession'), corresponding to Hebrew hod ('splendor').
- **110:6** — The Clementine Vulgate uses the future tense 'annuntiabit' ('He will declare'), differing from both the Hebrew perfect and the LXX aorist ('He has declared').
- **110:10** — Vulgate masculine singular 'eum' ('it') refers grammatically to 'timor Domini' ('the fear of the Lord'), whereas the Hebrew has a plural pronoun referring to His precepts and the LXX has a feminine singular referring to wisdom.
