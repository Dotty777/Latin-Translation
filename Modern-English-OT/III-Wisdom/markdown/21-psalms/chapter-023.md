## Text

**23:1** On the first day of the week. A psalm of David. To the Lord belong the earth and its fullness, the world and all who dwell in it.

**23:2** For He Himself founded it upon the seas and prepared it upon the rivers.

**23:3** Who will ascend to the mountain of the Lord? Or who will stand in His holy place?

**23:4** One innocent in hands and pure in heart, who has not received his soul in vain, nor sworn with deceit to his neighbor:

**23:5** this one will receive a blessing from the Lord, and mercy from God his Savior.

**23:6** This is the generation of those who seek Him, of those who seek the face of the God of Jacob.

**23:7** Lift up your gates, princes, and be raised up, eternal gates, and the King of glory will enter.

**23:8** Who is this King of glory? The Lord, strong and mighty, the Lord, mighty in battle.

**23:9** Lift up your gates, princes, and be raised up, eternal gates, and the King of glory will enter.

**23:10** Who is this King of glory? The Lord of hosts, He Himself is the King of glory.

## Translator Notes

- **23:1** — Prima sabbati ("On the first day of the week") is a liturgical superscription inherited from the Septuagint and absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **23:4** — Accepit in vano animam suam ("received his soul in vain") and proximo suo ("to his neighbor") follow the Vulgate and Septuagint text, whereas the Hebrew reads "lifted up his soul to what is false" and omits "to his neighbor."
- **23:7** — Attollite portas, principes, vestras ("Lift up your gates, princes") preserves the Vulgate and Septuagint reading (repeated in v. 9), whereas the Hebrew text reads "Lift up your heads, O gates." In v. 10, Dominus virtutum literally means "the Lord of powers" or "hosts."
