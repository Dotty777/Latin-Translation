## Text

**18:6** Now when David was returning after the Philistine had been struck down, the women came out from all the cities of Israel, singing and leading dances to meet King Saul, with tambourines of joy and with sistrums.

**18:7** And the women led the song, playing and saying, "Saul has struck down a thousand, and David ten thousand."

**18:8** Then Saul became exceedingly angry, and this saying was displeasing in his eyes; and he said, "They have given David ten thousand, and to me they have given a thousand; what remains for him, except the kingdom alone?"

**18:9** Therefore Saul did not look at David with favorable eyes from that day onward.

**18:12** And Saul feared David, because the Lord was with him and had withdrawn from Saul.

**18:13** Therefore Saul moved him away from himself and made him a tribune over a thousand men; and he went out and entered in the sight of the people.

**18:14** Also in all his ways David acted prudently, and the Lord was with him.

**18:15** And so Saul saw that he was exceedingly prudent, and he began to be wary of him.

**18:16** But all Israel and Judah loved David, for he himself went in and went out before them.

**18:20** Now Michal, the other daughter of Saul, loved David. And it was reported to Saul, and it pleased him.

**18:21** And Saul said, "I will give her to him, so that she may become a stumbling block to him, and that the hand of the Philistines may be upon him." And Saul said to David, "In two things you will be my son-in-law today."

**18:22** And Saul commanded his servants, "Speak to David secretly from me, saying, 'Look, you please the king, and all his servants love you; now therefore, be the son-in-law of the king.'"

**18:23** And the servants of Saul spoke all these words in the ears of David. And David said, "Does it seem a small thing to you to be the son-in-law of the king? Yet I am a poor and lowly man."

**18:24** And the servants of Saul reported back, saying, "David spoke words of this kind."

**18:25** Then Saul said, "Speak to David like this: 'The king has no need of betrothal gifts, except only a hundred foreskins of the Philistines, so that vengeance may be taken on the enemies of the king.'" However, Saul was planning to give David over into the hands of the Philistines.

**18:26** And when his servants had reported back to David the words that Saul had spoken, the saying was pleasing in the eyes of David, that he should become the son-in-law of the king.

**18:27** And after a few days, rising up, David departed with the men who were under him. And he struck down two hundred men from the Philistines, and brought their foreskins and counted them out to the king, so that he might be his son-in-law. And so Saul gave him his daughter Michal as a wife.

**18:28** And Saul saw and understood that the Lord was with David. And Michal, the daughter of Saul, loved him.

**18:29** And Saul began to fear David even more; and Saul became an enemy of David for all days.

## Translator Notes

- **18:6** — Vulgate reads "tambourines of joy" (tympanis lætitiæ) with a genitive modifier and renders the Hebrew musical instruments (shalishim) as sistris ("sistrums," metallic rattle instruments).
- **18:9** — Literally "not with straight eyes" (non rectis oculis), a Latin idiom expressing suspicion, hostility, or looking askance.
- **18:13** — Vulgate uses the Roman military title tribunum ("tribune") for a commander over a thousand men.
- **18:21** — Vulgate "In two things" (In duabus rebus) literally renders the Hebrew expression often understood as "a second time" (referring to a second offer of Saul's daughters). In 18:27 Vulgate likewise begins with "And after a few days" (Et post paucos dies), where the Hebrew closes 18:26 with "before the time had expired."
- **18:10 *(additional)*** — The Vulgate uses "prophetabat" ("was prophesying") here rather than a verb meaning "raved."
- **18:30 *(additional)*** — Literally rendering the Vulgate's "A principio autem egressionis eorum" ("from the beginning of their going out").
