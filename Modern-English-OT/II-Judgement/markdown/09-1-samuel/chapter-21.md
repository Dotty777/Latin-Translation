## Text

**21:1** And David rose and departed, and Jonathan also entered the city.

**21:2** Now David came to Nob, to Ahimelech the priest, and Ahimelech was astonished because David had come. And he said to him, "Why are you alone, and no one is with you?"

**21:3** And David said to Ahimelech the priest, "The king has charged me with a matter and said, 'Let no one know the matter for which you have been sent by me, and what kind of commands I have given you.' For I have also appointed the young men to meet at such and such a place.

**21:4** Now therefore, if you have anything at hand, at least five loaves of bread, give them to me, or whatever you find."

**21:5** And the priest answered David and said to him, "I do not have common loaves at hand, but only holy bread—if the young men are clean, especially from women?"

**21:6** And David answered the priest and said to him, "Indeed, if it is a question of women, we have abstained since yesterday and the day before yesterday when we set out, and the vessels of the young men were holy. Moreover, this journey is defiled, yet even it will be sanctified today in the vessels."

**21:7** The priest therefore gave him sanctified bread; for there was no bread there except the loaves of presentation alone, which had been removed from the presence of the Lord so that warm loaves might be set out.

**21:8** Now a certain man from among the servants of Saul was there on that day, inside the tabernacle of the Lord; and his name was Doeg the Edomite, the mightiest of the shepherds of Saul.

**21:9** And David said to Ahimelech, "Do you have a spear or a sword here at hand? For I did not bring my sword and my weapons with me, since the king's matter was urgent."

**21:10** And the priest said, "Here is the sword of Goliath the Philistine, whom you struck down in the Valley of the Terebinth; it is wrapped in a cloak behind the ephod. If you wish to take that one, take it, for there is no other here besides it." And David said, "There is no other like this one; give it to me."

**21:11** David therefore rose and fled on that day from the presence of Saul, and came to Achish, king of Gath.

**21:12** And the servants of Achish said to him when they had seen David, "Is this not David, the king of the land? Did they not sing of him in dances, saying, 'Saul struck down a thousand, and David ten thousand'?"

**21:13** Now David placed these words in his heart, and he was greatly afraid in the presence of Achish, king of Gath.

**21:14** And he changed his face before them and kept collapsing between their hands, and he kept striking against the doors of the gate, and his saliva flowed down onto his beard.

**21:15** And Achish said to his servants, "You have seen an insane man; why have you brought him to me?

**21:16** Do we lack raving madmen, that you have brought this one in to rave in my presence? Will this man enter my house?"

## Translator Notes

- **21:1** — The Vulgate numbers this verse 20:43 and the remaining verses of chapter 21 one lower than here.
- **21:6** — The Vulgate renders this difficult clause distinctly as "this journey is defiled, yet even it will be sanctified today in the vessels," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text is commonly understood as "even on an ordinary journey, how much more today will their vessels be holy."
- **21:8** — The Vulgate reads "inside the tabernacle of the Lord" (intus in tabernaculo Domini), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has "detained before the Lord."
- **21:10** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew place name Elah literally as "the Valley of the Terebinth" (Valle terebinthi).
- **21:14** — The Vulgate reads "kept collapsing between their hands" (collabebatur inter manus eorum) and "kept striking against the doors of the gate" (impingebat in ostia portae), whereas the Hebrew describes David acting madly in their hands and making marks on the doors.
