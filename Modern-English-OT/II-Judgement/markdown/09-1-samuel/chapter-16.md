## Text

**16:1** And the Lord said to Samuel, "How long will you mourn for Saul, since I have cast him aside from reigning over Israel? Fill your horn with oil and come, so that I may send you to Jesse the Bethlehemite; for I have provided a king for Myself among his sons."

**16:2** And Samuel said, "How can I go? For Saul will hear, and he will kill me." And the Lord said, "You will take a calf from the herd in your hand, and you will say, 'I have come to sacrifice to the Lord.'

**16:3** And you will call Jesse to the sacrifice, and I will show you what you should do, and you will anoint whomever I point out to you."

**16:4** Therefore Samuel did just as the Lord had spoken to him. And he came to Bethlehem, and the elders of the city were astonished as they came to meet him, and they said, "Is your coming peaceful?"

**16:5** And he said, "Peaceful; I have come to sacrifice to the Lord. Sanctify yourselves, and come with me so that I may sacrifice." So he sanctified Jesse and his sons, and called them to the sacrifice.

**16:6** And when they had entered, he saw Eliab and said, "Is His anointed before the Lord?"

**16:7** And the Lord said to Samuel, "Do not regard his face or the height of his stature, because I have rejected him, nor do I judge according to the sight of man; for man sees the things that appear, but the Lord looks closely at the heart."

**16:8** And Jesse called Abinadab and brought him before Samuel. And he said, "Neither has the Lord chosen this one."

**16:9** Then Jesse brought Shammah, of whom he said, "The Lord has not chosen this one either."

**16:10** So Jesse brought his seven sons before Samuel, and Samuel said to Jesse, "The Lord has not chosen from among these."

**16:11** And Samuel said to Jesse, "Are all the sons already here?" And he answered, "The youngest is still left, and he is tending the sheep." And Samuel said to Jesse, "Send and bring him, for we will not recline at table before he comes here."

**16:12** So he sent and brought him. Now he was ruddy, handsome in appearance, and attractive of face; and the Lord said, "Arise, anoint him, for he is the one."

**16:13** Therefore Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the midst of his brothers, and the Spirit of the Lord was directed upon David from that day onward. And Samuel rose and went away to Ramah.

**16:14** Now the Spirit of the Lord departed from Saul, and a wicked spirit from the Lord was tormenting him.

**16:15** And the servants of Saul said to him, "Look, an evil spirit from God is tormenting you.

**16:16** Let our lord command, and your servants who are before you will seek a man who knows how to play the lyre, so that when the evil spirit from the Lord seizes you, he may play with his hand, and you may bear it more easily."

**16:17** And Saul said to his servants, "Provide for me, then, someone who plays well, and bring him to me."

**16:18** And one of the young men answered and said, "Look, I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite who knows how to play, very strong in might, a warlike man, prudent in words, and a handsome man; and the Lord is with him."

**16:19** Therefore Saul sent messengers to Jesse, saying, "Send to me David your son, who is in the pastures."

**16:20** So Jesse took a donkey loaded with loaves of bread, and a flask of wine, and one kid from the goats, and sent them to Saul by the hand of David his son.

**16:21** And David came to Saul and stood before him; and he loved him greatly, and he became his armor-bearer.

**16:22** And Saul sent to Jesse, saying, "Let David stand in my presence, for he has found favor in my eyes."

**16:23** Therefore whenever the evil spirit from the Lord seized Saul, David took the lyre and struck it with his hand, and Saul was refreshed and felt better, for the evil spirit departed from him.

## Translator Notes

- **16:4** — Vulgate reads "were astonished" (admirati sunt) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "came trembling."
- **16:6** — Vulgate casts Samuel's exclamation as a question ("Is His anointed before the Lord?", Num...?) rather than the affirmative "Surely" of the Hebrew Masoretic Text; "anointed" renders Latin christus.
- **16:13** — Vulgate reads "was directed upon David" (directus est... in David), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text expresses the Spirit rushing mightily upon him.
- **16:23** — Vulgate specifies "the evil spirit from [lit. of] the Lord" (spiritus Domini malus; similarly in 16:16), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads simply "the spirit of God."
