## Text

**9:1** And there was a man of Benjamin named Kish, the son of Abiel, the son of Zeror, the son of Becorath, the son of Aphiah, the son of a man of Jemini, mighty in strength.

**9:2** And he had a son called Saul, choice and good, and there was no man among the sons of Israel better than he; from the shoulder and upward he towered above all the people.

**9:3** Now the female donkeys of Kish, Saul's father, had been lost; and Kish said to his son Saul, "Take one of the servants with you, and get up, go, and search for the female donkeys." And when they had passed through Mount Ephraim,

**9:4** and through the land of Shalishah, and had not found them, they passed also through the land of Shaalim, and they were not there; and also through the land of Jemini, and they did not find them at all.

**9:5** Now when they had come into the land of Zuph, Saul said to the servant who was with him, "Come and let us return, for fear that my father may have given up the female donkeys and be anxious about us."

**9:6** And he said to him, "Look, there is a man of God in this city, a distinguished man; everything that he speaks comes about without uncertainty. Now therefore let us go there, in case perhaps he may tell us about our way, on account of which we have come."

**9:7** And Saul said to his servant, "Look, we will go; what shall we bring to the man of God? The bread has run out in our provision bags, and we have no gift to give to the man of God, nor anything else."

**9:8** Again the servant answered Saul and said, "Look, a fourth part of a stater of silver is found in my hand; let us give it to the man of God, so that he may show us our way."

**9:9** (Formerly in Israel, everyone going to consult God used to speak like this: "Come, and let us go to the seer." For the one who is called a prophet today was formerly called a seer.)

**9:10** And Saul said to his servant, "Your word is very good. Come, let us go." And they went to the city in which the man of God was.

**9:11** And as they were going up the slope of the city, they found young women coming out to draw water, and they said to them, "Is the seer here?"

**9:12** And answering, they said to them, "He is here; look, he is ahead of you. Hurry now, for today he has come into the city, because there is a sacrifice of the people today at the high place.

**9:13** As you enter the city, you will immediately find him before he goes up to the high place to eat; for the people will not eat until he comes, because he himself blesses the sacrifice, and afterward those who are invited eat. Now therefore go up, because today you will find him."

**9:14** And they went up into the city. And as they were walking in the middle of the city, Samuel appeared, coming out to meet them in order to go up to the high place.

**9:15** Now the Lord had uncovered Samuel's ear one day before Saul came, saying,

**9:16** "At this very hour that it is now, tomorrow I will send a man to you from the land of Benjamin, and you shall anoint him as leader over My people Israel; and he will save My people from the hand of the Philistines, because I have looked upon My people, for their cry has come to Me."

**9:17** And when Samuel saw Saul, the Lord said to him, "Here is the man of whom I had spoken to you; this one will rule over My people."

**9:18** Now Saul approached Samuel in the middle of the gate and said, "Tell me, please, where the house of the seer is."

**9:19** And Samuel answered Saul, saying, "I am the seer. Go up ahead of me to the high place, so that you may eat with me today, and I will send you away in the morning, and I will tell you all that is in your heart.

**9:20** And concerning the female donkeys that you lost three days ago, do not be anxious, because they have been found. And whose will all the best things of Israel be? Will they not belong to you and to all the house of your father?"

**9:21** Now Saul answered and said, "Am I not a son of Jemini, from the smallest tribe of Israel, and is not my clan the least among all the families of the tribe of Benjamin? Why then have you spoken this word to me?"

**9:22** Therefore Samuel, taking Saul and his servant, brought them into the dining hall and gave them a place at the head of those who had been invited; for there were about thirty men.

**9:23** And Samuel said to the cook, "Give the portion that I gave to you and commanded you to set aside by yourself."

**9:24** Now the cook lifted up the shoulder and set it before Saul. And Samuel said, "Look, what has remained: set it before you and eat, because it was intentionally kept for you when I invited the people." And Saul ate with Samuel on that day.

**9:25** And they came down from the high place into the town, and he spoke with Saul on the rooftop; and Saul spread a bed on the rooftop and slept.

**9:26** And when they had risen in the morning, and it was already growing light, Samuel called Saul on the rooftop, saying, "Get up, and I will send you away." And Saul got up; and both of them went out, namely he himself and Samuel.

**9:27** And as they were going down at the edge of the city, Samuel said to Saul, "Tell the servant to go ahead of us and pass on; but you stand still for a little while, so that I may tell you the word of the Lord."

## Translator Notes

- **9:1** — Latin "filii viri Jemini" renders the Hebrew gentilic "Yemini" (Benjaminite) as the proper name Jemini alongside Benjamin (also in 9:4, 21). "Mighty in strength" literally translates Latin "fortis robore", rendering the Hebrew idiom for a man of standing or valor.
- **9:2** — Latin "electus et bonus" ("choice and good") renders the Hebrew phrase usually translated "a handsome young man", taking the Hebrew participle in its literal sense of "chosen" or "choice".
- **9:8** — Latin uses "stater" (a Greek silver coin equivalent to a shekel) to render Hebrew "shekel".
- **9:24** — Latin reads "armum" ("the shoulder") where Hebrew has "the thigh" (and omits "and what was on it"), and renders the following clause directly as "when I invited the people".
- **9:25** — The closing clause "and Saul spread a bed on the rooftop and slept" is present in the Clementine Vulgate (with a parallel in the Septuagint), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text ends the verse after they spoke on the roof.
