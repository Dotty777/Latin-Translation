## Text

**30:1** Now when David and his men had come to Ziklag on the third day, the Amalekites had made an attack from the southern side against Ziklag, and had struck Ziklag and burned it with fire.

**30:2** And they had led the women away captive from it, from the least to the great; and they had not killed anyone, but had led them along with them and were going on their way.

**30:3** So when David and his men came to the city and found it burned with fire, and their wives and their sons and daughters led away captive,

**30:4** David and the people who were with him lifted up their voices and wept until tears failed in them.

**30:5** Indeed, the two wives of David had also been led away captive: Ahinoam the Jezreelite and Abigail, the wife of Nabal of Carmel.

**30:6** And David was deeply grieved, for the people wanted to stone him, because the soul of every man was bitter over his sons and daughters; but David was strengthened in the Lord his God.

**30:7** And he said to Abiathar the priest, the son of Ahimelech, "Bring the ephod to me." And Abiathar brought the ephod to David.

**30:8** And David consulted the Lord, saying, "Should I pursue these raiders, and will I overtake them, or not?" And the Lord said to him, "Pursue, for without a doubt you will overtake them and recover the plunder."

**30:9** So David went, he and the six hundred men who were with him, and they came as far as the Besor stream; and some who were exhausted stayed behind.

**30:10** But David himself and four hundred men pursued, for two hundred had stayed behind who, being exhausted, could not cross the Besor stream.

**30:11** And they found an Egyptian man in the field and brought him to David; and they gave him bread to eat and water to drink,

**30:12** and also a piece of a cake of dried figs and two bunches of raisins. When he had eaten these, his spirit returned and he was revived, for he had not eaten bread nor drunk water for three days and three nights.

**30:13** Then David said to him, "To whom do you belong? Or where are you from? And where are you going?" And he said, "I am an Egyptian youth, the servant of an Amalekite man; and my master abandoned me because I began to fall ill the day before yesterday.

**30:14** Indeed, we made a raid against the southern region of the Cherethites, and against Judah, and against the south of Caleb, and we burned Ziklag with fire."

**30:15** And David said to him, "Can you lead me to this band?" And he said, "Swear to me by God that you will not kill me and will not hand me over into the hands of my master, and I will lead you to this band." And David swore to him.

**30:16** And when he had led him, look, they were reclining over the face of the whole land, eating and drinking, and as though celebrating a festival day, because of all the plunder and spoils that they had taken from the land of the Philistines and from the land of Judah.

**30:17** And David struck them down from evening until the evening of the next day, and not one of them escaped, except four hundred young men who had mounted camels and fled.

**30:18** So David recovered all that the Amalekites had taken, and he recovered his two wives.

**30:19** Nor was anything missing, from small to great, both of the sons and of the daughters, and of the spoils, and whatever they had seized; David brought back everything.

**30:20** And he took all the flocks and herds, and drove them before his face; and they said, "This is David's plunder."

**30:21** Then David came to the two hundred men who, being exhausted, had stayed behind and had not been able to follow David, and he had ordered them to remain at the Besor stream; and they went out to meet David and the people who were with him. And David, approaching the people, greeted them peacefully.

**30:22** And every vile and unjust man among the men who had gone with David answered and said, "Because they did not come with us, we will not give them anything of the plunder that we recovered; rather, let his wife and children be enough for each one, and when they have taken them, let them depart."

**30:23** But David said, "You must not do so, my brothers, concerning the things that the Lord has delivered to us; and He has guarded us and given the raiders who had made a raid against us into our hands;

**30:24** nor will anyone listen to you concerning this matter; for the share of the one who goes down to battle and of the one who remains by the baggage will be equal, and they will divide alike."

**30:25** And from that day onward this became established and prescribed, and like a law in Israel until this day.

**30:26** So David came to Ziklag and sent gifts from the plunder to the elders of Judah, his neighbors, saying, "Receive a blessing from the plunder of the enemies of the Lord,"

**30:27** to those who were in Bethel, and those in Ramoth toward the south, and those in Jether,

**30:28** and those in Aroer, and those in Siphmoth, and those in Eshtemoa,

**30:29** and those in Racal, and those in the cities of Jerahmeel, and those in the cities of the Kenites,

**30:30** and those in Arama, and those in the cistern of Ashan, and those in Athach,

**30:31** and those in Hebron, and to the rest who were in those places in which David had stayed, he and his men.

## Translator Notes

- **30:4** — Latin donec deficerent in eis lacrimae ("until tears failed in them") is a Vulgate-distinct rendering where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "until there was no more strength in them to weep."
- **30:13** — The Vulgate includes a third question, et quo pergis? ("And where are you going?"), absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, and uses nudiustertius ("the day before yesterday," by inclusive reckoning for "three days ago").
- **30:20** — The Vulgate reads singular minavit ante faciem suam ("drove them before his face"), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("they drove them ahead of the other livestock").
- **30:27** — The Vulgate reads Jether, a distinct form corresponding to "Jattir" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **30:30** — The Vulgate reads Arama (corresponding to "Hormah" in the Hebrew Masoretic Text) and translates the first element of Hebrew "Bor-ashan" or "Chor-ashan" as in lacu Asan ("in the cistern of Ashan," literally "cistern/lake of Ashan").
