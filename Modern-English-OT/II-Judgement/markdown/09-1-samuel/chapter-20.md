## Text

**20:1** Now David fled from Naioth, which is in Ramah, and came and spoke before Jonathan, "What have I done? What is my iniquity, and what is my sin against your father, that he seeks my life?"

**20:2** And he said to him, "Far be it! You will not die. For my father will not do anything great or small unless he first tells me. Has my father therefore hidden only this matter from me? That will by no means happen."

**20:3** And he swore again to David. And the other said, "Your father surely knows that I have found favor in your eyes, and he will say, 'Let Jonathan not know this, so that he may not be grieved.' Rather, as the Lord lives and as your soul lives, I and death are separated by only one step, so to speak."

**20:4** And Jonathan said to David, "Whatever your soul says to me, I will do for you."

**20:5** Then David said to Jonathan, "Look, tomorrow is the new moon, and by custom I usually sit beside the king to eat. Therefore let me go so that I may hide in the field until the evening of the third day.

**20:6** If your father looks about and asks for me, you will answer him, 'David asked me to go quickly to Bethlehem, his city, because there are solemn sacrifices there for all his fellow tribesmen.'

**20:7** If he says, 'Good,' there will be peace for your servant. But if he is angry, know that his malice is complete.

**20:8** Show mercy, therefore, to your servant, because you have caused me, your servant, to enter into a covenant of the Lord with you. But if there is any iniquity in me, put me to death yourself, and do not bring me in to your father."

**20:9** And Jonathan said, "Far be this from you! For it cannot happen that, if I know for certain that my father's malice against you is complete, I would not report it to you."

**20:10** And David answered Jonathan, "Who will report back to me if your father happens to answer you anything harshly about me?"

**20:11** And Jonathan said to David, "Come, and let us go outside into the field." And when both of them had gone out into the field,

**20:12** Jonathan said to David, "Lord God of Israel, if I search out my father's intention tomorrow or the day after tomorrow, and there is something good toward David, and I do not immediately send to you and make it known to you,

**20:13** may the Lord do these things to Jonathan, and add these as well. But if my father's malice persists against you, I will uncover your ear and send you away, so that you may go in peace; and may the Lord be with you, just as He was with my father.

**20:14** And if I live, you will show me the mercy of the Lord; but if I have died,

**20:15** you will not remove your mercy from my house forever, when the Lord uproots the enemies of David, every single one from the earth: may He remove Jonathan from his house, and may the Lord require it from the hand of the enemies of David."

**20:16** Therefore Jonathan made a covenant with the house of David, and the Lord required it from the hand of the enemies of David.

**20:17** And Jonathan again swore solemnly to David, because he loved him; for he loved him just as his own soul.

**20:18** And Jonathan said to him, "Tomorrow is the new moon, and you will be missed,

**20:19** for your seat will be missed until the day after tomorrow. Therefore you will go down quickly and come to the place where you are to be hidden on the day when it is lawful to work, and you will sit beside the stone whose name is Ezel.

**20:20** And I will send three arrows beside it, and shoot as if practicing at a target.

**20:21** I will also send a boy, saying to him, 'Go and bring me the arrows.'

**20:22** If I say to the boy, 'Look, the arrows are on this side of you; take them,' then you come to me, because there is peace for you and there is no harm, as the Lord lives. But if I speak like this to the boy, 'Look, the arrows are beyond you,' go in peace, because the Lord has sent you away.

**20:23** Now concerning the word that you and I have spoken, may the Lord be between me and you forever."

**20:24** Therefore David hid himself in the field, and the new moon came, and the king sat down to eat bread.

**20:25** And when the king had sat down on his seat, according to custom, which was beside the wall, Jonathan stood up, and Abner sat at Saul's side; and David's place appeared empty.

**20:26** And Saul did not speak anything on that day, for he thought that perhaps something had happened to him, so that he was not clean nor purified.

**20:27** And when the second day after the new moon had dawned, David's place again appeared empty. And Saul said to Jonathan his son, "Why did the son of Jesse come neither yesterday nor today to eat?"

**20:28** And Jonathan answered Saul, "He earnestly asked me to go to Bethlehem,

**20:29** and he said, 'Let me go, since there is a solemn sacrifice in the city, and one of my brothers has summoned me. Now therefore, if I have found favor in your eyes, I will go quickly and see my brothers.' For this reason he has not come to the king's table."

**20:30** Then Saul grew angry against Jonathan and said to him, "Son of a woman who of her own accord seizes a man, am I unaware that you love the son of Jesse to your own shame, and to the shame of your disgraceful mother?

**20:31** For all the days that the son of Jesse lives upon the earth, you will not be established, nor will your kingdom. Therefore send right now and bring him to me, because he is a son of death."

**20:32** But Jonathan answered Saul his father and said, "Why will he die? What has he done?"

**20:33** And Saul seized a spear to strike him. And Jonathan understood that it had been determined by his father to put David to death.

**20:34** Therefore Jonathan rose from the table in furious anger, and he did not eat bread on the second day of the new moon. For he was grieved over David, because his father had shamed him.

**20:35** And when morning had dawned, Jonathan came into the field according to the appointment with David, and a small boy was with him.

**20:36** And he said to his boy, "Go and bring me the arrows that I shoot." And when the boy had run, he shot another arrow past the boy.

**20:37** So the boy came to the place of the arrow that Jonathan had shot, and Jonathan called out behind the boy's back and said, "Look, the arrow is there, farther beyond you."

**20:38** And Jonathan called out again behind the boy's back, saying, "Hurry quickly; do not stand still!" So Jonathan's boy gathered the arrows and brought them to his master.

**20:39** And he was completely unaware of what was being done, for only Jonathan and David knew the matter.

**20:40** Therefore Jonathan gave his weapons to the boy and said to him, "Go and carry them into the city."

**20:41** And when the boy had gone away, David rose from the place that faced toward the south, and falling facedown to the ground, he bowed down three times. And kissing one another, they wept together, but David even more.

**20:42** Therefore Jonathan said to David, "Go in peace: whatever we both have sworn in the name of the Lord, saying, 'May the Lord be between me and you, and between my offspring and your offspring forever.'"

## Translator Notes

- **20:3** — In the Clementine Vulgate, Davidi is dative ("And he swore again to David"), making Jonathan the subject of the oath and shifting to David with Et ille ("And the other"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has David swearing again.
- **20:5** — Throughout chapter 20 (vv. 5, 18, 24, 27, 34), the Vulgate renders the monthly New Moon observance with the Latin term calendae (the Calends, the first day of the month).
- **20:15** — The Vulgate subjunctive clause auferat Jonathan de domo sua, et requirat Dominus ("may He remove Jonathan from his house, and may the Lord require it") treats Jonathan as the direct object of the Lord's action, diverging in syntax and verse division from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **20:19** — The Vulgate renders the workday reference explicitly as in die qua operari licet ("on the day when it is lawful to work").
- **20:30** — The Vulgate renders Saul's insult as Fili mulieris virum ultro rapientis ("Son of a woman who of her own accord seizes a man") and ignominiosae matris tuae ("of your disgraceful mother"), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("son of a perverse, rebellious woman ... shame of your mother's nakedness").
