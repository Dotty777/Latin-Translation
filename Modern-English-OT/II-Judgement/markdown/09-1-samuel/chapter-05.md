## Text

**5:1** Now the Philistines took the ark of God, and carried it away from the Stone of Help to Ashdod.

**5:2** And the Philistines took the ark of God, and brought it into the temple of Dagon, and set it beside Dagon.

**5:3** And when the Ashdodites had risen at dawn on the next day, look, Dagon was lying prone on the ground before the ark of the Lord; and they took Dagon and restored him to his place.

**5:4** And rising up again in the morning on the next day, they found Dagon lying upon his face on the ground before the ark of the Lord; now the head of Dagon and the two palms of his hands had been cut off upon the threshold.

**5:5** Meanwhile, only the trunk of Dagon had remained in its place. For this reason the priests of Dagon, and all who enter his temple, do not step upon the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod to the present day.

**5:6** Now the hand of the Lord weighed heavily upon the Ashdodites, and He devastated them, and He struck Ashdod and its borders in the more hidden part of the buttocks. And the villages and fields in the middle of that region teemed, and mice sprang up, and a confusion of great death occurred in the city.

**5:7** Now the men of Ashdod, seeing this kind of plague, said, "Let the ark of the God of Israel not remain with us, because His hand is harsh upon us, and upon Dagon our god."

**5:8** And sending out, they gathered all the satraps of the Philistines to themselves, and said, "What shall we do with the ark of the God of Israel?" And the Gittites answered, "Let the ark of the God of Israel be carried around." And they carried the ark of the God of Israel around.

**5:9** Now while they were carrying it around, the hand of the Lord throughout each city became one of exceedingly great slaughter, and it struck the men of every city, from the small to the great, and their protruding lower bowels were rotting. And the Gittites took counsel and made for themselves leather seats.

**5:10** Therefore they sent the ark of God to Ekron. And when the ark of God had come into Ekron, the Ekronites cried out, saying, "They have brought the ark of the God of Israel to us so that it may kill us and our people."

**5:11** Therefore they sent and gathered all the satraps of the Philistines, who said, "Send away the ark of the God of Israel, and let it return to its own place, and let it not kill us with our people."

**5:12** For a terror of death was arising in each city, and the hand of God was very heavy indeed. The men also who had not died were being struck in the more hidden part of the buttocks, and the wailing of every city went up to heaven.

## Translator Notes

- **5:5** — In the Vulgate, the opening clause about Dagon's trunk begins verse 5 and adds "in loco suo" ("in its place"), whereas in the Hebrew Masoretic Text it concludes verse 4 ("only Dagon was left to him").
- **5:6** — Jerome renders the affliction euphemistically as "in the more hidden part of the buttocks" (again in 5:12); the second sentence describing the swarming villages, mice, and confusion of great death is a Vulgate and Septuagint expansion absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **5:8** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate makes the Gittites (the inhabitants of Gath) the speakers who propose carrying the ark around, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text states that the Philistines decided to move the ark to Gath.
- **5:9** — The Vulgate vividly describes the plague as causing their protruding lower bowels ("extales") to rot, and concludes with a Vulgate and Septuagint addition in which the Gittites take counsel and make leather seats for themselves.
