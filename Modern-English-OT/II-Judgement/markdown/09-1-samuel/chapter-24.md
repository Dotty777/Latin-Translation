## Text

**24:1** So David went up from there and lived in the most secure places of En-gedi.

**24:2** And when Saul had returned after he pursued the Philistines, they reported to him, saying, "Look, David is in the wilderness of En-gedi."

**24:3** Therefore, taking three thousand chosen men from all Israel, Saul set out to search for David and his men, even over the steepest crags, which are passable only to wild goats.

**24:4** And he came to sheepfolds that presented themselves to a traveler, and there was a cave there, which Saul entered to relieve his bowels. Meanwhile David and his men were hiding in the inner part of the cave.

**24:5** And David's servants said to him, "Here is the day of which the Lord spoke to you: 'I will hand your enemy over to you, so that you may do to him as is pleasing in your eyes.'" So David rose up and silently cut off the hem of Saul's cloak.

**24:6** After these things, David's heart struck him because he had cut off the hem of Saul's cloak.

**24:7** And he said to his men, "May the Lord be merciful to me, that I should not do this thing to my lord, the anointed of the Lord, to lay my hand against him, because he is the anointed of the Lord."

**24:8** And David subdued his men with words and did not allow them to rise up against Saul. Meanwhile Saul, rising up from the cave, proceeded on the journey he had begun.

**24:9** Then David also rose up after him, and going out of the cave, he called out behind Saul's back, saying, "My lord the king." And Saul looked back behind him, and David, bowing himself face down to the ground, paid homage.

**24:10** And he said to Saul, "Why do you listen to the words of men who say, 'David seeks evil against you'?

**24:11** Look, today your eyes have seen that the Lord handed you over into my hand in the cave. And I thought to kill you, but my eye spared you; for I said, 'I will not stretch out my hand against my lord, because he is the anointed of the Lord.'

**24:12** Moreover, my father, look and recognize the hem of your cloak in my hand; for when I cut off the edge of your cloak, I was unwilling to stretch out my hand against you. Take note and see that there is no evil or iniquity in my hand, nor have I sinned against you; yet you lie in wait for my life to take it away.

**24:13** May the Lord judge between me and you, and may the Lord avenge me on you; but let my hand not be against you.

**24:14** Just as it is also said in the ancient proverb, 'From the wicked, wickedness will come forth.' Therefore let my hand not be against you.

**24:15** Whom are you pursuing, king of Israel? Whom are you pursuing? You are pursuing a dead dog, and a single flea.

**24:16** May the Lord be judge, and judge between me and you; and may He see, and judge my cause, and rescue me from your hand."

**24:17** Now when David had finished speaking words of this kind to Saul, Saul said, "Is this your voice, my son David?" And Saul lifted up his voice and wept.

**24:18** And he said to David, "You are more righteous than I; for you have rendered good things to me, whereas I have repaid evil things to you.

**24:19** And you have shown today what good things you have done for me: how the Lord handed me over into your hand, and you did not kill me.

**24:20** For who, when he finds his enemy, will let him go on a good way? Instead, may the Lord repay you this recompense for what you have done toward me today.

**24:21** And now, because I know that you will most certainly reign and will hold the kingdom of Israel in your hand,

**24:22** swear to me by the Lord that you will not blot out my offspring after me, nor remove my name from my father's house."

**24:23** And David swore to Saul. So Saul departed to his house, and David and his men went up to more secure places.

## Translator Notes

- **24:3** — Latin "etiam super abruptissimas petras, quae solis ibicibus perviae sunt" renders the Hebrew place name ("Rocks of the Wild Goats") descriptively as steep crags passable only to wild goats (ibexes).
- **24:4** — Latin "ut purgaret ventrem" ("to relieve his bowels") explicitly renders the Hebrew euphemism "to cover his feet."
- **24:7** — Latin "Propitius sit mihi Dominus" ("May the Lord be merciful to me") is the Vulgate's characteristic rendering of the Hebrew oath formula "Far be it from me from the Lord."
- **24:11** — The Vulgate explicitly reads first-person "et cogitavi ut occiderem te, sed pepercit tibi oculus meus" ("and I thought to kill you, but my eye spared you"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text is often taken impersonally ("some said to kill you") and omits "my eye."
