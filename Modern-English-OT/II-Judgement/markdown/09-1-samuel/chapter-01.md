## Text

**1:1** There was a certain man from Ramathaim-zophim, from Mount Ephraim, and his name was Elkanah, son of Jeroham, son of Elihu, son of Tohu, son of Zuph, an Ephrathite.

**1:2** And he had two wives; the name of one was Hannah, and the name of the second was Peninnah. And Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children.

**1:3** And that man used to go up from his city on the appointed days to worship and sacrifice to the Lord of hosts in Shiloh. Now the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, priests of the Lord, were there.

**1:4** So the day came, and Elkanah offered sacrifice, and he gave portions to his wife Peninnah and to all her sons and daughters.

**1:5** But to Hannah he gave one portion sorrowfully, because he loved Hannah. However, the Lord had closed her womb.

**1:6** Her rival also afflicted her and distressed her severely, to such an extent that she reproached her because the Lord had closed her womb.

**1:7** And so she did every single year: when, as the time returned, they went up to the temple of the Lord, so she provoked her; meanwhile she wept and would not take food.

**1:8** So her husband Elkanah said to her, "Hannah, why are you weeping? And why are you not eating? And for what reason is your heart afflicted? Am I not better to you than ten sons?"

**1:9** Now Hannah rose after she had eaten and drunk in Shiloh. And while Eli the priest was sitting on a seat before the doorposts of the temple of the Lord,

**1:10** Hannah, being bitter in spirit, prayed to the Lord, weeping profusely,

**1:11** and she vowed a vow, saying, "Lord of hosts, if looking on You see the affliction of Your female servant, and remember me, and do not forget Your maidservant, and give Your servant a male child, I will give him to the Lord all the days of his life, and no razor will come upon his head."

**1:12** Now it happened, while she was multiplying prayers before the Lord, that Eli watched her mouth.

**1:13** Now Hannah was speaking in her heart, and only her lips were moving, and no voice at all was heard. Therefore Eli considered her to be drunk,

**1:14** and he said to her, "How long will you be drunk? Work off for a little while the wine with which you are drenched."

**1:15** Answering, Hannah said, "By no means, my lord, for I am an exceedingly unhappy woman; and I have not drunk wine or anything that can intoxicate, but I have poured out my soul in the sight of the Lord.

**1:16** Do not reckon your maidservant as one of the daughters of Belial, for out of the abundance of my grief and sorrow I have spoken up to the present."

**1:17** Then Eli said to her, "Go in peace, and may the God of Israel grant you your petition that you have asked of Him."

**1:18** And she said, "May your maidservant find favor in your eyes." And the woman went on her way and ate, and her countenance was no longer changed in different ways.

**1:19** And they rose in the morning and worshiped before the Lord; and they returned and came to their house in Ramah. And Elkanah knew Hannah his wife, and the Lord remembered her.

**1:20** And it happened after the cycle of days that Hannah conceived and bore a son, and she called his name Samuel, because she had asked for him from the Lord.

**1:21** Now her husband Elkanah went up, and all his household, to offer to the Lord the solemn sacrifice and his vow.

**1:22** And Hannah did not go up, for she said to her husband, "I will not go until the infant is weaned, and I will bring him so that he may appear before the sight of the Lord and remain there continually."

**1:23** And her husband Elkanah said to her, "Do what seems good to you, and remain until you wean him; and I pray that the Lord may fulfill His word." So the woman remained and nursed her son until she took him off milk.

**1:24** And she brought him with her after she had weaned him, with three calves, and three modii of flour, and a jar of wine, and she brought him to the house of the Lord in Shiloh. Now the boy was still a very small child;

**1:25** and they sacrificed a calf, and they presented the boy to Eli.

**1:26** And Hannah said, "I beg you, my lord, as your soul lives, my lord, I am that woman who stood before you here, praying to the Lord.

**1:27** For this boy I prayed, and the Lord has given me my petition that I asked of Him.

**1:28** Therefore I also have lent him to the Lord for all the days in which he is lent to the Lord." And they worshiped the Lord there. And Hannah prayed and said:

## Translator Notes

- **1:5** — The Vulgate reads 'partem unam tristis' ("one portion sorrowfully"), interpreting the difficult Hebrew 'appayim' as Elkanah's sorrowful countenance rather than "a double portion."
- **1:16** — The Vulgate preserves the Hebrew transliteration 'Belial' (meaning "worthlessness" or "wickedness") as a name both here and in 2:12.
- **1:18** — The Vulgate reads 'vultusque illius non sunt amplius in diversa mutati' ("her countenance was no longer changed in different ways"), expanding the concise Hebrew idiom meaning her face was no longer downcast.
- **1:24** — The Vulgate has 'tribus modiis' ("three modii"), reflecting Jerome's reckoning of one Hebrew ephah as three modii (seahs), and retains 'vitulis tribus' ("three calves") rather than the Septuagint's "a three-year-old bull."
- **1:28** — The Vulgate reads 'quibus fuerit commodatus Domino' ("that he is lent to the Lord") rather than the Hebrew "as long as he lives," uses plural 'adoraverunt' ("they worshiped") with the Septuagint, and places "And Hannah prayed and said:" at the end of 1:28 rather than at 2:1.
