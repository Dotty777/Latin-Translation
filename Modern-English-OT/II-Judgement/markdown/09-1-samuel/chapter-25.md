## Text

**25:1** Now Samuel died, and all Israel was gathered together, and they mourned for him and buried him in his house in Ramah. And rising up, David went down into the wilderness of Paran.

**25:2** Now there was a certain man in the desert of Maon, and his property was in Carmel, and that man was exceedingly great. He had three thousand sheep and a thousand goats; and it happened that his flock was being sheared in Carmel.

**25:3** Now the name of that man was Nabal, and the name of his wife was Abigail. That woman was most prudent and beautiful, whereas her husband was harsh, very wicked, and malicious; and he was of the lineage of Caleb.

**25:4** Therefore, when David heard in the wilderness that Nabal was shearing his flock,

**25:5** he sent ten young men and said to them, "Go up to Carmel, and you will come to Nabal and greet him peacefully in my name.

**25:6** And you will say, 'May peace be to my brothers and to you, and peace to your house, and may peace be to all things that you have.

**25:7** I have heard that your shepherds, who were with us in the wilderness, are shearing. We were never troublesome to them, nor was anything from the flock ever missing for them during all the time that they were with us in Carmel.

**25:8** Ask your young men, and they will tell you. Now therefore let your young men find favor in your eyes, for we have come on a good day. Whatever your hand finds, give to your servants and to your son David.'"

**25:9** And when David's young men had come, they spoke all these words to Nabal in David's name, and they fell silent.

**25:10** But Nabal, answering David's young men, said, "Who is David? And who is the son of Jesse? Today servants who flee from their masters have multiplied.

**25:11** Should I therefore take my loaves of bread, and my water, and the meat of the livestock that I have slaughtered for my shearers, and give them to men when I do not know where they are from?"

**25:12** Accordingly, David's young men went back along their way, and returning, they came and reported to him all the words that he had spoken.

**25:13** Then David said to his young men, "Let each man strap on his sword." And each of them strapped on his sword, and David also strapped on his sword. And about four hundred men followed David, while two hundred remained with the baggage.

**25:14** Now one of her young men reported to Abigail, Nabal's wife, saying, "Look, David sent messengers from the wilderness to bless our master, and he spurned them.

**25:15** Those men had been very good to us and not troublesome, nor was anything ever lost during all the time that we associated with them in the wilderness.

**25:16** They were a wall to us both by night and by day, all the days that we pastured the flocks near them.

**25:17** For this reason, consider and think over what you should do, because evil is complete against your husband and against your house, and he himself is a son of Belial, so that no one can speak to him."

**25:18** Therefore Abigail hurried and took two hundred loaves of bread, and two skins of wine, and five cooked rams, and five measures of parched grain, and one hundred bundles of raisins, and two hundred cakes of dried figs, and placed them on donkeys.

**25:19** And she said to her young men, "Go ahead of me; look, I will follow behind you." However, she did not tell her husband Nabal.

**25:20** So when she had mounted a donkey and was going down to the foot of the mountain, David and his men were coming down to meet her, and she also met them.

**25:21** And David said, "Truly in vain I guarded everything that belonged to this man in the wilderness, and nothing was lost from all that pertained to him; and he has repaid me evil for good.

**25:22** May God do these things to the enemies of David, and add these things as well, if I leave until morning, out of all that belongs to him, one who urinates against a wall."

**25:23** Now when Abigail saw David, she hurried and got down from the donkey, and fell down before David on her face, and paid homage on the ground.

**25:24** And she fell at his feet and said, "On me, my lord, let this iniquity be. Let your maidservant speak, I beg you, in your ears, and listen to the words of your female servant.

**25:25** Please, let my lord the king not set his heart upon this wicked man Nabal, because according to his name he is a fool, and folly is with him. But I, your maidservant, did not see your young men, my lord, whom you sent.

**25:26** Now therefore, my lord, as the Lord lives and as your soul lives, He who kept you from coming to bloodshed and saved your hand for you: now let your enemies, and those who seek evil for my lord, become like Nabal.

**25:27** Therefore accept this blessing that your maidservant has brought to you, my lord, and give it to the young men who follow you, my lord.

**25:28** Take away the iniquity of your female servant; for the Lord will surely make for you, my lord, a faithful house, because you fight the battles of the Lord, my lord. Therefore let evil not be found in you all the days of your life.

**25:29** For if at any time a man rises up pursuing you and seeking your life, the life of my lord will be kept safe as in the bundle of the living with the Lord your God; whereas the life of your enemies will be whirled around, as in the force and circle of a sling.

**25:30** Therefore, when the Lord has done for you, my lord, all the good things that He has spoken concerning you, and has appointed you leader over Israel,

**25:31** this will not be a sobbing and a scruple of heart for you, my lord, that you shed innocent blood or avenged yourself. And when the Lord has done good to my lord, you will remember your maidservant."

**25:32** And David said to Abigail, "Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, who sent you today to meet me, and blessed be your speech,

**25:33** and blessed be you, who kept me today from going to bloodshed and from avenging myself by my own hand.

**25:34** Otherwise, as the Lord God of Israel lives, who kept me from doing evil to you, if you had not come quickly to meet me, there would not have remained to Nabal until the morning light one who urinates against a wall."

**25:35** Therefore David received from her hand everything that she had brought him, and said to her, "Go peacefully to your house; look, I have listened to your voice and have honored your face."

**25:36** Now Abigail came to Nabal, and look, he had a banquet in his house like the banquet of a king, and Nabal's heart was merry, for he was exceedingly drunk. And she did not tell him a word, small or great, until morning.

**25:37** At daybreak, however, when Nabal had digested the wine, his wife told him these words; and his heart died within him, and he became like a stone.

**25:38** And when ten days had passed, the Lord struck Nabal, and he died.

**25:39** When David heard that Nabal was dead, he said, "Blessed be the Lord, who has judged the cause of my reproach from the hand of Nabal, and has kept His servant from evil, and the Lord has returned the evil of Nabal upon his head." Therefore David sent and spoke to Abigail, to take her for himself as a wife.

**25:40** And David's young men came to Abigail in Carmel and spoke to her, saying, "David has sent us to you, to take you for himself as a wife."

**25:41** And rising up, she paid homage face down to the ground and said, "Look, let your female servant be a maidservant, to wash the feet of the servants of my lord."

**25:42** And Abigail hurried and rose up, and mounted a donkey, and five young women, her attendants, went with her; and she followed David's messengers, and she became his wife.

**25:43** Moreover, David also took Ahinoam from Jezreel, and each of the two was his wife.

**25:44** Now Saul gave his daughter Michal, David's wife, to Palti the son of Laish, who was from Gallim.

## Translator Notes

- **25:2** — The Vulgate reads "in solitudine Maon" ("in the desert of Maon"), specifying the desert where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has simply "in Maon."
- **25:6** — Latin "Sit fratribus meis et tibi pax" ("May peace be to my brothers and to you") preserves a distinctly Vulgate reading ("to my brothers") where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "to the living" or "for long life."
- **25:22** — Latin "mingentem ad parietem" ("one who urinates against a wall"; also in 25:34) literally renders an ancient idiom meaning any male.
- **25:25** — The Vulgate reads "dominus meus rex" ("my lord the king"), including the title "king" where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has only "my lord."
- **25:26** — Latin "et salvavit manum tuam tibi" ("and saved your hand for you") is a distinctly Vulgate clause where the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of keeping David from avenging himself by his own hand.
