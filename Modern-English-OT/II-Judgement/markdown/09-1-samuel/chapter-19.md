## Text

**19:1** Now Saul spoke to Jonathan his son and to all his servants, that they should kill David. But Jonathan, the son of Saul, loved David greatly.

**19:2** And Jonathan told David, saying, "Saul my father is seeking to kill you. Therefore, please be on your guard in the morning, and remain in secret and hide yourself.

**19:3** I, however, will go out and stand beside my father in the field, wherever you may be, and I will speak about you to my father, and whatever I see, I will report to you."

**19:4** Therefore Jonathan spoke good things about David to Saul his father, and he said to him, "Do not sin, king, against your servant David, because he has not sinned against you, and his deeds are very good for you.

**19:5** And he put his life in his hand and struck down the Philistine, and the Lord worked a great deliverance for all Israel. You saw it and rejoiced. Why then are you sinning against innocent blood, putting David to death, who is without fault?"

**19:6** When Saul heard this, appeased by the voice of Jonathan, he swore, "As the Lord lives, he will not be killed."

**19:7** So Jonathan called David and told him all these words. And Jonathan brought David in to Saul, and he was before him just as he had been yesterday and the day before.

**19:8** Now war was stirred up again, and David went out and fought against the Philistines, and he struck them with a great blow, and they fled from his presence.

**19:9** And an evil spirit of the Lord came upon Saul. Now he was sitting in his house and holding a spear, while David was playing music with his hand.

**19:10** And Saul tried to pin David to the wall with the spear, but David turned aside from Saul's presence, and the spear, with a fruitless blow, was driven into the wall; and David fled and was saved that night.

**19:11** Therefore Saul sent his guards to David's house to watch him, so that he would be put to death in the morning. When his wife Michal reported this to David, saying, "Unless you save yourself tonight, tomorrow you will die,"

**19:12** she lowered him through a window. And he went away and fled, and was saved.

**19:13** Then Michal took a statue and laid it on the bed, and she placed a hairy goatskin at its head and covered it with garments.

**19:14** Now Saul sent attendants to seize David, and the answer was given that he was sick.

**19:15** And again Saul sent messengers to see David, saying, "Bring him to me in the bed, so that he may be killed."

**19:16** And when the messengers came, an image was found on the bed, and a goatskin at its head.

**19:17** And Saul said to Michal, "Why have you deceived me like this and let my enemy go, so that he might flee?" And Michal answered Saul, "Because he himself spoke to me, 'Let me go; otherwise I will kill you.'"

**19:18** Now David fled and was saved, and he came to Samuel in Ramah and reported to him everything that Saul had done to him. And he and Samuel went away and stayed in Naioth.

**19:19** And it was reported to Saul by those who said, "Look, David is in Naioth in Ramah."

**19:20** Therefore Saul sent officers to seize David. When they saw a company of prophets prophesying, and Samuel standing over them, the Spirit of the Lord also came upon them, and they themselves also began to prophesy.

**19:21** When this was reported to Saul, he also sent other messengers, and they also prophesied. And again Saul sent a third group of messengers, and they themselves also prophesied. And Saul, enraged with anger,

**19:22** went himself also to Ramah, and came as far as the great cistern that is in Socoh. And he inquired and said, "In what place are Samuel and David?" And it was said to him, "Look, they are in Naioth in Ramah."

**19:23** And he went to Naioth in Ramah, and the Spirit of the Lord came upon him as well; and he walked along as he went and prophesied until he came to Naioth in Ramah.

**19:24** And he himself also stripped off his garments and prophesied with the rest before Samuel, and he fell down naked all that day and night. From this also the proverb went out, "Is Saul also among the prophets?"

## Translator Notes

- **19:10** — Latin casso vulnere ("with a fruitless blow") is a distinctive Vulgate addition describing the spear missing David and striking the wall.
- **19:20** — The Vulgate reads spiritus Domini ("the Spirit of the Lord") here and in 19:23, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "the Spirit of God"; Jerome also varies the terms for Saul's men across the chapter (satellites, apparitores, nuntios, lictores).
- **19:21** — The closing phrase Et iratus iracundia Saul ("And Saul, enraged with anger") agrees with the Septuagint and is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **19:22** — The Vulgate reads the place name Socho ("Socoh"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Secu."
