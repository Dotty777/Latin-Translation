## Text

**15:1** And Samuel said to Saul, "The Lord sent me to anoint you as king over His people Israel; now therefore listen to the voice of the Lord.

**15:2** This is what the Lord of hosts says: 'I have reviewed all that Amalek did to Israel, how he opposed him on the way when he was coming up from Egypt.

**15:3** Now therefore go and strike Amalek, and destroy everything that is his; do not spare him, and do not covet anything from his possessions, but kill from man to woman, both little child and nursing infant, ox and sheep, camel and donkey.'"

**15:4** So Saul commanded the people and counted them like lambs: two hundred thousand foot soldiers, and ten thousand men of Judah.

**15:5** And when Saul had come as far as the city of Amalek, he set an ambush in the torrent.

**15:6** And Saul said to the Kenites, "Go away, depart, and go down from Amalek, so that I might not perhaps entangle you with him; for you showed mercy to all the sons of Israel when they were coming up from Egypt." And the Kenites departed from the midst of Amalek.

**15:7** And Saul struck Amalek from Havilah until you come to Shur, which is opposite Egypt.

**15:8** And he captured Agag, king of Amalek, alive; but all the common people he killed with the edge of the sword.

**15:9** And Saul and the people spared Agag, and the best flocks of sheep and herds, and the garments and the rams, and everything that was beautiful, and they were unwilling to destroy them; but whatever was inferior and rejected, this they destroyed.

**15:10** Now the word of the Lord came to Samuel, saying,

**15:11** "I regret that I have appointed Saul as king, because he has abandoned Me and has not fulfilled My words in deed." And Samuel was grieved, and he cried out to the Lord all night.

**15:12** And when Samuel had risen in the night to go to Saul in the morning, it was reported to Samuel that Saul had come to Carmel and had erected for himself a triumphal arch, and having turned back, had passed on and gone down to Gilgal. Therefore Samuel came to Saul, and Saul was offering a burnt offering to the Lord from the first of the spoils that he had brought from Amalek.

**15:13** And when Samuel had come to Saul, Saul said to him, "Blessed are you by the Lord; I have fulfilled the word of the Lord."

**15:14** And Samuel said, "And what is this sound of flocks that resounds in my ears, and of herds that I hear?"

**15:15** And Saul said, "They brought them from Amalek; for the people spared the better sheep and herds so that they might be sacrificed to the Lord your God, while the rest we put to death."

**15:16** Then Samuel said to Saul, "Allow me, and I will tell you what the Lord spoke to me in the night." And he said to him, "Speak."

**15:17** And Samuel said, "When you were small in your own eyes, were you not made head among the tribes of Israel? And the Lord anointed you as king over Israel,

**15:18** and the Lord sent you on a journey and said, 'Go and kill the sinners of Amalek, and you will fight against them until their extermination.'

**15:19** Why then did you not listen to the voice of the Lord, but turned toward the spoil and did evil in the eyes of the Lord?"

**15:20** And Saul said to Samuel, "On the contrary, I did listen to the voice of the Lord, and I walked in the way by which the Lord sent me, and I brought Agag, king of Amalek, and I killed Amalek.

**15:21** But the people took sheep and oxen from the spoil, the firstfruits of those that were slaughtered, so that they might sacrifice to the Lord their God in Gilgal."

**15:22** And Samuel said, "Does the Lord desire burnt offerings and sacrifices, and not rather that the voice of the Lord be obeyed? For obedience is better than sacrifices, and to listen closely more than to offer the fat of rams.

**15:23** For to resist is like the sin of divination, and to refuse to yield is like the crime of idolatry. Therefore, because you have rejected the word of the Lord, the Lord has rejected you from being king."

**15:24** And Saul said to Samuel, "I have sinned, because I have transgressed the word of the Lord and your words, fearing the people and obeying their voice.

**15:25** But now, please bear my sin, and return with me, so that I may worship the Lord."

**15:26** And Samuel said to Saul, "I will not return with you, because you have cast aside the word of the Lord, and the Lord has cast you aside from being king over Israel."

**15:27** And Samuel turned around to go away, but he seized the edge of his cloak, which also tore.

**15:28** And Samuel said to him, "The Lord has torn the kingdom of Israel from you today, and has handed it over to your neighbor who is better than you.

**15:29** Moreover, the Victor in Israel will not spare, and will not be swayed by regret; for He is not a man that He should repent."

**15:30** But he said, "I have sinned; yet now honor me before the elders of my people and before Israel, and return with me, so that I may worship the Lord your God."

**15:31** So Samuel returned and followed Saul, and Saul worshiped the Lord.

**15:32** And Samuel said, "Bring Agag, king of Amalek, to me." And Agag was brought before him, very fat and trembling. And Agag said, "Does bitter death separate like this?"

**15:33** And Samuel said, "Just as your sword has made women childless, so among women your mother will be childless." And Samuel cut him into pieces before the Lord in Gilgal.

**15:34** Then Samuel went away to Ramah, while Saul went up to his house in Gibeah.

**15:35** And Samuel did not see Saul again until the day of his death; nevertheless Samuel mourned for Saul, because the Lord regretted that He had appointed him king over Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **15:4** — Vulgate renders the Hebrew place name Telaim (ba-Telaim) etymologically as "like lambs" (quasi agnos).
- **15:9** — Vulgate reads "garments" (vestibus) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has a term usually rendered "fatlings" or "secondlings" (ha-mishnim).
- **15:12** — Vulgate renders Hebrew yad ("monument") as "a triumphal arch" (fornicem triumphalem) and includes the closing clause describing Saul offering a burnt offering from the first of the spoils, which is absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **15:29** — Vulgate reads "the Victor [lit. Triumpher] in Israel will not spare" (triumphator in Israël non parcet), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text is usually rendered "the Glory of Israel will not lie."
- **15:32** — Vulgate describes Agag as "very fat and trembling" (pinguissimus et tremens) and casts his speech as a question, "Does bitter death separate like this?" (Siccine separat amara mors?), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text is commonly rendered "Surely the bitterness of death has passed."
