## Text

**1:1** In the first year of Cyrus king of the Persians, so that the word of the Lord from the mouth of Jeremiah might be fulfilled, the Lord roused the spirit of Cyrus king of the Persians, and he sent out a proclamation throughout his entire kingdom, also in writing, saying:

**1:2** "This is what Cyrus king of the Persians says: The Lord God of heaven has given me all the kingdoms of the earth, and He Himself has commanded me to build a house for Him in Jerusalem, which is in Judea.

**1:3** Who among you is of His entire people? May his God be with him. Let him go up to Jerusalem, which is in Judea, and build the house of the Lord God of Israel; He Himself is the God who is in Jerusalem.

**1:4** And all the rest, in every place wherever they dwell, let the men from his place assist him with silver and gold, with possessions, and with livestock, besides what they voluntarily offer to the temple of God, which is in Jerusalem."

**1:5** And the leaders of the fathers of Judah and Benjamin, and the priests, and the Levites, and everyone whose spirit God had roused, arose so that they might go up to build the temple of the Lord, which was in Jerusalem.

**1:6** And all those who were on every side assisted their hands with silver and gold vessels, with possessions and beasts of burden, with furnishings, besides those things which they had offered of their own accord.

**1:7** King Cyrus also brought out the vessels of the temple of the Lord, which Nebuchadnezzar had taken from Jerusalem, and he had placed them in the temple of his god.

**1:8** Now Cyrus king of the Persians brought them out by the hand of Mithredath the son of Gazabar and counted them out to Sheshbazzar, the leader of Judah.

**1:9** And this is their number: thirty gold basins, one thousand silver basins, twenty-nine knives, thirty gold goblets,

**1:10** four hundred ten silver goblets of a second type, one thousand other vessels.

**1:11** All the gold and silver vessels were five thousand four hundred; Sheshbazzar brought all of them with those who were going up from the exile of Babylon to Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **1:4** — The Vulgate shifts from plural ("all the rest... wherever they dwell... what they voluntarily offer") to singular ("assist him... from his place"), reflecting a shift back to the individual returnee of verse 3 or calquing the Hebrew singular. Here and in verses 5 and 7, the Vulgate renders "temple" (templum) where the Hebrew has "house."
- **1:6** — The Vulgate literally reads "assisted their hands" (adjuverunt manus eorum, versus Hebrew "strengthened"), joins both silver and gold as adjectives modifying "vessels" (in vasis argenteis et aureis), and renders the Hebrew term for "costly things" as "furnishings" (supellectili).
- **1:8** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew Persian-loanword title haggizbar ("the treasurer") as a patronymic proper name, filii Gazabar ("the son of Gazabar"). The Latin forms Mithridatis and Sassabasar are given in their standard English biblical spellings, Mithredath and Sheshbazzar.
- **1:9** — The Vulgate translates the rare Hebrew term mahalapim (variously understood as censers, knives, or duplicates) as cultri ("knives"). It also places "thirty gold goblets" (scyphi aurei triginta) at the end of verse 9 rather than at the beginning of verse 10 as in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **1:11** — Both the Vulgate and the Hebrew text state a total of five thousand four hundred vessels, although the itemized counts in verses 9-10 sum to two thousand four hundred ninety-nine (possibly because only major categories were listed). The Vulgate also reads active "those who were going up" (his qui ascendebant) from the "exile" (transmigratione, literally removal or deportation) of Babylon.
