## Text

**5:1** Now Haggai the prophet and Zechariah son of Iddo prophesied, prophesying to the Jews who were in Judea and Jerusalem in the name of the God of Israel.

**5:2** Then Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel and Joshua son of Jozadak arose and began to build the temple of God in Jerusalem, and with them were the prophets of God, helping them.

**5:3** At that very time Tattenai, who was governor beyond the river, and Shethar-bozenai, and their counselors came to them, and they said to them in this way: "Who gave you counsel to build this house and to restore its walls?"

**5:4** To this we answered them what the names were of the men who were the originators of that building.

**5:5** But the eye of their God was upon the elders of the Jews, and they could not stop them. And it seemed good that the matter should be referred to Darius, and then they would give satisfaction against that accusation.

**5:6** The copy of the letter that Tattenai, governor of the region beyond the river, and Shethar-bozenai, and his counselors, the Apharsachites, who were beyond the river, sent to King Darius.

**5:7** The message that they had sent to him was written as follows: "To King Darius, all peace.

**5:8** Let it be known to the king that we went to the province of Judea, to the house of the great God, which is being built with unpolished stone, and timbers are being laid in the walls; and that work is being diligently constructed and is growing in their hands.

**5:9** Therefore we questioned those elders, and we spoke to them in this way: 'Who gave you authority to build this house and to restore these walls?'

**5:10** Moreover, we asked them for their names so that we might inform you, and we wrote down the names of those men who are leaders among them.

**5:11** And they answered us with a reply of this sort, saying, 'We are the servants of the God of heaven and earth, and we are building the temple that had been constructed many years ago, and which a great king of Israel had built and constructed.

**5:12** But after our fathers provoked the God of heaven to anger, He delivered them into the hands of Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, the Chaldean; he also destroyed this house and deported its people to Babylon.

**5:13** However, in the first year of Cyrus, king of Babylon, King Cyrus issued an edict that this house of God should be built.

**5:14** For the gold and silver vessels of the temple of God also, which Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the temple that was in Jerusalem and had carried away into the temple of Babylon, King Cyrus brought out from the temple of Babylon, and they were given to one named Sheshbazzar, whom he also appointed ruler,

**5:15** and he said to him, "Take these vessels, and go, and place them in the temple that is in Jerusalem, and let the house of God be built in its place."

**5:16** So then that Sheshbazzar came and laid the foundations of the temple of God in Jerusalem, and from that time until now it is being built, and it has not yet been completed.'

**5:17** Now therefore, if it seems good to the king, let him search in the library of the king, which is in Babylon, whether an order was given by King Cyrus that the house of God in Jerusalem should be built, and let him send to us the will of the king concerning this matter."

## Translator Notes

- **5:4** — Retaining the first-person plural respondimus ("we answered"), the Vulgate presents this verse as the Jews' reply giving the builders' names, whereas many modern versions emend the Aramaic to third person ("they asked") following the Septuagint.
- **5:5** — Latin satisfacerent adversus accusationem illam ("give satisfaction against that accusation") is a Vulgate-distinct rendering where the Aramaic speaks of sending back a letter regarding the matter.
- **5:8** — The Vulgate renders Aramaic 'eben gelal ("huge/heavy stones") as lapide impolito ("unpolished stone").
- **5:17** — The Vulgate renders Aramaic beit ginzayya ("house of treasures" or "treasury") as bibliotheca regis ("the library of the king").
