## Text

**3:1** And now the seventh month had come, and the sons of Israel were in their cities; therefore the people were gathered together as one man in Jerusalem.

**3:2** And Joshua son of Jozadak, and his brothers the priests, and Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, and his brothers, arose and built the altar of the God of Israel to offer burnt offerings on it, as it is written in the law of Moses, the man of God.

**3:3** Now they set the altar of God on its bases, while the peoples of the lands all around were terrifying them; and they offered on it a burnt offering to the Lord morning and evening.

**3:4** And they kept the Feast of Tabernacles, as it is written, and offered the burnt offering day by day in order, according to the ordinance, the work of each day on its own day.

**3:5** And after these things, the continual burnt offering, both at the new moons and at all the feasts of the Lord that were consecrated, and on all occasions in which a gift was freely offered to the Lord.

**3:6** From the first day of the seventh month they began to offer a burnt offering to the Lord; however, the foundation of the temple of God had not yet been laid.

**3:7** Now they gave money to the stonecutters and masons, and also food, drink, and oil to the Sidonians and Tyrians, so that they would bring cedar timbers from Lebanon to the sea of Joppa, according to what Cyrus, king of the Persians, had commanded them.

**3:8** Now in the second year of their coming to the temple of God in Jerusalem, in the second month, Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, and Joshua son of Jozadak, and the rest of their brothers, the priests and the Levites, and all who had come from the captivity to Jerusalem, began, and they appointed the Levites from twenty years old and upward to press forward the work of the Lord.

**3:9** And Joshua and his sons and his brothers, Kadmiel and his sons, and the sons of Judah stood as one man to oversee those who were doing the work in the temple of God: the sons of Henadad, and their sons, and their brothers the Levites.

**3:10** Therefore, when the foundation of the temple of the Lord had been laid by the masons, the priests stood in their vestments with trumpets, and the Levites, the sons of Asaph, with cymbals, to praise God by the hands of David, king of Israel.

**3:11** And they sang together in hymns and thanksgiving to the Lord: "For He is good, for His mercy is forever over Israel." All the people also shouted with a loud shout in praising the Lord, because the foundation of the temple of the Lord had been laid.

**3:12** Very many also of the priests and Levites, and the chiefs of the fathers, and the elders, who had seen the former temple when it was founded, and this temple before their eyes, wept with a loud voice; and many, shouting in joy, lifted up their voice.

**3:13** Nor could anyone distinguish the sound of the shout of those rejoicing and the sound of the weeping of the people; for the people shouted mingled together with a loud shout, and the sound was heard far away.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — The Vulgate renders the high priest's name as Josue ("Joshua"), matching Haggai and Zechariah, whereas the Hebrew text of Ezra uses the shortened post-exilic form "Jeshua."
- **3:3** — The Vulgate specifies altare Dei ("the altar of God") and uses an ablative absolute (deterrentibus eos per circuitum populis terrarum, "while the peoples of the lands all around were terrifying them"), where the Hebrew has "the altar" and "because dread was upon them from the peoples of the lands."
- **3:10** — Latin per manus David ("by the hands of David") preserves the literal idiom meaning according to David's directions; the Vulgate also reads Deum ("God") where the Hebrew has the divine name.
- **3:12** — Following the Clementine Vulgate syntax (templum prius cum fundatum esset, et hoc templum), "when it was founded" modifies the former temple and et ("and") introduces "this temple," whereas the Hebrew connects the laying of the foundation directly to "this house."
