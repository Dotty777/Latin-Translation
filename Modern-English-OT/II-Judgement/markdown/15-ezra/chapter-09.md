## Text

**9:1** Now after these things were completed, the leaders approached me, saying, "The people of Israel, the priests and the Levites, are not separated from the peoples of the lands and their abominations, namely, of the Canaanite, and the Hittite, and the Perizzite, and the Jebusite, and of the Ammonites, and the Moabites, and the Egyptians, and the Amorites;

**9:2** for they have taken some of their daughters for themselves and for their sons, and they have mingled the holy seed with the peoples of the lands; indeed, the hand of the leaders and magistrates has been first in this transgression."

**9:3** And when I had heard this report, I tore my cloak and tunic, and pulled out hair from my head and beard, and sat down mourning.

**9:4** Then all who feared the word of the God of Israel gathered to me because of the transgression of those who had come from captivity, and I sat sorrowful until the evening sacrifice.

**9:5** And at the evening sacrifice, I rose up from my affliction, and with my cloak and tunic torn, I bowed my knees and spread out my hands to the Lord my God.

**9:6** And I said, "My God, I am ashamed and blush to lift up my face to You, because our iniquities have multiplied above our head, and our offenses have grown up to heaven,

**9:7** from the days of our fathers; and we ourselves also have sinned gravely up to this day, and in our iniquities we ourselves, and our kings, and our priests have been handed over into the hand of the kings of the lands, and to the sword, and to captivity, and to plunder, and to shame of face, as it is also on this day.

**9:8** And now, as it were for a little while and for a moment, our supplication has been made before the Lord our God, so that a remnant might be left to us, and a peg might be given to us in His holy place, and our God might enlighten our eyes, and give us a little life in our slavery;

**9:9** for we are slaves, and in our slavery our God has not abandoned us, but has inclined mercy upon us before the king of the Persians, to give us life, and to exalt the house of our God, and to rebuild its desolate places, and to give us a hedge in Judah and Jerusalem.

**9:10** And now, what shall we say, our God, after these things? For we have abandoned Your commandments,

**9:11** which You commanded by the hand of Your servants the prophets, saying, 'The land that you are entering so that you may possess it is an unclean land, according to the uncleanness of the peoples and of the other lands, with the abominations of those who have filled it from mouth to mouth in their defilement.

**9:12** Now therefore, do not give your daughters to their sons, and do not take their daughters for your sons, and do not seek their peace and their prosperity forever, so that you may be strengthened, and eat the good things of the land, and have your sons as heirs for all ages.'

**9:13** And after all that has come upon us in our most evil deeds and in our great offense, since You, our God, have delivered us from our iniquity, and have given us salvation as it is today,

**9:14** so that we would not turn back and nullify Your commandments, nor join in marriage with the peoples of these abominations. Are You angry with us even to complete destruction, so that You would not leave us a remnant for salvation?

**9:15** Lord God of Israel, You are righteous, for we have been left to be saved, as on this day. Look, we are before You in our offense, for no one can stand before You because of this."

## Translator Notes

- **9:8** — Taking the underlying Hebrew noun in its sense of "supplication" rather than "favor," the Vulgate reads "our supplication has been made before the Lord our God" (facta est deprecatio nostra apud Dominum Deum nostrum), whereas the Hebrew speaks of grace shown by God.
- **9:11** — "From mouth to mouth" (ab ore usque ad os) is the Vulgate's literal rendering of the Hebrew idiom meaning "from end to end"; the Vulgate also adds "and of the other lands" (ceterarumque terrarum).
- **9:13** — The Vulgate reads "have delivered us from our iniquity" (liberasti nos de iniquitate nostra), whereas the Hebrew reads "have punished us less than our iniquities deserve."
- **9:14** — Unlike the Hebrew, which begins verse 14 as a question, the Vulgate continues verse 13 with a negative purpose clause ("so that we would not turn back...") and omits the negative in the following question ("Are You angry...?" versus Hebrew "Would You not be angry...?").
