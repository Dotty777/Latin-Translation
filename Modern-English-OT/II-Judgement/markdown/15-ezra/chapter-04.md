## Text

**4:1** Now the enemies of Judah and Benjamin heard that the sons of the captivity were building a temple to the Lord God of Israel.

**4:2** And approaching Zerubbabel and the chiefs of the fathers, they said to them, "Let us build with you, for just like you, we seek your God; look, we have offered sacrifices since the days of Esarhaddon, king of Assyria, who brought us here."

**4:3** And Zerubbabel, and Joshua, and the rest of the chiefs of the fathers of Israel said to them, "It is not for you and us to build a house to our God, but we ourselves alone will build to the Lord our God, as Cyrus, king of the Persians, commanded us."

**4:4** So it happened that the people of the land hindered the hands of the people of Judah and troubled them in building.

**4:5** Moreover, they hired counselors against them to overthrow their counsel all the days of Cyrus, king of the Persians, and until the reign of Darius, king of the Persians.

**4:6** And in the reign of Ahasuerus, at the beginning of his reign, they wrote an accusation against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem.

**4:7** And in the days of Artaxerxes, Bishlam, Mithredath, and Tabeel, and the rest who were in their council, wrote to Artaxerxes, king of the Persians; and the letter of accusation was written in Syriac and was read in the Syrian language.

**4:8** Rehum Beelteem and Shimshai the scribe wrote one letter concerning Jerusalem to King Artaxerxes, of this sort:

**4:9** Rehum Beelteem, and Shimshai the scribe, and the rest of their counselors, the Dinaites, and the Apharsathchites, the Tarpelites, the Apharsites, the Archevites, the Babylonians, the Susanchites, the Dehavites, and the Elamites,

**4:10** and the rest of the nations whom the great and glorious Osnappar deported and made to dwell in the cities of Samaria and in the remaining regions beyond the river in peace

**4:11** (this is the copy of the letter that they sent to him): "To King Artaxerxes, your servants, the men who are beyond the river, send greetings.

**4:12** Let it be known to the king that the Jews who came up from you to us have come to Jerusalem, the rebellious and most wicked city, which they are building, erecting its walls and repairing the house walls.

**4:13** Now therefore let it be known to the king that if that city is built and its walls are restored, they will not pay tribute, toll, or yearly revenues, and this harm will reach even to the kings.

**4:14** Now we, mindful of the salt that we have eaten in the palace, and because we consider it wrong to see injuries to the king, have therefore sent and informed the king,

**4:15** so that you may search in the books of the histories of your fathers, and you will find it written in the records, and you will know that that city is a rebellious city, and harmful to kings and provinces, and wars have been stirred up in it from ancient days; for which reason the city itself was also destroyed.

**4:16** We inform the king that if that city is built and its walls are restored, you will have no possession beyond the river."

**4:17** The king sent word to Rehum Beelteem, and Shimshai the scribe, and to the rest who were in their council, inhabitants of Samaria, and to the others beyond the river, sending greetings and peace:

**4:18** "The accusation that you sent to us has been clearly read before me,

**4:19** and a command was given by me, and they searched and found that that city from ancient days has rebelled against kings, and that seditions and battles have been stirred up in it;

**4:20** for very mighty kings have also been in Jerusalem, who also ruled over the entire region that is beyond the river; they also received tribute, toll, and revenues.

**4:21** Now therefore hear the decree: stop those men, so that that city may not be built until perhaps an order is given by me.

**4:22** See to it that you do not carry this out negligently, and that evil does not gradually grow against the kings."

**4:23** Therefore the copy of the edict of King Artaxerxes was read before Rehum Beelteem, and Shimshai the scribe, and their counselors; and they went in haste to Jerusalem to the Jews, and stopped them by arm and strength.

**4:24** Then the work of the house of the Lord in Jerusalem was suspended, and it was not done until the second year of the reign of Darius, king of the Persians.

## Translator Notes

- **4:2** — The Vulgate reads ecce nos immolavimus victimas ("look, we have offered sacrifices"), omitting both "to Him" (Hebrew Qere) and "not" (Hebrew Ketiv).
- **4:3** — The Vulgate reads Domino Deo nostro ("to the Lord our God"), where the Hebrew reads "to the Lord, the God of Israel."
- **4:7** — The Vulgate adds accusationis ("of accusation"), renders Aramaic as syriace / sermone syro ("Syriac" / "the Syrian language"), and reads legebatur ("was read") where the Hebrew has "translated."
- **4:8** — Latin Reum Beelteem treats the Aramaic official title be'el-te'em ("commander" or "chancellor") as part of Rehum's proper name (likewise in 4:9, 4:17, and 4:23).
- **4:10** — The Vulgate renders the Aramaic epistolary transition uk'enet ("and now") as in pace ("in peace"), and similarly includes et pacem ("and peace") in 4:17.
