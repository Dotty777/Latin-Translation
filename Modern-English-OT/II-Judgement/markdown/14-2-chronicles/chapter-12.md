## Text

**12:1** And when the kingdom of Rehoboam had been strengthened and made firm, he abandoned the law of the Lord, and all Israel with him.

**12:2** Now in the fifth year of the reign of Rehoboam, Shishak king of Egypt went up against Jerusalem (because they had sinned against the Lord),

**12:3** with one thousand two hundred chariots and sixty thousand horsemen; and the multitude that had come with him out of Egypt was without number, namely Libyans, Troglodytes, and Ethiopians.

**12:4** And he captured the heavily fortified cities in Judah, and came all the way to Jerusalem.

**12:5** Then Shemaiah the prophet went in to Rehoboam and the leaders of Judah who had gathered in Jerusalem, fleeing from Shishak, and said to them, "This is what the Lord says: 'You have abandoned Me, and I have abandoned you in the hand of Shishak.'"

**12:6** And the leaders of Israel and the king were dismayed, and they said, "The Lord is just."

**12:7** And when the Lord saw that they had humbled themselves, the word of the Lord came to Shemaiah, saying, "Because they have humbled themselves, I will not destroy them, and I will give them a little help, and My wrath will not drip upon Jerusalem by the hand of Shishak.

**12:8** Nevertheless, they will serve him, so that they may know the difference between My service and the service of the kingdom of the lands."

**12:9** Therefore Shishak king of Egypt withdrew from Jerusalem, after taking away the treasures of the house of the Lord and of the house of the king; and he carried off everything with him, even the golden shields that Solomon had made,

**12:10** in place of which the king made bronze ones, and handed them over to the commanders of the shield-bearers who guarded the vestibule of the palace.

**12:11** And whenever the king entered the house of the Lord, the shield-bearers would come and carry them, and would bring them back again to their armory.

**12:12** Nevertheless, because they humbled themselves, the anger of the Lord turned away from them, and they were not completely destroyed, since good works were also found in Judah.

**12:13** Therefore King Rehoboam grew strong in Jerusalem and reigned; now he was forty-one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city that the Lord chose to establish His name there, from all the tribes of Israel; and his mother's name was Naamah the Ammonitess.

**12:14** And he did evil, and did not prepare his heart to seek the Lord.

**12:15** Now the deeds of Rehoboam, the first and the last, are written in the books of Shemaiah the prophet and of Iddo the seer, and carefully set forth; and Rehoboam and Jeroboam fought against each other all their days.

**12:16** And Rehoboam slept with his fathers, and was buried in the city of David; and Abijah his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **12:3** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate renders the Hebrew Sukkiim as "Troglodytes" (Troglodytae, cave-dwellers) and the Cushites as "Ethiopians" (Aethiopes).
- **12:7** — The Vulgate uses the vivid verb "drip" (stillabit) for God's wrath, where the Hebrew has "be poured out."
- **12:9** — A notable Vulgate-distinct reading: the Latin states that Shishak "withdrew from Jerusalem" (Recessit ... ab Jerusalem), following the narrative progression after 12:4, whereas the Hebrew reads "came up against Jerusalem."
- **12:12** — The Vulgate uses plural forms ("because they humbled themselves ... from them ... they were not completely destroyed") referring to king and people together, where the Hebrew uses the singular for Rehoboam, and renders Hebrew "good things" as "good works" (opera bona).
- **12:15** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew phrase for genealogical enrollment as "carefully set forth" (diligenter exposita).
