## Text

**33:1** Manasseh was twelve years of age when he began to reign, and for fifty-five years he reigned in Jerusalem.

**33:2** Yet he did evil before the Lord, according to the abominations of the nations that the Lord overthrew before the sons of Israel.

**33:3** Turning back, he restored the high places that his father Hezekiah had demolished; he erected altars to the Baals, made sacred groves, and worshiped the entire host of heaven and served it.

**33:4** He also constructed altars in the house of the Lord, concerning which the Lord had said, "In Jerusalem My name will be forever."

**33:5** Indeed, he built them for the entire army of heaven in the two courtyards of the house of the Lord.

**33:6** He also caused his sons to pass through fire in the valley of Ben-hinnom; he observed dreams, pursued omens, devoted himself to wicked arts, kept magicians and enchanters with him, and carried out many evil deeds before the Lord to provoke Him.

**33:7** He also set a carved and cast image in the house of God, concerning which God spoke to David and to his son Solomon, saying, "In this house, and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen out of all the tribes of Israel, I will place My name perpetually.

**33:8** And I will not cause the foot of Israel to be moved from the land that I delivered to their fathers, provided only that they take care to carry out what I have commanded them, and the entire law, and the ceremonies and judgments, through the hand of Moses."

**33:9** Thus Manasseh led Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem astray, so that they practiced evil beyond all the nations that the Lord had overthrown from before the face of the sons of Israel.

**33:10** And the Lord spoke to him and to his people, yet they refused to pay heed.

**33:11** For that reason He brought upon them the commanders of the army of the king of the Assyrians; and they seized Manasseh, and bound in chains and shackles, they led him away into Babylon.

**33:12** After he was brought into dire distress, he prayed to the Lord his God and repented greatly before the God of his fathers.

**33:13** He pleaded with Him and implored earnestly; and He heard his prayer and brought him back to Jerusalem into his kingdom, and Manasseh recognized that the Lord Himself was God.

**33:14** After these events he constructed a wall outside the city of David, to the west of Gihon in the valley, from the entrance of the Fish Gate all around as far as Ophel, and raised it very high; and he stationed commanders of the army in all the fortified cities of Judah.

**33:15** He also removed the foreign gods and the idol from the house of the Lord, together with the altars that he had built on the mountain of the house of the Lord and in Jerusalem, and cast them all outside the city.

**33:16** Furthermore, he restored the altar of the Lord and sacrificed upon it victims, peace offerings, and praise; and he commanded Judah to serve the Lord God of Israel.

**33:17** Even so, the people still offered sacrifices at the high places to the Lord their God.

**33:18** Now the rest of the deeds of Manasseh, and his supplication to his God, and also the words of the seers who spoke to him in the name of the Lord God of Israel, are contained in the records of the kings of Israel.

**33:19** His prayer as well and its favorable hearing, and all his sins and acts of contempt, and also the sites where he built high places and made sacred groves and statues before he repented, are written in the records of Hozai.

**33:20** So Manasseh slept with his fathers, and they buried him in his own house; and his son Amon reigned in his place.

**33:21** Amon was twenty-two years of age when he began to reign, and for two years he reigned in Jerusalem.

**33:22** And he did evil in the sight of the Lord, just as his father Manasseh had done; and to all the idols that Manasseh had crafted, he sacrificed and rendered service.

**33:23** Yet he did not show reverence before the face of the Lord, as his father Manasseh had shown reverence, and he committed far greater offenses.

**33:24** And after his servants conspired against him, they killed him in his own house.

**33:25** Then the remaining multitude of the people, having put to death those who had struck down Amon, established his son Josiah as king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **33:3** — Vulgate 'lucos' ("sacred groves") renders the Hebrew 'asherim' (sacred poles or Asherah images), a characteristic Vulgate rendering also used in 33:19.
- **33:6** — Vulgate 'observabat somnia' ("observed dreams") interprets the Hebrew divination verb as dream-omen observation.
- **33:11** — Vulgate 'catenis atque compedibus' ("in chains and shackles") renders the Hebrew "with hooks and bronze fetters" without the specific reference to hooks.
- **33:12** — Vulgate 'egit pœnitentiam' ("repented," repeated in 33:19) renders the Hebrew verb meaning "humbled himself."
- **33:23** — Vulgate 'non est reveritus faciem Domini' ("did not show reverence before the face of the Lord") differs from the Hebrew, which states that Amon "did not humble himself before the Lord."
