## Text

**1:1** So Solomon, the son of David, was strengthened in his kingdom, and the Lord his God was with him and magnified him to the heights.

**1:2** And Solomon gave orders to all Israel: to the tribunes and centurions, the leaders and judges of all Israel, and the heads of the families.

**1:3** And he went with the whole multitude to the high place of Gibeon, where the tent of the covenant of God was, which Moses the servant of God made in the wilderness.

**1:4** However, David had brought the ark of God from Kiriath-jearim to the place that he had prepared for it and where he had pitched a tent for it, that is, in Jerusalem.

**1:5** The bronze altar also, which Bezalel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, had fashioned, was there before the tent of the Lord; and Solomon and the whole assembly sought it out.

**1:6** And Solomon went up to the bronze altar before the tent of the covenant of the Lord, and offered one thousand sacrifices on it.

**1:7** Now look, on that very night God appeared to him, saying, "Ask what you wish, so that I may give it to you."

**1:8** And Solomon said to God, "You have shown great mercy to David my father, and have appointed me king in his place.

**1:9** Now therefore, Lord God, let Your word be fulfilled that You promised to David my father, for You have made me king over Your numerous people, who are as innumerable as the dust of the earth.

**1:10** Give me wisdom and understanding, so that I may go in and go out before Your people; for who can worthily judge this people of Yours, who are so great?"

**1:11** Then God said to Solomon, "Because this pleased your heart more, and you did not ask for riches, possessions, and glory, nor the lives of those who hated you, nor even very many days of life, but have requested wisdom and knowledge so that you may be able to judge My people over whom I have appointed you king,

**1:12** wisdom and knowledge have been given to you; moreover, I will give you riches, possessions, and glory, such that no one among the kings, before you or after you, will have been like you."

**1:13** So Solomon came from the high place of Gibeon to Jerusalem, before the tent of the covenant, and reigned over Israel.

**1:14** And he gathered for himself chariots and horsemen, and he came to have one thousand four hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen; and he placed them in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem.

**1:15** And the king supplied silver and gold in Jerusalem like stones, and cedars like the sycamores that grow in the plains in great abundance.

**1:16** Now horses were brought to him from Egypt and from Kue by the king's merchants, who went and bought them for a price:

**1:17** a four-horse chariot for six hundred pieces of silver, and a horse for one hundred fifty; similarly, purchasing was carried on from all the kingdoms of the Hittites and from the kings of Syria.

**1:18** Now Solomon resolved to build a house for the name of the Lord, and a palace for himself.

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — Latin uses Roman military titles (tribunis, et centurionibus, 'tribunes and centurions') for the Hebrew commanders of thousands and hundreds, and reads praecepit ('gave orders') where the Hebrew has 'spoke.'
- **1:3** — Latin tabernaculum foederis ('tent of the covenant,' also in 1:6, 1:13) is a distinctly Vulgate rendering of Hebrew 'tent of meeting.'
- **1:13** — Latin reads in Jerusalem coram tabernaculo foederis ('to Jerusalem, before the tent of the covenant'), placing coram ('before') after in Jerusalem rather than 'from before the tent of meeting' as in the Hebrew.
- **1:17** — Distinctly Vulgate reading: Latin de universis regnis Hethaeorum, et a regibus Syriae emptio celebrabatur describes purchasing horses and four-horse chariots from all the kingdoms of the Hittites and from the kings of Syria, whereas the Hebrew describes exporting them to the kings of the Hittites and Aram.
- **1:18** — In the Vulgate chapter division, 1:18 corresponds to 2:1 in the Hebrew Masoretic Text (shifting the verse numbers of chapter 2 by one); Latin also renders Hebrew 'house for his kingdom' as palatium ('palace').
