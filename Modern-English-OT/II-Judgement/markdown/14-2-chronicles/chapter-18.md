## Text

**18:1** So Jehoshaphat was very wealthy and renowned, and he was joined by marriage alliance to Ahab.

**18:2** And after some years he went down to him in Samaria. At his arrival, Ahab slaughtered very many rams and oxen for him and for the people who had come with him, and he persuaded him to go up to Ramoth-gilead.

**18:3** And Ahab king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat king of Judah, "Come with me to Ramoth-gilead." And he answered him, "As I am, so are you; as your people are, so also are my people; and we will be with you in the war."

**18:4** And Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, "Consult, I ask you, the word of the Lord right now."

**18:5** Therefore the king of Israel gathered four hundred men of the prophets and said to them, "Should we go to Ramoth-gilead to wage war, or refrain?" And they said, "Go up, and God will deliver it into the hand of the king."

**18:6** And Jehoshaphat said, "Is there not a prophet of the Lord here, so that we may inquire of him also?"

**18:7** And the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "There is one man from whom we can seek the will of the Lord, but I hate him, because he does not prophesy good for me, but evil at all times; he is Micaiah the son of Imlah." And Jehoshaphat said, "Do not speak in this way, king."

**18:8** So the king of Israel called one of the eunuchs and said to him, "Call Micaiah the son of Imlah quickly."

**18:9** Now the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah were each sitting on his throne, clothed in royal attire; and they were sitting on the threshing floor beside the gate of Samaria, and all the prophets were prophesying before them.

**18:10** And Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah made iron horns for himself and said, "This is what the Lord says: 'With these you will toss Syria until you crush it.'"

**18:11** And all the prophets were prophesying in the same way and saying, "Go up to Ramoth-gilead, and you will prosper, and the Lord will deliver them into the hand of the king."

**18:12** Now the messenger who had gone to call Micaiah said to him, "Look, the words of all the prophets with one mouth announce good things to the king; therefore I ask you that your word also not disagree with them, and that you speak favorable things."

**18:13** Micaiah answered him, "As the Lord lives, whatever my God says to me, this I will speak."

**18:14** So he came to the king. And the king said to him, "Micaiah, should we go to Ramoth-gilead to wage war, or refrain?" And he answered him, "Go up, for all things will turn out favorably, and the enemies will be delivered into your hands."

**18:15** And the king said, "Again and again I adjure you to speak to me nothing except what is true in the name of the Lord."

**18:16** And he said, "I saw all Israel scattered on the mountains, like sheep without a shepherd; and the Lord said, 'These have no masters; let each one return to his own house in peace.'"

**18:17** And the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "Did I not tell you that this man would not prophesy anything good for me, but the things that are evil?"

**18:18** And he said, "Therefore hear the word of the Lord: I saw the Lord sitting on His throne, and all the host of heaven standing beside Him on the right and on the left.

**18:19** And the Lord said, 'Who will deceive Ahab king of Israel, so that he may go up and fall at Ramoth-gilead?' And while one spoke in this way and another in another,

**18:20** a spirit came forward and stood before the Lord and said, 'I will deceive him.' And the Lord said to him, 'By what means will you deceive him?'

**18:21** And he answered, 'I will go out and be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.' And the Lord said, 'You will deceive and prevail; go out and do so.'

**18:22** Now therefore, look, the Lord has put a spirit of lying in the mouth of all your prophets, and the Lord has spoken evil things concerning you."

**18:23** Then Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah approached and struck Micaiah's cheek and said, "By what way did the Spirit of the Lord pass from me to speak to you?"

**18:24** And Micaiah said, "You yourself will see on that day, when you enter chamber after chamber to hide yourself."

**18:25** Then the king of Israel commanded, saying, "Take Micaiah and lead him to Amon the ruler of the city and to Joash the son of Amelech.

**18:26** And you will say, 'This is what the king says: Put this man in prison, and give him a little bread and a very little water, until I return in peace.'"

**18:27** And Micaiah said, "If you return in peace, the Lord has not spoken in me." And he said, "Hear, all peoples."

**18:28** Therefore the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramoth-gilead.

**18:29** And the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "I will change my attire and go into battle in this way, but you put on your own garments." And having changed his attire, the king of Israel came to the war.

**18:30** Now the king of Syria had commanded the commanders of his cavalry, saying, "Do not fight against the smallest or against the greatest, except against the king of Israel alone."

**18:31** Therefore, when the leaders of the cavalry saw Jehoshaphat, they said, "This is the king of Israel." And they surrounded him, fighting; but he cried out to the Lord, and He helped him and turned them away from him.

**18:32** For when the commanders of the cavalry saw that he was not the king of Israel, they left him.

**18:33** Now it happened that one of the people shot an arrow at random and struck the king of Israel between the neck and the shoulder blades. And he said to his charioteer, "Turn your hand around and lead me out of the battle line, because I am wounded."

**18:34** And the battle came to an end on that day; moreover, the king of Israel stood in his chariot facing the Syrians until evening, and he died as the sun was setting.

## Translator Notes

- **18:10** — Vulgate "toss" (ventilabis, literally "winnow" or "toss into the air") vividly renders the Hebrew verb meaning "gore" with horns.
- **18:15** — Vulgate "Again and again I adjure you" (Iterum atque iterum te adjuro) renders the Hebrew rhetorical question ("How many times must I make you swear?") as a solemn statement.
- **18:25** — Vulgate "son of Amelech" (filium Amelech) transliterates the Hebrew title hammelekh ("the king," i.e., "the king's son") as a personal name. In v. 26, Vulgate "a little bread and a very little water" renders the Hebrew "bread of affliction and water of affliction" as meager rations.
- **18:30** — Vulgate "cavalry" (equitatus) translates the Hebrew term for "chariots" throughout vv. 30-32.
- **18:33** — Vulgate "between the neck and the shoulder blades" (inter cervicem et scapulas) differs from the Hebrew "between the joints and the breastplate"; likewise in v. 34, Vulgate "the battle came to an end" (finita est pugna) contrasts with the Hebrew "the battle grew fierce."
