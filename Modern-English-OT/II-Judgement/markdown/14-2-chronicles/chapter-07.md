## Text

**7:1** And when Solomon had finished pouring out petitions, fire came down from heaven and devoured the burnt offerings and the sacrifices, and the majesty of the Lord filled the house.

**7:2** And the priests were not able to enter the temple of the Lord, because the majesty of the Lord had filled the temple of the Lord.

**7:3** Moreover, all the sons of Israel were watching the fire coming down and the glory of the Lord upon the house; and falling face down to the ground upon the pavement laid with stone, they worshiped and praised the Lord, because He is good, because His mercy is forever.

**7:4** Now the king and all the people were offering sacrifices before the Lord.

**7:5** So King Solomon slaughtered offerings: twenty-two thousand oxen and one hundred twenty thousand rams; and the king and the entire people dedicated the house of God.

**7:6** And the priests were standing in their duties, and the Levites at the instruments of the songs of the Lord, which King David made to praise the Lord, "For His mercy is eternal," singing the hymns of David by their hands; moreover, the priests were sounding trumpets before them, and all Israel was standing.

**7:7** Solomon also consecrated the middle of the courtyard before the temple of the Lord, for he had offered there the burnt offerings and the fat of the peace offerings, because the bronze altar that he had made could not hold the burnt offerings and the sacrifices and the fat.

**7:8** So Solomon observed the festival at that time for seven days, and all Israel with him, a very great assembly, from the entrance of Hamath to the brook of Egypt.

**7:9** And on the eighth day he held a convocation, because he had dedicated the altar for seven days and had celebrated the festival for seven days.

**7:10** Therefore, on the twenty-third day of the seventh month, he sent the peoples away to their tents, joyful and glad over the good that the Lord had done for David, and for Solomon, and for His people Israel.

**7:11** And Solomon completed the house of the Lord and the house of the king, and everything that he had planned in his heart to do in the house of the Lord and in his own house, and he prospered.

**7:12** Now the Lord appeared to him by night and said, "I have heard your prayer, and I have chosen this place for Myself as a house of sacrifice.

**7:13** If I shut heaven and rain does not fall, and I command and order the locust to devour the land, and I send pestilence among My people,

**7:14** and My people, upon whom My name has been invoked, turning back, plead with Me and seek out My face and repent from their most wicked ways, then I will hear from heaven, and be merciful toward their sins, and heal their land.

**7:15** My eyes also will be open, and My ears alert to the prayer of the one who prays in this place.

**7:16** For I have chosen and consecrated this place, so that My name may be there forever, and My eyes and My heart may remain there for all days.

**7:17** As for you also, if you walk before Me just as your father David walked, and act according to everything that I have commanded you, and keep My statutes and judgments,

**7:18** I will raise up the throne of your kingdom, just as I promised to your father David, saying, 'A man will not be taken away from your lineage to be ruler in Israel.'

**7:19** But if you turn away and abandon My statutes and My precepts that I have set before you, and going off you serve foreign gods and worship them,

**7:20** I will uproot you from My land that I have given to you, and this house, which I have consecrated for My name, I will cast away from My presence, and I will hand it over as a parable and as an example to all peoples.

**7:21** And this house will become a proverb to all who pass by, and they will say in astonishment, 'Why has the Lord done so to this land and to this house?'

**7:22** And they will answer, 'Because they abandoned the Lord God of their fathers, who brought them out of the land of Egypt, and took hold of foreign gods, and worshiped them and revered them; therefore all these evils have come upon them.'"

## Translator Notes

- **7:5** — The Vulgate specifies arietum ("rams") where the Hebrew text uses the broader term for sheep or flocks.
- **7:6** — The phrase "by their hands" (per manus suas) literally reproduces a Hebrew idiom meaning "through their ministry" or "under their direction."
- **7:14** — The Vulgate reads conversus ("turning back") where the Hebrew text reads "humble themselves," and intensifies "wicked ways" with the superlative pessimis ("most wicked").
- **7:21** — The Vulgate reads erit in proverbium ("will become a proverb"), differing from the Hebrew text ("which was exalted").
