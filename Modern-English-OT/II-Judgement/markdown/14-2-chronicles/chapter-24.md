## Text

**24:1** Joash was seven years old when he began to reign, and he reigned forty years in Jerusalem; the name of his mother was Zibiah of Beersheba.

**24:2** And he did what is good before the Lord all the days of Jehoiada the priest.

**24:3** Now Jehoiada took two wives for him, by whom he fathered sons and daughters.

**24:4** After these things, it pleased Joash to restore the house of the Lord.

**24:5** And he gathered the priests and the Levites and said to them, "Go out to the cities of Judah, and collect money from all Israel for the repairs of the temple of your God year by year, and do this quickly." However, the Levites acted rather negligently.

**24:6** So the king summoned Jehoiada the chief and said to him, "Why did you not take care to compel the Levites to bring in from Judah and from Jerusalem the money that was decreed by Moses, the servant of the Lord, that the whole multitude of Israel should bring it into the tabernacle of the testimony?"

**24:7** For the most wicked Athaliah and her sons destroyed the house of God, and with all the things that had been consecrated in the temple of the Lord they adorned the shrine of the Baals.

**24:8** Therefore the king commanded, and they made a chest and placed it beside the gate of the house of the Lord, on the outside.

**24:9** And it was proclaimed in Judah and Jerusalem that each one should bring to the Lord the payment that Moses, the servant of God, decreed upon all Israel in the wilderness.

**24:10** And all the leaders and all the people rejoiced; and entering, they brought it into the chest of the Lord and put it in so that it was filled.

**24:11** And whenever it was time to bring the chest before the king by the hands of the Levites (for they saw much money), the king's scribe and the one whom the chief priest had appointed would come in and pour out the money that was in the chest, while they carried the chest back to its place; and thus they did day by day. And a vast amount of money was gathered,

**24:12** which the king and Jehoiada gave to those who were in charge of the works of the house of the Lord; and they hired with it stonecutters and craftsmen of every kind of work to restore the house of the Lord, and also smiths of iron and bronze, so that what had begun to fall might be shored up.

**24:13** And those who were working acted diligently, and the scar of the walls was closed over by their hands; and they raised up the house of the Lord to its former state and made it stand firmly.

**24:14** And when they had completed all the works, they brought the remaining part of the money before the king and Jehoiada, from which were made vessels of the temple for the service and for burnt offerings, bowls also, and other vessels of gold and silver; burnt offerings were offered in the house of the Lord continually all the days of Jehoiada.

**24:15** Now Jehoiada grew old, full of days, and he died when he was one hundred thirty years old;

**24:16** and they buried him in the city of David with the kings, because he had done good toward Israel and toward his house.

**24:17** Now after Jehoiada died, the leaders of Judah came in and bowed down to the king; and he, charmed by their deference, yielded to them.

**24:18** And they abandoned the temple of the Lord God of their fathers, and served sacred groves and carved images; and wrath came against Judah and Jerusalem because of this sin.

**24:19** And He kept sending prophets to them so that they might return to the Lord, yet they refused to listen to them testifying.

**24:20** Then the Spirit of God clothed Zechariah the priest, son of Jehoiada, and he stood in the sight of the people and said to them, "This is what the Lord God says: 'Why do you transgress the command of the Lord, which will not profit you, and why have you abandoned the Lord, so that He has abandoned you?'"

**24:21** And gathering together against him, they threw stones according to the king's command in the court of the house of the Lord.

**24:22** And King Joash did not remember the mercy that Jehoiada, his father, had shown to him, but he put his son to death. And as he was dying, he said, "May the Lord see and require it."

**24:23** And when a year had elapsed, the army of Syria went up against him, and came into Judah and Jerusalem, and put all the leaders of the people to death, and they sent all the plunder to the king in Damascus.

**24:24** And indeed, although a very small number of Syrians had come, the Lord delivered a vast multitude into their hands, because they had abandoned the Lord God of their fathers; upon Joash also they executed shameful judgments.

**24:25** And departing, they left him in severe illnesses; then his own servants rose up against him in vengeance for the blood of the son of Jehoiada the priest, and killed him on his bed, and he died; and they buried him in the city of David, but not in the tombs of the kings.

**24:26** Now those who conspired against him were Zabad son of Shimeath the Ammonitess, and Jehozabad son of Shimrith the Moabitess.

**24:27** Now his sons, and the sum of money that had been gathered together under him, and the restoration of the house of God are written more carefully in the book of the kings; and his son Amaziah reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **24:13** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew healing metaphor for temple repair vividly as 'the scar of the walls was closed over by their hands' (obducebatur parietum cicatrix per manus eorum).
- **24:16** — Unlike the Hebrew ('toward God and His house'), the Vulgate omits 'God' here, reading simply 'toward Israel and toward his house' (cum Israel, et cum domo ejus).
- **24:20** — By placing 'the priest' (sacerdotem) in the accusative, the Vulgate explicitly applies the priestly title to Zechariah himself; it also reads 'in the sight of the people' (in conspectu populi) rather than 'above the people' and 'the Lord God' (Dominus Deus) rather than 'God'.
- **24:25** — The Vulgate reads singular 'the son of Jehoiada the priest' (filii Jojadae sacerdotis), matching vv. 20-22, where the Masoretic Hebrew has the plural 'sons'.
- **24:27** — Rendering the Hebrew term for 'burden/oracle' in a financial sense and 'commentary' adverbially, the Vulgate speaks of 'the sum of money that had been gathered together under him' (summa pecuniae quae adunata fuerat sub eo) and states that these matters 'are written more carefully in the book of the kings' (scripta sunt diligentius in libro regum).
