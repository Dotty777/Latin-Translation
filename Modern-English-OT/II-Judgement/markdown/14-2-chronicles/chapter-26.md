## Text

**26:1** Now all the people of Judah made his son Uzziah, sixteen years old, king in place of Amaziah his father.

**26:2** He himself built Elath and restored it to the dominion of Judah, after the king slept with his fathers.

**26:3** Uzziah was sixteen years old when he began to reign, and he reigned for fifty-two years in Jerusalem; his mother's name was Jecoliah from Jerusalem.

**26:4** And he did what was right in the eyes of the Lord, according to all that Amaziah his father had done.

**26:5** And he sought out the Lord in the days of Zechariah, who understood and saw God; and while he sought the Lord, He directed him in all things.

**26:6** Then he went out and fought against the Philistines, and tore down the wall of Gath, and the wall of Jabneh, and the wall of Ashdod; he also built towns in Ashdod and among the Philistines.

**26:7** And God helped him against the Philistines, and against the Arabs who lived in Gur-baal, and against the Ammonites.

**26:8** And the Ammonites weighed out gifts to Uzziah, and his name became widely known as far as the entrance of Egypt because of his frequent victories.

**26:9** And Uzziah built towers in Jerusalem over the Corner Gate and over the Valley Gate, and others on the same side of the wall, and fortified them.

**26:10** He also constructed towers in the wilderness and dug very many cisterns, because he had much livestock both in the plains and in the vastness of the desert; he also had vineyards and vinedressers in the mountains and in Carmel, for he was a man devoted to agriculture.

**26:11** Now there was an army of his warriors who marched out to battles under the hand of Jeiel the scribe and Maaseiah the teacher, and under the hand of Hananiah, who was one of the king's commanders.

**26:12** And the entire number of the leaders by families, valiant men, was two thousand six hundred.

**26:13** And under them was the entire army of three hundred and seven thousand five hundred, who were fit for wars and fought for the king against adversaries.

**26:14** Uzziah also prepared for them, that is, for the whole army, shields, and spears, and helmets, and coats of mail, and bows and slings for hurling stones.

**26:15** And he made in Jerusalem machines of various kinds, which he placed on the towers and on the corners of the walls to launch arrows and large stones. And his name went out far, because the Lord was helping him and had strengthened him.

**26:16** But when he had been strengthened, his heart was lifted up to his own ruin, and he neglected the Lord his God; and entering the temple of the Lord, he wanted to burn incense on the altar of incense.

**26:17** And immediately Azariah the priest, having entered after him, and with him eighty priests of the Lord, very valiant men,

**26:18** resisted the king and said, "It is not your office, Uzziah, to burn incense to the Lord, but that of the priests, that is, the sons of Aaron, who have been consecrated for this kind of ministry. Go out from the sanctuary; do not show contempt, because this will not be credited to you for glory by the Lord God."

**26:19** And Uzziah became angry, and holding a censer in his hand to burn incense, he threatened the priests. And immediately leprosy broke out on his forehead before the priests, in the house of the Lord, by the altar of incense.

**26:20** And when Azariah the high priest and all the other priests looked at him, they saw the leprosy on his forehead and hastily drove him out. Indeed, he himself, terrified, hurried to go out, because he had immediately felt the stroke of the Lord.

**26:21** Therefore King Uzziah was a leper until the day of his death, and he lived in a separate house, full of leprosy, because of which he had been cast out from the house of the Lord. Meanwhile, Jotham his son governed the king's house and judged the people of the land.

**26:22** Now Isaiah son of Amoz, the prophet, wrote the rest of the accounts of Uzziah, first and last.

**26:23** And Uzziah slept with his fathers, and they buried him in the field of the royal tombs because he was a leper; and Jotham his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **26:5** — Latin intelligentis et videntis Deum literally reads "who understood and saw God," where the Masoretic Hebrew reads "who had understanding in the visions of God."
- **26:7** — The Vulgate reads "against the Ammonites" (matching 26:8), whereas the Masoretic Hebrew reads "against the Meunites."
- **26:8** — The Vulgate reads "because of his frequent victories" (propter crebras victorias), where the Masoretic Hebrew reads "for he became exceedingly strong."
- **26:11** — Latin doctoris ("teacher" or "instructor") is Jerome's rendering of the Hebrew title for an administrative official (shoter).
- **26:18** — Latin ne contempseris gives a negative command ("do not show contempt"), whereas the Masoretic Hebrew gives a statement ("for you have acted unfaithfully").
