## Text

**5:1** Therefore Solomon brought in all the things that David his father had vowed; he placed the silver, and the gold, and all the vessels in the treasuries of the house of God.

**5:2** After these things he assembled the elders of Israel, and all the leaders of the tribes, and the heads of the families from the sons of Israel to Jerusalem, so that they might bring the ark of the covenant of the Lord from the city of David, which is Zion.

**5:3** So all the men of Israel came to the king on the festival day of the seventh month.

**5:4** And when all the elders of Israel had come, the Levites carried the ark,

**5:5** and they brought it in, and all the furnishings of the tabernacle. Moreover, the priests with the Levites carried the vessels of the sanctuary that were in the tabernacle.

**5:6** Now King Solomon, and the entire assembly of Israel, and all who were assembled before the ark, were sacrificing rams and oxen without any number, for so great was the multitude of the sacrifices.

**5:7** And the priests brought the ark of the covenant of the Lord into its place, that is, to the inner sanctuary of the temple, into the Holy of Holies, beneath the wings of the cherubim,

**5:8** so that the cherubim spread out their wings over the place in which the ark had been placed, and covered the ark itself along with its poles.

**5:9** Now the ends of the poles by which the ark was carried, because they were a little longer, were visible before the inner sanctuary; but if anyone was a little outside, he could not see them. So the ark has been there until the present day.

**5:10** And there was nothing else in the ark except the two tablets that Moses had placed at Horeb, when the Lord gave the law to the sons of Israel as they came out of Egypt.

**5:11** Now when the priests had come out of the sanctuary (for all the priests who could be found there were sanctified, and at that time the courses and the order of the ministries had not yet been divided among them),

**5:12** both the Levites and the singers, that is, those who were under Asaph, and those who were under Heman, and those who were under Jeduthun, their sons and their brothers, clothed in fine linen, were resounding with cymbals, and harps, and lyres, standing at the eastern side of the altar; and with them were one hundred and twenty priests sounding trumpets.

**5:13** Therefore, when all of them together were sounding in unison with trumpets, and voice, and cymbals, and instruments, and various kinds of music, and lifting their voice on high, the sound was heard far away, so that when they had begun to praise the Lord and to say, "Give thanks to the Lord, because He is good, because His mercy is forever," the house of God was filled with a cloud,

**5:14** and the priests could not stand and minister because of the thick darkness. For the glory of the Lord had filled the house of God.

## Translator Notes

- **5:5** — Latin omnem paraturam tabernaculi ("all the furnishings of the tabernacle") renders Hebrew "the tent of meeting," and "the priests with the Levites" separates two groups where the Hebrew reads "the Levitical priests."
- **5:7** — Latin oraculum (literally "oracle," the place of divine utterance, also in 4:20 and 5:9) is the Vulgate's regular rendering of Hebrew debir, the inner sanctuary of the temple.
- **5:10** — The Vulgate reads "when the Lord gave the law" (legem dedit) where the Hebrew reads "when the LORD made a covenant."
- **5:11** — The Vulgate states that the priestly courses and order of ministries had not yet been divided among them at that time, whereas the Hebrew indicates that the priests consecrated themselves without keeping to their divisions.
- **5:13** — The Vulgate expands the musical account with "voice," "instruments, and various kinds of music," and "the sound was heard far away," and in 5:14 shifts from "cloud" (nube) to "thick darkness" (caliginem).
