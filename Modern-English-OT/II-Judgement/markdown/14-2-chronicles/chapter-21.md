## Text

**21:1** Now Jehoshaphat slept with his fathers, and he was buried with them in the city of David; and his son Jehoram reigned in his place.

**21:2** He had brothers, sons of Jehoshaphat: Azariah, and Jehiel, and Zechariah, and Azariah, and Michael, and Shephatiah; all these were sons of Jehoshaphat king of Judah.

**21:3** And their father gave them many gifts of silver and gold, and revenues, along with very strongly fortified cities in Judah; but the kingdom he handed over to Jehoram, because he was the firstborn.

**21:4** Therefore Jehoram rose up over the kingdom of his father; and when he had established himself, he killed all his brothers with the sword, and some of the princes of Israel as well.

**21:5** Jehoram was thirty-two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem.

**21:6** And he walked in the ways of the kings of Israel, just as the house of Ahab had done, for the daughter of Ahab was his wife; and he did evil in the sight of the Lord.

**21:7** Yet the Lord was unwilling to destroy the house of David because of the covenant that He had entered into with him, and because He had promised to give a lamp to him and to his sons at all times.

**21:8** In those days Edom rebelled so as not to be subject to Judah, and appointed a king for itself.

**21:9** And when Jehoram had crossed over with his princes and all the cavalry that was with him, he rose up by night and struck Edom, which had surrounded him, and all the commanders of its cavalry.

**21:10** Nevertheless, Edom rebelled so as not to be under the dominion of Judah up to this day; at that time Libnah also withdrew so as not to be under his hand. For he had abandoned the Lord God of his fathers;

**21:11** moreover, he also built high places in the cities of Judah, and caused the inhabitants of Jerusalem to commit fornication, and Judah to transgress.

**21:12** And a letter was brought to him from Elijah the prophet, in which it was written, "This is what the Lord God of your father David says: 'Because you have not walked in the ways of your father Jehoshaphat, and in the ways of Asa king of Judah,

**21:13** but have proceeded along the path of the kings of Israel, and have caused Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem to commit fornication, imitating the fornication of the house of Ahab, and furthermore have also killed your brothers, the house of your father, who were better than you,

**21:14** look, the Lord will strike you with a great blow, along with your people, and your sons, and your wives, and all your possessions.

**21:15** And you yourself will be sick with a most grievous illness of your abdomen, until your vital organs come out little by little, day by day.'"

**21:16** Therefore the Lord stirred up against Jehoram the spirit of the Philistines and of the Arabs who border on the Ethiopians;

**21:17** and they went up into the land of Judah and devastated it, and plundered all the possessions that were found in the king's house, and moreover his sons and his wives; and no son remained to him except Jehoahaz, who was the youngest.

**21:18** And over and above all these things, the Lord struck him with an incurable illness of the bowels.

**21:19** And as day succeeded day, and the spans of time rolled on, the cycle of two years was completed; and thus, consumed by a long wasting, so that he even discharged his own intestines, he lost both his illness and his life together. And he died in a most grievous sickness, and the people did not perform funeral rites for him according to the custom of burning, as they had done for his ancestors.

**21:20** He was thirty-two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem. And he did not walk rightly, and they buried him in the city of David, though not in the tomb of the kings.

## Translator Notes

- **21:2** — Vulgate reads "king of Judah" (regis Juda), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "king of Israel."
- **21:3** — Vulgate renders the Hebrew term for precious gifts or valuables as pensitationes ("revenues" or "payments").
- **21:9** — Vulgate reads "cavalry" (equitatu) twice here where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "chariots."
- **21:11** — Vulgate reads "in the cities of Judah" (in urbibus Juda), agreeing with the Septuagint against the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "in the hill country of Judah."
- **21:20** — Vulgate interprets the Hebrew clause "he departed with no one's regret" morally as Ambulavitque non recte ("And he did not walk rightly").
