## Text

**32:1** After these events and truthfulness of this sort, Sennacherib, king of the Assyrians, came, and having entered Judah, he besieged the fortified cities, wishing to capture them.

**32:2** When Hezekiah saw this, namely that Sennacherib had arrived and that the whole thrust of the war was being turned against Jerusalem,

**32:3** having taken counsel with the leaders and the bravest men in order to block up the heads of the springs that were outside the city, and as the judgment of everyone decided upon this,

**32:4** he assembled a very large crowd, and they blocked up all the springs and the stream that flowed through the middle of the land, saying, "Let the kings of the Assyrians not come and find an abundance of waters."

**32:5** Acting diligently, he also built up every wall that had been broken down, erected towers above it, and constructed another wall on the outside; he restored Millo in the city of David and fashioned weaponry of every kind and shields.

**32:6** He appointed commanders of the warriors in the army, summoned all of them into the open square of the city gate, and spoke to their heart, saying,

**32:7** "Act courageously and be strengthened; do not be afraid, nor be terrified of the king of the Assyrians and the entire throng that is with him, for far more are with us than with him.

**32:8** For with him is an arm of flesh, while with us is the Lord our God, who is our helper and fights on our behalf." And the people were strengthened by words of this sort from Hezekiah, king of Judah.

**32:9** After these things had taken place, Sennacherib, king of the Assyrians, sent his servants to Jerusalem (for he himself with his entire army was besieging Lachish) to Hezekiah, king of Judah, and to all the people who were in the city, saying,

**32:10** "This is what Sennacherib, king of the Assyrians, says: In what are you placing confidence as you sit besieged in Jerusalem?

**32:11** Is Hezekiah deceiving you to hand you over to death in hunger and thirst, asserting that the Lord your God will free you from the hand of the king of the Assyrians?

**32:12** Is this not that very Hezekiah who tore down His high places and altars, and instructed Judah and Jerusalem, saying, 'Before a single altar you will worship, and upon it you will burn incense'?

**32:13** Or are you unaware of what I and my fathers have done to all the peoples of the lands? Did the gods of the nations and of all the lands prevail to free their territory from my hand?

**32:14** Who is there among all the gods of the nations that my fathers laid waste who was able to rescue his people from my hand, so that your God also could rescue you from this hand?

**32:15** Therefore, do not let Hezekiah deceive you, nor mislead you with empty persuasion, and do not believe him. For if no god of all the nations and kingdoms was able to free his people from my hand and from the hand of my fathers, consequently neither will your God be able to rescue you from my hand."

**32:16** Moreover, his servants spoke many other things against the Lord God and against His servant Hezekiah.

**32:17** He also wrote letters filled with blasphemy against the Lord God of Israel, and spoke against Him: "Just as the gods of the remaining nations could not free their people from my hand, so too the God of Hezekiah will not be able to rescue His people from this hand."

**32:18** In addition, with a loud cry in the Judean language, he called out against the people who were sitting upon the walls of Jerusalem, so as to terrify them and capture the city.

**32:19** And he spoke against the God of Jerusalem just as against the gods of the peoples of the earth, the works of human hands.

**32:20** King Hezekiah and Isaiah son of Amoz, the prophet, therefore prayed against this blasphemy, and cried aloud all the way to heaven.

**32:21** And the Lord dispatched an angel, who struck down every stalwart man, warrior, and commander of the army of the king of the Assyrians; and he returned in disgrace to his own land. And when he had entered the house of his god, sons who had issued from his own body killed him with the sword.

**32:22** Thus the Lord saved Hezekiah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem from the hand of Sennacherib, king of the Assyrians, and from the hand of everyone, and granted them rest on every side.

**32:23** Many also brought victims and sacrifices to the Lord in Jerusalem, along with gifts to Hezekiah, king of Judah; and after these events he was exalted before all the nations.

**32:24** In those days Hezekiah fell ill to the point of death, and he prayed to the Lord; and He heard him and gave him a sign.

**32:25** Yet he did not repay according to the benefits he had received, because his heart was lifted up; and wrath arose against him, and against Judah and Jerusalem.

**32:26** Afterward, however, he humbled himself because his heart had been exalted, both he and the inhabitants of Jerusalem; and for that reason the wrath of the Lord did not come upon them during the days of Hezekiah.

**32:27** Now Hezekiah was wealthy and exceedingly renowned, and he gathered for himself abundant treasuries of silver and gold, of precious stone, of spices, of weapons of every sort, and of vessels of great worth.

**32:28** Storehouses as well for grain, wine, and oil, and stalls for all beasts of burden, and folds for flocks,

**32:29** and cities he built for himself; for indeed he owned countless flocks of sheep and herds of cattle, because the Lord had bestowed upon him exceedingly abundant wealth.

**32:30** This same Hezekiah is the one who blocked up the upper spring of the waters of Gihon and directed them underground toward the west side of the city of David; in all his works he carried out prosperously what he desired.

**32:31** Nevertheless, regarding the embassy of the princes of Babylon who had been sent to him to inquire about the portent that had occurred upon the land, God left him alone so that he might be tested, and that everything in his heart might become known.

**32:32** Now the remainder of the accounts of Hezekiah and of his acts of mercy are recorded in the vision of Isaiah son of Amoz, the prophet, and in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.

**32:33** And Hezekiah slept with his fathers, and they buried him above the tombs of the sons of David; and all Judah and all the inhabitants of Jerusalem celebrated his funeral rites, and his son Manasseh reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **32:1** — Vulgate 'hujuscemodi veritatem' literally reads "truthfulness of this sort" (rendering Hebrew 'emet', faithfulness/truth), and 'rex Assyriorum' reads "king of the Assyrians" rather than "king of Assyria."
- **32:18** — Unlike the Hebrew plural ("they cried out... to frighten... and capture"), the Vulgate uses singular verbs ('personabat', 'terreret', 'caperet', continuing in 32:19 'Locutusque est'), attributing the proclamation directly to Sennacherib or his spokesman.
- **32:20** — The Vulgate spells Isaiah's father's name as 'Amos' (identical to the prophet Amos), whereas the Hebrew distinguishes Amoz from Amos.
- **32:22** — Vulgate 'præstitit eis quietem per circuitum' ("granted them rest on every side") follows the Septuagint reading rather than the Hebrew Masoretic Text's "guided them on every side."
- **32:33** — Vulgate 'celebravit ejus exequias' ("celebrated his funeral rites") specifies funeral ceremonies, whereas the Hebrew reads more generally "did him honor at his death."
