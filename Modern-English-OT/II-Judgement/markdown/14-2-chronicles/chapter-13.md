## Text

**13:1** In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam, Abijah began to reign over Judah.

**13:2** He reigned three years in Jerusalem, and his mother's name was Micaiah the daughter of Uriel of Gibeah; and there was war between Abijah and Jeroboam.

**13:3** And when Abijah entered into battle and had very warlike men, even four hundred thousand chosen men, Jeroboam drew up a battle line opposite him of eight hundred thousand men, who were themselves also chosen and exceedingly valiant for battle.

**13:4** Then Abijah stood on Mount Semeron, which was in Ephraim, and said, "Listen, Jeroboam and all Israel!

**13:5** Do you not know that the Lord God of Israel gave the kingdom over Israel to David forever, to him and to his sons as a covenant of salt?

**13:6** Yet Jeroboam the son of Nebat, the servant of Solomon the son of David, rose up and rebelled against his master.

**13:7** And utterly worthless men and sons of Belial gathered to him, and they prevailed against Rehoboam the son of Solomon; now Rehoboam was inexperienced and fearful of heart, and could not resist them.

**13:8** Now therefore you say that you can resist the kingdom of the Lord, which He possesses through the sons of David, and you have a great multitude of people, as well as the golden calves that Jeroboam made for you as gods.

**13:9** And you have driven out the priests of the Lord, the sons of Aaron, and the Levites, and have made priests for yourselves like all the peoples of the lands; whoever comes and consecrates his hand with a bull from the herd and with seven rams becomes a priest of those who are not gods.

**13:10** But our Lord is God, whom we do not abandon, and priests from the sons of Aaron minister to the Lord, and the Levites are in their order;

**13:11** they also offer burnt offerings to the Lord every single day, morning and evening, and incense prepared according to the precepts of the law, and the loaves are set out on the purest table, and with us is the golden lampstand and its lamps, to be lit always at evening; for we keep the precepts of the Lord our God, whom you have abandoned.

**13:12** Therefore in our army God is the leader, and His priests, who sound the trumpets and resound against you. Sons of Israel, do not fight against the Lord God of your fathers, because it is not to your advantage."

**13:13** While he was speaking these things, Jeroboam was preparing an ambush from behind. And while he stood facing the enemy, he surrounded unsuspecting Judah with his army.

**13:14** And looking back, Judah saw that battle was pressing in from in front and from behind, and cried out to the Lord, and the priests began to sound the trumpets.

**13:15** And all the men of Judah raised a shout; and look, as they shouted, God terrified Jeroboam and all Israel who stood facing Abijah and Judah.

**13:16** And the sons of Israel fled from Judah, and God delivered them into their hand.

**13:17** Therefore Abijah and his people struck them with a great slaughter, and five hundred thousand valiant men of Israel fell wounded.

**13:18** And the sons of Israel were humbled at that time, and the sons of Judah grew exceedingly strong, because they had placed their hope in the Lord God of their fathers.

**13:19** Now Abijah pursued the fleeing Jeroboam and captured his cities: Bethel and its daughters, and Jeshanah with its daughters, and Ephron also and its daughters;

**13:20** and Jeroboam was no longer able to resist in the days of Abijah, and the Lord struck him, and he died.

**13:21** Therefore Abijah, with his rule strengthened, took fourteen wives, and he fathered twenty-two sons and sixteen daughters.

**13:22** Now the rest of the words of Abijah, and of his ways and his deeds, are written most carefully in the book of Iddo the prophet.

## Translator Notes

- **13:4** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate names this mountain "Mount Semeron" (montem Semeron), where the Hebrew reads "Mount Zemaraim."
- **13:10** — The Vulgate word order reads "our Lord is God" (Noster autem Dominus, Deus est) and uses the present tense "we do not abandon" (relinquimus), contrasting with the perfect "you have abandoned" (reliquistis) in 13:11.
- **13:17** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew term for battle casualties (chalalim) literally as "wounded" (vulnerati).
- **13:19** — The Vulgate retains the Hebrew idiom "daughters" (filias) to designate the dependent villages and surrounding settlements belonging to each city.
- **13:22** — Where the Hebrew refers to the "midrash" or story of Iddo the prophet, the Vulgate renders "most carefully in the book of Iddo the prophet" (diligentissime in libro Addo prophetae).
