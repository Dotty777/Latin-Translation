## Text

**16:1** Now in the thirty-sixth year of his reign, Baasha king of Israel went up against Judah and was surrounding Ramah with a wall, so that no one could safely go out and come in from the kingdom of Asa.

**16:2** Therefore Asa brought out silver and gold from the treasuries of the house of the Lord and from the treasuries of the king, and sent them to Ben-hadad king of Syria, who lived in Damascus, saying,

**16:3** "There is a covenant between me and you; my father and your father also had an accord. For this reason I have sent you silver and gold, so that, breaking the covenant that you have with Baasha king of Israel, you may make him withdraw from me."

**16:4** Upon learning this, Ben-hadad sent the commanders of his armies against the cities of Israel, and they struck Ijon, Dan, and Abel-maim, and all the walled cities of Naphtali.

**16:5** When Baasha heard this, he stopped building Ramah and discontinued his work.

**16:6** Then King Asa took all Judah, and they carried off the stones from Ramah and the timber that Baasha had prepared for the building, and with them he built Geba and Mizpah.

**16:7** In that time Hanani the prophet came to Asa king of Judah and said to him, "Because you had confidence in the king of Syria, and not in the Lord your God, for that reason the army of the king of Syria has escaped from your hand.

**16:8** Were not the Ethiopians and the Libyans far more numerous, with chariots, horsemen, and an immense multitude, whom He delivered into your hand when you trusted in the Lord?

**16:9** For the eyes of the Lord survey the whole earth, and provide strength to those who believe in Him with a perfect heart. Therefore you have acted foolishly, and because of this, from the present time wars will rise up against you."

**16:10** And Asa, angry with the seer, ordered him to be put in the stocks, for he had become deeply indignant over this; and he killed very many of the people in that time.

**16:11** Now the deeds of Asa, first and last, are written in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.

**16:12** Asa also fell ill in the thirty-ninth year of his reign with a most severe pain in his feet, and even in his illness he did not seek the Lord, but rather trusted in the skill of physicians.

**16:13** And he slept with his fathers, and died in the forty-first year of his reign.

**16:14** And they buried him in his tomb that he had dug for himself in the city of David; and they laid him upon his bed, filled with spices and prostitutes' ointments, which were compounded by the skill of perfumers, and they burned them over him with excessive pomp.

## Translator Notes

- **16:4** — The Vulgate specifies 'walled cities' (urbes muratas), whereas the Hebrew reads 'store cities.'
- **16:9** — The Vulgate states that the Lord's eyes 'survey' (contemplantur) the whole earth and provide strength to those who 'believe in Him' (credunt in eum), where the Hebrew speaks of His eyes ranging throughout the earth to support those whose heart is wholly His.
- **16:10** — The Vulgate states that Asa 'killed very many' (interfecit plurimos) of the people, whereas the Hebrew reads that he oppressed or crushed some of them.
- **16:14** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew zenim ('various kinds [of spices]') via the similar root for harlotry as 'prostitutes' ointments' (unguentibus meretriciis), and describes the great funeral fire as burning them over him 'with excessive pomp' (ambitione nimia).
