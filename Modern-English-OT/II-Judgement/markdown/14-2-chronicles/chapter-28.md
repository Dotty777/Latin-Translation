## Text

**28:1** Ahaz was twenty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. He did not do what was right in the sight of the Lord like David his father,

**28:2** but walked in the ways of the kings of Israel, and moreover cast statues for the Baals.

**28:3** He is the one who burned incense in the valley of Ben-hinnom, and purified his sons in fire according to the rite of the nations whom the Lord killed at the arrival of the sons of Israel.

**28:4** He also sacrificed and kindled incense on the high places, and on the hills, and under every leafy tree.

**28:5** And the Lord his God delivered him into the hand of the king of Syria, who struck him and took great plunder from his realm and brought it to Damascus; he was also delivered into the hands of the king of Israel and struck with a great blow.

**28:6** And Pekah son of Remaliah killed one hundred twenty thousand from Judah in one day, all fighting men, because they had abandoned the Lord God of their fathers.

**28:7** At the same time Zichri, a mighty man from Ephraim, killed Maaseiah the king's son, and Azrikam the leader of his house, and also Elkanah, second after the king.

**28:8** And the sons of Israel captured from their brothers two hundred thousand women, boys, and girls, and boundless plunder, and carried it into Samaria.

**28:9** At that time a prophet of the Lord, named Oded, was there, who went out to meet the army coming to Samaria and said to them, "Look, the Lord God of your fathers, angry against Judah, delivered them into your hands, and you killed them savagely, so that your cruelty reached up to heaven.

**28:10** Moreover, you want to subjugate the sons of Judah and Jerusalem for yourselves as male and female slaves, which by no means needs to be done, for you have sinned in this against the Lord your God.

**28:11** But listen to my counsel, and lead back the captives whom you have brought from your brothers, for the great wrath of the Lord hangs over you."

**28:12** Therefore men from the leaders of the sons of Ephraim, Azariah son of Johanan, Berechiah son of Meshillemoth, Hezekiah son of Shallum, and Amasa son of Hadlai, stood up against those who were coming from the battle,

**28:13** and said to them, "You will not bring the captives in here, so that we do not sin against the Lord. Why do you want to add to our sins and heap up old offenses? For it is a great sin, and the anger of the wrath of the Lord hangs over Israel."

**28:14** And the fighting men left the plunder and everything that they had captured before the leaders and the entire multitude.

**28:15** And the men whom we mentioned above stood up, and taking the captives, they clothed all who were naked from the spoils; and when they had clothed them, put sandals on them, refreshed them with food and drink, anointed them because of their weariness, and given them care, they placed all who could not walk and were weak in body on pack animals and brought them to Jericho, the city of palm trees, to their brothers; and they themselves returned to Samaria.

**28:16** At that time King Ahaz sent to the king of the Assyrians, asking for help.

**28:17** And the Edomites came and struck many from Judah, and took great plunder.

**28:18** The Philistines also spread out through the cities of the plain and toward the south of Judah, and they captured Beth-shemesh, Aijalon, and Gederoth, and also Soco, Timnah, and Gimzo, with their villages, and lived in them.

**28:19** For the Lord had humbled Judah because of Ahaz king of Judah, because he had stripped it of help and had held the Lord in contempt.

**28:20** And He brought against him Tiglath-pileser king of the Assyrians, who both afflicted him and devastated him with no one resisting.

**28:21** Therefore Ahaz, having plundered the house of the Lord and the house of the kings and leaders, gave gifts to the king of the Assyrians, and yet it was of no benefit to him.

**28:22** And moreover, in the time of his distress, he increased contempt toward the Lord, King Ahaz himself in person;

**28:23** he sacrificed victims to the gods of Damascus, who struck him, and said, "The gods of the kings of Syria help them; I will appease them with sacrifices, and they will stand by me," whereas on the contrary they were a ruin to him and to all Israel.

**28:24** Therefore, having seized and shattered all the vessels of the house of God, Ahaz shut the doors of the temple of God and made altars for himself in all the corners of Jerusalem.

**28:25** In all the cities of Judah also he built altars for burning incense, and provoked the Lord God of his fathers to anger.

**28:26** Now the rest of his words, and of all his deeds, first and last, are written in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.

**28:27** And Ahaz slept with his fathers, and they buried him in the city of Jerusalem, for they did not receive him into the tombs of the kings of Israel. And Hezekiah his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **28:3** — Latin lustravit ("purified") denotes ritual purification by fire, and interfecit ... in adventu reads "killed ... at the arrival," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "burned" and "drove out before."
- **28:10** — Latin quod nequaquam facto opus est ("which by no means needs to be done") is a distinctly Vulgate statement, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text asks rhetorically whether Israel does not also have sins against the Lord.
- **28:15** — Latin quos supra memoravimus ("whom we mentioned above") renders Hebrew "who were designated by name," and propter laborem, et adhibuissent eis curam ("because of their weariness, and given them care") is a Vulgate explanatory expansion.
- **28:19** — Latin regem Juda ("king of Judah") and nudasset eum auxilio ("stripped it of help") are Vulgate-distinct readings where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "king of Israel" and "cast off restraint in Judah."
- **28:20** — Latin Adduxitque makes the Lord (from v. 19) the subject who brought Tiglath-pileser (Latin Thelgathphalnasar) against Ahaz, and nullo resistente vastavit ("devastated him with no one resisting") differs from the Hebrew "did not strengthen him."
