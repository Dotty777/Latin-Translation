## Text

**4:1** He also made a bronze altar, twenty cubits in length, and twenty cubits in width, and ten cubits in height.

**4:2** He also made a cast sea, ten cubits from rim to rim, round all around; it had a height of five cubits, and a cord of thirty cubits went around its circumference.

**4:3** There was also a likeness of oxen beneath it, and for ten cubits certain carvings on the outside encircled the belly of the sea, as it were in two rows. Now the oxen were cast,

**4:4** and the sea itself was set upon twelve oxen, of which three faced toward the north, and another three toward the west; moreover, three others faced the south, and the three that remained, the east, having the sea placed upon them; and the hindquarters of the oxen were inward under the sea.

**4:5** Furthermore, its thickness had the measure of a handbreadth, and its rim was like the rim of a cup, or of a lily curving outward; and it held three thousand metretes.

**4:6** He also made ten basins, and he placed five on the right and five on the left, so that they might wash in them all the things that they were about to offer for a burnt offering; whereas the priests washed themselves in the sea.

**4:7** Now he also made ten golden lampstands according to the form in which they had been commanded to be made, and he placed them in the temple, five on the right and five on the left.

**4:8** He also made ten tables, and he placed them in the temple, five on the right and five on the left; and also one hundred golden bowls.

**4:9** He also made the court of the priests, and the great hall, and doors in the hall, which he covered with bronze.

**4:10** Moreover, he placed the sea on the right side, facing the east, toward the south.

**4:11** Now Hiram made the pots, and the meat forks, and the bowls; and he completed all the work of the king in the house of God:

**4:12** that is, the two pillars, and the capitals, and the heads, and, as it were, certain networks that would cover the heads upon the capitals;

**4:13** also four hundred pomegranates, and two networks, so that two rows of pomegranates were joined to each network, which would cover over the capitals and the heads of the pillars.

**4:14** He also made the bases, and the basins, which he placed upon the bases:

**4:15** one sea, and also twelve oxen under the sea,

**4:16** and the pots, and the meat forks, and the bowls. Hiram his father made all the vessels for Solomon in the house of the Lord out of the purest bronze.

**4:17** In the region of the Jordan, the king cast them in clay ground between Succoth and Zeredathah.

**4:18** Now the multitude of the vessels was innumerable, so that the weight of the bronze was unknown.

**4:19** And Solomon made all the vessels of the house of God, and the golden altar, and the tables, and upon them the loaves of presentation;

**4:20** also the lampstands with their lamps, of the purest gold, so that they might shine before the oracle according to the rite;

**4:21** and certain floral ornaments, and the lamps, and the golden tongs; all were made of the purest gold.

**4:22** Also the incense burners, and the censers, and the bowls, and the small mortars were of the purest gold. And he carved the doors of the inner temple, that is, into the Holy of Holies, and the doors of the temple on the outside were golden. And thus all the work that Solomon did in the house of the Lord was completed.

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — The Vulgate reads "for ten cubits" (decem cubitis) and "certain carvings on the outside" encircling the "belly" (alvum) of the sea, whereas the Hebrew describes ten figures per cubit encircling the sea and notes that the oxen were cast in a single casting with it.
- **4:5** — Latin metretas refers to the Greco-Roman liquid measure (roughly nine to ten gallons), which the Vulgate uses here for the Hebrew bath.
- **4:12** — Latin epistylia ("capitals/architraves") and capita ("heads/tops") render the Hebrew bowls and capitals atop the two pillars, with the Vulgate placing the heads above the capitals.
- **4:16** — Latin Hiram pater ejus ("Hiram his father") renders Hebrew Huram-abi, an honorific title ("master craftsman" or "counselor") rather than biological paternity; in 4:11 and 4:16 the Vulgate also reads creagras ("meat forks") where the Hebrew in 4:11 has "shovels."
- **4:22** — The Vulgate explicitly adds "he carved" (cælavit) and lists "small mortars" (mortariola), and includes the closing sentence ("And thus all the work...") in 4:22 rather than as the opening of 5:1 as in the Hebrew text.
