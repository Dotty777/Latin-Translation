## Text

**15:1** Now Azariah the son of Oded, when the Spirit of God came upon him,

**15:2** went out to meet Asa and said to him, "Listen to me, Asa, and all Judah and Benjamin: the Lord is with you, because you have been with Him. If you seek Him, you will find Him; but if you abandon Him, He will abandon you.

**15:3** Now many days passed in Israel without the true God, and without a teaching priest, and without law.

**15:4** And when they turn back in their distress to the Lord God of Israel and seek Him, they will find Him.

**15:5** In that time there will be no peace for the one going out and the one coming in, but terrors on every side upon all the inhabitants of the lands;

**15:6** for nation has fought against nation, and city against city, because the Lord will throw them into turmoil in every distress.

**15:7** You, therefore, be strong, and do not let your hands grow slack, for there will be a reward for your work."

**15:8** When Asa had heard this, namely, the words and the prophecy of Azariah the son of Oded the prophet, he was strengthened, and he removed the idols from all the land of Judah and from Benjamin, and from the cities of Mount Ephraim that he had captured; and he dedicated the altar of the Lord that was before the portico of the Lord.

**15:9** And he gathered all Judah and Benjamin, and the newcomers with them from Ephraim, and from Manasseh, and from Simeon; for many had fled to him from Israel, seeing that the Lord his God was with him.

**15:10** And when they had come to Jerusalem in the third month, in the fifteenth year of the reign of Asa,

**15:11** they sacrificed to the Lord on that day, from the spoils and plunder that they had brought, seven hundred oxen and seven thousand rams.

**15:12** And he entered according to custom to confirm the covenant, that they would seek the Lord God of their fathers with their whole heart and with their whole soul.

**15:13** "And if anyone," he said, "does not seek the Lord God of Israel, let him die, from the least to the greatest, from man to woman."

**15:14** And they swore to the Lord with a loud voice, with joyful shouting, and with the blast of the trumpet, and with the sound of horns,

**15:15** all who were in Judah, with a curse; for they swore with all their heart, and with their whole will they sought Him and found Him; and the Lord granted them rest all around.

**15:16** Moreover, he also deposed Maacah, the mother of King Asa, from her august authority, because she had made an image of Priapus in a grove, and he crushed all of it, and smashing it into pieces, he burned it in the brook Kidron.

**15:17** However, the high places were left in Israel; nevertheless, the heart of Asa was perfect all his days,

**15:18** and those things that his father and he himself had vowed, he brought into the house of the Lord: silver and gold, and varied furnishings of vessels.

**15:19** And there was no war until the thirty-fifth year of the reign of Asa.

## Translator Notes

- **15:4** — In 15:3-6, the Vulgate exhibits a distinctive sequence of tenses—imperfect 'passed' (Transibant, 15:3), future 'will find' and 'will be' (reperient, erit, 15:4-5), and perfect 'has fought' paired with future 'will throw them into turmoil' (pugnavit, conturbabit, 15:6)—treating part of Azariah's speech prophetically where the Hebrew looks back historically.
- **15:8** — The Vulgate explicitly reads 'Azariah the son of Oded the prophet' (Azariæ filii Oded prophetæ), whereas the Hebrew omits 'Azariah the son of'; Latin also reads 'dedicated' (dedicavit) rather than Hebrew 'repaired' or 'renewed.'
- **15:12** — The Vulgate uses a singular verb referring to Asa and includes 'according to custom' (intravit ex more), whereas the Hebrew uses a plural verb ('they entered into a covenant') without that phrase.
- **15:15** — Instead of the Hebrew 'all Judah rejoiced over the oath,' the Vulgate links 'all who were in Judah' to 'swore' in 15:14 and specifies 'with a curse' (cum execratione), indicating a solemn self-imprecatory oath.
- **15:16** — Jerome renders the Hebrew office of queen mother with the Roman imperial phrase 'august authority' (augusto imperio) and interprets Maacah's abominable Asherah image as a statue of the classical fertility god Priapus (simulacrum Priapi). The Clementine spelling 'in frustra' stands for 'in frusta' ('into pieces').
