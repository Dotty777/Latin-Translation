## Text

**22:1** Then the inhabitants of Jerusalem appointed his youngest son Ahaziah king in his place; for the raiders of the Arabs who had rushed into the camp had killed all the older ones who had been before him; and Ahaziah son of Jehoram king of Judah reigned.

**22:2** Ahaziah was forty-two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned one year in Jerusalem; and the name of his mother was Athaliah daughter of Omri.

**22:3** He also walked along the ways of the house of Ahab, for his mother incited him to act wickedly.

**22:4** Therefore he did evil in the sight of the Lord, like the house of Ahab; for they themselves were his counselors after the death of his father, to his destruction;

**22:5** and he walked in their counsels. And he went with Jehoram son of Ahab, king of Israel, to war against Hazael king of Syria at Ramoth-gilead; and the Syrians wounded Jehoram.

**22:6** He returned to be healed in Jezreel, for he had received many wounds in the aforementioned battle. Therefore Ahaziah son of Jehoram, king of Judah, went down to visit Jehoram son of Ahab in Jezreel, who was sick.

**22:7** For it was the will of God against Ahaziah that he should come to Jehoram, and that when he had come, he should also go out with him against Jehu son of Nimshi, whom the Lord anointed to destroy the house of Ahab.

**22:8** Therefore, while Jehu was overthrowing the house of Ahab, he found the princes of Judah and the sons of Ahaziah's brothers, who were serving him, and he killed them.

**22:9** Searching for Ahaziah himself as well, he captured him hiding in Samaria; and when he had been brought to him, he killed him; and they buried him, because he was the son of Jehoshaphat, who had sought the Lord with all his heart. And there was no longer any hope that anyone from the lineage of Ahaziah would reign.

**22:10** For indeed, his mother Athaliah, seeing that her son was dead, rose up and killed all the royal lineage of the house of Jehoram.

**22:11** But Jehoshabeath, the daughter of the king, took Joash son of Ahaziah, and stole him away from the midst of the king's sons while they were being killed, and hid him with his nurse in the chamber of the beds; now Jehoshabeath, who had hidden him, was the daughter of King Jehoram, the wife of Jehoiada the high priest, and the sister of Ahaziah; and for that reason Athaliah did not kill him.

**22:12** So he was hidden with them in the house of God for six years, during which Athaliah reigned over the land.

## Translator Notes

- **22:2** — Vulgate preserves "forty-two" (quadraginta duorum) in agreement with the Hebrew Masoretic Text here, in contrast to "twenty-two" in 2 Kings 8:26 and the Septuagint.
- **22:6** — Vulgate summarizes the Hebrew reference to the battle at Ramah as "in the aforementioned battle" (in supradicto certamine), and reads "Ahaziah" (Ochozias) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text has the variant "Azariah."
- **22:7** — Vulgate reads "it was the will of God" (Voluntatis quippe fuit Dei), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "the downfall of Ahaziah was from God."
- **22:9** — Vulgate renders the final clause as "there was no longer any hope" (Nec erat ultra spes aliqua), whereas the Hebrew text states that the house of Ahaziah had no one able to retain strength for the kingdom.
- **22:10** — Vulgate reads "of the house of Jehoram" (domus Joram), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "of the house of Judah."
