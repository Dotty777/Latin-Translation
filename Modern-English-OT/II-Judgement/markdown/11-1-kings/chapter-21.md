## Text

**21:1** Now Ben-hadad, king of Syria, gathered his entire army, and thirty-two kings with him, and horses and chariots; and going up, he was fighting against Samaria and besieging it.

**21:2** And sending messengers to Ahab, king of Israel, into the city,

**21:3** he said, "This is what Ben-hadad says: Your silver and your gold are mine, and your wives and your best sons are mine."

**21:4** And the king of Israel answered, "According to your word, my lord the king, I am yours, and all that is mine."

**21:5** And returning, the messengers said, "This is what Ben-hadad, who sent us to you, says: Your silver and your gold, and your wives and your sons, you will give to me.

**21:6** Therefore tomorrow at this same hour I will send my servants to you, and they will search your house and the house of your servants; and everything that pleases them they will put in their hands and carry away."

**21:7** Now the king of Israel called all the elders of the land and said, "Take notice and see that he is plotting against us; for he sent to me for my wives and sons, and for silver and gold, and I did not refuse."

**21:8** And all the elders and the entire people said to him, "Do not listen, nor consent to him."

**21:9** Therefore he answered the messengers of Ben-hadad, "Say to my lord the king, 'Everything for which you sent to me, your servant, at the beginning, I will do; but this thing I cannot do.'"

**21:10** And the messengers returned and reported to him. And he sent back and said, "May the gods do these things to me, and add these things as well, if the dust of Samaria suffices for handfuls for all the people who follow me."

**21:11** And answering, the king of Israel said, "Tell him, 'Do not let the one who has fastened his belt boast equally with the one who has unfastened it.'"

**21:12** Now it happened, when Ben-hadad had heard this word, that he himself and the kings were drinking in the pavilions, and he said to his servants, "Surround the city." And they surrounded it.

**21:13** And look, a certain prophet, approaching Ahab, king of Israel, said to him, "This is what the Lord says: Surely you have seen all this vast multitude? Look, I will deliver it into your hand today, so that you may know that I am the Lord."

**21:14** And Ahab said, "By whom?" And he said to him, "This is what the Lord says: By the attendants of the governors of the provinces." And he said, "Who will begin to do battle?" And he said, "You."

**21:15** Therefore he mustered the young men of the governors of the provinces, and found the number to be two hundred thirty-two; and after them he mustered the people, all the sons of Israel, seven thousand.

**21:16** And they went out at noon. Now Ben-hadad was drinking, drunk, in his pavilion, and the thirty-two kings with him, who had come to his aid.

**21:17** Now the young men of the governors of the provinces went out in the front line. Therefore Ben-hadad sent out, and they reported to him, saying, "Men have come out from Samaria."

**21:18** And he said, "Whether they are coming for peace, seize them alive; or whether to do battle, capture them alive."

**21:19** Therefore the young men of the governors of the provinces went out, and the rest of the army was following;

**21:20** and each one struck down the man who was coming against him; and the Syrians fled, and Israel pursued them. Ben-hadad, king of Syria, also fled on a horse with his horsemen.

**21:21** Moreover, the king of Israel went out and struck the horses and chariots, and he struck Syria with a great blow.

**21:22** Now the prophet, approaching the king of Israel, said to him, "Go and strengthen yourself, and know and see what you should do; for in the following year the king of Syria will go up against you."

**21:23** But the servants of the king of Syria said to him, "Gods of the mountains are their gods; therefore they overpowered us; yet it is better that we fight against them on the plains, and we will prevail over them.

**21:24** You therefore do this thing: remove each of the kings from your army, and place governors in their place,

**21:25** and restore the number of soldiers who fell from yours, and horses like the former horses, and chariots like the chariots that you had before; and we will fight against them on the plains, and you will see that we will prevail over them." He believed their counsel and did so.

**21:26** Therefore, after a year had passed, Ben-hadad mustered the Syrians and went up to Aphek to fight against Israel.

**21:27** Now the sons of Israel were mustered, and having received provisions, they set out opposite them and pitched camp against them like two small flocks of goats; but the Syrians filled the land.

**21:28** (And approaching, a certain man of God said to the king of Israel, "This is what the Lord says: Because the Syrians have said, 'The Lord is a God of the mountains, and is not a God of the valleys,' I will give all this great multitude into your hand, and you will know that I am the Lord.")

**21:29** And for seven days both sides were drawing up battle lines opposite each other, but on the seventh day battle was joined; and the sons of Israel struck down one hundred thousand foot soldiers of the Syrians in one day.

**21:30** Now those who had remained fled into Aphek, into the city; and the wall fell upon twenty-seven thousand men who had remained. Meanwhile Ben-hadad, fleeing, entered the city, into a chamber that was inside a chamber.

**21:31** And his servants said to him, "Look, we have heard that the kings of the house of Israel are merciful. Therefore let us put sackcloth around our waists and ropes on our heads, and let us go out to the king of Israel; perhaps he will save our lives."

**21:32** They bound sackcloth around their waists and put ropes on their heads, and came to the king of Israel and said to him, "Your servant Ben-hadad says, 'Let my soul live, I beg you.'" And he said, "If he is still alive, he is my brother."

**21:33** And the men took this as an omen; and hurrying, they snatched the word from his mouth and said, "Your brother Ben-hadad." And he said to them, "Go and bring him to me." Therefore Ben-hadad came out to him, and he lifted him up into his chariot.

**21:34** And he said to him, "The cities that my father took from your father I will return, and make streets for yourself in Damascus, just as my father did in Samaria; and I, bound by a covenant, will depart from you." Therefore he made a covenant and let him go.

**21:35** Then a certain man of the sons of the prophets said to his companion by the word of the Lord, "Strike me." But he refused to strike.

**21:36** And he said to him, "Because you refused to listen to the voice of the Lord, look, you will depart from me, and a lion will strike you." And when he had departed a little way from him, a lion found him and struck him.

**21:37** And finding another man as well, he said to him, "Strike me." And he struck him and wounded him.

**21:38** Therefore the prophet went off and met the king on the road, and he altered his face and his eyes by a sprinkling of dust.

**21:39** And when the king had passed by, he cried out to the king and said, "Your servant went out to do battle at close quarters; and when one man had fled, someone brought him to me and said, 'Guard this man; if he slips away, your life will be for his life, or you will weigh out a talent of silver.'

**21:40** Now while I, distracted, was turning this way and that, suddenly he disappeared." And the king of Israel said to him, "This is your judgment, which you yourself have decreed."

**21:41** But he immediately wiped the dust from his face, and the king of Israel recognized him, that he was one of the prophets.

**21:42** And he said to him, "This is what the Lord says: Because you have let a man worthy of death go from your hand, your life will be for his life, and your people for his people."

**21:43** Therefore the king of Israel returned to his house, scorning to listen, and came into Samaria furious.

## Translator Notes

- **21:6** — Latin omne quod eis placuerit ("everything that pleases them," i.e., Ben-hadad's servants) aligns with the Septuagint and differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "whatever is desirable in your eyes."
- **21:11** — Latin accinctus ("girded/fastened") and discinctus ("ungirded/unfastened") are military terms referring to putting on and taking off a war-belt or armor before and after battle.
- **21:34** — In the Vulgate (et ego foederatus recedam a te), Ben-hadad continues speaking ("and I, bound by a covenant, will depart from you"), whereas in the Hebrew Masoretic Text Ahab speaks this clause ("and I will let you go with this covenant").
- **21:38** — Vulgate aspersione pulveris ("by a sprinkling of dust," continued in 21:41 abstersit pulverem, "wiped the dust") reflects reading the rare Hebrew word afer ("headband/bandage") as epher ("ashes/dust").
- **21:43** — Vulgate audire contemnens ("scorning to listen") renders the Hebrew adjective sar ("sullen" or "resentful") as stubborn refusal to heed the prophet's rebuke.
