## Text

**19:1** Now Ahab reported to Jezebel all that Elijah had done, and how he had killed all the prophets with the sword.

**19:2** And Jezebel sent a messenger to Elijah, saying, "May the gods do these things to me, and add these things, unless by this hour tomorrow I have made your life like the life of one of them."

**19:3** Therefore Elijah was afraid, and rising up, he went away wherever his will carried him; and he came to Beersheba of Judah and left his servant there.

**19:4** And he went on into the wilderness, a journey of one day. And when he had come and was sitting under a single juniper tree, he asked for his soul that he might die, and said, "It is enough for me, Lord; take my life, for I am no better than my fathers."

**19:5** And he threw himself down and fell asleep in the shade of the juniper tree; and look, an angel of the Lord touched him and said to him, "Rise and eat."

**19:6** He looked around, and look, at his head was bread baked under the ashes and a vessel of water. So he ate and drank, and fell asleep again.

**19:7** And the angel of the Lord returned a second time and touched him, and said to him, "Rise, eat, for a great journey remains for you."

**19:8** And when he had risen, he ate and drank, and walked in the strength of that food forty days and forty nights all the way to the mountain of God, Horeb.

**19:9** And when he had come there, he stayed in a cave; and look, the word of the Lord came to him, and He said to him, "What are you doing here, Elijah?"

**19:10** And he answered, "With zeal I have been zealous for the Lord God of hosts, because the sons of Israel have abandoned Your covenant; they have destroyed Your altars, they have killed Your prophets with the sword, I alone am left, and they are seeking my life to take it away."

**19:11** And He said to him, "Go out and stand on the mountain before the Lord." And look, the Lord was passing by; and a great and strong wind was overturning mountains and crushing rocks before the Lord; the Lord was not in the wind. And after the wind, a shaking; the Lord was not in the shaking.

**19:12** And after the shaking, a fire; the Lord was not in the fire. And after the fire, a whisper of a light breeze.

**19:13** And when Elijah had heard this, he covered his face with his cloak, and going out, he stood at the entrance of the cave. And look, a voice came to him, saying, "What are you doing here, Elijah?" And he answered:

**19:14** "With zeal I have been zealous for the Lord God of hosts, because the sons of Israel have abandoned Your covenant; they have destroyed Your altars, they have killed Your prophets with the sword, I alone am left, and they are seeking my life to take it away."

**19:15** And the Lord said to him, "Go and return on your way through the wilderness to Damascus; and when you arrive there, you will anoint Hazael king over Syria,

**19:16** and you will anoint Jehu the son of Nimshi king over Israel; and you will anoint Elisha the son of Shaphat, who is from Abel-meholah, as prophet in your place.

**19:17** And it will be: whoever escapes the sword of Hazael, Jehu will kill him; and whoever escapes the sword of Jehu, Elisha will put him to death.

**19:18** And I will leave for Myself in Israel seven thousand men, whose knees have not been bowed before Baal, and every mouth that has not worshiped him by kissing the hands."

**19:19** So departing from there, Elijah found Elisha the son of Shaphat plowing with twelve yokes of oxen. And he himself was one among the twelve yokes of oxen that were plowing; and when Elijah had come to him, he threw his cloak over him.

**19:20** And immediately, leaving the oxen, he ran after Elijah and said, "Please let me kiss my father and my mother, and then I will follow you." And he said to him, "Go and return, for what was mine, I have done to you."

**19:21** And returning from him, he took a pair of oxen and slaughtered them, and with the plow of the oxen he cooked the meat and gave it to the people, and they ate; and rising up, he went away and followed Elijah, and served him.

## Translator Notes

- **19:3** — The Vulgate reads Timuit ("was afraid"), agreeing with the Septuagint against the Masoretic vocalization "saw," and renders Hebrew "for his life" as quocumque eum ferebat voluntas ("wherever his will carried him").
- **19:12** — Latin sibilus aurae tenuis ("a whisper of a light breeze") is a distinctive Vulgate rendering of the Hebrew phrase literally meaning "a sound of thin silence."
- **19:18** — The Vulgate specifies virorum ("men") and renders the Hebrew "has not kissed him" as non adoravit eum osculans manus ("has not worshiped him by kissing the hands"), reflecting the ancient gesture of homage toward idols.
- **19:19** — Latin in duodecim jugis boum arantibus unus erat ("was one among the twelve yokes of oxen that were plowing") differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which states that Elisha was "with the twelfth."
- **19:20** — The Vulgate renders Elijah's reply as a declarative statement, quod enim meum erat, feci tibi ("for what was mine, I have done to you"), whereas the Hebrew has a rhetorical question ("for what have I done to you?").
