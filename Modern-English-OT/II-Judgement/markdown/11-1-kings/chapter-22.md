## Text

**22:1** So three years passed without war between Syria and Israel.

**22:2** Now in the third year, Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, went down to the king of Israel.

**22:3** And the king of Israel said to his servants, "Do you not know that Ramoth-Gilead is ours, and we neglect to take it from the hand of the king of Syria?"

**22:4** And he said to Jehoshaphat, "Will you come with me to fight at Ramoth-Gilead?"

**22:5** And Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, "As I am, so also are you; my people and your people are one, and my horsemen are your horsemen." And Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, "Seek, I ask you, the word of the Lord today."

**22:6** Therefore the king of Israel gathered the prophets, about four hundred men, and said to them, "Should I go to Ramoth-Gilead to wage war, or remain still?" And they answered, "Go up, and the Lord will give it into the hand of the king."

**22:7** But Jehoshaphat said, "Is there not some prophet of the Lord here, so that we may inquire through him?"

**22:8** And the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "There remains one man through whom we can inquire of the Lord, but I hate him, because he does not prophesy good for me, but evil: Micaiah, son of Imlah." And Jehoshaphat said to him, "Do not speak like that, king."

**22:9** Therefore the king of Israel called a certain eunuch and said to him, "Hurry to bring Micaiah, son of Imlah."

**22:10** Now the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, were each sitting on his own throne, dressed in royal attire, on a threshing floor next to the entrance of the gate of Samaria, and all the prophets were prophesying in their sight.

**22:11** Zedekiah, son of Chenaanah, also made iron horns for himself and said, "This is what the Lord says: 'With these you will toss Syria until you destroy it.'"

**22:12** And all the prophets were prophesying likewise, saying, "Go up to Ramoth-Gilead, and go prosperously, and the Lord will deliver it into the hands of the king."

**22:13** Now the messenger who had gone to call Micaiah spoke to him, saying, "Look, the words of the prophets with one mouth proclaim good things to the king; therefore let your word be like theirs, and speak good things."

**22:14** And Micaiah said to him, "As the Lord lives, whatever the Lord says to me, this I will speak."

**22:15** So he came to the king, and the king said to him, "Micaiah, should we go to Ramoth-Gilead to fight, or refrain?" And he answered him, "Go up, and go prosperously, and the Lord will deliver it into the hands of the king."

**22:16** But the king said to him, "Again and again I adjure you to speak to me nothing except what is true, in the name of the Lord."

**22:17** And he said, "I saw all Israel scattered on the mountains, like sheep having no shepherd. And the Lord said, 'These have no master; let each one return to his own house in peace.'"

**22:18** Therefore the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "Did I not tell you that he does not prophesy good for me, but always evil?"

**22:19** But he continued and said, "Therefore, hear the word of the Lord: I saw the Lord sitting upon His throne, and all the host of heaven standing beside Him on the right and on the left,

**22:20** and the Lord said, 'Who will deceive Ahab, king of Israel, so that he may go up and fall at Ramoth-Gilead?' And one spoke words of this kind, and another differently.

**22:21** Then a spirit came out and stood before the Lord, and said, 'I will deceive him.' And the Lord spoke to him, 'By what means?'

**22:22** And he said, 'I will go out, and I will be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.' And the Lord said, 'You will deceive, and you will prevail; go out and do so.'

**22:23** Now therefore, look, the Lord has put a spirit of lying in the mouth of all your prophets who are here, and the Lord has spoken evil against you."

**22:24** Then Zedekiah, son of Chenaanah, approached and struck Micaiah on the cheek, and said, "Has the Spirit of the Lord then left me and spoken to you?"

**22:25** And Micaiah said, "You will see on that day when you enter a room within a room to hide yourself."

**22:26** And the king of Israel said, "Take Micaiah, and let him remain with Amon, the governor of the city, and with Joash, son of Amelech,

**22:27** and say to them, 'This is what the king says: Put this man in prison, and sustain him with bread of affliction and water of distress, until I return in peace.'"

**22:28** And Micaiah said, "If you return in peace, the Lord has not spoken in me." And he said, "Hear, all peoples."

**22:29** So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, went up to Ramoth-Gilead.

**22:30** So the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "Take up arms, and enter the battle, and put on your own garments." Meanwhile, the king of Israel changed his attire and entered the war.

**22:31** Now the king of Syria had commanded the thirty-two commanders of the chariots, saying, "You are not to fight against anyone small or great, except against the king of Israel alone."

**22:32** Therefore, when the commanders of the chariots saw Jehoshaphat, they suspected that he was the king of Israel, and making a charge, they began fighting against him; and Jehoshaphat cried out.

**22:33** And the commanders of the chariots realized that he was not the king of Israel, and they broke off from him.

**22:34** Now a certain man drew a bow, aiming an arrow at random, and by chance struck the king of Israel between the lung and the stomach. And he said to his charioteer, "Turn your hand and bring me out of the army, for I am severely wounded."

**22:35** So the battle was joined on that day, and the king of Israel was standing in his chariot facing the Syrians, and he died in the evening; and the blood of the wound was flowing into the hollow of the chariot.

**22:36** And a herald called out throughout the entire army before the sun set, saying, "Let each one return to his city and to his own land."

**22:37** Now the king died and was brought to Samaria, and they buried the king in Samaria.

**22:38** And they washed his chariot in the pool of Samaria, and the dogs licked his blood, and they washed the reins, according to the word of the Lord which He had spoken.

**22:39** Now the rest of the accounts of Ahab, and everything that he did, and the ivory house that he built, and all the cities that he constructed, are these not written in the book of the accounts of the days of the kings of Israel?

**22:40** So Ahab slept with his fathers, and Ahaziah his son reigned in his place.

**22:41** Now Jehoshaphat, son of Asa, had begun to reign over Judah in the fourth year of Ahab, king of Israel.

**22:42** He was thirty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned twenty-five years in Jerusalem; the name of his mother was Azubah, daughter of Shilhi.

**22:43** And he walked in the entire way of Asa his father, and did not turn aside from it; and he did what was right in the sight of the Lord.

**22:44** Nevertheless, he did not remove the high places, for the people were still sacrificing and burning incense on the high places.

**22:45** And Jehoshaphat had peace with the king of Israel.

**22:46** Now the rest of the matters of Jehoshaphat, and his works that he performed, and the battles, are these not written in the book of the words of the days of the kings of Judah?

**22:51** And Jehoshaphat slept with his fathers, and was buried with them in the city of David his father; and Jehoram his son reigned in his place.

**22:52** Now Ahaziah, son of Ahab, had begun to reign over Israel in Samaria in the seventeenth year of Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, and he reigned over Israel for two years.

**22:53** And he did evil in the sight of the Lord, and walked in the way of his father and of his mother, and in the way of Jeroboam, son of Nebat, who made Israel sin.

**22:54** He also served Baal and worshiped him, and he provoked the Lord God of Israel, according to all that his father had done.

## Translator Notes

- **22:24** — The Vulgate renders Zedekiah's question as "Has the Spirit of the Lord then left me and spoken to you?" (Mene ergo dimisit spiritus Domini), whereas the Hebrew text asks which way the Spirit passed from him to speak to Micaiah.
- **22:26** — The Vulgate reads "let him remain with" (maneat apud) instead of the Hebrew "take him back to," and transliterates the Hebrew title hammelekh ("the king") as the proper name Amelech ("son of Amelech").
- **22:30** — The Vulgate has Ahab instruct Jehoshaphat in the second person ("Take up arms, and enter the battle"), whereas the Hebrew text has Ahab announce that he himself will disguise himself and enter the battle.
- **22:34** — The Vulgate translates the target of the arrow anatomically as "between the lung and the stomach" (inter pulmonem et stomachum) and adds "by chance" (casu), whereas the Hebrew refers to the gap between parts of the king's armor.
- **22:38** — The Vulgate renders this clause "and they washed the reins" (et habenas laverunt), taking the underlying Hebrew term as chariot harness or gear, whereas the Masoretic Hebrew reads "and the prostitutes washed themselves."
