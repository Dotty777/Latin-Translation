## Text

**10:1** Now the queen of Sheba also, having heard of Solomon's fame in the name of the Lord, came to test him with riddles.

**10:2** And having entered Jerusalem with a large retinue and riches, with camels carrying spices, and an exceedingly vast amount of gold, and precious gems, she came to King Solomon and spoke to him of everything that she had in her heart.

**10:3** And Solomon explained to her all the matters that she had put forward; there was not a word that could be hidden from the king so that he did not answer her.

**10:4** Now when the queen of Sheba saw all the wisdom of Solomon, and the house that he had built,

**10:5** and the foods of his table, and the dwellings of his servants, and the ranks of the attendants, and their garments, and the cupbearers, and the burnt offerings that he offered in the house of the Lord, she had no spirit left.

**10:6** And she said to the king, "The report is true that I heard in my land

**10:7** concerning your words and concerning your wisdom; and I did not believe those who told me, until I myself came and saw with my own eyes, and confirmed that half had not been reported to me; your wisdom and works are greater than the rumor that I heard.

**10:8** Happy are your men, and happy are your servants, who stand before you always and hear your wisdom.

**10:9** May the Lord your God be blessed, who was pleased with you and set you upon the throne of Israel, because the Lord has loved Israel forever, and appointed you king to administer judgment and justice."

**10:10** So she gave the king one hundred twenty talents of gold, and exceedingly abundant spices, and precious gems; never again were so many spices brought as those that the queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon.

**10:11** (Moreover, Hiram's fleet, which carried gold from Ophir, brought from Ophir exceedingly abundant thyine wood and precious gems.

**10:12** And from the thyine wood the king made supports for the house of the Lord and for the royal house, as well as harps and lyres for the singers; no thyine wood of this kind has been brought, nor seen, up to the present day.)

**10:13** Now King Solomon gave the queen of Sheba everything that she wanted and asked of him, apart from those things that he had freely offered her as a royal gift. Then she returned and departed to her own land with her servants.

**10:14** Now the weight of gold that was brought to Solomon every year was six hundred sixty-six talents of gold,

**10:15** apart from what was brought by the men who were over the revenues, and the merchants, and all those selling small wares, and all the kings of Arabia, and the governors of the land.

**10:16** King Solomon also made two hundred large shields of purest gold; he put six hundred shekels of gold into the plates of one shield.

**10:17** And three hundred small shields of tested gold; three hundred minas of gold overlaid one small shield; and the king placed them in the house of the forest of Lebanon.

**10:18** King Solomon also made a great throne of ivory, and overlaid it with gold of an exceedingly deep yellow,

**10:19** which had six steps; and the top of the throne was round at the back; and there were two arms on this side and that holding the seat; and two lions stood beside each arm.

**10:20** And twelve small lions were standing upon the six steps, on this side and that; such a work has not been made in all the kingdoms.

**10:21** Moreover, all the vessels from which King Solomon drank were of gold, and all the furnishings of the house of the forest of Lebanon were of purest gold; there was no silver, nor was it considered of any value in the days of Solomon.

**10:22** This is the sum of the expenses that King Solomon provided for building the house of the Lord and his own house, and Millo, and the wall of Jerusalem, and Hazor, and Megiddo, and Gezer. So Solomon built Gezer, and lower Beth-horon, and Baalath, and Palmyra in the land of the wilderness. And he fortified all the villages that belonged to him and were without a wall, and the cities of chariots and the cities of horsemen, and whatever pleased him to build in Jerusalem, and in Lebanon, and in all the land of his dominion. All the people who had remained from the Amorites, and the Hittites, and the Perizzites, and the Hivites, and the Jebusites, who are not of the sons of Israel: their sons who had remained in the land, namely those whom the sons of Israel had not been able to wipe out, Solomon made subject to tribute up to this day. But of the sons of Israel Solomon did not appoint anyone to serve; rather, they were fighting men, and his ministers, and princes, and commanders, and officers of chariots and horses. For the king's fleet went by sea with Hiram's fleet once every three years to Tarshish, bringing from there gold, and silver, and elephant tusks, and apes, and peacocks.

**10:23** So King Solomon was magnified above all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom.

**10:24** And all the earth longed for the presence of Solomon, to hear his wisdom, which God had given in his heart.

**10:25** And each one brought him gifts: vessels of silver and gold, garments and weapons of war, spices also, and horses and mules, every year.

**10:26** And Solomon had forty thousand stalls for chariot horses and twelve thousand riding horses. And Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen, and he had one thousand four hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen; and he stationed them throughout the fortified cities, and with the king in Jerusalem.

**10:27** And he caused there to be as great an abundance of silver in Jerusalem as of stones; and he supplied a multitude of cedars like the sycamores that grow in the plains.

**10:28** And horses were brought out for Solomon from Egypt and from Kue. For the king's merchants bought them from Kue and brought them at a fixed price.

**10:29** Now a chariot came out from Egypt for six hundred shekels of silver, and a horse for one hundred fifty. And in this way all the kings of the Hittites and of Syria sold horses.

## Translator Notes

- **10:5** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew "seating" as "dwellings" (habitacula) and reads "burnt offerings" (holocausta) rather than "ascent" or "stairway," reflecting a well-known ambiguity in the Hebrew consonantal text.
- **10:11** — Latin "ligna thyina" ("thyine wood," an aromatic North African conifer timber) is the Vulgate rendering of Hebrew "almug wood."
- **10:17** — The Vulgate reads "three hundred minas" (trecentæ minæ), matching the number three hundred in 2 Chronicles 9:16 and several Septuagint witnesses, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text of 1 Kings reads "three minas."
- **10:22** — Following the Septuagint 3 Kingdoms order, this verse incorporates the account of Solomon's building projects and tribute labor (placed at 9:15, 17-22 in the Masoretic Text) before the "For the king's fleet" (quia) clause continuing 10:21. Distinctively Vulgate readings include "sum of the expenses" (summa expensarum) for Hebrew "forced labor," "villages... without a wall" for "storage cities," and "Palmyra" (Palmiram) for Hebrew Tamar/Tadmor.
- **10:26** — The opening sentence on Solomon's forty thousand stalls for chariot horses and twelve thousand riding horses stands here following the Septuagint 3 Kingdoms placement (compare 1 Kings 4:26 in the Hebrew text), and the Vulgate reads "fortified cities" (civitates munitas) where the Hebrew has "chariot cities."
- **10:29** — The Vulgate states that the kings of the Hittites and of Syria "sold horses" (equos venundabant), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text describes Solomon's merchants exporting horses and chariots to those kings.
