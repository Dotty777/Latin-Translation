## Text

**17:1** And Elijah the Tishbite, of the inhabitants of Gilead, said to Ahab, "As the Lord God of Israel lives, in whose sight I stand, there will be no dew or rain during these years, except according to the words of my mouth."

**17:2** And the word of the Lord came to him, saying,

**17:3** "Depart from here, and go toward the east, and hide yourself at the stream Cherith, which is opposite the Jordan,

**17:4** and there you will drink from the stream; and I have commanded the ravens to feed you there."

**17:5** So he went and did according to the word of the Lord; and when he had gone, he settled at the stream Cherith, which is opposite the Jordan.

**17:6** The ravens also brought him bread and meat in the morning, and likewise bread and meat in the evening, and he drank from the stream.

**17:7** After some days, however, the stream dried up, for it had not rained upon the earth.

**17:8** Then the word of the Lord came to him, saying,

**17:9** "Rise and go to Zarephath of the Sidonians, and you will stay there; for I have commanded a widowed woman there to feed you."

**17:10** He rose and went to Zarephath. And when he had come to the gate of the city, a widowed woman appeared to him gathering wood; and he called to her and said to her, "Give me a little water in a vessel so that I may drink."

**17:11** And as she was going to bring it, he called out behind her back, saying, "Bring me, please, a morsel of bread in your hand as well."

**17:12** She answered, "As the Lord your God lives, I have no bread, except as much flour as a hand can hold in a jar, and a little oil in a flask. Look, I am gathering two pieces of wood so that I may go in and prepare it for myself and my son, so that we may eat and die."

**17:13** Elijah said to her, "Do not be afraid, but go and do as you have said; yet first make for me from that little bit of flour a small loaf baked under the ashes and bring it to me, and for yourself and your son you will make it afterward.

**17:14** For this is what the Lord God of Israel says: 'The jar of flour will not run out, nor will the flask of oil be reduced, until the day on which the Lord will give rain upon the face of the earth.'"

**17:15** So she went and did according to the word of Elijah; and he, she, and her household ate; and from that day

**17:16** the jar of flour did not run out, and the flask of oil was not reduced, according to the word of the Lord which He had spoken by the hand of Elijah.

**17:17** Now it happened after these things that the son of the woman, the mistress of the house, fell ill, and the illness was very severe, so that no breath remained in him.

**17:18** So she said to Elijah, "What is there between me and you, man of God? Have you come in to me so that my iniquities would be remembered and you would kill my son?"

**17:19** And Elijah said to her, "Give me your son." And he took him from her bosom, and carried him into the upper room where he himself was staying, and laid him on his own bed.

**17:20** And he cried out to the Lord and said, "Lord my God, have You also afflicted the widow with whom I am somehow sustained, so as to kill her son?"

**17:21** And he stretched himself out and measured himself over the boy three times, and cried out to the Lord and said, "Lord my God, please let the soul of this boy return into his inward parts."

**17:22** And the Lord heard the voice of Elijah, and the soul of the boy returned within him, and he lived again.

**17:23** And Elijah took the boy, and brought him down from the upper room into the lower part of the house, and gave him to his mother, and said to her, "Look, your son is alive."

**17:24** And the woman said to Elijah, "Now by this I know that you are a man of God, and the word of the Lord in your mouth is true."

## Translator Notes

- **17:1** — The Vulgate preserves the Hebraistic elliptical oath formula 'Vivit Dominus... si erit...' (lit. 'The Lord... lives, if there will be dew and rain...'), which idiomatically expresses a solemn negation, and specifies plural 'words of my mouth' ('oris mei verba').
- **17:13** — Latin 'subcinericium panem parvulum' literally describes a small loaf baked under the ashes, paired with the diminutive 'farinula' ('little bit of flour').
- **17:15** — Where the Hebrew ends verse 15 with 'many days' and begins a new sentence in verse 16, the Vulgate closes verse 15 with 'et ex illa die' ('and from that day'), running the sentence directly into verse 16.
- **17:20** — Vulgate 'apud quam ego utcumque sustentor' ('with whom I am somehow sustained') is a distinctive rendering of the Hebrew phrase for sojourning with the widow.
