## Text

**18:1** After many days the word of the Lord came to Elijah, in the third year, saying, "Go and show yourself to Ahab, so that I may give rain upon the face of the earth."

**18:2** So Elijah went to show himself to Ahab. Now there was a severe famine in Samaria.

**18:3** And Ahab called Obadiah, the steward of his house; now Obadiah feared the Lord greatly.

**18:4** For when Jezebel was killing the prophets of the Lord, he took one hundred prophets and hid them fifty by fifty in caves, and fed them with bread and water.

**18:5** So Ahab said to Obadiah, "Go into the land to all the springs of water and into all the valleys; perhaps we can find grass and save the horses and mules, so that the livestock do not completely perish."

**18:6** And they divided the regions between themselves to travel through them: Ahab went by one road, and Obadiah by the other road separately.

**18:7** And while Obadiah was on the road, Elijah met him; and when he recognized him, he fell upon his face and said, "Is it really you, my lord Elijah?"

**18:8** He answered him, "It is I. Go and say to your lord, 'Elijah is here.'"

**18:9** And he said, "How have I sinned, that you are handing me, your servant, over into the hand of Ahab so that he may kill me?

**18:10** As the Lord your God lives, there is no nation or kingdom where my lord has not sent seeking you; and when all answered, 'He is not here,' he made every individual kingdom and nation swear an oath because you were not found at all.

**18:11** And now you say to me, 'Go and say to your lord, "Elijah is here."'

**18:12** And when I have departed from you, the Spirit of the Lord will carry you away to a place that I do not know; and I will go in and report to Ahab, and when he does not find you, he will kill me; yet your servant has feared the Lord from his childhood.

**18:13** Has it not been reported to you, my lord, what I did when Jezebel was killing the prophets of the Lord, that I hid one hundred men of the prophets of the Lord, fifty by fifty, in caves, and fed them with bread and water?

**18:14** And now you say, 'Go and say to your lord, "Elijah is here,"' so that he may kill me?"

**18:15** And Elijah said, "As the Lord of hosts lives, before whose face I stand, today I will appear to him."

**18:16** So Obadiah went to meet Ahab and reported to him; and Ahab came to meet Elijah.

**18:17** And when he saw him, he said, "Are you the one who troubles Israel?"

**18:18** And he said, "I have not troubled Israel, but you and the house of your father, who have abandoned the commandments of the Lord and have followed the Baals.

**18:19** Yet now send and gather to me all Israel on Mount Carmel, and the four hundred fifty prophets of Baal, and the four hundred prophets of the groves, who eat from Jezebel's table."

**18:20** Ahab sent to all the sons of Israel and gathered the prophets on Mount Carmel.

**18:21** Now Elijah came near to all the people and said, "How long are you limping between two sides? If the Lord is God, follow Him; but if Baal, follow him." And the people did not answer him a word.

**18:22** And Elijah said again to the people, "I alone remain a prophet of the Lord, while the prophets of Baal are four hundred and fifty men.

**18:23** Let two bulls be given to us, and let them choose one bull for themselves, and cutting it into pieces let them lay it on the wood, but let them put no fire underneath; and I will prepare the other bull and place it on the wood, and I will put no fire underneath.

**18:24** Call upon the names of your gods, and I will call upon the name of my Lord; and the God who answers by fire, let Him be God." Answering, all the people said, "An excellent proposal."

**18:25** So Elijah said to the prophets of Baal, "Choose one bull for yourselves and prepare it first, because you are more numerous; and call upon the names of your gods, and put no fire underneath."

**18:26** When they had taken the bull that he had given them, they prepared it; and they called upon the name of Baal from morning until noon, saying, "Baal, answer us." Yet there was no voice, nor anyone who answered; and they kept leaping over the altar that they had made.

**18:27** And when it was already noon, Elijah mocked them, saying, "Shout with a louder voice, for he is a god; and perhaps he is talking, or he is at an inn, or on a journey, or surely he is asleep, so that he may be awakened."

**18:28** So they shouted with a loud voice and cut themselves according to their custom with knives and lancets, until they were drenched with blood.

**18:29** Now after noon had passed, and while they were prophesying the time had come at which the sacrifice is customarily offered, and no voice was heard, nor did anyone answer, nor did anyone pay attention to those who were praying,

**18:30** Elijah said to all the people, "Come to me." And when the people approached him, he repaired the altar of the Lord that had been destroyed.

**18:31** And he took twelve stones according to the number of the tribes of the sons of Jacob, to whom the word of the Lord came, saying, "Israel will be your name."

**18:32** And from the stones he built an altar in the name of the Lord; and he made a water channel, about the space of two small furrows, around the altar,

**18:33** and arranged the wood, and divided the bull limb by limb, and laid it upon the wood,

**18:34** and said, "Fill four jars with water, and pour it over the burnt offering and over the wood." And again he said, "Do this a second time as well." When they had done it a second time, he said, "Do the very same thing a third time as well." And they did it a third time,

**18:35** and the waters ran around the altar, and the trench of the water channel was filled.

**18:36** And when it was now time for the burnt offering to be offered, Elijah the prophet came near and said, "Lord God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, show today that You are the God of Israel, and that I am Your servant, and that according to Your command I have done all these things.

**18:37** Answer me, Lord, answer me, so that this people may learn that You are the Lord God, and that You have turned their heart back again."

**18:38** Then the fire of the Lord fell and devoured the burnt offering, and the wood, and the stones, and the dust as well, licking up even the water that was in the water channel.

**18:39** When all the people saw this, they fell on their faces and said, "The Lord Himself is God! The Lord Himself is God!"

**18:40** And Elijah said to them, "Seize the prophets of Baal, and do not let even one of them escape." When they had seized them, Elijah led them to the stream Kishon and killed them there.

**18:41** And Elijah said to Ahab, "Go up, eat and drink, for there is the sound of heavy rain."

**18:42** Ahab went up to eat and drink, but Elijah went up to the summit of Carmel, and bowing down to the ground, he placed his face between his knees,

**18:43** and said to his servant, "Go up and look out toward the sea." When he had gone up and looked, he said, "There is nothing." And again he said to him, "Go back seven times."

**18:44** Now on the seventh time, look, a tiny cloud like a man's footprint was rising from the sea. And he said, "Go up and say to Ahab, 'Harness your chariot and go down, so that the rain does not overtake you.'"

**18:45** And as he turned this way and that, look, the skies grew dark, and there were clouds and wind, and a heavy rain fell. So Ahab mounted up and went to Jezreel,

**18:46** and the hand of the Lord came upon Elijah, and having girded his waist, he ran ahead of Ahab until he came to Jezreel.

## Translator Notes

- **18:19** — Vulgate 'lucorum' ('of the groves') is Jerome's standard rendering of Hebrew 'Asherah,' denoting sacred groves or cultic wooden poles.
- **18:27** — Vulgate 'in diversorio est' ('he is at an inn') is a distinctive rendering of Elijah's second taunt, where the Hebrew expression is often taken to mean stepping aside or relieving himself.
- **18:32** — Vulgate 'quasi per duas aratiunculas' ('about the space of two small furrows') expresses the trench size in terms of plowed furrows where the Hebrew speaks of a measure of two seahs of seed.
- **18:44** — Vulgate 'vestigium hominis' literally means 'a man's footprint' (reflecting Hebrew 'kaf,' which can denote either a palm or the sole of a foot), and narrates the sighting directly rather than as speech by the servant.
- **18:45** — Vulgate 'Cumque se verteret huc atque illuc' ('And as he turned this way and that') translates the Hebrew temporal idiom 'in a little while' (lit. 'until here and until here') as a physical turning.
