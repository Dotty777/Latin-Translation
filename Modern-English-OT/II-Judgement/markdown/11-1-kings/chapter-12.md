## Text

**12:1** Now Rehoboam came to Shechem, for all Israel had gathered there to appoint him king.

**12:2** Meanwhile, Jeroboam the son of Nebat, while he was still in Egypt as a fugitive from the face of King Solomon, upon hearing of his death, returned from Egypt.

**12:3** And they sent and called him; therefore Jeroboam and the entire multitude of Israel came and spoke to Rehoboam, saying,

**12:4** "Your father placed a most harsh yoke upon us; now therefore, lessen a little of the most harsh rule of your father, and of the heaviest yoke that he placed upon us, and we will serve you."

**12:5** And he said to them, "Go until the third day, and return to me." And when the people had departed,

**12:6** King Rehoboam took counsel with the elders who stood before Solomon his father while he was still alive, and he said, "What counsel do you give me, so that I may answer this people?"

**12:7** And they said to him, "If today you obey this people, and serve them, and yield to their petition, and speak gentle words to them, they will be servants to you through all days."

**12:8** But he abandoned the counsel of the old men that they had given him, and consulted the young men who had been raised with him and stood before him,

**12:9** and he said to them, "What counsel do you give me, so that I may answer this people, who have said to me, 'Make lighter the yoke that your father placed upon us'?"

**12:10** And the young men who had been raised with him said to him, "In this way you shall speak to this people, who have spoken to you, saying, 'Your father made our yoke heavy; you relieve us.' In this way you shall speak to them: 'My little finger is thicker than the back of my father.

**12:11** And now my father laid a heavy yoke upon you, but I will add to your yoke; my father beat you with whips, but I will beat you with scorpions.'"

**12:12** Therefore Jeroboam and all the people came to Rehoboam on the third day, just as the king had spoken, saying, "Return to me on the third day."

**12:13** And the king answered the people harshly, having abandoned the counsel of the elders that they had given him,

**12:14** and he spoke to them according to the counsel of the young men, saying, "My father made your yoke heavy, but I will add to your yoke; my father beat you with whips, but I will beat you with scorpions."

**12:15** And the king did not yield to the people, because the Lord had turned away from him, so that He might establish His word which He had spoken by the hand of Ahijah the Shilonite to Jeroboam the son of Nebat.

**12:16** Therefore the people, seeing that the king had been unwilling to listen to them, answered him, saying, "What share do we have in David? Or what inheritance in the son of Jesse? Go to your tents, Israel! Now see to your own house, David!" And Israel went away to their tents.

**12:17** But Rehoboam reigned over all the sons of Israel who lived in the cities of Judah.

**12:18** King Rehoboam therefore sent Adoram, who was over the tributes, and all Israel stoned him, and he died. Meanwhile, King Rehoboam hastily mounted a chariot and fled to Jerusalem;

**12:19** and Israel withdrew from the house of David to the present day.

**12:20** Now it happened when all Israel had heard that Jeroboam had returned, that they sent and called him after gathering the assembly, and appointed him king over all Israel; and no one followed the house of David except the tribe of Judah alone.

**12:21** Now Rehoboam came to Jerusalem and gathered the entire house of Judah and the tribe of Benjamin, one hundred and eighty thousand chosen fighting men, to fight against the house of Israel and restore the kingdom to Rehoboam the son of Solomon.

**12:22** But the word of the Lord came to Shemaiah, the man of God, saying,

**12:23** "Speak to Rehoboam the son of Solomon, king of Judah, and to all the house of Judah and Benjamin, and the rest of the people, saying,

**12:24** 'This is what the Lord says: You shall not go up, nor shall you wage war against your brothers, the sons of Israel; let each man return to his house, for from Me this thing has come about.'" They listened to the word of the Lord and turned back from the journey, just as the Lord had commanded them.

**12:25** Now Jeroboam built Shechem on Mount Ephraim and lived there; and going out from there, he built Penuel.

**12:26** And Jeroboam said in his heart, "Now the kingdom will return to the house of David,

**12:27** if this people goes up to offer sacrifices in the house of the Lord in Jerusalem; then the heart of this people will turn back to their lord, Rehoboam king of Judah, and they will kill me and return to him."

**12:28** And having devised a plan, he made two golden calves and said to them, "Do not go up to Jerusalem any longer; here are your gods, Israel, who brought you out of the land of Egypt!"

**12:29** And he placed one in Bethel, and the other in Dan;

**12:30** and this thing became a sin, for the people went to worship the calf as far as Dan.

**12:31** And he made shrines on the high places, and priests from the lowest of the people, who were not from the sons of Levi.

**12:32** And he appointed a solemn feast day in the eighth month, on the fifteenth day of the month, in the likeness of the solemn feast that was celebrated in Judah. And going up to the altar, he did likewise in Bethel, to offer sacrifices to the calves that he had fashioned; and he appointed in Bethel priests of the high places that he had made.

**12:33** And he went up onto the altar that he had constructed in Bethel, on the fifteenth day of the eighth month, which he had invented out of his own heart; and he made a solemn feast for the sons of Israel, and went up onto the altar to burn incense.

## Translator Notes

- **12:2** — Native Clementine Vulgate places Jeroboam's return at 12:2 (grouped at Saints.Bible 11:43 following 3 Kingdoms).
- **12:10** — The Vulgate reads "the back of my father" (dorso patris mei), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "my father's loins" or "waist."
- **12:15** — The Vulgate reads "because the Lord had turned away from him" (quoniam aversatus fuerat eum Dominus), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "for it was a turn of affairs from the Lord."
- **12:24** — Native Clementine Vulgate places Abijah's illness and Ahijah's prophecy in chapter 14 (14:1-18), rather than joining them to 12:24 as in Saints.Bible.
- **12:30** — The Vulgate explicitly reads "to worship the calf" (ad adorandum vitulum) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "before the one," and in v. 31 renders the origin of the priests as "from the lowest of the people" (de extremis populi).
