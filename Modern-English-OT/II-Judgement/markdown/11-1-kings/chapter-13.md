## Text

**13:1** And look, a man of God came from Judah by the word of the Lord to Bethel, while Jeroboam was standing over the altar and casting incense.

**13:2** And he cried out against the altar by the word of the Lord and said, "Altar, altar, this is what the Lord says: 'Look, a son will be born to the house of David, Josiah by name, and he will sacrifice upon you the priests of the high places who now kindle incense on you, and he will burn human bones upon you.'"

**13:3** And on that day he gave a sign, saying, "This will be the sign that the Lord has spoken: look, the altar will be split apart, and the ashes that are on it will be poured out."

**13:4** And when the king heard the word of the man of God that he had cried out against the altar in Bethel, he stretched out his hand from the altar, saying, "Seize him." And his hand, which he had stretched out against him, withered, and he was not able to pull it back to himself.

**13:5** The altar also was split apart, and the ashes were poured out from the altar, according to the sign that the man of God had foretold by the word of the Lord.

**13:6** And the king said to the man of God, "Implore the face of the Lord your God, and pray for me, so that my hand may be restored to me." And the man of God prayed to the face of the Lord, and the king's hand returned to him and became as it had been before.

**13:7** Now the king spoke to the man of God, "Come home with me to dine, and I will give you gifts."

**13:8** And the man of God answered the king, "If you give me half of your house, I will not come with you, nor will I eat bread or drink water in this place;

**13:9** for thus it was commanded to me by the word of the Lord, who ordered, 'You must not eat bread, nor drink water, nor return by the way by which you came.'"

**13:10** So he went away by another way, and did not return by the road by which he had come to Bethel.

**13:11** Now a certain old prophet lived in Bethel, to whom his sons came, and they recounted to him all the deeds that the man of God had done that day in Bethel; and the words that he had spoken to the king they recounted to their father.

**13:12** And their father said to them, "By which way did he go?" His sons showed him the way by which the man of God who had come from Judah had gone.

**13:13** And he said to his sons, "Saddle the donkey for me." When they had saddled it, he mounted,

**13:14** and went after the man of God, and found him sitting under a terebinth tree; and he said to him, "Are you the man of God who came from Judah?" He answered, "I am."

**13:15** And he said to him, "Come home with me to eat bread."

**13:16** He said, "I cannot return, nor come with you, nor will I eat bread or drink water in this place,

**13:17** for the Lord spoke to me by the word of the Lord, saying, 'You must not eat bread and you must not drink water there, nor return by the way by which you went.'"

**13:18** He said to him, "I too am a prophet like you, and an angel spoke to me by the word of the Lord, saying, 'Bring him back with you into your house, so that he may eat bread and drink water.'" He deceived him,

**13:19** and brought him back with him; so he ate bread in his house and drank water.

**13:20** And while they were sitting at the table, the word of the Lord came to the prophet who had brought him back.

**13:21** And he cried out to the man of God who had come from Judah, saying, "This is what the Lord says: 'Because you were not obedient to the mouth of the Lord and did not keep the command that the Lord your God commanded you,

**13:22** and returned and ate bread and drank water in the place in which He commanded you not to eat bread or drink water, your corpse will not be brought into the tomb of your fathers.'"

**13:23** And when he had eaten and drunk, he saddled his donkey for the prophet whom he had brought back.

**13:24** When he had departed, a lion found him on the road and killed him, and his corpse was thrown on the path; now the donkey was standing beside him, and the lion was standing beside the corpse.

**13:25** And look, men passing by saw the corpse thrown on the road and the lion standing beside the corpse. And they came and spread the news in the city in which that old prophet lived.

**13:26** When that prophet who had brought him back from the road heard this, he said, "It is the man of God who was disobedient to the mouth of the Lord, and the Lord handed him over to the lion, and it crushed him and killed him, according to the word of the Lord that He spoke to him."

**13:27** And he said to his sons, "Saddle a donkey for me." And when they had saddled it,

**13:28** and he had departed, he found his corpse thrown on the road, and the donkey and the lion standing beside the corpse; the lion had not eaten from the corpse, nor had it harmed the donkey.

**13:29** So the prophet lifted up the corpse of the man of God and placed it upon the donkey, and returning, he brought it into the city of the old prophet to mourn him.

**13:30** And he laid his corpse in his own tomb, and they mourned him: "Oh, oh, my brother!"

**13:31** And when they had mourned him, he said to his sons, "When I die, bury me in the tomb in which the man of God is buried; lay my bones beside his bones.

**13:32** For surely the word that he foretold by the word of the Lord will come about against the altar that is in Bethel, and against all the shrines of the high places that are in the cities of Samaria."

**13:33** After these words Jeroboam did not turn back from his most wicked way, but on the contrary he made priests of the high places from the lowest of the people; whoever wished filled his hand and became a priest of the high places.

**13:34** And for this reason the house of Jeroboam sinned, and was overthrown and wiped out from the surface of the earth.

## Translator Notes

- **13:6** — Latin "Deprecare faciem Domini" ("implore the face of the Lord") and "oravit faciem Domini" ("prayed to the face of the Lord") preserve a literal Hebrew idiom for seeking God's favor.
- **13:12** — Latin "Ostenderunt ei filii sui" ("His sons showed him") agrees with the Septuagint vocalization against the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("his sons had seen"), and Latin uses plural "filii" throughout vv. 11-12.
- **13:17** — Latin "locutus est Dominus ad me in sermone Domini" explicitly states "the Lord spoke to me by the word of the Lord," where the Hebrew has an impersonal passive ("it was said to me by the word of the Lord").
- **13:29** — Latin ends with "ut plangeret eum" ("to mourn him") alone, without the additional phrase "and to bury him" found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **13:33** — Latin "de novissimis populi" ("from the lowest of the people") interprets Hebrew "from all sorts of people," and "implebat manum suam" ("filled his hand") reflects the biblical idiom for priestly consecration, with the implied subject understood either as the applicant or Jeroboam.
