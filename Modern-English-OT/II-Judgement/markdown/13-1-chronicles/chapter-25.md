## Text

**25:1** Therefore David and the officers of the army set apart for service the sons of Asaph, Heman, and Jeduthun, who were to prophesy with lyres, harps, and cymbals according to their number, serving in the office assigned to them.

**25:2** Of the sons of Asaph: Zaccur, Joseph, Nethaniah, and Asarelah, the sons of Asaph, under the hand of Asaph, who prophesied beside the king.

**25:3** As for Jeduthun, the sons of Jeduthun: Gedaliah, Zeri, Jeshaiah, Hashabiah, and Mattithiah, six, under the hand of their father Jeduthun, who prophesied with a lyre over those giving thanks to and praising the Lord.

**25:4** As for Heman also, the sons of Heman: Bukkiah, Mattaniah, Uzziel, Shebuel, Jerimoth, Hananiah, Hanani, Eliathah, Giddalti, Romamti-ezer, Joshbekashah, Mallothi, Hothir, and Mahazioth.

**25:5** All these were the sons of Heman, the king's seer in the words of God, to exalt the horn; and God gave Heman fourteen sons and three daughters.

**25:6** All of them were distributed under the hand of their father for singing in the temple of the Lord, with cymbals, harps, and lyres, for the services of the house of the Lord beside the king: namely, Asaph, Jeduthun, and Heman.

**25:7** Now their number, together with their brothers who taught the song of the Lord, all of them teachers, was two hundred eighty-eight.

**25:8** And they cast lots by their turns, equally, the greater as well as the lesser, the learned and the unlearned alike.

## Translator Notes

- **25:2** — Latin 'juxta regem' literally means 'beside' or 'next to the king' (likewise in 25:6), rendering the Hebrew idiom for 'under the direction of the king.'
- **25:3** — Although the text counts six sons of Jeduthun, only five are named here (the sixth is presumably Shemaiah, Latin Semeias, who receives the tenth lot in 25:17, where the Hebrew has Shimei). The Vulgate also renders the final phrase with plural participles ('super confitentes et laudantes', 'over those giving thanks to and praising'), whereas the Hebrew has infinitives ('in giving thanks and praising').
- **25:7** — Distinctively Vulgate: Latin 'qui erudiebant ... cuncti doctores' reads actively ('who taught ... all of them teachers'), whereas the Hebrew describes them passively as 'trained' and 'skillful.'
- **25:8** — Latin 'doctus pariter et indoctus' ('the learned and the unlearned alike') renders the Hebrew contrast between 'teacher' and 'pupil.'
- **25:17** — The Vulgate reads 'Semeiæ' (Shemaiah, also 'Semeias' in 27:27) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'Shimei.'
