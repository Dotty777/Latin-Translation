## Text

**13:1** Now David took counsel with the tribunes, and the centurions, and all the leaders,

**13:2** and he said to the whole assembly of Israel, "If it pleases you, and the word that I speak goes forth from the Lord our God, let us send to our remaining brothers in all the regions of Israel, and to the priests and Levites who live in the outskirts of the cities, that they may be gathered to us,

**13:3** and let us bring back the ark of our God to us, for we did not seek it in the days of Saul."

**13:4** And the entire multitude answered that it should be done so, for the word had pleased all the people.

**13:5** Therefore David gathered all Israel, from Shihor of Egypt until you enter Hamath, to bring the ark of God from Kiriath-jearim.

**13:6** And David went up, and every man of Israel, to the hill of Kiriath-jearim, which is in Judah, to bring from there the ark of the Lord God who sits above the cherubim, where His name was invoked.

**13:7** And they placed the ark of God upon a new cart from the house of Abinadab; and Uzzah and his brother were driving the cart.

**13:8** Now David and all Israel were celebrating before God with all strength in songs, and with lyres, and harps, and tambourines, and cymbals, and trumpets.

**13:9** Now when he had arrived at the threshing floor of Chidon, Uzzah stretched out his hand to steady the ark, for an unruly ox had tilted it a little.

**13:10** Therefore the Lord became angry against Uzzah and struck him because he had touched the ark; and he died there before the Lord.

**13:11** And David was grieved because the Lord had divided Uzzah; and he called that place Division of Uzzah, until the present day.

**13:12** And he feared God at that time, saying, "How can I bring the ark of God in to me?"

**13:13** And for this reason he did not bring it to himself, that is, into the city of David, but turned it aside into the house of Obed-edom the Gittite.

**13:14** Therefore the ark of God remained in the house of Obed-edom for three months; and the Lord blessed his house and all that he had.

## Translator Notes

- **13:1** — The Vulgate uses the Roman military titles 'tribunes' (tribunis) and 'centurions' (centurionibus) to render the Hebrew commanders of thousands and commanders of hundreds.
- **13:6** — The Vulgate reads 'every man of Israel' (omnis vir Israel) and translates Baalah as 'the hill of Kiriath-jearim' (ad collem Cariathiarim), concluding with 'where His name was invoked' (ubi invocatum est nomen ejus).
- **13:7** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew proper name Ahio as the common noun 'his brother' (frater ejus).
- **13:9** — The Vulgate has a singular verb ('when he had arrived,' pervenisset) and describes 'an unruly ox' that 'had tilted it a little' (bos quippe lasciviens paululum inclinaverat eam), where the Hebrew says the oxen stumbled.
- **13:11** — The Vulgate reads 'was grieved' (contristatus est) rather than 'was angry,' and renders both the verb and the place name Perez-uzza using the Latin root for 'divide' (divisisset ... Divisio Ozae).
