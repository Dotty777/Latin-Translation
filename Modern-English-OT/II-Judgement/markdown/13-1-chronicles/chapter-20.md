## Text

**20:1** Now it happened after the cycle of the year, at that time when kings are accustomed to march out to wars, that Joab gathered the army and the strength of the military force, and laid waste the land of the sons of Ammon; and he marched on and besieged Rabbah. Meanwhile, David was staying in Jerusalem when Joab struck Rabbah and destroyed it.

**20:2** And David took the crown of Milcom from his head, and found in it a talent's weight of gold and most precious gems, and from it he made a diadem for himself; he also took very abundant spoils of the city;

**20:3** and he brought out the people who were in it, and made threshing sledges, harrows, and iron-shod carts pass over them, so that they were cut in pieces and crushed. Thus David did to all the cities of the sons of Ammon, and he returned with all his people to Jerusalem.

**20:4** After these things war broke out in Gezer against the Philistines, in which Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Sippai, of the lineage of the Rephaim, and humbled them.

**20:5** Another war was also waged against the Philistines, in which Adeodatus, the son of Saltus, the Bethlehemite, struck down the brother of Goliath the Gittite, the wooden shaft of whose spear was like a weavers' beam.

**20:6** And yet another war occurred in Gath, in which there was a very tall man having six digits apiece, that is, twenty-four altogether, who himself also had been born from the stock of Rapha.

**20:7** This man reviled Israel, and Jonathan the son of Shimea, the brother of David, struck him down. These are the sons of Rapha in Gath who fell by the hand of David and his servants.

## Translator Notes

- **20:2** — The Vulgate reads the proper name Melchom (Milcom, the Ammonite deity) where the Hebrew consonants can also be vocalized as "their king," and adds "and from it he made a diadem for himself" where the Hebrew states that it was placed on David's head.
- **20:3** — The Vulgate adds "so that they were cut in pieces and crushed," explicitly interpreting the treatment of the Ammonite captives as execution rather than forced labor.
- **20:5** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew names Elhanan ("God has been gracious") and Jair (read as "forest") into Latin as Adeodatus and Saltus, and omits the name Lahmi, identifying the slain Philistine simply as "the brother of Goliath the Gittite."
- **20:7** — In the Clementine Vulgate, chapter 20 contains seven verses; the final sentence of verse 7 corresponds to verse 8 in the Hebrew text.
