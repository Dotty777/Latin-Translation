## Text

**29:1** And King David spoke to the whole assembly: "God has chosen my son Solomon alone, still a boy and very tender; for the work is great, and indeed a dwelling is not being prepared for man, but for God.

**29:2** Now with all my strength I have prepared the supplies for the house of my God: gold for gold vessels, and silver for silver ones, bronze for bronze ones, iron for iron ones, wood for wooden ones, and onyx stones, and stones like antimony and of various colors, and every precious stone, and Parian marble in greatest abundance;

**29:3** and in addition to these things which I have offered for the house of my God, from my own private wealth I give gold and silver for the temple of my God, apart from those things which I have prepared for the holy sanctuary:

**29:4** three thousand talents of gold, of the gold of Ophir, and seven thousand talents of most refined silver for gilding the walls of the temple.

**29:5** And wherever gold is needed, from the gold, and wherever silver is needed, from the silver, let the works be done by the hands of craftsmen; and if anyone offers willingly, let him fill his hand today and offer what he wishes to the Lord."

**29:6** Therefore the leaders of the families and the nobles of the tribes of Israel, the tribunes also and the centurions, and the leaders of the king's possessions made pledges.

**29:7** And they gave for the works of the house of God five thousand talents of gold and ten thousand solidi; ten thousand talents of silver and eighteen thousand talents of bronze; and also one hundred thousand talents of iron.

**29:8** And anyone with whom stones were found gave them into the treasuries of the house of the Lord by the hand of Jehiel the Gershonite.

**29:9** And the people rejoiced when they willingly promised their vows, because with a whole heart they were offering them to the Lord; and King David also rejoiced with great joy.

**29:10** And he blessed the Lord before the entire multitude, and said: "Blessed are You, Lord God of Israel our father, from eternity to eternity.

**29:11** Yours, Lord, are magnificence, and power, and glory, and victory, and to You belongs praise; for all things that are in heaven and on earth are Yours; Yours, Lord, is the kingdom, and You are above all rulers.

**29:12** Yours are riches, and Yours is glory; You rule over all. In Your hand are strength and power; in Your hand are greatness and dominion over all.

**29:13** Now therefore, our God, we give thanks to You, and we praise Your renowned name.

**29:14** Who am I, and who is my people, that we should be able to promise all these things to You? All things are Yours, and what we have received from Your hand we have given to You.

**29:15** For we are foreigners before You, and sojourners, just like all our fathers. Our days are like a shadow on the earth, and there is no lingering.

**29:16** Lord our God, all this abundance which we have prepared so that a house might be built for Your holy name is from Your hand, and all things are Yours.

**29:17** I know, my God, that You test hearts and love sincerity; therefore I also, in the sincerity of my heart, have gladly offered all these things, and with immense joy I have seen Your people who are found here offering gifts to You.

**29:18** Lord God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel our fathers, keep forever this willingness of their heart, and may this mind always remain in reverence toward You.

**29:19** To my son Solomon also give a perfect heart, so that he may keep Your commandments, Your testimonies, and Your ceremonies, and do everything, and build the sanctuary whose supplies I have prepared."

**29:20** Then David commanded the entire assembly: "Bless the Lord our God." And the whole assembly blessed the Lord God of their fathers; and they bowed themselves down, and worshiped God, and then the king.

**29:21** And they sacrificed victims to the Lord, and on the following day they offered burnt offerings: one thousand bulls, one thousand rams, one thousand lambs, with their drink offerings, and with every rite in greatest abundance for all Israel.

**29:22** And they ate and drank before the Lord on that day with great gladness. And they anointed Solomon the son of David a second time. Now they anointed him to the Lord as ruler, and Zadok as high priest.

**29:23** And Solomon sat on the throne of the Lord as king in place of his father David, and he pleased everyone, and all Israel obeyed him.

**29:24** Moreover, all the leaders, and the mighty men, and all the sons of King David gave their hand and were subject to King Solomon.

**29:25** Therefore the Lord magnified Solomon over all Israel, and gave him a glory of kingship such as no king of Israel before him had.

**29:26** Thus David the son of Jesse reigned over all Israel.

**29:27** And the days during which he reigned over Israel were forty years: in Hebron he reigned seven years, and in Jerusalem thirty-three years.

**29:28** And he died in a good old age, full of days, and of riches, and of glory; and his son Solomon reigned in his place.

**29:29** Now the acts of King David, first and last, are written in the book of Samuel the seer, and in the book of Nathan the prophet, and in the scroll of Gad the seer,

**29:30** and those of his entire kingdom, and of his might, and of the times that passed under him, whether in Israel or in all the kingdoms of the lands.

## Translator Notes

- **29:2** — Latin "quasi stibinos" ("like antimony," i.e., dark gleaming stones resembling stibnite/kohl) and "marmor Parium" ("Parian marble," famed white marble from Paros) are distinctive Vulgate renderings of the Hebrew descriptions of decorative stones and marble.
- **29:7** — Latin "solidos" translates the Hebrew darics using the Roman gold coin (solidus), matching the Vulgate's use of Roman military titles ("tribunes" and "centurions") in 29:6.
- **29:15** — Latin "et nulla est mora" ("and there is no lingering" or "no delay") is a Vulgate-distinct reading where the Hebrew has "and there is no hope" (or "abiding").
- **29:20** — The Vulgate reads "Deo nostro" ("our God") where the Hebrew has "your God," and adds "deinde" ("and then") before "the king" to distinguish the assembly's worship ("adoraverunt") of God from royal homage to David.
- **29:23** — Latin "et cunctis placuit" ("and he pleased everyone") is a Vulgate-distinct reading where the Hebrew reads "and he prospered."
