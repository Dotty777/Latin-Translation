## Text

**22:1** And David said, "This is the house of God, and this is the altar for burnt offering for Israel."

**22:2** And he commanded that all the proselytes from the land of Israel be gathered together, and from among them he appointed stonecutters to cut and polish stones, so that the house of God might be built.

**22:3** David also prepared iron in great quantity for the nails of the doors and for the joints and fastenings, and an innumerable weight of bronze.

**22:4** Cedar timbers also could not be estimated, which the Sidonians and the Tyrians had transported to David.

**22:5** And David said, "My son Solomon is a very young and delicate boy, and the house that I want to be built for the Lord must be such that it is named in all regions; therefore I will prepare what is necessary for it." And for this reason, before his death, he prepared all the supplies.

**22:6** And he called his son Solomon and commanded him to build a house for the Lord God of Israel.

**22:7** And David said to Solomon, "My son, it was my desire to build a house for the name of the Lord my God,

**22:8** but the word of the Lord came to me, saying, 'You have shed much blood and have waged very many wars; you will not be able to build a house for My name, since so much blood has been shed before Me.

**22:9** The son who will be born to you will be a most tranquil man, for I will make him rest from all his enemies all around; and for this reason he will be called Peaceful, and I will give peace and quiet in Israel all his days.

**22:10** He himself will build a house for My name, and he himself will be a son to Me, and I will be a father to him; and I will establish the throne of his kingdom over Israel forever.'

**22:11** Now therefore, my son, may the Lord be with you, and prosper, and build a house for the Lord your God, just as He has spoken concerning you.

**22:12** May the Lord also grant prudence and understanding, so that you may be able to rule Israel and to keep the law of the Lord your God.

**22:13** For then you will be able to succeed, if you keep the commands and judgments that the Lord commanded Moses to teach Israel. Be strong and act courageously; do not fear, and do not be terrified.

**22:14** Look, in my poverty I have prepared the supplies for the house of the Lord: one hundred thousand talents of gold and one million talents of silver; and for bronze and iron there is no weighing, for the number is surpassed by the magnitude; I have prepared timber and stones for all the expenses.

**22:15** You also have very many craftsmen: stonecutters and masons, and craftsmen of wood, and men most skilled in all crafts for doing the work,

**22:16** in gold and silver and bronze and iron, of which there is no number. Rise therefore and do it, and the Lord will be with you."

**22:17** David also commanded all the leaders of Israel to help his son Solomon,

**22:18** saying, "You see that the Lord your God is with you, and has given you rest all around, and has delivered all your enemies into your hands, and the land has been subdued before the Lord and before His people.

**22:19** Give therefore your hearts and your souls to seek the Lord your God; and rise up and build a sanctuary for the Lord God, so that the ark of the covenant of the Lord and the vessels consecrated to the Lord may be brought into the house that is being built for the name of the Lord."

## Translator Notes

- **22:2** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate renders Hebrew gerim ("resident aliens" or "sojourners") as proselyti ("proselytes").
- **22:9** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew name Solomon (Shelomoh) into Latin as Pacificus ("Peaceful"), preserving its wordplay with "peace" (pacem) in the next clause.
- **22:14** — The Vulgate reads "in my poverty" (in paupertate mea) where the Hebrew text means "in my affliction" or "with great pains," and concludes with "for all the expenses" rather than "and you may add to them."
- **22:18** — The Vulgate renders the opening clause as a declarative statement ("You see") rather than a rhetorical question, and reads "all your enemies into your hands" where the Hebrew has "the inhabitants of the land into my hand."
