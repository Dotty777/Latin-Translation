## Text

**12:1** These also came to David at Ziklag while he was still fleeing from Saul the son of Kish; they were very mighty and distinguished fighters,

**12:2** drawing the bow, and with either hand hurling stones from slings and shooting arrows, from the brothers of Saul, of Benjamin.

**12:3** The chief was Ahiezer, and Joash, the sons of Shemaah the Gibeathite; and Jeziel and Pelet, the sons of Azmaveth; and Beracah, and Jehu the Anathothite.

**12:4** Also Shemaiah the Gibeonite, the mightiest among the thirty and over the thirty. Jeremiah, and Jahaziel, and Johanan, and Jozabad the Gederathite.

**12:6** And Eluzai, and Jerimoth, and Bealiah, and Shemariah, and Shephatiah the Haruphite.

**12:7** Elkanah, and Isshiah, and Azarel, and Joezer, and Jashobeam of the Korahites;

**12:8** Joelah also, and Zebadiah, the sons of Jeroham of Gedor.

**12:9** Moreover, some from the Gadites also defected to David while he was hiding in the wilderness, very strong men and the finest fighters, holding shield and spear; their faces were like the face of a lion, and they were swift like gazelles on the mountains:

**12:10** Ezer the chief, Obadiah the second, Eliab the third,

**12:11** Mishmannah the fourth, Jeremiah the fifth,

**12:12** Attai the sixth, Eliel the seventh,

**12:13** Johanan the eighth, Elzabad the ninth,

**12:14** Jeremiah the tenth, Machbannai the eleventh.

**12:15** These from the sons of Gad were leaders of the army: the least was over a hundred soldiers, and the greatest over a thousand.

**12:16** These are the ones who crossed the Jordan in the first month, when it is accustomed to flood over its banks; and they put to flight all who were living in the valleys toward the eastern region and the western.

**12:17** Now some also came from Benjamin and from Judah to the stronghold in which David was staying.

**12:18** And David went out to meet them and said, "If you have come peacefully to me to help me, let my heart be joined to you; but if you are plotting against me on behalf of my adversaries, although I have no iniquity in my hands, may the God of our fathers see and judge."

**12:19** Then the Spirit clothed Amasai, chief among the thirty, and he said, "We are yours, David, and with you, son of Jesse! Peace, peace to you, and peace to your helpers, for your God helps you." Therefore David received them and appointed them leaders of the troop.

**12:20** Moreover, some from Manasseh defected to David when he was coming with the Philistines against Saul to fight; yet he did not fight with them, because after taking counsel, the leaders of the Philistines sent him back, saying, "At the peril of our heads he will return to his master Saul."

**12:21** Therefore, when he returned to Ziklag, there defected to him from Manasseh Adnah, and Jozabad, and Jediael, and Michael, and Adnah, and Jozabad, and Elihu, and Zillethai, leaders of thousands in Manasseh.

**12:22** These gave help to David against the raiders, for they were all very mighty men, and they became leaders in the army.

**12:23** Moreover, day by day men were coming to David to help him, until there came to be a great number, like an army of God.

**12:24** This also is the number of the leaders of the army who came to David when he was in Hebron, to transfer the kingdom of Saul to him, according to the word of the Lord.

**12:25** The sons of Judah carrying shield and spear: six thousand eight hundred, ready for battle.

**12:26** Of the sons of Simeon, very mighty men for fighting: seven thousand one hundred.

**12:27** Of the sons of Levi: four thousand six hundred.

**12:28** Jehoiada also, leader of the lineage of Aaron, and with him three thousand seven hundred.

**12:29** Zadok also, a young man of distinguished character, and the house of his father: twenty-two leaders.

**12:30** And of the sons of Benjamin, the brothers of Saul: three thousand; for a great part of them was still following the house of Saul.

**12:31** Furthermore, of the sons of Ephraim: twenty thousand eight hundred, very mighty in strength, renowned men in their clans.

**12:32** And from the half-tribe of Manasseh: eighteen thousand, each individually by their names, came to appoint David king.

**12:33** Of the sons of Issachar also, learned men who knew each of the times so as to direct what Israel ought to do: two hundred leaders; and all the rest of the tribe followed their counsel.

**12:34** Moreover, of Zebulun, those who went out to battle and stood in battle array equipped with weapons of war: fifty thousand came to help, not with a double heart.

**12:35** And of Naphtali: one thousand leaders, and with them, equipped with shield and spear, thirty-seven thousand.

**12:36** Of Dan also, prepared for battle: twenty-eight thousand six hundred.

**12:37** And of Asher, going out to fight and challenging in battle array: forty thousand.

**12:38** And across the Jordan, of the sons of Reuben, and of Gad, and of the half part of the tribe of Manasseh, equipped with weapons of war: one hundred twenty thousand.

**12:39** All these men of war, ready to fight, came with a perfect heart to Hebron to appoint David king over all Israel; and indeed all the rest of Israel were of one heart, that David should become king.

**12:40** And they were there with David for three days, eating and drinking, for their brothers had made preparations for them.

**12:41** Moreover, those who were near them, as far as Issachar and Zebulun and Naphtali, were bringing loaves on donkeys, and camels, and mules, and oxen for eating: flour, fig cakes, raisins, wine, oil, oxen, and rams in full abundance; for indeed there was joy in Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **12:4** — The Clementine Vulgate combines what are verses 4 and 5 in Hebrew and most modern editions into 12:4 (leaving no separate 12:5), and reads Samaias (Shemaiah) where the Hebrew has Ishmaiah.
- **12:9** — The Vulgate reads 'while he was hiding in the wilderness' (cum lateret in deserto), whereas the Hebrew text reads 'to the stronghold in the wilderness.'
- **12:20** — The Vulgate reads 'yet he did not fight with them' (et non dimicavit cum eis), referring to David, where the Hebrew reads 'they did not help them.'
- **12:21** — The Clementine Vulgate lists eight leaders from Manasseh by repeating Adnah and Jozabad (Ednas, et Jozabad) twice, whereas the Hebrew text lists seven names (repeating only Jozabad).
- **12:41** — The Vulgate reads 'rams' (arietes) for the Hebrew collective 'sheep/flocks,' and specifies 'loaves ... for eating' (panes ... ad vescendum).
