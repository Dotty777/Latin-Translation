## Text

**19:1** Now it happened that Nahash king of the sons of Ammon died, and his son reigned in his place.

**19:2** And David said, "I will show mercy to Hanun son of Nahash, for his father showed me favor." And David sent messengers to console him over the death of his father. And when they had arrived in the land of the sons of Ammon to console Hanun,

**19:3** the princes of the sons of Ammon said to Hanun, "Perhaps you think that David, out of honor for your father, has sent those who would console you, and you do not notice that his servants have come to you to explore, and investigate, and examine your land."

**19:4** Therefore Hanun made David's attendants bald and shaved them, and cut off their tunics from the buttocks down to the feet, and sent them away.

**19:5** When they had gone away and reported this to David, he sent to meet them (for they had endured a great disgrace) and commanded that they remain in Jericho until their beards grew, and then return.

**19:6** Now the sons of Ammon, seeing that they had done an injury to David, both Hanun and the rest of the people, sent a thousand talents of silver to hire for themselves chariots and horsemen from Mesopotamia, and from Syria-Maacah, and from Zobah.

**19:7** And they hired thirty-two thousand chariots, and the king of Maacah with his people. When they had come, they encamped opposite Medeba. The sons of Ammon also, gathered from their cities, came to war.

**19:8** When David had heard this, he sent Joab and the entire army of mighty men;

**19:9** and the sons of Ammon went out and drew up the battle line beside the gate of the city, while the kings who had come to his help stood separately in the field.

**19:10** Therefore Joab, realizing that battle was being waged against him in front and at his back, chose the mightiest men from all Israel and marched against the Syrians.

**19:11** And the remaining part of the people he placed under the hand of Abishai his brother, and they marched against the sons of Ammon.

**19:12** And he said, "If the Syrians overpower me, you will be a help to me; but if the sons of Ammon overcome you, I will be a defense for you.

**19:13** Be strong, and let us act like men for our people and for the cities of our God; and the Lord will do what is good in His sight."

**19:14** Therefore Joab and the people who were with him marched against the Syrians to battle, and he put them to flight.

**19:15** Now the sons of Ammon, seeing that the Syrians had fled, themselves also fled from Abishai his brother and entered the city; and Joab also returned to Jerusalem.

**19:16** Now the Syrians, seeing that they had fallen before Israel, sent messengers and brought the Syrians who were across the river; and Shophach, commander of Hadarezer's army, was their leader.

**19:17** When this had been reported to David, he gathered all Israel and crossed the Jordan, and rushed upon them and drew up the battle line opposite them, while they fought against him.

**19:18** And the Syrians fled from Israel, and David killed from among the Syrians seven thousand chariots and forty thousand foot soldiers, and Shophach, commander of the army.

**19:19** Now the servants of Hadarezer, seeing that they had been overcome by Israel, defected to David and served him; and Syria was no longer willing to provide help to the sons of Ammon.

## Translator Notes

- **19:3** — The Vulgate uses three reconnaissance verbs ("explore, and investigate, and examine"), whereas the Hebrew has "search, overthrow, and spy out."
- **19:4** — The Vulgate reads decalvavit, et rasit ("made ... bald and shaved") and a natibus usque ad pedes ("from the buttocks down to the feet"), expanding on the Hebrew ("shaved them and cut off their garments in the middle at the hips").
- **19:18** — The Vulgate literally states that David killed "seven thousand chariots" (septem millia curruum), a metonymy for chariot crews or charioteers.
- **19:19** — The Vulgate reads transfugerunt ad David ("defected to David"), whereas the Hebrew has "made peace with David."
