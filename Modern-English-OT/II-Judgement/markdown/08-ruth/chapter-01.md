## Text

**1:1** In the days of one judge, when the judges were in authority, a famine arose in the land. And a man departed from Bethlehem of Judah to sojourn in the region of Moab with his wife and two children.

**1:2** He was called Elimelech, and his wife Naomi, and two sons, one Mahlon and the other Chilion, Ephrathites from Bethlehem of Judah. And entering the region of Moab, they stayed there.

**1:3** And Elimelech, the husband of Naomi, died, and she herself was left with her sons.

**1:4** They took Moabite wives, one of whom was called Orpah, and the other Ruth. And they remained there for ten years,

**1:5** and both died, namely Mahlon and Chilion; and the woman was left bereft of her two children and her husband.

**1:6** And she arose to go to her homeland with both of her daughters-in-law from the region of Moab, for she had heard that the Lord had looked upon His people and had given them food.

**1:7** Therefore she went out from the place of her sojourning with both daughters-in-law, and now set on the road to return to the land of Judah,

**1:8** she said to them, "Go to the house of your mother. May the Lord show mercy to you, just as you have done with the dead and with me.

**1:9** May He grant you to find rest in the houses of the husbands whom you are going to obtain." And she kissed them. And raising their voices, they began to weep,

**1:10** and to say, "We will go with you to your people."

**1:11** She answered them, "Return, my daughters; why do you come with me? Do I still have sons in my womb, so that you could hope for husbands from me?

**1:12** Return, my daughters, and depart, for I am already worn out with old age and not fit for the bond of marriage. Even if I were able to conceive tonight and bear sons,

**1:13** if you were willing to wait for them until they grow up and complete the years of maturity, you will be old women before you marry. Do not do so, I beg you, my daughters, because your distress presses upon me more, and the hand of the Lord has gone out against me."

**1:14** Therefore, raising their voices, they began to weep again. Orpah kissed her mother-in-law and returned, while Ruth clung to her mother-in-law,

**1:15** and Naomi said to her, "Look, your relative has returned to her people and to her gods; go with her."

**1:16** She answered, "Do not oppose me so that I leave you and depart; for wherever you go, I will go, and where you stay, I likewise will stay. Your people are my people, and your God is my God.

**1:17** Whatever land receives you as you die, in it I will die, and there I will receive a place of burial. May the Lord do these things to me, and add these things, unless death alone separates me and you."

**1:18** Seeing, therefore, that Ruth had resolved with a steadfast mind to go with her, Naomi was unwilling to oppose her, nor any longer to persuade her to return to her own people;

**1:19** and they set out together and came to Bethlehem. When they had entered the city, a swift report spread widely among everyone, and the women were saying, "This is that Naomi."

**1:20** She said to them, "Do not call me Naomi (that is, beautiful), but call me Mara (that is, bitter), because the Almighty has greatly filled me with bitterness.

**1:21** I went out full, and the Lord has brought me back empty. Why then do you call me Naomi, whom the Lord has humbled and the Almighty has afflicted?"

**1:22** Therefore Naomi came with Ruth the Moabite, her daughter-in-law, from the land of her sojourning, and returned to Bethlehem when the barley was first being reaped.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — The Vulgate uniquely specifies "in the days of one judge" (in diebus unius judicis) before adding "when the judges were in authority," whereas the Hebrew reads simply "in the days when the judges judged."
- **1:13** — The Vulgate renders this clause distinctly as "you will be old women before you marry" (ante eritis vetulae quam nubatis), whereas the Hebrew asks rhetorically whether they would refrain from marrying.
- **1:17** — The Vulgate expands the brief Hebrew "where you die, I will die" into the fuller expression "Whatever land receives you as you die, in it I will die, and there I will receive a place of burial."
- **1:20** — Jerome inserted the parenthetical etymological explanations "(that is, beautiful)" and "(that is, bitter)" directly into the Latin text; neither gloss appears in the Hebrew or Greek.
