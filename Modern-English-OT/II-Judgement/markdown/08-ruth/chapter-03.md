## Text

**3:1** Now after she had returned to her mother-in-law, she heard from her, "My daughter, I will seek rest for you, and I will provide so that it may go well for you.

**3:2** This Boaz, whose young women you joined in the field, is our close relative, and tonight he is winnowing the barley threshing floor.

**3:3** Therefore, wash yourself, and anoint yourself, and put on finer garments, and go down to the threshing floor; do not let the man see you until he has finished eating and drinking.

**3:4** Now when he goes to sleep, note the place in which he sleeps; and you will come and uncover the cloak with which he is covered at his feet, and lay yourself down, and lie there; and he himself will say what you ought to do."

**3:5** She answered, "Whatever you command, I will do."

**3:6** And she went down to the threshing floor and did everything that her mother-in-law had ordered her.

**3:7** And when Boaz had eaten and drunk and had become quite cheerful, and had gone to sleep beside the heap of sheaves, she came secretly, and uncovering the cloak, she laid herself down at his feet.

**3:8** And look, already in the middle of the night the man was startled and troubled, and he saw a woman lying at his feet,

**3:9** and he said to her, "Who are you?" And she answered, "I am Ruth, your maidservant. Spread your cloak over your servant, because you are a close relative."

**3:10** And he said, "You are blessed by the Lord, daughter, and you have surpassed your earlier mercy with the later, because you have not followed young men, whether poor or rich.

**3:11** Therefore do not be afraid, but whatever you say to me, I will do for you. For all the people who live within the gates of my city know that you are a woman of virtue.

**3:12** Nor do I deny that I am a close relative, but there is another closer than I am.

**3:13** Rest tonight, and when morning comes, if he is willing to keep you by right of close kinship, the matter is well settled; but if he is unwilling, I myself will take you without any doubt, as the Lord lives. Sleep until morning."

**3:14** So she slept at his feet until the passing of the night. She arose, therefore, before people could recognize one another, and Boaz said, "Take care that no one knows that you came here."

**3:15** And again he said, "Spread out your cloak with which you cover yourself, and hold it with both hands." As she stretched it out and held it, he measured out six modii of barley and placed it upon her. Carrying it, she entered the city,

**3:16** and came to her mother-in-law. She said to her, "What have you done, daughter?" And she recounted to her everything that the man had done for her.

**3:17** And she said, "Look, he gave me six modii of barley, and he said, 'I do not want you to return empty to your mother-in-law.'"

**3:18** And Naomi said, "Wait, daughter, until we see what outcome the matter has, for the man will not rest until he has completed what he has spoken."

## Translator Notes

- **3:10** — The Clementine Latin text reads third-person "est secuta" ("she has not followed"), a known textual variant for second-person "es secuta" ("you have not followed"), which the surrounding second-person address ("superasti," "Noli," "dixeris") requires.
- **3:13** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew kinsman-redeemer terminology in legal kinship terms ("by right of close kinship," propinquitatis jure) and adds the emphatic phrase "without any doubt" (absque ulla dubitatione).
- **3:15** — Where the Hebrew leaves the unit unnamed ("six of barley") and has a masculine verb ("he entered"), the Vulgate specifies "six modii of barley" and uses the feminine participle and verb ("Carrying it, she entered").
- **3:16** — Where the Hebrew asks literally "Who are you, my daughter?" (an idiom for "How did you fare?"), the Vulgate renders it directly as "What have you done, daughter?" (Quid egisti, filia?).
