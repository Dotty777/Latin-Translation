## Text

**4:1** Boaz therefore went up to the gate and sat down there. And when he saw the close relative passing by, about whom mention had been made earlier, he said to him, "Turn aside for a little while and sit down here," calling him by his name. And he turned aside and sat down.

**4:2** Then Boaz, taking ten men from the elders of the city, said to them, "Sit down here."

**4:3** When they were seated, he spoke to the close relative, "Naomi, who has returned from the region of Moab, will sell the portion of our brother Elimelech's field;

**4:4** this I wanted you to hear, and I wanted to tell it to you in the presence of all those seated and of the elders of my people. If you wish to possess it by right of kinship, buy it and possess it; but if it does not please you, inform me of this very thing, so that I may know what I must do, for there is no close relative except you, who are first, and me, who am second." And he answered, "I will buy the field."

**4:5** Boaz said to him, "When you buy the field from the hand of the woman, you must also take Ruth the Moabite, who was the wife of the deceased, so that you may raise up the name of your close relative in his inheritance."

**4:6** He answered, "I relinquish the right of kinship, for I must not wipe out the posterity of my family; you use my privilege, which I declare that I willingly forgo."

**4:7** Now this was the custom in former times in Israel among close relatives, that whenever one relinquished his right to another, so that the concession would be binding, a man would untie his sandal and give it to his neighbor; this was the testimony of relinquishment in Israel.

**4:8** Boaz therefore said to his close relative, "Take off your sandal." And immediately he untied it from his foot.

**4:9** Then he said to the elders and to all the people, "You are witnesses today that I have taken possession of all that belonged to Elimelech and Chilion and Mahlon, with Naomi handing it over,

**4:10** and have taken Ruth the Moabite, the wife of Mahlon, in marriage, in order that I may raise up the name of the deceased in his inheritance, so that his name may not be wiped out from his family and brothers and people. You, I say, are witnesses of this matter."

**4:11** All the people who were in the gate, and the elders, answered, "We are witnesses. May the Lord make this woman, who is entering your house, like Rachel and Leah, who built the house of Israel, so that she may be an example of virtue in Ephrathah and may have a renowned name in Bethlehem;

**4:12** and may your house become like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah, from the seed that the Lord will give you by this young woman."

**4:13** So Boaz took Ruth and received her as his wife, and he went in to her, and the Lord granted her to conceive and bear a son.

**4:14** And the women said to Naomi, "Blessed be the Lord, who has not allowed a successor of your family to be lacking; and may his name be called in Israel,

**4:15** and may you have one who will comfort your soul and nourish your old age, for he has been born of your daughter-in-law, who loves you and is much better to you than if you had seven sons."

**4:16** And taking up the boy, Naomi placed him in her bosom, and she performed the duty of a nurse and of one who carried him.

**4:17** Now the woman's female neighbors, congratulating her and saying, "A son has been born to Naomi," called his name Obed. He is the father of Jesse, the father of David.

**4:18** These are the generations of Perez: Perez fathered Hezron;

**4:19** Hezron fathered Aram; Aram fathered Amminadab;

**4:20** Amminadab fathered Nahshon; Nahshon fathered Salmon;

**4:21** Salmon fathered Boaz; Boaz fathered Obed;

**4:22** Obed fathered Jesse; Jesse fathered David.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — The Vulgate narrates "calling him by his name" (vocans eum nomine suo), whereas the Hebrew places a placeholder expression ("so-and-so") inside Boaz's speech. Throughout this chapter, the Vulgate also uses propinquus ("close relative") and jus propinquitatis ("right of kinship") for the Hebrew kinsman-redeemer terminology.
- **4:8** — In the Vulgate, Boaz is the subject who tells his close relative, "Take off your sandal," and the relative immediately unties it; the Hebrew instead has the relative say to Boaz, "Buy it for yourself," before removing the sandal.
- **4:11** — The Vulgate applies the final two clauses in the third person to Ruth ("so that she may be an example of virtue... and may have a renowned name"), whereas the Hebrew addresses Boaz in the second person.
- **4:17** — The Clementine Vulgate reads Vicinae autem mulieris (genitive singular, "the woman's female neighbors") and adds congratulantes ei ("congratulating her"), where other Latin witnesses read mulieres ("the neighbor women") and the Hebrew lacks "congratulating her."
- **4:19** — The Vulgate preserves the name Aram (matching the Septuagint and the New Testament genealogies in Matthew 1:3-4 and Luke 3:33), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads Ram.
