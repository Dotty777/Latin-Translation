## Text

**2:1** Now her husband Elimelech had a blood relative, a powerful man of great wealth, named Boaz.

**2:2** And Ruth the Moabite said to her mother-in-law, "If you command, I will go into the field and gather the ears of grain that escape the hands of the reapers, wherever I find the favor of a master of a household who is kind toward me." She answered her, "Go, my daughter."

**2:3** So she departed and was gathering ears of grain behind the reapers. Now it happened that the field had an owner named Boaz, who was of the kin of Elimelech.

**2:4** And look, he himself was coming from Bethlehem, and he said to the reapers, "The Lord be with you." And they answered him, "May the Lord bless you."

**2:5** And Boaz said to the young man who was in charge of the reapers, "Whose young woman is this?"

**2:6** He answered him, "This is the Moabite woman who came with Naomi from the region of Moab,

**2:7** and she asked to gather the remaining ears of grain, following the footsteps of the reapers; and from morning until now she has been standing in the field, and she has not returned home even for a moment."

**2:8** And Boaz said to Ruth, "Listen, daughter, do not go into another field to gather, nor depart from this place, but join yourself to my young women,

**2:9** and where they reap, follow. For I have instructed my servants that no one is to trouble you; and also if you grow thirsty, go to the packs and drink the water from which the servants also drink."

**2:10** Falling on her face and bowing down upon the ground, she said to him, "How has this come to me, that I should find favor before your eyes, and that you would deign to acknowledge me, a foreign woman?"

**2:11** He answered her, "Everything that you have done for your mother-in-law after the death of your husband has been reported to me, and that you left your parents and the land in which you were born, and came to a people whom you did not know before.

**2:12** May the Lord repay you for your work, and may you receive a full reward from the Lord God of Israel, to whom you have come, and under whose wings you have taken refuge."

**2:13** She said, "I have found favor in your eyes, my lord, since you have comforted me and spoken to the heart of your maidservant, though I am not like one of your young women."

**2:14** And Boaz said to her, "When the hour for eating comes, come here, and eat bread, and dip your morsel in the vinegar." So she sat at the side of the reapers, and heaped up roasted grain for herself, and ate and was satisfied, and took the leftovers.

**2:15** And she arose from there to gather ears of grain as usual. Now Boaz commanded his servants, saying, "Even if she wants to reap with you, do not forbid her;

**2:16** and also drop some from your own sheaves on purpose, and allow them to remain, so that she may gather without embarrassment, and let no one rebuke her as she gathers."

**2:17** So she gathered in the field until evening, and beating and shaking out with a stick what she had gathered, she found about the measure of an ephah of barley, that is, three modii.

**2:18** Carrying these, she returned to the city and showed them to her mother-in-law; moreover, she brought out and gave her some of the leftovers of her food with which she had been satisfied.

**2:19** And her mother-in-law said to her, "Where did you gather today, and where did you do your work? Blessed be the one who has had mercy on you." And she told her with whom she had worked, and told her the name of the man, that he was called Boaz.

**2:20** Naomi answered her, "May he be blessed by the Lord, because the same favor that he had shown to the living he has kept also for the dead." And again she said, "The man is our close relative."

**2:21** And Ruth said, "He also commanded me this, that I should stay joined to his reapers until all the crops are reaped."

**2:22** Her mother-in-law said to her, "It is better, my daughter, that you go out with his young women to reap, so that no one in another's field may oppose you."

**2:23** So she joined herself to the young women of Boaz, and reaped with them until the barley and the wheat were stored in the granaries.

## Translator Notes

- **2:7** — Where the Hebrew of the final clause is difficult (often taken as "except resting a little in the shelter"), the Vulgate reads clearly "and she has not returned home even for a moment."
- **2:14** — Following the Latin grammar with no change of subject and the reflexive pronoun "sibi," the clause reads "she heaped up roasted grain for herself." Some interpreters take Late Latin "sibi" here with Boaz as the implied subject ("he heaped up roasted grain for her"), matching the Hebrew.
- **2:17** — The Vulgate adds the explanatory gloss "that is, three modii" (id est, tres modios) to interpret the Hebrew ephah; a Roman modius was a dry measure of roughly one peck (about 8.7 liters).
- **2:20** — The implied subject of "had shown ... has kept" is grammatically Boaz (the subject of "May he be blessed"), though as in the Hebrew it is also sometimes taken to refer to the Lord.
