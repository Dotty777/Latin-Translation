## Text

**19:1** There was a certain Levite man living on the side of Mount Ephraim, who took a wife from Bethlehem of Judah,

**19:2** who left him and returned to her father's house in Bethlehem, and stayed with him for four months.

**19:3** And her husband followed her, wanting to be reconciled to her, and to speak tenderly to her, and to bring her back with him, having in his company a servant and two donkeys; and she received him and brought him into her father's house. When his father-in-law heard this and saw him, he went to meet him joyfully,

**19:4** and embraced the man. And the son-in-law stayed in the house of his father-in-law for three days, eating and drinking with him on friendly terms.

**19:5** On the fourth day, rising while it was still night, he wanted to set out; but his father-in-law detained him and said to him, "First taste a little bit of bread and strengthen your stomach, and then you will set out."

**19:6** And they sat down together, and ate and drank. And the father of the young woman said to his son-in-law, "I urge you to stay here today, and let us rejoice together."

**19:7** But rising up, he began wanting to set out. And nevertheless his father-in-law earnestly detained him and made him stay with him.

**19:8** When morning came, the Levite was preparing for the journey. "I ask you," his father-in-law said to him again, "to take a little food, and having regained strength until the day advances, to set out afterward." So they ate together.

**19:9** And the young man rose up to go on his way with his wife and servant. His father-in-law spoke to him again, "Consider that the day is declining further toward sunset and is drawing near to evening; stay with me today also and spend a joyful day, and tomorrow you will set out to go to your own house."

**19:10** The son-in-law would not consent to his words, but went on immediately and came opposite Jebus, which by another name is called Jerusalem, leading with him two loaded donkeys and the concubine.

**19:11** And now they were near Jebus, and the day was turning into night; and the servant said to his master, "Come, I implore you, let us turn aside to the city of the Jebusites and stay in it."

**19:12** His master answered him, "I will not enter a town of a foreign nation, which is not of the sons of Israel, but I will pass on as far as Gibeah,

**19:13** and when I arrive there, we will stay in it, or at least in the city of Ramah."

**19:14** So they passed by Jebus and continued on the journey they had begun, and the sun set on them near Gibeah, which is in the tribe of Benjamin.

**19:15** And they turned aside to it to stay there. When they had entered it, they sat in the square of the city, and no one was willing to receive them for lodging.

**19:16** And look, an old man appeared, returning from the field and from his work in the evening, who himself was also from Mount Ephraim and was living as a foreigner in Gibeah; but the men of that region were sons of Jemini.

**19:17** And lifting up his eyes, the old man saw the man sitting with his small bundles in the square of the city, and he said to him, "Where are you coming from, and where are you going?"

**19:18** He answered him, "We have set out from Bethlehem of Judah, and we are going to our own place, which is on the side of Mount Ephraim, from where we had gone to Bethlehem; and now we are going to the house of God, and no one is willing to receive us under his roof,

**19:19** though we have straw and hay for fodder for the donkeys, and bread and wine for my use and that of your maidservant, and of the servant who is with me; we need nothing except lodging."

**19:20** The old man answered him, "Peace be with you; I will provide everything that is necessary; only, please, do not stay in the square."

**19:21** And he brought him into his house and provided fodder for the donkeys; and after they washed their feet, he received them to a feast.

**19:22** While they were feasting and refreshing their bodies with food and drink after the toil of the journey, the men of that city came, sons of Belial (that is, without a yoke), and surrounding the old man's house, they began to pound on the doors, shouting to the master of the house and saying, "Bring out the man who entered your house, so that we may abuse him."

**19:23** And the old man went out to them and said, "No, brothers, do not do this evil, because the man has entered my lodging; and cease from this folly.

**19:24** I have a virgin daughter, and this man has a concubine; I will bring them out to you so that you may humble them and satisfy your lust; only, I implore you, do not commit this crime against nature upon the man."

**19:25** They would not consent to his words; seeing this, the man brought his concubine out to them and handed her over to them to be violated; and after they had abused her all night, they let her go in the morning.

**19:26** But the woman, as the darkness was receding, came to the door of the house where her master was staying, and collapsed there.

**19:27** When morning came, the man rose up and opened the door to complete the journey he had begun; and look, his concubine was lying before the door with her hands spread out on the threshold.

**19:28** Thinking she was resting, he spoke to her, "Rise up, and let us go." When she answered nothing, realizing that she was dead, he took her and placed her on the donkey, and returned to his own house.

**19:29** When he had entered it, he seized a sword, and cutting the corpse of his wife, together with her bones, into twelve parts and pieces, he sent them into all the territories of Israel.

**19:30** When each one saw this, they cried out together, "Never has such a thing been done in Israel, from the day on which our fathers came up out of Egypt until the present time; render a judgment, and decide in common what needs to be done."

## Translator Notes

- **19:2** — Vulgate reads "who left him" (quae reliquit eum), aligning with a Septuagint tradition rather than the Masoretic Hebrew "was unfaithful to him"; in 19:1, 19:9, and 19:29 the Vulgate also calls her "wife" (uxor) alongside "concubine" (concubina) elsewhere in the chapter.
- **19:16** — Vulgate preserves the distinct form "sons of Jemini" (filii Jemini, reflecting Hebrew bene yemini, meaning Benjaminites), contrasting with "Benjamin" in 19:14.
- **19:18** — Vulgate reads "the house of God" (domum Dei), agreeing with the Septuagint against the Masoretic Hebrew "my house."
- **19:22** — The parenthetical gloss "(that is, without a yoke)" (id est, absque jugo) is Jerome's etymological explanation of Belial embedded directly in the Vulgate text.
- **19:24** — Vulgate explicitly renders the Hebrew "this disgraceful thing" as "this crime against nature" (scelus hoc contra naturam), and similarly renders Hebrew "know" as "abuse" (abutamur) in 19:22.
