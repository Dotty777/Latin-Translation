## Text

**13:1** And again the sons of Israel did evil in the sight of the Lord, who handed them over into the hands of the Philistines for forty years.

**13:2** Now there was a certain man of Zorah, and of the lineage of Dan, named Manoah, having a barren wife.

**13:3** The angel of the Lord appeared to her and said to her, "You are barren and without children, but you will conceive and bear a son.

**13:4** Be careful, therefore, not to drink wine or strong drink, and do not eat anything unclean,

**13:5** for you will conceive and bear a son, whose head no razor will touch; for he will be a Nazirite of God from his infancy and from his mother's womb, and he himself will begin to deliver Israel from the hand of the Philistines."

**13:6** When she came to her husband, she said to him, "A man of God came to me, having an angelic appearance, exceedingly terrifying. When I asked him who he was, and where he had come from, and by what name he was called, he was unwilling to tell me,

**13:7** but he answered this: 'Look, you will conceive and bear a son; be careful not to drink wine or strong drink, and not to eat anything unclean, for the boy will be a Nazirite of God from his infancy, from his mother's womb until the day of his death.'"

**13:8** So Manoah prayed to the Lord and said, "I implore You, Lord, that the man of God whom You sent may come again and teach us what we ought to do concerning the boy who is about to be born."

**13:9** And the Lord heard Manoah as he pleaded, and the angel of God appeared again to his wife as she was sitting in the field; but Manoah her husband was not with her. And when she saw the angel,

**13:10** she hurried and ran to her husband, and told him, saying, "Look, the man whom I had seen before has appeared to me."

**13:11** And he arose and followed his wife; and coming to the man, he said to him, "Are you the one who spoke to the woman?" And he answered, "I am."

**13:12** Manoah said to him, "When your word has been fulfilled, what do you want the boy to do? Or from what will he have to guard himself?"

**13:13** And the angel of the Lord said to Manoah, "Let her abstain from all that I spoke of to your wife,

**13:14** and let her not eat whatever grows from the vine; let her not drink wine or strong drink; let her eat nothing unclean; and let her fulfill and keep what I commanded her."

**13:15** And Manoah said to the angel of the Lord, "I beg you to consent to my requests, and let us prepare for you a young goat from the goats."

**13:16** The angel answered him, "If you compel me, I will not eat your bread; but if you want to make a burnt offering, offer it to the Lord." And Manoah did not know that he was the angel of the Lord.

**13:17** And he said to him, "What is your name, so that, if your word has been fulfilled, we may honor you?"

**13:18** He answered him, "Why do you ask my name, which is wonderful?"

**13:19** So Manoah took a young goat from the goats and drink offerings, and placed them on the rock, offering them to the Lord, who performs wonders; and he himself and his wife were looking on.

**13:20** And as the flame of the altar ascended into heaven, the angel of the Lord likewise ascended in the flame. When Manoah and his wife saw this, they fell face down to the ground,

**13:21** and the angel of the Lord no longer appeared to them. And immediately Manoah realized that he was the angel of the Lord,

**13:22** and he said to his wife, "We will certainly die, because we have seen God."

**13:23** The woman answered him, "If the Lord wanted to kill us, He would not have accepted a burnt offering and drink offerings from our hands, nor would He have shown us all these things, nor would He have told us the things that are to come."

**13:24** So she bore a son and called his name Samson. And the boy grew, and the Lord blessed him.

**13:25** And the Spirit of the Lord began to be with him in the camp of Dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol.

## Translator Notes

- **13:5** — Vulgate includes the doublet ab infantia sua et ex matris utero ("from his infancy and from his mother's womb," repeated in v. 7), where the Hebrew text reads simply "from the womb."
- **13:6** — Vulgate states that the woman asked the visitor who he was, where he had come from, and his name, and he was unwilling to tell her; the Hebrew text instead states that she did not ask where he was from, and he did not tell her his name.
- **13:19** — Vulgate renders Hebrew minchah ("grain offering") as libamenta ("drink offerings," also in v. 23), and makes qui facit mirabilia ("who performs wonders") a relative clause describing the Lord rather than an action performed by the angel during the offering.
- **13:25** — Vulgate reads esse cum eo ("to be with him"), whereas the Hebrew text reads "to stir him" or "to impel him."
