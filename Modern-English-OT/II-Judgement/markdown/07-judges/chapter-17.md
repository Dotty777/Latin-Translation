## Text

**17:1** There was at that time a certain man from Mount Ephraim named Micah,

**17:2** who said to his mother, "The one thousand one hundred pieces of silver that you had set apart for yourself, and over which you had sworn in my hearing, look, I have them, and they are with me." She answered him, "Blessed be my son by the Lord."

**17:3** Therefore he returned them to his mother, who had said to him, "I have consecrated and vowed this silver to the Lord, that my son may receive it from my hand and make a carved image and a cast image; and now I hand it over to you."

**17:4** So he returned them to his mother; and she took two hundred pieces of silver and gave them to a silversmith, so that he would make from them a carved image and a cast image, which was in the house of Micah.

**17:5** He also set apart in it a small shrine for a god, and he made an ephod and teraphim, that is, a priestly garment and idols; and he filled the hand of one of his sons, and he became a priest for him.

**17:6** In those days there was no king in Israel, but each one did what seemed right to himself.

**17:7** There was also another young man from Bethlehem of Judah, from its clan; and he himself was a Levite, and he lived there.

**17:8** And going out from the city of Bethlehem, he wanted to sojourn wherever he might find it suitable for himself. And when he had come into Mount Ephraim while making his journey, and had turned aside briefly into the house of Micah,

**17:9** he was asked by him where he had come from. He answered, "I am a Levite from Bethlehem of Judah, and I am going to live where I am able and perceive it to be useful for me."

**17:10** And Micah said, "Stay with me, and be a father and a priest to me; and I will give you ten pieces of silver each year, and a double garment, and the things that are necessary for sustenance."

**17:11** He agreed and stayed with the man, and he was to him like one of his sons.

**17:12** And Micah filled his hand, and he had the youth as a priest with him,

**17:13** saying, "Now I know that God will do good to me, since I have a priest of the Levitical lineage."

## Translator Notes

- **17:2** — Where the Hebrew reads 'that were taken from you, and about which you uttered a curse,' the Vulgate reads 'that you had set apart for yourself, and over which you had sworn' (quos separaveras tibi, et super quibus ... juraveras).
- **17:5** — The Vulgate inserts an explanatory gloss for 'ephod and teraphim' ('that is, a priestly garment and idols') and preserves the literal idiom 'filled the hand' (implevit ... manum, also in verse 12), meaning to ordain or install as a priest.
- **17:10** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew 'suit of clothes' as 'a double garment' (vestem duplicem).
- **17:13** — The Vulgate reads 'God' (Deus) here, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has the divine name ('the Lord').
