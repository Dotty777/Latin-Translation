## Text

**8:1** And the men of Ephraim said to him, "What is this that you wanted to do, that you did not call us when you went to battle against Midian?" quarreling fiercely and nearly inflicting violence.

**8:2** He answered them, "For what have I been able to do like what you have done? Is not a grape cluster of Ephraim better than the grape harvests of Abiezer?

**8:3** Into your hands the Lord has handed over the princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb; what have I been able to do like what you have done?" When he had spoken this, their spirit, with which they were swelling against him, subsided.

**8:4** And when Gideon had come to the Jordan, he crossed it with the three hundred men who were with him; and because of exhaustion, they were unable to pursue those who were fleeing.

**8:5** And he said to the men of Succoth, "Give, I beg you, loaves of bread to the people who are with me, because they have grown very faint, so that we may be able to pursue Zebah and Zalmunna, the kings of Midian."

**8:6** The princes of Succoth answered, "Perhaps the palms of the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna are in your hand, and for that reason you ask that we give loaves of bread to your army."

**8:7** To them he said, "Therefore, when the Lord has handed over Zebah and Zalmunna into my hands, I will crush your flesh with the thorns and thistles of the wilderness."

**8:8** And going up from there, he came to Penuel, and he spoke similar things to the men of that place. And they also answered him just as the men of Succoth had answered.

**8:9** So he said to them also, "When I have returned as a victor in peace, I will destroy this tower."

**8:10** Now Zebah and Zalmunna were resting with all their army. For fifteen thousand men had remained out of all the troops of the eastern peoples, after one hundred twenty thousand warriors drawing the sword had been cut down.

**8:11** And Gideon, going up by the way of those who lived in tents on the eastern side of Nobah and Jogbehah, struck the camp of the enemies, who were secure and suspected nothing adverse.

**8:12** And Zebah and Zalmunna fled, and Gideon pursued and captured them, after all their army had been thrown into confusion.

**8:13** And returning from the war before the rising of the sun,

**8:14** he seized a boy from the men of Succoth, and he asked him the names of the princes and elders of Succoth, and he wrote down seventy-seven men.

**8:15** And he came to Succoth and said to them, "Here are Zebah and Zalmunna, concerning whom you reproached me, saying, 'Perhaps the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna are in your hands, and for that reason you ask that we give loaves of bread to the men who are weary and have grown faint.'"

**8:16** Therefore he took the elders of the city, and thorns and thistles of the wilderness, and with them he crushed and shattered the men of Succoth.

**8:17** He also overthrew the tower of Penuel, after killing the inhabitants of the city.

**8:18** And he said to Zebah and Zalmunna, "What kind of men were those whom you killed at Tabor?" They answered, "Like you, and one of them was like the son of a king."

**8:19** He answered them, "They were my brothers, the sons of my mother. As the Lord lives, if you had spared them, I would not kill you."

**8:20** And he said to Jether his firstborn, "Rise, and kill them." But he did not draw his sword, for he was afraid, because he was still a boy.

**8:21** And Zebah and Zalmunna said, "Rise yourself, and fall upon us, because a man's strength is according to his age." Gideon rose and killed Zebah and Zalmunna, and he took the ornaments and medallions with which the necks of royal camels are accustomed to be adorned.

**8:22** And all the men of Israel said to Gideon, "Rule over us, you, and your son, and your son's son, because you have delivered us from the hand of Midian."

**8:23** To them he said, "I will not rule over you, nor will my son rule over you, but the Lord will rule over you."

**8:24** And he said to them, "I ask one request of you: give me the earrings from your plunder." For the Ishmaelites were accustomed to have golden earrings.

**8:25** They answered, "We will most gladly give them." And spreading a cloak upon the ground, they threw onto it the earrings from the plunder.

**8:26** And the weight of the requested earrings was one thousand seven hundred shekels of gold, apart from the ornaments, and necklaces, and purple clothing that the kings of Midian were accustomed to use, and besides the golden collars of the camels.

**8:27** And Gideon made an ephod out of it and placed it in his city Ophrah. And all Israel prostituted itself with it, and it became a ruin to Gideon and to all his house.

**8:28** Now Midian was humbled before the sons of Israel, and they could no longer lift up their necks; but the land had rest for forty years, during which Gideon presided.

**8:29** So Jerubbaal, the son of Joash, went away and lived in his own house.

**8:30** And he had seventy sons who came out from his thigh, because he had many wives.

**8:31** Now his concubine, whom he had in Shechem, bore him a son named Abimelech.

**8:32** And Gideon, the son of Joash, died in a good old age, and he was buried in the tomb of Joash his father in Ophrah, of the family of Ezri.

**8:33** Now after Gideon died, the sons of Israel turned away and prostituted themselves with the Baals. And they made a covenant with Baal, that he would be a god to them;

**8:34** and they did not remember the Lord their God, who rescued them from the hands of all their enemies all around,

**8:35** nor did they show mercy to the house of Jerubbaal Gideon, according to all the good things that he had done for Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **8:4** — Whereas the Hebrew reads 'weary, yet pursuing,' the Vulgate reads et prae lassitudine, fugientes persequi non poterant ('and because of exhaustion, they were unable to pursue those who were fleeing').
- **8:10** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew ba-Karkor ('in Karkor') as the verb requiescebant ('were resting') rather than as a place name.
- **8:13** — The Vulgate translates Hebrew 'from the ascent of Heres' as ante solis ortum ('before the rising of the sun'), interpreting heres in its sense of 'sun.'
- **8:32** — The Vulgate reads de familia Ezri ('of the family of Ezri'), corresponding to the Hebrew 'of the Abiezrites' (cf. Abiezer in 8:2).
- **8:33** — Instead of retaining the proper name 'Baal-berith' as in the Hebrew, the Vulgate translates berit as a common noun in Percusseruntque cum Baal foedus ('And they made a covenant with Baal').
