## Text

**7:1** So Jerubbaal, who is also Gideon, rising in the night, and all the people with him, came to the spring that is called Harod. Now the camp of Midian was in the valley on the northern side of the high hill.

**7:2** And the Lord said to Gideon, "The people with you are too numerous, and Midian will not be handed over into their hands, so that Israel may not boast against Me and say, 'By my own strength I have been delivered.'

**7:3** Speak to the people, and proclaim in the hearing of all, 'Whoever is fearful and timid, let him return.'" And they departed from Mount Gilead, and twenty-two thousand men of the people returned, and only ten thousand remained.

**7:4** And the Lord said to Gideon, "The people are still too numerous; lead them to the water, and there I will test them. And the one of whom I say to you that he is to go with you, let him go on; the one whom I forbid to go, let him return."

**7:5** And when the people had gone down to the water, the Lord said to Gideon, "Those who lap the water with their tongue, just as dogs are accustomed to lap, you will set them apart by themselves; but those who drink with bent knees will be on the other side."

**7:6** So the number of those who lapped the water, tossing it with their hand to their mouth, was three hundred men; but all the rest of the multitude had drunk on bent knee.

**7:7** And the Lord said to Gideon, "With the three hundred men who lapped the water I will deliver you, and I will hand Midian over into your hand; but let all the rest of the multitude return to their own place."

**7:8** So having taken provisions and trumpets according to their number, he ordered all the rest of the multitude to depart to their tents; and he himself, with the three hundred men, committed himself to the battle. Now the camp of Midian was below in the valley.

**7:9** That same night the Lord said to him, "Rise, and go down into the camp, because I have handed them over into your hand.

**7:10** But if you are afraid to go alone, let Purah your servant go down with you.

**7:11** And when you hear what they are saying, then your hands will be strengthened, and you will go down more confidently to the camp of the enemies." Therefore he himself and Purah his servant went down to the part of the camp where the watches of armed men were.

**7:12** Now Midian and Amalek, and all the eastern peoples, lay spread out in the valley like a multitude of locusts; the camels also were innumerable, like the sand that lies on the shore of the sea.

**7:13** And when Gideon had come, someone was telling a dream to his companion, and in this manner he was recounting what he had seen: "I saw a dream, and it seemed to me as if a barley loaf baked under the ashes was rolling and coming down into the camp of Midian; and when it had reached the tent, it struck it, and overturned it, and leveled it completely with the ground."

**7:14** The one to whom he was speaking answered, "This is nothing other than the sword of Gideon, the son of Joash, an Israelite man; for the Lord has handed over Midian and all its camp into his hands."

**7:15** And when Gideon had heard the dream and its interpretation, he worshiped; and he returned to the camp of Israel and said, "Rise, for the Lord has handed over the camp of Midian into our hands."

**7:16** And he divided the three hundred men into three divisions, and he put trumpets in their hands, and empty jars, and torches in the middle of the jars.

**7:17** And he said to them, "What you see me doing, do that: I will enter a part of the camp, and what I do, follow.

**7:18** When the trumpet sounds in my hand, you also blow all around the camp and shout together, 'For the Lord and for Gideon!'"

**7:19** And Gideon, and the three hundred men who were with him, entered a part of the camp as the watches of the middle of the night were beginning; and when the guards had been roused, they began to blow the horns and to smash the jars against one another.

**7:20** And when they were sounding in three places all around the camp and had shattered the water jars, they held the torches in their left hands and the sounding trumpets in their right hands, and they shouted, "The sword of the Lord and of Gideon!"

**7:21** standing each in his own place all around the enemy camp. So the entire camp was thrown into confusion, and shouting and wailing, they fled.

**7:22** And nonetheless the three hundred men persisted, sounding the horns. And the Lord sent the sword into the entire camp, and they cut one another down in mutual slaughter,

**7:23** fleeing as far as Beth-shittah and the edge of Abel-meholah in Tabbath. Now the men of Israel from Naphtali, and Asher, and all Manasseh, calling out together, pursued Midian.

**7:24** And Gideon sent messengers into all the hill country of Ephraim, saying, "Go down to meet Midian, and seize the waters as far as Beth-barah and the Jordan." And all Ephraim called out, and seized the waters and the Jordan beforehand as far as Beth-barah.

**7:25** And having captured two men of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb, they killed Oreb at the rock of Oreb, and Zeeb at the winepress of Zeeb. And they pursued Midian, carrying the heads of Oreb and Zeeb to Gideon across the streams of the Jordan.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew gib'at ha-Moreh ('hill of Moreh') descriptively as collis excelsi ('the high hill') and has de nocte consurgens ('rising in the night') where the Hebrew says 'rising early.'
- **7:3** — Unlike the Hebrew, where departing from Mount Gilead is part of the proclamation, the Vulgate puts Recesseruntque de monte Galaad ('And they departed from Mount Gilead') in the past indicative as narrative.
- **7:19** — The Vulgate reads trecenti viri ('the three hundred men') rather than the Hebrew 'one hundred men' for the group entering the camp with Gideon, and specifies that they smashed the jars inter se ('against one another').
- **7:23** — The Vulgate omits 'toward Zererah' (present in the Hebrew after Beth-shittah) and reads in Tebbath ('in Tabbath').
- **7:25** — The Vulgate reads duos viros Madian ('two men of Midian') here rather than 'two princes of Midian' (contrast 8:3, where it uses principes).
