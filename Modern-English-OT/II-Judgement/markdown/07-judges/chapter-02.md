## Text

**2:1** And the angel of the Lord went up from Gilgal to the Place of Those Who Weep, and said, "I led you out of Egypt, and brought you into the land concerning which I swore to your fathers; and I promised that I would not make My covenant with you void forever,

**2:2** provided only that you would not strike a covenant with the inhabitants of this land, but would overthrow their altars; yet you were unwilling to listen to My voice. Why have you done this?

**2:3** For this reason I was unwilling to wipe them out from before your face, so that you may have enemies, and their gods may be a ruin to you."

**2:4** And as the angel of the Lord spoke these words to all the sons of Israel, they lifted up their voice and wept.

**2:5** And the name of that place was called the Place of Those Who Weep, or of Tears; and there they offered sacrifices of the Lord.

**2:6** Therefore Joshua dismissed the people, and the sons of Israel went away, each one to his own possession, to obtain it;

**2:7** and they served the Lord all his days, and those of the elders who lived for a long time after him and knew all the works of the Lord that He had done with Israel.

**2:8** Now Joshua the son of Nun, the servant of the Lord, died at one hundred and ten years of age,

**2:9** and they buried him within the borders of his possession in Timnath-serah in the hill country of Ephraim, on the northern side of Mount Gaash.

**2:10** And all that generation was gathered to their fathers; and others arose who did not know the Lord or the works that He had done with Israel.

**2:11** And the sons of Israel did evil in the sight of the Lord, and served the Baals.

**2:12** And they abandoned the Lord God of their fathers, who had led them out of the land of Egypt, and followed foreign gods and the gods of the peoples who lived around them, and worshiped them; and they provoked the Lord to anger,

**2:13** abandoning Him and serving Baal and Ashtaroth.

**2:14** And becoming angry against Israel, the Lord handed them over into the hands of plunderers, who captured them and sold them to the enemies who lived all around; nor were they able to resist their adversaries,

**2:15** but wherever they wished to go, the hand of the Lord was upon them, just as He spoke and swore to them, and they were severely afflicted.

**2:16** And the Lord raised up judges to rescue them from the hands of those who ravaged them; yet they were unwilling to listen even to them,

**2:17** fornicating with foreign gods and worshiping them. Quickly they deserted the way by which their fathers had walked; and hearing the commands of the Lord, they did everything to the contrary.

**2:18** And whenever the Lord raised up judges, in their days He was moved with mercy, and heard the groans of the afflicted, and rescued them from the slaughter of those who ravaged them.

**2:19** But after the judge had died, they turned back and did much worse things than their fathers had done, following foreign gods, serving them, and worshiping them. They did not abandon their own devices, nor the most stubborn way along which they were accustomed to walk.

**2:20** And the fury of the Lord was aroused against Israel, and He said, "Because this nation has made My covenant void, which I had made with their fathers, and has scorned to listen to My voice,

**2:21** I also will not wipe out the nations that Joshua left behind when he died,

**2:22** so that through them I may test Israel, whether they keep the way of the Lord and walk in it, just as their fathers kept it, or not."

**2:23** Therefore the Lord left all these nations, and was unwilling to overthrow them quickly, nor did He hand them over into the hands of Joshua.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew place name Bochim into Latin as Locum flentium ("the Place of Those Who Weep"), and adds the explanatory gloss sive lacrimarum ("or of Tears") in 2:5.
- **2:3** — Latin nolui delere... ut habeatis hostes, et dii eorum sint vobis in ruinam ("I was unwilling to wipe them out... so that you may have enemies, and their gods may be a ruin to you") differs from the Hebrew, which reads "I also said, I will not drive them out... they will be in your sides, and their gods will be a snare to you."
- **2:5** — The Clementine Vulgate reads the genitive hostias Domini ("sacrifices of the Lord," an objective genitive denoting offerings presented to the Lord) where the Hebrew and some Latin manuscripts have the dative "to the Lord."
- **2:9** — Latin Thamnathsare corresponds to "Timnath-serah" (agreeing with Joshua 19:50 and 24:31), whereas the Masoretic Text of Judges 2:9 reads "Timnath-heres."
- **2:14** — In the Vulgate, the plural relative clause qui ceperunt eos, et vendiderunt makes the plunderers the subject who captured and sold Israel to surrounding enemies, whereas in the Hebrew the Lord Himself is the subject who sold them; similarly, in 2:17 audientes mandata Domini, omnia fecere contraria applies to the rebellious Israelites rather than to their obedient fathers.
