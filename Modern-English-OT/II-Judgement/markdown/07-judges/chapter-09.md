## Text

**9:1** Now Abimelech, the son of Jerubbaal, went to Shechem to his mother's brothers and spoke to them and to all the kindred of the house of his mother's father, saying,

**9:2** "Speak to all the men of Shechem: 'Which is better for you, that seventy men, all the sons of Jerubbaal, have dominion over you, or that one man have dominion? And at the same time consider that I am your bone and your flesh.'"

**9:3** And his mother's brothers spoke all these words concerning him to all the men of Shechem, and they inclined their heart after Abimelech, saying, "He is our brother."

**9:4** And they gave him seventy pounds of silver from the temple of Baal-berith. With it he hired for himself destitute and wandering men, and they followed him.

**9:5** And he came to his father's house in Ophrah and killed his brothers, the sons of Jerubbaal, seventy men, upon one stone; yet Jotham, the youngest son of Jerubbaal, remained and was hidden.

**9:6** Now all the men of Shechem and all the families of the city of Millo gathered together, and they went and appointed Abimelech as king beside the oak that stood in Shechem.

**9:7** When this was reported to Jotham, he went and stood on the summit of Mount Gerizim; and lifting up his voice, he cried out and said, "Listen to me, men of Shechem; so may God listen to you.

**9:8** The trees went to anoint a king over themselves, and they said to the olive tree, 'Rule over us.'

**9:9** It answered, 'Can I abandon my richness, which both gods and men use, and come to be promoted among the trees?'

**9:10** And the trees said to the fig tree, 'Come, and receive the kingdom over us.'

**9:11** It answered them, 'Can I abandon my sweetness and most delightful fruits, and go to be promoted among the rest of the trees?'

**9:12** And the trees spoke to the vine, 'Come, and rule over us.'

**9:13** It answered them, 'Can I abandon my wine, which gladdens God and men, and be promoted among the rest of the trees?'

**9:14** And all the trees said to the bramble, 'Come, and rule over us.'

**9:15** It answered them, 'If you truly appoint me as king for yourselves, come and rest under my shade; but if you are not willing, let fire come out from the bramble and devour the cedars of Lebanon.'

**9:16** Now therefore, if you have rightly and without sin appointed Abimelech as king over yourselves, and have dealt well with Jerubbaal and with his house, and have rendered a return for the benefits of him who fought for you,

**9:17** and gave his life over to dangers so that he might rescue you from the hand of Midian,

**9:18** you who have now risen up against my father's house, and killed his sons, seventy men, upon one stone, and appointed Abimelech, the son of his female servant, as king over the inhabitants of Shechem, because he is your brother;

**9:19** if then you have dealt rightly and without fault with Jerubbaal and his house, rejoice today in Abimelech, and let him rejoice in you.

**9:20** But if perversely, let fire come out from him and consume the inhabitants of Shechem and the town of Millo; and let fire come out from the men of Shechem and from the town of Millo and devour Abimelech."

**9:21** When he had said these things, he fled and went away to Beer, and he lived there out of fear of his brother Abimelech.

**9:22** So Abimelech reigned over Israel for three years.

**9:23** And the Lord sent a most evil spirit between Abimelech and the inhabitants of Shechem, who began to detest him

**9:24** and to lay the crime of the killing of the seventy sons of Jerubbaal, and the shedding of their blood, upon Abimelech their brother and upon the rest of the rulers of the Shechemites who had helped him.

**9:25** And they set ambushes against him on the top of the mountains; and while they waited for his arrival, they practiced robbery, carrying off plunder from those passing by; and it was reported to Abimelech.

**9:26** Now Gaal, the son of Obed, came with his brothers and passed over into Shechem. Emboldened at his arrival, the inhabitants of Shechem

**9:27** went out into the fields, laying waste the vineyards and treading the grapes; and having formed choirs of singers, they entered the temple of their god, and amid feasts and cups they cursed Abimelech,

**9:28** while Gaal, the son of Obed, cried out, "Who is Abimelech, and what is Shechem, that we should serve him? Is he not the son of Jerubbaal, and did he not appoint Zebul his servant as ruler over the men of Hamor, the father of Shechem? Why then will we serve him?

**9:29** If only someone would place this people under my hand, so that I might remove Abimelech from the midst!" And it was said to Abimelech, "Gather a multitude of an army, and come."

**9:30** For Zebul, the ruler of the city, having heard the words of Gaal, the son of Obed, became very angry,

**9:31** and secretly sent messengers to Abimelech, saying, "Look, Gaal, the son of Obed, has come to Shechem with his brothers, and is assailing the city against you.

**9:32** Rise up therefore by night with the people who are with you, and lie hidden in the field;

**9:33** and early in the morning, as the sun rises, rush upon the city. And when he comes out against you with his people, do to him whatever you can."

**9:34** So Abimelech rose up by night with all his army and set ambushes beside Shechem in four places.

**9:35** And Gaal, the son of Obed, went out and stood at the entrance of the gate of the city. Then Abimelech, and all the army with him, rose up from the place of ambush.

**9:36** And when Gaal saw the people, he said to Zebul, "Look, a multitude is coming down from the mountains." He answered him, "You are seeing the shadows of the mountains like heads of men, and you are deceived by this error."

**9:37** And again Gaal said, "Look, people are coming down from the navel of the land, and one column is coming by the road that faces the oak."

**9:38** Zebul said to him, "Where is your mouth now, with which you were saying, 'Who is Abimelech, that we should serve him?' Is this not the people whom you despised? Go out and fight against them."

**9:39** Therefore Gaal went out, while the people of the Shechemites looked on, and fought against Abimelech,

**9:40** who pursued him as he fled and drove him into the city; and very many on his side fell, as far as the gate of the city.

**9:41** And Abimelech stayed in Rumah; but Zebul drove Gaal and his companions out of the city and did not allow them to remain in it.

**9:42** On the following day, therefore, the people went out into the plain. When this was reported to Abimelech,

**9:43** he took his army and divided it into three divisions, setting ambushes in the fields. And seeing that the people were coming out of the city, he rose up and rushed upon them

**9:44** with his column, assailing and besieging the city; while the two divisions pursued the adversaries scattered across the plain.

**9:45** Moreover, Abimelech assailed the city all that day; and he captured it, having killed its inhabitants and destroyed the city itself, so that he scattered salt in it.

**9:46** When those who lived in the tower of the Shechemites heard this, they entered the temple of their god Berith, where they had made a covenant with him, and from it the place had received its name; and it was very strongly fortified.

**9:47** Abimelech also, hearing that the men of the tower of the Shechemites were massed together,

**9:48** went up Mount Zalmon with all his people; and seizing an axe, he cut off a branch of a tree, and carrying it placed upon his shoulder, he said to his companions, "What you see me doing, do quickly."

**9:49** Therefore, eagerly cutting off branches from the trees, they followed their leader. Surrounding the stronghold, they set it on fire; and thus it happened that by smoke and fire a thousand people were killed, men and women alike, of the inhabitants of the tower of Shechem.

**9:50** Then Abimelech, setting out from there, came to the town of Thebez, which he surrounded and besieged with his army.

**9:51** Now there was a high tower in the middle of the city, to which men and women together, and all the rulers of the city, had fled for refuge, with the door shut very securely, and standing on the roof of the tower along the battlements.

**9:52** And Abimelech, coming up beside the tower, fought vigorously; and approaching the doorway, he strove to put fire beneath it;

**9:53** and look, one woman, throwing a piece of a millstone from above, dashed it against the head of Abimelech and crushed his brain.

**9:54** He quickly called his armor-bearer and said to him, "Unsheathe your sword and strike me, so that it may not perhaps be said that I was killed by a woman." Carrying out the orders, he killed him.

**9:55** And when he was dead, all those from Israel who were with him returned to their own dwellings;

**9:56** and God repaid the evil that Abimelech had done against his father by killing his seventy brothers.

**9:57** What they had done was also repaid to the Shechemites, and the curse of Jotham, the son of Jerubbaal, came upon them.

## Translator Notes

- **9:9** — Latin reads 'which both gods (dii) and men use (utuntur)' and 'be promoted (promovear) among the trees' (also vv. 11, 13), whereas the Hebrew speaks of honoring God/gods and men and swaying over the trees; Latin also distinguishes plural dii ('gods') here from singular Deum ('God') in v. 13.
- **9:23** — Latin reads Dominus ('the Lord') where the Hebrew has Elohim ('God'), and links vv. 23-24 syntactically so that the inhabitants of Shechem themselves begin to detest Abimelech and lay the killing of Jerubbaal's seventy sons upon him and the Shechemite rulers.
- **9:26** — Latin consistently gives the name of Gaal's father as Obed (vv. 26, 28, 30, 31, 35), following a Greek textual tradition, whereas the Hebrew reads Ebed.
- **9:28** — Latin renders the clause concerning Zebul as a continuation of Gaal's rhetorical question ('and did he not appoint Zebul...'), whereas the Hebrew contains an imperative ('Serve the men of Hamor...'); in v. 29 Latin similarly has a passive ('And it was said to Abimelech') rather than Gaal continuing to speak.
- **9:41** — Latin reads in Ruma ('in Rumah'), reflecting a place name without the initial vowel of Hebrew Arumah.
- **9:46** — Latin reads 'their god Berith' (omitting El- of Hebrew El-berith) and includes an explanatory expansion ('where they had made a covenant with him, and from it the place had received its name; and it was very strongly fortified') interpreting the meaning of Berith ('covenant') and the fortified stronghold.
