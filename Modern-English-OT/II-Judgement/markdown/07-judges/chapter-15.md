## Text

**15:1** Now after a short time, when the days of the wheat harvest were at hand, Samson came, wanting to visit his wife, and he brought her a young goat from the goats. And when he wanted to enter her inner room as usual, her father stopped him, saying,

**15:2** "I thought that you hated her, and therefore I gave her to your friend; but she has a sister who is younger and more beautiful than she is. Let her be a wife for you in her place."

**15:3** Samson answered him, "From this day there will be no blame on me against the Philistines, for I will do harm to you."

**15:4** And he went and caught three hundred foxes, and joined their tails tail to tail, and bound torches in the middle.

**15:5** Setting the torches on fire, he let the foxes go so that they would run here and there. They immediately went into the grainfields of the Philistines. When these were set on fire, both the crops already gathered and those still standing on the stalk were burned up, to such an extent that the flame consumed the vineyards and olive groves as well.

**15:6** And the Philistines said, "Who did this thing?" It was said to them, "Samson, the son-in-law of the Timnite; because he took his wife and gave her to another, he has done these things." And the Philistines went up and burned both the woman and her father.

**15:7** Samson said to them, "Even though you have done these things, yet I will still exact vengeance from you, and then I will rest."

**15:8** And he struck them with a great blow, so that, stunned, they placed calf upon thigh. And going down, he lived in the cave of the rock of Etam.

**15:9** Therefore the Philistines, going up into the land of Judah, encamped in the place that afterward was called Lehi, that is, Jawbone, where their army was spread out.

**15:10** And those from the tribe of Judah said to them, "Why have you come up against us?" They answered, "We have come to bind Samson and to repay him what he has done against us."

**15:11** Therefore three thousand men of Judah went down to the cavern of the crag of Etam, and they said to Samson, "Do you not know that the Philistines rule over us? Why did you want to do this?" He said to them, "Just as they did to me, so I did to them."

**15:12** "We have come," they said, "to bind you and to hand you over into the hands of the Philistines." Samson said to them, "Swear and pledge to me that you will not kill me."

**15:13** They said, "We will not kill you, but we will hand you over bound." And they bound him with two new ropes and brought him from the rock of Etam.

**15:14** When he had come to the place of the Jawbone, and the Philistines had come shouting to meet him, the Spirit of the Lord rushed upon him; and just as flax is accustomed to be consumed at the scent of fire, so the bonds with which he had been bound were broken apart and loosened.

**15:15** And finding a jawbone, that is, the lower jaw of a donkey, which was lying there, he seized it and killed a thousand men with it.

**15:16** And he said, "With the jawbone of a donkey, with the lower jaw of the colt of female donkeys, I have destroyed them and struck a thousand men."

**15:17** And when he had finished singing these words, he threw the lower jaw from his hand, and he called the name of that place Ramath-lehi, which is interpreted, Lifting Up of the Jawbone.

**15:18** And being very thirsty, he cried out to the Lord and said, "You have given into the hand of Your servant this very great deliverance and victory; look, I am dying of thirst, and I will fall into the hands of the uncircumcised."

**15:19** Therefore the Lord opened a molar tooth in the jawbone of the donkey, and waters came out from it. Having drunk them, he refreshed his spirit and recovered his strength. For that reason the name of that place was called Spring of the One Calling from the Jawbone, until the present day.

**15:20** And he judged Israel in the days of the Philistines for twenty years.

## Translator Notes

- **15:8** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew idiom 'hip upon thigh' (denoting ruthless slaughter) as a result clause describing the Philistines' astonishment: 'so that, stunned, they placed calf upon thigh' (ita ut stupentes suram femori imponerent).
- **15:16** — Where the Hebrew plays on the word chamor ('donkey'/'heap') as 'heaps upon heaps,' the Vulgate renders a poetic parallel: 'with the lower jaw of the colt of female donkeys, I have destroyed them' (in mandibula pulli asinarum, delevi eos).
- **15:17** — The Vulgate adds 'singing' (canens) and supplies an explanatory Latin gloss for the Hebrew place name Ramath-lehi ('Lifting Up of the Jawbone,' Elevatio maxillæ), as it also does for Lehi in verse 9.
- **15:19** — The Vulgate understands Hebrew makhtesh ('hollow' or 'tooth socket') as 'a molar tooth in the jawbone of the donkey' (molarem dentem in maxilla asini) rather than a hollow in the ground at Lehi, and translates the place name En-hakkore as 'Spring of the One Calling from the Jawbone.'
