## Text

**14:1** Then Samson went down to Timnah; and seeing a woman there from the daughters of the Philistines,

**14:2** he went up and told his father and his mother, saying, "I saw a woman in Timnah from the daughters of the Philistines; I ask that you take her for me as a wife."

**14:3** His father and his mother said to him, "Is there not a woman among the daughters of your brothers, and among all my people, that you want to take a wife from the Philistines, who are uncircumcised?" And Samson said to his father, "Take this one for me, because she has pleased my eyes."

**14:4** Now his parents did not know that the matter was coming about from the Lord, and that He was seeking an occasion against the Philistines; for at that time the Philistines were ruling over Israel.

**14:5** So Samson went down with his father and mother to Timnah. And when they came to the vineyards of the town, a fierce and roaring young lion appeared and came to meet him.

**14:6** Then the Spirit of the Lord rushed upon Samson, and he tore the lion apart, as though ripping a young goat into pieces, having nothing at all in his hand; and he was unwilling to reveal this to his father and mother.

**14:7** And he went down and spoke to the woman who had pleased his eyes.

**14:8** And after some days, returning to take her, he turned aside to see the carcass of the lion, and look, a swarm of bees was in the mouth of the lion, and a honeycomb.

**14:9** When he had taken it in his hands, he ate along the way; and coming to his father and mother, he gave them a portion, and they themselves also ate; yet he did not want to reveal to them that he had taken the honey from the body of the lion.

**14:10** So his father went down to the woman, and he made a feast for his son Samson, for young men were accustomed to do so.

**14:11** Then, when the townspeople of that place saw him, they gave him thirty companions to be with him.

**14:12** Samson spoke to them, "I will propose a riddle to you; if you solve it for me within the seven days of the feast, I will give you thirty linen garments and just as many tunics;

**14:13** but if you are not able to solve it, you will give me thirty linen garments and the same number of tunics." And they answered him, "Propose the riddle, so that we may hear it."

**14:14** And he said to them, "From the eater came food, and from the strong came sweetness." And for three days they were not able to solve the riddle.

**14:15** And when the seventh day arrived, they said to Samson's wife, "Coax your husband and persuade him to reveal to you what the riddle means; but if you are unwilling to do this, we will burn you and your father's house. Or did you invite us to the wedding for this reason, so that you might plunder us?"

**14:16** And she poured out tears before Samson and complained, saying, "You hate me and do not love me; for that reason you do not want to explain to me the riddle that you proposed to the sons of my people." But he answered, "I was unwilling to tell my father and mother, and will I be able to reveal it to you?"

**14:17** Therefore, during the seven days of the feast she wept before him; and at last on the seventh day, because she was troubling him, he explained it. And she immediately revealed it to her townspeople.

**14:18** And they said to him on the seventh day before sunset, "What is sweeter than honey, and what is stronger than a lion?" And he said to them, "If you had not plowed with my heifer, you would not have found out my riddle."

**14:19** Then the Spirit of the Lord rushed upon him, and he went down to Ashkelon and struck down thirty men there, whose garments he took and gave to those who had solved the riddle. And exceedingly angry, he went up to his father's house,

**14:20** but his wife took as a husband one of his friends and groomsmen.

## Translator Notes

- **14:6** — Clementine Vulgate prints in frustra, a traditional spelling variant of in frusta ("into pieces").
- **14:8** — Vulgate specifically places the swarm of bees in ore leonis ("in the mouth of the lion"), whereas the Hebrew text reads "in the carcass/body of the lion" (compare de corpore leonis in v. 9).
- **14:10** — Vulgate makes Samson's father the subject who gave the feast (fecit filio suo Samson convivium, "made a feast for his son Samson"), whereas the Hebrew text states that Samson himself made the feast there.
- **14:20** — Vulgate makes Samson's wife the active subject (accepit maritum, "took as a husband"), whereas the Hebrew text uses a passive construction ("was given to").
