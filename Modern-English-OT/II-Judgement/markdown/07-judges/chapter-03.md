## Text

**3:1** These are the nations that the Lord left behind so that He might instruct Israel through them, and all who had not known the wars of the Canaanites,

**3:2** so that afterward their sons might learn to fight with enemies and to have the practice of waging war:

**3:3** the five satraps of the Philistines, and every Canaanite, and the Sidonian, and the Hivite who lived on Mount Lebanon, from Mount Baal-hermon as far as the entrance of Hamath.

**3:4** And He left them so that through them He might test Israel, whether they would listen to the commandments of the Lord that He had commanded their fathers by the hand of Moses, or not.

**3:5** So the sons of Israel lived in the midst of the Canaanite, and the Hittite, and the Amorite, and the Perizzite, and the Hivite, and the Jebusite;

**3:6** and they took their daughters as wives, and they themselves gave their own daughters to their sons, and they served their gods.

**3:7** And they did evil in the sight of the Lord, and they forgot their God, serving the Baals and Ashtaroth.

**3:8** And angered against Israel, the Lord handed them over into the hands of Cushan-rishathaim, king of Mesopotamia, and they served him for eight years.

**3:9** And they cried out to the Lord, who raised up a savior for them and delivered them, namely, Othniel the son of Kenaz, the younger brother of Caleb.

**3:10** And the Spirit of the Lord was in him, and he judged Israel. And he went out to battle, and the Lord handed over into his hands Cushan-rishathaim, king of Syria, and he overpowered him.

**3:11** And the land rested for forty years, and Othniel the son of Kenaz died.

**3:12** Now the sons of Israel again did evil in the sight of the Lord, who strengthened Eglon, king of Moab, against them, because they did evil in His sight.

**3:13** And He joined to him the sons of Ammon and Amalek; and he went and struck Israel, and took possession of the city of palms.

**3:14** And the sons of Israel served Eglon, king of Moab, for eighteen years.

**3:15** And afterward they cried out to the Lord, who raised up for them a savior named Ehud, the son of Gera, the son of Jemini, who used either hand as a right hand. And the sons of Israel sent gifts through him to Eglon, king of Moab.

**3:16** And he made for himself a double-edged sword, having in the middle a hilt the length of the palm of a hand, and he fastened it on beneath his cloak on his right thigh.

**3:17** And he presented the gifts to Eglon, king of Moab. Now Eglon was exceedingly fat.

**3:18** And when he had presented the gifts to him, he escorted the companions who had come with him.

**3:19** And having returned from Gilgal, where the idols were, he said to the king, "I have a secret word for you, king." And he commanded silence; and when all who were around him had gone out,

**3:20** Ehud went in to him; now he was sitting alone in the summer upper room; and he said, "I have a word of God for you." And he immediately rose from the throne.

**3:21** And Ehud stretched out his left hand and took the dagger from his right thigh, and drove it into his belly

**3:22** so forcefully that the hilt followed the blade into the wound and was gripped tightly by the thickest fat. Nor did he draw out the sword, but just as he had struck, he left it in the body; and immediately through the secret passages of nature the excrement of the bowels burst forth.

**3:23** Now Ehud, having very carefully closed the doors of the upper room and secured them with the bolt,

**3:24** went out through the back door. And the servants of the king came in and saw the doors of the upper room closed, and they said, "Perhaps he is emptying his bowels in the summer chamber."

**3:25** And waiting a long time until they were embarrassed, and seeing that no one was opening them, they took the key; and opening them, they found their lord lying dead on the ground.

**3:26** Meanwhile Ehud, while they were in confusion, escaped and passed by the place of the idols from which he had turned back. And he came to Seirah,

**3:27** and immediately he sounded the trumpet in Mount Ephraim, and the sons of Israel went down with him, while he himself marched at the front.

**3:28** And he said to them, "Follow me, for the Lord has handed over our enemies the Moabites into our hands." And they went down after him and seized the fords of the Jordan that lead across into Moab, and they did not allow anyone to cross over;

**3:29** rather, they struck down the Moabites at that time, about ten thousand, all sturdy and strong men. Not one of them was able to escape.

**3:30** And Moab was humbled on that day under the hand of Israel, and the land rested for eighty years.

**3:31** After him was Shamgar the son of Anath, who struck down six hundred men of the Philistines with a plowshare; and he also defended Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **3:10** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew Aram-naharaim as Mesopotamia in 3:8 but as Syria here in 3:10, and reads 'in him' (in eo) where the Hebrew has 'upon him.'
- **3:13** — Latin copulavit ei uses the non-reflexive pronoun ('to him'), grammatically continuing the Lord as subject from 3:12, whereas the Hebrew is commonly read reflexively of Eglon ('he gathered to himself').
- **3:15** — Latin renders the Hebrew gentilic 'the Benjamite' literally as a patronymic (filii Jemini, 'the son of Jemini'), and interprets Hebrew 'restricted in his right hand' (left-handed) as ambidexterity ('used either hand as a right hand').
- **3:16** — Where the Hebrew Masoretic Text specifies the sword's length as a gomed (a short cubit), the Vulgate describes a central hilt (in medio capulum) the length of the palm of a hand.
- **3:31** — Vulgate vomere ('with a plowshare') differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's 'with an oxgoad.'
