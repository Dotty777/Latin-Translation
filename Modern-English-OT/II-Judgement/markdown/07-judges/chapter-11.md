## Text

**11:1** At that time there was Jephthah the Gileadite, a very mighty man and a warrior, the son of a woman who was a prostitute, who was born of Gilead.

**11:2** Now Gilead had a wife by whom he had sons; and after they had grown up, they drove out Jephthah, saying, "You will not be able to be an heir in our father's house, because you were born of another mother."

**11:3** Fleeing from them and avoiding them, he lived in the land of Tob; and men who were destitute and practiced banditry gathered to him, and they followed him as a chief.

**11:4** In those days the sons of Ammon were fighting against Israel.

**11:5** While they were pressing fiercely, the elders of Gilead set out to bring Jephthah from the land of Tob to their aid;

**11:6** and they said to him, "Come and be our chief, and fight against the sons of Ammon."

**11:7** He answered them, "Are you not the ones who hated me and drove me out of my father's house? And now you have come to me, driven by necessity."

**11:8** And the chiefs of Gilead said to Jephthah, "For this reason, therefore, we have now come to you, so that you may set out with us and fight against the sons of Ammon, and be the leader of all who live in Gilead."

**11:9** Jephthah also said to them, "If you have truly come to me so that I may fight for you against the sons of Ammon, and the Lord gives them over into my hands, will I be your chief?"

**11:10** They answered him, "The Lord, who hears these things, He Himself is mediator and witness that we will carry out our promises."

**11:11** So Jephthah went with the chiefs of Gilead, and all the people made him their chief. And Jephthah spoke all his words before the Lord in Mizpah.

**11:12** And he sent messengers to the king of the sons of Ammon to say on his behalf, "What is there between me and you, that you have come against me to devastate my land?"

**11:13** He answered them, "Because Israel took my land when he went up from Egypt, from the borders of the Arnon as far as the Jabbok and the Jordan; now, therefore, return it to me in peace."

**11:14** Through them Jephthah sent word again and commanded them to say to the king of Ammon,

**11:15** "This is what Jephthah says: Israel did not take the land of Moab, nor the land of the sons of Ammon;

**11:16** but when they went up from Egypt, he walked through the wilderness as far as the Red Sea and came to Kadesh.

**11:17** And he sent messengers to the king of Edom, saying, 'Let me pass through your land.' Yet he refused to yield to his pleas. He also sent to the king of Moab, who likewise disdained to grant passage. Therefore he remained in Kadesh,

**11:18** and went around along the side of the land of Edom and the land of Moab, and came opposite the eastern region of the land of Moab, and encamped across the Arnon; nor did he wish to enter the territory of Moab. (For the Arnon is the boundary of the land of Moab.)

**11:19** So Israel sent messengers to Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon, and they said to him, 'Let me pass through your land as far as the river.'

**11:20** He too, despising the words of Israel, did not allow him to pass through his territory; instead, having gathered a countless multitude, he went out against him at Jahaz and resisted vigorously.

**11:21** And the Lord gave him over, together with all his army, into the hands of Israel, who struck him down and possessed all the land of the Amorite, the inhabitant of that region,

**11:22** and all its borders, from the Arnon as far as the Jabbok, and from the wilderness as far as the Jordan.

**11:23** Therefore the Lord, the God of Israel, overthrew the Amorite while His people Israel fought against him, and do you now want to possess his land?

**11:24** Are not the things that Chemosh your god possesses owed to you by right? Yet the things that the Lord our God has obtained as victor will pass into our possession;

**11:25** unless perhaps you are better than Balak the son of Zippor, king of Moab, or can prove that he contended against Israel and fought against him,

**11:26** when he lived in Heshbon and its villages, and in Aroer and its settlements, or in all the cities beside the Jordan, for three hundred years. Why in so long a time did you attempt nothing regarding this claim?

**11:27** Therefore I am not sinning against you, but you are acting wrongly against me, declaring unjust wars on me. May the Lord, the arbiter of this day, judge between Israel and the sons of Ammon."

**11:28** And the king of the sons of Ammon refused to yield to the words of Jephthah, which he had sent through the messengers.

**11:29** Therefore the Spirit of the Lord came upon Jephthah, and going around Gilead and Manasseh, and also Mizpah of Gilead, and crossing over from there to the sons of Ammon,

**11:30** he vowed a vow to the Lord, saying, "If You give the sons of Ammon over into my hands,

**11:31** whoever first comes out of the doors of my house and meets me as I return in peace from the sons of Ammon, him I will offer as a burnt offering to the Lord."

**11:32** And Jephthah crossed over to the sons of Ammon to fight against them, whom the Lord gave over into his hands.

**11:33** And he struck from Aroer until you come to Minnith, twenty cities, and as far as Abel, which is planted with vineyards, with a very great blow; and the sons of Ammon were humbled by the sons of Israel.

**11:34** Now as Jephthah was returning to Mizpah, to his house, his only-born daughter met him with tambourines and dances; for he had no other children.

**11:35** When he saw her, he tore his garments and said, "Alas for me, my daughter! You have deceived me, and you yourself have been deceived; for I have opened my mouth to the Lord, and I will not be able to do otherwise."

**11:36** She answered him, "My father, if you have opened your mouth to the Lord, do to me whatever you have promised, since vengeance and victory over your enemies have been granted to you."

**11:37** And she said to her father, "Grant me this one thing that I ask: let me go so that for two months I may go around the mountains and mourn my virginity with my companions."

**11:38** He answered her, "Go." And he let her go for two months. And when she had gone away with her friends and companions, she wept over her virginity in the mountains.

**11:39** And when the two months were completed, she returned to her father, and he did to her just as he had vowed, she who did not know a man. From then on a custom became widespread in Israel, and a practice was kept,

**11:40** that after the cycle of a year the daughters of Israel gather together and mourn the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite for four days.

## Translator Notes

- **11:19** — The Vulgate reads 'usque ad fluvium' ('as far as the river,' i.e., the Jordan) where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'to my place.'
- **11:31** — The Vulgate uses masculine personal pronouns ('quicumque primus ... eum,' 'whoever first ... him') and moves directly to 'him I will offer as a burnt offering to the Lord,' without the Hebrew's preceding clause 'shall be the Lord's.'
- **11:33** — The Vulgate translates the second element of the Hebrew place-name Abel-keramim as a relative clause: 'Abel, quae est vineis consita' ('Abel, which is planted with vineyards').
- **11:35** — The Vulgate renders Jephthah's exclamation as 'decepisti me, et ipsa decepta es' ('you have deceived me, and you yourself have been deceived') where the Hebrew reads 'you have brought me very low, and you have become among those who trouble me.'
- **11:40** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew verb 'letannot' (often understood as 'to commemorate' or 'to recount') as 'plangant' ('mourn' or 'lament'), matching 'plangam' in 11:37.
