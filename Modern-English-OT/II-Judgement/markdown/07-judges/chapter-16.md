## Text

**16:1** He also went to Gaza, and saw there a woman who was a prostitute, and he went in to her.

**16:2** When the Philistines had heard this, and it had spread widely among them that Samson had entered the city, they surrounded him, stationing guards at the gate of the city and waiting there all night in silence, so that when morning came they might kill him as he went out.

**16:3** Now Samson slept until midnight; and rising up from there, he seized both doors of the gate with their posts and bar, and placing them on his shoulders, he carried them to the top of the mountain that faces Hebron.

**16:4** After these things he loved a woman who lived in the valley of Sorek and was called Delilah.

**16:5** And the rulers of the Philistines came to her and said, "Deceive him, and learn from him in what he has such great strength, and how we may be able to overcome him and afflict him when bound; and if you do this, we will each give you one thousand one hundred pieces of silver."

**16:6** Therefore Delilah spoke to Samson, "Tell me, please, in what your greatest strength lies, and what it is with which, being bound, you cannot break free?"

**16:7** Samson answered her, "If I am bound with seven sinew cords not yet dry and still moist, I will be weak like other men."

**16:8** And the satraps of the Philistines brought to her seven cords, as he had said, with which she bound him.

**16:9** While an ambush was hiding with her and waiting in the inner room for the outcome of the matter, she cried out to him, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" And he broke the bonds, just as if someone were to break a thread twisted from the refuse of tow when it has caught the scent of fire; and it was not known in what his strength lay.

**16:10** And Delilah said to him, "Look, you have mocked me and spoken falsely; at least now tell me with what you should be bound."

**16:11** He answered her, "If I am bound with new ropes that have never been in use, I will be weak and like other men."

**16:12** With these Delilah bound him again and cried out, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" with an ambush prepared in the inner room. And he broke the bonds as though they were threads of webs.

**16:13** And Delilah said to him again, "How long will you deceive me and speak falsely? Show me with what you should be bound." Samson answered her, "If you weave the seven locks of my head with the warp, and drive a peg wrapped with them into the ground, I will be weak."

**16:14** When Delilah had done this, she said to him, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" And rising up from sleep, he pulled out the peg with the locks and the warp.

**16:15** And Delilah said to him, "How do you say that you love me, when your mind is not with me? Three times you have lied to me, and you have not been willing to say in what your greatest strength lies."

**16:16** And when she was troublesome to him and continually pressed upon him for many days, giving no space for rest, his soul fainted and was wearied even to death.

**16:17** Then, revealing the truth of the matter, he said to her, "Iron has never come upon my head, because I am a Nazirite, that is, consecrated to God, from my mother's womb. If my head is shaved, my strength will depart from me, and I will grow faint, and I will be like other men."

**16:18** And seeing that he had confessed to her his whole mind, she sent to the rulers of the Philistines and sent word, "Come up once more, because now he has opened his heart to me." And they came up, taking the money that they had promised.

**16:19** And she made him sleep upon her knees and rest his head in her lap. And she called a barber and shaved his seven locks, and she began to drive him away and push him back from herself, for immediately strength departed from him.

**16:20** And she said, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" And rising up from sleep, he said in his mind, "I will go out as I did before and shake myself free," not knowing that the Lord had departed from him.

**16:21** When the Philistines had seized him, they immediately gouged out his eyes and led him to Gaza bound with chains; and having shut him up in prison, they made him grind.

**16:22** And already his hair began to grow back.

**16:23** And the rulers of the Philistines gathered together to offer magnificent sacrifices to Dagon their god and to feast, saying, "Our god has handed over our enemy Samson into our hands."

**16:24** Seeing this as well, the people praised their god and said the same things, "Our god has handed over our adversary into our hands, who destroyed our land and killed very many."

**16:25** And rejoicing throughout the banquets, having already partaken of the feast, they commanded that Samson be called and play before them. Brought from the prison, he played before them, and they made him stand between two pillars.

**16:26** And he said to the boy guiding his steps, "Let go of me, so that I may touch the pillars on which the whole house rests, and lean upon them, and rest a little."

**16:27** Now the house was full of men and women, and all the rulers of the Philistines were there, and from the roof and terrace about three thousand of both sexes were watching Samson play.

**16:28** But having called upon the Lord, he said, "Lord God, remember me, and restore to me now my former strength, my God, that I may avenge myself on my enemies, and for the loss of my two eyes may receive one vengeance."

**16:29** And seizing both pillars on which the house rested, and holding one of them with his right hand and the other with his left,

**16:30** he said, "Let my soul die with the Philistines." And when he had forcefully shaken the pillars, the house fell upon all the rulers and the rest of the multitude that was there; and dying, he killed many more than he had killed before while alive.

**16:31** Now his brothers and his entire clan went down, took his body, and buried it between Zorah and Eshtaol in the tomb of his father Manoah. And he judged Israel for twenty years.

## Translator Notes

- **16:8** — The Clementine Vulgate prints eam ('her') here, a well-known printing slip for eum ('him'), which the context and the following verse require.
- **16:13** — Like the Septuagint, the Vulgate includes the instruction about fastening the woven hair with a peg (clavum) driven into the ground, a clause absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **16:17** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew morah ('razor') as 'iron' (Ferrum) and adds the explanatory gloss 'that is, consecrated to God' (id est, consecratus Deo) for 'Nazirite.'
- **16:28** — The Vulgate reads 'restore to me now my former strength' and 'for the loss of my two eyes' (pro amissione duorum luminum, literally 'two lights'), whereas the Hebrew has 'strengthen me just this once' and 'for one of my two eyes.'
