## Text

**5:1** And Deborah and Barak the son of Abinoam sang on that day, saying:

**5:2** "You of Israel who willingly offered your lives to danger, bless the Lord.

**5:3** Hear, kings; give ear, princes: it is I, it is I, who will sing to the Lord; I will sing praises to the Lord, the God of Israel.

**5:4** Lord, when You went out from Seir and passed through the regions of Edom, the earth was shaken, and the heavens and the clouds dripped with waters.

**5:5** The mountains flowed before the face of the Lord, and Sinai before the face of the Lord, the God of Israel.

**5:6** In the days of Shamgar the son of Anath, in the days of Jael, the paths rested, and those who traveled along them walked along out-of-the-way tracks.

**5:7** The mighty men in Israel ceased and rested, until Deborah arose, arose as a mother in Israel.

**5:8** The Lord chose new wars, and He Himself overthrew the gates of the enemies; if shield and spear appeared among forty thousand of Israel.

**5:9** My heart loves the princes of Israel; you who of your own will offered yourselves to peril, bless the Lord.

**5:10** You who ride on gleaming donkeys, and sit in judgment, and walk along the way, speak.

**5:11** Where the chariots were smashed together and the army of the enemies was suffocated, there let the righteous deeds of the Lord be recounted, and clemency toward the mighty men of Israel; then the people of the Lord went down to the gates and obtained dominion.

**5:12** Arise, arise, Deborah; arise, arise, and speak a song; arise, Barak, and seize your captives, son of Abinoam.

**5:13** The remnant of the people was saved; the Lord fought among the mighty men.

**5:14** Out of Ephraim He destroyed them in Amalek, and after him out of Benjamin against your peoples, Amalek; from Machir princes came down, and from Zebulun those who would lead the army to wage war.

**5:15** The leaders of Issachar were with Deborah, and they followed the footsteps of Barak, who gave himself over to peril as if into a precipice and an abyss; with Reuben divided against himself, a contention of great-hearted men was found.

**5:16** Why do you dwell between two borders, to hear the whistlings of the flocks? With Reuben divided against himself, a contention of great-hearted men was found.

**5:17** Gilead rested beyond the Jordan, and Dan was occupied with ships; Asher dwelt on the shore of the sea and stayed in the harbors.

**5:18** But Zebulun and Naphtali offered their lives to death in the region of Merome.

**5:19** The kings came and fought; the kings of Canaan fought at Taanach beside the waters of Megiddo, and yet they carried off nothing while plundering.

**5:20** From heaven battle was fought against them; the stars, remaining in their order and course, fought against Sisera.

**5:21** The torrent of Kishon dragged away their corpses, the torrent of Kedumim, the torrent of Kishon; trample down the strong, my soul.

**5:22** The hooves of the horses fell off as the mightiest of the enemies fled in a rush and plunged headlong.

**5:23** 'Curse the land of Meroz,' said the angel of the Lord, 'curse its inhabitants, because they did not come to the help of the Lord, to the assistance of His mightiest ones.'

**5:24** Blessed among women be Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, and may she be blessed in her tent.

**5:25** To him who asked for water she gave milk, and in a bowl of princes she offered butter.

**5:26** She put her left hand to the peg and her right hand to the hammers of craftsmen. And she struck Sisera, seeking in his head a place for the wound, and forcefully piercing through his temple;

**5:27** between her feet he collapsed; he fainted and died; he rolled before her feet and lay lifeless and wretched.

**5:28** Looking out through the window, his mother wailed, and from the upper room she spoke: 'Why does his chariot delay in returning? Why have the feet of his four-horse chariots been slow?'

**5:29** One wiser than the rest of his wives answered her mother-in-law with these words:

**5:30** 'Perhaps he is now dividing the spoils, and the most beautiful of the women is being chosen for him; garments of diverse colors are being handed over to Sisera as plunder, and varied finery is being gathered to adorn necks.'

**5:31** So may all Your enemies perish, Lord; but those who love You, just as the sun shines at its rising, so may they shine brightly."

## Translator Notes

- **5:8** — The Vulgate reads "The Lord chose new wars, and He Himself overthrew the gates of the enemies," whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text is commonly rendered "they chose new gods; then war was in the gates." The conditional clause "if shield and spear appeared" reproduces a Hebraistic rhetorical idiom implying that none were seen.
- **5:11** — A distinctively Vulgate rendering ("Where the chariots were smashed together and the army of the enemies was suffocated... and obtained dominion"), diverging markedly from the obscure Hebrew text usually understood to mention singers or archers at the watering places.
- **5:15** — The Vulgate interprets Hebrew peluggot ("divisions" or "streams") as internal faction ("with Reuben divided against himself") and renders the following phrase as "a contention of great-hearted men was found," repeated in 5:16.
- **5:18** — The Vulgate takes the Hebrew phrase merome sadeh ("heights of the field") as a geographical proper name, in regione Merome ("in the region of Merome").
- **5:21** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew qedumim (commonly translated "ancient" or "onrushing") as a proper name, torrens Cadumim ("the torrent of Kedumim").
