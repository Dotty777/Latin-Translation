## Text

**13:1** "The greatest King Artaxerxes, from India to Ethiopia, to the princes and leaders of the one hundred twenty-seven provinces who are subject to his dominion: greeting.

**13:2** Although I ruled over very many nations and had subjected the entire world to my authority, I wished by no means to abuse the greatness of my power, but to govern my subjects with clemency and gentleness, so that, passing their life in quietness without any terror, they might enjoy the peace desired by all mortals.

**13:3** Now when I asked my counselors how this might be accomplished, one who surpassed the rest in wisdom and loyalty, and was second after the king, Haman by name,

**13:4** informed me that throughout the whole world a people is scattered who make use of strange laws, and, acting contrary to the custom of all nations, despise the commands of kings, and violate the harmony of all nations by their dissension.

**13:5** Having learned this, seeing that one rebellious nation against the entire human race makes use of perverse laws, opposes our commands, and disturbs the peace and harmony of the provinces subject to us,

**13:6** we have commanded that all whom Haman, who is set over all the provinces and is second to the king, and whom we honor in the place of a father, points out shall be destroyed with their wives and children by their enemies, and that no one have pity on them, on the fourteenth day of the twelfth month, Adar, of the present year,

**13:7** so that these wicked men, going down to the underworld in a single day, may restore to our empire the peace that they had disturbed." And Mordecai went and did everything that Esther had commanded him.

**13:8** Now Mordecai implored the Lord, mindful of all His works,

**13:9** and he said, "Lord, Lord, almighty King, for in Your authority all things are placed, and there is no one who can resist Your will, if You have decreed to save Israel.

**13:10** You made heaven and earth, and whatever is contained within the compass of heaven.

**13:11** You are Lord of all, and there is no one who resists Your majesty.

**13:12** You know all things, and You know that it was not out of pride and insolence, or any desire for glory, that I did this, refusing to bow down before the most proud Haman

**13:13** (for I would willingly be ready to kiss even the footprints of his feet for the salvation of Israel),

**13:14** but I feared that I might transfer the honor of my God to a man, and that I might worship anyone except my God.

**13:15** And now, Lord King, God of Abraham, have mercy on Your people, because our enemies wish to destroy us and to wipe out Your inheritance.

**13:16** Do not despise Your portion, which You redeemed for Yourself out of Egypt.

**13:17** Hear my entreaty, and be gracious to Your lot and Your allotted portion, and turn our mourning into joy, so that living we may praise Your name, Lord; and do not close the mouths of those who sing to You."

**13:18** All Israel also cried out to the Lord with like mind and supplication, because certain death hung over them.

## Translator Notes

- **13:4** — Latin novis ... legibus literally means 'new' or 'strange/unusual laws.'
- **13:6** — Vulgate 13:6 specifies the fourteenth day of Adar (quartadecima die), whereas 3:13 and 16:20 specify the thirteenth.
- **13:7** — Latin ad inferos ('to the underworld') uses the edition's standard rendering. The closing sentence repeats 4:17 as Jerome's editorial bridge anchoring Mordecai's and Esther's prayers (13:8-14:19, Addition C).
- **13:12** — Latin adorarem ('bow down before' or 'worship', repeated in 13:14) carries both courtly prostration and religious worship.
- **13:17** — Latin funiculo tuo literally means 'Your measuring cord/line,' a biblical idiom for an allotted inheritance.
