## Text

**9:1** Therefore, on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which we have already said before is called Adar, when killing was being prepared for all the Jews and their foes were craving blood, in reversal the Jews began to prevail and to avenge themselves on their adversaries.

**9:2** And they gathered together in each city, town, and place to stretch out a hand against their enemies and persecutors. And no one dared to resist, because dread of their greatness had penetrated all peoples.

**9:3** For even the judges of the provinces, and the commanders, and the governors, and every dignitary who presided over each place and work, exalted the Jews out of fear of Mordecai,

**9:4** whom they knew to be the prince of the palace and to have very great power; the fame of his name also grew daily and flew through the mouths of all.

**9:5** Therefore, the Jews struck their enemies with a great blow and killed them, repaying them for what they had prepared to do to them:

**9:6** to such an extent that even in Susa they killed five hundred men, besides the ten sons of Haman the Agagite, foe of the Jews, whose names are these:

**9:7** Parshandatha, and Dalphon, and Aspatha,

**9:8** and Poratha, and Adalia, and Aridatha,

**9:9** and Parmashta, and Arisai, and Aridai, and Jezatha.

**9:10** When they had killed them, they refused to touch plunder from their possessions.

**9:11** And immediately the number of those who had been killed in Susa was reported to the king.

**9:12** He said to the queen, "In the city of Susa the Jews have killed five hundred men, and ten others, the sons of Haman; how great a slaughter do you think they are carrying out in all the provinces? What further do you ask, and what do you want me to command to be done?"

**9:13** To him she answered, "If it pleases the king, let authority be given to the Jews that just as they have done today in Susa, so they may also do tomorrow, and let the ten sons of Haman be hanged on gallows."

**9:14** And the king commanded that it be done so. And immediately the edict hung in Susa, and the ten sons of Haman were hanged.

**9:15** When the Jews had gathered together on the fourteenth day of the month of Adar, three hundred men were killed in Susa, nor was their property plundered by them.

**9:16** Moreover, throughout all the provinces that were subject to the dominion of the king, the Jews stood for their lives, having killed their foes and persecutors, to such an extent that the number of those killed reached seventy-five thousand, and no one touched anything from their possessions.

**9:17** Now the thirteenth day of the month of Adar was the first day of killing among all of them, and on the fourteenth day they ceased to slaughter. That day they appointed to be a solemn day, so that on it, at all times thereafter, they might devote themselves to feasts, joy, and banquets.

**9:18** But those who had carried out the slaughter in the city of Susa were engaged in slaughter on the thirteenth and fourteenth day of the same month; on the fifteenth day, however, they ceased to strike. And therefore they appointed that same day as a solemn day of feasts and gladness.

**9:19** Those Jews, however, who lived in unwalled towns and villages decreed the fourteenth day of the month of Adar as a day of banquets and joy, so that they exult on it and send one another portions of feasts and food.

**9:20** So Mordecai wrote all these things, and, set down in writing, sent them to the Jews who lived in all the provinces of the king, both those situated nearby and those far away,

**9:21** that they should accept the fourteenth and fifteenth day of the month of Adar as feast days, and, as the year always returns, celebrate them with solemn honor,

**9:22** because on those very days the Jews avenged themselves on their enemies, and mourning and sorrow were turned into cheerfulness and joy; and that these days should be days of feasts and gladness, and that they should send one another portions of food and bestow small gifts upon the poor.

**9:23** And the Jews accepted as a solemn rite all that they had begun to do at that time, and what Mordecai had commanded in letters to be done.

**9:24** For Haman, son of Hammedatha of the lineage of Agag, foe and adversary of the Jews, plotted evil against them, to kill and destroy them; and he cast Pur, which in our language is translated as lot.

**9:25** And afterward Esther went in to the king, pleading that his attempts might be made void by the king's letters, and that the evil which he had plotted against the Jews might return upon his head. Finally, they fastened both him and his sons to a cross,

**9:26** and from that time these days were called Purim, that is, of lots, because Pur, that is, the lot, had been cast into the urn. And all the things that were done are contained in the scroll of this letter, that is, of this book:

**9:27** and what they endured, and what was afterward changed, the Jews took upon themselves and their seed, and upon all who wished to be joined to their religion, so that no one is permitted to pass these two days without solemn observance, which the writing attests and fixed times require, as the years continually succeed one another.

**9:28** These are the days that no oblivion will ever blot out, and throughout every generation all provinces in the entire world will celebrate them; nor is there any city in which the days of Purim, that is, of lots, are not observed by the Jews and by their progeny, which is bound by these ceremonies.

**9:29** And Queen Esther, daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew wrote a second letter as well, so that with all diligence this solemn day might be ratified for the future:

**9:30** and they sent it to all the Jews who lived in the one hundred twenty-seven provinces of King Ahasuerus, so that they might have peace and accept the truth,

**9:31** observing the days of lots, and that they should celebrate them in their proper time with joy, just as Mordecai and Esther had appointed; and they accepted to be observed by themselves and by their seed the fasts, and the cries, and the days of lots,

**9:32** and all things that are contained in the history of this book, which is called Esther.

## Translator Notes

- **9:5** — In the Latin, the reflexive pronoun refers back to the Jews, the subject of the main clause.
- **9:6** — The Vulgate introduces Haman's ten sons here as an addition to the five hundred killed ("besides the ten sons of Haman... whose names are these"), restructuring the sequence of verses 6-10 in the Hebrew.
- **9:9** — The Vulgate reads "Jezatha," omitting the initial syllable of the Hebrew name "Vaizatha."
- **9:26** — The Vulgate adds that the lot was cast "into the urn" (in urnam) and explicitly glosses "this letter" with "that is, of this book" (epistolæ, id est, libri hujus volumine).
- **9:32** — Where the Hebrew closes with Esther's decree confirming Purim and its recording in the book, the Vulgate continues the list from verse 31 and explicitly names "this book, which is called Esther."
