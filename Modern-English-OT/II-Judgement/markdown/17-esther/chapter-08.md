## Text

**8:1** On that day King Ahasuerus gave to Queen Esther the house of Haman, the adversary of the Jews, and Mordecai entered before the face of the king. For Esther had confessed to him that he was her uncle.

**8:2** And the king took the ring that he had ordered to be taken back from Haman, and delivered it to Mordecai. And Esther appointed Mordecai over her house.

**8:3** And not content with these things, she fell down at the feet of the king, wept, and speaking to him, implored that he would command the malice of Haman the Agagite and his most wicked schemes, which he had devised against the Jews, to be made void.

**8:4** But he, according to custom, extended the golden scepter with his hand, by which a sign of clemency was shown; and rising up, she stood before him,

**8:5** and said, "If it pleases the king, and if I have found favor in his eyes, and my plea does not seem contrary to him, I plead that by new letters the old letters of Haman, the plotter and foe of the Jews, by which he had commanded them to perish in all the provinces of the king, may be corrected.

**8:6** For how will I be able to endure the slaughter and killing of my people?"

**8:7** And King Ahasuerus answered Queen Esther and Mordecai the Jew, "I have granted the house of Haman to Esther, and I have ordered him to be fastened to a cross, because he dared to lay a hand on the Jews.

**8:8** Write therefore to the Jews, as it pleases you, in the king's name, sealing the letters with my ring." For this was the custom, that no one would dare to contradict letters that were sent in the king's name and were sealed with his ring.

**8:9** And when the scribes and copyists of the king had been summoned (now it was the time of the third month, which is called Sivan), on its twenty-third day letters were written, as Mordecai had desired, to the Jews, and to the princes, governors, and judges who presided over the one hundred and twenty-seven provinces from India as far as Ethiopia, to province after province and to people after people according to their languages and scripts, and to the Jews, just as they were able to read and hear.

**8:10** And the letters themselves, which were sent in the king's name, were sealed with his ring and sent by couriers, who, speeding through all the provinces, were to forestall the old letters with new messages.

**8:11** The king commanded them to meet with the Jews in each city and order them to gather together into one, so that they might stand for their lives, and kill and destroy all their enemies, with their wives and children and all their households, and plunder their spoils.

**8:12** And one day of vengeance was appointed throughout all the provinces, that is, the thirteenth of the twelfth month, Adar.

**8:13** And the summary of the letter was this: that in all lands and peoples that were subject to the rule of King Ahasuerus, it should be made known that the Jews were ready to take vengeance on their foes.

**8:14** And the swift couriers went out carrying the messages, and the king's edict hung in Susa.

**8:15** Now Mordecai, going out from the palace and from the presence of the king, shone in royal garments, namely violet and sky-blue, wearing a golden crown on his head and wrapped in a cloak of silk and purple. And the entire city exulted and rejoiced.

**8:16** But for the Jews a new light seemed to rise: joy, honor, and jubilation.

**8:17** Among all peoples, cities, and provinces, wherever the king's orders came, there was marvelous exultation, feasts and banquets, and a festive day, to such an extent that many of another nation and sect joined themselves to their religion and ceremonies. For a great terror of the Jewish name had seized everyone.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — The Vulgate explicitly identifies Mordecai as Esther's paternal uncle (patruus, matching Vulgate 2:7, 15), whereas the Hebrew says only that she told the king what Mordecai was to her.
- **8:7** — The Vulgate employs Roman crucifixion language, "fastened to a cross" (affigi cruci; cf. 9:25), where the Hebrew says "hanged on the wood."
- **8:8** — The second sentence shifts in the Vulgate to third-person past-tense narration ("For this was the custom... sealed with his ring") explaining Persian royal protocol, rather than continuing the king's direct speech as in the Hebrew.
- **8:10** — Instead of listing breeds of royal horses as in the Hebrew, the Vulgate supplies a purpose clause stating that the couriers were to forestall the old letters with new messages.
- **8:15** — The Vulgate describes Mordecai's royal garments as "violet and sky-blue" (hyacinthinis... et æriis, for aeriis) and his cloak as "silk" (serico), where the Hebrew has "blue and white" and "fine linen."
