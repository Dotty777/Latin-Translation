## Text

**2:1** When these things had been done in this way, after the indignation of King Ahasuerus had subsided, he remembered Vashti, and what she had done or what she had suffered.

**2:2** And the king's attendants and his ministers said, "Let attractive young women who are virgins be sought for the king,

**2:3** and let men be sent to look out throughout all the provinces for attractive young women who are virgins, and let them bring them to the city of Susa and hand them over into the house of the women under the hand of Hegai the eunuch, who is overseer and keeper of the royal women; and let them receive women's cosmetics and the other things necessary for their use.

**2:4** And whichever one among them all pleases the eyes of the king, let her reign in place of Vashti." The word pleased the king, and he ordered it to be done just as they had suggested.

**2:5** There was a Jewish man in the city of Susa, named Mordecai, the son of Jair, the son of Shimei, the son of Kish, of the lineage of Jemini,

**2:6** who had been carried away from Jerusalem at the time when Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, had carried away Jeconiah, king of Judah,

**2:7** who was the foster father of his brother's daughter Hadassah, who by another name was called Esther and had lost both parents; she was exceedingly beautiful and graceful in appearance. And when her father and mother had died, Mordecai adopted her for himself as a daughter.

**2:8** And when the king's command had spread abroad, and according to his order many beautiful virgins were being brought to Susa and handed over to Hegai the eunuch, Esther also among the other young women was handed over to him to be kept in the number of the women.

**2:9** And she pleased him and found favor in his sight. And he commanded a eunuch to hasten the women's cosmetics, and to give her her portions and seven most attractive young women from the king's house, and to adorn and care for both her and her maidservants.

**2:10** She was unwilling to disclose to him her people and her homeland, for Mordecai had commanded her to remain completely silent about this matter.

**2:11** And he walked about daily in front of the forecourt of the house in which the chosen virgins were kept, caring for the welfare of Esther and wanting to know what would happen to her.

**2:12** Now when the time came for each young woman in turn to go in to the king, after everything that pertained to women's adornment had been completed, the twelfth month was coming around; specifically in this way, that for six months they were anointed with oil of myrrh, and for the other six they used certain ointments and spices.

**2:13** And when going in to the king, they received whatever they had asked for pertaining to attire; and dressed just as it had pleased them, they passed from the hall of the women to the king's chamber.

**2:14** And the one who had gone in at evening went out in the morning, and from there was led away into the second house, which was under the hand of Shaashgaz the eunuch, who presided over the king's concubines; nor did she have authority to return to the king any longer, unless the king wished it and ordered her to come by name.

**2:15** Now when the time had elapsed in turn, the day was approaching on which Esther, the daughter of Abihail the brother of Mordecai, whom he had adopted for himself as a daughter, was to go in to the king. She did not seek women's adornment, but whatever Hegai the eunuch, the keeper of the virgins, wanted, these things he gave her for attire. For she was exceedingly lovely and of incredible beauty; to the eyes of all she appeared gracious and lovable.

**2:16** Therefore she was led to the chamber of King Ahasuerus in the tenth month, which is called Tebeth, in the seventh year of his reign.

**2:17** And the king loved her deeply, more than all the women, and she had favor and mercy before him above all the women; and he placed the diadem of the kingdom on her head and made her reign in place of Vashti.

**2:18** And he ordered a most magnificent banquet to be prepared for all his princes and servants for the union and wedding of Esther. And he gave rest to all the provinces, and bestowed gifts generously according to sovereign magnificence.

**2:19** And when virgins were sought and gathered a second time, Mordecai remained at the king's gate.

**2:20** Esther had not yet disclosed her homeland and her people, according to his order. For whatever he commanded, Esther observed; and she did all things just as she had been accustomed at the time when he was raising her as a little girl.

**2:21** Therefore, at that time when Mordecai was staying at the king's gate, Bigthan and Teresh, two eunuchs of the king who were gatekeepers and presided at the first threshold of the palace, became angry and wanted to rise up against the king and kill him.

**2:22** This was not hidden from Mordecai, and immediately he reported it to Queen Esther, and she to the king in the name of Mordecai, who had brought the matter to her.

**2:23** It was investigated and found out, and each of them was hanged on a gallows. And it was committed to the histories and handed over to the annals before the king.

## Translator Notes

- **2:5** — Vulgate de stirpe Jemini ('of the lineage of Jemini') preserves the Hebrew gentilic yemini ('a Benjaminite') as an ancestral name, contrasting with de tribu Benjamin in 1:1.
- **2:9** — Vulgate praecepit eunucho ('he commanded a eunuch') has Hegai delegate Esther's care to a subordinate eunuch, whereas the Hebrew describes Hegai himself hastening to provide her cosmetics and portions.
- **2:15** — The closing sentence ('For she was exceedingly lovely and of incredible beauty; to the eyes of all she appeared gracious and lovable') is a distinctive Vulgate expansion of the briefer Hebrew statement that Esther won favor in the sight of all who saw her.
- **2:17** — Vulgate repeats mulieres ('women') in both clauses, whereas the Hebrew has 'women' in the first clause and 'virgins' in the second.
