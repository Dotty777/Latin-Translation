## Text

**15:1** And he commanded her (no doubt it was Mordecai) to go in to the king and plead for her people and for her homeland.

**15:2** "Remember," he said, "the days of your lowliness, how you were nourished by my hand, because Haman, who is second to the king, has spoken against us for death;

**15:3** and you, call upon the Lord, and speak to the king for us, and deliver us from death."

**15:4** Now on the third day she laid aside her garments of adornment and was clothed with her glory.

**15:5** And when she shone in royal attire and had called upon God, the ruler and savior of all, she took two maids,

**15:6** and she leaned upon one indeed, as though, because of luxury and excessive delicacy, she could not bear to support her own body;

**15:7** while the other of the maids followed her mistress, holding up the garments that flowed down to the ground.

**15:8** She herself, with her face suffused with a rosy color and with charming and shining eyes, concealed a sorrowful mind contracted with extreme fear.

**15:9** Having therefore entered all the doors in order, she stood opposite the king, where he sat upon the throne of his kingdom, clothed in royal garments and shining with gold and precious stones; and he was terrifying in appearance.

**15:10** And when he had lifted his face and with blazing eyes had revealed the fury of his breast, the queen collapsed, and with her color changed to paleness, she leaned her weary head upon the little maid.

**15:11** And God turned the spirit of the king into gentleness, and in haste and fear he leaped down from his throne, and supporting her in his arms until she came back to herself, he soothed her with these words:

**15:12** "What is the matter with you, Esther? I am your brother; do not be afraid.

**15:13** You will not die, for this law was established not for you, but for all.

**15:14** Come near therefore, and touch the scepter."

**15:15** And when she remained silent, he took the golden scepter and placed it upon her neck, and kissed her, and said, "Why do you not speak to me?"

**15:16** And she answered, "I saw you, my lord, like an angel of God, and my heart was thrown into turmoil for fear of your glory.

**15:17** For you are very wonderful, my lord, and your face is full of graces."

**15:18** And while she was speaking, she collapsed again and nearly fainted away.

**15:19** Now the king was distressed, and all his attendants were comforting her.

## Translator Notes

- **15:1** — The parenthetical (haud dubium quin esset Mardochæus) is Jerome's explicit editorial gloss preserved in the Clementine Vulgate introducing Addition D.
- **15:4** — The Clementine Vulgate literally reads vestimenta ornatus sui ('her garments of adornment', a historic Vulgate form where 'garments of her prayer/service' is expected in context), contrasting with circumdata est gloria sua ('was clothed with her glory').
- **15:16** — Latin domine ('my lord') is lowercase as courtly address to the king (also in 15:17).
