## Text

**10:1** Now King Ahasuerus made the entire land and all the islands of the sea subject to tribute.

**10:2** His might and dominion, and the honor and eminence with which he exalted Mordecai, are written in the books of the Medes and Persians,

**10:3** and how Mordecai, of Jewish descent, was second to King Ahasuerus, and great among the Jews, and pleasing to the populace of his brothers, seeking good things for his people and speaking the things that pertained to the peace of his offspring. And Mordecai said, "These things have been done by God. I remembered the dream that I had seen, which signified these same things, and not one of them was in vain. The little spring, which grew into a river, and was turned into light and the sun, and overflowed into abundant waters, is Esther, whom the king took as a wife and willed to be queen. Now the two dragons are I and Haman. The nations that had assembled are those who attempted to blot out the name of the Jews. But my nation is Israel, which cried out to the Lord, and the Lord saved His people; and He freed us from all evils and performed great signs and wonders among the nations; and He commanded that there be two lots, one for the people of God, and the other for all the nations. And each of the two lots came to the day already fixed from that time before God for all the nations, and the Lord remembered His people and showed mercy to His inheritance. And these days will be observed in the month of Adar on the fourteenth and fifteenth day of the same month, with all eagerness and joy, in one assembly of the gathered people, for all succeeding generations of the people of Israel."

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — The Vulgate adds "entire" (omnem) and "all" (cunctas) and renders "islands of the sea" (maris insulas) with "made ... subject to tribute" (fecit tributarias), where the Hebrew MT has "laid a tribute on the land and the coastlands of the sea."
- **10:2** — The Vulgate gives a declarative statement ("are written") citing the plural "books of the Medes and Persians" and the "honor and eminence" with which the king exalted Mordecai, whereas the Hebrew MT poses a rhetorical question citing "the book of the chronicles of the kings of Media and Persia."
- **10:3** — In this Vulgate text, Mordecai's interpretation of his dream (deuterocanonical Addition F, numbered 10:4-13 in many editions) is appended directly to 10:3. Notable Vulgate-distinct readings include "commanded that there be two lots" (praecepit, Greek "made") and the dense clause "to the day already fixed from that time before God for all the nations" (in statutum ex illo jam tempore diem coram Deo universis gentibus, taking the prepositionless dative universis gentibus as "for all the nations," where the Greek reads "to the hour and time and day of judgment before God and among all the nations").
