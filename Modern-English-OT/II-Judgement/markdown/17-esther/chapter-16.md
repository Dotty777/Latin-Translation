## Text

**16:1** "The great King Artaxerxes, from India to Ethiopia, to the leaders and princes of the one hundred twenty-seven provinces who obey our command, sends greeting.

**16:2** Many have abused the goodness of princes and the honor that was conferred upon them into pride;

**16:3** and not only do they strive to oppress those subject to kings, but not enduring the glory given to them, they plot ambushes against the very ones who gave it.

**16:4** Nor are they content not to give thanks for kindnesses and to violate the laws of humanity within themselves, but they even suppose that they can escape the sentence of God, who sees all things.

**16:5** And they have broken out into such madness that they attempt to undermine by tunnels of lies those who diligently keep the offices entrusted to them and conduct everything so as to be worthy of everyone's praise,

**16:6** while with clever deceit they deceive the simple ears of princes who judge others from their own nature.

**16:7** And this matter is proved both from ancient histories and from the things that take place daily, how the aims of kings are corrupted by the evil suggestions of certain men.

**16:8** For this reason, provision must be made for the peace of all the provinces.

**16:9** Nor ought you to think, if we give different commands, that this comes from the fickleness of our mind, but that we pass judgment according to the character and necessity of the times, as the welfare of the commonwealth demands.

**16:10** And so that you may understand more clearly what we say: Haman the son of Hammedatha, a Macedonian in both spirit and nation, and alien to the blood of the Persians, and staining our goodness with his cruelty, was received by us as a foreigner,

**16:11** and experienced such great kindness toward himself that he was called our father, and was bowed down to by all as second after the king;

**16:12** who was lifted up into such a swelling of arrogance that he strove to deprive us of both kingdom and breath.

**16:13** For by certain new and unheard-of schemes he sought to bring to death Mordecai, by whose faithfulness and kindnesses we live, and Esther, the partner of our kingdom, together with her entire nation;

**16:14** intending this: that once they were killed, he might lay an ambush against our isolation and transfer the kingdom of the Persians to the Macedonians.

**16:15** We, however, have found the Jews, who were marked for slaughter by the worst of mortals, in no fault at all, but on the contrary making use of just laws,

**16:16** and sons of the Most High and greatest and ever-living God, by whose kindness the kingdom was handed down both to our fathers and to us, and is guarded to this very day.

**16:17** Therefore, know that those letters which he had sent out under our name are void.

**16:18** For which crime, before the gates of this city, that is, Susa, both he himself who schemed it and all his kindred hang on gallows, with not us, but God repaying him what he deserved.

**16:19** Now let this edict, which we are now sending, be posted in all the cities, so that it may be permitted for the Jews to follow their own laws.

**16:20** To them you must be a support, so that they may be able to put to death those who had prepared themselves for their slaughter on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which is called Adar.

**16:21** For this day of grief and mourning, almighty God has turned into joy for them.

**16:22** Therefore you too shall keep this day among the other festive days, and celebrate it with all gladness, so that even in the future it may be known

**16:23** that all who faithfully obey the Persians receive a worthy reward for their loyalty, whereas those who plot against their kingdom perish for their crime.

**16:24** Now let every province and city that refuses to share in this observance perish by sword and fire, and be so destroyed that it becomes impassable forever not only to humans, but even to beasts, as an example of contempt and disobedience."

## Translator Notes

- **16:5** — Latin mendaciorum cuniculis ('by tunnels of lies') uses the military image of subterranean sapping/mining.
- **16:12** — Latin spiritu here means 'breath' or 'life.'
- **16:18** — Latin patibulis ('gallows') matches 7:10 and 9:13.
