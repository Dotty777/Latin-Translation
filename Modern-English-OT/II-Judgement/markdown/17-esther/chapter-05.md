## Text

**5:1** Now on the third day Esther clothed herself in royal garments and stood in the courtyard of the royal house, which was the inner one, opposite the king's hall; while he was sitting upon his throne in the council chamber of the palace opposite the entrance of the house.

**5:2** And when he saw Queen Esther standing, she pleased his eyes, and he extended toward her the golden scepter that he held in his hand; and she approached and kissed the tip of his scepter.

**5:3** And the king said to her, "What do you wish, Queen Esther? What is your petition? Even if you ask for half of the kingdom, it will be given to you."

**5:4** And she answered, "If it pleases the king, I implore you to come to me today, and Haman with you, to the banquet that I have prepared."

**5:5** And immediately the king said, "Call Haman quickly, so that he may obey Esther's will." So the king and Haman came to the banquet that the queen had prepared for them.

**5:6** And the king said to her, after he had drunk wine abundantly, "What do you ask to be given to you? And for what matter are you making a request? Even if you ask for half of my kingdom, you will obtain it."

**5:7** Esther answered him, "My petition and pleas are these:

**5:8** if I have found favor in the sight of the king, and if it pleases the king to give me what I request and to fulfill my petition, let the king and Haman come to the banquet that I have prepared for them, and tomorrow I will reveal my will to the king."

**5:9** So Haman went out on that day glad and cheerful. And when he saw Mordecai sitting before the gates of the palace, and saw that he not only did not rise for him, but did not even move from the place where he was sitting, he was greatly indignant;

**5:10** and, concealing his anger, he returned to his house and called together to himself his friends and Zeresh his wife,

**5:11** and recounted to them the greatness of his riches, and the multitude of his sons, and with how much glory the king had elevated him above all his princes and servants.

**5:12** And after these things he said, "Queen Esther also invited no one else to the banquet with the king except me; and tomorrow also I am going to dine with her along with the king.

**5:13** And although I have all these things, I think I have nothing as long as I see Mordecai the Jew sitting before the royal gates."

**5:14** And Zeresh his wife and his other friends answered him, "Order a tall beam to be prepared, having a height of fifty cubits, and tell the king in the morning that Mordecai should be hanged on it, and so you will go gladly with the king to the banquet." The advice pleased him, and he ordered a tall cross to be prepared.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — Jerome uses Roman imperial architectural terms here: basilica ("hall") for the royal building and consistorium ("council chamber") for the king's formal audience hall.
- **5:2** — Latin virgam auream ('golden scepter', matching 4:11 and 8:4) and osculata est summitatem virgae ejus ('kissed the tip of his scepter', whereas the Hebrew reads 'touched the tip of the scepter') are distinctive Vulgate details restored in Stage 1.
- **5:6** — Where the Hebrew reads "at the banquet of wine," the Vulgate renders "after he had drunk wine abundantly" (postquam vinum biberat abundanter).
- **5:8** — Instead of the Hebrew "tomorrow I will do according to the word of the king," the Vulgate has Esther promise, "tomorrow I will reveal my will to the king" (aperiam regi voluntatem meam).
- **5:14** — For the single Hebrew noun denoting the wooden execution structure ("tree/wood"), the Vulgate employs two distinct Latin terms here, first trabs ("beam") and then crux ("cross"), and later patibulum ("gallows") in 6:4.
