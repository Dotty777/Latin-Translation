## Text

**3:1** After these things King Ahasuerus exalted Haman the son of Hammedatha, who was of the lineage of Agag, and placed his throne above all the princes whom he had.

**3:2** And all the king's servants who stayed at the doors of the palace bent their knees and paid homage to Haman, for so the emperor had commanded them; Mordecai alone did not bend the knee or pay homage to him.

**3:3** And the king's attendants who presided at the doors of the palace said to him, "Why, unlike the others, do you not observe the king's order?"

**3:4** And when they said this repeatedly and he was unwilling to listen, they reported it to Haman, desiring to know whether he would persist in his resolve; for he had told them that he was a Jew.

**3:5** When Haman heard this, and had verified by experience that Mordecai did not bend the knee to him or pay homage to him, he became exceedingly angry,

**3:6** and he counted it as nothing to lay his hands on Mordecai alone, for he had heard that he was of the Jewish people; and instead he wanted to destroy the entire nation of the Jews who were in the kingdom of Ahasuerus.

**3:7** In the first month (whose name is Nisan), in the twelfth year of the reign of Ahasuerus, a lot, which in Hebrew is called Pur, was cast into an urn before Haman, as to what day and what month the Jewish people should be killed; and the twelfth month came out, which is called Adar.

**3:8** And Haman said to King Ahasuerus, "There is a people scattered throughout all the provinces of your kingdom and separated from one another, using new laws and ceremonies, and moreover despising the decrees of the king; and you know very well that it is not advantageous for your kingdom for it to grow insolent through license.

**3:9** If it pleases you, decree that it perish, and I will weigh out ten thousand talents to the treasurers of your treasury."

**3:10** Therefore the king took the ring that he used from his hand and gave it to Haman the son of Hammedatha, of the offspring of Agag, the enemy of the Jews,

**3:11** and said to him, "Let the silver that you promise be yours; with the people, do what pleases you."

**3:12** And the king's scribes were summoned in the first month, Nisan, on the thirteenth day of the same month; and it was written, as Haman had ordered, to all the satraps of the king, and to the judges of the provinces and of the various peoples, as each people was able to read and hear according to the variety of their languages, in the name of King Ahasuerus; and the letters, sealed with his own ring,

**3:13** were sent by the king's couriers to all the provinces, that they should kill and wipe out all the Jews, from boy to old man, little children and women, on one day, that is, the thirteenth of the twelfth month, which is called Adar, and plunder their goods.

**3:14** Now the summary of the letters was this: that all the provinces should know and prepare themselves for the day named beforehand.

**3:15** The couriers who had been sent hurried to carry out the king's command. And immediately the edict was hung up in Susa, while the king and Haman were celebrating a banquet and all the Jews who were in the city were weeping.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — Vulgate uses Roman imperial terminology here, calling the king imperator ('emperor') and using adorabant ('paid homage to') for court prostration.
- **3:7** — Vulgate distinctively adds in urnam ('into an urn') and specifies 'as to what day and what month the Jewish people should be killed', where the Hebrew describes casting the lot from day to day and month to month.
- **3:8** — Vulgate characterizes Jewish practices as 'new' (novis, where the Hebrew has 'different from every people') and adds the warning against growing 'insolent through license' (ut insolescat per licentiam).
- **3:15** — Vulgate concludes with 'all the Jews who were in the city were weeping' (flentibus), whereas the Hebrew states that the city of Susa was thrown into confusion.
