## Text

**7:1** So the king and Haman went in to drink with the queen.

**7:2** And the king said to her on the second day as well, after he had grown warm with wine, "What is your petition, Esther, that it may be given to you? And what do you want to be done? Even if you ask for half of my kingdom, you will obtain it."

**7:3** To him she answered, "If I have found favor in your eyes, king, and if it pleases you, grant me my life, for which I ask, and my people, for whom I plead.

**7:4** For we have been handed over, I and my people, to be crushed, to be slaughtered, and to perish. And if only we were being sold as male slaves and female slaves: it would be a bearable evil, and, groaning, I would keep silent; but now our foe is one whose cruelty rebounds upon the king."

**7:5** And King Ahasuerus answered and said, "Who is this man, and of what power is he, that he dares to do these things?"

**7:6** And Esther said, "Our worst foe and enemy is this Haman." Hearing this, he was immediately stunned, unable to bear the face of the king and the queen.

**7:7** Then the king rose up in anger, and from the place of the banquet went into the garden planted with trees. Haman also rose up to beg Queen Esther for his life, for he understood that evil had been prepared for him by the king.

**7:8** When he had returned from the garden planted with groves and had entered the place of the banquet, he found that Haman had fallen upon the couch on which Esther was lying, and he said, "He even wants to assault the queen, with me present, in my house!" The word had not yet gone out from the mouth of the king, and immediately they covered his face.

**7:9** And Harbona, one of the eunuchs who stood in the service of the king, said, "Look, the timber that he had prepared for Mordecai, who spoke for the king, stands in the house of Haman, having a height of fifty cubits." To him the king said, "Hang him on it."

**7:10** So Haman was hanged on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai, and the king's anger subsided.

## Translator Notes

- **7:2** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew "at the banquet of wine" as "after he had grown warm with wine" (postquam vino incaluerat).
- **7:4** — The Vulgate reads "we have been handed over" (traditi sumus, versus Hebrew "sold"), expands Esther's lament with "it would be a bearable evil, and, groaning," and renders the difficult final Hebrew clause as "whose cruelty rebounds upon the king" (cujus crudelitas redundat in regem).
- **7:5** — Where the Hebrew asks "and where is he," the Vulgate asks "and of what power is he" (cujus potentiæ).
- **7:9** — Across these chapters the Vulgate uses three distinct terms for the instrument of execution: "timber" (lignum) here, "gallows" (patibulum) in 7:10 and 9:13, and "cross" (crux) in 8:7 and 9:25.
