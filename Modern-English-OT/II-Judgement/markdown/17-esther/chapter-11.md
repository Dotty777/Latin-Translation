## Text

**11:1** In the fourth year, while Ptolemy and Cleopatra were reigning, Dositheus, who claimed to be a priest and of Levitical descent, and Ptolemy his son brought this letter of Purim, which they said Lysimachus the son of Ptolemy had translated in Jerusalem.

**11:2** In the second year, while Artaxerxes the Great was reigning, on the first day of the month of Nisan, Mordecai the son of Jair, the son of Shimei, the son of Kish, of the tribe of Benjamin, saw a dream:

**11:3** a Jewish man who lived in the city of Susa, a great man and among the foremost of the royal court.

**11:4** Now he was of that number of captives whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had carried away from Jerusalem with Jeconiah king of Judah.

**11:5** And this was his dream: voices appeared, and tumults, and thunderings, and earthquakes, and turmoil upon the earth;

**11:6** and look, two great dragons, ready for battle against each other.

**11:7** At their cry all the nations were stirred up to fight against the nation of the righteous.

**11:8** And that day was a day of darkness and peril, of tribulation and distress, and great dread upon the earth.

**11:9** And the nation of the righteous was thrown into turmoil, fearing their own evils, and prepared for death.

**11:10** And they cried out to God; and as they cried aloud, a little spring grew into a very great river and overflowed into abundant waters.

**11:11** Light and the sun rose, and the humble were exalted and devoured the renowned.

**11:12** When Mordecai had seen this and had risen from his bed, he pondered what God intended to do; and he held it fixed in his mind, desiring to know what the dream signified.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — In native Clementine Vulgate order, chapters 11-16 gather the Greek-derived Additions translated by Jerome at the end of the book; 11:1 is the Purim colophon (Addition F, end of Greek 10:3), while 11:2 begins Addition A.
- **11:2** — Relocated from old English 1:1 to its native Clementine Vulgate position at 11:2 (Addition A; mapped to Saints.Bible 1:1). In the Additions (11:2, 12:2, 13:1, 16:1), the Vulgate names the king Artaxerxes rather than Ahasuerus (Assuerus). Final period changed to colon to match Latin punctuation introducing 11:3.
- **11:10** — The adopted Clementine text reads creavit (a Clementine textual variant/form for crevit, 'grew', as in 10:6); rendered 'grew' in keeping with the Latin syntax in fluvium maximum.
