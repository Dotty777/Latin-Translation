## Text

**6:1** The king spent that night sleepless, and he ordered the histories and annals of former times to be brought to him. While these were being read in his presence,

**6:2** they came to that place where it was written how Mordecai had reported the plot of Bigthan and Teresh, the eunuchs, who desired to murder King Ahasuerus.

**6:3** When the king heard this, he said, "What honor and reward has Mordecai obtained for this loyalty?" His servants and attendants said to him, "He has received no recompense at all."

**6:4** And immediately the king said, "Who is in the courtyard?" For Haman had entered the inner courtyard of the royal house to suggest to the king that he order Mordecai to be fastened to the gallows that had been prepared for him.

**6:5** The young servants answered, "Haman is standing in the courtyard." And the king said, "Let him come in."

**6:6** And when he had come in, he said to him, "What ought to be done for the man whom the king desires to honor?" Now Haman, thinking in his heart and considering that the king wished to honor no one else except himself,

**6:7** answered, "The man whom the king desires to honor,

**6:8** ought to be clothed in royal garments, and be placed upon a horse that is of the king's saddle, and receive the royal diadem upon his head;

**6:9** and let the foremost of the royal princes and rulers hold his horse, and walking through the square of the city, let him cry out and say, 'Thus will anyone be honored whom the king wishes to honor.'"

**6:10** And the king said to him, "Hurry, and having taken the robe and the horse, do, as you have spoken, for Mordecai the Jew, who sits before the gates of the palace. Be careful not to omit any of these things that you have spoken."

**6:11** So Haman took the robe and the horse, and in the square of the city he walked ahead of Mordecai, who had been clothed and placed upon the horse, and cried out, "Worthy of this honor is anyone whom the king wishes to honor."

**6:12** And Mordecai returned to the gate of the palace, and Haman hurried to go to his house, mourning and with his head covered;

**6:13** and he told Zeresh his wife and his friends everything that had happened to him. The wise men whom he had in counsel, and his wife, answered him, "If Mordecai, before whom you have begun to fall, is of the seed of the Jews, you will not be able to resist him, but you will fall in his sight."

**6:14** While they were still speaking, the king's eunuchs came and compelled him to proceed quickly to the banquet that the queen had prepared.

## Translator Notes

- **6:2** — The Vulgate spells the first eunuch's name Bagathan (matching Bigthan in 2:21 rather than Hebrew Bigthana here) and renders "sought to lay hands on" with the vivid verb jugulare (lit. "to cut the throat of").
- **6:4** — Distinctively in the Vulgate, Haman enters "the inner courtyard" (interius atrium), whereas the Hebrew specifies the outer court.
- **6:8** — Latin qui de sella regis est (lit. "that is of the king's saddle") idiomatically denotes a horse the king himself rides. Moreover, the Vulgate explicitly places the royal diadem on the honored man's head (super caput suum), whereas the Hebrew syntax places it on the horse's head.
- **6:11** — While the Hebrew repeats the exact proclamation of 6:9, the Vulgate varies Haman's cry here to Hoc honore condignus est ("Worthy of this honor is...").
