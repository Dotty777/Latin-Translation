## Text

**12:1** Now at that time he was staying in the king's court with Bagatha and Thara, eunuchs of the king, who were doorkeepers of the palace.

**12:2** And when he had understood their thoughts and examined their schemes more carefully, he learned that they were attempting to lay hands on King Artaxerxes, and he reported it to the king.

**12:3** And when an inquiry had been held concerning both of them, after they confessed, he ordered them to be led away to death.

**12:4** Now the king wrote what had happened in the records; and Mordecai also committed the memory of the event to writing.

**12:5** And the king commanded him to stay in the court of the palace, having given him gifts for the disclosure.

**12:6** But Haman the son of Hammedatha, the Bugaean, was most highly honored before the king, and he wanted to harm Mordecai and his people because of the two eunuchs of the king who had been put to death. And they plundered their goods, or possessions. Now this was the copy of the letter:

## Translator Notes

- **12:1** — Latin Bagatha and Thara reflect the Greek-derived forms of the two eunuchs' names in Addition A (corresponding to Bigthan and Teresh in 2:21).
- **12:6** — Vulgate Bugaeus preserves the Addition A epithet (cf. 'of the lineage of Agag' in 3:1 and 'Macedonian' in 16:10). The final two sentences are Jerome's editorial bridge repeating the close of 3:13 to anchor Artaxerxes's first decree (chapter 13, Addition B).
