## Text

**1:1** In the days of Ahasuerus, who reigned from India to Ethiopia over one hundred twenty-seven provinces,

**1:2** when he sat on the throne of his kingdom, the city of Susa was the beginning of his kingdom.

**1:3** Therefore, in the third year of his reign, he held a great banquet for all his princes and attendants, the mightiest of the Persians and the renowned of the Medes, and the governors of the provinces before him,

**1:4** so that he might display the riches of the glory of his kingdom, and the greatness and ostentation of his power, for a long time, namely one hundred and eighty days.

**1:5** And when the days of the banquet were completed, he invited all the people who were found in Susa, from the greatest to the least, and ordered a banquet to be prepared for seven days in the forecourt of the garden and of the grove that had been planted by royal care and hand.

**1:6** And on every side hung canopies of sky-blue, linen, and violet color, held up by cords of fine linen and purple, which had been passed through ivory rings and were supported by marble pillars. Couches of gold and silver were also arranged on a pavement laid with emerald and Parian stone, which painted work adorned with marvelous variety.

**1:7** Now those who had been invited drank from golden cups, and foods were brought in on ever-different vessels. Wine also, as was worthy of royal magnificence, was set out abundant and choice.

**1:8** Nor was there anyone who compelled those who were unwilling to drink, but it was just as the king had appointed, setting one of his princes over each table so that each person might take what he wanted.

**1:9** Queen Vashti also held a banquet for the women in the palace where King Ahasuerus was accustomed to reside.

**1:10** And so on the seventh day, when the king was quite merry and after excessive drinking had grown warm with unmixed wine, he commanded Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha, Abagtha, Zethar, and Carcas, the seven eunuchs who served in his presence,

**1:11** to bring Queen Vashti before the king, with the diadem placed on her head, so that he might display her beauty to all the peoples and princes, for she was exceedingly beautiful.

**1:12** But she refused, and disdained to come at the king's command that he had sent through the eunuchs. At this the king, angry and inflamed with excessive fury,

**1:13** questioned the wise men who by royal custom were always at his side, and by whose counsel he did all things, men who knew the laws and ordinances of the ancestors

**1:14** (now the foremost and closest were Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena, and Memucan, the seven leaders of the Persians and of the Medes, who saw the king's face and were accustomed to sit first after him),

**1:15** as to what sentence Queen Vashti should be subject to, because she had refused to carry out the command of King Ahasuerus that he had sent through the eunuchs.

**1:16** And Memucan answered in the hearing of the king and the princes, "Not only has Queen Vashti wronged the king, but also all the peoples and princes who are in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus.

**1:17** For the report of the queen will go out to all women, so that they will despise their husbands and say, 'King Ahasuerus ordered Queen Vashti to come in to him, and she refused.'

**1:18** And by this example all the wives of the princes of the Persians and of the Medes will think little of the commands of their husbands; therefore the indignation of the king is just.

**1:19** If it pleases you, let an edict go out from your presence, and let it be written according to the law of the Persians and of the Medes, which it is unlawful to transgress, that Vashti may never again come in to the king, but that another who is better than she may receive her kingdom.

**1:20** And let this be published throughout the entire empire of your provinces (which is vast), and let all wives, both of the greater and of the lesser, give honor to their husbands."

**1:21** His counsel pleased the king and the princes, and the king acted according to the counsel of Memucan,

**1:22** and sent letters to all the provinces of his kingdom, as each people was able to hear and read, in various languages and scripts, that men are the rulers and superiors in their own houses, and that this should be published among all the peoples.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Stage 1 restored the canonical Clementine Vulgate opening (Esther 1:1) here; Mordecai's dream opening (previously placed at 1:1 to mirror Greek Addition A) belongs at its native Vulgate position in 11:2.
- **1:2** — Vulgate exordium ('beginning') distinctively renders the Hebrew phrase for 'Susa the citadel' (habbirah), presenting Susa as the starting seat of the king's realm.
- **1:6** — Vulgate eburneis circulis ('ivory rings') differs from the Hebrew 'silver rings', and smaragdino et pario lapide ('emerald and Parian stone') freely renders the Hebrew list of precious paving stones.
- **1:10** — Vulgate post nimiam potationem incaluisset mero ('after excessive drinking had grown warm with unmixed wine') expands the Hebrew 'when the heart of the king was merry with wine'.
- **1:18** — Vulgate unde regis justa est indignatio ('therefore the indignation of the king is just') differs notably from the Hebrew 'and there will be plenty of contempt and wrath'.
