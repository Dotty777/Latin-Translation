## Text

**4:1** When Mordecai heard these things, he tore his garments and clothed himself in sackcloth, scattering ashes on his head; and in the square in the middle of the city he was crying out with a loud voice, showing the bitterness of his spirit,

**4:2** and walking with this wailing as far as the gates of the palace. For it was not permitted for one clothed in sackcloth to enter the king's court.

**4:3** Also in all the provinces, towns, and places to which the king's cruel decree had come, there was immense mourning among the Jews, fasting, wailing, and weeping, with many using sackcloth and ashes for a bed.

**4:4** Now Esther's maids and eunuchs came in and reported it to her. Hearing this, she was dismayed, and she sent clothing so that, once the sackcloth was removed, they might clothe him; but he refused to accept it.

**4:5** Then, having summoned Hathach the eunuch, whom the king had given to her as an attendant, she commanded him to go to Mordecai and learn from him why he was doing this.

**4:7** He disclosed to him everything that had happened: how Haman had promised to bring silver into the king's treasuries for the slaughter of the Jews.

**4:8** He also gave him a copy of the edict that was hanging in Susa, so that he might show it to the queen and urge her to go in to the king and plead with him for her people.

**4:9** Hathach returned and reported to Esther everything that Mordecai had said.

**4:10** She answered him and ordered him to say to Mordecai:

**4:11** "All the king's servants and all the provinces that are under his dominion know that whether a man or a woman enters the inner courtyard of the king without being called, he is to be put to death immediately without any delay, unless perhaps the king extends the golden scepter toward him as a sign of clemency, and so he may be able to live. How then will I be able to go in to the king, who for thirty days now have not been called to him?"

**4:12** When Mordecai heard this,

**4:13** he sent word to Esther again, saying, "Do not think that you will save your life alone because you are in the king's house above all the Jews;

**4:14** for if you remain silent now, the Jews will be saved through another opportunity, and you and your father's house will perish. And who knows whether you have come to the kingdom for this reason, that you might be prepared at such a time?"

**4:15** And Esther again sent these words to Mordecai:

**4:16** "Go and gather all the Jews whom you find in Susa, and pray for me. Do not eat and do not drink for three days and three nights; and I with my maidservants will fast in the same way, and then I will go in to the king, acting against the law, without being called, and handing myself over to death and danger."

**4:17** So Mordecai went and did everything that Esther had commanded him.

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — The Vulgate expands the Hebrew ("in every province, the place where the king's word and law arrived") by adding "towns, and places" (oppidis, ac locis) and calling the decree "cruel" (crudele).
- **4:8** — Latin pendebat literally means "was hanging" (that is, publicly posted), whereas the Hebrew reads "had been given."
- **4:11** — The Vulgate adds "as a sign of clemency" (pro signo clementiae) and the emphatic "immediately without any delay" (absque ulla cunctatione statim), neither of which has a direct equivalent in the Hebrew.
- **4:14** — Distinctively in the Vulgate, deliverance comes "through another opportunity" (per aliam occasionem, vs. Hebrew "from another place") and the closing clause adds the verb "that you might be prepared at such a time" (ut in tali tempore parareris, vs. Hebrew "for such a time as this").
- **4:16** — The Vulgate introduces an explicit call to prayer (orate pro me, "pray for me," where the Hebrew has "fast for me") and replaces the Hebrew "if I perish, I perish" with "handing myself over to death and danger" (tradensque me morti et periculo).
