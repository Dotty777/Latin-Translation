## Text

**13:1** Now it happened after these things that Amnon the son of David fell deeply in love with a very beautiful sister of Absalom the son of David, named Tamar,

**13:2** and pined desperately for her, so much so that he became sick because of his love for her; for since she was a virgin, it seemed difficult to him to act dishonorably in any way with her.

**13:3** Now Amnon had a friend named Jonadab, the son of Shimeah, David's brother, a very shrewd man.

**13:4** And he said to him, "Why are you so wasted with leanness, son of the king, day by day? Why do you not tell me?" And Amnon said to him, "I love Tamar, the sister of my brother Absalom."

**13:5** Jonadab answered him, "Lie down on your bed and pretend illness; and when your father comes to visit you, say to him, 'Please let Tamar my sister come, so that she may give me food and prepare a savory dish, that I may eat from her hand.'"

**13:6** So Amnon lay down and began to act as if he were sick; and when the king had come to visit him, Amnon said to the king, "I beg you, let Tamar my sister come, so that she may make two little broths before my eyes, and I may take food from her hand."

**13:7** Therefore David sent home to Tamar, saying, "Come to the house of Amnon your brother, and make a savory dish for him."

**13:8** And Tamar came to the house of Amnon her brother, and he was lying down. And taking flour, she mixed it, and making it liquid, she cooked the little broths before his eyes.

**13:9** And taking what she had cooked, she poured it out and set it before him, yet he refused to eat. And Amnon said, "Put everyone out from me." And when they had put everyone out,

**13:10** Amnon said to Tamar, "Bring the food into the inner chamber, so that I may eat from your hand." So Tamar took the little broths that she had made and brought them to Amnon her brother into the inner chamber.

**13:11** And when she had offered the food to him, he seized her and said, "Come, lie with me, my sister."

**13:12** And she answered him, "No, my brother, do not force me, for this is not lawful in Israel; do not commit this folly.

**13:13** For I will not be able to bear my disgrace, and you will be like one of the fools in Israel. Rather, speak to the king, and he will not deny me to you."

**13:14** But he refused to yield to her pleas, and prevailing in strength, he forced her and lay with her.

**13:15** Then Amnon hated her with a very great hatred, so that the hatred with which he hated her was greater than the love with which he had loved her before. And Amnon said to her, "Get up and go."

**13:16** And she answered him, "This evil that you are now doing against me, driving me out, is greater than what you did before." Yet he refused to listen to her;

**13:17** instead, having called the young man who attended him, he said, "Put this woman outside away from me, and shut the door behind her."

**13:18** Now she was clothed in an ankle-length tunic, for the virgin daughters of the king used to wear garments of this kind. So his attendant put her outside, and shut the doors behind her.

**13:19** And sprinkling ashes upon her head, with her ankle-length tunic torn and her hands placed upon her head, she went walking along and crying out.

**13:20** Then Absalom her brother said to her, "Has Amnon your brother lain with you? But now, sister, keep silent; he is your brother; do not afflict your heart over this matter." So Tamar remained, wasting away, in the house of Absalom her brother.

**13:21** Now when King David heard these words, he was greatly grieved; yet he was unwilling to grieve the spirit of Amnon his son, because he loved him, since he was his firstborn.

**13:22** Moreover, Absalom spoke neither evil nor good to Amnon, for Absalom hated Amnon because he had violated Tamar his sister.

**13:23** Now it happened after a period of two years that Absalom's sheep were being sheared in Baal-hazor, which is near Ephraim; and Absalom invited all the sons of the king,

**13:24** and came to the king and said to him, "Look, the sheep of your servant are being sheared; please let the king come with his servants to his servant."

**13:25** And the king said to Absalom, "No, my son, do not ask that we all come and burden you." And although he pressed him, he was unwilling to go, yet he blessed him.

**13:26** And Absalom said, "If you do not wish to come, I beg you, let at least Amnon my brother come with us." And the king said to him, "It is not necessary that he go with you."

**13:27** So Absalom pressed him, and he let Amnon and all the sons of the king go with him. And Absalom had prepared a feast like the feast of a king.

**13:28** Now Absalom had commanded his young men, saying, "Watch when Amnon is drunk with wine and I say to you, 'Strike him and kill him.' Do not be afraid, for it is I who command you; be strong and be valiant men."

**13:29** So Absalom's young men did against Amnon just as Absalom had commanded them. And rising up, all the sons of the king each mounted his mule and fled.

**13:30** And while they were still going on the way, a report reached David, saying, "Absalom has struck down all the sons of the king, and not even one of them has remained."

**13:31** So the king rose up, and tore his garments, and fell upon the ground; and all his servants who stood by him tore their garments.

**13:32** Then Jonadab, the son of Shimeah, David's brother, answered and said, "Let not my lord the king suppose that all the young men, the sons of the king, have been killed; Amnon alone is dead, for he was placed in the mouth of Absalom from the day on which he forced Tamar his sister.

**13:33** Now therefore, let not my lord the king lay this word upon his heart, saying, 'All the sons of the king have been killed,' for Amnon alone is dead."

**13:34** Meanwhile Absalom fled. And the young watchman lifted up his eyes and gazed, and look, many people were coming along a byway from the side of the mountain.

**13:35** Then Jonadab said to the king, "Look, the sons of the king are here; according to the word of your servant, so it has happened."

**13:36** And when he had finished speaking, the sons of the king also appeared; and entering, they lifted up their voice and wept; and the king also, and all his servants, wept with a very great weeping.

**13:37** Now Absalom, fleeing, went away to Talmai, the son of Ammihud, king of Geshur. So David mourned for his son every day.

**13:38** And Absalom, after he had fled and had come to Geshur, was there for three years.

**13:39** And King David ceased to pursue Absalom, because he had been comforted over the death of Amnon.

## Translator Notes

- **13:6** — Latin sorbitiunculas ("little broths") reflects the Vulgate's understanding of Tamar's preparation as a liquid invalid's broth (cf. liquefaciens, "making it liquid," in v. 8), where the Hebrew levivot refers to small cakes or dumplings.
- **13:21** — The clause "yet he was unwilling to grieve the spirit of Amnon his son, because he loved him, since he was his firstborn" is a major Vulgate reading shared with the Septuagint and 4QSam-a but absent from the Masoretic Text.
- **13:27** — The concluding sentence, "And Absalom had prepared a feast like the feast of a king," is present in the Vulgate (alongside the Septuagint and 4QSam-a) but absent from the Masoretic Text.
- **13:32** — Latin in ore Absalom erat positus literally means "he was placed in the mouth of Absalom," a Semitic idiom indicating that Amnon had been marked for death by Absalom's spoken decree or fixed intention.
- **13:39** — The Vulgate reads the difficult Hebrew clause as David ceasing "to pursue Absalom" (persequi Absalom), whereas the Masoretic Text is commonly understood as David longing to go out to Absalom.
