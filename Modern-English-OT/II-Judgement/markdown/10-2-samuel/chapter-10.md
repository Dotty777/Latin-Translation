## Text

**10:1** Now it happened after these things that the king of the sons of Ammon died, and Hanun his son reigned in his place.

**10:2** And David said, "I will show mercy to Hanun the son of Nahash, just as his father showed mercy to me." Therefore David sent to comfort him through his servants over the death of his father. Now when the servants of David had come into the land of the sons of Ammon,

**10:3** the princes of the sons of Ammon said to Hanun their lord, "Do you think that for the honor of your father David has sent comforters to you, and has David not sent his servants to you for this purpose, to investigate and spy out the city and overthrow it?"

**10:4** Therefore Hanun took the servants of David, and shaved off half of their beards and cut off their garments in the middle as far as the buttocks, and sent them away.

**10:5** When this was reported to David, he sent to meet them, for the men were very shamefully humiliated; and David instructed them, "Stay in Jericho until your beards grow, and then return."

**10:6** Now the sons of Ammon, seeing that they had done an injury to David, sent and hired for wages the Syrians of Rehob and the Syrians of Zobah, twenty thousand foot soldiers, and from the king of Maacah one thousand men, and from Ishtob twelve thousand men.

**10:7** When David heard this, he sent Joab and the entire army of warriors.

**10:8** So the sons of Ammon went out and drew up the battle line before the very entrance of the gate, while the Syrians of Zobah and Rehob, and Ishtob and Maacah, were apart in the field.

**10:9** Therefore Joab, seeing that battle had been prepared against him both in front and behind, chose from all the chosen men of Israel and arrayed the battle line against the Syrians;

**10:10** and the remaining part of the people he handed over to Abishai his brother, who drew up the battle line against the sons of Ammon.

**10:11** And Joab said, "If the Syrians prevail against me, you will be a help to me; but if the sons of Ammon prevail against you, I will come to your aid.

**10:12** Be a strong man, and let us fight for our people and the city of our God; and the Lord will do what is good in His sight."

**10:13** So Joab, and the people who were with him, entered into combat against the Syrians, who immediately fled from before him.

**10:14** Now when the sons of Ammon saw that the Syrians had fled, they themselves also fled from before Abishai and entered the city; and Joab returned from the sons of Ammon and came to Jerusalem.

**10:15** Therefore the Syrians, seeing that they had fallen before Israel, gathered together.

**10:16** And Hadarezer sent and led out the Syrians who were beyond the river, and brought their army; and Shobach, the commander of Hadarezer's army, was their leader.

**10:17** When this was reported to David, he gathered all Israel, and crossed the Jordan, and came to Helam; and the Syrians drew up the battle line opposite David and fought against him.

**10:18** And the Syrians fled from before Israel, and David killed of the Syrians seven hundred chariots and forty thousand horsemen; and he struck down Shobach, the commander of the army, who immediately died.

**10:19** Now when all the kings who were in support of Hadarezer saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they were terrified, and fifty-eight thousand fled before Israel. And they made peace with Israel and served them; and the Syrians were afraid to give aid to the sons of Ammon any longer.

## Translator Notes

- **10:6** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew idiom "made themselves odious" as "done an injury" (injuriam fecissent), and treats Istob (Ishtob) as a proper name where the Hebrew is usually read as "the men of Tob."
- **10:12** — The Vulgate reads singular "city" (civitate), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has plural "cities."
- **10:16** — The Vulgate preserves the form Adarezer ("Hadarezer," matching 1 Chronicles and the Septuagint) rather than the Masoretic "Hadadezer," and reads "brought their army" (adduxit eorum exercitum) where the Masoretic Text has "they came to Helam."
- **10:19** — The clause "they were terrified, and fifty-eight thousand fled before Israel" is a distinctly Vulgate reading (stemming from the Old Latin tradition) absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text; likewise "in support of" (in præsidio) corresponds to Hebrew "servants/vassals."
