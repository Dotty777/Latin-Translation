## Text

**9:1** And David said, "Do you think there is anyone remaining from the house of Saul, so that I may show mercy to him for the sake of Jonathan?"

**9:2** Now there was a servant of the house of Saul named Ziba. When the king had called him to himself, he said to him, "Are you Ziba?" And he answered, "I am your servant."

**9:3** And the king said, "Does anyone still remain from the house of Saul, so that I may show the mercy of God to him?" And Ziba said to the king, "A son of Jonathan remains, crippled in his feet."

**9:4** "Where is he?" he said. And Ziba said to the king, "Look, he is in the house of Machir son of Ammiel, in Lo-debar."

**9:5** So King David sent and brought him from the house of Machir son of Ammiel, from Lo-debar.

**9:6** Now when Mephibosheth son of Jonathan, son of Saul, had come to David, he fell on his face and paid homage. And David said, "Mephibosheth?" And he answered, "Here I am, your servant."

**9:7** And David said to him, "Do not be afraid, for I will surely show mercy to you for the sake of your father Jonathan, and I will restore to you all the fields of your father Saul; and you will eat bread at my table always."

**9:8** And paying homage to him, he said, "Who am I, your servant, that you have looked upon a dead dog like me?"

**9:9** So the king called Ziba, Saul's attendant, and said to him, "Everything that belonged to Saul and all his house I have given to the son of your master.

**9:10** Cultivate the land for him, therefore, you and your sons and your servants, and you will bring in food for the son of your master so that he may be fed; but Mephibosheth, the son of your master, will always eat bread at my table." Now Ziba had fifteen sons and twenty servants.

**9:11** And Ziba said to the king, "Just as you have commanded your servant, my lord the king, so your servant will do; and Mephibosheth will eat at my table, like one of the sons of the king."

**9:12** Now Mephibosheth had a young son named Mica; and all the family of the house of Ziba served Mephibosheth.

**9:13** Furthermore, Mephibosheth lived in Jerusalem, because he ate continually from the king's table; and he was lame in both feet.

## Translator Notes

- **9:11** — Vulgate "comedet super mensam meam" ("will eat at my table") uses the future tense and retains the Masoretic Text's first-person "my table" without a change of speaker, whereas the Septuagint reads "David's table" as narrator commentary.
- **9:12** — Vulgate "cognatio" ("family" or "kindred") renders the Hebrew noun more commonly translated "all who dwelt in the house."
