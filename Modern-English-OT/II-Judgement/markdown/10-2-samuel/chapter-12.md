## Text

**12:1** Therefore the Lord sent Nathan to David; and when he had come to him, he said to him, "Two men were in one city, one rich and the other poor.

**12:2** The rich man had very many sheep and cattle.

**12:3** But the poor man had nothing at all except one little sheep which he had bought and nourished, and which had grown up with him together with his sons, eating from his bread and drinking from his cup and sleeping in his bosom; and it was to him like a daughter.

**12:4** Now when a certain traveler had come to the rich man, refraining from taking from his own sheep and from his cattle to provide a feast for that traveler who had come to him, he took the sheep of the poor man and prepared food for the man who had come to him."

**12:5** Then David became exceedingly angry with indignation against that man, and he said to Nathan, "As the Lord lives, surely the man who did this is a son of death.

**12:6** He shall repay the sheep fourfold, because he did this thing and showed no pity."

**12:7** Then Nathan said to David, "You are that man. This is what the Lord God of Israel says: 'I anointed you as king over Israel, and I rescued you from the hand of Saul,

**12:8** and I gave you the house of your master and the wives of your master in your bosom, and I gave you the house of Israel and of Judah; and if these things are small, I will add much greater things to you.

**12:9** Why then have you despised the word of the Lord, to do evil in My sight? You struck down Uriah the Hittite with the sword, and took his wife to be your wife, and killed him with the sword of the sons of Ammon.

**12:10** For this reason the sword will not depart from your house forever, because you have despised Me and have taken the wife of Uriah the Hittite so that she might be your wife.'

**12:11** Therefore, this is what the Lord says: 'Look, I will raise up evil against you out of your own house, and I will take your wives before your eyes and give them to your neighbor, and he will sleep with your wives before the eyes of this sun.

**12:12** For you acted in secret, but I will do this thing in the sight of all Israel and in the sight of the sun.'"

**12:13** And David said to Nathan, "I have sinned against the Lord." And Nathan said to David, "The Lord also has removed your sin; you will not die.

**12:14** Nevertheless, because you have caused the enemies of the Lord to blaspheme on account of this thing, the son who has been born to you will surely die."

**12:15** Then Nathan returned to his house. And the Lord struck the little child whom the wife of Uriah had borne to David, and he was despaired of.

**12:16** And David pleaded with the Lord for the little child; and David fasted with a fast, and going inside by himself, he lay upon the ground.

**12:17** Then the elders of his house came, urging him to get up from the ground; yet he was unwilling, nor did he eat food with them.

**12:18** Now it happened on the seventh day that the infant died. And David's servants were afraid to report to him that the little child was dead, for they said, "Look, while the little child was still alive, we spoke to him, and he did not listen to our voice; how much more, if we say, 'The boy is dead,' will he afflict himself?"

**12:19** Therefore when David saw his servants whispering, he understood that the little infant was dead; and he said to his servants, "Is the boy dead?" And they answered him, "He is dead."

**12:20** Then David rose from the ground, and washed and anointed himself; and when he had changed his clothing, he entered the house of the Lord and worshiped; and he came into his own house and asked that they set bread before him, and he ate.

**12:21** Then his servants said to him, "What is this thing that you have done? On account of the infant, while he was still alive, you fasted and were weeping; but when the boy died, you rose and ate bread."

**12:22** And he said, "On account of the infant, while he was still alive, I fasted and wept; for I was saying, 'Who knows whether perhaps the Lord may grant him to me, and the infant may live?'

**12:23** But now that he is dead, why should I fast? Will I be able to call him back any longer? Rather, I will go to him, whereas he will not return to me."

**12:24** And David comforted Bathsheba his wife, and went in to her and slept with her; and she bore a son, and called his name Solomon; and the Lord loved him.

**12:25** And He sent by the hand of Nathan the prophet, and called his name Beloved of the Lord, because the Lord loved him.

**12:26** Now Joab was fighting against Rabbah of the sons of Ammon, and was storming the royal city.

**12:27** And Joab sent messengers to David, saying, "I have fought against Rabbah, and the City of Waters is about to be captured.

**12:28** Now therefore, gather the remaining part of the people, and besiege the city, and capture it, so that when the city has been laid waste by me, the victory may not be attributed to my name."

**12:29** So David gathered all the people and set out against Rabbah; and when he had fought, he captured it.

**12:30** And he took the crown of their king from his head, weighing a talent of gold, having most precious gems; and it was placed upon the head of David. Moreover, he also carried away very much plunder from the city.

**12:31** Bringing out its people as well, he sawed them, and drove iron-shod carts over them, and cut them apart with knives, and passed them through in the mold of bricks; thus he did to all the cities of the sons of Ammon. And David and all the army returned to Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **12:8** — The Vulgate renders this condition with a present and future indicative ("if these things are small, I will add"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text is usually understood as a past contrafactual ("if that had been too little, I would have added").
- **12:15** — Latin desperatus est literally means "he was despaired of" (that is, his recovery was considered hopeless), corresponding to the Hebrew "he became gravely ill."
- **12:25** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew name Jedidiah into Latin as Amabilis Domino ("Beloved of the Lord"), preserving the wordplay with the following clause; grammatically, "He" continues the Lord as subject from the end of v. 24.
- **12:27** — Latin capienda est ("is about to be captured") and the preceding imperfect expugnabat ("was storming" in v. 26) portray the capture of the City of Waters as imminent rather than already completed, contrasting with the Hebrew perfect verbs.
- **12:31** — The Vulgate renders the difficult Hebrew idioms as explicit acts of execution ("sawed them," "drove iron-shod carts over them," "cut them apart with knives") and translates the obscure term malben (brick-kiln or brick-mold) literally as in typo laterum ("in the mold of bricks").
