## Text

**8:1** Now it happened after these things that David struck the Philistines and humbled them, and David took the bridle of tribute from the hand of the Philistines.

**8:2** And he struck Moab and measured them with a cord, making them level with the ground; and he measured two cords, one to put to death and one to keep alive; and Moab came to serve David under tribute.

**8:3** And David struck Hadarezer son of Rehob, king of Zobah, when he set out to rule over the Euphrates River.

**8:4** And when David had captured from his side one thousand seven hundred horsemen and twenty thousand foot soldiers, he hamstrung all the chariot horses, but of them he left a hundred chariots.

**8:5** Syria of Damascus also came to bring aid to Hadarezer king of Zobah, and David struck twenty-two thousand men of Syria.

**8:6** And David placed a garrison in Syria of Damascus, and Syria came to serve David under tribute; and the Lord preserved David in all things wherever he went.

**8:7** And David took the golden arms that the servants of Hadarezer had, and brought them to Jerusalem.

**8:8** And from Betah and from Berothai, cities of Hadarezer, King David took a very great amount of bronze.

**8:9** Now Tou king of Hamath heard that David had struck all the strength of Hadarezer,

**8:10** and Tou sent his son Joram to King David to greet him with congratulations and to give thanks, because he had overcome Hadarezer in battle and struck him; for Hadarezer was an enemy of Tou. And in his hand were vessels of gold, and vessels of silver, and vessels of bronze.

**8:11** These also King David consecrated to the Lord, together with the silver and gold that he had consecrated from all the nations that he had subdued,

**8:12** from Syria, and Moab, and the sons of Ammon, and the Philistines, and Amalek, and from the spoils of Hadarezer son of Rehob, king of Zobah.

**8:13** David also made a name for himself when he returned after Syria had been captured in the Valley of Salt Pits, with eighteen thousand killed;

**8:14** and he placed guards in Idumea and stationed a garrison; and all Idumea came to serve David, and the Lord preserved David in all things wherever he went.

**8:15** And David reigned over all Israel; and David also administered judgment and justice to all his people.

**8:16** Now Joab son of Zeruiah was over the army; and Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was keeper of the records;

**8:17** and Zadok son of Ahitub and Ahimelech son of Abiathar were priests; and Seraiah was scribe;

**8:18** and Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and the Pelethites; and the sons of David were priests.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — Vulgate "frenum tributi" ("the bridle of tribute") interprets the obscure Hebrew phrase "Metheg-ammah," which many translations leave as a place name.
- **8:2** — Vulgate "duos funiculos, unum ad occidendum, et unum ad vivificandum" counts two cords in total (one for death and one for life), whereas the Masoretic Text specifies two cords to put to death and one full cord to keep alive.
- **8:3** — Vulgate "Adarezer" (Hadarezer) here, like "Thou" (Tou) in 8:9-10, preserves the name forms matching 1 Chronicles 18 rather than Masoretic 2 Samuel's "Hadadezer" and "Toi."
- **8:13** — Vulgate "capta Syria in valle Salinarum" reads "Syria" (with the Masoretic Text's "Aram" against the Septuagint's "Edom") and renders the valley literally as "Salt Pits"; in 8:14 the Vulgate uses the Greco-Latin name "Idumea" for Edom.
