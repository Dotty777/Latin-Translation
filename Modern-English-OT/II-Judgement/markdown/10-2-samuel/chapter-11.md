## Text

**11:1** Now it happened, at the turn of the year, at the time when kings are accustomed to march out to war, that David sent Joab, and his servants with him, and all Israel, and they devastated the sons of Ammon and besieged Rabbah; but David remained in Jerusalem.

**11:2** While these things were taking place, it happened that David rose from his bed after midday and walked about on the roof of the royal house; and he saw a woman washing herself opposite him on her roof; and the woman was very beautiful.

**11:3** Therefore the king sent and inquired who the woman was. And it was reported to him that she was Bathsheba the daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite.

**11:4** So David, having sent messengers, took her; and when she had come in to him, he slept with her, and immediately she was purified from her uncleanness,

**11:5** and she returned to her house, having conceived a child. And she sent and informed David, and said, "I have conceived."

**11:6** Now David sent to Joab, saying, "Send Uriah the Hittite to me." And Joab sent Uriah to David.

**11:7** And Uriah came to David. And David asked how well Joab and the people were doing, and how the war was being conducted.

**11:8** And David said to Uriah, "Go to your house and wash your feet." And Uriah went out from the king's house, and a royal meal followed him.

**11:9** But Uriah slept before the gate of the royal house with the other servants of his lord, and did not go down to his house.

**11:10** And it was reported to David by those who said, "Uriah did not go to his house." And David said to Uriah, "Have you not come from a journey? Why did you not go down to your house?"

**11:11** And Uriah said to David, "The ark of God and Israel and Judah dwell in tents, and my lord Joab and the servants of my lord remain on the face of the earth; and shall I enter my house to eat and drink and sleep with my wife? By your welfare and by the welfare of your soul, I will not do this thing."

**11:12** Therefore David said to Uriah, "Stay here today also, and tomorrow I will send you away." Uriah stayed in Jerusalem on that day and the next;

**11:13** and David summoned him to eat before him and drink, and made him drunk; and going out in the evening, he slept on his bed with the servants of his lord, and did not go down to his house.

**11:14** So morning came, and David wrote a letter to Joab, and sent it by the hand of Uriah,

**11:15** writing in the letter, "Place Uriah at the front of the war, where the battle is fiercest, and abandon him, so that he may be struck down and perish."

**11:16** Therefore, while Joab was besieging the city, he placed Uriah in the place where he knew the strongest men were.

**11:17** And the men went out from the city and fought against Joab, and some of the people of David's servants fell, and Uriah the Hittite also died.

**11:18** Therefore Joab sent and reported to David all the matters of the battle;

**11:19** and he instructed the messenger, saying, "When you have finished all the accounts of the war to the king,

**11:20** if you see him become indignant, and he says, 'Why did you approach the wall to fight? Did you not know that many missiles are hurled from above off the wall?

**11:21** Who struck down Abimelech the son of Jerubbaal? Did not a woman cast a fragment of a millstone on him from the wall and kill him in Thebez? Why did you come near the wall?' you will say, 'Your servant Uriah the Hittite has also fallen.'"

**11:22** So the messenger departed, and came and told David everything that Joab had instructed him.

**11:23** And the messenger said to David, "The men prevailed against us and came out to us into the field, but having made an assault, we pursued them as far as the gate of the city.

**11:24** And the archers directed missiles at your servants from the wall above, and some of the king's servants died; moreover, your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead."

**11:25** And David said to the messenger, "You will say these things to Joab: 'Do not let this matter break you, for the outcome of war is varied; the sword consumes now this one and now that one. Strengthen your warriors against the city so that you may destroy it, and encourage them.'"

**11:26** Now the wife of Uriah heard that Uriah her husband had died, and she mourned for him.

**11:27** Now when the mourning had passed, David sent and brought her into his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son. And this thing that David had done was displeasing before the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **11:2** — The Vulgate distinctly locates this scene "after midday" (post meridiem, rather than Hebrew "at evening") and places Bathsheba "on her roof" (super solarium suum), whereas the Hebrew says David saw her "from the roof."
- **11:4** — Latin statimque sanctificata est ("and immediately she was purified/sanctified") places Bathsheba's ritual purification immediately after the encounter, whereas translations of the Hebrew commonly treat it as a parenthetical explanation of her preceding bath.
- **11:11** — The Vulgate explicitly reads "the ark of God" (Arca Dei, where the Hebrew has simply "the ark") and "on the face of the earth" (super faciem terræ, where the Hebrew has "in the open field").
- **11:21** — The Vulgate preserves Gideon's original name Jerobaal ("Jerubbaal," matching Judges and the Septuagint) rather than the Masoretic dysphemism "Jerubbesheth."
- **11:25** — The Vulgate addresses Joab with "Strengthen your warriors ... and encourage them" (bellatores tuos ... exhortare eos), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "intensify your battle ... and encourage him" (directing the messenger to encourage Joab).
