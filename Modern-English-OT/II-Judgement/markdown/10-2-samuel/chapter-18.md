## Text

**18:1** Therefore, having reviewed his people, David appointed tribunes and centurions over them,

**18:2** and he placed a third part of the people under the hand of Joab, and a third part under the hand of Abishai the son of Zeruiah, the brother of Joab, and a third part under the hand of Ittai, who was from Gath. And the king said to the people, "I also will go out with you."

**18:3** And the people answered, "You will not go out; for if we flee, it will not greatly matter to them about us, or if half of us fall, they will not care much, because you alone are counted for ten thousand. Therefore it is better that you be a protection for us in the city."

**18:4** To them the king said, "What seems right to you, this I will do." So the king stood beside the gate, and the people were going out in their companies, by hundreds and by thousands.

**18:5** And the king commanded Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, saying, "Preserve the boy Absalom for me." And all the people heard the king commanding all the leaders on behalf of Absalom.

**18:6** So the people went out into the field against Israel, and the battle took place in the forest of Ephraim.

**18:7** And the people of Israel were struck down there by the army of David, and a great slaughter took place on that day, of twenty thousand.

**18:8** Now the battle there was spread over the face of all the land, and there were many more of the people whom the forest had consumed than those whom the sword had devoured on that day.

**18:9** Now it happened that Absalom encountered the servants of David while riding on a mule; and when the mule had gone under a thick and great oak, his head caught fast in the oak, and while he was suspended between heaven and earth, the mule on which he had been riding passed on.

**18:10** Now someone saw this and reported it to Joab, saying, "I saw Absalom hanging from an oak."

**18:11** And Joab said to the man who had reported it to him, "If you saw him, why did you not pierce him to the ground, and I would have given you ten shekels of silver and one belt?"

**18:12** And he said to Joab, "If you were to weigh out a thousand pieces of silver in my hands, I would by no means lay my hand on the king's son; for in our hearing the king commanded you, Abishai, and Ittai, saying, 'Guard the boy Absalom for me.'

**18:13** But even if I had acted boldly against my own life, this could by no means have been hidden from the king, and you would stand on the opposite side?"

**18:14** And Joab said, "Not as you wish, but I will attack him before you." So he took three lances in his hand and drove them into the heart of Absalom; and while he was still quivering, caught in the oak,

**18:15** ten young men, armor-bearers of Joab, ran up and, striking him, killed him.

**18:16** Then Joab sounded the trumpet and held back the people, so that they would not pursue fleeing Israel, wanting to spare the multitude.

**18:17** And they took Absalom and threw him into a great pit in the forest, and they piled up a very great heap of stones over him; meanwhile, all Israel fled to their tents.

**18:18** Now Absalom, while he was still living, had erected for himself the pillar which is in the Valley of the King; for he had said, "I have no son, and this will be a memorial of my name." And he called the pillar by his own name, and it is called the Hand of Absalom to this day.

**18:19** Now Ahimaaz the son of Zadok said, "I will run and report to the king that the Lord has rendered judgment for him from the hand of his enemies."

**18:20** To him Joab said, "You will not be a messenger on this day, but you will bring news on another; today I do not want you to bring news, for the king's son is dead."

**18:21** And Joab said to Cushi, "Go and report to the king what you have seen." Cushi bowed down to Joab and ran.

**18:22** Then Ahimaaz the son of Zadok said again to Joab, "What prevents it if I also run after Cushi?" And Joab said to him, "Why do you want to run, my son? You will not be a bearer of good news."

**18:23** And he answered, "What then if I run?" And he said to him, "Run." So Ahimaaz, running by the shorter way, passed Cushi.

**18:24** Now David was sitting between the two gates; and the watchman, who was on the top of the gate above the wall, lifting up his eyes, saw a man running alone.

**18:25** And crying out, he informed the king; and the king said, "If he is alone, there is good news in his mouth." Now as he was hurrying and coming nearer,

**18:26** the watchman saw another man running, and shouting on the roof, he said, "Another man appears to me, running alone." And the king said, "This one also is a good messenger."

**18:27** Now the watchman said, "I observe the running of the first to be like the running of Ahimaaz the son of Zadok." And the king said, "He is a good man, and he comes carrying good news."

**18:28** Then Ahimaaz, calling out, said to the king, "Hail, king." And bowing down to the king before him, prostrate to the ground, he said, "Blessed be the Lord your God, who has delivered up the men who lifted up their hands against my lord the king."

**18:29** And the king said, "Is there peace for the boy Absalom?" And Ahimaaz said, "I saw a great uproar when your servant Joab, king, sent me, your servant; I know nothing else."

**18:30** To him the king said, "Step aside and stand here." And when he had stepped aside and was standing,

**18:31** Cushi appeared, and as he came, he said, "I bring good news, my lord the king; for the Lord has judged in your favor today from the hand of all who rose up against you."

**18:32** And the king said to Cushi, "Is there peace for the boy Absalom?" Answering him, Cushi said, "May the enemies of my lord the king, and all who rise up against him for evil, become like the boy."

## Translator Notes

- **18:5** — The Vulgate reads "Servate mihi puerum Absalom" ("Preserve the boy Absalom for me"), agreeing with the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew reads "Deal gently for my sake with the young man Absalom."
- **18:14** — The Vulgate reads "Non sicut tu vis, sed aggrediar eum coram te" ("Not as you wish, but I will attack him before you"), whereas the Hebrew has "I will not linger like this with you." Later in the verse, the Vulgate uses "palpitaret" ("was still quivering") where the Hebrew has "was still alive."
- **18:18** — The Vulgate renders the name of the monument literally as "Manus Absalom" ("the Hand of Absalom"), reflecting the Hebrew idiom in which "hand" denotes a memorial pillar.
- **18:21** — Throughout verses 21-32, the Vulgate treats "Chusi" (Cushi) as a personal name rather than the gentilic "the Cushite" found in the Hebrew.
- **18:22** — The Vulgate reads "non eris boni nuntii bajulus" ("You will not be a bearer of good news"), whereas the Hebrew reads "you will have no reward for the news."
