## Text

**4:1** Now Ish-bosheth the son of Saul heard that Abner had fallen in Hebron, and his hands went limp, and all Israel was thrown into turmoil.

**4:2** Now the son of Saul had two men who were captains of raiders: the name of the one was Baanah, and the name of the other was Rechab, sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, of the sons of Benjamin; for Beeroth also was reckoned in Benjamin.

**4:3** And the Beerothites fled to Gittaim, and they were foreigners there until that time.

**4:4** Now Jonathan the son of Saul had a son who was disabled in his feet; for he was five years old when the news concerning Saul and Jonathan came from Jezreel. So his nurse picked him up and fled; and as she hurried to flee, he fell and became lame. And he had the name Mephibosheth.

**4:5** Therefore the sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, Rechab and Baanah, came and entered the house of Ish-bosheth in the heat of the day, while he was sleeping on his couch at midday. And the female doorkeeper of the house, while cleaning wheat, fell asleep.

**4:6** And they entered the house secretly, taking ears of wheat; and Rechab and Baanah his brother struck him in the groin and fled.

**4:7** Now when they had entered the house, he was sleeping on his bed in the bedroom, and striking him, they killed him; and having taken off his head, they went away by the road of the wilderness all night,

**4:8** and they brought the head of Ish-bosheth to David in Hebron, and said to the king, "Here is the head of Ish-bosheth the son of Saul, your enemy, who was seeking your life; and the Lord has given to my lord the king vengeance today on Saul and on his offspring."

**4:9** But David, answering Rechab and Baanah his brother, the sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, said to them, "As the Lord lives, who has rescued my life from every distress,

**4:10** surely the one who had reported to me and had said, 'Saul is dead,' who thought he was announcing good news, I seized and killed him in Ziklag, to whom it was fitting to give a reward for the message.

**4:11** How much more now, when wicked men have killed an innocent man in his own house, upon his own bed, shall I not require his blood from your hand, and remove you from the earth?"

**4:12** Therefore David commanded his servants, and they killed them; and cutting off their hands and feet, they hung them over the pool in Hebron. But they took the head of Ish-bosheth and buried it in the tomb of Abner in Hebron.

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — The Vulgate reads usque ad tempus illud ("until that time"), whereas the Hebrew reads "to this day."
- **4:5** — The Vulgate includes the sleeping female doorkeeper cleaning wheat (shared with the Septuagint) at the end of verse 5 alongside the assassins' secret entry taking ears of wheat in verse 6 (reflecting the Hebrew), preserving both textual traditions.
- **4:10** — The Vulgate reads cui oportebat mercedem dare pro nuntio ("to whom it was fitting to give a reward for the message"), an ironic rendering of a difficult Hebrew clause often translated "which was the reward I gave him for his news."
