## Text

**14:1** Now Joab the son of Zeruiah, perceiving that the king's heart was turned toward Absalom,

**14:2** sent to Tekoa and brought a wise woman from there, and said to her, "Pretend to be mourning, and put on a mourning garment, and do not anoint yourself with oil, so that you may be like a woman already mourning a dead person for a very long time;

**14:3** and you will go in to the king and speak words of this kind to him." And Joab put the words in her mouth.

**14:4** Therefore, when the Tekoite woman had gone in to the king, she fell to the ground before him and bowed in homage, and said, "Save me, king."

**14:5** And the king said to her, "What matter do you have?" She answered, "Alas, I am a widowed woman, for my husband has died.

**14:6** And your maidservant had two sons, who quarreled against each other in the field, and there was no one who could restrain them; and the one struck the other and killed him.

**14:7** And look, the entire clan, rising up against your maidservant, says, 'Hand over the one who struck his brother, so that we may put him to death for the life of his brother whom he killed, and destroy the heir.' And they are seeking to extinguish my spark that remains, so that neither name nor remnant may survive for my husband upon the earth."

**14:8** And the king said to the woman, "Go to your house, and I will give an order on your behalf."

**14:9** And the Tekoite woman said to the king, "Upon me, my lord the king, let the iniquity be, and upon my father's house; but let the king and his throne be innocent."

**14:10** And the king said, "Whoever speaks against you, bring him to me, and he will not touch you again."

**14:11** And she said, "Let the king remember the Lord his God, so that the kinsmen of blood may not multiply to take vengeance, and by no means kill my son." And he said, "As the Lord lives, not one of the hairs of your son will fall to the ground."

**14:12** Therefore the woman said, "Let your maidservant speak a word to my lord the king." And he said, "Speak."

**14:13** And the woman said, "Why have you devised a thing of this kind against the people of God, and why has the king spoken this word so that he sins and does not bring back his banished one?

**14:14** We all die, and like waters that do not return we flow away into the earth; nor does God wish a soul to perish, but He reconsiders, taking thought so that the one who has been cast out may not completely perish.

**14:15** Now therefore I have come to speak this word to my lord the king in the presence of the people. And your maidservant said, 'I will speak to the king, if somehow the king may carry out the word of his maidservant.'

**14:16** And the king listened, so as to deliver his maidservant from the hand of all who wanted to destroy me and my son together from the inheritance of God.

**14:17** Let your maidservant therefore say, may the word of my lord the king be like a sacrifice. For like an angel of God, so is my lord the king, so that he is moved neither by blessing nor by curse; for this reason the Lord your God is also with you."

**14:18** And answering, the king said to the woman, "Do not hide from me the thing that I am asking you." And the woman said to him, "Speak, my lord the king."

**14:19** And the king said, "Is the hand of Joab with you in all these things?" The woman answered and said, "By the welfare of your soul, my lord the king, it is neither to the left nor to the right of all these things that my lord the king has spoken; for your servant Joab, he himself commanded me, and he himself put all these words into the mouth of your maidservant.

**14:20** In order that I might change the form of this speech, your servant Joab commanded this; but you, my lord the king, are wise, just as an angel of God has wisdom, so that you understand everything on the earth."

**14:21** And the king said to Joab, "Look, being appeased, I have carried out your word; go therefore, and call back the young man Absalom."

**14:22** And falling on his face to the ground, Joab bowed in homage and blessed the king; and Joab said, "Today your servant has understood that I have found favor in your eyes, my lord the king, for you have carried out the word of your servant."

**14:23** Therefore Joab arose and went away to Geshur, and brought Absalom to Jerusalem.

**14:24** But the king said, "Let him return to his own house, and let him not see my face." So Absalom returned to his own house, and he did not see the king's face.

**14:25** Moreover, there was no man in all Israel handsome and exceedingly graceful like Absalom; from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head there was not any blemish in him.

**14:26** And when he cut his hair (for it was cut once a year, because his thick hair weighed him down), he weighed the hair of his head at two hundred shekels by the public weight.

**14:27** Now three sons were born to Absalom, and one daughter named Tamar, of elegant form.

**14:28** And Absalom remained in Jerusalem for two years, and he did not see the king's face.

**14:29** Therefore he sent for Joab, so that he might send him to the king, but he refused to come to him. And when he had sent a second time, and he had refused to come to him,

**14:30** he said to his servants, "You know Joab's field next to my field, which has a harvest of barley; go therefore and set it on fire." So Absalom's servants set the crop on fire. And Joab's servants, coming with their clothes torn, said, "Absalom's servants have set part of the field on fire."

**14:31** And Joab arose and came to Absalom at his house, and said, "Why have your servants set my crop on fire?"

**14:32** And Absalom answered Joab, "I sent to you pleading that you would come to me, so that I might send you to the king and you might say to him, 'Why have I come from Geshur? It was better for me to be there.' Therefore I plead that I may see the king's face; and if he remembers my iniquity, let him kill me."

**14:33** So Joab went in to the king and reported everything to him; and Absalom was summoned, and he entered before the king and bowed in homage upon the face of the ground before him; and the king kissed Absalom.

## Translator Notes

- **14:11** — Vulgate reads plural 'so that the kinsmen of blood may not multiply to take vengeance' (ut non multiplicentur proximi sanguinis ad ulciscendum), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to the singular avenger of blood increasing destruction.
- **14:14** — Vulgate distinctively reads 'nor does God wish a soul to perish, but He reconsiders, taking thought' (nec vult Deus perire animam, sed retractat cogitans), where the Hebrew reads 'God does not take away life, but devises plans.'
- **14:15** — Vulgate reads 'in the presence of the people' (praesente populo), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'because the people made me afraid.'
- **14:17** — Vulgate follows the Septuagint in reading 'like a sacrifice' (sicut sacrificium) rather than the Hebrew 'for rest/comfort,' and distinctively renders the next clause 'so that he is moved neither by blessing nor by curse' (ut nec benedictione, nec maledictione moveatur) where the Hebrew reads 'to discern good and evil.'
- **14:30** — The final sentence reporting that Joab's servants came with torn clothes to announce the burning of the field is present in the Vulgate and Septuagint, though absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
