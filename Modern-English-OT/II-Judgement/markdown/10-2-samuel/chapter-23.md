## Text

**23:1** Now these are the final words of David. David the son of Jesse said; the man to whom it was appointed concerning the anointed of the God of Jacob, the eminent psalmist of Israel, said:

**23:2** "The Spirit of the Lord has spoken through me, and His word through my tongue.

**23:3** The God of Israel said to me, the Strong One of Israel spoke: 'A ruler of men, a just ruler in the fear of God,

**23:4** is like the light of dawn glowing when the sun rises in a morning without clouds, and like grass sprouting from the earth by rains.'

**23:5** Nor is my house so great before God that He should enter into an eternal covenant with me, firm in all things and secured. For it is all my salvation and every desire, nor is there anything from it that does not sprout.

**23:6** But transgressors will all be torn out like thorns, which are not picked up with hands.

**23:7** And if anyone wishes to touch them, he will be armed with iron and a lance shaft; and kindled with fire, they will be burned up to nothing."

**23:8** These are the names of the mighty men of David: the one sitting in the chair, the wisest chief among the three; he is like the most tender little worm of wood, who killed eight hundred in a single assault.

**23:9** After him was Eleazar the son of his uncle, an Ahohite, among the three mighty men who were with David when they taunted the Philistines, and they were gathered there for battle.

**23:10** And when the men of Israel had gone up, he himself stood and struck down the Philistines until his hand gave out and grew stiff with the sword; and the Lord worked a great deliverance on that day; and the people who had fled returned to strip the spoils of those who were killed.

**23:11** And after him was Shammah the son of Agee from Harar. And the Philistines were gathered at a garrison, for there was a field there full of lentils. And when the people had fled from before the Philistines,

**23:12** he stood in the middle of the field and defended it, and struck down the Philistines; and the Lord worked a great deliverance.

**23:13** Moreover, three who were chiefs among the thirty had also gone down earlier and had come at harvest time to David in the cave of Adullam; and the camp of the Philistines was pitched in the Valley of Giants.

**23:14** And David was in the stronghold, while the garrison of the Philistines was then in Bethlehem.

**23:15** Therefore David had a longing and said, "Oh, if only someone would give me a drink of water from the cistern that is in Bethlehem beside the gate!"

**23:16** So the three mighty men broke through the camp of the Philistines, and drew water from the cistern of Bethlehem that was beside the gate, and brought it to David; but he would not drink, and instead poured it out as a libation to the Lord,

**23:17** saying, "May the Lord be merciful to me, that I not do this! Shall I drink the blood of these men who went out, and the peril of their lives?" Therefore he would not drink. The three strongest men did these things.

**23:18** Abishai also, the brother of Joab, the son of Zeruiah, was chief of the three. He is the one who raised his spear against three hundred, whom he killed; he was renowned among the three,

**23:19** and more honored among the three, and he was their chief, yet he had not attained to the first three.

**23:20** And Benaiah the son of Jehoiada, a most valiant man of great deeds, from Kabzeel: he himself struck down two lions of Moab, and he went down and struck down a lion in the middle of a cistern in the days of snow.

**23:21** He also killed an Egyptian man, a man who was a spectacle to see, having a spear in his hand; and so, when he had gone down to him with a staff, he wrenched the spear by force from the hand of the Egyptian and killed him with his own spear.

**23:22** Benaiah the son of Jehoiada did these things.

**23:23** And he too was renowned among the three strong men who were more honored among the thirty; nevertheless, he had not attained to the three. And David made him his confidential counselor, in charge of secret affairs.

**23:24** Asahel the brother of Joab was among the thirty; Elhanan the son of his uncle, from Bethlehem,

**23:25** Shammah from Harod, Elika from Harod,

**23:26** Helez from Palti, Ira the son of Ikkesh from Tekoa,

**23:27** Abiezer from Anathoth, Mebunnai from Hushah,

**23:28** Zalmon the Ahohite, Maharai the Netophathite,

**23:29** Heled the son of Baanah, also a Netophathite, Ittai the son of Ribai from Gibeah of the sons of Benjamin,

**23:30** Benaiah the Pirathonite, Hiddai from the brook of Gaash,

**23:31** Abialbon the Arbathite, Azmaveth from Barhum,

**23:32** Eliahba from Shaalbon; the sons of Jashen, Jonathan,

**23:33** Shammah from Harar, Ahiam the son of Sharar the Hararite,

**23:34** Eliphelet the son of Ahasbai, the son of the Maacathite, Eliam the son of Ahithophel the Gilonite,

**23:35** Hezrai from Carmel, Paarai from Arab,

**23:36** Igal the son of Nathan from Zobah, Bani from Gad,

**23:37** Zelek from Ammon, Naharai the Beerothite, armor-bearer of Joab the son of Zeruiah,

**23:38** Ira the Ithrite, Gareb also an Ithrite,

**23:39** Uriah the Hittite: thirty-seven in all.

## Translator Notes

- **23:1** — The Vulgate reads cui constitutum est de christo Dei Jacob ("to whom it was appointed concerning the anointed of the God of Jacob"), giving a messianic nuance where the Hebrew reads "the man who was raised on high, the anointed of the God of Jacob."
- **23:5** — The Vulgate expresses David's humility ("Nor is my house so great before God that He should enter into an eternal covenant with me"), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's rhetorical question ("Is not my house so with God? For he has made with me an everlasting covenant").
- **23:8** — Rather than transliterating the Hebrew proper names (Josheb-basshebeth the Tahchemonite and Adino the Eznite), the Vulgate translates them etymologically as "the one sitting in the chair, the wisest chief" and "like the most tender little worm of wood."
- **23:9** — The Vulgate translates Hebrew ben-dodo literally as "the son of his uncle" (filius patrui ejus) rather than treating Dodo as a personal name (so also in 23:24).
- **23:23** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew term for David's bodyguard (mishma'at, derived from the root "to hear") as auricularium, a secreto, denoting a confidential counselor in charge of secret affairs.
