## Text

**3:1** So there arose a long conflict between the house of Saul and the house of David: David was advancing and ever stronger than himself, while the house of Saul was diminishing daily.

**3:2** And sons were born to David in Hebron, and his firstborn was Amnon, by Ahinoam the Jezreelitess.

**3:3** And after him was Chileab, by Abigail the wife of Nabal of Carmel; and the third was Absalom, the son of Maacah the daughter of Talmai, king of Geshur.

**3:4** And the fourth was Adonijah, the son of Haggith; and the fifth was Shephatiah, the son of Abital.

**3:5** And the sixth as well was Ithream, by Eglah the wife of David. These were born to David in Hebron.

**3:6** So while there was war between the house of Saul and the house of David, Abner the son of Ner was ruling the house of Saul.

**3:7** Now Saul had had a concubine named Rizpah, the daughter of Aiah. And Ish-bosheth said to Abner:

**3:8** "Why have you gone in to my father's concubine?" And he, exceedingly angry because of the words of Ish-bosheth, said, "Am I a dog's head against Judah today, I who have shown mercy toward the house of Saul your father, and toward his brothers and his relatives, and have not handed you over into the hands of David, and yet you have searched in me for something to fault on account of a woman today?

**3:9** May God do these things to Abner, and add these things to him, unless, just as the Lord swore to David, so I do with him,

**3:10** that the kingdom may be transferred from the house of Saul, and the throne of David may be raised up over Israel and over Judah, from Dan even to Beersheba."

**3:11** And he could not answer him anything, because he feared him.

**3:12** So Abner sent messengers to David on his own behalf, saying, "Whose is the land?" and to say, "Make friendship with me, and my hand will be with you, and I will bring all Israel back to you."

**3:13** And he said, "Very well; I will make friendship with you, but one thing I ask of you, saying, 'You will not see my face before you bring Michal the daughter of Saul; and thus you will come and see me.'"

**3:14** Now David sent messengers to Ish-bosheth the son of Saul, saying, "Give back my wife Michal, whom I betrothed to myself for a hundred foreskins of the Philistines."

**3:15** So Ish-bosheth sent and took her from her husband, Paltiel the son of Laish.

**3:16** And her husband kept following her, weeping as far as Bahurim; and Abner said to him, "Go, and return." And he returned.

**3:17** Abner also brought word to the elders of Israel, saying, "Both yesterday and the day before yesterday you were seeking David to reign over you.

**3:18** Now therefore do it, because the Lord has spoken to David, saying, 'By the hand of My servant David I will save My people Israel from the hand of the Philistines and of all their enemies.'"

**3:19** Now Abner also spoke to Benjamin. And he went to speak to David in Hebron about all the things that had pleased Israel and all Benjamin.

**3:20** And he came to David in Hebron with twenty men; and David made a feast for Abner and for his men who had come with him.

**3:21** And Abner said to David, "I will arise, that I may gather all Israel to you, my lord the king, and enter into a covenant with you, and that you may rule over all, just as your soul desires." So when David had escorted Abner off, and he had gone in peace,

**3:22** immediately the servants of David and Joab arrived, having struck down the raiders, with exceedingly great plunder; but Abner was not with David in Hebron, because he had already sent him away, and he had departed in peace.

**3:23** And Joab and the whole army that was with him arrived afterward; so it was reported to Joab by those telling him, "Abner the son of Ner came to the king, and he sent him away, and he went off in peace."

**3:24** Then Joab went in to the king and said, "What have you done? Look, Abner came to you; why did you send him away, and he went away and withdrew?

**3:25** Do you not know Abner the son of Ner, that he came to you for this purpose, to deceive you, and to know your going out and your coming in, and to learn everything that you do?"

**3:26** So when Joab had gone out from David, he sent messengers after Abner and brought him back from the cistern of Sirah, with David unaware.

**3:27** And when Abner had returned to Hebron, Joab drew him aside to the middle of the gate to speak to him, in deceit; and he struck him there in the groin, and he died, in vengeance for the blood of Asahel his brother.

**3:28** And when David heard of this, after the deed was already done, he said, "I and my kingdom are clean before the Lord forever from the blood of Abner the son of Ner;

**3:29** and may it come upon the head of Joab, and upon all his father's house; and may there not be lacking from the house of Joab one who suffers a discharge of seed, and a leper, and one who holds a spindle, and one who falls by the sword, and one who is in need of bread."

**3:30** Therefore Joab and Abishai his brother killed Abner, because he had killed Asahel their brother in Gibeon in battle.

**3:31** Then David said to Joab and to all the people who were with him, "Tear your garments, and bind yourselves with sackcloth, and mourn before the funeral of Abner." And King David followed the bier.

**3:32** And when they had buried Abner in Hebron, King David lifted up his voice and wept over the tomb of Abner; and all the people wept as well.

**3:33** And the king, mourning and lamenting Abner, said, "By no means did Abner die as cowards are accustomed to die.

**3:34** Your hands were not bound, and your feet were not weighed down with fetters; but just as men are accustomed to fall before the sons of iniquity, so you fell down." And all the people redoubled their weeping over him.

**3:35** And when the entire crowd came to take food with David, while the day was still bright, David swore, saying, "May God do these things to me, and add these things, if before the setting of the sun I taste bread or anything else."

**3:36** And all the people heard, and all the things that the king did in the sight of all the people pleased them.

**3:37** And all the common people and all Israel knew on that day that it had not been brought about by the king that Abner the son of Ner was killed.

**3:38** The king also said to his servants, "Do you not know that a prince and a very great man has fallen today in Israel?

**3:39** And I am still delicate, and anointed king; yet these men, the sons of Zeruiah, are hard for me. May the Lord repay the one who does evil according to his malice."

## Translator Notes

- **3:8** — The Vulgate reads adversum Judam ("against Judah"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "that belongs to Judah."
- **3:21** — The Vulgate has the first-person singular verb ineam ("that I may enter into a covenant with you"), whereas the Hebrew has the third-person plural ("that they may make a covenant with you").
- **3:22** — The Vulgate reads caesis latronibus ("having struck down the raiders"), whereas the Hebrew reads simply "from a raid."
- **3:29** — Vulgate tenens fusum ("one who holds a spindle") translates a debated Hebrew phrase that may mean "leaning on a crutch" or "staff," suggesting someone unfit for warfare and reduced to spinning.
- **3:33** — The Vulgate renders David's lament as a forceful statement (Nequaquam ... mortuus est, "By no means did Abner die") using ignavi ("cowards"), whereas the Hebrew poses a rhetorical question ("Should Abner die as a fool dies?").
