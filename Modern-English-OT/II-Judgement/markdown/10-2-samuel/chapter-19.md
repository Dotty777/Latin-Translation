## Text

**19:1** So the king, deeply grieved, went up to the upper room of the gate and wept. And as he walked, he kept saying this: "My son Absalom, Absalom my son! Who would grant me that I might die in your place, Absalom my son, my son Absalom?"

**19:2** Now it was reported to Joab that the king was weeping and mourning for his son.

**19:3** And the victory was turned into mourning on that day for all the people, for the people heard it said on that day, "The king is grieving over his son."

**19:4** And on that day the people shrank from entering the city, just as a people routed and fleeing from battle is accustomed to shrink away.

**19:5** Meanwhile, the king covered his head and kept crying out with a loud voice, "My son Absalom, Absalom my son, my son!"

**19:6** Then Joab went into the house to the king and said, "Today you have put to shame the faces of all your servants, who saved your life, and the lives of your sons and your daughters, and the lives of your wives, and the lives of your concubines.

**19:7** You love those who hate you, and you hate those who love you. And today you have shown that you do not care about your commanders and your servants; and now I truly know that if Absalom were alive and all of us had died, then it would please you.

**19:8** Now therefore, rise, go out, and speak to your servants to satisfy them; for I swear to you by the Lord that if you do not go out, not even one will remain with you this night, and this will be worse for you than all the evils that have come upon you from your youth until the present."

**19:9** So the king rose and sat in the gate, and it was reported to all the people that the king was sitting in the gate. And the entire multitude came before the king; but Israel fled to their tents.

**19:10** All the people also were disputing throughout all the tribes of Israel, saying, "The king delivered us from the hand of our enemies; he himself saved us from the hand of the Philistines, and now he has fled from the land because of Absalom.

**19:11** But Absalom, whom we anointed over us, has died in battle. How long will you keep silent and not bring the king back?"

**19:12** Now King David sent to Zadok and Abiathar the priests, saying, "Speak to the elders of Judah, saying, 'Why are you coming last to bring the king back to his house?'" (Now the word of all Israel had reached the king in his house.)

**19:13** "'You are my brothers; you are my bone and my flesh. Why are you the last to bring the king back?'

**19:14** And say to Amasa, 'Are you not my bone and my flesh? May God do this to me, and add this as well, if you are not commander of the army before me at all times in place of Joab.'"

**19:15** And he inclined the heart of all the men of Judah as of one man, and they sent to the king, saying, "Return, you and all your servants."

**19:16** So the king returned and came as far as the Jordan; and all Judah came as far as Gilgal to meet the king and to bring him across the Jordan.

**19:17** Now Shimei the son of Gera, the son of Jemini, from Bahurim, hurried and went down with the men of Judah to meet King David,

**19:18** with a thousand men from Benjamin, and Ziba, the attendant of the house of Saul; and his fifteen sons and twenty servants were with him. And rushing into the Jordan, before the king

**19:19** they crossed the fords to bring the king's household across and to do according to his command. Now Shimei the son of Gera, having fallen prostrate before the king when he had already crossed the Jordan,

**19:20** said to him, "Do not count iniquity against me, my lord, and do not remember the wrongs of your servant on the day you went out from Jerusalem, my lord the king, and do not take it to heart, king.

**19:21** For I, your servant, acknowledge my sin; and for this reason I have come today as the first of all the house of Joseph, and I have come down to meet my lord the king."

**19:22** Then Abishai the son of Zeruiah answered and said, "Should Shimei not be put to death for these words, because he cursed the Lord's anointed?"

**19:23** And David said, "What have I to do with you, sons of Zeruiah? Why are you becoming an adversary to me today? Should a man be put to death in Israel today? Or do I not know that today I have been made king over Israel?"

**19:24** And the king said to Shimei, "You will not die." And he swore to him.

**19:25** Mephibosheth the son of Saul also came down to meet the king, with unwashed feet and an untrimmed beard; and he had not washed his clothes from the day the king had gone out until the day of his return in peace.

**19:26** And when he met the king in Jerusalem, the king said to him, "Why did you not come with me, Mephibosheth?"

**19:27** And he answered and said, "My lord the king, my servant despised me; and I, your servant, told him to saddle a donkey for me so that I might mount it and go with the king, for I, your servant, am lame.

**19:28** Moreover, he has also accused me, your servant, to you, my lord the king. But you, my lord the king, are like an angel of God; do what is pleasing to you.

**19:29** For my father's house was nothing except liable to death before my lord the king, yet you placed me, your servant, among the guests at your table. What just complaint, then, do I have, or what more can I cry out to the king?"

**19:30** Then the king said to him, "Why do you speak any more? What I have spoken is settled: you and Ziba, divide the possessions."

**19:31** And Mephibosheth answered the king, "Let him even take it all, since my lord the king has returned peacefully to his house."

**19:32** Barzillai the Gileadite also, coming down from Rogelim, brought the king across the Jordan, ready even to escort him beyond the river.

**19:33** Now Barzillai the Gileadite was very old, that is, eighty years old; and he himself had provided food for the king while he was staying in the camp, for he was an exceedingly wealthy man.

**19:34** So the king said to Barzillai, "Come with me, so that you may rest securely with me in Jerusalem."

**19:35** And Barzillai said to the king, "How many are the days of the years of my life, that I should go up with the king to Jerusalem?

**19:36** I am eighty years old today. Are my senses strong enough to discern sweet or bitter? Or can food and drink delight your servant? Or can I any longer hear the voice of male and female singers? Why should your servant be a burden to my lord the king?

**19:37** I, your servant, will go a little way from the Jordan with you; I do not need this repayment,

**19:38** but I beg that I, your servant, may return and die in my city, and be buried beside the tomb of my father and my mother. But there is your servant Chimham; let him go with you, my lord the king, and do for him whatever seems good to you."

**19:39** So the king said to him, "Let Chimham cross over with me, and I will do for him whatever pleases you; and everything you ask of me, you will obtain."

**19:40** And when all the people and the king had crossed the Jordan, the king kissed Barzillai and blessed him; and he returned to his place.

**19:41** Then the king passed on to Gilgal, and Chimham with him. Now all the people of Judah had brought the king across, and only half of the people of Israel had been present.

**19:42** Therefore all the men of Israel, coming together to the king, said to him, "Why have our brothers, the men of Judah, stolen you away and brought the king and his household across the Jordan, and all the men of David with him?"

**19:43** And every man of Judah answered the men of Israel, "Because the king is closer to me. Why are you angry over this matter? Have we eaten anything from the king, or have gifts been given to us?"

**19:44** And the man of Israel answered the men of Judah and said, "I am greater by ten parts with the king, and David belongs more to me than to you. Why have you done me wrong, and why was it not reported to me first, so that I might bring back my king?" But the men of Judah answered the men of Israel more harshly.

## Translator Notes

- **19:1** — Vulgate 19:1 corresponds to 18:33 in the Masoretic Text, shifting all subsequent verse numbers in this chapter ahead by one.
- **19:4** — Vulgate declinavit ... ingredi ("shrank from entering") and versus ("routed") differ from the Masoretic Text, which describes the people stealing secretly into the city like troops ashamed after fleeing battle.
- **19:17** — Latin filii Jemini ("son of Jemini") renders the Hebrew gentilic ben-hayemini ("the Benjaminite") literally as a patronymic.
- **19:23** — Latin in satan uses the untranslated Hebrew loanword meaning "adversary" rather than the proper name of the devil.
- **19:27** — Vulgate contempsit me ("despised me") differs from the Masoretic Text reading "deceived me."
- **19:33** — Vulgate in castris ("in the camp") translates the Hebrew place-name Mahanaim literally.
