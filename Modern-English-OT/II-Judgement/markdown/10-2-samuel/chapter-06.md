## Text

**6:1** Now David again gathered all the chosen men from Israel, thirty thousand.

**6:2** And David arose and went, and all the people who were with him of the men of Judah, to bring the ark of God, over which the name of the Lord of hosts is invoked, who sits upon the cherubim over it.

**6:3** And they placed the ark of God upon a new cart, and brought it out of the house of Abinadab, who was in Gibeah; and Uzzah and Ahio, the sons of Abinadab, were driving the new cart.

**6:4** And when they had brought it out of the house of Abinadab, who was in Gibeah, Ahio, guarding the ark of God, was going ahead of the ark.

**6:5** Now David and all Israel were celebrating before the Lord with all crafted wooden instruments, and with harps, lyres, tambourines, sistrums, and cymbals.

**6:6** But after they came to the threshing floor of Nacon, Uzzah stretched out his hand to the ark of God and took hold of it, because the oxen were kicking and tilted it.

**6:7** And the Lord was angry with indignation against Uzzah, and struck him for his rashness; and he died there beside the ark of God.

**6:8** And David was grieved because the Lord had struck Uzzah; and the name of that place has been called Striking of Uzzah until this day.

**6:9** And David was afraid of the Lord on that day, saying, "How will the ark of the Lord come in to me?"

**6:10** And he was unwilling to turn the ark of the Lord aside to himself into the city of David, but he turned it aside into the house of Obed-edom the Gittite.

**6:11** And the ark of the Lord dwelt in the house of Obed-edom the Gittite for three months, and the Lord blessed Obed-edom and all his household.

**6:12** And it was reported to King David that the Lord had blessed Obed-edom and all that belonged to him because of the ark of God. So David went and brought the ark of God from the house of Obed-edom into the city of David with joy; and there were with David seven choirs, and a sacrificial calf.

**6:13** And when those who were carrying the ark of the Lord had advanced six paces, he sacrificed an ox and a ram;

**6:14** and David was dancing with all his strength before the Lord; moreover, David was girded with a linen ephod.

**6:15** And David and all the house of Israel were bringing the ark of the covenant of the Lord with jubilation and with the blast of the trumpet.

**6:16** And when the ark of the Lord had entered into the city of David, Michal the daughter of Saul, looking out through the window, saw King David leaping and dancing before the Lord; and she despised him in her heart.

**6:17** And they brought in the ark of the Lord, and placed it in its place in the middle of the tent that David had pitched for it; and David offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before the Lord.

**6:18** And when he had finished offering the burnt offerings and the peace offerings, he blessed the people in the name of the Lord of hosts.

**6:19** And he distributed to the entire multitude of Israel, both man and woman, to each one, one cake of bread, and one piece of roasted beef, and fine flour fried in oil; and all the people departed, each one to his own house.

**6:20** And David returned to bless his household; and Michal the daughter of Saul came out to meet David and said, "How glorious the king of Israel was today, uncovering himself before the maidservants of his servants, and he exposed himself as if one of the buffoons were to expose himself!"

**6:21** And David said to Michal, "Before the Lord, who chose me rather than your father and rather than all his house, and commanded me to be leader over the people of the Lord in Israel,

**6:22** I will both celebrate and become even more lowly than I have been made, and I will be humble in my own eyes; and with the maidservants of whom you spoke, I will appear more glorious."

**6:23** Therefore no son was born to Michal the daughter of Saul until the day of her death.

## Translator Notes

- **6:2** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew Baale-judah as a descriptive phrase, "of the men of Judah" (de viris Juda), rather than a place name.
- **6:3** — The Vulgate treats in Gabaa ("in Gibeah") as a proper name modifying Abinadab ("who was in Gibeah," also in 6:4), where the Hebrew may be read "on the hill"; in 6:4 the Vulgate also specifies that Ahio was "guarding the ark of God" (custodiens arcam Dei).
- **6:8** — The Vulgate reads "was grieved" (contristatus est) where the Hebrew reads "was angry," and translates the place name Perez-uzzah literally into Latin as Percussio Ozae ("Striking of Uzzah").
- **6:12** — The final clause ("and there were with David seven choirs, and a sacrificial calf") is a Vulgate reading shared with the Septuagint tradition and absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text; similarly in 6:13 the Vulgate reads "a ram" (arietem) rather than "a fatling," and in 6:15 "the ark of the covenant of the Lord" (arcam testamenti Domini).
- **6:19** — The Vulgate renders two rare Hebrew food terms as "one piece of roasted beef" (assaturam bubulae carnis unam) and "fine flour fried in oil" (similam frixam oleo), where translations from the Hebrew often read a portion of meat or date cake and a raisin cake.
