## Text

**2:1** Therefore, after these things, David consulted the Lord, saying, "Should I go up into one of the cities of Judah?" And the Lord said to him, "Go up." And David said, "Where should I go up?" And He answered him, "To Hebron."

**2:2** So David went up, and his two wives, Ahinoam the Jezreelite and Abigail the wife of Nabal of Carmel;

**2:3** and David also brought the men who were with him, each one with his household; and they lived in the towns of Hebron.

**2:4** And the men of Judah came, and there they anointed David to reign over the house of Judah. And it was reported to David that the men of Jabesh-gilead had buried Saul.

**2:5** So David sent messengers to the men of Jabesh-gilead and said to them, "May you be blessed by the Lord, since you have shown this mercy to your lord Saul and have buried him.

**2:6** And now the Lord will indeed repay you with mercy and truth; and I also will return favor, because you have done this thing.

**2:7** Let your hands be strengthened, and be sons of valor; for although your lord Saul is dead, yet the house of Judah has anointed me as king for themselves."

**2:8** Now Abner son of Ner, commander of Saul's army, took Ish-bosheth son of Saul and led him around through the camp,

**2:9** and appointed him king over Gilead, and over Geshuri, and over Jezreel, and over Ephraim, and over Benjamin, and over all Israel.

**2:10** Ish-bosheth son of Saul was forty years old when he began to reign over Israel, and he reigned two years; but the house of Judah alone followed David.

**2:11** And the number of the days that David remained, ruling in Hebron over the house of Judah, was seven years and six months.

**2:12** And Abner son of Ner and the young men of Ish-bosheth son of Saul went out from the camp to Gibeon.

**2:13** Meanwhile Joab son of Zeruiah and the young men of David went out and met them beside the pool of Gibeon. And when they had come together, they sat down opposite one another, these on one side of the pool and those on the other.

**2:14** And Abner said to Joab, "Let the young men rise and play before us." And Joab answered, "Let them rise."

**2:15** So they rose and crossed over by number: twelve from Benjamin, on the side of Ish-bosheth son of Saul, and twelve from the young men of David.

**2:16** And each one, seizing the head of his counterpart, drove his sword into the side of his opponent, and they fell together; and the name of that place was called Field of the Strong, in Gibeon.

**2:17** And a very fierce battle arose on that day, and Abner and the men of Israel were put to flight by the young men of David.

**2:18** Now the three sons of Zeruiah were there: Joab, Abishai, and Asahel; and Asahel was a very swift runner, like one of the roe deer that live in the woods.

**2:19** And Asahel pursued Abner, and he did not turn aside to the right or to the left so as to stop pursuing Abner.

**2:20** So Abner looked behind his back and said, "Are you Asahel?" And he answered, "I am."

**2:21** And Abner said to him, "Go to the right or to the left, and seize one of the young men, and take his spoils for yourself." But Asahel refused to stop pressing him.

**2:22** And again Abner spoke to Asahel, "Withdraw, do not follow me, so that I am not forced to pierce you to the ground, and then I will not be able to lift my face to your brother Joab."

**2:23** But he scorned to listen and refused to turn aside; so Abner struck him in the groin with the butt end of his spear and pierced him through, and he died in that very place. And all who passed through that place where Asahel had fallen and died came to a halt.

**2:24** Now while Joab and Abishai were pursuing the fleeing Abner, the sun went down; and they came as far as the hill of the aqueduct, which is opposite the valley of the wilderness road in Gibeon.

**2:25** And the sons of Benjamin gathered together to Abner, and massed together into a single wedge, they stood on the summit of one hill.

**2:26** And Abner called out to Joab and said, "Will your blade rage on to utter destruction? Or do you not know that desperation is dangerous? How long before you tell the people to stop pursuing their brothers?"

**2:27** And Joab said, "As the Lord lives, if you had spoken, the people would have withdrawn in the morning from pursuing their brother."

**2:28** So Joab sounded the trumpet, and the whole army halted, and they did not pursue Israel any further, nor did they enter into combat.

**2:29** Now Abner and his men went away through the plains all that night; and they crossed the Jordan, and having traversed all of Beth-horon, they came to the camp.

**2:30** Meanwhile Joab returned, having left off from Abner, and gathered all the people; and nineteen men were missing from David's young men, apart from Asahel.

**2:31** But the servants of David struck down three hundred and sixty from Benjamin and from the men who were with Abner, who also died.

**2:32** And they took up Asahel and buried him in his father's tomb in Bethlehem; and Joab and the men who were with him walked all night, and at the very dawn they arrived in Hebron.

## Translator Notes

- **2:8** — The Vulgate translates the Hebrew place name Mahanaim ("Two Camps") as the common noun castra ("the camp"; likewise in 2:12 and 2:29) and reads circumduxit ("led him around") rather than "brought him over."
- **2:9** — Latin Gessuri ("Geshuri," i.e., Geshurites) differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "the Ashurites."
- **2:16** — Latin Ager robustorum ("Field of the Strong") translates the Hebrew place name Helkath-hazzurim (meaning "Field of Sword-Edges" or "Field of Flints").
- **2:24** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew place names Ammah and Giah as common nouns: collem aquæductus ("hill of the aqueduct") and vallis ("valley").
- **2:26** — Latin an ignoras quod periculosa sit desperatio? ("Or do you not know that desperation is dangerous?") is a distinctly Vulgate rendering where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "Do you not know that it will be bitter in the end?"
- **2:27** — The Clementine Vulgate reads si locutus fuisses ("if you had spoken") without the negative found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("if you had not spoken"), and uses Dominus ("the Lord") where the Hebrew has "God."
- **2:29** — The Vulgate gives the proper name Beth-horon where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads Bithron (a district or ravine east of the Jordan).
