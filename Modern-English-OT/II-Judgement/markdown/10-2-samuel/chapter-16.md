## Text

**16:1** And when David had passed a little beyond the summit of the mountain, Ziba, the servant of Mephibosheth, appeared to meet him with two donkeys that were loaded with two hundred loaves of bread, one hundred bundles of raisins, one hundred cakes of dried figs, and a skin of wine.

**16:2** And the king said to Ziba, "What do these mean?" And Ziba answered, "The donkeys are for the king's household to ride on, the loaves of bread and the fig cakes are for your servants to eat, and the wine is so that anyone who grows faint in the wilderness may drink."

**16:3** And the king said, "Where is the son of your master?" And Ziba answered the king, "He has remained in Jerusalem, saying, 'Today the house of Israel will restore to me the kingdom of my father.'"

**16:4** And the king said to Ziba, "Let all that belonged to Mephibosheth be yours." And Ziba said, "I beg that I may find favor before you, my lord the king."

**16:5** So King David came as far as Bahurim, and now a man from the clan of the house of Saul was coming out from there, named Shimei, the son of Gera; he kept moving forward as he came out and kept cursing,

**16:6** and he kept throwing stones at David and at all the servants of King David, while all the people and all the warriors were marching on the right and on the left side of the king.

**16:7** Now Shimei was speaking in this way as he cursed the king: "Get out, get out, man of bloodshed and man of Belial!

**16:8** The Lord has returned upon you all the blood of the house of Saul, because you seized the kingdom in his place, and the Lord has given the kingdom into the hand of Absalom your son; and look, your own evils are pressing upon you, because you are a man of bloodshed."

**16:9** Then Abishai, the son of Zeruiah, said to the king, "Why is this dead dog cursing my lord the king? I will go and cut off his head."

**16:10** And the king said, "What is there between me and you, sons of Zeruiah? Leave him alone so that he may curse, for the Lord commanded him to curse David; and who is there who would dare to say why he has done so?"

**16:11** And the king said to Abishai and to all his servants, "Look, my son, who came forth from my body, is seeking my life; how much more now a son of Jemini? Leave him alone so that he may curse according to the command of the Lord,

**16:12** if perhaps the Lord may look upon my affliction, and the Lord may return good to me for this cursing today."

**16:13** Therefore David and his companions were walking along the road with him. Meanwhile Shimei was walking along the ridge of the mountain on the side opposite him, cursing, throwing stones at him, and scattering dirt.

**16:14** So the king and all the people with him arrived weary, and they were refreshed there.

**16:15** Now Absalom and all his people entered Jerusalem, and Ahithophel also was with him.

**16:16** Now when Hushai the Archite, the friend of David, came to Absalom, he spoke to him: "Hail, king! Hail, king!"

**16:17** Absalom said to him, "Is this your kindness to your friend? Why did you not go with your friend?"

**16:18** And Hushai answered Absalom, "Not at all, for I will belong to the one whom the Lord, and all this people, and all Israel have chosen, and with him I will remain.

**16:19** And to add this as well, whom am I going to serve? Is it not the king's son? Just as I obeyed your father, so I will obey you also."

**16:20** Then Absalom said to Ahithophel, "Take counsel on what we ought to do."

**16:21** And Ahithophel said to Absalom, "Go in to your father's concubines, whom he left behind to guard the house, so that when all Israel hears that you have defiled your father, their hands may be strengthened with you."

**16:22** So they pitched a tent for Absalom on the rooftop, and he went in to his father's concubines in the sight of all Israel.

**16:23** Now the counsel of Ahithophel that he gave in those days was as if someone consulted God; such was all the counsel of Ahithophel, both when he was with David and when he was with Absalom.

## Translator Notes

- **16:1** — The Vulgate describes the donkeys as "loaded" (onerati erant) rather than "saddled" as in the Masoretic Text, and renders the Hebrew "summer fruit" specifically as "cakes of dried figs" (massis palatharum).
- **16:10** — Latin uses a third-person singular indirect question ("why he has done so," quare sic fecerit), referring primarily to Shimei's cursing, though it may also be understood as referring to the Lord's command.
- **16:11** — Latin filius Jemini ("a son of Jemini") translates the Hebrew gentilic for a Benjaminite literally; Latin de utero meo ("from my womb/belly") is rendered "from my body" in keeping with its sense here for a father's offspring.
- **16:14** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew term as the adjective "weary" (lassus), whereas some interpreters take the Hebrew form (Ayephim) as a place name.
