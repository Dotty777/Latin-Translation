## Text

**1:1** Now it happened, after Saul had died, that David returned from the slaughter of Amalek and remained in Ziklag two days.

**1:2** On the third day, a man appeared coming from the camp of Saul, with his garment torn and his head sprinkled with dust; and when he came to David, he fell on his face and paid homage.

**1:3** And David said to him, "Where are you coming from?" And he said to him, "I have fled from the camp of Israel."

**1:4** And David said to him, "What is the matter that has happened? Tell me." And he said, "The people have fled from the battle, and many from the people have fallen and died; moreover, both Saul and his son Jonathan have perished."

**1:5** And David said to the young man who was reporting to him, "How do you know that Saul is dead, and his son Jonathan?"

**1:6** And the young man who was reporting to him said, "By chance I came onto Mount Gilboa, and Saul was leaning on his spear; meanwhile, chariots and horsemen were drawing near to him,

**1:7** and turning behind his back and seeing me, he called out. When I had answered him, 'Here I am,'

**1:8** he said to me, 'Who are you?' And I said to him, 'I am an Amalekite.'

**1:9** And he spoke to me, 'Stand over me and kill me, for anguish holds me, and all my life is still in me.'

**1:10** And standing over him, I killed him, for I knew that he could not live after his fall; and I took the diadem that was on his head and the armlet from his arm, and I have brought them here to you, my lord."

**1:11** Then David took hold of his garments and tore them, as did all the men who were with him,

**1:12** and they lamented and wept and fasted until evening over Saul, and over his son Jonathan, and over the people of the Lord, and over the house of Israel, because they had fallen by the sword.

**1:13** And David said to the young man who had reported to him, "Where are you from?" And he answered, "I am the son of a resident alien, an Amalekite."

**1:14** And David said to him, "Why were you not afraid to extend your hand to kill the anointed of the Lord?"

**1:15** And calling one of his young men, David said, "Approach and fall upon him." And he struck him, and he died.

**1:16** And David said to him, "Your blood be on your head, for your mouth has spoken against you, saying, 'I killed the anointed of the Lord.'"

**1:17** Then David lamented with a lament of this kind over Saul and over his son Jonathan

**1:18** (and he commanded that they should teach the sons of Judah the bow, as it is written in the book of the righteous), and he said, "Take thought, Israel, for those who have died, wounded on your high places.

**1:19** The renowned of Israel have been killed upon your mountains; how have the valiant fallen?

**1:20** Do not announce it in Gath, nor announce it at the crossroads of Ashkelon, so that the daughters of the Philistines may not rejoice, and the daughters of the uncircumcised may not exult.

**1:21** Mountains of Gilboa, may neither dew nor rain come upon you, nor may there be fields of first fruits; for there the shield of the valiant was cast aside, the shield of Saul, as though not anointed with oil.

**1:22** From the blood of those killed, from the fat of the valiant, the arrow of Jonathan never turned back, and the sword of Saul did not return empty.

**1:23** Saul and Jonathan, beloved and gracious in their life, were not divided in death either: swifter than eagles, stronger than lions.

**1:24** Daughters of Israel, weep over Saul, who clothed you in scarlet with luxuries, who provided golden ornaments for your attire.

**1:25** How have the valiant fallen in battle? Has Jonathan been killed on your high places?

**1:26** I grieve over you, my brother Jonathan, exceedingly gracious and beloved beyond the love of women. Just as a mother loves her only son, so I loved you.

**1:27** How have the strong fallen, and the weapons of war perished?"

## Translator Notes

- **1:18** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew Sefer ha-Yashar ("Book of Jashar") as libro justorum ("book of the righteous") and includes the additional opening line "Take thought, Israel, for those who have died, wounded on your high places," reflecting the Septuagint tradition alongside the Hebrew-based line in 1:19.
- **1:21** — Latin abjectus est ("was cast aside") differs from the Masoretic Text ("was defiled"), and masculine unctus ("anointed") leaves open whether it refers to Saul or to his oiled shield.
- **1:26** — The final sentence, "Just as a mother loves her only son, so I loved you," is a distinctly Vulgate (and Old Latin) reading not present in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
