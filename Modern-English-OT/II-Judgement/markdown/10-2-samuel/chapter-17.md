## Text

**17:1** Then Ahithophel said to Absalom, "I will choose for myself twelve thousand men, and rising up, I will pursue David tonight.

**17:2** And rushing upon him (since he is weary and his hands are weak), I will strike him; and when all the people who are with him have fled, I will strike the abandoned king.

**17:3** And I will bring back all the people, just as one man usually returns; for it is one man whom you are seeking, and all the people will be at peace."

**17:4** And his speech pleased Absalom and all the elders of Israel.

**17:5** Then Absalom said, "Call Hushai the Archite, and let us hear what he himself also says."

**17:6** And when Hushai had come to Absalom, Absalom said to him, "Ahithophel has spoken this kind of speech; ought we to do it or not? What counsel do you give?"

**17:7** And Hushai said to Absalom, "The counsel that Ahithophel has given this time is not good."

**17:8** And again Hushai added, "You know that your father and the men who are with him are very mighty and bitter in spirit, just as if a she-bear robbed of her cubs were raging in the forest; moreover, your father is a warrior, and he will not stay with the people.

**17:9** Perhaps he is now hiding in pits, or in some place, whichever he chooses; and when anyone at all falls at the beginning, whoever hears will hear of it and say, 'A slaughter has taken place among the people who were following Absalom.'

**17:10** And even the mightiest man, whose heart is like that of a lion, will melt with terror; for all the people of Israel know that your father is mighty, and that all who are with him are strong.

**17:11** Rather, this seems to me to be right counsel. Let all Israel be gathered to you, from Dan as far as Beersheba, countless like the sand of the sea, and you will be in their midst.

**17:12** And we will rush upon him in whatever place he may be found, and we will cover him just as dew usually falls upon the ground; and of the men who are with him, we will not leave even a single one.

**17:13** And if he has entered any city, all Israel will place ropes around that city, and we will drag it into the stream, so that not even a pebble of it will be found."

**17:14** And Absalom and all the men of Israel said, "The counsel of Hushai the Archite is better than the counsel of Ahithophel." Yet by the Lord's will the useful counsel of Ahithophel was thwarted, so that the Lord might bring calamity upon Absalom.

**17:15** And Hushai said to Zadok and Abiathar the priests, "In such and such a way Ahithophel gave counsel to Absalom and the elders of Israel, and I gave such and such counsel.

**17:16** Now therefore, send quickly and report to David, saying, 'Do not stay tonight in the plains of the wilderness, but cross over without delay, so that the king and all the people who are with him may not be swallowed up.'"

**17:17** Now Jonathan and Ahimaaz were standing beside the spring of Rogel; a maidservant went and reported to them. And they set out to carry the message to King David, for they could not be seen or enter the city.

**17:18** However, a certain young man saw them and told Absalom; but they went with hurried step into the house of a certain man in Bahurim who had a well in his courtyard, and they went down into it.

**17:19** Then the woman took and spread a covering over the mouth of the well, as if drying crushed barley; and thus the matter remained hidden.

**17:20** And when the servants of Absalom came into the house, they said to the woman, "Where are Ahimaaz and Jonathan?" And the woman answered them, "They passed on in haste, after tasting a little water." And those who were searching, when they did not find them, returned to Jerusalem.

**17:21** And when they had gone away, the others came up out of the well, and going on, they reported to King David and said, "Rise up and cross the river quickly, because Ahithophel has given counsel of this kind against you."

**17:22** So David rose up, and all the people who were with him, and they crossed the Jordan until daybreak; and not even a single one was left who had not crossed the river.

**17:23** Now Ahithophel, seeing that his counsel had not been carried out, saddled his donkey, and rose up and went away to his house and to his city; and having put his house in order, he died by hanging, and he was buried in the tomb of his father.

**17:24** Now David came into the camp, and Absalom crossed the Jordan, he and all the men of Israel with him.

**17:25** And Absalom appointed Amasa in place of Joab over the army. Now Amasa was the son of a man who was called Ithra of Jezreel, who went in to Abigail, the daughter of Nahash, the sister of Zeruiah, who was the mother of Joab.

**17:26** And Israel encamped with Absalom in the land of Gilead.

**17:27** And when David had come into the camp, Shobi the son of Nahash, from Rabbah of the sons of Ammon, and Machir the son of Ammiel, from Lo-debar, and Barzillai the Gileadite, from Rogelim,

**17:28** brought to him bedding, rugs, and earthenware vessels; wheat, barley, flour, parched grain, beans, lentils, and roasted chickpeas,

**17:29** and honey, butter, sheep, and fatted calves; and they gave them to David and to the people who were with him to eat, for they supposed that the people were exhausted by hunger and thirst in the wilderness.

## Translator Notes

- **17:3** — Distinctively from the Masoretic Text and the Septuagint, the Vulgate renders Ahithophel's comparison as "just as one man usually returns" (quomodo unus homo reverti solet), connecting it to Absalom's search for a single man.
- **17:11** — The Vulgate reads "in their midst" (in medio eorum), reflecting a reading of the Hebrew consonants as qerev ("midst") rather than the Masoretic vocalization qerav ("battle").
- **17:20** — Translating an obscure Hebrew phrase (mikhal ha-mayim, perhaps "the brook of water"), the Vulgate reads "after tasting a little water" (gustata paululum aqua).
- **17:24** — Here and in 17:27, the Vulgate translates the Hebrew place name Mahanaim ("two camps") etymologically as a common noun, "into the camp" (in castra).
- **17:25** — The Vulgate identifies Ithra as being "of Jezreel" (de Jezraëli), whereas the Masoretic Text reads "the Israelite" and 1 Chronicles 2:17 reads "the Ishmaelite."
- **17:29** — The Vulgate renders an obscure Hebrew expression for dairy curds or cheese as "fatted calves" (pingues vitulos), alongside similarly distinct Vulgate renderings in 17:28 such as "rugs" (tapetia) and "roasted chickpeas" (frixum cicer).
