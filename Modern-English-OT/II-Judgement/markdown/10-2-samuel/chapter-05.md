## Text

**5:1** And all the tribes of Israel came to David in Hebron, saying, "Look, we are your bone and your flesh.

**5:2** Moreover, even yesterday and the day before, when Saul was king over us, you were leading out and bringing back Israel; and the Lord said to you, 'You will shepherd My people Israel, and you will be leader over Israel.'"

**5:3** The elders of Israel also came to the king in Hebron, and King David struck a covenant with them in Hebron before the Lord; and they anointed David as king over Israel.

**5:4** David was thirty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned for forty years.

**5:5** In Hebron he reigned over Judah for seven years and six months; and in Jerusalem he reigned for thirty-three years over all Israel and Judah.

**5:6** And the king and all the men who were with him went to Jerusalem, to the Jebusite, the inhabitant of the land; and it was said to David by them, "You will not enter here unless you remove the blind and the lame saying, 'David will not enter here.'"

**5:7** Yet David captured the citadel of Zion; this is the city of David.

**5:8** For David had offered a reward on that day to whoever would strike the Jebusite, and reach the pipes of the rooftops, and remove the blind and the lame who hated the soul of David. Therefore it is said in a proverb, "The blind and the lame will not enter into the temple."

**5:9** And David dwelt in the citadel, and called it the city of David; and he built all around from Millo and inward.

**5:10** And he went forward, advancing and growing greater, and the Lord God of hosts was with him.

**5:11** Hiram king of Tyre also sent messengers to David, and cedar timber, and craftsmen of wood, and craftsmen of stone for walls; and they built a house for David.

**5:12** And David knew that the Lord had established him as king over Israel, and that He had exalted his kingdom over His people Israel.

**5:13** So David took still more concubines and wives from Jerusalem, after he had come from Hebron; and other sons and daughters were also born to David.

**5:14** And these are the names of those who were born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon,

**5:15** Ibhar, Elishua, Nepheg,

**5:16** Japhia, Elishama, Eliada, and Eliphelet.

**5:17** Then the Philistines heard that they had anointed David as king over Israel, and all of them went up to seek David; and when David heard this, he went down to the stronghold.

**5:18** Now the Philistines came and spread out in the valley of Rephaim.

**5:19** And David consulted the Lord, saying, "Shall I go up to the Philistines? And will You give them into my hand?" And the Lord said to David, "Go up, for I will certainly deliver the Philistines into your hand."

**5:20** So David came to Baal-perazim, and struck them there, and said, "The Lord has divided my enemies before me, just as waters are divided." Therefore the name of that place was called Baal-perazim.

**5:21** And they left their carved images there, which David and his men carried away.

**5:22** And the Philistines went up yet again, and spread out in the valley of Rephaim.

**5:23** And David consulted the Lord, "Shall I go up against the Philistines, and will You deliver them into my hands?" He answered, "Do not go up against them, but circle around to their rear, and you will come to them opposite the pear trees.

**5:24** And when you hear the sound of one marching in the tops of the pear trees, then you will enter into battle, for then the Lord will go out before your face to strike the camp of the Philistines."

**5:25** So David did just as the Lord had commanded him, and struck the Philistines from Geba until you come to Gezer.

## Translator Notes

- **5:8** — The Vulgate explicitly supplies "had offered a reward" (proposuerat ... praemium) where the Hebrew sentence is elliptical, renders the difficult Hebrew term tsinnor ("water shaft") as "the pipes of the rooftops" (domatum fistulas), and reads "into the temple" (in templum) where the Hebrew reads "into the house."
- **5:12** — The Vulgate reads "over His people Israel" (super populum suum Israel), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "for the sake of His people Israel."
- **5:20** — The Vulgate translates the verb explaining Baal-perazim as "has divided ... are divided" (divisit ... dividuntur), rather than the Hebrew "has broken through."
- **5:23** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text, the Vulgate includes David's direct question to the Lord ("Shall I go up against the Philistines, and will You deliver them into my hands?"), and renders the Hebrew trees (bekha'im, often "balsam" or "mulberry trees") as "pear trees" (pyrorum, also in 5:24).
