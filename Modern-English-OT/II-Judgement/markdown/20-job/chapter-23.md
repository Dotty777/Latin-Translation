## Text

**23:1** Then Job responded and said:

**23:2** "Even now my speech is in bitterness, and the hand of my affliction is heavier than my groaning.

**23:3** Who would grant me to know and find Him, and to come all the way to His throne?

**23:4** I will lay a case before Him, and I will fill my mouth with reproaches,

**23:5** so that I may know the words He answers me, and understand what He speaks to me.

**23:6** I do not want Him to contend with me in great strength, nor to press me down with the weight of His greatness.

**23:7** Let Him set forth fairness against me, and let my case reach victory.

**23:8** If I go to the east, He does not appear; if to the west, I will not perceive Him.

**23:9** If to the left, what am I to do? I will not lay hold of Him; if I turn to the right, I will not see Him.

**23:10** Yet He Himself knows my way, and He has tested me like gold that passes through fire.

**23:11** My foot has followed His footsteps; His way I have kept, and I have not turned aside from it.

**23:12** From the commands of His lips I have not departed, and in my bosom I have hidden the words of His mouth.

**23:13** For He Himself is alone, and no one can turn aside His thought; and whatever His soul has willed, that He has done.

**23:15** And for that reason I am troubled before His face, and as I consider Him, I am disquieted with fear.

**23:16** God has made my heart weak, and the Almighty has thrown me into turmoil.

**23:17** For I have not perished because of the looming darkness, nor has thick darkness covered my face."

## Translator Notes

- **23:2** — The Vulgate reads manus plagae meae (literally "the hand of my blow/wound"), a distinctive rendering compared with the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("my hand is heavy") and Septuagint ("His hand").
- **23:7** — The Vulgate makes God the subject of Proponat ("Let Him set forth fairness against me"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text describes an upright man reasoning with God.
- **23:10** — The Vulgate uses the perfect tense probavit ("He has tested me like gold that passes through fire"), differing from the conditional future statement in the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("when he has tried me, I shall come out as gold").
- **23:12** — The Vulgate reads in sinu meo ("in my bosom"), agreeing with the Septuagint against the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("more than my portion").
