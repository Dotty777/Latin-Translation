## Text

**30:1** But now those younger in age mock me, whose fathers I did not deign to place with the dogs of my flock,

**30:2** the strength of whose hands was as nothing to me, and they were considered unworthy of life itself,

**30:3** barren from want and hunger, who gnawed in the wilderness, squalid with calamity and misery.

**30:4** And they chewed herbs and the bark of trees, and the root of junipers was their food;

**30:5** snatching these things from the valleys, whenever they found individual ones, they ran to them with shouting.

**30:6** They lived in the desolate places of the torrents, and in the caverns of the earth, or upon the gravel;

**30:7** they rejoiced among such things, and counted it a delight to be under the briers,

**30:8** sons of fools and of the ignoble, and not visible at all in the land.

**30:9** Now I have been turned into their song, and I have been made a proverb to them.

**30:10** They abhor me, and they flee far from me, and they do not fear to spit on my face.

**30:11** For He has opened His quiver and afflicted me, and He has placed a bridle in my mouth.

**30:12** At the right hand of the east my calamities rose up at once; they overturned my feet and overwhelmed them as with waves by their paths.

**30:13** They broke up my roads; they lay in wait for me and prevailed, and there was no one to bring help.

**30:14** As with a broken wall and an open gate, they rushed upon me, and rolled down to my miseries.

**30:15** I have been reduced to nothing; like the wind You have carried away my desire, and like a cloud my safety has passed by.

**30:16** But now my soul withers within myself, and days of affliction possess me.

**30:17** At night my bone is pierced with pains, and those who eat me do not sleep.

**30:18** In their multitude my garment is consumed, and like the collar of a tunic they have bound me around.

**30:19** I have been compared to mud, and I have been made like cinders and ashes.

**30:20** I cry out to You, and You do not hear me; I stand, and You do not look at me.

**30:21** You have changed into a cruel one toward me, and in the hardness of Your hand You oppose me.

**30:22** You have lifted me up, and as if placing me upon the wind, You have dashed me down violently.

**30:23** I know that You will hand me over to death, where a house is appointed for every living being.

**30:24** Nevertheless, You do not put forth Your hand for their destruction, and if they fall down, You Yourself will save them.

**30:25** Once I wept over the one who was afflicted, and my soul had compassion on the poor.

**30:26** I expected good things, and evil things came to me; I waited for light, and darkness burst forth.

**30:27** My inward parts have boiled up without any rest; days of affliction have overtaken me.

**30:28** I went about mourning, without fury; rising up, I cried out in the crowd.

**30:29** I have been a brother of dragons, and a companion of ostriches.

**30:30** My skin has turned black upon me, and my bones have dried up from the scorching heat.

**30:31** My lyre has been turned into mourning, and my flute into the voice of those who weep.

## Translator Notes

- **30:2** — Vulgate-distinct reading "et vita ipsa putabantur indigni" ("and they were considered unworthy of life itself"), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text states that their vigor had perished.
- **30:5** — Vulgate-distinct reading describing the outcasts eagerly snatching wild plants from the valleys ("qui de convallibus ista rapientes..."), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text depicts them being driven out from the community with shouts as after a thief.
- **30:11** — Vulgate-distinct "Pharetram enim suam aperuit" ("For He has opened His quiver") and third-person singular "posuit" ("He has placed"), attributing the bridle as well as the affliction to God, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of loosing the bowstring and of Job's mockers casting off restraint.
- **30:24** — The Vulgate reads "non ad consumptionem eorum emittis manum tuam ... ipse salvabis" ("You do not put forth Your hand for their destruction ... You Yourself will save them"), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text is obscure and is usually understood of a fallen person stretching out a hand for help.
- **30:28** — Vulgate-distinct "sine furore" ("without fury"), reflecting Jerome's reading of the Hebrew word for "sun" as "wrath" or "fury."
