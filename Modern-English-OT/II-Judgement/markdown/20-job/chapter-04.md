## Text

**4:1** Now Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said:

**4:2** "If we begin to speak to you, perhaps you will take it badly; yet who will be able to hold back a speech once conceived?

**4:3** Look, you have taught many, and you have strengthened weary hands;

**4:4** your words have steadied those who were staggering, and you have fortified trembling knees.

**4:5** But now a scourge has come upon you, and you have grown faint; it has touched you, and you are deeply troubled.

**4:6** Where is your fear, your strength, your endurance, and the perfection of your ways?

**4:7** Remember, I implore you, what innocent person ever perished? Or when were the upright blotted out?

**4:8** Rather, I have seen that those who practice iniquity, and sow sorrows, and harvest them,

**4:9** perished when God blew, and were consumed by the breath of His wrath.

**4:10** The roar of the lion, and the voice of the lioness, and the teeth of the cubs of lions have been crushed.

**4:11** The tiger has perished because it had no prey, and the cubs of the lion have been scattered.

**4:12** Moreover, a hidden word was spoken to me, and as though stealthily my ear received the veins of its whisper.

**4:13** In the horror of a night vision, when deep sleep usually overtakes people,

**4:14** dread gripped me, and trembling, and all my bones were terrified;

**4:15** and as a spirit passed by in my presence, the hairs of my flesh bristled.

**4:16** Someone stood there whose face I did not recognize, an image before my eyes, and I heard a voice like that of a gentle breeze:

**4:17** 'Will a human being be justified in comparison with God? Or will a man be purer than his Maker?

**4:18** Look, those who serve Him are not constant, and in His angels He finds perversity;

**4:19** how much more will those who inhabit houses of clay, who have an earthly foundation, be consumed as though by a moth?

**4:20** From morning until evening they will be cut down; and because no one understands, they will perish forever.

**4:21** And those who remain will be taken away from them; they will die, and not in wisdom.'"

## Translator Notes

- **4:5** — The Vulgate supplies the explicit subject "plaga" ("a scourge" or "stroke"), whereas the Hebrew leaves the subject of "comes" and "touches" unstated.
- **4:6** — The Vulgate frames the verse as a single question listing four virtues ("Where is your fear..."), whereas the Hebrew has two equational rhetorical questions ("Is not your fear your confidence...").
- **4:11** — The Vulgate renders the poetic Hebrew term for a fierce lion ("layish") as "tigris" ("the tiger"), distinct from both the Hebrew and the Septuagint ("ant-lion").
- **4:12** — The Vulgate translates a rare Hebrew word for a faint whisper as "venas susurri ejus" ("the veins of its whisper").
- **4:21** — The Vulgate reads the Hebrew noun "yeter" in the sense of "remnant" ("those who remain") rather than "tent-cord," yielding "those who remain will be taken away from them."
