## Text

**27:1** Job also continued, taking up his parable, and said:

**27:2** "As God lives, who has taken away my justice, and the Almighty, who has led my soul to bitterness:

**27:3** surely, as long as breath remains in me, and the spirit of God is in my nostrils,

**27:4** my lips will not speak injustice, nor will my tongue meditate falsehood.

**27:5** Far be it from me to judge you to be righteous; until I expire, I will not withdraw from my innocence.

**27:6** My justification, which I have begun to hold, I will not abandon; for my heart does not reproach me in all my life.

**27:7** May my enemy be like an ungodly man, and my adversary like an unjust man.

**27:8** For what is the hope of the hypocrite, if he seizes greedily and God does not deliver his soul?

**27:9** Will God really hear his outcry when anguish comes upon him?

**27:10** Or will he be able to delight in the Almighty and invoke God at all times?

**27:11** I will teach you through the hand of God the things that the Almighty has, and I will not hide them.

**27:12** Look, all of you know this; and why do you speak empty things without cause?

**27:13** This is the portion of an ungodly man with God, and the inheritance of the violent, which they will receive from the Almighty.

**27:14** If his sons are multiplied, they will be under the sword, and his descendants will not be satisfied with bread;

**27:15** those who remain from him will be buried in ruin, and his widows will not weep.

**27:16** If he piles up silver like earth and prepares garments like clay,

**27:17** he will indeed prepare them, but the righteous will be clothed in them, and the innocent will divide the silver.

**27:18** He has built his house like a moth, and like a watchman he has made a booth.

**27:19** The rich man, when he sleeps, will carry nothing away with him; he will open his eyes and find nothing.

**27:20** Poverty will seize him like water; at night a storm will overwhelm him.

**27:21** A scorching wind will lift him up and carry him off, and like a whirlwind it will tear him from his place.

**27:22** And it will hurl upon him and will not spare; fleeing, he will flee from its hand.

**27:23** It will clasp its hands over him and hiss at him, gazing upon his place."

## Translator Notes

- **27:8** — Vulgate-distinct reading: Latin translates Hebrew chaneph ("godless") as hypocritae ("hypocrite") and frames the clause around greedily seizing (si avare rapiat) while God does not deliver his soul.
- **27:13** — The source text prints ob Omnipotente, an apparent typographical variant for the standard Clementine Vulgate ab Omnipotente ("from the Almighty"), confirmed by the ablative case of Omnipotente.
- **27:19** — Vulgate-distinct reading: Latin interprets the rich man's lying down as death leaving him empty-handed (cum dormierit, nihil secum auferet, "when he sleeps, will carry nothing away with him") and reads nihil inveniet ("will find nothing").
- **27:20** — Vulgate-distinct lexical choice: Latin reads inopia ("poverty") where the Hebrew reads "terrors."
- **27:22** — The third-person singular subject of vv. 22-23 is unexpressed in Latin, grammatically continuing the personified scorching wind/whirlwind of v. 21 (though interpreters have also understood God in v. 22 or a mocking bystander in v. 23); fugiens fugiet ("fleeing, he will flee") preserves the emphatic Latin Hebraism, and in v. 23 intuens locum ejus ("gazing upon his place") is a Vulgate-distinct reading.
