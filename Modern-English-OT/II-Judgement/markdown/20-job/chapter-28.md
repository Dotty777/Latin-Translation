## Text

**28:1** Silver has the sources of its veins, and for gold there is a place in which it is smelted.

**28:2** Iron is taken up from the earth, and stone dissolved by heat is turned into copper.

**28:3** He has set a time for darkness, and he himself examines the end of all things: the stone of gloom also, and the shadow of death.

**28:4** A torrent separates from the traveling people those whom the foot of a needy man has forgotten, and who are inaccessible.

**28:5** The earth from which bread sprang forth has been overturned in its place by fire.

**28:6** Its stones are the place of sapphire, and its clods are gold.

**28:7** No bird has known the path, nor has the eye of the vulture gazed upon it.

**28:8** The sons of traders have not trodden it, nor has the lioness passed through it.

**28:9** He has stretched out his hand to the flint; he has overturned mountains from the roots.

**28:10** In the rocks he has cut out channels, and his eye has seen everything precious.

**28:11** He has searched the depths of the rivers also, and has brought hidden things out into the light.

**28:12** But where is wisdom found? And what is the place of understanding?

**28:13** Man does not know its price, nor is it found in the land of those who live pleasantly.

**28:14** The abyss says, 'It is not in me,' and the sea speaks, 'It is not with me.'

**28:15** Fine gold will not be given for it, nor will silver be weighed out in exchange for it.

**28:16** It will not be compared to the dyed colors of India, nor to the most precious sardonyx stone or sapphire.

**28:17** Gold or glass will not be equal to it, nor will vessels of gold be exchanged for it.

**28:18** Lofty and prominent things will not be spoken of in comparison with it; yet wisdom is drawn from hidden places.

**28:19** The topaz from Ethiopia will not be equal to it, nor will it be matched with the purest dye.

**28:20** From where, then, does wisdom come? And what is the place of understanding?

**28:21** It is hidden from the eyes of all who live; it also lies concealed from the birds of the sky.

**28:22** Destruction and death have said, 'With our ears we have heard its report.'

**28:23** God understands its way, and He Himself knows its place.

**28:24** For He Himself gazes upon the ends of the world, and observes all things that are under heaven.

**28:25** He made a weight for the winds, and weighed out the waters by measure.

**28:26** When He was setting a law for the rains, and a way for the resounding storms,

**28:27** then He saw it and recounted it, and prepared it, and searched it out.

**28:28** And He said to man, 'Look, the fear of the Lord, that itself is wisdom; and to withdraw from evil is understanding.'"

## Translator Notes

- **28:3** — Latin reads Tempus posuit tenebris ("He has set a time for darkness") where the Hebrew has "puts an end/limit to darkness." The unexpressed subject of vv. 3 and 9-11 is taken here as the human miner (continuing vv. 1-2), though some traditional Vulgate commentators apply v. 3 to God.
- **28:4** — A notoriously difficult verse where the Vulgate offers a distinct reading, depicting a torrent cutting off inaccessible places forgotten by "the foot of a needy man" (pes egentis hominis, reading the Hebrew verb for "dangling" from the root for "needy").
- **28:8** — Latin filii institorum ("sons of traders/merchants") is a Vulgate-distinct rendering of Hebrew "proud beasts," paired here with the feminine leaena ("lioness").
- **28:13** — The Vulgate distinctively adds suaviter ("pleasantly") to "the land of the living," pointing to those who live in earthly ease or delight.
- **28:16** — Latin tinctis Indiae coloribus ("the dyed colors of India") is a Vulgate-distinct reading for Hebrew "gold of Ophir," taking Hebrew ketem as "dye" (as also in v. 19, tincturae mundissimae, "the purest dye") and identifying Ophir with India.
