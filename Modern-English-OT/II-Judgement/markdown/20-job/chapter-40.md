## Text

**40:1** And the Lord continued and spoke to Job:

**40:2** "Does he who contends with God grow quiet so easily? Surely he who reproves God ought to answer Him."

**40:3** Now Job, answering the Lord, said:

**40:4** "I who have spoken lightly, what can I answer? I will place my hand over my mouth.

**40:5** I have spoken one thing that I wish I had not said, and a second, to which I will add nothing further."

**40:6** Now the Lord, answering Job from the whirlwind, said:

**40:7** "Gird your loins like a man; I will question you, and declare to Me.

**40:8** Will you render My judgment void, and condemn Me so that you yourself may be justified?

**40:9** And do you have an arm like God? And do you thunder with a similar voice?

**40:10** Wrap yourself in beauty, and lift yourself on high, and be glorious, and clothe yourself in stately garments.

**40:11** Scatter the proud in your fury, and looking upon every arrogant one, humble him.

**40:12** Look upon all the proud and put them to shame, and crush the wicked in their place.

**40:13** Conceal them together in the dust, and sink their faces into the pit.

**40:14** And I Myself will acknowledge that your right hand can save you.

**40:15** Look at Behemoth, whom I made with you; he will eat grass like an ox.

**40:16** His strength is in his loins, and his might is in the navel of his belly.

**40:17** He tightens his tail like a cedar; the sinews of his testicles are intertwined.

**40:18** His bones are like tubes of bronze; his cartilage is like iron plates.

**40:19** He himself is the beginning of the ways of God; He who made him will apply his sword.

**40:20** For him the mountains yield plants; all the beasts of the field will play there.

**40:21** Beneath the shadow he sleeps, in the hiding place of the reed and in damp places.

**40:22** Shadows protect his shadow; the willows of the torrent will surround him.

**40:23** Look, he will swallow up a river and will not be astonished, and he has confidence that the Jordan flows into his mouth.

**40:24** In his eyes one will capture him as with a hook, and with stakes will pierce his nostrils.

**40:25** Will you be able to draw Leviathan out with a hook, and bind his tongue with a rope?

**40:26** Will you place a ring in his nostrils, or pierce his jaw with a hoop?

**40:27** Will he multiply pleas to you, or speak gentle words to you?

**40:28** Will he strike a covenant with you, and will you take him as a perpetual servant?

**40:29** Will you toy with him like a bird, or bind him for your maidservants?

**40:30** Will friends cut him up? Will traders divide him?

**40:31** Will you fill dragnets with his hide, and a fish hut with his head?

**40:32** Place your hand on him; remember the battle, and do not speak any further."

## Translator Notes

- **40:17** — Latin testiculorum ejus ("of his testicles") is a distinctive Vulgate rendering of Hebrew pahadaw, usually taken to mean "his thighs."
- **40:19** — The pronoun ejus in applicabit gladium ejus ("will apply his sword") is debated: grammatically non-reflexive ejus points to Behemoth's own weapon directed by his Maker, though many interpreters take it as God's sword brought near against him.
- **40:21** — Here and in v. 22 (Protegunt umbrae umbram ejus, "Shadows protect his shadow"), the Vulgate renders Hebrew tse'elim ("lotus trees") as "shadow" and "shadows."
- **40:24** — The Vulgate presents this verse as a declarative statement with an unexpressed third-person singular subject (capiet ... perforabit, rendered indefinitely as "one" and traditionally also referred to God from v. 19), whereas the Hebrew is commonly read as a rhetorical question.
- **40:31** — Latin sagenas ("dragnets") and gurgustium piscium ("a fish hut") are distinctive Vulgate renderings where the Hebrew refers to barbed irons and fish spears.
