## Text

**35:1** Therefore Elihu spoke these things again:

**35:2** "Does your thinking seem fair to you, that you should say, 'I am more righteous than God'?

**35:3** For you have said, 'What is upright does not please You; or what will it benefit You if I sin?'

**35:4** Therefore I will respond to your statements, and to your friends with you.

**35:5** Look up to heaven and gaze, and observe the upper sky, that it is higher than you.

**35:6** If you sin, how will you harm Him? And if your iniquities are multiplied, what will you do against Him?

**35:7** Moreover, if you act righteously, what will you give Him? Or what will He receive from your hand?

**35:8** Your wickedness will harm a man who is like you, and your righteousness will help a son of man.

**35:9** Because of the multitude of false accusers they will cry out, and they will wail because of the force of the arm of tyrants.

**35:10** And he has not said, 'Where is God who made me, who gave songs in the night,

**35:11** who teaches us more than the beasts of the earth, and instructs us more than the birds of heaven?'

**35:12** There they will cry out, and He will not answer, because of the pride of evil people.

**35:13** Therefore God will not hear in vain, and the Almighty will examine the cases of each one.

**35:14** Even when you say, 'He does not observe,' be judged before Him, and wait for Him.

**35:15** For now He does not inflict His fury, nor does He severely punish wrongdoing.

**35:16** Therefore Job opens his mouth in vain, and without knowledge he multiplies words."

## Translator Notes

- **35:3** — In the Vulgate, Job addresses God directly ('What is upright does not please You; or what will it benefit You if I sin?'), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has Job asking what advantage righteousness brings to himself.
- **35:9** — Latin calumniatorum literally means 'false accusers' or 'slanderers,' a Vulgate rendering corresponding to 'oppressions' in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **35:13** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text, where both clauses are negative ('God does not hear an empty cry, nor does the Almighty regard it'), the Vulgate leaves the second clause affirmative: God does not hear in vain, and the Almighty examines the cases of each one.
- **35:14** — Latin judicare is a passive or middle imperative ('be judged' / 'submit to judgment'), rendering the Hebrew phrase 'the case is before him' as a direct command to Job.
