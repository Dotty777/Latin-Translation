## Text

**26:1** Now Job answered and said:

**26:2** "Whose helper are you? Is it of one who is feeble? And do you support the arm of him who is not strong?

**26:3** To whom have you given counsel? Perhaps to one who has no wisdom; and you have displayed your abundant understanding.

**26:4** Whom did you wish to teach? Was it not Him who made breath?

**26:5** Look, the giants groan beneath the waters, and those who live with them.

**26:6** The underworld is naked before Him, and destruction has no covering.

**26:7** He stretches out the north over emptiness, and suspends the earth over nothing.

**26:8** He binds the waters in His clouds, so that they do not burst forth downward together.

**26:9** He withholds the face of His throne, and spreads out His mist over it.

**26:10** He has placed a boundary around the waters, until light and darkness come to an end.

**26:11** The pillars of heaven quake and are terrified at His nod.

**26:12** By His strength the seas were suddenly gathered together, and His understanding struck the proud one.

**26:13** His Spirit adorned the heavens, and with His hand acting as midwife, the twisting serpent was brought forth.

**26:14** Look, these things have been spoken as part of His ways; and since we have barely heard a small drop of His word, who will be able to gaze upon the thunder of His greatness?"

## Translator Notes

- **26:4** — Vulgate-distinct reading: where the Hebrew asks to whom words have been uttered and whose spirit came forth from the speaker, Latin asks whom Bildad wished to teach, namely, Him who made breath (eum qui fecit spiramentum).
- **26:5** — Following the Septuagint, the Vulgate renders Hebrew rephaim ("shades/the departed dead") as gigantes ("giants").
- **26:12** — Vulgate-distinct rendering: Latin states that the seas were "suddenly gathered together" (repente maria congregata sunt) rather than stilled or churned up, and translates Hebrew Rahab as superbum ("the proud one").
- **26:13** — Striking Vulgate-distinct image: Latin takes the Hebrew verb in the sense of midwifery (obstetricante manu ejus, "with His hand acting as midwife") bringing forth "the twisting serpent" (coluber tortuosus), rather than piercing a fleeing serpent.
- **26:14** — Vulgate-distinct imagery: Latin reads "a small drop" (parvam stillam) where the Hebrew has "a whisper," and pairs the visual verb intueri ("gaze upon") with "the thunder of His greatness."
