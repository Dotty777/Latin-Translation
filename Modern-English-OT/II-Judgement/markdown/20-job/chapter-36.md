## Text

**36:1** Elihu also continued and spoke these things:

**36:2** "Bear with me a little, and I will show you, for I still have something to speak on behalf of God.

**36:3** I will trace my knowledge from the beginning, and I will prove my Maker righteous.

**36:4** For truly my words are without falsehood, and perfect knowledge will be proved to you.

**36:5** God does not reject the mighty, since He Himself is also mighty;

**36:6** but He does not save the wicked, and He grants justice to the poor.

**36:7** He will not withdraw His eyes from the righteous, and He places kings on the throne forever, and they are lifted up.

**36:8** And if they are in chains and are bound with cords of poverty,

**36:9** He will show them their deeds and their crimes, because they were violent.

**36:10** He will also open their ear to correct them, and He will speak so that they may turn back from iniquity.

**36:11** If they listen and obey, they will complete their days in good and their years in glory;

**36:12** but if they do not listen, they will pass through the sword and be consumed in folly.

**36:13** Hypocrites and the crafty provoke the wrath of God, nor will they cry out when they are bound.

**36:14** Their soul will die in a storm, and their life among the effeminate.

**36:15** He will rescue the poor man from his distress, and He will open his ear in affliction.

**36:16** Therefore He will save you most broadly from a mouth that is narrow and has no foundation beneath it, and the rest of your table will be full of fatness.

**36:17** Your cause has been judged like that of a wicked man; you will receive cause and judgment.

**36:18** Therefore do not let wrath overpower you so that you oppress anyone, nor let a great number of gifts sway you.

**36:19** Lay down your greatness without affliction, and all the mighty in strength.

**36:20** Do not prolong the night, so that peoples may ascend in their place.

**36:21** Take care that you do not turn aside to iniquity, for you have begun to follow this after misery.

**36:22** Look, God is exalted in His strength, and no one is like Him among lawgivers.

**36:23** Who will be able to search out His ways? Or who can say to Him, 'You have done iniquity'?

**36:24** Remember that you do not know His work, about which men have sung.

**36:25** All people see Him; each one gazes from afar.

**36:26** Look, God is great, surpassing our knowledge; the number of His years is incalculable.

**36:27** He draws up the drops of rain and pours out showers like torrents,

**36:28** which flow from the clouds that cover all things from above.

**36:29** If He wills to spread out the clouds like His tent,

**36:30** and to flash with His light from above, He will also cover the hinges of the sea.

**36:31** For through these things He judges peoples and gives food to many mortals.

**36:32** In His hands He hides the light, and He has commanded it to come again.

**36:33** He announces concerning it to His friend, that it is his possession and that he can ascend to it."

## Translator Notes

- **36:3** — Latin 'a principio' ("from the beginning") is a distinctly Vulgate reading where the Hebrew reads "from afar."
- **36:14** — Latin 'in tempestate' ("in a storm") is a distinctly Vulgate rendering; the Hebrew reads "in youth."
- **36:19** — The Vulgate construes this difficult verse as a direct imperative ('Depone magnitudinem tuam', "Lay down your greatness"), whereas the Hebrew is commonly taken as a question about whether cries or strength avail in distress.
- **36:24** — Latin 'quod ignores' ("that you do not know") is a notable Vulgate-distinct reading where the Hebrew has "to magnify."
- **36:33** — Latin 'amico suo' ("to His friend") and the clause about ascending to the light reflect a distinctly Vulgate interpretation of a notoriously obscure Hebrew verse often rendered as thunder announcing the storm.
