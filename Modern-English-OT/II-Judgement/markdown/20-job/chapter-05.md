## Text

**5:1** "Call out, then, if there is anyone who will answer you, and turn to one of the holy ones.

**5:2** Truly, anger kills a fool, and envy puts a little one to death.

**5:3** I myself have seen a fool with a firm root, and at once I cursed his beauty.

**5:4** His sons will be far from safety, and they will be crushed in the gate, and there will be no one to rescue them.

**5:5** The hungry will eat his harvest, and an armed man will seize even him, and the thirsty will drink his wealth.

**5:6** Nothing on earth happens without a cause, and sorrow does not arise from the ground.

**5:7** Man is born for toil, and a bird for flight.

**5:8** For this reason I myself will plead with the Lord, and I will set forth my utterance to God:

**5:9** who performs great and inscrutable things, and marvels without number;

**5:10** who gives rain on the face of the earth, and waters everything with waters;

**5:11** who places the humble on high, and raises the grieving up in safety;

**5:12** who thwarts the designs of the malicious, so that their hands cannot complete what they had begun;

**5:13** who catches the wise in their cunning, and thwarts the counsel of the perverse.

**5:14** By day they will run into darkness, and as if at night, so they will grope at midday.

**5:15** Yet He will save the needy from the sword of their mouth, and the poor from the hand of the violent one.

**5:16** And there will be hope for the needy; but iniquity will close its mouth.

**5:17** Blessed is the man who is corrected by God; therefore do not reject the reproof of the Lord:

**5:18** for He Himself wounds and heals; He strikes, and His hands will make whole.

**5:19** In six tribulations He will deliver you, and in the seventh no evil will touch you.

**5:20** In famine He will rescue you from death, and in war from the hand of the sword.

**5:21** From the whip of the tongue you will be hidden, and you will not fear calamity when it comes.

**5:22** In devastation and famine you will laugh, and you will not dread the beasts of the earth.

**5:23** But your covenant will be with the stones of the regions, and the beasts of the earth will be at peace with you.

**5:24** And you will know that your tent has peace; and inspecting your beauty, you will not sin.

**5:25** You will also know that your seed will be manifold, and your descendants like the grass of the earth.

**5:26** In abundance you will enter the grave, just as a heap of wheat is carried in during its season.

**5:27** See, as we have investigated this, so it is: now that it has been heard, ponder it carefully in your mind."

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew "navehu" ("his dwelling") as "pulchritudini ejus" ("his beauty"), connecting it to the similar root meaning "comely" or "beautiful" (cf. 5:24).
- **5:5** — The Vulgate interprets the Hebrew term for "thorns" ("tsinnim") in the sense of shields or armed men ("armatus") and makes the fool himself the object ("ipsum"), rather than the harvest being taken from among thorns.
- **5:6** — The Vulgate renders the first clause as "Nihil in terra sine causa fit" ("Nothing on earth happens without a cause"), taking Hebrew "aven" ("affliction/iniquity") in its sense of "nothingness" rather than "affliction does not come forth from the dust."
- **5:7** — Following an ancient interpretive tradition shared with the Septuagint, the Vulgate renders the poetic Hebrew expression "sons of flame" (sparks) as "avis" ("a bird") made for flight.
- **5:24** — As in 5:3, the Vulgate renders Hebrew "naveh" ("dwelling/fold") as "speciem" ("beauty" or "appearance"), and translates the Hebrew verb "chata" in its moral sense ("you will not sin") rather than "you will miss nothing."
