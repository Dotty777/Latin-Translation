## Text

**42:1** Then Job, answering the Lord, said:

**42:2** "I know that You can do all things, and no thought lies hidden from You.

**42:3** 'Who is this who conceals counsel without knowledge?' Therefore I have spoken foolishly, and things that surpassed my knowledge beyond measure.

**42:4** 'Listen, and I will speak; I will question you, and answer Me.'

**42:5** By the hearing of the ear I have heard You, but now my eye sees You.

**42:6** For this reason I reproach myself, and I repent in cinders and ashes."

**42:7** Now after the Lord had spoken these words to Job, He said to Eliphaz the Temanite, "My wrath is provoked against you and against your two friends, because you have not spoken before Me what is right, as My servant Job has.

**42:8** Therefore take for yourselves seven bulls and seven rams, and go to My servant Job, and offer a burnt offering for yourselves; and My servant Job will pray for you. I will accept his face, so that folly may not be charged to you; for you have not spoken to Me things that are right, as My servant Job has."

**42:9** So Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite went away and did just as the Lord had spoken to them; and the Lord accepted Job's face.

**42:10** The Lord also turned toward Job's repentance when he prayed for his friends; and the Lord added all that had belonged to Job, twofold.

**42:11** Then all his brothers, all his sisters, and all who had formerly known him came to him, and ate bread with him in his house; and they shook their heads over him and comforted him over all the evil that the Lord had brought upon him; and each one gave him one sheep and one gold earring.

**42:12** And the Lord blessed Job's latter days more than his beginning; and he came to have fourteen thousand sheep, and six thousand camels, and a thousand yokes of oxen, and a thousand female donkeys.

**42:13** And he had seven sons and three daughters.

**42:14** And he called the name of the first, Day; and the name of the second, Cassia; and the name of the third, Horn of Antimony.

**42:15** Now no women were found as beautiful as the daughters of Job in the whole earth; and their father gave them an inheritance among their brothers.

**42:16** And Job lived one hundred and forty years after these things, and saw his sons and the sons of his sons up to the fourth generation; and he died old and full of days.

## Translator Notes

- **42:2** — Vulgate reads "no thought lies hidden from You" (nulla te latet cogitatio), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "no purpose of Yours can be thwarted."
- **42:4** — Job here quotes God's earlier summons in 38:3, where the Vulgate has the identical wording "I will question you, and answer Me" (interrogabo te, et responde mihi). Traditional Latin commentators also read the verse directly as Job humbly asking God to listen and instruct him.
- **42:6** — Vulgate reads "I reproach myself, and I repent in cinders and ashes" (ipse me reprehendo, et ago poenitentiam in favilla et cinere), using the Latin pair for cinders and ashes where the Hebrew has "dust and ashes."
- **42:10** — Vulgate distinctively reads "turned toward Job's repentance" (conversus est ad poenitentiam Job), where the Hebrew phrase means "restored the fortunes" (or "turned the captivity").
- **42:11** — Vulgate reads "one sheep" (ovem unam), agreeing with the Septuagint, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text specifies a qesitah (a piece of money).
- **42:14** — Rather than transliterating the Hebrew names Jemimah, Keziah, and Keren-happuch, the Vulgate translates their meanings into Latin as Dies ("Day"), Cassia ("Cassia"), and Cornustibii ("Horn of Antimony," a vessel for eye-paint).
