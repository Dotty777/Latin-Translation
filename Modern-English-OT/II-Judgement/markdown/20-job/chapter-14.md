## Text

**14:1** Man, born from a woman, living a brief time, is filled with many woes.

**14:2** He emerges like a flower and is crushed, and he flees like a shadow, and never remains in the same condition.

**14:3** And do You consider it worthy to open Your eyes upon such a one, and to bring him into judgment with You?

**14:4** Who can make clean the one conceived from unclean seed? Is it not You, who alone are?

**14:5** Brief are the days of man; the number of his months is with You; You have set his limits, which cannot be crossed.

**14:6** Withdraw a little from him, so that he may rest, until his longed-for day comes, like that of a hired worker.

**14:7** A tree has hope: if it is cut down, it grows green again, and its branches sprout.

**14:8** If its root grows old in the earth, and its stump dies away in the dust,

**14:9** at the scent of water it will bud, and produce foliage just as when it was first planted.

**14:10** But when a man dies, and is stripped bare and consumed, where, I ask, is he?

**14:11** Just as if waters were to recede from the sea, and a river, emptied out, were to dry up:

**14:12** so a man, when he has fallen asleep, will not rise again; until heaven is worn away, he will not wake up, nor will he rise up from his sleep.

**14:13** Who would grant me this, that You would protect me in the underworld, and hide me until Your fury passes by, and set a time for me in which You would remember me?

**14:14** Do You suppose that a dead man will live again? All the days in which I now serve as a soldier, I am waiting until my change comes.

**14:15** You will call me, and I will answer You; to the work of Your hands You will extend Your right hand.

**14:16** You have indeed counted my steps, yet spare my sins.

**14:17** You have sealed my offenses as though in a pouch, yet You have healed my iniquity.

**14:18** A falling mountain slips away, and a rock is shifted from its place:

**14:19** waters hollow out stones, and by flooding the earth is gradually consumed; and therefore You will likewise destroy man.

**14:20** You made him strong for a little while, so that he would pass away forever; You will alter his face and send him away.

**14:21** Whether his sons are noble or ignoble, he will not understand.

**14:22** Yet still, his flesh, while he lives, will feel pain, and his soul will mourn over himself."

## Translator Notes

- **14:4** — Vulgate distinctively expands with "conceptum semine" ("conceived from unclean seed") and renders the closing clause as a question addressed to God, "nonne tu qui solus es?" ("Is it not You, who alone are?"), whereas the Hebrew reads "Not one."
- **14:13** — Vulgate "in inferno" literally means "in the lower realm" or "underworld," denoting the abode of the dead.
- **14:15** — Vulgate "porriges dexteram" ("You will extend Your right hand") is a distinctive rendering for the Hebrew "You will long for," and in 14:16-17 Vulgate likewise reads a plea for mercy and healing ("sed parce peccatis meis ... sed curasti iniquitatem meam").
- **14:20** — Vulgate "Roborasti eum paululum" ("You made him strong for a little while") is a distinctive reading where the Hebrew reads "You overpower him forever."
- **14:22** — Vulgate includes the temporal clause "dum vivet" ("while he lives"), which has no direct equivalent in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
