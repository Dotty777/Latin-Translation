## Text

**21:1** Then Job answered and said:

**21:2** "Hear my words, I beg you, and repent.

**21:3** Bear with me, and I will speak; and after my words, if it seems good, laugh.

**21:4** Is my dispute against a man, so that I should not rightly be grieved?

**21:5** Look at me and be astounded, and place a finger over your mouth.

**21:6** Even I, when I remember, am terrified, and trembling shakes my flesh.

**21:7** Why then do the wicked live, and why have they been lifted up and strengthened with riches?

**21:8** Their seed remains before them, a crowd of relatives and grandchildren in their sight.

**21:9** Their houses are safe and peaceful, and the rod of God is not on them.

**21:10** Their cow has conceived and has not miscarried; the cow has given birth and has not been deprived of her young.

**21:11** Their little ones go out like flocks, and their children exult in play.

**21:12** They hold the tambourine and the lyre, and they rejoice at the sound of the pipe.

**21:13** They spend their days in prosperity, and in a moment they descend to the underworld.

**21:14** And they said to God, 'Depart from us, and we do not want the knowledge of Your ways.

**21:15** Who is the Almighty, so that we should serve Him? And what benefit is it to us if we pray to Him?'

**21:16** Nevertheless, because their good things are not in their hand, may the counsel of the wicked be far from me.

**21:17** How often will the lamp of the wicked be extinguished, and will a flood come upon them, and will He apportion the pains of His wrath?

**21:18** They will be like chaff before the face of the wind, and like ash that a whirlwind scatters.

**21:19** God will reserve for his sons the pain of their father, and when He repays, then he will know.

**21:20** His eyes will see his own slaughter, and he will drink from the wrath of the Almighty.

**21:21** For what does it matter to him about his house after him, even if the number of his months is cut in half?

**21:22** Will anyone teach knowledge to God, who judges those on high?

**21:23** This one dies strong and healthy, rich and happy;

**21:24** his inward parts are full of fat, and his bones are moistened with marrow;

**21:25** but another dies in bitterness of soul, without any wealth;

**21:26** and yet together in the dust they will sleep, and worms will cover them.

**21:27** Surely I know your thoughts and unjust judgments against me.

**21:28** For you say, 'Where is the house of the prince? And where are the tents of the wicked?'

**21:29** Ask any of the travelers, and you will recognize that he understands these same things:

**21:30** that the evil man is reserved for the day of destruction, and will be led to the day of wrath.

**21:31** Who will reprove his way before him? And who will repay him for what he has done?

**21:32** He himself will be led to the tombs, and in the heap of the dead he will keep watch.

**21:33** He was sweet to the gravel of Cocytus, and he will draw every man after him, and before him countless ones.

**21:34** How then do you console me in vain, when your response has been shown to oppose the truth?"

## Translator Notes

- **21:2** — Vulgate-distinct reading: "repent" (agite pœnitentiam, literally "do repentance"), taking the Hebrew root for consolation in its sense of repentance.
- **21:10** — Vulgate-distinct reading: Latin Bos eorum concepit uses bos as feminine ("cow") and translates the Hebrew clause about a bull breeding without fail as conceiving without miscarrying.
- **21:19** — The Vulgate renders the entire verse as a statement in the future tense; in the Hebrew the second clause can be read as a wish ("let Him repay him, that he may know").
- **21:22** — The Clementine Vulgate prints nominative Deus alongside nominative quispiam ("anyone"); because quispiam can only be the subject and the relative clause refers to God, the clause is translated as teaching knowledge to God (matching Deum in other Vulgate witnesses).
- **21:33** — Vulgate-distinct proper name and reading: Jerome renders Hebrew "clods of the valley" as glareis Cocyti ("the gravel of Cocytus"), naming Cocytus, the classical underworld river of wailing, and making the dead man himself ("he was sweet") the subject.
