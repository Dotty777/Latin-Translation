## Text

**22:1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said:

**22:2** "Can a man be compared to God, even when he possesses perfect knowledge?

**22:3** What does it profit God if you are righteous? Or what do you bring to Him if your way is spotless?

**22:4** Is it because He is afraid that He will reprove you and come into judgment with you,

**22:5** and not because of your very great wickedness and your endless iniquities?

**22:6** For you took away the pledge of your brothers without cause, and stripped the naked of their garments.

**22:7** You did not give water to the weary, and from the hungry you withheld bread.

**22:8** By the strength of your arm you possessed the land, and as the most powerful you held it.

**22:9** You sent widows away empty-handed, and you crushed the arms of orphans.

**22:10** Therefore you are surrounded by snares, and sudden terror dismays you.

**22:11** And did you think that you would not see darkness, and that you would not be overwhelmed by the rush of flooding waters?

**22:12** Or do you not consider that God is higher than heaven, and is exalted above the summit of the stars?

**22:13** And you say, 'What indeed does God know? And He judges as if through thick darkness.

**22:14** Clouds are His hiding place, and He does not consider our affairs, and He walks around the poles of heaven.'

**22:15** Do you long to keep the path of the ages, which wicked men have trodden,

**22:16** who were carried off before their time, and a river overturned their foundation?

**22:17** They kept saying to God, 'Depart from us!' and they regarded Him as though the Almighty could do nothing,

**22:18** even though He had filled their houses with good things; may their opinion be far from me.

**22:19** The righteous will see and rejoice, and the innocent will mock them:

**22:20** 'Has not their elevation been cut down? And has not fire devoured their remnants?'

**22:21** Yield to Him, therefore, and have peace, and through these things you will have the finest fruits.

**22:22** Receive the law from His mouth, and place His words in your heart.

**22:23** If you return to the Almighty, you will be built up, and you will remove iniquity far from your tent.

**22:24** He will give flint in place of earth, and in place of flint, golden torrents.

**22:25** And the Almighty will be against your enemies, and silver will be piled up for you.

**22:26** Then you will overflow with delights in the Almighty, and you will lift up your face to God.

**22:27** You will pray to Him, and He will heed you, and you will fulfill your vows.

**22:28** You will decide a matter, and it will come to you, and light will shine upon your ways.

**22:29** For the one who has been humbled will be in glory, and the one who lowers his eyes will himself be saved.

**22:30** The innocent will be saved; indeed, he will be saved through the cleanness of his hands."

## Translator Notes

- **22:2** — The Vulgate reads comparari ("be compared"), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("be profitable").
- **22:4** — The Vulgate renders timens ("fearing") with God as the subject ("because He is afraid"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text refers to Job's reverence ("because of your piety").
- **22:8** — The Vulgate casts this verse in the second person as a direct accusation against Job, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks in the third person of a powerful man.
- **22:24** — The Vulgate reads Dabit pro terra silicem, et pro silice torrentes aureos ("He will give flint in place of earth, and in place of flint, golden torrents"), a distinctly Vulgate promise differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's command to lay gold in the dust. Similarly, v. 25 reads contra hostes tuos ("against your enemies") rather than "your gold."
- **22:30** — The Vulgate reads Salvabitur innocens ("The innocent will be saved"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "he will deliver the one who is not innocent."
