## Text

**33:1** "Hear, therefore, Job, my utterances, and listen closely to all my words.

**33:2** Look, I have opened my mouth; let my tongue speak in my throat.

**33:3** My words are from my sincere heart, and my lips will speak a pure conviction.

**33:4** The Spirit of God has made me, and the breath of the Almighty has given life to me.

**33:5** If you are able, answer me, and take your stand before my face.

**33:6** Look, God made me just as He made you, and from the same clay I too was formed.

**33:7** Nevertheless, do not let my wonder terrify you, nor let my eloquence be heavy upon you.

**33:8** You said, therefore, in my ears, and I heard the sound of your words:

**33:9** 'I am clean and without offense, unblemished, and there is no iniquity in me.

**33:10** Because He has found complaints against me, therefore He has regarded me as an enemy to Himself.

**33:11** He has placed my feet in the stocks; He has watched all my paths.'

**33:12** This, therefore, is the point in which you are not justified: I will answer you, for God is greater than man.

**33:13** Do you contend against Him because He has not answered you concerning all words?

**33:14** God speaks once, and He does not repeat the same thing a second time.

**33:15** Through a dream, in a night vision, when deep sleep comes upon men and they sleep on a bed,

**33:16** then He opens the ears of men, and teaching them, He instructs them with discipline,

**33:17** so that He may turn a man away from the things he does, and deliver him from pride,

**33:18** rescuing his soul from corruption, and his life, so that it may not pass over to the sword.

**33:19** He also rebukes him through pain on a bed, and causes all his bones to wither.

**33:20** Bread becomes detestable to him in his life, and to his soul the food that was formerly desirable.

**33:21** His flesh will waste away, and the bones that had been covered will be laid bare.

**33:22** His soul has drawn close to corruption, and his life to those who bring death.

**33:23** If there is an angel speaking on his behalf, one out of thousands, to declare a man's uprightness,

**33:24** He will show mercy to him and say, 'Deliver him, so that he does not descend into corruption; I have found that by which I may be gracious to him.'

**33:25** His flesh has been consumed by torments; let him return to the days of his youth.

**33:26** He will plead with God, and He will be favorable to him; and he will see His face with rejoicing, and He will restore to the man his righteousness.

**33:27** He will look upon men and say, 'I have sinned and truly committed an offense, and I did not receive as I deserved.'

**33:28** He has delivered his soul, so that it might not go into destruction, but living might see the light.

**33:29** Look, God works all these things three times with each individual,

**33:30** so that He may call back their souls from corruption, and illuminate them with the light of the living.

**33:31** Pay attention, Job, and listen to me; and be silent while I speak.

**33:32** But if you have something to say, answer me; speak, for I want you to appear righteous.

**33:33** But if you do not, listen to me; be silent, and I will teach you wisdom."

## Translator Notes

- **33:7** — The Vulgate reads miraculum meum ("my wonder") and eloquentia mea ("my eloquence"), where the Hebrew has "my terror" and "my pressure" (or "hand").
- **33:14** — The Vulgate reads et secundo idipsum non repetit ("and He does not repeat the same thing a second time"), whereas the Hebrew says God speaks in one way and in two, though a person does not perceive it.
- **33:18** — Here and in verses 22, 24, and 30, the Vulgate renders Hebrew shachat ("the pit") as corruptio ("corruption"), matching the Septuagint's diaphthora.
- **33:24** — The Vulgate translates Hebrew "I have found a ransom" as inveni in quo ei propitier ("I have found that by which I may be gracious to him"), reflecting divine propitiation and indicating God as the speaker.
- **33:25** — The Vulgate reads Consumpta est caro ejus a suppliciis ("His flesh has been consumed by torments"), contrasting with the Hebrew reading that his flesh becomes fresher than a child's.
