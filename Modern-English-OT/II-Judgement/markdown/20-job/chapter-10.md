## Text

**10:1** "My soul is tired of my life; I will release my speech against myself; I will speak in the bitterness of my soul.

**10:2** I will say to God, 'Do not condemn me; show me why You judge me in this way.

**10:3** Does it seem good to You if You falsely accuse me, and oppress me, the work of Your hands, and assist the counsel of the wicked?

**10:4** Do You have eyes of flesh? Or as a man sees, will You also see?

**10:5** Are Your days like the days of a man, and are Your years like human times,

**10:6** that You should seek my iniquity and examine my sin,

**10:7** and know that I have done nothing wicked, when there is no one who can rescue from Your hand?

**10:8** Your hands made me and formed me completely all around; and do You cast me down so suddenly?

**10:9** Remember, I pray, that You made me like clay, and You will return me to dust.

**10:10** Did You not milk me like milk, and curdle me like cheese?

**10:11** With skin and flesh You clothed me; with bones and sinews You joined me together.

**10:12** Life and mercy You granted me, and Your visitation guarded my spirit.

**10:13** Even though You hide these things in Your heart, still I know that You remember all things.

**10:14** If I have sinned, and You spared me for an hour, why do You not allow me to be clean from my iniquity?

**10:15** And if I am wicked, woe to me; and if righteous, I will not lift my head, sated with affliction and misery.

**10:16** And because of pride You will capture me like a lioness, and returning, You torment me astonishingly.

**10:17** You renew Your witnesses against me, and multiply Your anger against me, and punishments wage war against me.

**10:18** Why did You bring me out of the womb? If only I had been consumed, so that no eye might see me.

**10:19** I would have been as though I were not, carried across from the womb to the tomb.

**10:20** Will not the small number of my days soon be ended? Leave me alone, therefore, so that I may mourn my pain a little,

**10:21** before I go, and do not return, to a land dark and covered with the gloom of death:

**10:22** a land of misery and darkness, where the shadow of death is, and no order, but eternal horror dwells.'"

## Translator Notes

- **10:10** — The Vulgate reads mulsisti ("milked"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "poured out."
- **10:14** — The Vulgate distinctively reads ad horam pepercisti mihi ("You spared me for an hour"), whereas the Hebrew reads "You would watch over/take note of me."
- **10:16** — The Vulgate reads propter superbiam ("because of pride") and places leaenam ("lioness") in the accusative as the quarry being captured, whereas the Hebrew depicts God hunting Job like a lion if his head lifts itself up.
- **10:20** — The Vulgate reads ut plangam paululum dolorem meum ("so that I may mourn my pain a little"), whereas the Hebrew reads "that I may have a little cheer."
- **10:22** — The Vulgate concludes with sed sempiternus horror inhabitat ("but eternal horror dwells"), whereas the Hebrew reads "and where it shines like darkness."
