## Text

**11:1** Then Zophar the Naamathite answered and said:

**11:2** "Will the one who speaks much not also hear? Or will a talkative man be justified?

**11:3** Will people stay silent for you alone? And when you have mocked others, will you be refuted by no one?

**11:4** For you have said, 'My speech is pure, and I am clean in Your sight.'

**11:5** And if only God would speak with you, and open His lips to you,

**11:6** so that He might show you the secrets of wisdom, and that His law is manifold; and you would understand that far less is demanded of you by Him than your iniquity deserves!

**11:7** Will you perhaps comprehend the footsteps of God, and discover the Almighty even to perfection?

**11:8** He is higher than heaven, and what will you do? Deeper than the underworld, and how will you come to know?

**11:9** His measure is longer than the earth, and wider than the sea.

**11:10** If He overturns all things, or compresses them into one, who will speak against Him?

**11:11** For He Himself knows the emptiness of people; and seeing iniquity, does He not consider it?

**11:12** An empty man is raised up into pride, and thinks himself born free like the colt of a wild donkey.

**11:13** But you have made your heart firm, and have stretched out your hands to Him.

**11:14** If you remove from yourself the iniquity that is in your hand, and injustice does not remain in your tent,

**11:15** then you will be able to lift your face without blemish; and you will be steadfast, and you will not fear.

**11:16** You will also forget misery, and remember it like waters that have passed by.

**11:17** And radiance like noonday will rise up for you at evening; and when you think yourself consumed, you will rise like the morning star.

**11:18** And you will have confidence, with hope set before you; and buried, you will sleep securely.

**11:19** You will rest, and there will be no one to frighten you; and very many will plead before your face.

**11:20** But the eyes of the wicked will fail, and escape will perish from them; and their hope is an abomination of the soul."

## Translator Notes

- **11:6** — The Vulgate reads multiplex esset lex ejus ("His law is manifold") and "far less is demanded of you by Him than your iniquity deserves," where the Hebrew speaks of sound wisdom having two sides and God forgetting part of Job's iniquity.
- **11:7** — The Vulgate renders vestigia Dei ("the footsteps of God"), whereas the Hebrew asks whether Job can discover the "depths" of God.
- **11:12** — The Vulgate reads Vir vanus in superbiam erigitur, et tamquam pullum onagri se liberum natum putat ("An empty man is raised up into pride, and thinks himself born free like the colt of a wild donkey"), differing notably from the difficult Hebrew proverb about a hollow man gaining understanding when a wild donkey's colt is born a man.
- **11:17** — The Vulgate reads ad vesperam ("at evening") and cum te consumptum putaveris ("when you think yourself consumed"), and uses lucifer in its classical Latin sense of "the morning star."
- **11:18** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew verb "dig/search" with the passive participle defossus ("buried" or "entrenched"), traditionally understood either as resting safely in the grave or sleeping secure as in an entrenched camp.
