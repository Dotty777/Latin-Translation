## Text

**20:1** Then Zophar the Naamathite answered and said:

**20:2** "For this reason my varied thoughts follow one another, and my mind is swept away in different directions.

**20:3** I will hear the instruction by which you reprove me, and the spirit of my understanding will respond to me.

**20:4** I know this from the beginning, ever since man was placed upon the earth,

**20:5** that the praise of the wicked is brief, and the joy of the hypocrite is like a moment.

**20:6** If his pride ascends up to heaven, and his head touches the clouds,

**20:7** he will be destroyed like a dung heap in the end, and those who had seen him will say, 'Where is he?'

**20:8** Like a dream flying away, he will not be found; he will pass away like a vision of the night.

**20:9** The eye that had seen him will not see him, nor will his place look upon him any longer.

**20:10** His sons will be worn down by poverty, and his hands will repay him his pain.

**20:11** His bones will be filled with the vices of his youth, and they will sleep with him in the dust.

**20:12** For when evil is sweet in his mouth, he will hide it under his tongue.

**20:13** He will spare it and will not abandon it, and he will conceal it in his throat.

**20:14** His bread in his stomach will be turned into the gall of asps within.

**20:15** He will vomit up the riches that he devoured, and God will draw them out of his belly.

**20:16** He will suck the head of asps, and the tongue of a viper will kill him.

**20:17** (May he not see the streams of the river, the torrents of honey and butter.)

**20:18** He will pay for all that he has done, and yet he will not be consumed; according to the multitude of his devices, so also he will suffer.

**20:19** For he crushed and stripped the poor; he seized a house and did not build it.

**20:20** Nor was his belly satisfied; and when he has the things he had coveted, he will not be able to possess them.

**20:21** Nothing was left of his food, and therefore nothing will remain of his goods.

**20:22** When he has been satisfied, he will be hemmed in; he will burn, and every pain will rush upon him.

**20:23** Oh, that his belly may be filled, so that He may send forth upon him the anger of His wrath, and rain His war upon him.

**20:24** He will flee from iron weapons, and he will rush into a bronze bow.

**20:25** Drawn out and emerging from its sheath, and flashing in its bitterness: terrifying ones will go and come upon him.

**20:26** All darkness is hidden in his secret places; a fire that is not kindled will devour him; the one left in his tent will be afflicted.

**20:27** The heavens will uncover his iniquity, and the earth will rise against him.

**20:28** The offspring of his house will be laid open; it will be pulled down on the day of the wrath of God.

**20:29** This is the share of a wicked man from God, and the inheritance of his words from the Lord."

## Translator Notes

- **20:11** — Vulgate-distinct reading: "the vices of his youth" (vitiis adolescentiæ ejus), whereas the Hebrew text refers to his youthful vigor.
- **20:16** — Vulgate-distinct reading: Latin Caput aspidum ("the head of asps") renders the Hebrew word rosh ("poison" or "head") literally as "head."
- **20:23** — Vulgate-distinct reading: "rain His war upon him" (pluat super illum bellum suum), understanding the Hebrew term (commonly translated as "food" or "flesh") from the root for war.
- **20:25** — The Latin opens with an abrupt participial phrase ("Drawn out... flashing in its bitterness," rendering Hebrew "gallbladder" as amaritudine) before shifting to the masculine plural substantive horribiles ("terrifying ones").
- **20:29** — Vulgate-distinct reading: "the inheritance of his words" (hæreditas verborum ejus), rendering the Hebrew term for an appointed decree from the root meaning "word."
