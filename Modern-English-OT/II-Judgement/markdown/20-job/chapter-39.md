## Text

**39:1** "Do you know the birthing time of the ibexes on the rocks, or have you observed the female deer in labor?

**39:2** Have you counted the months of their pregnancy, and have you known their birthing time?

**39:3** They crouch down for their young, and give birth, and let out groans.

**39:4** Their young are separated, and go off to pasture; they go out, and do not return to them.

**39:5** Who let the wild donkey go free, and who untied its bonds?

**39:6** To it I gave a house in the wilderness, and its dwellings in a land of salt.

**39:7** It scorns the multitude of the city; it does not hear the shout of the taskmaster.

**39:8** It surveys the mountains of its pasture, and searches out every green thing.

**39:9** Will the rhinoceros be willing to serve you, or will it remain at your manger?

**39:10** Will you bind the rhinoceros with your strap to plow, or will it break the clods of the valleys behind you?

**39:11** Will you place confidence in its great strength, and leave your labors to it?

**39:12** Will you trust it to return the seed to you, and gather your threshing floor?

**39:13** The ostrich's wing resembles the wings of the heron and the hawk.

**39:14** When she leaves her eggs on the ground, will you perhaps warm them in the dust?

**39:15** She forgets that a foot may trample them, or a beast of the field may crush them.

**39:16** She is hardened toward her young, as though they were not hers; she has labored in vain, with no fear compelling her.

**39:17** For God has deprived her of wisdom, and has not given her understanding.

**39:18** When the time comes, she raises her wings on high; she mocks the horse and its rider.

**39:19** Will you provide strength to the horse, or surround its neck with neighing?

**39:20** Will you rouse it like locusts? The majesty of its nostrils is terror.

**39:21** It digs the earth with its hoof; it exults boldly; it advances to meet armed men.

**39:22** It scorns dread, and does not yield to the sword.

**39:23** Over it the quiver will resound; the spear and the shield will flash;

**39:24** seething and snorting, it swallows the ground, and does not heed that the blast of the trumpet is sounding.

**39:25** When it hears the horn, it says, 'Aha!' From afar it scents war: the exhortation of the commanders, and the shouting of the army.

**39:26** Is it by your wisdom that the hawk grows feathers, spreading its wings toward the south?

**39:27** Will the eagle rise up at your command, and set its nest on the heights?

**39:28** It remains on the rocks, and dwells on sheer flint crags and inaccessible cliffs.

**39:29** From there it watches for food, and from far off its eyes look out.

**39:30** Its young will lap up blood; and wherever a carcass is, immediately it is there."

**39:31** And the Lord continued and spoke to Job:

**39:32** "Does he who contends with God grow quiet so easily? Surely he who reproves God ought to answer Him."

**39:33** Now Job, answering the Lord, said:

**39:34** "I who have spoken lightly, what can I answer? I will place my hand over my mouth.

**39:35** I have spoken one thing that I wish I had not said, and a second, to which I will add nothing further."

## Translator Notes

- **39:9** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew wild ox (re'em) as "the rhinoceros" (rhinoceros), a distinctive Latin reading that continues in 39:10.
- **39:13** — Whereas the Hebrew contrasts the ostrich's wing with the stork's feathers, the Vulgate states positively that the ostrich's wing resembles the wings of the heron (herodii) and the hawk.
- **39:14** — The Vulgate shifts to the second person singular ("will you perhaps warm them," tu forsitan ... calefacies ea), directly addressing Job rather than describing the ostrich warming her eggs.
- **39:19** — The Vulgate renders the rare Hebrew word ra'mah (often understood as "mane" or "thunder") as "neighing" (hinnitum).
- **39:26** — Connecting the Hebrew verb with the noun for feather or pinion, the Vulgate translates it as "grows feathers" (plumescit) rather than "soars."
- **39:31** — Native Vulgate places Saints 40:1-5 at 39:31-35, Saints 40:6-32 + 41:1 at 40:1-28, and Saints 41:2-26 at 41:1-25; keep native Vulgate numbering in the Latin edition and map cleanly to Saints.Bible parallel slots.
