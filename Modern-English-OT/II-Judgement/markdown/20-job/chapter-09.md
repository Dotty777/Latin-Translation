## Text

**9:1** And Job answered and said:

**9:2** "Truly I know that it is so, and that a man is not justified when compared with God.

**9:3** If he wishes to contend with Him, he will not be able to answer Him one for a thousand.

**9:4** He is wise in heart and strong in might; who has resisted Him and had peace?

**9:5** He who moved the mountains, and those whom He overturned in His fury did not know.

**9:6** He who shakes the earth from its place, and its pillars are shaken.

**9:7** He who commands the sun, and it does not rise, and shuts up the stars as under a seal.

**9:8** He who alone stretches out the heavens, and treads upon the waves of the sea.

**9:9** He who makes Arcturus and Orion, and the Hyades and the inner regions of the south.

**9:10** He who does great and incomprehensible things, and wonders of which there is no number.

**9:11** If He comes to me, I will not see Him; if He departs, I will not understand.

**9:12** If He suddenly questions, who will answer Him? Or who can say, 'Why do You do so?'

**9:13** God, whose wrath no one can resist, and beneath whom those who bear the world bow down.

**9:14** How great am I, then, that I should answer Him and speak with Him in my words?

**9:15** Even if I have anything just, I will not answer, but I will implore my Judge.

**9:16** And when He listens to me as I call, I do not believe that He has heard my voice.

**9:17** For in a whirlwind He will crush me, and He will multiply my wounds, even without cause.

**9:18** He does not allow my spirit to rest, and He fills me with bitterness.

**9:19** If strength is sought, He is the mightiest; if equity of judgment, no one dares to bear witness for me.

**9:20** If I wish to justify myself, my mouth will condemn me; if I show myself innocent, it will prove me perverse.

**9:21** Even if I am guileless, my soul will not know this very thing, and I will be weary of my life.

**9:22** There is one thing that I have spoken: He Himself consumes both the innocent and the ungodly.

**9:23** If He scourges, let Him kill once for all, and let Him not laugh at the punishments of the innocent.

**9:24** The earth has been given into the hands of the ungodly; He covers the face of its judges. And if it is not He, who then is it?

**9:25** My days were swifter than a runner; they fled, and they did not see good.

**9:26** They passed by like ships carrying fruit, like an eagle flying to its prey.

**9:27** When I say, 'I will by no means speak like this,' I change my countenance, and I am tormented with pain.

**9:28** I dreaded all my works, knowing that You would not spare the offender.

**9:29** Yet if even so I am ungodly, why have I labored in vain?

**9:30** If I wash myself as with waters of snow, and my hands shine as though completely clean,

**9:31** yet You will plunge me in filth, and my garments will abhor me.

**9:32** For it is not a man who is like me that I will answer, nor one who can be heard with me on equal terms in judgment.

**9:33** There is no one who is able to reprove each of the two, and to lay his hand upon both.

**9:34** Let Him remove His rod from me, and let His dread not terrify me.

**9:35** I will speak, and I will not fear Him; for I cannot answer while afraid."

## Translator Notes

- **9:9** — Vulgate names the constellations Arcturum et Oriona, et Hyadas ("Arcturus and Orion, and the Hyades"), where the Hebrew text names the Bear, Orion, and the Pleiades.
- **9:13** — Vulgate reads qui portant orbem ("those who bear the world"), a distinctive rendering where the Hebrew text refers to "the helpers of Rahab."
- **9:23** — Vulgate frames the verse as a jussive plea about God's scourging ("If He scourges, let Him kill once for all, and let Him not laugh at the punishments of the innocent"), whereas the Hebrew text describes a sudden scourge and mockery at the despair of the innocent.
- **9:26** — Vulgate reads naves poma portantes ("ships carrying fruit"), a well-known Vulgate reading where the Hebrew text speaks of "ships of reed" (papyrus skiffs).
- **9:35** — Vulgate reads neque enim possum metuens respondere ("for I cannot answer while afraid"), a distinct reading where the Hebrew text says "for I am not so in myself."
