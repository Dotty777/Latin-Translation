## Text

**37:1** "At this my heart has shuddered and has been moved out of its place.

**37:2** Listen attentively in the terror of His voice, and to the sound proceeding from His mouth.

**37:3** Beneath all the heavens He Himself observes, and His light is over the ends of the earth.

**37:4** After Him a sound will roar; He will thunder with the voice of His greatness, and it will not be traced out when His voice is heard.

**37:5** God will thunder marvelously with His voice, He who does great and unsearchable things;

**37:6** who commands the snow to descend to the earth, and the winter rains, and the shower of His strength;

**37:7** who sets a seal in the hand of all people, so that each one may know his own works.

**37:8** The wild beast will enter a hiding place and will remain in its den.

**37:9** From the inner recesses the storm will come forth, and from Arcturus the cold.

**37:10** When God blows, frost congeals, and again very wide waters are poured out.

**37:11** Grain longs for clouds, and the clouds scatter their light.

**37:12** They move around in a circuit wherever the will of Him who governs leads them, for everything that He commands them over the face of the world:

**37:13** whether in one tribe, or in His land, or in whatever place of His mercy He commands them to be found.

**37:14** Listen to these things, Job; stand still and consider the marvels of God.

**37:15** Do you know when God commanded the rains to show the light of His clouds?

**37:16** Do you know the great paths of the clouds and perfect knowledge?

**37:17** Are your garments not warm when the earth is blown over by the south wind?

**37:18** Perhaps you fashioned the heavens with Him, which are most solid, as if cast of bronze.

**37:19** Show us what we should say to Him, for indeed we are wrapped in darkness.

**37:20** Who will tell Him what I speak? Even if a man speaks, he will be devoured.

**37:21** But now they do not see the light: suddenly the air will be thickened into clouds, and a passing wind will drive them away.

**37:22** From the north gold comes, and to God is fearful praise.

**37:23** We cannot worthily find Him; He is great in strength, and in judgment, and in righteousness, and He cannot be fully declared.

**37:24** Therefore men will fear Him, and all who seem to themselves to be wise will not dare to gaze upon Him."

## Translator Notes

- **37:7** — Latin 'opera sua' inside the purpose clause with subject 'singuli' most directly means "each one's own works," though traditional Vulgate commentators also note the sense "His [God's] works" (matching the Hebrew).
- **37:9** — The Vulgate retains the proper name 'Arcturus' (the northern star/constellation) where the Hebrew has 'mezarim' ("scattering winds" or north winds).
- **37:11** — Latin 'Frumentum desiderat nubes' ("Grain longs for clouds") is a distinctly Vulgate reading, interpreting Hebrew 'beri' ("moisture") as related to 'bar' ("grain").
- **37:13** — Latin 'in una tribu' ("in one tribe") renders Hebrew 'shevet' in its sense "tribe" rather than "rod [of correction]."
- **37:23** — Latin 'Digne' ("worthily") renders the Hebrew divine title 'Shaddai' ("the Almighty") by connecting it with sufficiency/worthiness; similarly in v. 22, 'formidolosa laudatio' ("fearful praise") corresponds to Hebrew "awesome majesty."
