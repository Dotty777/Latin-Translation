## Text

**2:1** Now it came about, when on a certain day the sons of God had come and were standing before the Lord, and Satan had also come among them and was standing in His sight,

**2:2** that the Lord said to Satan, "Where do you come from?" Answering, he said, "I have traveled around the earth and walked throughout it."

**2:3** And the Lord said to Satan, "Have you observed My servant Job, that there is no one like him on the earth, a sincere and upright man, fearing God and departing from evil, and still retaining innocence? Yet you have moved Me against him to afflict him for nothing."

**2:4** Answering Him, Satan said, "Skin for skin, and a man will give everything that he has for his life;

**2:5** otherwise, put out Your hand and touch his bone and flesh, and then You will see that he will bless You to Your face."

**2:6** Therefore the Lord said to Satan, "Look, he is in your hand; yet preserve his life."

**2:7** So Satan went out from the presence of the Lord and struck Job with a most severe sore, from the sole of his foot all the way to the crown of his head;

**2:8** and he scraped away the discharge with a potsherd, sitting on a dunghill.

**2:9** Now his wife said to him, "Do you still remain in your sincerity? Bless God, and die."

**2:10** He said to her, "You have spoken like one of the foolish women. If we have received good things from the hand of God, why should we not receive evil things?" In all these things Job did not sin with his lips.

**2:11** Now when the three friends of Job heard of all the evil that had happened to him, they came, each one from his own place: Eliphaz the Temanite, and Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite. For they had agreed to come together to visit him and console him.

**2:12** And when they lifted up their eyes from a distance, they did not recognize him; and crying out, they wept, and having torn their garments, they scattered dust upon their heads toward heaven.

**2:13** And they sat with him on the ground for seven days and seven nights; and no one spoke a word to him, for they saw that his pain was intense.

## Translator Notes

- **2:5** — Vulgate alioquin ("otherwise") differs from the Hebrew adversative ("but now"), and benedicat ("bless") again preserves the euphemism for "curse."
- **2:8** — Vulgate saniem ("the discharge") explicitly specifies corrupt matter from the sore, and in sterquilinio ("on a dunghill") follows the Septuagint tradition rather than the Hebrew "among the ashes."
- **2:9** — Vulgate simplicitate ("sincerity") echoes simplex in 1:1, 1:8, and 2:3, and Benedic Deo ("Bless God") retains the antiphrastic euphemism for cursing God.
