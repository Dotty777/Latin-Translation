## Text

**15:1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said:

**15:2** "Will a wise man reply like one speaking into the wind, and fill his stomach with scorching heat?

**15:3** You reprove with words one who is not equal to you, and you speak what does not profit you.

**15:4** As far as lies in you, you have nullified fear and taken away prayers before God.

**15:5** For your iniquity has instructed your mouth, and you imitate the tongue of those who blaspheme.

**15:6** Your mouth will condemn you, and not I; and your lips will answer you.

**15:7** Were you born as the first man, and formed before the hills?

**15:8** Did you hear the counsel of God, and will His wisdom be inferior to you?

**15:9** What do you know of which we are ignorant? What do you understand that we do not know?

**15:10** Both old men and aged men are among us, much older than your fathers.

**15:11** Is it a great thing that God should comfort you? But your perverse words prevent this.

**15:12** Why does your heart lift you up, and why, as though contemplating great things, do you have astonished eyes?

**15:13** Why does your spirit swell against God, so that you bring forth speeches of this kind from your mouth?

**15:14** What is man, that he should be spotless, and that one born of a woman should appear righteous?

**15:15** Look, among His holy ones no one is unchangeable, and the heavens are not clean in His sight.

**15:16** How much more abominable and useless is man, who drinks iniquity like water?

**15:17** I will show you; listen to me; what I have seen I will recount to you.

**15:18** Wise men acknowledge and do not conceal their fathers,

**15:19** to whom alone the land was given, and no foreigner passed through them.

**15:20** All his days the ungodly man is arrogant, and the number of the years of his tyranny is uncertain.

**15:21** The sound of terror is always in his ears; and when there is peace, he always suspects an ambush.

**15:22** He does not believe that he can return from darkness to light, looking around on all sides for the sword.

**15:23** When he moves to seek bread, he knows that a day of darkness is prepared in his hand.

**15:24** Tribulation will terrify him, and anguish will besiege him, like a king who is made ready for battle.

**15:25** For he has stretched out his hand against God, and has made himself strong against the Almighty.

**15:26** He has run against Him with neck held erect, and has armed himself with a fat neck.

**15:27** Fatness has covered his face, and fat hangs down from his flanks.

**15:28** He has lived in desolate cities, and in deserted houses that have been reduced to mounds.

**15:29** He will not become rich, nor will his wealth endure, nor will he send his root into the earth.

**15:30** He will not depart from darkness; a flame will wither his branches, and he will be carried away by the breath of his own mouth.

**15:31** He will not believe, deceived in vain by error, that he is to be redeemed at any price.

**15:32** Before his days are fulfilled, he will perish, and his hands will wither.

**15:33** His cluster of grapes will be harmed like a vine in its first blossom, and like an olive tree casting off its blossom.

**15:34** For the assembly of the hypocrite is barren, and fire will devour the tents of those who gladly accept bribes.

**15:35** He has conceived pain, and has given birth to iniquity, and his womb prepares deceptions."

## Translator Notes

- **15:3** — The Vulgate reads 'Arguis verbis eum qui non est aequalis tibi' ("You reprove with words one who is not equal to you," traditionally understood as God), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text speaks of arguing with unprofitable talk.
- **15:11** — The Vulgate asks whether it is a great thing for God to comfort Job and adds that his perverse words prevent this, differing sharply from the Hebrew ("Are the consolations of God too small for you, and a word dealt gently with you?").
- **15:15** — The Vulgate reads 'inter sanctos ejus nemo immutabilis' ("among His holy ones no one is unchangeable"), whereas the Hebrew reads that God puts no trust in His holy ones.
- **15:30** — Latin 'spiritu oris sui' uses the reflexive possessive 'sui', pointing grammatically to the ungodly man's own mouth (cf. 15:6), whereas many translations from Hebrew refer the breath to God's mouth.
- **15:31** — The Vulgate reads 'Non credet, frustra errore deceptus, quod aliquo pretio redimendus sit' ("He will not believe... that he is to be redeemed at any price"), whereas the Hebrew warns him not to trust in emptiness because emptiness will be his recompense.
