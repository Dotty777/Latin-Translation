## Text

**6:1** Now Job answered and said:

**6:2** "If only my sins by which I have deserved wrath, and the calamity that I endure, were weighed on a scale!

**6:3** Like the sand of the sea, this would seem heavier; for this reason my words too are full of pain,

**6:4** because the arrows of the Lord are in me, whose indignation drinks up my spirit, and the terrors of the Lord wage war against me.

**6:5** Will a wild donkey bray when it has grass? Or will an ox bellow when it stands before a full manger?

**6:6** Or can something tasteless be eaten that is not seasoned with salt? Or can anyone taste what, once tasted, brings death?

**6:7** The things that my soul previously refused to touch, now, because of distress, are my food.

**6:8** Who would grant that my request might come, and that God would give me what I await?

**6:9** And that He who began would Himself crush me, release His hand, and cut me off?

**6:10** And may this be my consolation, that while afflicting me with pain He would not spare, nor would I contradict the words of the Holy One.

**6:11** For what is my strength, that I should endure? Or what is my end, that I should act patiently?

**6:12** Neither is my strength the strength of stones, nor is my flesh of bronze.

**6:13** Look, there is no help for me in myself, and my close companions too have departed from me.

**6:14** The one who takes mercy away from his friend abandons the fear of the Lord.

**6:15** My brothers have passed me by, like a torrent that swiftly passes in the valleys.

**6:16** Those who fear frost, snow will rush upon them.

**6:17** At the time when they are scattered, they will perish; and when it grows hot, they will dissolve from their place.

**6:18** The paths of their steps are tangled; they will walk into emptiness and perish.

**6:19** Examine the paths of Tema, the routes of Sheba, and wait a little while.

**6:20** They were confounded because I hoped; they came right up to me as well, and were covered with shame.

**6:21** Now you have come; and now, seeing my wound, you are afraid.

**6:22** Did I say, 'Bring to me, and from your possessions give to me'?

**6:23** Or, 'Free me from the hand of the enemy, and rescue me from the hand of the strong'?

**6:24** Teach me, and I will keep silent; and if by chance I have been ignorant of anything, instruct me.

**6:25** Why have you disparaged the words of truth, when there is no one among you who can refute me?

**6:26** You compose speeches only to rebuke, and you utter words into the wind.

**6:27** You rush upon the orphan, and you strive to overthrow your friend.

**6:28** Nevertheless, finish what you have begun; lend an ear, and see whether I am lying.

**6:29** Answer, I beg you, without contention; and speaking what is just, judge.

**6:30** And you will not find iniquity on my tongue, nor will folly resound in my throat."

## Translator Notes

- **6:2** — The Vulgate reads peccata mea quibus iram merui ("my sins by which I have deserved wrath"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "my vexation" without mention of deserving wrath.
- **6:3** — In the Vulgate, feminine singular haec ("this") refers back to calamitas ("calamity"), comparing its weight on the scale to the sand of the sea, whereas the Hebrew states that it would be heavier than the sand of the seas.
- **6:4** — The Vulgate reads quarum indignatio ebibit spiritum meum ("whose indignation drinks up my spirit"), making the arrows' indignation the subject, whereas the Hebrew has "my spirit drinks their poison."
- **6:6** — The Vulgate renders the difficult Hebrew phrase (possibly "egg white" or "juice of the purslane") as quod gustatum affert mortem ("what, once tasted, brings death").
- **6:19** — In 6:19-20 the Vulgate uses second-person imperatives ("Examine... wait") and first-person singular ("because I hoped"; "right up to me"), whereas the Hebrew uses third-person indicatives describing the caravans looking and trusting.
