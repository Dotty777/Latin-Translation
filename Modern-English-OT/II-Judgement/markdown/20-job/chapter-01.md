## Text

**1:1** There was a man in the land of Uz named Job; and that man was sincere and upright, fearing God and departing from evil.

**1:2** And to him were born seven sons and three daughters.

**1:3** And his possessions were seven thousand sheep and three thousand camels, also five hundred yoke of oxen and five hundred female donkeys, and an exceedingly large household; and that man was great among all those of the east.

**1:4** And his sons would go and hold a banquet from house to house, each one on his own day. And sending word, they would invite their three sisters so that they might eat and drink with them.

**1:5** And when the days of the banquet had passed in their cycle, Job would send to them and sanctify them; and rising at daybreak, he would offer burnt offerings for each one. For he would say, "Perhaps my sons have sinned and blessed God in their hearts." Thus Job did every day.

**1:6** Now one day, when the sons of God had come to stand in the presence of the Lord, Satan too was there among them.

**1:7** To him the Lord said, "Where do you come from?" Answering, he said, "I have traveled around the earth and walked throughout it."

**1:8** And the Lord said to him, "Have you observed My servant Job, that there is no one like him on the earth, a sincere and upright man, fearing God and departing from evil?"

**1:9** Answering Him, Satan said, "Does Job fear God for nothing?

**1:10** Have You not fenced around him and his house and all his wealth on every side? You have blessed the works of his hands, and his possessions have increased in the land.

**1:11** But stretch out Your hand a little and touch everything that he possesses, and he will certainly bless You to Your face."

**1:12** Therefore the Lord said to Satan, "Look, everything that he has is in your hand; only do not stretch out your hand against him." And Satan went out from the presence of the Lord.

**1:13** Now on a certain day, while his sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their firstborn brother,

**1:14** a messenger came to Job, saying, "The oxen were plowing, and the female donkeys were grazing beside them;

**1:15** and the Sabeans rushed in and carried off everything, and struck down the servants with the sword; and I alone escaped to report to you."

**1:16** And while he was still speaking, another came and said, "The fire of God fell from heaven, touched the sheep and the servants, and consumed them; and I alone fled away to report to you."

**1:17** And while he too was still speaking, another came and said, "The Chaldeans formed three bands, and raided the camels and carried them off, and also struck down the servants with the sword; and I alone fled to report to you."

**1:18** He was still speaking, and look, another entered and said, "While your sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their firstborn brother,

**1:19** suddenly a violent wind rushed in from the region of the desert and shook the four corners of the house; and collapsing, it crushed your children, and they died; and I alone fled away to report to you."

**1:20** Then Job rose and tore his garments; and having shaved his head, he fell to the ground and worshiped,

**1:21** and said, "Naked I came out from the womb of my mother, and naked I will return there. The Lord gave, the Lord took away; just as it pleased the Lord, so it has been done. May the name of the Lord be blessed."

**1:22** In all these things Job did not sin with his lips, nor did he speak anything foolish against God.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Vulgate simplex (lit. "simple, single-hearted, guileless") renders Hebrew tam ("blameless, complete") and corresponds to simplicitate ("sincerity") in 2:9.
- **1:5** — Vulgate benedixerint (lit. "blessed," recurring in 1:11, 2:5, and 2:9) preserves the Hebrew antiphrastic euphemism substituting "bless" for "curse" to avoid speaking a curse against God.
- **1:11** — Vulgate nisi ... benedixerit (lit. "unless he blesses") renders the Hebrew emphatic oath idiom meaning "he will certainly," with benedixerit again acting as a euphemism for "curse."
- **1:21** — The clause sicut Domino placuit, ita factum est ("just as it pleased the Lord, so it has been done") is a distinctly Vulgate reading shared with the Septuagint but absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **1:22** — Vulgate labiis suis ("with his lips") appears here as well as in 2:10, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text includes the phrase only in 2:10.
