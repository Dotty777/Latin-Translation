## Text

**24:1** Times are not hidden from the Almighty, yet those who know Him do not know His days.

**24:2** Some have moved boundary markers; they have plundered flocks and pastured them.

**24:3** They have driven away the donkey of orphans, and they have taken away the ox of a widow as a pledge.

**24:4** They have overturned the way of the poor, and they have oppressed the meek of the earth together.

**24:5** Others, like wild donkeys in the desert, go out to their work; watching for prey, they prepare bread for their children.

**24:6** They reap a field not their own, and they harvest the vineyard of the one whom they have oppressed by force.

**24:7** Taking away their garments, they send men away naked who have no covering in the cold;

**24:8** the rains of the mountains drench them, and having no shelter, they embrace the stones.

**24:9** They have done violence, plundering orphans, and they have stripped the poor populace.

**24:10** From the naked and those who go about without clothing, and from the hungry, they have taken away the heads of grain.

**24:11** Among their heaps they have rested at midday, those who, having trodden the winepresses, go thirsty.

**24:12** From the cities they have made men groan, and the soul of the wounded has cried out; and God does not allow it to go unavenged.

**24:13** They themselves have been rebels against the light; they have not known its ways, nor have they returned along its paths.

**24:14** Early in the morning the murderer rises; he kills the needy and the poor, but through the night he will be like a thief.

**24:15** The eye of the adulterer watches for thick darkness, saying, 'No eye will see me,' and he will cover his face.

**24:16** In darkness he digs through houses, just as they had agreed among themselves by day, and they have not known the light.

**24:17** If the dawn suddenly appears, they regard it as the shadow of death, and thus they walk in darkness as though in light.

**24:18** He is swift upon the face of the water; may his portion on the earth be cursed, and let him not walk along the way of the vineyards.

**24:19** Let him pass from the snow waters to excessive heat, and his sin all the way to the underworld.

**24:20** Let mercy forget him; his sweetness is worms; let him not be in remembrance, but let him be crushed like an unfruitful tree.

**24:21** For he has fed the barren woman who does not give birth, and to the widow he has done no good.

**24:22** He has dragged down the strong in his strength, and when he stands, he will not trust his own life.

**24:23** God has given him a place for repentance, and he abuses it for pride; yet His eyes are upon his ways.

**24:24** They have been lifted up for a little while, and they will not endure; and they will be humbled like all things, and will be taken away, and like the tops of heads of grain they will be crushed.

**24:25** And if it is not so, who can convict me of having lied, and set my words before God?"

## Translator Notes

- **24:1** — The Vulgate frames this verse as a declarative statement rather than the rhetorical question ("Why are times not kept...") found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
- **24:12** — The Vulgate reverses the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("yet God pays no attention to wrong"), reading et Deus inultum abire non patitur ("and God does not allow it to go unavenged").
- **24:19** — The Vulgate casts this verse as a curse on the wicked man ("Let him pass...") and renders inferos ("the underworld," the realm of the dead), differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text's comparison of drought and Sheol. In v. 20, the Vulgate likewise reads misericordia ("mercy") where the Hebrew has "the womb."
- **24:21** — The Vulgate renders Pavit ("has fed"), reflecting the Hebrew root for "pasture/feed" rather than the homonymous root meaning "harm/mistreat."
- **24:23** — The Vulgate reads locum poenitentiae ("a place for repentance") abused for pride, differing from the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("gives them security"). In v. 25, the Vulgate likewise reads ante Deum ("before God") where the Hebrew has "to nothing."
