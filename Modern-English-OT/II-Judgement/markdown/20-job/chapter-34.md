## Text

**34:1** And so Elihu, speaking out, also said these things:

**34:2** "Hear my words, you who are wise, and listen to me, you who are learned.

**34:3** For the ear tests words, and the throat discerns foods by taste.

**34:4** Let us choose judgment for ourselves, and among ourselves let us see what is better.

**34:5** For Job has said, 'I am righteous, and God has subverted my justice.

**34:6** For there is falsehood in judging me; my arrow is violent without any sin.'

**34:7** What man is like Job, who drinks derision like water,

**34:8** who goes with those who work iniquity and walks with wicked men?

**34:9** For he has said, 'A man will not please God, even if he runs with Him.'

**34:10** Therefore, prudent men, hear me: may wickedness be far from God, and iniquity from the Almighty.

**34:11** For He will repay a man's work to him, and according to the ways of each one He will recompense them.

**34:12** For truly God will not condemn without cause, nor will the Almighty subvert justice.

**34:13** Whom else has He appointed over the earth? Or whom has He placed over the world that He fashioned?

**34:14** If He directs His heart toward him, He will draw his spirit and breath to Himself.

**34:15** All flesh will fail together, and man will return to ashes.

**34:16** Therefore, if you have understanding, hear what is said, and listen to the voice of my speech:

**34:17** Can one who does not love justice be healed? And how do you condemn Him who is righteous to such an extent?

**34:18** He who says to a king, 'Apostate,' who calls leaders wicked,

**34:19** who does not show partiality to princes, nor did He regard the tyrant when he contended against the poor man; for all of them are the work of His hands.

**34:20** Suddenly they will die, and in the middle of the night the peoples will be thrown into turmoil; and they will pass away, and they will remove the violent one without a hand.

**34:21** For His eyes are on the ways of men, and He observes all their steps.

**34:22** There is no darkness, and there is no shadow of death, where those who work iniquity can be hidden;

**34:23** for it is no longer in a man's power to come to God for judgment.

**34:24** He will crush many, and countless ones, and He will make others stand in their place.

**34:25** For He knows their works, and for that reason He will bring on night, and they will be crushed.

**34:26** As wicked men He struck them in the place of onlookers,

**34:27** who as if on purpose turned away from Him, and were unwilling to understand all His ways,

**34:28** so that they caused the cry of the needy one to come to Him, and He would hear the voice of the poor.

**34:29** For when He Himself grants peace, who is there who can condemn? From the moment He hides His face, who is there who can gaze upon Him, both over nations and over all men?

**34:30** He who makes a hypocritical man reign because of the sins of the people.

**34:31** Since therefore I have spoken to God, I will not restrain you either.

**34:32** If I have erred, you teach me; if I have spoken iniquity, I will add nothing further.

**34:33** Does God demand it from you because it displeased you? For you began to speak, and not I; now if you know anything better, speak.

**34:34** Let men with understanding speak to me, and let a wise man hear me.

**34:35** But Job has spoken foolishly, and his words do not ring with instruction.

**34:36** My Father, let Job be tested all the way to the end; do not leave off from the man of iniquity.

**34:37** Because he adds blasphemy on top of his sins, let him be bound among us in the meantime, and then let him challenge God to judgment with his statements."

## Translator Notes

- **34:6** — The Vulgate renders the opening clause 'there is falsehood in judging me' (In judicando enim me mendacium est), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'concerning my right I am considered a liar.' 'My arrow' (sagitta mea) idiomatically denotes the grievous wound inflicted on Job (cf. Job 6:4).
- **34:14** — The Vulgate distinguishes reflexive pronouns referring to God (cor suum, ad se) from non-reflexive pronouns referring to man (ad eum, illius).
- **34:17** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew verb meaning 'bind up' or 'govern' in its medical sense as 'be healed' (sanari), rather than 'govern.'
- **34:30** — Whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads negatively ('that a godless man should not reign, nor ensnare the people'), the Vulgate reads affirmatively that God makes a hypocritical man reign because of the sins of the people.
- **34:36** — The Vulgate translates the difficult Hebrew particle as the vocative 'My Father' (Pater mi), addressing God directly, and continues with the petition 'do not leave off from the man of iniquity' (ne desinas ab homine iniquitatis), where the Hebrew reads 'because of his answers like wicked men.'
