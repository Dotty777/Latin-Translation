## Text

**18:1** Then Bildad the Shuhite answered and said:

**18:2** "How long will you fling out words? Understand first, and thus let us speak.

**18:3** Why have we been counted as beasts and become vile before you?

**18:4** You who destroy your soul in your fury, will the earth be abandoned because of you, and will the cliffs be moved from their place?

**18:5** Will not the light of the ungodly be extinguished, nor will the flame of his fire shine?

**18:6** The light will grow dark in his tent, and the lamp that is above him will be extinguished.

**18:7** The strides of his strength will be hemmed in, and his own counsel will throw him down.

**18:8** For he has thrust his feet into a net, and he walks in its meshes.

**18:9** The sole of his foot will be caught in a snare, and thirst will blaze up against him.

**18:10** A noose for him is hidden in the ground, and a trap for him on the path.

**18:11** On every side terrors will frighten him, and they will entangle his feet.

**18:12** May his strength be weakened by hunger, and may starvation assail his ribs.

**18:13** May it devour the beauty of his skin; may firstborn death consume his arms.

**18:14** May his confidence be torn from his tent, and may destruction trample over him like a king.

**18:15** May the companions of the one who is not dwell in his tent; may sulfur be scattered in his tent.

**18:16** Below, may his roots be dried up; and above, may his harvest be crushed.

**18:17** May his memory perish from the earth, and may his name not be celebrated in the streets.

**18:18** He will expel him from light into darkness and remove him from the world.

**18:19** There will be no seed of his, nor progeny among his people, nor any remnants in his regions.

**18:20** At his day the last will be astonished, and horror will assail the first.

**18:21** These therefore are the tents of the unrighteous, and this is the place of the one who does not know God."

## Translator Notes

- **18:9** — Vulgate renders the Hebrew term for "snare" or "robber" as "sitis" ("thirst"), creating the distinct image of thirst blazing up against the ungodly.
- **18:14** — Vulgate reads "calcet super eum, quasi rex, interitus" ("may destruction trample over him like a king"), whereas the Hebrew refers to being marched before "the king of terrors."
- **18:15** — Latin "socii ejus qui non est" literally reads "the companions of the one who is not," reflecting the Hebrew phrase for "that which is not his" (strangers or nothingness).
- **18:18** — Vulgate uses third-person singular active verbs ("expellet", "transferet") without an explicit subject, traditionally understood as God, whereas the Hebrew has an indefinite plural ("they drive him" or passive "he is driven").
