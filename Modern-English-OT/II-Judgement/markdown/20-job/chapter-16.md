## Text

**16:1** Then Job answered and said:

**16:2** "I have frequently heard such things; all of you are burdensome comforters.

**16:3** Will windy words have an end? Or is anything troublesome to you if you speak?

**16:4** I also could speak things like yours, and if only your soul were in place of my soul:

**16:5** I too would comfort you with words, and I would shake my head over you;

**16:6** I would strengthen you with my mouth, and I would move my lips as though sparing you.

**16:7** But what am I to do? If I speak, my pain will not grow quiet, and if I remain silent, it will not depart from me.

**16:8** But now my pain has crushed me, and all my limbs have been reduced to nothing.

**16:9** My wrinkles give testimony against me, and a liar is raised up against my face, contradicting me.

**16:10** He has gathered his fury against me, and threatening me, he has gnashed his teeth against me; my enemy has stared at me with terrible eyes.

**16:11** They have opened their mouths against me, and taunting me, they have struck my cheek; they are sated with my torments.

**16:12** God has confined me with the unrighteous man, and has given me over into the hands of the ungodly.

**16:13** I, the one once wealthy, was suddenly crushed; He seized my neck, shattered me, and set me up for Himself as a target.

**16:14** He surrounded me with His spears; He severely wounded my loins; He did not spare, and He poured out my entrails on the ground.

**16:15** He slashed me with wound upon wound; He rushed against me like a giant.

**16:16** I have stitched sackcloth over my skin, and covered my flesh with ashes.

**16:17** My face has swollen from weeping, and my eyelids have grown dim.

**16:18** I have suffered these things without iniquity in my hand, while I had pure prayers to God.

**16:19** Earth, do not cover my blood, nor let my cry find a place of hiding in you;

**16:20** for look, my witness is in heaven, and He who knows me is on high.

**16:21** My friends are full of words; my eye streams toward God,

**16:22** and if only a man were judged with God just as a son of man is judged with his fellow.

**16:23** For look, the brief years pass by, and I walk a path along which I will not return.

## Translator Notes

- **16:5** — Restored native Clementine Vulgate verse 16:5 (parallel to the second half of Saints/Hebrew 16:4); 'shake my head' renders Latin 'moverem caput meum' (lit. 'move my head').
- **16:6** — Native Vulgate 16:6 corresponds to Saints/Hebrew 16:5; the preceding clause on shaking the head now stands in restored native Vulgate 16:5 (parallel to Saints/Hebrew 16:4b). Native Vulgate Job 16:6-23 runs one verse ahead of Saints/Hebrew 16:5-22 because native Vulgate 16:5 occupies its own verse slot.
- **16:8** — The Vulgate attributes Job's distress to 'dolor meus' ("my pain") and reads 'omnes artus mei' ("all my limbs"), whereas the Hebrew speaks of God wearing Job out and desolating all his company.
- **16:9** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew 'kachashi' ("my leanness") as 'falsiloquus' ("a liar"), which serves as the grammatical subject of the hostile actions in native Vulgate 16:10 ('hostis meus', "my enemy"; Saints 16:9) before God is introduced in native Vulgate 16:12 (Saints 16:11).
- **16:16** — The Vulgate reads 'operui cinere carnem meam' ("covered my flesh with ashes"), whereas the Hebrew has "laid my horn in the dust."
- **16:22** — The Vulgate uses passive verbs ('judicaretur ... judicatur', "were judged ... is judged") for Job's desire for a fair hearing with God, whereas the Hebrew uses an active form ("that one might argue for a man with God").
