## Text

**8:1** Now Bildad the Shuhite answered and said:

**8:2** "How long will you speak such things, and will the words of your mouth be a manifold wind?

**8:3** Does God overthrow judgment, or does the Almighty subvert what is just?

**8:4** Even if your children sinned against Him, and He left them in the hand of their own iniquity,

**8:5** yet if you rise up at daybreak to God and implore the Almighty;

**8:6** if you walk pure and upright, immediately He will awaken for you and make peaceful the dwelling place of your righteousness,

**8:7** so much so that if your former things were small, your latter things too will be multiplied exceedingly.

**8:8** For inquire of the former generation, and diligently search out the memory of the fathers

**8:9** (for indeed we are of yesterday and do not know, because our days upon the earth are like a shadow),

**8:10** and they themselves will teach you, speak to you, and from their heart bring forth sayings.

**8:11** Can a rush live without moisture, or sedge grow without water?

**8:12** While it is still in bloom and is not plucked by hand, it withers before all plants.

**8:13** Such are the ways of all who forget God, and the hope of the hypocrite will perish.

**8:14** His folly will not please him, and his confidence is like a web of spiders.

**8:15** He will lean upon his house, and it will not stand; he will prop it up, and it will not rise.

**8:16** He appears moist before the sun comes, and at its rising his shoot will go forth.

**8:17** Upon a heap of rocks his roots will grow dense, and among the stones he will dwell.

**8:18** If one swallows him up from his place, it will deny him and say, 'I do not know you.'

**8:19** For this is the joy of his way, that from the earth others may sprout again.

**8:20** God will not cast away the guileless one, nor extend a hand to the wicked,

**8:21** until your mouth is filled with laughter, and your lips with jubilation.

**8:22** Those who hate you will be clothed with shame, and the tent of the ungodly will not endure."

## Translator Notes

- **8:5** — Vulgate reads diluculo consurrexeris ("rise up at daybreak"), reflecting Jerome's literal etymological rendering of the Hebrew verb for seeking earnestly (related to the noun for dawn).
- **8:8** — Vulgate reads patrum memoriam ("the memory of the fathers"), whereas the Hebrew text speaks of what their fathers have searched out.
- **8:14** — Vulgate reads Non ei placebit vecordia sua ("His folly will not please him"), reflecting a distinct Latin reading where the Hebrew text speaks of his confidence being cut off or fragile.
- **8:16** — The Clementine Vulgate reads in ortu suo ("at its rising," from fourth-declension ortus), rather than in horto suo ("in his garden," corresponding to the Hebrew text).
- **8:18** — The Latin subject of absorbuerit ("swallows up") is unexpressed (variously understood as an indefinite agent, God, or the sun from v. 16), and Non novi te ("I do not know you") differs from Hebrew "I have not seen you."
