## Text

**3:1** After these things Job opened his mouth and cursed his day,

**3:2** and spoke:

**3:3** "May the day perish on which I was born, and the night in which it was said, 'A man has been conceived.'

**3:4** May that day be turned into darkness; may God not seek it from above, and may it not be illuminated with light.

**3:5** May darkness and the shadow of death obscure it; may dense gloom seize it, and may it be wrapped in bitterness.

**3:6** May a dark whirlwind possess that night; may it not be counted among the days of the year, nor numbered among the months.

**3:7** May that night be solitary, and not worthy of praise.

**3:8** May those curse it who curse the day, who are ready to rouse Leviathan.

**3:9** May the stars be darkened by its gloom; may it wait for light and not see it, nor the appearing of the rising dawn;

**3:10** because it did not shut the doors of the womb that carried me, nor take away evils from my eyes.

**3:11** Why did I not die in the womb? Having come out from the womb, why did I not perish at once?

**3:12** Why was I received upon knees? Why was I nursed at breasts?

**3:13** For now, sleeping, I would be silent, and in my sleep I would rest

**3:14** with kings and consuls of the earth, who build desolate places for themselves,

**3:15** or with princes who possess gold and fill their houses with silver;

**3:16** or like a hidden miscarried child I would not exist, or like those who were conceived and did not see light.

**3:17** There the wicked have ceased from turmoil, and there those exhausted in strength have rested.

**3:18** And those once bound, together without trouble, have not heard the voice of the taskmaster.

**3:19** The small and the great are there, and the servant is free from his master.

**3:20** Why has light been given to the wretched, and life to those who are in bitterness of soul,

**3:21** who wait for death, and it does not come, like those digging for a treasure,

**3:22** and who rejoice exceedingly when they have found the grave?

**3:23** To a man whose way is hidden, and whom God has surrounded with darkness?

**3:24** Before I eat, I sigh; and just like flooding waters, so is my roaring;

**3:25** because the fear that I was fearing has come to me, and what I dreaded has happened.

**3:26** Did I not hold back? Did I not keep silent? Did I not remain at rest? And indignation came upon me."

## Translator Notes

- **3:5** — Vulgate involvatur amaritudine ("be wrapped in bitterness") reflects Jerome's reading of the rare Hebrew term for blackness or gloom from the root meaning "bitter."
- **3:6** — Vulgate tenebrosus turbo ("a dark whirlwind") renders the Hebrew word for thick darkness as a dark storm.
- **3:14** — Vulgate consulibus ("consuls") renders Hebrew "counselors" using a Roman civic title, and solitudines ("desolate places") translates Hebrew "ruins."
- **3:23** — Vulgate circumdedit eum Deus tenebris ("whom God has surrounded with darkness") explicitly supplies "with darkness" where the Hebrew reads "hedged him in."
- **3:26** — Vulgate casts the verse as three rhetorical questions with Nonne ("Did I not...?")—including dissimulavi (lit. "concealed, held back")—followed by indignatio ("indignation"), whereas the Hebrew consists of declarative negative statements about lacking ease, quiet, and rest.
