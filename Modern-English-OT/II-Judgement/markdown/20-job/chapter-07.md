## Text

**7:1** "Warfare is the life of man on the earth, and his days are like the days of a hired worker.

**7:2** Just as a slave longs for the shade, and just as a hired worker waits for the end of his work,

**7:3** so I too have had empty months, and I have counted out toilsome nights for myself.

**7:4** If I sleep, I will say, 'When will I rise?' And again I will wait for the evening, and I will be filled with pains until darkness.

**7:5** My flesh is clothed with decay and the filth of dust; my skin has dried up and contracted.

**7:6** My days have passed more swiftly than a web is cut off by a weaver, and they have been consumed without any hope.

**7:7** Remember that my life is wind, and my eye will not return to see good things.

**7:8** Nor will the sight of man look upon me; Your eyes are on me, and I will not remain.

**7:9** Just as a cloud is consumed and passes away, so the one who descends to the underworld will not ascend.

**7:10** Nor will he return any more to his house, nor will his place recognize him any longer.

**7:11** Therefore I too will not spare my mouth; I will speak in the tribulation of my spirit; I will converse with the bitterness of my soul.

**7:12** Am I a sea, or a sea monster, that You have surrounded me with a prison?

**7:13** If I say, 'My bed will console me, and I will be relieved talking to myself on my couch,'

**7:14** You will terrify me through dreams, and through visions You will shake me with horror.

**7:15** For this reason my soul has chosen hanging, and my bones death.

**7:16** I have despaired; by no means will I live any longer; spare me, for my days are nothing.

**7:17** What is man, that You magnify him? Or why do You set Your heart toward him?

**7:18** You visit him at daybreak, and suddenly You test him.

**7:19** How long will You not spare me, nor let me go so that I may swallow my saliva?

**7:20** I have sinned; what shall I do to You, guardian of men? Why have You placed me contrary to You, and I have become burdensome to my very self?

**7:21** Why do You not take away my sin, and why do You not remove my iniquity? Look, now I will sleep in the dust, and if You seek me in the morning, I will not remain."

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The Vulgate opens with the declarative statement Militia est vita hominis super terram ("Warfare [or military service] is the life of man on the earth"), whereas the Hebrew asks a rhetorical question about man's hard service.
- **7:6** — The Vulgate reads quam a texente tela succiditur ("than a web is cut off by a weaver"), whereas the Hebrew compares the swiftness of his days to a weaver's shuttle.
- **7:9** — Latin ad inferos literally means "to those below" or "to the underworld," the Vulgate's standard rendering of Hebrew Sheol (the realm of the dead), distinct from gehenna.
- **7:15** — The Vulgate reads et mortem ossa mea ("and my bones [have chosen] death"), whereas the Hebrew reads "death rather than my bones."
- **7:20** — The Vulgate renders Peccavi as a declarative statement ("I have sinned") rather than a conditional clause ("If I have sinned"), and contrarium tibi ("contrary to You") where the Hebrew has "as a target."
