## Text

**32:1** Now these three men stopped answering Job, because he seemed righteous to himself.

**32:2** And Elihu, son of Barachel the Buzite, from the family of Ram, became angry and indignant; he was angry against Job because he said that he was righteous before God.

**32:3** Moreover, he was indignant against his friends because they had not found a reasonable answer, but had merely condemned Job.

**32:4** Therefore, Elihu waited for Job while he was speaking, because those who were speaking were older.

**32:5** But when he saw that the three had not been able to answer, he became intensely angry.

**32:6** And Elihu, son of Barachel the Buzite, answered and said, "I am younger in years, while you are older; for that reason, with my head bowed, I was afraid to declare my opinion to you.

**32:7** For I was hoping that a more advanced age would speak, and that a multitude of years would teach wisdom.

**32:8** But, as I see, there is a spirit in human beings, and the inspiration of the Almighty gives understanding.

**32:9** The long-lived are not the wise, nor do the elderly understand judgment.

**32:10** Therefore I will say, 'Listen to me; I too will show you my wisdom.'

**32:11** For I waited for your words; I listened to your prudence while you were debating with words,

**32:12** and as long as I thought you were saying something, I paid careful attention; but, as I see, there is no one among you who can refute Job and answer his words.

**32:13** Beware that you do not say, 'We have found wisdom; God has thrown him down, not man.'

**32:14** He spoke nothing to me, and for my part I will not answer him according to your words.

**32:15** They became afraid and answered no longer, and they removed speech from themselves.

**32:16** Since, therefore, I waited and they did not speak—they stood still and did not answer any further—

**32:17** I too will answer my part, and I will show my knowledge.

**32:18** For I am full of words, and the spirit within my belly constrains me.

**32:19** Look, my belly is like new wine without a vent, which bursts new flasks.

**32:20** I will speak and catch my breath a little; I will open my lips and answer.

**32:21** I will not show partiality toward a man, and I will not equate God with a human being.

**32:22** For I do not know how long I will endure, and whether after a little while my Maker may take me away."

## Translator Notes

- **32:2** — The Vulgate reads coram Deo ("before God"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "rather than God" (or "more than God").
- **32:6** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew expression for holding back with the vivid Latin idiom demisso capite ("with my head bowed").
- **32:21** — The Vulgate reads et Deum homini non æquabo ("and I will not equate God with a human being"), diverging notably from the Hebrew, which speaks of not using flattering titles for any man.
- **32:22** — The Vulgate reads Nescio enim quamdiu subsistam ("For I do not know how long I will endure"), whereas the Hebrew has "For I do not know how to flatter."
