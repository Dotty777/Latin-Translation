## Text

**16:1** And John went up from Gazara and reported to his father Simon what Cendebeus had done among their people.

**16:2** And Simon called his two older sons, Judas and John, and said to them, "I, and my brothers, and the house of my father have waged war against the enemies of Israel from youth until this day; and it has prospered in our hands to deliver Israel several times.

**16:3** Now, however, I have grown old; instead, stand in place of me and my brothers, and go out and fight for our nation; and may help from heaven be with you."

**16:4** And he chose from the country twenty thousand fighting men and horsemen; and they set out against Cendebeus, and slept in Modin.

**16:5** And they rose in the morning and went into the plain; and look, a numerous army of foot soldiers and horsemen was coming to meet them, and a torrent stream was between them.

**16:6** And he and his people moved camp facing them, and he saw the people afraid to cross the torrent; and he crossed over first, and the men saw him and crossed after him.

**16:7** And he divided the people, with the horsemen in the middle of the foot soldiers; now the cavalry of the adversaries was exceedingly numerous.

**16:8** And they sounded out with the sacred trumpets, and Cendebeus and his camp were turned to flight; and many of them fell wounded, while the rest fled into the stronghold.

**16:9** Then Judas, the brother of John, was wounded; but John pursued them until he came to Kedron, which he had built;

**16:10** and they fled as far as the towers that were in the fields of Azotus, and he burned them with fire. And two thousand men of them fell, and he returned to Judea in peace.

**16:11** And Ptolemy, the son of Abubus, had been appointed commander in the plain of Jericho, and he had much silver and gold;

**16:12** for he was the son-in-law of the high priest.

**16:13** And his heart was lifted up, and he wanted to gain possession of the country, and he plotted treachery against Simon and his sons, so that he might do away with them.

**16:14** Now Simon, traveling through the cities that were in the country of Judea and taking care of them, went down to Jericho, he himself, and his son Mattathias, and Judas, in the one hundred and seventy-seventh year, in the eleventh month; this is the month of Shebat.

**16:15** And the son of Abubus received them with treachery into the small stronghold called Dok, which he had built; and he made a great banquet for them, and hid men there.

**16:16** And when Simon and his sons had become drunk, Ptolemy rose up with his men, and they took their weapons and entered the banquet; and they killed him, and his two sons, and some of his servants;

**16:17** and he committed great deception in Israel, and repaid evil for good.

**16:18** And Ptolemy wrote these things and sent to the king, that he might send him an army for aid, and he would hand over to him the country, and their cities, and the tributes.

**16:19** And he sent others to Gazara to do away with John; and to the tribunes he sent letters, that they should come to him, and he would give them silver, and gold, and gifts.

**16:20** And he sent others to seize Jerusalem and the mountain of the temple.

**16:21** And someone running ahead reported to John in Gazara that his father and his brothers had perished, and, "He has sent for you also to be killed."

**16:22** Now when he heard this, he was greatly terrified; and he seized the men who had come to destroy him and killed them, for he knew that they were seeking to destroy him.

**16:23** And the rest of the accounts of John, and of his wars, and of the noble deeds of valor in which he acted bravely, and of the building of the walls that he constructed, and of his deeds,

**16:24** look, these are written in the book of the days of his priesthood, from the time he was made high priest after his father.

## Translator Notes

- **16:3** — Vulgate reads "loco meo, et fratres mei" (plural "and my brothers," echoing 16:2), whereas the Greek has the singular genitive "and of my brother."
- **16:8** — Vulgate specifies "sacris tubis" ("sacred trumpets"), whereas the Greek simply reads "trumpets."
- **16:18** — Vulgate adds "et tributa" ("and the tributes") to Ptolemy's offer, a phrase absent from the standard Greek text.
- **16:21** — Following the Greek, the Vulgate shifts abruptly from third-person indirect report ("his father and his brothers") to second-person direct address ("you also") after the repeated conjunction "quia."
