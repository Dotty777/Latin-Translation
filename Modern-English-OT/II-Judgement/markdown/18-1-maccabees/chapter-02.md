## Text

**2:1** In those days Mattathias, son of John, son of Simeon, a priest of the sons of Joarib, arose from Jerusalem and settled on Mount Modin.

**2:2** And he had five sons: John, who bore the surname Gaddi;

**2:3** and Simon, who bore the surname Thassi;

**2:4** and Judas, who was called Maccabeus;

**2:5** and Eleazar, who bore the surname Avaran; and Jonathan, who bore the surname Apphus.

**2:6** These men saw the evils that were being done among the people of Judah and in Jerusalem.

**2:7** And Mattathias said, "Woe to me! Why was I born to see the ruin of my people and the ruin of the holy city, and to sit there while she is given into the hands of enemies?

**2:8** The holy places have come into the hand of foreigners; her temple is like a man without honor.

**2:9** The vessels of her glory have been carried away captive; her old men have been slaughtered in the streets, and her young men have fallen by the sword of enemies.

**2:10** What nation has not inherited her kingdom and has not obtained her spoils?

**2:11** All her adornment has been taken away. She who was free has become a slave woman.

**2:12** And look, our holy places, and our beauty, and our splendor have been laid waste, and the nations have defiled them.

**2:13** Why, therefore, should we live any longer?"

**2:14** And Mattathias and his sons tore their clothes, and covered themselves with sackcloth, and mourned deeply.

**2:15** And those who had been sent by King Antiochus came there to force those who had fled into the city of Modin to offer sacrifice, and to burn incense, and to depart from the law of God.

**2:16** And many of the people of Israel, consenting, came forward to them, but Mattathias and his sons stood firm.

**2:17** And those who had been sent by Antiochus answered and said to Mattathias, "You are a leader, both most distinguished and great in this city, and adorned with sons and brothers.

**2:18** Therefore, come forward first and carry out the king's decree, just as all the nations have done, and the men of Judah, and those who remained in Jerusalem; and you and your sons will be among the friends of the king, and enriched with gold and silver and many gifts."

**2:19** And Mattathias answered and said with a loud voice, "Even if all the nations obey King Antiochus, so that each one departs from the service of the law of his fathers and consents to his commands,

**2:20** I and my sons and my brothers will obey the law of our fathers.

**2:21** May God be gracious to us; it is not beneficial for us to abandon the law and the ordinances of God.

**2:22** We will not listen to the words of King Antiochus, nor will we sacrifice, transgressing the commands of our law to go by another way."

**2:23** And when he stopped speaking these words, a certain Jew came forward in the sight of all to sacrifice to idols on the altar in the city of Modin, according to the king's decree.

**2:24** And Mattathias saw it and was grieved, and his inward parts trembled, and his fury was kindled according to the judgment of the law; and leaping forward, he slaughtered him on the altar.

**2:25** And at that very time he also killed the man whom King Antiochus had sent, who was forcing them to offer sacrifice, and he destroyed the altar.

**2:26** And he was zealous for the law, just as Phinehas did to Zimri, son of Salomi.

**2:27** And Mattathias cried out with a loud voice in the city, saying, "Everyone who has zeal for the law, upholding the covenant, let him come out after me."

**2:28** And he and his sons fled into the mountains, and left behind whatever they had in the city.

**2:29** Then many who were seeking justice and righteousness went down into the desert,

**2:30** and they settled there, they themselves and their sons and their wives and their livestock, because evils had flooded over them.

**2:31** And it was reported to the king's men and to the army that was in Jerusalem, the city of David, that certain men who had broken the king's command had departed to hidden places in the desert, and that many had gone after them.

**2:32** And immediately they went to them and prepared battle against them on the Sabbath day.

**2:33** And they said to them, "Do you still resist even now? Come out, and do according to the word of King Antiochus, and you will live."

**2:34** And they said, "We will not come out, nor will we do the word of the king so as to profane the Sabbath day."

**2:35** And they launched battle against them.

**2:36** And they did not answer them, nor did they throw a stone at them, nor did they block up the hidden places,

**2:37** saying, "Let us all die in our innocence; and heaven and earth will be witnesses over us that you are destroying us unjustly."

**2:38** And they brought war upon them on the Sabbath; and they died, they themselves, and their wives, and their children, and their livestock, up to a thousand human lives.

**2:39** And Mattathias and his friends learned of it, and they grieved greatly over them.

**2:40** And each man said to his neighbor, "If we all do just as our brothers have done and do not fight against the nations for our lives and our statutes, now they will quickly destroy us from the earth."

**2:41** And they resolved on that day, saying, "Every man, whoever comes against us in war on the Sabbath day, let us fight against him, and we will not all die, as our brothers died in the hidden places."

**2:42** Then a company of Hasideans, mighty in strength from Israel, was gathered to them, everyone willingly devoted to the law;

**2:43** and all who were fleeing from the evils were added to them and became a support to them.

**2:44** And they gathered an army, and struck down sinners in their wrath and wicked men in their indignation; and the rest fled to the nations to escape.

**2:45** And Mattathias and his friends went around and destroyed the altars;

**2:46** and they circumcised the uncircumcised boys, as many as they found within the borders of Israel, even by force.

**2:47** And they pursued the sons of pride, and the work prospered in their hands;

**2:48** and they secured the law from the hands of the nations and from the hands of the kings, and they had not given a horn to the sinner.

**2:49** And the days drew near for Mattathias to die, and he said to his sons, "Now pride has grown strong, and chastisement, and a time of overthrow, and the wrath of indignation.

**2:50** Now therefore, sons, be zealous for the law, and give your lives for the covenant of your fathers,

**2:51** and remember the works of the fathers, which they did in their generations; and you will receive great glory and an eternal name.

**2:52** Was not Abraham found faithful in testing, and it was counted to him as righteousness?

**2:53** Joseph in the time of his distress kept the command, and became lord of Egypt.

**2:54** Phinehas our father, by being zealous with the zeal of God, received the covenant of an eternal priesthood.

**2:55** Joshua, when he fulfilled the word, became a leader in Israel.

**2:56** Caleb, when he testified in the assembly, received an inheritance.

**2:57** David in his mercy obtained the throne of the kingdom forever.

**2:58** Elijah, while he was zealous with zeal for the law, was received into heaven.

**2:59** Hananiah, Azariah, and Mishael, believing, were delivered from the flame.

**2:60** Daniel in his innocence was delivered from the mouth of lions.

**2:61** And so consider through generation after generation that all who hope in Him do not grow weak.

**2:62** And do not be afraid of the words of a sinful man, because his glory is dung and a worm;

**2:63** today he is exalted, and tomorrow he will not be found, because he has returned to his earth, and his thought has perished.

**2:64** You, therefore, sons, be strong and act courageously in the law, because in it you will be glorious.

**2:65** And look, Simon your brother, I know that he is a man of counsel; listen to him always, and he will be a father to you.

**2:66** And Judas Maccabeus, mighty in strength from his youth, let him be commander of the army for you, and he himself will wage the war of the people.

**2:67** And you will bring to yourselves all the doers of the law, and execute the vengeance of your people.

**2:68** Repay retribution to the nations, and pay heed to the precept of the law."

**2:69** And he blessed them, and was joined to his fathers.

**2:70** And he died in the one hundred and forty-sixth year; and he was buried by his sons in the tombs of his fathers in Modin, and all Israel mourned him with great mourning.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The Vulgate adds "Mount" (in monte Modin), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads simply "in Modein."
- **2:9** — The Vulgate reads "her old men" (senes ejus), whereas the Greek Septuagint has "her infants" (nepia).
- **2:21** — Literally "May God be gracious to us" (propitius sit nobis Deus), a biblical oath idiom equivalent to "Far be it from us."
- **2:26** — The Vulgate reads "Salomi" (matching Greek "Salom"), whereas Hebrew Numbers 25:14 calls Zimri's father "Salu."
- **2:31** — The Vulgate places "and that many had gone after them" within the report to the king's men as indirect speech, whereas the Greek Septuagint begins verse 32 with "many pursued them," referring to the king's forces.
- **2:48** — "Give a horn" (dederant cornu) is a biblical idiom meaning to yield power, strength, or victory.
- **2:54** — The Vulgate specifies "the zeal of God" (zelum Dei), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads simply "being zealous with zeal."
- **2:55** — The Latin uses "Jesus" (the Greek/Latin form of "Joshua") and calls him a "leader" (dux), whereas the Greek Septuagint calls him a "judge" (krites).
- **2:63** — The Vulgate reads "his earth" (terram suam), whereas the Greek Septuagint has "his dust" (choun autou).
