## Text

**12:1** And Jonathan saw that the time favored him, and he chose men and sent them to Rome to establish and renew friendship with them.

**12:2** And to the Spartans and to other places he sent letters according to the same form.

**12:3** And they went to Rome and entered the senate house, and they said, "Jonathan the high priest and the nation of the Jews have sent us so that we might renew friendship and alliance as before."

**12:4** And they gave them letters to those in each place, so that they would escort them into the land of Judah in peace.

**12:5** And this is the copy of the letters that Jonathan wrote to the Spartans:

**12:6** "Jonathan the high priest, and the elders of the nation, and the priests, and the rest of the people of the Jews, to the Spartan brothers, greetings.

**12:7** Long ago letters had been sent to Onias the high priest from Arius, who reigned among you, stating that you are our brothers, just as the copy that is appended below contains.

**12:8** And Onias welcomed with honor the man who had been sent, and he received the letters in which declaration was made concerning alliance and friendship.

**12:9** Although we had no need of any of these things, having for comfort the holy books that are in our hands,

**12:10** we preferred to send to you to renew brotherhood and friendship, so that we might not by chance become estranged from you; for many seasons have passed since you sent to us.

**12:11** We therefore at every time without ceasing, on festival days and on the other days on which it is proper, are mindful of you in the sacrifices that we offer and in the observances, as it is right and fitting to remember brothers.

**12:12** Therefore we rejoice over your glory.

**12:13** But many afflictions and many battles have surrounded us, and the kings who are all around us have fought against us.

**12:14** Therefore we did not wish to be burdensome to you, nor to our other allies and friends, in these battles;

**12:15** for we had help from heaven, and we ourselves were delivered, and our enemies were humbled.

**12:16** Therefore we chose Numenius the son of Antiochus and Antipater the son of Jason, and we sent them to the Romans to renew the former friendship and alliance with them.

**12:17** Therefore we instructed them to come to you also, and to greet you, and to deliver to you our letters concerning the renewal of our brotherhood.

**12:18** And now you will do well in answering us regarding these things."

**12:19** And this is the copy of the letters that he had sent to Onias:

**12:20** "Arius, king of the Spartans, to Onias the great priest, greetings.

**12:21** It has been found in a writing concerning the Spartans and the Jews that they are brothers, and that they are of the lineage of Abraham.

**12:22** And now, since we have learned these things, you do well in writing to us concerning your peace.

**12:23** Moreover, we also have written back to you: 'Our livestock and our possessions are yours, and yours are ours.' Therefore we have commanded that these things be reported to you."

**12:24** And Jonathan heard that the commanders of Demetrius had returned with a great army, more than before, to fight against him.

**12:25** And he went out from Jerusalem and met them in the region of Hamath, for he had not given them opportunity to enter his region.

**12:26** And he sent spies into their camp, and when they returned, they reported that they were planning to come upon them by night.

**12:27** Now when the sun had set, Jonathan commanded his men to keep watch and to be under arms, ready for battle all night, and he stationed guards all around the camp.

**12:28** And the adversaries heard that Jonathan with his men was ready for war, and they feared and were terrified in their heart, and they lit fires in their camp.

**12:29** But Jonathan and those who were with him did not know until morning, and they saw the lights burning.

**12:30** And Jonathan pursued them, and he did not overtake them, for they had crossed the Eleutherus River.

**12:31** And Jonathan turned aside against the Arabs who are called Zabadeans, and he struck them and took their spoils.

**12:32** And he broke camp and came to Damascus, and he traveled through all that region.

**12:33** Now Simon went out and came as far as Ashkelon and to the nearby fortresses, and he turned aside to Joppa and occupied it

**12:34** (for he had heard that they wished to hand over the fortress to the party of Demetrius), and he stationed guards there to guard it.

**12:35** And Jonathan returned and called together the elders of the people, and he planned with them to build fortresses in Judea,

**12:36** and to build walls in Jerusalem, and to raise a great height between the citadel and the city, so that he might separate it from the city, so that it might be by itself, and so that they might neither buy nor sell.

**12:37** And they gathered together to build the city; and the wall that was above the brook on the east collapsed, and he repaired it, the one that is called Caphetetha.

**12:38** And Simon built Adiada in the Shephelah, and fortified it, and set up gates and bars.

**12:39** And when Trypho had planned to reign over Asia, and to take up the diadem, and to stretch out his hand against King Antiochus,

**12:40** fearing that perhaps Jonathan would not permit him, but would fight against him, he sought to seize him and kill him. And rising up, he went to Beth-shan.

**12:41** And Jonathan went out to meet him with forty thousand chosen men for battle, and he came to Beth-shan.

**12:42** And Trypho saw that Jonathan had come with a great army to stretch out his hands against him; he was afraid,

**12:43** and he received him with honor, and commended him to all his friends, and gave him gifts; and he commanded his armies to obey him just as himself.

**12:44** And he said to Jonathan, "Why have you troubled the entire people when we have no war?

**12:45** And now send them back to their homes, but choose for yourself a few men to be with you, and come with me to Ptolemais, and I will hand it over to you, and the remaining fortresses, and the army, and all those in charge of affairs; and I will turn back and depart, for this is why I came."

**12:46** And he trusted him and did as he said; and he dismissed the army, and they went away into the land of Judah.

**12:47** However, he kept three thousand men with himself, of whom he sent back two thousand into Galilee, while one thousand came with him.

**12:48** Now when Jonathan entered Ptolemais, the people of Ptolemais shut the gates of the city and seized him, and all who had entered with him they killed with the sword.

**12:49** And Trypho sent an army and horsemen into Galilee and into the great plain to destroy all the companions of Jonathan.

**12:50** But when they learned that Jonathan had been seized and had perished, and all who were with him, they encouraged one another and went out ready for battle.

**12:51** And those who had pursued them, seeing that it was a matter of life for them, turned back;

**12:52** but they all came in peace into the land of Judah. And they lamented Jonathan and those who had been with him greatly, and Israel mourned with great mourning.

**12:53** And all the nations that were around them sought to crush them, for they said,

**12:54** "They have no leader and helper; now therefore let us fight against them and remove their memory from among men."

## Translator Notes

- **12:7** — The Vulgate reads Arius (also in 12:20), referring to King Areus I of Sparta (Greek Areios, corrupted in some Greek manuscripts).
- **12:11** — Vulgate in observationibus ("in the observances") is a distinct Latin reading where Greek manuscripts read "in prayers" or "in remembrances."
- **12:32** — Latin junxit literally means "he yoked up," an elliptical military expression rendering Greek anazeuxas ("having yoked up [the pack animals]," meaning "breaking camp").
- **12:37** — Caphetetha is the Vulgate-distinct form of the wall name (Greek Chaphenatha); similarly in 12:38 the Vulgate reads Adiada (Greek Adida, Hebrew Hadid).
- **12:42** — The Vulgate construes ut extenderet in eum manus ("to stretch out his hands against him") as the apparent purpose of Jonathan's advance that frightened Trypho, whereas the Greek reads that Trypho "feared to stretch out his hands against him."
- **12:53** — Native Clementine Vulgate divides Saints.Bible 12:53 across 12:53 and 12:54.
