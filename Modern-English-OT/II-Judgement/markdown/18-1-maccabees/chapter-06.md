## Text

**6:1** And King Antiochus was traveling through the upper regions, and he heard that there was a city, Elymais, in Persia, most renowned and abounding in silver and gold,

**6:2** and an exceedingly wealthy temple in it, and that golden coverings, breastplates, and shields were there, which Alexander, son of Philip, the Macedonian king, who reigned first in Greece, had left behind.

**6:3** And he came and sought to capture the city and plunder it, and he could not, because the matter became known to those who were in the city.

**6:4** And they rose up for battle, and he fled from there, and departed with great grief, and returned to Babylonia.

**6:5** And someone came to report to him in Persia that the armies which were in the land of Judah were put to flight,

**6:6** and that Lysias went out with a strong force at first, and was put to flight before the face of the Jews, and they grew strong in weapons, in might, and in many spoils, which they took from the armies that they cut down,

**6:7** and that they tore down the abomination which he had built upon the altar that was in Jerusalem, and surrounded the sanctuary with high walls, as before, and also Beth-zur, their city.

**6:8** And it happened that when the king heard these words, he was terrified and greatly shaken; and he fell onto his bed and fell into sickness from grief, because it did not happen for him as he had planned.

**6:9** And he was there for many days, because great grief was renewed in him, and he thought that he was dying.

**6:10** And he called all his friends and said to them, "Sleep has departed from my eyes, and I have fallen and collapsed in heart from anxiety;

**6:11** and I said in my heart, 'Into what great tribulation I have come, and into what waves of grief, in which I now am, I who was cheerful and beloved in my power!'

**6:12** But now I remember the evils that I did in Jerusalem, from which I also took away all the golden and silver spoils that were in it, and I sent to remove the inhabitants of Judea without cause.

**6:13** Therefore I know that for this reason these evils have found me; and look, I am perishing from great grief in a foreign land."

**6:14** And he called Philip, one of his friends, and set him over his entire kingdom,

**6:15** and gave him the diadem, his robe, and the ring, so that he might bring his son Antiochus, and raise him, and that he might reign.

**6:16** And King Antiochus died there in the one hundred and forty-ninth year.

**6:17** And Lysias learned that the king had died, and he appointed his son Antiochus, whom he had raised as a youth, to reign; and he called his name Eupator.

**6:18** And those who were in the citadel had shut Israel in around the holy places, and were always seeking harm for them and support for the Gentiles.

**6:19** And Judas planned to destroy them, and he summoned the entire people so that they might besiege them.

**6:20** And they gathered together and besieged them in the one hundred and fiftieth year, and they made catapults and siege engines.

**6:21** And some of those who were being besieged went out, and some ungodly men from Israel joined themselves to them,

**6:22** and they went to the king and said, "How long will you not execute judgment and avenge our brothers?

**6:23** We resolved to serve your father, and to walk in his precepts, and to obey his edicts;

**6:24** and because of this the sons of our people were estranging themselves from us, and whoever of us was found was killed, and our inheritances were plundered.

**6:25** And they have stretched out their hand not only against us, but also against all our borders;

**6:26** and look, today they have encamped against the citadel of Jerusalem to seize it, and they have fortified the fortress of Beth-zur;

**6:27** and unless you forestall them quickly, they will do greater things than these, and you will not be able to restrain them."

**6:28** And the king became angry when he heard these things, and he summoned all his friends, and the commanders of his army, and those who were over the horsemen;

**6:29** and also from other kingdoms and from the islands of the sea, mercenary armies came to him.

**6:30** And the number of his army was one hundred thousand foot soldiers, and twenty thousand horsemen, and thirty-two elephants, trained for battle.

**6:31** And they came through Idumea, and encamped against Beth-zur, and fought for many days; and they made siege engines, and they came out and burned them with fire, and fought bravely.

**6:32** And Judas withdrew from the citadel, and moved camp to Beth-zechariah, opposite the camp of the king.

**6:33** And the king rose before dawn, and urged the armies into a charge toward the road to Beth-zechariah; and the armies prepared themselves for battle, and sounded the trumpets;

**6:34** and to the elephants they showed the blood of grapes and mulberries, to incite them for battle;

**6:35** and they divided the beasts among the legions, and beside each elephant stood a thousand men in linked coats of mail, and bronze helmets were on their heads; and five hundred selected horsemen were assigned to each beast.

**6:36** These were there ahead of time wherever the beast was, and wherever it went, they went, and they did not depart from it.

**6:37** And also sturdy wooden towers were upon them, giving protection upon each beast, and upon them were engines, and upon each one were thirty-two men of valor who fought from above, and the Indian master of the beast.

**6:38** And he stationed the rest of the cavalry on this side and on that in two divisions, to rouse the army with trumpets and to press forward those packed closely together in his legions.

**6:39** And when the sun shone upon the golden and bronze shields, the mountains gleamed from them, and they gleamed like torches of fire.

**6:40** And part of the king's army was divided across the high mountains, and another part across the low places; and they marched cautiously and in order.

**6:41** And all the inhabitants of the land were shaken at the sound of the multitude, and the marching of the throng, and the clashing of weapons; for the army was exceedingly great and strong.

**6:42** And Judas and his army drew near for battle, and six hundred men from the king's army fell.

**6:43** And Eleazar, son of Saura, saw one of the beasts armored with the king's armor, and it towered above the other beasts, and it seemed to him that the king was on it;

**6:44** and he gave himself so that he might deliver his people and gain for himself an everlasting name.

**6:45** And he ran boldly toward it in the midst of the legion, killing on the right and on the left, and they fell before him on this side and on that.

**6:46** And he went under the feet of the elephant, and placed himself beneath it, and killed it; and it fell to the ground upon him, and he died there.

**6:47** And seeing the might of the king and the charge of his army, they turned away from them.

**6:48** Now the armies of the king went up against them to Jerusalem, and the armies of the king encamped against Judea and Mount Zion.

**6:49** And he made peace with those who were in Beth-zur; and they came out of the city, because there were no provisions for them there while they were shut in, because it was a sabbath for the land.

**6:50** And the king took Beth-zur, and stationed a garrison there to guard it.

**6:51** And he turned his camp against the place of the sanctuary for many days, and he set up there catapults, and siege engines, and missiles of fire, and engines for hurling stones, and javelins, and scorpions for shooting arrows, and slings.

**6:52** But they themselves also made siege engines against their siege engines, and they fought for many days.

**6:53** But there was no food in the city, because it was the seventh year, and those who had remained in Judea from among the Gentiles had consumed their remaining stores, which had been laid up.

**6:54** And few men remained in the holy places, because famine had overpowered them; and they were scattered, each one to his own place.

**6:55** And Lysias heard that Philip, whom King Antiochus had appointed while he was still alive to raise his son Antiochus and that he might reign,

**6:56** had returned from Persia and Media, along with the army that had gone with him, and that he was seeking to take over the affairs of the kingdom.

**6:57** He hastened to go and say to the king and to the leaders of the army, "We are failing daily, and our food is scant, and the place that we are besieging is fortified, and it falls upon us to set the kingdom in order.

**6:58** Now therefore let us give our right hands to these men, and let us make peace with them and with all their nation,

**6:59** and let us decree for them that they may walk in their laws as before; for because of their own laws, which we despised, they became angry and did all these things."

**6:60** And the word was pleasing in the sight of the king and the commanders; and he sent to them to make peace, and they accepted it.

**6:61** And the king and the commanders swore to them, and they came out of the fortress.

**6:62** And the king entered Mount Zion, and saw the fortification of the place; and he quickly broke the oath that he had sworn, and gave orders to destroy the wall all around.

**6:63** And he departed hastily, and returned to Antioch, and found Philip ruling over the city; and he fought against him and seized the city.

## Translator Notes

- **6:7** — Latin civitatem suam uses the reflexive possessive, which grammatically refers to the plural subject ("their city," that is, the Jews'), whereas the Greek reads singular "his city" (referring to King Antiochus).
- **6:12** — Vulgate reads omnia spolia aurea et argentea ("all the golden and silver spoils"), whereas the Greek has "all the vessels of silver and gold."
- **6:18** — Latin quærebant eis mala semper, et firmamentum gentium literally pairs "evils for them" and "a support [or stronghold] of the Gentiles" as objects of "were seeking," mirroring the concise syntax of the Greek.
- **6:25** — Vulgate reads in omnes fines nostros ("against all our borders"), whereas the Greek reads "against all their borders."
- **6:26** — Vulgate reads munitionem Bethsuram munierunt ("they have fortified the fortress of Beth-zur"), whereas the Greek reads "they have fortified the sanctuary and Beth-zur."
- **6:37** — Vulgate reads et super eas machinæ ("and upon them were engines"), whereas the Greek describes the wooden towers as fastened onto each beast with contrivances.
- **6:38** — Vulgate reads tubis exercitum commovere, et perurgere constipatos in legionibus ejus ("to rouse the army with trumpets and to press forward those packed closely together in his legions"), differing notably from the Greek, which speaks of shielding the army and being armored in his phalanxes.
- **6:41** — Vulgate reads omnes inhabitantes terram ("all the inhabitants of the land"), whereas the Greek reads "all who heard."
- **6:43** — Vulgate reads Eleazar filius Saura ("Eleazar, son of Saura"), a Vulgate-distinct name reflecting a Greek textual variant where the standard Greek reads "Eleazar called Avaran."
- **6:53** — Vulgate reads in civitate ("in the city") and qui remanserant ("those who had remained"), whereas the Greek reads "in the storage vessels" and "those who had been brought to safety."
