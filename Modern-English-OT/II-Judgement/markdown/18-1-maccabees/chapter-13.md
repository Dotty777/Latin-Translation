## Text

**13:1** And Simon heard that Trypho had gathered a large army to come into the land of Judah and devastate it.

**13:2** Seeing that the people were in trembling and in fear, he went up to Jerusalem and gathered the people.

**13:3** And encouraging them, he said, "You know what great things I and my brothers and my father's house have done for the laws and for the holy places, what battles and distresses we have seen.

**13:4** For the sake of these things all my brothers have perished on account of Israel, and I alone am left.

**13:5** And now may it not happen to me to spare my life in any time of tribulation, for I am not better than my brothers.

**13:6** Therefore I will avenge my nation and the holy places, and our children and wives as well, because all the nations have gathered together to crush us out of hostility."

**13:7** And the spirit of the people was kindled as soon as they heard these words.

**13:8** And they answered with a loud voice, saying, "You are our commander in place of Judas and your brother Jonathan.

**13:9** Fight our battle, and everything that you tell us we will do."

**13:10** And gathering all the fighting men, he hastened to complete all the walls of Jerusalem, and he fortified it all around.

**13:11** And he sent Jonathan the son of Absalom, and with him a new army, to Joppa; and having driven out those who were in it, he himself remained there.

**13:12** And Trypho set out from Ptolemais with a great army to come into the land of Judah, and Jonathan was with him under guard.

**13:13** But Simon encamped at Addus, facing the plain.

**13:14** And when Trypho learned that Simon had risen up in place of his brother Jonathan, and that he was about to engage in battle with him, he sent envoys to him,

**13:15** saying, "On account of the silver that your brother Jonathan owed to the king's account because of the affairs that he had, we have detained him.

**13:16** And now send one hundred talents of silver and two of his sons as hostages, so that when released he will not flee from us, and we will send him back."

**13:17** And Simon knew that he was speaking to him with deceit; yet he ordered the silver and the boys to be given, so that he would not incur great hostility from the people of Israel, who would say,

**13:18** "Because he did not send him the silver and the boys, for that reason he perished."

**13:19** And he sent the boys and one hundred talents; and he lied, and did not release Jonathan.

**13:20** And after these things Trypho came into the region to crush it; and they circled around by the road that leads to Ador, and Simon and his camp marched to every place wherever they went.

**13:21** Now those who were in the citadel sent envoys to Trypho, that he should hurry to come through the wilderness and send them provisions.

**13:22** And Trypho prepared all the cavalry to come that night; but there was very much snow, and he did not come into Gilead.

**13:23** And when he had approached Bascama, he killed Jonathan and his sons there.

**13:24** And Trypho turned back and departed to his own land.

**13:25** And Simon sent and took the bones of his brother Jonathan, and buried them in Modin, the city of his fathers.

**13:26** And all Israel lamented him with a great lamentation, and mourned him for many days.

**13:27** And Simon built over the tomb of his father and his brothers a structure high to the sight, with polished stone behind and in front.

**13:28** And he set up seven pyramids, one opposite another, for his father and mother and four brothers.

**13:29** And around these he placed great columns, and upon the columns armor for an eternal memorial, and beside the armor carved ships, so that they might be seen by all who sail the sea.

**13:30** This is the tomb that he made in Modin, to this day.

**13:31** Now Trypho, while traveling with the young king Antiochus, killed him by deceit.

**13:32** And he reigned in his place, and placed upon himself the diadem of Asia, and caused a great calamity in the land.

**13:33** And Simon built the strongholds of Judea, fortifying them with high towers and great walls and gates and bars, and he placed provisions in the fortifications.

**13:34** And Simon chose men and sent them to King Demetrius, that he might grant remission to the region, because all the deeds of Trypho had been carried out through plunder.

**13:35** And King Demetrius answered him concerning these words, and wrote a letter as follows:

**13:36** "King Demetrius to Simon, the high priest and friend of kings, and to the elders and the nation of the Jews: greetings.

**13:37** We have received the golden crown and the palm branch that you sent, and we are ready to make a great peace with you and to write to the king's officials to remit to you what we have granted.

**13:38** For whatever we have decreed stands firm for you; let the fortifications that you have built be yours.

**13:39** We also remit the oversights and sins up to this present day, and the crown that you owed; and if anything else was subject to tribute in Jerusalem, let it no longer be subject to tribute.

**13:40** And if any of you are fit to be enrolled among our men, let them be enrolled, and let there be peace between us."

**13:41** In the one hundred and seventieth year, the yoke of the nations was taken away from Israel.

**13:42** And the people of Israel began to write on documents and public records, "In the first year under Simon, the high priest, great commander, and prince of the Jews."

**13:43** In those days Simon encamped against Gaza and surrounded it with a camp; and he made siege engines and brought them up to the city, and he struck one tower and captured it.

**13:44** And those who were inside the siege engine burst out into the city, and a great commotion arose in the city.

**13:45** And those who were in the city went up with their wives and children upon the wall, with their tunics torn, and cried out with a loud voice, asking Simon that right hands be given to them,

**13:46** and they said, "Do not repay us according to our evil deeds, but according to your mercies."

**13:47** And Simon, being moved, did not make war against them; yet he drove them out of the city, and cleansed the houses in which the idols had been, and then he entered into it with hymns, blessing the Lord.

**13:48** And having cast out all uncleanness from it, he settled in it men who would practice the law; and he fortified it, and made a dwelling for himself.

**13:49** Now those who were in the citadel of Jerusalem were prevented from going out and entering the region, and from buying and selling; and they became very hungry, and many of them perished from hunger.

**13:50** And they cried out to Simon that they might receive right hands, and he gave them to them; and he drove them out from there, and cleansed the citadel from defilements.

**13:51** And they entered into it on the twenty-third day of the second month, in the one hundred and seventy-first year, with praise, and palm branches, and lyres, and cymbals, and harps, and hymns, and songs, because a great enemy had been crushed out of Israel.

**13:52** And he decreed that every year these days should be observed with gladness.

**13:53** And Simon saw his son John, that he was a valiant man of battle, and he appointed him commander of all the forces; and he lived in Gazara.

## Translator Notes

- **13:11** — The Vulgate reads exercitum novum ("a new army"), reflecting a Greek variant (kainen, "new") where the Septuagint reads hikanen ("a considerable force").
- **13:13** — The Vulgate proper name Addus corresponds to Adida in the Greek Septuagint.
- **13:22** — The Vulgate omits an intervening Greek clause ("because of the snow, and he departed and came"), yielding the distinct Latin reading that Trypho did not come into Gilead.
- **13:23** — The Vulgate reads occidit Jonathan et filios ejus illic ("killed Jonathan and his sons there"), where the Greek Septuagint reads "killed Jonathan, and he was buried there."
- **13:37** — "The palm branch" translates the Vulgate's rare transliteration bahem (from Greek baine, "palm branch"), paired here with the golden crown.
- **13:43** — The Vulgate names the city Gaza (Gazam), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads Gazara (compare Gazaris, "Gazara," in 13:53).
- **13:45** — "Right hands" literally renders Latin dextras (also in 13:50), an idiom denoting pledges of peace or terms of surrender.
