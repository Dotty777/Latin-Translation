## Text

**7:1** In the one hundred and fifty-first year, Demetrius, the son of Seleucus, went out from the city of Rome, and went up with a few men into a coastal city, and reigned there.

**7:2** And it happened that, as he entered the house of the kingdom of his fathers, the army seized Antiochus and Lysias so that they might bring them to him.

**7:3** And the matter became known to him, and he said, "Do not show me their faces."

**7:4** And the army killed them. And Demetrius sat upon the throne of his kingdom.

**7:5** And wicked and ungodly men from Israel came to him, and their leader was Alcimus, who wanted to be made priest.

**7:6** And they accused the people before the king, saying, "Judas and his brothers have destroyed all your friends, and he has scattered us from our land.

**7:7** Now therefore, send a man whom you trust, so that he may go and see all the destruction that he has done to us and to the regions of the king, and punish all his friends and their helpers."

**7:8** And the king chose from among his friends Bacchides, who ruled beyond the great river in the kingdom and was faithful to the king, and he sent him,

**7:9** so that he might see the destruction that Judas had done; and he also appointed the ungodly Alcimus to the priesthood, and commanded him to take vengeance on the sons of Israel.

**7:10** And they arose and came with a great army into the land of Judah, and they sent messengers and spoke to Judas and to his brothers with peaceful words in deceit.

**7:11** And they did not pay attention to their words, for they saw that they had come with a great army.

**7:12** And an assembly of scribes came together to Alcimus and Bacchides to seek what is just,

**7:13** and the first were the Hasideans who were among the sons of Israel, and they sought peace from them.

**7:14** For they said, "A man who is a priest from the seed of Aaron has come; he will not deceive us."

**7:15** And he spoke peaceful words with them, and swore to them, saying, "We will not bring harm upon you, nor upon your friends."

**7:16** And they trusted him; and he seized sixty men from among them and killed them in one day, according to the word that is written:

**7:17** "The flesh of Your holy ones and their blood they poured out around Jerusalem, and there was no one to bury them."

**7:18** And fear and trembling fell upon all the people, for they said, "There is no truth or justice in them, for they have transgressed the agreement and the oath that they swore."

**7:19** And Bacchides moved camp from Jerusalem and encamped at Bethzecha; and he sent and seized many of those who had fled from him, and he slaughtered some of the people and threw them into a great pit.

**7:20** And he entrusted the region to Alcimus, and left a force with him to assist him. And Bacchides departed to the king.

**7:21** And Alcimus strove earnestly for the leadership of his priesthood,

**7:22** and all who were troubling their own people came together to him, and they gained control of the land of Judah and inflicted a great blow in Israel.

**7:23** And Judas saw all the evils that Alcimus and those who were with him had done to the sons of Israel, much more than the nations,

**7:24** and he went out into all the borders of Judea all around, and executed vengeance on the men who were deserters, and they stopped going out any longer into the region.

**7:25** Now Alcimus saw that Judas and those who were with him had prevailed, and he realized that he could not withstand them; and he returned to the king and accused them of many crimes.

**7:26** And the king sent Nicanor, one of his more noble princes, who was pursuing enmity against Israel, and he commanded him to overthrow the people.

**7:27** And Nicanor came to Jerusalem with a great army, and he sent to Judas and to his brothers with peaceful words, with deceit,

**7:28** saying, "Let there be no battle between me and you; I will come with a few men, so that I may see your faces in peace."

**7:29** And he came to Judas, and they greeted one another peacefully; and the enemies were ready to carry off Judas.

**7:30** And the report became known to Judas that he had come to him with deceit, and he was terrified of him and did not want to see his face any longer.

**7:31** And Nicanor realized that his plan had been laid bare, and he went out to meet Judas in battle near Capharsalama.

**7:32** And about five thousand men of Nicanor's army fell, and they fled into the city of David.

**7:33** And after these things, Nicanor went up to Mount Zion; and some of the priests of the people went out to greet him in peace and to show him the burnt offerings that were being offered for the king.

**7:34** And mocking them, he despised them and defiled them, and spoke arrogantly,

**7:35** and swore in anger, saying, "Unless Judas and his army are handed over into my hands, as soon as I return in peace, I will burn down this house." And he went out in great anger.

**7:36** And the priests entered and stood in front of the altar and the temple, and weeping, they said,

**7:37** "You, Lord, chose this house for Your name to be called upon in it, so that it might be a house of prayer and supplication for Your people.

**7:38** Execute vengeance on this man and his army, and let them fall by the sword; remember their blasphemies, and do not grant that they remain."

**7:39** And Nicanor went out from Jerusalem and pitched camp at Beth-horon, and an army of Syria met him.

**7:40** And Judas encamped at Adarsa with three thousand men; and Judas prayed and said,

**7:41** "Lord, because those who had been sent by King Sennacherib blasphemed You, an angel went out and struck down one hundred and eighty-five thousand of them;

**7:42** so crush this army in our sight today, and let the rest know that he has spoken evil against Your sanctuary, and judge him according to his wickedness."

**7:43** And the armies joined battle on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar; and the camp of Nicanor was crushed, and he himself fell first in the battle.

**7:44** Now when his army saw that Nicanor had fallen, they threw away their weapons and fled;

**7:45** and they pursued them a day's journey from Adazer until one comes to Gazara, and they sounded trumpets after them with signals;

**7:46** and they went out from all the villages of Judea all around, and tossed them with horns, and they were turned back again toward them, and they all fell by the sword, and not even one of them was left.

**7:47** And they took their spoils as plunder, and they cut off the head of Nicanor and his right hand, which he had stretched out arrogantly, and brought them and hung them up opposite Jerusalem.

**7:48** And the people rejoiced greatly, and they kept that day in great joy.

**7:49** And he decreed that this day be kept every year on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar.

**7:50** And the land of Judah was quiet for a few days.

## Translator Notes

- **7:8** — The Vulgate construes magnum ("great") with "the river" (trans flumen magnum in regno), whereas the Greek applies "great" to Bacchides ("great in the kingdom"); in 7:9 the Vulgate also adds "so that he might see the destruction that Judas had done."
- **7:19** — The Vulgate preserves the distinctive place name Bethzecha (Greek: Beth-zaith/Bezeth) and reads qui a se effugerant ("who had fled from him"), whereas the Greek reads "who had deserted to him."
- **7:32** — The Vulgate reads quinque millia ("five thousand"), whereas Greek manuscripts read "five hundred."
- **7:40** — The Vulgate gives the distinctive place-name spellings Adarsa here and Adazer in 7:45 (both corresponding to Greek Adasa); in 7:41 it also explicitly names King Sennacherib and reads angelus ("an angel") rather than "the king" and "your angel."
- **7:46** — The Vulgate reads ventilabant eos cornibus ("tossed them with horns," an idiom akin to Ps 43:6), rendering literally the Greek military verb for "outflanked them."
