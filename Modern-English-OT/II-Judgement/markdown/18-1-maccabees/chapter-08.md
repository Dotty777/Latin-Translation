## Text

**8:1** And Judas heard the name of the Romans, that they are mighty in strength, and consent to all things that are requested of them, and whoever approached them established friendships with them, and that they are mighty in strength.

**8:2** And they heard of their battles and the good deeds of valor that they performed in Galatia, that they conquered them and brought them under tribute;

**8:3** and what great things they did in the region of Spain, and that they brought under their power the mines of silver and gold that are there, and took possession of the entire place by their counsel and patience,

**8:4** and the places that were very far from them; and they crushed the kings who had come against them from the ends of the earth, and struck them with a great blow; but the rest give them tribute every year.

**8:5** And in war they crushed Philip and Perseus, king of the Kittim, and the others who had taken up arms against them, and conquered them;

**8:6** and that Antiochus the Great, king of Asia, who had waged battle against them, having one hundred twenty elephants, and cavalry, and chariots, and a very great army, was crushed by them;

**8:7** and that they captured him alive, and decreed for him that he himself, and those who would reign after him, should give a great tribute, and that he should give hostages, and a settlement,

**8:8** and the region of the Indians, and the Medes, and the Lydians, from their best regions; and having received them from them, they gave them to King Eumenes;

**8:9** and that those who were in Greece wanted to go and do away with them, and the word became known to them,

**8:10** and they sent one commander to them, and fought against them, and many of them fell, and they led their wives and children away captive, and plundered them, and took possession of their land, and tore down their walls, and brought them into servitude to this day;

**8:11** and they destroyed the remaining kingdoms and islands that had at any time resisted them, and brought them under their power.

**8:12** But with their friends and those who had rest in them, they preserved friendship, and they conquered the kingdoms that were near and those that were far; for whoever heard their name feared them;

**8:13** indeed, those whom they wished to help, so that they might reign, reigned; while those whom they wished, they cast down from the kingdom; and they were greatly exalted.

**8:14** And in all these things no one wore a diadem, nor was clothed in purple so that he might be magnified in it.

**8:15** And that they made a senate house for themselves, and daily they consulted three hundred twenty men, always taking counsel concerning the multitude, so that they may carry out the things that are worthy;

**8:16** and they entrust their magistracy to one man each year to rule over their entire land, and all obey one, and there is neither envy nor jealousy among them.

**8:17** And Judas chose Eupolemus, the son of John, the son of Jacob, and Jason, the son of Eleazar, and sent them to Rome to establish friendship and an alliance with them,

**8:18** and so that they might remove from them the yoke of the Greeks, because they saw that they were pressing the kingdom of Israel into servitude.

**8:19** And they went to Rome, a very long journey, and entered the senate house, and said:

**8:20** "Judas Maccabeus, and his brothers, and the people of the Jews have sent us to you to establish an alliance and peace with you, and to enroll us as your allies and friends."

**8:21** And the word was pleasing in their sight.

**8:22** And this is the copy that they wrote in reply on bronze tablets, and sent to Jerusalem, so that it might be with them there as a memorial of peace and alliance:

**8:23** "May it be well with the Romans and with the nation of the Jews at sea and on land forever; and may sword and enemy be far from them.

**8:24** Now if war comes upon the Romans first, or upon all their allies in all their dominion,

**8:25** the nation of the Jews will bring help, as the time dictates, with a full heart;

**8:26** and to those waging battle they will not give nor supply wheat, arms, money, ships, as it has pleased the Romans; and they will keep their commands, receiving nothing from them.

**8:27** Likewise also, if war comes upon the nation of the Jews first, the Romans will help from the heart, as the time permits them;

**8:28** and to those who help there will not be given wheat, arms, money, ships, as it has pleased the Romans; and they will keep their commands without deceit.

**8:29** According to these words the Romans have established it for the people of the Jews.

**8:30** Now if after these words the one party or the other wishes to add anything to these things or take anything away from them, they will do so according to their own purpose; and whatever they add or take away will be valid.

**8:31** Moreover, concerning the evils that King Demetrius has done against them, we have written to him, saying, 'Why have you made your yoke heavy upon our friends and allies, the Jews?

**8:32** If therefore they appeal to us again, we will render judgment for them against you, and we will fight against you by sea and by land.'"

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — The Clementine Vulgate reads acquiescunt ad omnia quae postulantur ab eis ("consent to all things that are requested of them"), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads "they are favorably disposed to all who approach them."
- **8:2** — The Vulgate reads Et audierunt ("And they heard"), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads "And they told him."
- **8:8** — The Vulgate uses plural ethnic terms ("the region of the Indians, and the Medes, and the Lydians") and plural ab eis ("from them", vs. Greek "from him"), preserving the Septuagint tradition of India, Media, and Lydia (historically associated with Ionia, Mysia, and Lydia).
- **8:15** — The Clementine Vulgate puts trecentos viginti in the accusative case as the object of consulebant ("they consulted three hundred twenty men"), whereas the Greek Septuagint uses the nominative ("three hundred twenty took counsel").
- **8:17** — The Vulgate reads filii Jacob ("the son of Jacob"), a Vulgate-distinct proper name where the Greek Septuagint reads Akkos (Hakkoz).
