## Text

**1:1** And it happened that after Alexander son of Philip, the Macedonian, who first reigned in Greece, having gone out from the land of Kittim, struck Darius, king of the Persians and Medes,

**1:2** he waged many battles, and captured the fortifications of all, and killed the kings of the earth,

**1:3** and he passed through as far as the ends of the earth, and took the spoils of a multitude of nations, and the earth fell silent in his sight.

**1:4** And he gathered a force and an exceedingly strong army, and his heart was exalted and lifted up.

**1:5** And after these things he fell upon his bed, and knew that he was dying.

**1:6** And he called his noble servants, who had been brought up with him from youth, and divided his kingdom among them while he was still living.

**1:7** And Alexander reigned twelve years, and he died.

**1:8** And his servants obtained the kingdom, each one in his own place,

**1:9** and they all placed diadems on themselves after his death, and their sons after them for many years, and evils were multiplied on the earth.

**1:10** And a sinful root came out from them, Antiochus the Illustrious, son of Antiochus the king, who had been a hostage in Rome; and he reigned in the one hundred thirty-seventh year of the kingdom of the Greeks.

**1:11** In those days, wicked sons came out from Israel and persuaded many, saying, "Let us go and establish a covenant with the nations that are around us, because since we departed from them, many evils have found us."

**1:12** And the saying appeared good in their eyes.

**1:13** And some of the people were determined, and they went to the king, and he gave them authority to practice the ordinance of the nations.

**1:14** And they built a gymnasium in Jerusalem according to the laws of the nations,

**1:15** and they made foreskins for themselves, and departed from the holy covenant, and were joined to the nations, and were sold to do evil.

**1:16** And the kingdom was made ready in the sight of Antiochus, and he began to reign in the land of Egypt so that he might reign over two kingdoms.

**1:17** And he entered into Egypt with a massive multitude, with chariots, and elephants, and horsemen, and a vast multitude of ships,

**1:18** and he waged war against Ptolemy, king of Egypt; and Ptolemy was afraid before his face, and fled, and many fell wounded.

**1:19** And he seized the fortified cities in the land of Egypt, and took the spoils of the land of Egypt.

**1:20** And Antiochus turned back after he struck Egypt in the one hundred forty-third year, and he went up to Israel.

**1:21** And he entered into the sanctuary with arrogance, and took the golden altar, and the lampstand of light, and all its vessels, and the table of presentation, and the libation cups, and the bowls, and the small golden mortars, and the veil, and the crowns, and the golden ornament that was on the front of the temple; and he shattered everything.

**1:22** And he went up to Jerusalem with a massive multitude.

**1:23** And he took the silver, and the gold, and the desirable vessels, and he took the hidden treasures that he found; and having carried everything off, he departed into his own land.

**1:24** And he carried out a slaughter of people, and spoke in great arrogance.

**1:25** And great mourning arose in Israel, and in every place of theirs,

**1:26** and the rulers and elders groaned; virgins and young men grew weak, and the beauty of the women was altered.

**1:27** Every husband took up a lament, and those who sat on the marriage bed were grieving,

**1:28** and the land was shaken over those dwelling in it, and the entire house of Jacob put on shame.

**1:29** And after two years of days, the king sent the chief of tributes into the cities of Judah, and he came to Jerusalem with a great crowd.

**1:30** And he spoke peaceful words to them in deceit, and they believed him.

**1:31** And he took the spoils of the city, and burned it with fire, and destroyed its houses and its walls all around,

**1:32** and they led the women away as captives, and took possession of the children and the livestock.

**1:33** And they built up the city of David with a great and strong wall and strong towers, and it became a citadel for them,

**1:34** and they placed there a sinful nation, wicked men, and they grew strong in it; and they laid up weapons and food, and gathered the spoils of Jerusalem,

**1:35** and stored them there; and they became a great snare.

**1:36** And this became an ambush against the sanctuary, and an evil adversary in Israel,

**1:37** and they shed innocent blood all around the sanctuary, and they polluted the sanctuary.

**1:38** And the inhabitants of Jerusalem fled because of them, and she became a dwelling of strangers, and she became a stranger to her own offspring, and her children abandoned her.

**1:39** Her sanctuary was made desolate like a wilderness; her feast days were turned into mourning, her Sabbaths into reproach, her honors into nothing.

**1:40** According to her glory her dishonor was multiplied, and her loftiness was turned into mourning.

**1:41** And King Antiochus wrote to his whole kingdom that all the people should be one, and that each one should abandon his own law.

**1:42** And all the nations agreed according to the word of King Antiochus,

**1:43** and many from Israel agreed to his service, and sacrificed to idols, and defiled the Sabbath.

**1:44** And the king sent letters through the hands of messengers into Jerusalem and into all the cities of Judah, that they should follow the laws of the nations of the land,

**1:45** and prohibit burnt offerings and sacrifices and propitiations from being made in the temple of God;

**1:46** and he commanded the holy things and the holy people of Israel to be defiled.

**1:47** And he commanded altars and temples and idols to be built, and the flesh of swine and profane livestock to be offered in sacrifice,

**1:48** and to leave their sons uncircumcised, and their souls to be defiled with all unclean things and abominations, so that they would forget the law and alter all the ordinances of God,

**1:50** and that whoever did not act according to the word of King Antiochus should die.

**1:51** According to all these words he wrote to his whole kingdom, and he appointed rulers over the people who would compel these things to be done.

**1:52** And many from the people gathered to those who had abandoned the law of the Lord, and they did evils on the earth;

**1:53** and they drove the people of Israel into hidden places and into secret places of fugitives.

**1:54** On the fifteenth day of the month of Chislev, in the one hundred forty-fifth year, King Antiochus built an abominable idol of desolation upon the altar of God, and throughout all the cities of Judah all around they built altars;

**1:55** and before the doors of the houses and in the streets they were burning incense and sacrificing;

**1:56** and they burned the books of the law of God with fire, tearing them apart;

**1:57** and with whomever the books of the covenant of the Lord were found, and whoever observed the law of the Lord, according to the edict of the king they slaughtered him.

**1:58** In their power they were doing these things to the people of Israel who were found in each and every month in the cities.

**1:59** And on the twenty-fifth day of the month they were sacrificing upon the altar that was opposite the altar.

**1:60** And the women who circumcised their sons were slaughtered according to the command of King Antiochus,

**1:61** and they hanged the boys by the necks throughout all their houses, and they slaughtered those who had circumcised them.

**1:62** And many from the people of Israel resolved within themselves that they would not eat unclean things, and they chose to die rather than to be defiled by unclean foods;

**1:63** and they were unwilling to break the holy law of God, and they were slaughtered;

**1:64** and very great wrath came upon the people.

## Translator Notes

- **1:4** — The Vulgate places the lifting up of Alexander's heart at the end of verse 4 (with both passive verbs agreeing with 'his heart') and omits the Greek clause stating that he ruled over countries, nations, and rulers who became tributary to him.
- **1:10** — Latin 'illustris' ('the Illustrious') translates the Greek royal epithet 'Epiphanes' ('Manifest' or 'Illustrious') of Antiochus IV.
- **1:16** — The Vulgate reads 'cœpit regnare' ('he began to reign'), whereas the Greek reads that he intended or conceived the plan to reign.
- **1:22** — In the Clementine Vulgate, this clause stands after the plundering of the sanctuary in verse 21 (where Latin 'comminuit' also reads 'shattered' rather than the Greek 'stripped off'), whereas in the Greek it appears at the end of verse 20.
- **1:30** — The Clementine Vulgate omits the latter part of verse 30 found in the Greek, which recounts the sudden assault on the city and the destruction of many people of Israel.
- **1:36** — Latin 'diabolum malum' (literally 'an evil devil' or 'adversary') follows the Greek loanword 'diabolon' (representing Hebrew 'satan', adversary); the Vulgate also omits 'continually' at the end of the verse.
- **1:44** — The Vulgate reads 'leges gentium terræ' ('the laws of the nations of the land'), whereas the Greek reads 'customs foreign to the land.'
- **1:45** — Latin 'placationes' ('propitiations') differs from the Greek 'sponden' ('drink offering'), and in verse 46 the Vulgate omits the Greek clause about profaning Sabbaths and feasts.
- **1:48** — In the Clementine Vulgate, the clause numbered as verse 49 in the Greek ('so that they would forget the law and alter all the ordinances of God') is joined to the end of verse 48, so verse 49 is omitted in numbering.
- **1:59** — Latin distinguishes 'ara' (a pagan altar) from 'altare' (the temple altar of God), and the Vulgate reads 'contra' ('opposite') rather than the Greek 'upon.'
