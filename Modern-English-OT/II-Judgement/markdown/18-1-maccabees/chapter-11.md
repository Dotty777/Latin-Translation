## Text

**11:1** And the king of Egypt gathered an army like the sand that is along the shore of the sea, and many ships; and he sought to obtain the kingdom of Alexander by deceit and to add it to his own kingdom.

**11:2** And he went out into Syria with peaceful words, and the cities opened to him and came to meet him, because King Alexander had given orders to go out to meet him, since he was his father-in-law.

**11:3** Now whenever Ptolemy entered a city, he placed garrisons of soldiers in each city.

**11:4** And when he drew near to Azotus, they showed him the temple of Dagon burned with fire, and Azotus and the rest of it demolished, and the bodies cast out, and the burial mounds of those who had been killed in the war, which they had made beside the road.

**11:5** And they told the king that Jonathan had done these things, so that they might stir up ill will against him; and the king remained silent.

**11:6** And Jonathan met the king at Joppa with glory, and they greeted one another, and they slept there.

**11:7** And Jonathan went with the king as far as the river that is called Eleutherus, and he returned to Jerusalem.

**11:8** Now King Ptolemy obtained dominion over the cities as far as Seleucia by the sea, and he was devising evil plans against Alexander.

**11:9** And he sent envoys to Demetrius, saying, "Come, let us make a covenant between us, and I will give you my daughter, whom Alexander has, and you will reign in your father's kingdom;

**11:10** for I regret that I gave him my daughter, for he sought to kill me."

**11:11** And he found fault with him because he had coveted his kingdom.

**11:12** And he took away his daughter and gave her to Demetrius, and he estranged himself from Alexander, and his enmity became evident.

**11:13** And Ptolemy entered Antioch and placed two diadems on his head, those of Egypt and of Asia.

**11:14** Now King Alexander was in Cilicia in those times, because those who were in those places were rebelling.

**11:15** And Alexander heard, and he came against him to war; and King Ptolemy led out the army and met him with a strong hand, and put him to flight.

**11:16** And Alexander fled into Arabia so that he might be protected there, but King Ptolemy was exalted.

**11:17** And Zabdiel the Arab took off Alexander's head and sent it to Ptolemy.

**11:18** And King Ptolemy died on the third day, and those who were in the strongholds perished at the hands of those who were inside the camps.

**11:19** And Demetrius began to reign in the one hundred and sixty-seventh year.

**11:20** In those days Jonathan gathered those who were in Judea so that they might storm the citadel that is in Jerusalem, and they made many siege engines against it.

**11:21** And certain wicked men who hated their own nation went to King Demetrius and reported to him that Jonathan was besieging the citadel.

**11:22** And when he heard, he became angry; and immediately he came to Ptolemais and wrote to Jonathan not to besiege the citadel, but to meet him for a conference with haste.

**11:23** Now when Jonathan heard, he ordered the siege to continue, and he chose some of the elders of Israel and of the priests, and put himself in danger.

**11:24** And he took gold, and silver, and clothing, and many other gifts, and went to the king at Ptolemais; and he found favor in his sight,

**11:25** and certain wicked men from his own nation were bringing complaints against him.

**11:26** And the king did for him just as those who had been before him had done for him, and he exalted him in the sight of all his friends,

**11:27** and he established for him the chief office of the priesthood and whatever other precious things he had previously held, and he made him chief of the friends.

**11:28** And Jonathan requested of the king that he make Judea, and the three districts, and Samaria and its bordering territories, exempt from tribute; and he promised him three hundred talents.

**11:29** And the king agreed, and he wrote letters to Jonathan concerning all these things, containing these terms:

**11:30** "King Demetrius to his brother Jonathan, greetings, and to the nation of the Jews.

**11:31** We have sent to you a copy of the letter that we wrote to our father Lasthenes concerning you, so that you may know:

**11:32** 'King Demetrius to his father Lasthenes, greetings.

**11:33** To the nation of the Jews, our friends and keepers of what is just toward us, we have resolved to do good because of their goodwill that they have toward us.

**11:34** Therefore we have established for them that all the territories of Judea, and the three cities, Lydda and Ramathan, which were added to Judea from Samaria, and all their bordering territories, be set apart for all who sacrifice in Jerusalem, in place of those things that the king previously received from them each year, and in place of the produce of the land and of the fruit trees.

**11:35** And from this time we remit to them the other things of the tithes and tributes that belonged to us; and the salt pans and the crowns that were brought to us,

**11:36** all of these we grant to them; and none of these things will be void, from this time and for all time.

**11:37** Now therefore take care to make a copy of these things, and let it be given to Jonathan, and let it be placed on the holy mountain, in a prominent place.'"

**11:38** And King Demetrius, seeing that the land was quiet in his sight and nothing resisted him, dismissed his entire army, each one to his own place, except the foreign army that he had drawn together from the islands of the nations; and all the armies of his fathers were hostile to him.

**11:39** Now Trypho was formerly one of Alexander's party; and he saw that the entire army was grumbling against Demetrius, and he went to Emalchuel the Arab, who was raising Antiochus, the son of Alexander;

**11:40** and he stayed persistently with him so that he would hand him over to him, that he might reign in place of his father; and he told him all that Demetrius had done, and the enmity of his armies against him. And he remained there many days.

**11:41** And Jonathan sent to King Demetrius that he should drive out those who were in the citadel in Jerusalem and those who were in the garrisons, because they were fighting against Israel.

**11:42** And Demetrius sent to Jonathan, saying, "Not only will I do these things for you and for your nation, but I will make you and your nation renowned with glory when it is opportune.

**11:43** Now therefore you will do well if you send men to my aid, because my entire army has departed."

**11:44** And Jonathan sent him three thousand valiant men to Antioch; and they came to the king, and the king was delighted at their arrival.

**11:45** And those who were of the city assembled, one hundred and twenty thousand men, and they wanted to kill the king.

**11:46** And the king fled into the palace, and those who were of the city seized the streets of the city and began to fight.

**11:47** And the king called the Jews to his aid, and they all assembled together to him, and they all dispersed throughout the city;

**11:48** and they killed on that day one hundred thousand people, and they set fire to the city, and took much plunder on that day, and rescued the king.

**11:49** And those who were of the city saw that the Jews had gained control of the city just as they wanted, and they grew weak in their mind, and cried out to the king with pleas, saying,

**11:50** "Give us your right hand, and let the Jews stop attacking us and the city."

**11:51** And they threw down their weapons and made peace, and the Jews were glorified in the sight of the king and in the sight of all who were in his kingdom, and they became renowned in the kingdom; and they returned to Jerusalem having much plunder.

**11:52** And King Demetrius sat on the throne of his kingdom, and the land was quiet in his sight.

**11:53** And he proved false to all that he had said, and estranged himself from Jonathan, and did not repay him according to the benefits that he had bestowed on him, and he greatly harassed him.

**11:54** Now after these things Trypho returned, and with him Antiochus, a young boy, and he began to reign and placed the diadem on himself.

**11:55** And all the armies that Demetrius had dispersed gathered to him, and they fought against him, and he fled and turned his back.

**11:56** And Trypho took the beasts and gained control of Antioch.

**11:57** And the young Antiochus wrote to Jonathan, saying, "I establish for you the priesthood, and I appoint you over the four cities, so that you may be among the friends of the king."

**11:58** And he sent him gold vessels for service, and gave him authority to drink from gold, and to be in purple, and to have a gold clasp;

**11:59** and he appointed his brother Simon commander from the borders of Tyre as far as the boundaries of Egypt.

**11:60** And Jonathan went out and traveled through the cities beyond the river, and the entire army of Syria gathered to him to his aid; and he came to Ashkelon, and those from the city met him with honor.

**11:61** And he went from there to Gaza, and those who were in Gaza shut themselves in; and he besieged it, and set fire to the things that were around the city and plundered them.

**11:62** And the people of Gaza appealed to Jonathan, and he gave them his right hand; and he took their sons as hostages and sent them to Jerusalem; and he traveled through the region as far as Damascus.

**11:63** And Jonathan heard that the commanders of Demetrius had transgressed in Kedesh, which is in Galilee, with a large army, wishing to remove him from the business of the kingdom;

**11:64** and he went to meet them, while he left his brother Simon within the province.

**11:65** And Simon encamped against Beth-zur and assaulted it for many days, and shut them in.

**11:66** And they requested to receive the right hand from him, and he gave it to them; and he drove them out from there, and took the city, and placed a garrison in it.

**11:67** And Jonathan and his forces encamped by the water of Gennesar, and before dawn they kept watch in the plain of Hazor;

**11:68** and look, the forces of the foreigners were coming to meet him in the plain, and they were laying an ambush for him in the mountains; but he himself met them from the opposite side.

**11:69** Now the ambushers rose up from their places and joined battle.

**11:70** And all those who were on Jonathan's side fled, and no one was left of them except Mattathias, the son of Absalom, and Judas, the son of Chalphi, commander of the troops of the army.

**11:71** And Jonathan tore his garments, and put earth on his head, and prayed.

**11:72** And Jonathan turned back to them into battle, and turned them to flight, and they fought.

**11:73** And those of his side who were fleeing saw it and returned to him, and they all pursued with him as far as Kedesh, to their camp, and they reached all the way there;

**11:74** and three thousand men of the foreigners fell on that day; and Jonathan returned to Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **11:4** — The Clementine Vulgate renders the surroundings of Azotus broadly as cetera ejus ("the rest of it") and reads tumulos ("burial mounds") of those killed in the war, whereas the Greek refers to its surrounding towns and to heaps of burned bodies piled along the road.
- **11:18** — Latin perierunt ab his qui erant intra castra literally means "perished by those who were inside the camps," distinguishing castra ("camps") from munitionibus ("strongholds") where the Greek repeats "in the strongholds."
- **11:28** — The Clementine Vulgate coordinates Samaria and its bordering territories alongside the three districts (Latin toparchias), whereas most Greek witnesses read "the three districts of Samaria."
- **11:34** — The Clementine Vulgate mentions "three cities" while naming only Lydda and Ramathan (Greek Rathamin) because the third Greek place name, Aphairema, was translated into Latin as the verb sequestrari ("to be set apart").
- **11:50** — Latin dextras (literally "right hands," singular dexteram in 11:62) is an idiom for extending a hand as a pledge of truce or peace.
- **11:56** — Latin bestias ("the beasts") refers to the Seleucid war elephants captured by Trypho.
- **11:59** — The Clementine Vulgate reads a terminis Tyri ("from the borders of Tyre"), whereas the Greek refers to "the Ladder of Tyre," a coastal promontory pass south of Tyre.
- **11:63** — Vulgate prævaricati sunt ("had transgressed") reflects a Greek variant meaning "crossed over/transgressed" rather than the standard Greek reading "had arrived."
- **11:72** — The Clementine Vulgate reads et pugnaverunt ("and they fought"), where the Greek reads "and they fled" (probably an early Latin shift from fugerunt to pugnaverunt).
