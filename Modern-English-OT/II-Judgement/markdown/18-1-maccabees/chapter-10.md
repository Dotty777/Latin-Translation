## Text

**10:1** And in the one hundred and sixtieth year, Alexander, the son of Antiochus, who was surnamed the Noble, went up and occupied Ptolemais; and they received him, and he reigned there.

**10:2** And King Demetrius heard, and gathered a very large army, and went out to meet him in battle.

**10:3** And Demetrius sent a letter to Jonathan with peaceful words, so as to exalt him.

**10:4** For he said, "Let us act first to make peace with him, before he does so with Alexander against us;

**10:5** for he will remember all the evils that we have done against him, and against his brother, and against his nation."

**10:6** And he gave him authority to gather an army, and to manufacture weapons, and himself to be his ally; and he ordered the hostages who were in the citadel to be handed over to him.

**10:7** And Jonathan came into Jerusalem, and read the letters in the hearing of all the people, and of those who were in the citadel.

**10:8** And they feared with a great fear, because they heard that the king had given him authority to gather an army.

**10:9** And the hostages were handed over to Jonathan, and he returned them to their parents;

**10:10** and Jonathan lived in Jerusalem, and began to build and renew the city.

**10:11** And he told those doing the work to build up the walls, and Mount Zion all around with squared stones for fortification; and they did so.

**10:12** And the foreigners who were in the fortresses that Bacchides had built fled;

**10:13** and each one left his place and went away to his own land;

**10:14** only in Beth-zur did some remain of those who had abandoned the law and the precepts of God, for it was a refuge for them.

**10:15** And King Alexander heard the promises that Demetrius had promised to Jonathan; and they recounted to him the battles, and the valiant deeds that he himself and his brothers had done, and the toils through which they had labored;

**10:16** and he said, "Will we find any such man? And now we will make him our friend and ally."

**10:17** And he wrote a letter and sent it to him according to these words, saying:

**10:18** "King Alexander, to his brother Jonathan, greetings.

**10:19** We have heard about you that you are a man mighty in strength, and you are fit to be our friend;

**10:20** and now we appoint you today high priest of your nation, and to be called the king's friend" (and he sent him a purple robe and a golden crown) "and to be of one mind with us in our affairs, and to preserve friendship toward us."

**10:21** And Jonathan clothed himself in the holy robe in the seventh month, in the one hundred and sixtieth year, on the festival day of Tabernacles; and he gathered an army and made weapons in abundance.

**10:22** And Demetrius heard these words, and was greatly grieved, and said,

**10:23** "Why have we done this, that Alexander has gotten ahead of us to secure the friendship of the Jews for his own defense?

**10:24** I also will write to them words of appeal, and honors, and gifts, so that they may be with me as a help."

**10:25** And he wrote to them in these words: "King Demetrius, to the nation of the Jews, greetings.

**10:26** Because you have kept the pact with us, and have remained in our friendship, and have not gone over to our enemies, we have heard and have rejoiced.

**10:27** And now continue still to keep faith with us, and we will repay you good things for what you have done with us;

**10:28** and we will remit for you many dues, and we will give you grants.

**10:29** And now I release you and all the Jews from tributes, and I waive the prices of salt, and I remit the crowns, and the thirds of the seed;

**10:30** and half of the fruit of the tree, which is of my portion, I leave to you from this day onward, so that it may not be taken from the land of Judah and from the three cities that were added to it from Samaria and Galilee, from this day and for all time;

**10:31** and let Jerusalem be holy and free, with its borders, and let the tithes and tributes be its own.

**10:32** I also relinquish authority over the citadel that is in Jerusalem, and I give it to the high priest, so that he may appoint in it whichever men he himself chooses to guard it.

**10:33** And every soul of the Jews who is captive from the land of Judah in all my kingdom I leave free without charge, so that all may be released from tributes, even those of their livestock.

**10:34** And all the festival days, and the Sabbaths, and the new moons, and the appointed days, and three days before a festival day, and three days after a festival day, let all of them be days of immunity and remission for all the Jews who are in my kingdom;

**10:35** and no one will have authority to take any action or stir up trouble against any of them in any case.

**10:36** And let there be enrolled from among the Jews in the king's army up to thirty thousand men; and provisions will be given to them as is proper for all the armies of the king, and some of them will be assigned to be in the fortresses of the great king;

**10:37** and some of these will be appointed over the affairs of the kingdom that are conducted in trust, and let commanders be from among them, and let them walk in their own laws, just as the king has commanded in the land of Judah.

**10:38** And let the three cities that were added to Judea from the region of Samaria be reckoned with Judea, so that they may be under one, and not obey any other authority except that of the high priest.

**10:39** Ptolemais and its bordering territories, these I have given as a gift to the holy ones who are in Jerusalem, for the necessary expenses of the sanctuary.

**10:40** And I give each year fifteen thousand shekels of silver from the king's accounts that belong to me;

**10:41** and all that remains, which those who were over the affairs had not paid in previous years, they will give from now on for the works of the house.

**10:42** And in addition to these, the five thousand shekels of silver that they used to collect from the account of the sanctuary each year: let these also belong to the priests who perform the ministry.

**10:43** And all who take refuge in the temple that is in Jerusalem and within all its borders, being liable to the king in any matter, let them be released, and let them hold free all that is theirs in my kingdom.

**10:44** And for building or restoring the works of the sanctuary, the expenses will be given from the king's account;

**10:45** and for building up the walls of Jerusalem and fortifying them all around, the expenses will be given from the king's account, and for constructing the walls in Judea."

**10:46** Now when Jonathan and the people heard these words, they did not believe them, nor did they accept them, because they remembered the great wickedness that he had done in Israel, and he had greatly afflicted them.

**10:47** And they were well pleased with Alexander, because he himself had been to them the originator of words of peace, and they gave him help all the days.

**10:48** And King Alexander gathered a great army, and moved his camp against Demetrius.

**10:49** And the two kings joined battle, and the army of Demetrius fled, and Alexander pursued him and pressed hard upon them.

**10:50** And the battle grew exceedingly fierce until the sun set, and Demetrius fell on that day.

**10:51** And Alexander sent envoys to Ptolemy, king of Egypt, according to these words, saying:

**10:52** "Since I have returned to my kingdom, and have sat on the throne of my fathers, and have obtained the rule, and have crushed Demetrius, and have taken possession of our region,

**10:53** and have joined battle with him, and he himself and his camp have been crushed by us, and we have sat on the throne of his kingdom;

**10:54** and now let us establish friendship with one another; and give me your daughter as a wife, and I will be your son-in-law, and I will give gifts to you, and to her, worthy of you."

**10:55** And King Ptolemy answered, saying, "Happy is the day on which you returned to the land of your fathers, and sat on the throne of their kingdom.

**10:56** And now I will do for you what you have written; but meet me at Ptolemais, so that we may see one another, and I may make a betrothal with you, just as you have said."

**10:57** And Ptolemy went out from Egypt, he himself and Cleopatra his daughter, and came to Ptolemais in the one hundred and sixty-second year.

**10:58** And King Alexander met him, and he gave him Cleopatra his daughter; and he celebrated her wedding at Ptolemais, as kings do, in great glory.

**10:59** And King Alexander wrote to Jonathan to come to meet him.

**10:60** And he went with glory to Ptolemais, and met the two kings there, and gave them much silver, and gold, and gifts; and he found favor in their sight.

**10:61** And pestilent men from Israel assembled against him, lawless men bringing accusations against him; and the king paid no attention to them.

**10:62** And he ordered Jonathan to be stripped of his garments and to be clothed in a purple robe; and they did so. And the king placed him to sit with him.

**10:63** And he said to his officials, "Go out with him into the middle of the city, and proclaim that no one is to bring an accusation against him concerning any matter, nor is anyone to trouble him on any account."

**10:64** And it happened, when those who were bringing accusations saw his glory that was being proclaimed, and him clad in a purple robe, they all fled;

**10:65** and the king exalted him, and enrolled him among the first friends, and set him as commander and a sharer in the rule.

**10:66** And Jonathan returned to Jerusalem with peace and gladness.

**10:67** In the one hundred and sixty-fifth year, Demetrius, the son of Demetrius, came from Crete into the land of his fathers.

**10:68** And King Alexander heard, and was greatly grieved, and returned to Antioch.

**10:69** And King Demetrius appointed Apollonius, who was over Coele-Syria, as commander; and he gathered a great army and approached Jamnia, and sent to Jonathan the high priest,

**10:70** saying, "You alone resist us; and I have become a mockery and a reproach because you exercise authority against us in the mountains.

**10:71** Now therefore, if you trust in your forces, come down to us into the plain, and let us match ourselves against one another there; for with me is the power of wars.

**10:72** Ask and learn who I am, and the rest who are a help to me, who also say that your foot cannot stand before our face, because twice your fathers were put to flight in their own land;

**10:73** and now how will you be able to withstand the cavalry and so great an army in the plain, where there is no stone, nor rock, nor place to flee?"

**10:74** Now when Jonathan heard the words of Apollonius, he was stirred in spirit; and he chose ten thousand men and went out from Jerusalem, and Simon his brother met him as a help;

**10:75** and they brought their camp up to Joppa, and it shut him out from the city, because a garrison of Apollonius was in Joppa; and he attacked it.

**10:76** And those who were in the city, being terrified, opened to him, and Jonathan obtained Joppa.

**10:77** And Apollonius heard, and he brought up three thousand horsemen and a large army.

**10:78** And he went to Azotus as though making a journey, and immediately went out into the plain, because he had a multitude of horsemen and trusted in them. And Jonathan pursued him to Azotus, and they joined battle.

**10:79** And Apollonius left a thousand horsemen in the camp behind them secretly.

**10:80** And Jonathan learned that an ambush was behind him; and they surrounded his camp and cast darts at the people from morning until evening.

**10:81** But the people stood, just as Jonathan had commanded, and their horses grew weary.

**10:82** And Simon brought out his army and engaged against the legion, for the horsemen were exhausted; and they were crushed by him and fled.

**10:83** And those who were scattered across the plain fled into Azotus, and entered into Beth-dagon, their idol, so that they might deliver themselves there.

**10:84** And Jonathan burned Azotus and the cities that were all around it, and took their spoils; and the temple of Dagon, and all who fled into it, he burned with fire.

**10:85** And those who fell by the sword, together with those who were burned, were about eight thousand men.

**10:86** And Jonathan moved his camp from there, and brought it up to Ashkelon; and they came out from the city to meet him in great glory.

**10:87** And Jonathan returned to Jerusalem with his men, having many spoils.

**10:88** And it happened, when King Alexander heard these words, he proceeded still further to glorify Jonathan.

**10:89** And he sent him a golden clasp, just as is the custom to be given to the kinsmen of kings. And he gave him Ekron and all its borders as a possession.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — The Vulgate renders the Greek royal epithet Epiphanes as Nobilis ("the Noble").
- **10:5** — The Vulgate has singular fratrem ejus ("his brother"), whereas the Greek text reads plural ("his brothers").
- **10:31** — The Vulgate reads et decimæ et tributa ipsius sint ("and let the tithes and tributes be its own"), assigning those revenues to Jerusalem itself rather than phrasing them as exemptions as in the Greek.
- **10:36** — The Vulgate reads in munitionibus regis magni ("in the fortresses of the great king"), construing magni with "king" rather than with "fortresses" ("the great fortresses of the king") as in the Greek.
- **10:39** — The Vulgate renders the Greek phrase for "the sanctuary" with masculine sanctis qui sunt ("the holy ones who are"), while sanctorum at the end of the verse and in vv. 42 and 44 refers to the sanctuary.
- **10:49** — The Vulgate reads fugit exercitus Demetrii, et insecutus est eum Alexander ("the army of Demetrius fled, and Alexander pursued him"), reversing the Greek text, in which Alexander's army flees and Demetrius pursues.
- **10:68** — The Clementine Vulgate uses the masculine accusative form Antiochum (without a preposition, as a city of destination) in place of Antiochiam ("to Antioch").
- **10:71** — The Vulgate reads virtus bellorum ("the power of wars"), whereas the Greek text has "the power of the cities."
- **10:79** — The Vulgate adds in castris ("in the camp"), a detail absent from the Greek text.
- **10:83** — The Vulgate renders the Greek "their idol-temple" literally as idolum suum ("their idol"), and omits the Greek subject "the cavalry" before qui dispersi sunt ("those who were scattered").
