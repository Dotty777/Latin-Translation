## Text

**5:1** And a great outcry of the people and their wives arose against their brothers, the Jews.

**5:2** And there were some who said, "Our sons and our daughters are far too many; let us get grain for their price, and eat, and live."

**5:3** And there were some who said, "Let us pledge our fields, our vineyards, and our houses, and let us get grain in the famine."

**5:4** And others said, "Let us borrow money for the king's taxes, and let us give our fields and vineyards;

**5:5** and now, just as the flesh of our brothers is, so is our flesh, and just as their sons are, so also are our sons. Look, we are subjecting our sons and our daughters to servitude, and some of our daughters are maidservants, and we have nothing with which they can be redeemed, and others possess our fields and our vineyards."

**5:6** And I became exceedingly angry when I heard their outcry according to these words.

**5:7** And my heart deliberated within me, and I rebuked the nobles and the magistrates, and said to them, "Are each of you exacting interest from your brothers?" And I gathered a great assembly against them,

**5:8** and said to them, "We, as you know, have redeemed our brothers, the Jews, who had been sold to the nations, according to our ability; and will you then sell your brothers, and shall we redeem them?" And they were silent, and found nothing to answer.

**5:9** And I said to them, "It is not a good thing that you are doing; why have you not walked in the fear of our God, so that we may not be reproached by the nations, our enemies?

**5:10** I also, and my brothers, and my servants, have lent money and grain to very many. Let us together not demand this back; let us forgive the debt that is owed to us.

**5:11** Restore to them today their fields, their vineyards, their olive groves, and their houses; moreover, give on their behalf even the hundredth part of the money, grain, wine, and oil that you are accustomed to exact from them."

**5:12** And they said, "We will restore them, and we will require nothing from them; and we will do just as you say." And I called the priests and put them under oath to do according to what I had said.

**5:13** Furthermore, I shook out the fold of my garment and said, "So may God shake out every man who does not fulfill this word from his house and from his labors; so may he be shaken out and become empty." And the entire multitude said, "Amen," and they praised God. Therefore the people did as had been said.

**5:14** Now from that day on which the king had commanded me to be governor in the land of Judah, from the twentieth year until the thirty-second year of King Artaxerxes, for twelve years, I and my brothers did not eat the provisions that were owed to the governors.

**5:15** But the former governors, who had been before me, burdened the people and took from them in bread, wine, and money forty shekels every day; moreover, their attendants also oppressed the people. But I did not do so because of the fear of God;

**5:16** rather, I built in the work of the wall, and I did not buy a field, and all my servants were gathered for the work.

**5:17** The Jews also and the magistrates, one hundred fifty men, and those who came to us from the nations that are around us, were at my table.

**5:18** Now there was prepared for me each day one ox and six choice rams, besides birds, and every ten days various wines, and I provided many other things; furthermore, I also did not require the provisions of my governorship, for the people were greatly weakened.

**5:19** Remember me, my God, for good, according to all that I have done for this people.

## Translator Notes

- **5:2** — The Vulgate adds pro pretio eorum ('for their price'), making explicit the exchange of children for grain.
- **5:8** — The Vulgate adds ut scitis ('as you know') and reads et redimemus eos ('and shall we redeem them?') where the Hebrew has 'so that they may be sold to us.'
- **5:10** — Aes alienum is the standard Latin idiom for 'debt' (lit. 'another's money'), and the Vulgate reads 'forgive the debt that is owed to us' rather than the Hebrew reference to leaving off interest.
- **5:15** — The Vulgate adds quotidie ('every day') and groups bread, wine, and money into forty shekels daily, whereas the Hebrew mentions bread and wine 'besides forty shekels of silver' without 'daily.'
