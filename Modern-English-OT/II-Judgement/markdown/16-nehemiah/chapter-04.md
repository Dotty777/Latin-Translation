## Text

**4:1** Now it happened, when Sanballat, Tobiah, the Arabs, the Ammonites, and the Ashdodites heard that the scar of the wall of Jerusalem had closed over and that the breaches had begun to be closed up, that they became exceedingly angry.

**4:2** And they all assembled together to come and fight against Jerusalem and to contrive ambushes.

**4:3** And we prayed to our God, and we set guards on the wall day and night against them.

**4:4** Now Judah said, "The strength of the bearer has weakened, and there is too much earth, and we will not be able to build the wall."

**4:5** And our adversaries said, "Let them not know and let them remain unaware until we come into their midst, and kill them, and make the work cease."

**4:6** Now it happened, as the Jews who lived near them came and told us ten times from all the places from which they had come to us,

**4:7** that I stationed the people in order in the place behind the wall all around, with their swords, spears, and bows.

**4:8** And I looked closely and rose up, and I said to the nobles and magistrates, and to the rest of the common people, "Do not be afraid before their face; remember the great and fearsome Lord, and fight for your brothers, your sons, your daughters, your wives, and your houses."

**4:9** Now it happened, when our enemies heard that it had been reported to us, that God frustrated their plan. And we all returned to the walls, each one to his own work.

**4:10** And it happened from that day that half of their young men did the work, and half were ready for war; and there were spears, shields, bows, and coats of mail, and the leaders were behind them in all the house of Judah.

**4:11** Of those building on the wall, and carrying burdens, and loading them, each one did the work with one hand and held a sword with the other.

**4:12** For each one of those building was girded at his waist with a sword. And they were building, and they were sounding the horn beside me.

**4:13** And I said to the nobles, and to the magistrates, and to the rest of the common people, "The work is great and wide, and we are separated on the wall, far from one another;

**4:14** in whatever place you hear the blast of the trumpet, run together to us there; our God will fight for us.

**4:15** And let us ourselves do the work, and let half of us hold spears from the rising of the dawn until the stars come out."

**4:16** At that time also I said to the people, "Let each one with his servant remain in the midst of Jerusalem, and let us have turns by night and day for working."

**4:17** Now I and my brothers, and my servants, and the guards who were behind me, did not take off our clothes; each one undressed only for washing.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — The Vulgate renders the repair of the wall with the vivid medical idiom 'the scar had closed over' (obducta esset cicatrix), reflecting the Hebrew figure of healing.
- **4:6** — The Vulgate construes the difficult final clause (ex omnibus locis quibus venerant ad nos) as identifying where the Jews had come from rather than quoting direct speech, and in v. 7 reads in ordinem ('in order') where the Hebrew has 'by families.'
- **4:10** — The Vulgate reads juvenum eorum ('of their young men') rather than the Hebrew 'my young men' or 'my servants.'
- **4:15** — With the hortatory subjunctives faciamus ('let us do') and teneat ('let ... hold') and the pronoun nostrum ('of us'), the Vulgate continues Nehemiah's speech through v. 15 rather than shifting to past-tense narrative as in the Hebrew; similarly, v. 12 has plural verbs (aedificabant, et clangebant) rather than a singular trumpeter.
- **4:17** — The Vulgate renders an obscure Hebrew clause mentioning water as unusquisque tantum nudabatur ad baptismum ('each one undressed only for washing,' lit. 'for baptism/bathing').
