## Text

**6:1** Now it happened, when Sanballat, Tobiah, Geshem the Arab, and the rest of our enemies heard that I had built the wall and that no breach remained in it (though up to that time I had not set the doors in the gates),

**6:2** that Sanballat and Geshem sent to me, saying, "Come, and let us make a covenant together in the villages in the plain of Ono." But they themselves were planning to do me harm.

**6:3** Therefore I sent messengers to them, saying, "I am doing a great work, and I cannot come down, so that it may not by chance be neglected when I come and go down to you."

**6:4** And they sent to me according to this word four times, and I answered them according to the former statement.

**6:5** And Sanballat sent his servant to me a fifth time according to the former word, and he had a letter in his hand written in this way:

**6:6** "It has been heard among the nations, and Geshem has said, that you and the Jews are planning to rebel, and therefore you are building the wall, and you wish to raise yourself up as king over them; for which reason

**6:7** you have also appointed prophets to proclaim about you in Jerusalem, saying, 'There is a king in Judea.' The king is going to hear these words; therefore come now, so that we may take counsel together."

**6:8** And I sent to them, saying, "It has not happened according to these words that you speak, for out of your own heart you are inventing these things."

**6:9** For all of these were trying to frighten us, thinking that our hands would cease from the work and we would rest; for which reason I strengthened my hands all the more.

**6:10** And I entered the house of Shemaiah son of Delaiah, son of Mehetabel, in secret. And he said, "Let us confer together in the house of God, in the midst of the temple, and let us shut the doors of the sanctuary, for they are coming to kill you, and by night they are coming to put you to death."

**6:11** And I said, "Does anyone like me flee? And who like me will enter the temple and live? I will not enter."

**6:12** And I understood that God had not sent him, but that he had spoken to me as though prophesying, and that Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him.

**6:13** For he had received payment so that, being frightened, I would do it and sin, and they would have something evil with which to reproach me.

**6:14** Remember me, Lord, on account of Tobiah and Sanballat, according to such deeds of theirs, and also Noadiah the prophet and the rest of the prophets who were trying to frighten me.

**6:15** Now the wall was completed on the twenty-fifth day of the month of Elul, in fifty-two days.

**6:16** Therefore it happened, when all our enemies heard, that all the nations that were around us were afraid, and collapsed within themselves, and knew that this work had been done by God.

**6:17** And in those days also, many letters from the nobles of the Jews were being sent to Tobiah, and from Tobiah were coming to them.

**6:18** For there were many in Judea who had his oath, because he was the son-in-law of Shecaniah son of Arah, and his son Johanan had taken the daughter of Meshullam son of Berechiah;

**6:19** moreover, they were also praising him before me, and they were reporting my words to him; and Tobiah was sending letters to frighten me.

## Translator Notes

- **6:2** — The Vulgate translates percutiamus foedus ('let us make a covenant') where the Hebrew reads 'let us meet together,' and renders in viculis ('in the villages') as a common noun rather than a proper place name.
- **6:9** — The Vulgate renders the final clause as first-person narrative (confortavi manus meas, 'I strengthened my hands') rather than an imperative prayer ('Strengthen my hands!') as in the Hebrew.
- **6:10** — The Vulgate reads secreto ('in secret') where the Hebrew describes Shemaiah as 'shut in' or 'confined.'
- **6:14** — The Vulgate adds mei ('Remember me') with Domine ('Lord,' rather than 'my God') and uses the masculine noun in Noadiae prophetae ('Noadiah the prophet') rather than the Hebrew 'prophetess.'
- **6:16** — The Vulgate reads conciderent intra semetipsos ('collapsed within themselves') rather than the Hebrew 'fell greatly in their own eyes,' and a Deo ('by God') without 'our.'
