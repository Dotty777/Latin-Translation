## Text

**13:1** Now on that day it was read in the scroll of Moses in the hearing of the people, and it was found written in it that an Ammonite and a Moabite must not enter into the assembly of God forever,

**13:2** because they did not meet the sons of Israel with bread and water, and they hired Balaam against them to curse them; and our God turned the curse into a blessing.

**13:3** Now it happened, when they had heard the law, that they separated every foreigner from Israel.

**13:4** And Eliashib the priest was over this, who had been placed in charge of the treasury of the house of our God and was closely related to Tobiah.

**13:5** Therefore he made for himself a large treasury, and there before him they had been storing offerings, frankincense, vessels, and the tithe of grain, wine, and oil, the portions of the Levites, the singers, and the gatekeepers, and the priestly firstfruits.

**13:6** Now during all these things I was not in Jerusalem, because in the thirty-second year of Artaxerxes king of Babylon I went to the king, and at the end of some days I asked the king.

**13:7** And I came to Jerusalem, and I understood the evil that Eliashib had done for Tobiah, in making for him a treasure room in the forecourts of the house of God.

**13:8** And it seemed very evil to me. And I threw the furnishings of the house of Tobiah outside from the treasury;

**13:9** and I commanded, and they cleansed the treasuries; and I brought back there the vessels of the house of God, the sacrifice, and the frankincense.

**13:10** And I learned that the portions of the Levites had not been given, and that each one of the Levites, and of the singers, and of those who ministered had fled to his own region.

**13:11** And I brought a case against the magistrates and said, "Why have we abandoned the house of God?" And I gathered them together and made them stand at their stations.

**13:12** And all Judah brought the tithe of grain, wine, and oil into the storehouses.

**13:13** And we appointed over the storehouses Shelemiah the priest, and Zadok the scribe, and Pedaiah of the Levites, and next to them Hanan the son of Zaccur, the son of Mattaniah, because they were proved faithful, and to them were entrusted the portions of their brothers.

**13:14** Remember me, my God, for this, and do not blot out my acts of mercy that I have done in the house of my God and in its ceremonies.

**13:15** In those days I saw in Judah people treading winepresses on the Sabbath, carrying heaps, and loading onto donkeys wine, grapes, figs, and every load, and bringing them into Jerusalem on the Sabbath day. And I gave solemn warning that they should sell on a day on which it was lawful to sell.

**13:16** And Tyrians lived in it, bringing in fish and all kinds of merchandise, and they were selling on the Sabbaths to the sons of Judah in Jerusalem.

**13:17** And I rebuked the nobles of Judah and said to them, "What is this evil thing that you are doing, profaning the Sabbath day?

**13:18** Did not our fathers do these things, and our God bring all this evil upon us and upon this city? Yet you are adding wrath upon Israel by violating the Sabbath."

**13:19** Now it happened, when the gates of Jerusalem had grown quiet on the Sabbath day, that I spoke, and they shut the doors, and I commanded that they should not open them until after the Sabbath; and I appointed some of my servants over the gates, so that no one would bring in a load on the Sabbath day.

**13:20** And the merchants and those selling every kind of merchandise stayed outside Jerusalem once and twice.

**13:21** And I solemnly warned them and said to them, "Why are you staying opposite the wall? If you do this a second time, I will lay a hand on you." Therefore from that time on they did not come on the Sabbath.

**13:22** I also told the Levites that they should cleanse themselves and come to guard the gates and to sanctify the Sabbath day. Therefore for this also remember me, my God, and spare me according to the multitude of Your mercies.

**13:23** Moreover in those days I saw Jews taking Ashdodite, Ammonite, and Moabite wives.

**13:24** And their children spoke half in the language of Ashdod, and they did not know how to speak in the language of Judah, and they spoke according to the language of each people.

**13:25** And I rebuked them and cursed them. And I struck some of the men among them and made them bald, and I made them swear by God that they would not give their daughters to their sons, and would not take any of their daughters for their sons or for themselves, saying,

**13:26** "Did not Solomon king of Israel sin in a matter of this kind? And surely among many nations there was no king like him, and he was beloved by his God, and God set him as king over all Israel; yet foreign women led even him into sin.

**13:27** Shall we also, being disobedient, do all this great evil, to transgress against our God and take foreign wives?"

**13:28** Now one of the sons of Joiada, the son of Eliashib the high priest, was son-in-law to Sanballat the Horonite, and I drove him away from me.

**13:29** Remember, Lord my God, against those who defile the priesthood and the priestly and Levitical ordinance.

**13:30** Therefore I cleansed them from all foreigners, and I appointed the orders of the priests and of the Levites, each one in his ministry,

**13:31** and in the offering of wood at the appointed times, and in the firstfruits. Remember me, my God, for good. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **13:5** — The Vulgate reads 'for himself' (sibi) and 'before him' (ante eum), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads 'for him' (referring to Tobiah) and 'previously'.
- **13:11** — The Vulgate reads the first-person plural active 'Why have we forsaken' (Quare dereliquimus), including Nehemiah himself in the reproach, whereas the Hebrew reads passively 'Why is the house of God forsaken?'
- **13:15** — The Vulgate reads 'that they should sell on a day on which it was lawful to sell' (ut in die qua vendere liceret, venderent), whereas the Hebrew reads 'on the day they sold food'.
- **13:19** — The Vulgate reads 'when the gates of Jerusalem had grown quiet on the Sabbath day' (cum quievissent portæ Jerusalem in die sabbati), whereas the Hebrew refers to shadows falling on the gates before the Sabbath.
- **13:27** — The Vulgate reads 'Shall we also, being disobedient, do' (Numquid et nos inobedientes faciemus), whereas the Hebrew reads 'Shall we then listen to you to do'.
