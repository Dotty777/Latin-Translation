## Text

**8:1** And the seventh month had arrived, and the sons of Israel were in their cities. And all the people gathered together as one man to the square that is before the Water Gate, and they told Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law of Moses, which the Lord had commanded Israel.

**8:2** Therefore Ezra the priest brought the law before the multitude of men and women, and all who were able to understand, on the first day of the seventh month.

**8:3** And he read in it plainly in the square that was before the Water Gate, from morning until midday, in the sight of the men and women and the wise; and the ears of all the people were alert to the book.

**8:4** And Ezra the scribe stood upon a wooden platform, which he had made for speaking; and beside him stood Mattithiah, and Shemaiah, and Anaiah, and Uriah, and Hilkiah, and Maaseiah, at his right hand, and on the left, Pedaiah, Mishael, and Malchijah, and Hashum, and Hashbaddanah, Zechariah, and Meshullam.

**8:5** And Ezra opened the book before all the people, for indeed he was elevated above the entire people; and when he had opened it, all the people stood up.

**8:6** And Ezra blessed the Lord, the great God; and all the people answered, "Amen, amen," lifting up their hands; and they bowed down and worshiped God, prostrate to the ground.

**8:7** Furthermore, Jeshua, and Bani, and Sherebiah, Jamin, Akkub, Shabbethai, Hodiah, Maaseiah, Kelita, Azariah, Jozabad, Hanan, Pelaiah, the Levites, were maintaining silence among the people for the hearing of the law; and the people were standing in their station.

**8:8** And they read in the book of the law of God distinctly and plainly for understanding; and they understood as it was being read.

**8:9** And Nehemiah (that is, the Tirshatha), and Ezra the priest and scribe, and the Levites who were interpreting, said to the entire people, "The day is consecrated to the Lord our God; do not mourn, and do not weep." For all the people were weeping as they heard the words of the law.

**8:10** And he said to them, "Go, eat rich foods, and drink honeyed wine, and send portions to those who have not prepared for themselves, for the day of the Lord is holy; and do not be saddened, for indeed the joy of the Lord is our strength."

**8:11** And the Levites were maintaining silence among all the people, saying, "Be silent, for the day is holy, and do not grieve."

**8:12** So all the people went away to eat, and to drink, and to send portions, and to make great rejoicing, because they had understood the words that he had taught them.

**8:13** And on the second day the leaders of the families of the entire people, the priests and the Levites, gathered to Ezra the scribe, so that he might interpret to them the words of the law.

**8:14** And they found written in the law that the Lord had commanded by the hand of Moses that the sons of Israel should dwell in booths on the festival day, in the seventh month,

**8:15** and that they should proclaim and publish word in all their cities and in Jerusalem, saying, "Go out to the mountain, and bring boughs of olive, and boughs of the most beautiful tree, boughs of myrtle, and branches of palms, and boughs of a thickly leafed tree, so that booths may be made, as it is written."

**8:16** And the people went out and brought them. And they made booths for themselves, each one on his roof, and in their courtyards, and in the courtyards of the house of God, and in the square of the Water Gate, and in the square of the Gate of Ephraim.

**8:17** Therefore the entire assembly of those who had returned from captivity made booths and dwelt in booths; for the sons of Israel had not done so from the days of Joshua the son of Nun until that day. And there was exceedingly great rejoicing.

**8:18** And he read in the book of the law of God day by day, from the first day until the last day. And they observed the festival for seven days, and on the eighth day a solemn assembly according to the rite.

## Translator Notes

- **8:4** — The Vulgate reads singular 'quem fecerat ad loquendum' ("which he had made for speaking") rather than the Hebrew plural "which they had made for the purpose," and renders the second name as 'Semeia' (Shemaiah; Hebrew: Shema).
- **8:7** — The Vulgate reads 'silentium faciebant in populo ad audiendam legem' ("were maintaining silence among the people for the hearing of the law"), whereas the Hebrew has the Levites explaining the law to the people.
- **8:9** — Latin 'Athersatha' ("the Tirshatha") transliterates the Persian official title 'ha-tirshatha' ("the governor").
- **8:10** — The Vulgate translates sweet drink specifically as 'mulsum' ("honeyed wine") and reads first-person plural 'fortitudo nostra' ("our strength"), where the Hebrew has second-person plural ("your strength").
- **8:15** — The Vulgate reads 'ligni pulcherrimi' ("the most beautiful tree") where the Hebrew specifies the wild olive ("oil tree").
