## Text

**1:1** The words of Nehemiah son of Hilkiah. And it happened in the month of Chislev, in the twentieth year, that I was in the citadel of Susa.

**1:2** And Hanani, one of my brothers, came, he and men from Judah; and I questioned them about the Jews who had remained and survived from the captivity, and about Jerusalem.

**1:3** And they said to me, "Those who remained and were left from the captivity there in the province are in great affliction and in reproach; and the wall of Jerusalem is torn down, and its gates have been burned with fire."

**1:4** And when I had heard words of this kind, I sat down and wept and mourned for many days; I was fasting and praying before the face of the God of heaven,

**1:5** and I said, "I implore You, Lord God of heaven, strong, great, and fearsome, who keep covenant and mercy with those who love You and keep Your commandments:

**1:6** let Your ears be listening and Your eyes open, so that You may hear the prayer of Your servant, which I am praying before You today, night and day, for the sons of Israel, Your servants; and I confess for the sins of the sons of Israel, by which they have sinned against You; I and the house of my father have sinned;

**1:7** we have been led astray by vanity, and we have not kept Your commandment, and the ceremonies, and the judgments which You prescribed to Moses Your servant.

**1:8** Remember the word that You commanded Moses Your servant, saying, 'When you have transgressed, I will scatter you among the peoples;

**1:9** and if you return to Me, and keep My precepts, and do them, even if you have been carried away to the farthest parts of heaven, from there I will gather you and bring you back into the place which I have chosen so that My name might dwell there.'

**1:10** And they themselves are Your servants and Your people, whom You redeemed by Your great strength and by Your mighty hand.

**1:11** I plead with You, Lord, let Your ear be attentive to the prayer of Your servant, and to the prayer of Your servants who desire to fear Your name; and direct Your servant today, and give him mercy before this man." For I was the cupbearer of the king.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — "Hilkiah" translates the Vulgate's Helchiae (matching a Septuagint variant), where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads Hacaliah.
- **1:5** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew ha-El ("the God") as the adjective fortis ("strong"), producing three attributes: "strong, great, and fearsome."
- **1:7** — "We have been led astray by vanity" follows the Vulgate's vanitate seducti sumus, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "we have acted very corruptly against you." The Vulgate also reads singular "commandment" and "ceremonies" (caeremonias) for Hebrew "statutes."
- **1:11** — "Direct Your servant" translates the Vulgate's dirige servum tuum, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text asks God to "grant success" to His servant.
