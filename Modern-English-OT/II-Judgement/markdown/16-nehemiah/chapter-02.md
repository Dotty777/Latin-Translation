## Text

**2:1** Now it happened in the month of Nisan, in the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes, that wine was before him, and I lifted up the wine and gave it to the king; and I was as though ailing before his face.

**2:2** And the king said to me, "Why is your countenance sad, though I do not see you sick? This is not for nothing; rather, some evil or other is in your heart." And I feared greatly, even exceedingly,

**2:3** and I said to the king, "King, live forever! Why should my countenance not grieve, since the city, the house of the tombs of my father, is desolate, and its gates have been burned with fire?"

**2:4** And the king said to me, "For what thing are you asking?" And I prayed to the God of heaven,

**2:5** and I said to the king, "If it seems good to the king, and if your servant is pleasing before your face, send me into Judea, to the city of the tomb of my father, and I will build it."

**2:6** And the king said to me, and the queen who was sitting beside him, "Until what time will your journey be, and when will you return?" And it was pleasing before the countenance of the king, and he sent me; and I appointed a time for him.

**2:7** And I said to the king, "If it seems good to the king, let him give me letters to the governors of the region beyond the river, so that they may conduct me across until I come into Judea,

**2:8** and a letter to Asaph, the keeper of the king's forest, so that he may give me timber, that I may be able to cover the gates of the tower of the house, and the walls of the city, and the house that I will enter." And the king gave them to me according to the good hand of my God with me.

**2:9** And I came to the governors of the region beyond the river, and I gave them the letters of the king. Now the king had sent with me commanders of soldiers and horsemen.

**2:10** And Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite servant heard, and they were grieved with great affliction because a man had come who sought the prosperity of the sons of Israel.

**2:11** And I came to Jerusalem, and I was there for three days.

**2:12** And I rose up by night, I and a few men with me, and I did not tell anyone what God had put in my heart to do in Jerusalem; and there was no beast with me except the animal on which I was riding.

**2:13** And I went out through the Valley Gate by night, and before the Fountain of the Dragon, and to the Dung Gate, and I was inspecting the torn-down wall of Jerusalem and its gates consumed by fire.

**2:14** And I passed on to the Fountain Gate and to the king's aqueduct, and there was no room for the beast on which I was riding to pass through.

**2:15** And I went up along the torrent by night, and I was inspecting the wall; and turning back, I came to the Valley Gate and returned.

**2:16** Now the magistrates did not know where I had gone or what I was doing; and indeed, up to that point I had not told anything to the Jews, nor to the priests, nor to the nobles, nor to the magistrates, nor to the rest who were doing the work.

**2:17** And I said to them, "You know the affliction in which we are, that Jerusalem is desolate and its gates have been consumed by fire; come, and let us build the walls of Jerusalem, and let us no longer be a reproach."

**2:18** And I told them of the hand of my God, that it was good with me, and the words of the king which he had spoken to me, and I said, "Let us rise and build." And their hands were strengthened for good.

**2:19** Now Sanballat the Horonite, and Tobiah the Ammonite servant, and Geshem the Arab heard, and they mocked us and despised us, and said, "What is this thing that you are doing? Are you rebelling against the king?"

**2:20** And I returned them an answer, and said to them, "The God of heaven Himself helps us, and we are His servants; let us rise and build; for you, however, there is no portion, nor justice, nor memorial in Jerusalem."

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — "And I was as though ailing before his face" follows the Vulgate's affirmative et eram quasi languidus ante faciem ejus, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text has a negative ("and I had not been sad in his presence").
- **2:14** — "The king's aqueduct" translates the Vulgate's aquaeductum regis, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "the King's Pool."
- **2:18** — "And I said" follows the Vulgate's first-person singular et aio, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads third-person plural ("and they said").
- **2:20** — The Vulgate uses present indicative "helps us" (juvat) and hortatory subjunctive "let us rise and build" (surgamus et aedificemus), where the Hebrew uses future indicatives ("will give us success... we will arise and build").
