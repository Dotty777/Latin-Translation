## Text

**16:1** The lot of the sons of Joseph also fell from the Jordan opposite Jericho and its waters on the east: the wilderness that goes up from Jericho to the mountain of Bethel,

**16:2** and it goes out from Bethel, Luz, and crosses the border of Archi, Ataroth,

**16:3** and goes down to the west along the border of Japhleti, as far as the borders of Lower Beth-horon and Gezer; and its regions end at the Great Sea.

**16:4** And the sons of Joseph, Manasseh and Ephraim, took possession.

**16:5** And the border of the sons of Ephraim by their clans, and their possession toward the east, was Ataroth-addar as far as Upper Beth-horon.

**16:6** And the borders go out to the sea; now Michmethath faces the north, and it goes around the borders toward the east to Taanath-shiloh, and passes along on the east of Janoah.

**16:7** And it goes down from Janoah to Ataroth and Naarah, and reaches Jericho, and goes out to the Jordan.

**16:8** From Tappuah it passes along toward the sea to the valley of the reed bed, and its extremities are at the Saltiest Sea. This is the possession of the tribe of the sons of Ephraim by their families.

**16:9** And cities were set apart for the sons of Ephraim in the midst of the possession of the sons of Manasseh, and their villages.

**16:10** And the sons of Ephraim did not kill the Canaanite who lived in Gezer, and the Canaanite lived in the midst of Ephraim until this day as a tributary.

## Translator Notes

- **16:2** — Latin de Bethel Luza and terminum Archi, Ataroth omit directional prepositions (where Hebrew reads 'from Bethel to Luz' and 'the border of the Archites to Ataroth'), placing the names in apposition (cf. 18:13 identifying Luz with Bethel).
- **16:3** — Latin mari magno specifies 'the Great Sea' (the Mediterranean), whereas Hebrew has simply 'the sea.'
- **16:8** — Latin translates Hebrew Nahal Qanah ('Brook Kanah') as vallem arundineti ('the valley of the reed bed') and reads mare salsissimum ('the Saltiest Sea,' the Vulgate's usual name for the Dead Sea, cf. 18:19), whereas Hebrew reads simply 'to the sea' (the Mediterranean).
- **16:10** — Latin interfecerunt ('killed') differs from Hebrew horishu ('drove out' or 'dispossessed'), as also in 17:13.
