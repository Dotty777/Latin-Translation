## Text

**4:1** When they had crossed over, the Lord said to Joshua:

**4:2** "Choose twelve men, one from each tribe,

**4:3** and command them to take up from the middle of the riverbed of the Jordan, where the feet of the priests stood, twelve very hard stones, which you shall set in the place of the camp where you pitch tents tonight."

**4:4** And Joshua called the twelve men whom he had chosen from the sons of Israel, one from each tribe,

**4:5** and said to them, "Go before the ark of the Lord your God to the middle of the Jordan, and carry from there one stone each on your shoulders, according to the number of the sons of Israel,

**4:6** so that it may be a sign among you; and when your children ask you tomorrow, saying, 'What do these stones mean?'

**4:7** you shall answer them, 'The waters of the Jordan failed before the ark of the covenant of the Lord when it crossed over it; for this reason these stones have been set as a memorial for the sons of Israel forever.'"

**4:8** Therefore the sons of Israel did just as Joshua commanded them, carrying twelve stones from the middle of the riverbed of the Jordan, as the Lord had ordered him, according to the number of the sons of Israel, all the way to the place where they encamped, and they set them there.

**4:9** Joshua also set twelve other stones in the middle of the riverbed of the Jordan, where the priests who were carrying the ark of the covenant stood; and they are there until the present day.

**4:10** Now the priests who were carrying the ark remained standing in the middle of the Jordan until everything was completed that the Lord had commanded Joshua to speak to the people, and that Moses had told him. And the people hurried and crossed over.

**4:11** And when all had crossed over, the ark of the Lord also crossed over, and the priests proceeded before the people.

**4:12** The sons of Reuben and Gad and the half-tribe of Manasseh also went ahead of the sons of Israel armed, just as Moses had commanded them;

**4:13** and forty thousand warriors, by squadrons and columns, marched across the flatlands and plains of the city of Jericho.

**4:14** On that day the Lord magnified Joshua before all Israel, so that they feared him just as they had feared Moses while he was still alive.

**4:15** And He said to him:

**4:16** "Command the priests who carry the ark of the covenant to come up from the Jordan."

**4:17** And he commanded them, saying, "Come up from the Jordan."

**4:18** And when those carrying the ark of the covenant of the Lord had come up and had begun to tread on dry ground, the waters returned to their riverbed and flowed just as they had been accustomed to before.

**4:19** Now the people came up from the Jordan on the tenth day of the first month, and encamped at Gilgal opposite the eastern side of the city of Jericho.

**4:20** The twelve stones also, which they had taken from the riverbed of the Jordan, Joshua set at Gilgal,

**4:21** and said to the sons of Israel, "When your children ask their fathers tomorrow and say to them, 'What do these stones mean?'

**4:22** you shall teach them and say, 'Across the dry riverbed Israel crossed this Jordan,

**4:23** as the Lord your God dried up its waters in your sight until you crossed over,

**4:24** so that all the peoples of the lands may learn the most mighty hand of the Lord, so that you also may fear the Lord your God at all times.'"

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — Latin durissimos ("very hard") is a Vulgate-distinct rendering where the Hebrew refers to the firmness of the priests' standing-place.
- **4:5** — Latin juxta numerum filiorum Israël ("according to the number of the sons of Israel") omits "tribes," referring directly to the twelve patriarchs; the same Vulgate reading recurs in 4:8.
- **4:9** — Latin Alios quoque duodecim lapides ("twelve other stones") explicitly distinguishes a second monument set in the riverbed from the twelve stones carried to Gilgal, whereas the Hebrew lacks the word "other."
- **4:13** — Latin per turmas, et cuneos ("by squadrons and columns") renders the armed vanguard using Roman military terms for cavalry squadrons and wedge formations, where the Hebrew simply describes troops equipped for battle.
- **4:23** — This Latin text ends with Israel's crossing of the Jordan without the comparative clause about drying up the Red Sea found in the Hebrew and some Vulgate editions.
