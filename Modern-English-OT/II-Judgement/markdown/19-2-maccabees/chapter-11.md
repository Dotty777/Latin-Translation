## Text

**11:1** But a short time afterward, Lysias, the king's guardian and relative, and overseer of affairs, being greatly displeased over the things that had happened,

**11:2** having gathered eighty thousand and all the cavalry, was marching against the Jews, thinking that he would make the captured city a dwelling place for the Gentiles,

**11:3** and would hold the temple for monetary gain, like the other shrines of the Gentiles, and the priesthood for sale each year.

**11:4** In no way considering the power of God, but unbridled in mind, he trusted in the multitude of foot soldiers, and in the thousands of horsemen, and in the eighty elephants.

**11:5** Now having entered Judea and approaching Beth-zur, which was in a narrow place at a distance of five stadia from Jerusalem, he was assaulting that stronghold.

**11:6** And when Maccabeus and those who were with him learned that the strongholds were being assaulted, they and the whole crowd together pleaded with the Lord with weeping and tears, that He would send a good angel for the deliverance of Israel.

**11:7** And Maccabeus himself, having first taken up arms, urged the others to face danger together with him and to bring help to their brothers.

**11:8** And as they were advancing together with an eager spirit, there appeared at Jerusalem a horseman going ahead of them in bright white clothing and golden armor, brandishing a spear.

**11:9** Then all together they blessed the merciful Lord and grew strong in spirit, ready to penetrate not only men, but even the fiercest beasts and iron walls.

**11:10** Therefore they went forward eagerly, having a helper from heaven and the Lord showing mercy upon them.

**11:11** And rushing with force upon the enemies like lions, they struck down from among them eleven thousand foot soldiers and one thousand six hundred horsemen.

**11:12** And they put all of them to flight, and many of them escaped wounded and naked. Even Lysias himself escaped by fleeing shamefully.

**11:13** And because he was not senseless, considering within himself the loss that had happened to him, and understanding that the Hebrews were invincible, relying on the help of almighty God, he sent to them,

**11:14** and promised that he would consent to all things that are just, and that he would compel the king to become a friend.

**11:15** Now Maccabeus agreed to the requests of Lysias, having regard for what was beneficial in all things; and whatever Maccabeus wrote to Lysias concerning the Jews, the king granted.

**11:16** For letters had been written to the Jews by Lysias to this effect: "Lysias to the people of the Jews, greetings.

**11:17** John and Absalom, who had been sent by you, delivering the written documents, requested that I fulfill the things that were indicated through them.

**11:18** Therefore, whatever could be brought before the king, I laid out; and what the situation permitted, he granted.

**11:19** Therefore, if you keep good faith in affairs, I will also try from now on that there may be a cause of good things for you.

**11:20** And concerning the rest of the matters point by point, I have instructed by word of mouth both those men and these who have been sent by me to confer with you.

**11:21** Fare well. In the one hundred forty-eighth year, on the twenty-fourth day of the month of Dioscorus."

**11:22** Now the king's letter contained these things: "King Antiochus to his brother Lysias, greetings.

**11:23** Since our father has been transferred among the gods, we, wishing that those who are in our kingdom live without disturbance and apply diligence to their own affairs,

**11:24** have heard that the Jews did not consent to my father that they be transferred to the practice of the Greeks, but wish to hold to their own way of life, and for that reason request from us that their own lawful customs be granted to them.

**11:25** Wishing therefore that this nation also be at peace, we have resolved and decided that the temple be restored to them, so that they may live according to the custom of their ancestors.

**11:26** Therefore you will do well if you send to them and give the right hand, so that, having come to know our will, they may be in good spirits and devote themselves to their own interests."

**11:27** And to the Jews, the king's letter was as follows: "King Antiochus to the senate of the Jews and to the rest of the Jews, greetings.

**11:28** If you are well, you are as we wish; and we ourselves are also doing well.

**11:29** Menelaus has come to us, saying that you wish to go down to your own people who are with us.

**11:30** Therefore, to those who come and go up to the thirtieth day of the month of Xanthicus, we give right hands of security,

**11:31** so that the Jews may use their own foods and laws, just as before, and that none of them may in any way suffer trouble concerning the things that were done through ignorance.

**11:32** And we have also sent Menelaus to speak to you.

**11:33** Farewell. In the one hundred forty-eighth year, on the fifteenth day of the month of Xanthicus."

**11:34** And the Romans also sent a letter to this effect: "Quintus Memmius and Titus Manilius, envoys of the Romans, to the people of the Jews, greetings.

**11:35** As for the things that Lysias, the king's kinsman, granted to you, we also have granted them.

**11:36** But concerning the things that he judged should be referred to the king, send someone immediately, conferring more carefully among yourselves, so that we may decide as is fitting for you; for we are going to Antioch.

**11:37** And therefore hurry to write back, so that we also may know what your will is.

**11:38** Fare well. In the one hundred forty-eighth year, on the fifteenth day of the month of Xanthicus."

## Translator Notes

- **11:5** — The Vulgate reads in angusto loco ("in a narrow place"), whereas the Greek describes Beth-zur as a fortified place; both Latin and Greek state five stadia, though Beth-zur lies much farther south of Jerusalem.
- **11:19** — The Clementine Vulgate prints the third-person subjunctive esset ("that there may be a cause of good things for you"), whereas the Greek and some Latin witnesses have the infinitive esse ("to be a cause of good things for you").
- **11:21** — The Vulgate reads the month name Dioscori ("Dioscorus"), corresponding to the obscure Greek name Dioscorinthios, whose exact calendar identification is uncertain.
- **11:29** — Vulgate ad vestros, qui sunt apud nos ("to your own people who are with us") is a distinct Latin reading where the Greek speaks of going down to attend to one's own affairs or homes.
- **11:36** — The Clementine Vulgate prints the infinitive mittere with imperative force ("send"), where some Latin editions read mittite; Vulgate decernamus ("we may decide") also differs from the Greek ("we may explain").
