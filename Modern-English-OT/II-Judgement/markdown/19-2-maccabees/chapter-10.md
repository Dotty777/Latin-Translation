## Text

**10:1** Now Maccabeus and those who were with him, with the Lord protecting them, recovered the temple and the city.

**10:2** However, he demolished the altars that the foreigners had built throughout the streets, and likewise the shrines.

**10:3** And after the temple was purified, they made another altar, and having kindled fire from fiery stones, they offered sacrifices after two years, and they set out incense, lamps, and the loaves of the presentation.

**10:4** When these things had been done, they pleaded with the Lord, prostrate on the ground, that they would not again fall into such evils, but also, if at any time they should sin, that they would be disciplined more gently by Him and not be handed over to barbarous and blasphemous men.

**10:5** Now on the day on which the temple had been defiled by the foreigners, it happened that on that same day the purification took place, on the twenty-fifth day of the month that was Chislev.

**10:6** And with joy they celebrated for eight days in the manner of Tabernacles, remembering that a short time before they had kept the solemn day of Tabernacles in the mountains and in caves like wild beasts.

**10:7** For this reason they carried wreathed staffs, green branches, and palms before Him who had granted success for His place to be cleansed.

**10:8** And they decreed by a common ordinance and decree for the entire nation of the Jews to observe these days every year.

**10:9** And indeed, the departure from life of Antiochus, who was called the Noble, took place in this way.

**10:10** Now, however, we will recount the things that occurred concerning Eupator, the son of the ungodly Antiochus, summarizing the evils that occurred in the wars.

**10:11** For he, having assumed the kingdom, appointed over the affairs of the kingdom a certain Lysias, commander of the army of Phoenicia and Syria.

**10:12** For Ptolemy, who was called Macer, resolved to hold fast to justice toward the Jews, and especially because of the injustice that had been done against them, and to deal peacefully with them.

**10:13** But because of this, he was accused by the friends before Eupator; and since he frequently heard himself called a traitor because he had abandoned Cyprus, which had been entrusted to him by Philometor, and having gone over to Antiochus the Noble had also withdrawn from him, he ended his life with poison.

**10:14** Now Gorgias, since he was commander of the districts, having taken on strangers, frequently waged war against the Jews.

**10:15** Moreover, the Jews who held the strategic fortresses were taking in those driven out from Jerusalem and attempting to wage war.

**10:16** But those who were with Maccabeus, pleading with the Lord through prayers that He would be a helper to them, launched an assault against the fortresses of the Idumeans.

**10:17** And pressing on with great force, they captured the places, killed those who came against them, and altogether slaughtered no fewer than twenty thousand.

**10:18** Now when certain men had fled into two strongly fortified towers, having all the equipment for fighting back,

**10:19** Maccabeus, having left behind for their capture Simon, Joseph, and also Zacchaeus, along with those who were with them in considerable numbers, himself turned to the battles that were more pressing.

**10:20** But those who were with Simon, led by greed, were persuaded with money by some who were in the towers; and having accepted seventy thousand didrachmas, they let some escape.

**10:21** Now when it was reported to Maccabeus what had been done, having assembled the leaders of the people, he charged that they had sold their brothers for money, having released their adversaries.

**10:22** Therefore he put to death these men who had become traitors, and immediately seized the two towers.

**10:23** And conducting everything successfully with weapons and hands, he killed more than twenty thousand in the two fortresses.

**10:24** But Timothy, who previously had been defeated by the Jews, having summoned an army of a foreign multitude and having gathered Asian cavalry, arrived as though about to capture Judea by arms.

**10:25** Now Maccabeus and those who were with him, as he approached, implored the Lord, sprinkling their heads with earth, having their waists wrapped in sackcloth,

**10:26** falling down at the base of the altar, that He would be gracious to them, but an enemy to their enemies, and oppose their adversaries, just as the law says.

**10:27** And so after the prayer, having taken up their weapons, advancing some distance from the city and drawing close to the enemy, they halted.

**10:28** Now at the first rising of the sun, both sides engaged in battle: the one side having, along with valor, the Lord as the guarantor of victory and prosperity, while the other side had passion as their leader in war.

**10:29** But when the battle was fierce, five men on horses appeared from heaven to the adversaries, splendid with golden bridles, providing leadership to the Jews.

**10:30** Two of them, holding Maccabeus in the middle, preserved him unharmed, surrounded by their armor, while against the adversaries they hurled missiles and thunderbolts; because of this, both confounded with blindness and filled with turmoil, they fell.

**10:31** And twenty thousand five hundred were killed, and six hundred horsemen.

**10:32** But Timothy fled to Gazara, a fortified stronghold, over which Chaereas was in command.

**10:33** Now Maccabeus and those who were with him, rejoicing, besieged the stronghold for four days.

**10:34** But those who were inside, trusting in the strength of the place, cursed beyond measure and hurled abominable words.

**10:35** But when the fifth day was dawning, twenty young men from those who were with Maccabeus, inflamed in spirit because of the blasphemy, bravely approached the wall, and advancing with fierce courage, they began to climb up.

**10:36** And others also, climbing up in the same way, undertook to set fire to the towers and gates and to burn those revilers themselves alive.

**10:37** Now after the stronghold had been laid waste for two continuous days, they put to death Timothy, who was found hiding himself in a certain place, and they killed his brother Chaereas and Apollophanes.

**10:38** When these things had been done, with hymns and thanksgivings they blessed the Lord, who did great things in Israel and gave victory to them.

## Translator Notes

- **10:7** — The Vulgate omits the Greek reference to offering hymns, making the wreathed staffs (Latin thyrsos, festive staffs entwined with foliage), green branches, and palms the direct objects carried before the Lord.
- **10:9** — "The Noble" renders Latin Nobilis, the Vulgate's translation of the Greek royal epithet Epiphanes (so also in 10:13).
- **10:12** — The Clementine Vulgate gives Ptolemy's surname as Macer, whereas the Greek text reads Macron.
- **10:15** — The Clementine Vulgate reads Judaei ("the Jews"), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads "the Idumeans" (compare 10:16).
- **10:20** — The Vulgate specifies didrachmas (double drachmas), whereas the Greek text has seventy thousand drachmas.
