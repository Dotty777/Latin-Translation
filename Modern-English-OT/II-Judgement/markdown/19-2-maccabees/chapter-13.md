## Text

**13:1** In the one hundred forty-ninth year, Judas learned that Antiochus Eupator was coming with a multitude against Judea,

**13:2** and with him Lysias, the guardian and overseer of affairs, having with him one hundred ten thousand foot soldiers, and five thousand horsemen, and twenty-two elephants, and three hundred chariots with scythes.

**13:3** Menelaus also joined himself with them, and with great deceit appealed to Antiochus, not for the safety of his homeland, but hoping to be appointed to the leadership.

**13:4** But the King of kings roused the anger of Antiochus against the sinner; and when Lysias suggested that this man was the cause of all the evils, he ordered (as is their custom) that he be seized and put to death in that same place.

**13:5** Now in that same place there was a tower of fifty cubits, having a heap of ashes all around; this tower looked down over a sheer drop.

**13:6** From there he ordered the sacrilegious man to be thrown down into the ashes, while all pushed him forward to destruction.

**13:7** And by such a law it happened that the transgressor of the law died, and Menelaus was not given to the earth.

**13:8** And indeed justly enough; for because he committed many offenses against the altar of God, whose fire and ashes were holy, he himself was condemned to a death of ashes.

**13:9** But the king was coming, unbridled in mind, intending to show himself to the Jews more wicked than his father.

**13:10** When Judas learned of these things, he commanded the people to call upon the Lord day and night, so that, as always, He might help them now also,

**13:11** since indeed they feared being deprived of the law, their homeland, and the holy temple; and so that He would not allow the people, who had recently caught their breath a little, to be subjected again to blasphemous nations.

**13:12** Therefore, while all did this together and sought mercy from the Lord with weeping and fasts, lying prostrate for three continuous days, Judas urged them to prepare themselves.

**13:13** And he himself, together with the elders, resolved, before the king brought his army up to Judea and took possession of the city, to go out and commit the outcome of the matter to the judgment of the Lord.

**13:14** Therefore, giving authority over all things to God, the Creator of the world, and having urged his men to fight bravely and to stand even to death for the laws, the temple, the city, the homeland, and the citizens, he stationed the army around Modin.

**13:15** And having given his men the watchword of God's victory, with the bravest young men chosen, he attacked the royal pavilion by night and killed four thousand men in the camp, and the largest of the elephants along with those who had been placed upon it;

**13:16** and filling the camp of the enemies with the greatest fear and confusion, having carried out their undertaking successfully, they departed.

**13:17** And this took place as day was dawning, with the protection of the Lord helping him.

**13:18** But the king, having received a taste of the boldness of the Jews, tested the difficulty of the terrain by stratagem,

**13:19** and moved his camp up to Beth-zur, which was a fortified stronghold of the Jews; but he was driven back, he stumbled, he was diminished.

**13:20** And Judas kept sending necessities to those who were inside.

**13:21** But a certain Rhodocus from the Jewish army disclosed secrets to the enemies; when tracked down, he was seized and imprisoned.

**13:22** Again the king held talks with those who were in Beth-zur; he gave the right hand, received it, departed;

**13:23** he engaged Judas in battle, and was defeated. But when he learned that Philip, who had been left over affairs, had rebelled in Antioch, dismayed in mind, appealing to the Jews and yielding to them, he swore concerning all things that seemed just; and having been reconciled, he offered a sacrifice, honored the temple, and presented gifts.

**13:24** He embraced Maccabeus and made him commander and ruler from Ptolemais to the Gerrenians.

**13:25** But when he came to Ptolemais, the people of Ptolemais took the agreement of friendship badly, being indignant, for fear that perhaps they might violate the treaty.

**13:26** Then Lysias ascended the tribunal, and explained the reason, and calmed the people, and returned to Antioch; and in this way the king's departure and return proceeded.

## Translator Notes

- **13:2** — The Vulgate reads 'five thousand' horsemen, whereas the Greek has 'five thousand three hundred.'
- **13:4** — The Vulgate reads in eodem loco ('in that same place'; so also in v. 5), whereas the Greek names the city of Beroea.
- **13:7** — The Vulgate creates a deliberate wordplay between tali lege ('by such a law') and praevaricatorem legis ('the transgressor of the law'), where the Greek reads 'by such a fate.'
- **13:24** — In the Vulgate, the king appoints Maccabeus himself as commander and ruler (ducem et principem), whereas the Greek states that he left Hegemonides as governor.
- **13:25** — The Vulgate clause indignantes ne forte foedus irrumperent ('being indignant, for fear that perhaps they might violate the treaty') diverges in syntax from the Greek, which states that they were angry at the stipulations and wanted to annul the agreements.
