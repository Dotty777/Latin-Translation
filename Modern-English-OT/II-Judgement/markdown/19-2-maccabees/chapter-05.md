## Text

**5:1** At the same time, Antiochus prepared a second expedition into Egypt.

**5:2** Now it happened throughout the entire city of Jerusalem that for forty days there were seen horsemen riding back and forth through the air, wearing gilded robes and armed with spears, like cohorts,

**5:3** and charges of horses arranged in ranks, and clashes taking place at close quarters, and movements of shields, and a multitude of helmeted men with drawn swords, and the hurling of missiles, and the gleam of golden armor and of breastplates of every kind.

**5:4** Therefore everyone prayed that the portents might be turned to good.

**5:5** But when a false rumor had gone out as though Antiochus had departed from life, Jason, taking no fewer than a thousand men, suddenly attacked the city; and while the citizens rushed together to the wall, when the city was at last captured, Menelaus fled into the citadel.

**5:6** Jason, however, did not spare his own citizens in the slaughter, nor did he consider that success against kinsmen is the greatest evil, supposing that he was going to capture trophies of enemies and not of citizens.

**5:7** And indeed he did not obtain the leadership, but received disgrace as the end of his plots, and as a fugitive he went away again into Ammanitis.

**5:8** At last, hemmed in to his own ruin by Aretas, tyrant of the Arabs, fleeing from city to city, hateful to all as a deserter of the laws and detestable as an enemy of his homeland and citizens, he was driven out into Egypt;

**5:9** and he who had driven many out of his homeland perished in a foreign land, having set out to the Lacedaemonians as though he would have a refuge there on account of kinship;

**5:10** and he who had cast out many unburied was himself cast out both unmourned and unburied, neither receiving a foreign burial nor sharing in an ancestral tomb.

**5:11** So when these things had taken place, the king suspected that the Jews were about to abandon the alliance; and because of this, setting out from Egypt with ferocious rage, he captured the city by force of arms.

**5:12** And he ordered the soldiers to kill, not to spare those they encountered, and to slaughter those going up through the houses.

**5:13** So there took place slaughters of young men and old men, destructions of women and children, and killings of virgins and infants.

**5:14** Now in three whole days there were eighty thousand killed, forty thousand bound, and no fewer sold.

**5:15** But not even these things are enough: he also dared to enter the temple holier than the whole earth, with Menelaus as his guide, who was a traitor to the laws and his homeland;

**5:16** and taking with wicked hands the holy vessels that had been placed by other kings and cities for the adornment and glory of the place, he handled them unworthily and defiled them.

**5:17** Thus, beside himself in mind, Antiochus did not consider that because of the sins of those inhabiting the city, God had been angry for a little while, on account of which disregard also came about concerning the place.

**5:18** Otherwise, had it not happened that they were entangled in many sins, just like Heliodorus, who was sent by King Seleucus to plunder the treasury, this man too, immediately upon arriving, would certainly have been flogged and driven back from his audacity.

**5:19** Yet God did not choose the nation for the sake of the place, but the place for the sake of the nation.

**5:20** Therefore the place itself also became a sharer in the evils of the people, but afterward it will become a partner in good things; and that which was abandoned in the wrath of Almighty God will again be exalted with the highest glory in the reconciliation of the great Lord.

**5:21** Therefore Antiochus, having carried off one thousand eight hundred talents from the temple, swiftly returned to Antioch, imagining in his pride that he would bring the land to be sailed on and the sea to be traveled over, because of the haughtiness of his mind.

**5:22** He also left governors to afflict the nation: in Jerusalem, Philip, a Phrygian by birth, more cruel in character than the very one by whom he was appointed;

**5:23** and at Gerizim, Andronicus and Menelaus, who pressed upon the citizens more severely than the rest.

**5:24** And since he was set against the Jews, he sent the hateful commander Apollonius with an army of twenty-two thousand, commanding him to kill all of full age and to sell the women and youths.

**5:25** When he had come to Jerusalem, pretending peace, he remained quiet until the holy day of the Sabbath; and then, while the Jews were keeping the day of rest, he commanded his men to take up arms.

**5:26** And he slaughtered all who had come out to the spectacle; and rushing through the city with armed men, he put to death a huge multitude.

**5:27** But Judas Maccabeus, who had been the tenth, had withdrawn into a deserted place, and there he passed his life among wild beasts in the mountains with his men; and feeding on grass for food, they remained there, so that they might not be sharers in the defilement.

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — The Vulgate reads galeatorum ("of helmeted men"), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads kamaton ("of spear-shafts").
- **5:14** — The Vulgate reads vincti ("bound"), differing from the Greek Septuagint, which describes forty thousand killed in hand-to-hand combat.
- **5:23** — The Vulgate groups Menelaus together with Andronicus at Gerizim with a plural verb (imminebant), whereas the Greek Septuagint separates Menelaus from Andronicus at Gerizim.
- **5:24** — The Vulgate renders Greek mysarchen ("commander of the Mysians") as odiosum principem ("the hateful commander").
- **5:27** — Latin qui decimus fuerat ("who had been the tenth") literally translates a Greek idiom meaning Judas withdrew with about nine others, forming a band of ten.
