## Text

**12:1** When these agreements had been made, Lysias proceeded to the king, while the Jews attended to agriculture.

**12:2** But those who had remained behind, Timothy, and Apollonius the son of Gennaeus, and also Hieronymus, and Demophon besides these, and Nicanor the Cypriarch, did not allow them to live in stillness and quiet.

**12:3** And the people of Joppa perpetrated a certain outrage of this kind: they asked the Jews with whom they lived to board the boats that they had prepared, with their wives and children, as though no hostility lay hidden between them.

**12:4** Therefore, according to the common decree of the city, while they themselves consented and for the sake of peace had no suspicion, when they had gone out into deep water, they drowned no fewer than two hundred.

**12:5** When Judas learned of this cruelty committed against the people of his own nation, he gave orders to the men who were with him; and having called upon God, the righteous Judge,

**12:6** he came against the murderers of his brothers, and set fire to the harbor at night, burned up the boats, and put to death with the sword those who had fled from the fire.

**12:7** And when he had done these things in this way, he departed as though about to return again and uproot all the people of Joppa.

**12:8** But when he learned that those who were in Jamnia also intended to act in the same way toward the Jews living with them,

**12:9** he came upon the people of Jamnia also by night and set fire to the harbor with the ships, so that the light of the fire appeared in Jerusalem from two hundred forty stadia away.

**12:10** When they had already gone nine stadia from there and were marching toward Timothy, Arabs engaged him in battle, five thousand men and five hundred horsemen.

**12:11** And when a fierce battle took place and by the help of God turned out favorably, the remaining Arabs, defeated, asked Judas for the right hand to be given to them, promising that they would give pastures and be helpful in other matters.

**12:12** And Judas, judging them truly useful in many respects, promised peace; and after right hands were received, they departed to their tents.

**12:13** He also attacked a certain strong city enclosed by bridges and walls, which was inhabited by crowds of mixed nations, whose name was Caspin.

**12:14** But those who were inside, trusting in the firmness of the walls and their supply of provisions, acted rather carelessly, provoking Judas with curses and blaspheming, and speaking what is not lawful.

**12:15** Maccabeus, however, having called upon the great Ruler of the world, who without battering rams and siege engines threw down Jericho in the days of Joshua, charged fiercely at the walls;

**12:16** and having captured the city through the will of the Lord, he carried out countless slaughters, so that the adjacent lake, two stadia in width, seemed to flow with the blood of those who were killed.

**12:17** From there they marched seven hundred fifty stadia and came to Charax, to the Jews who are called Tubians;

**12:18** and they did not capture Timothy in those places, and having accomplished nothing he had withdrawn, leaving a very strong garrison in a certain place.

**12:19** Now Dositheus and Sosipater, who were commanders with Maccabeus, killed those left by Timothy in the garrison, ten thousand men.

**12:20** But Maccabeus, having arranged six thousand around himself and organized them by cohorts, advanced against Timothy, who had with him one hundred twenty thousand foot soldiers and two thousand five hundred horsemen.

**12:21** When the arrival of Judas became known, Timothy sent ahead the women and children and the rest of the baggage to the stronghold that is called Carnion; for it was impregnable and difficult of access because of the narrowness of the terrain.

**12:22** And when the first cohort of Judas appeared, terror was struck into the enemies from the presence of God, who sees all things; and they were turned to flight one from another, so that they were cast down more by their own men and crippled by the blows of their own swords.

**12:23** And Judas pressed on fiercely, punishing the profane, and struck down thirty thousand men from among them.

**12:24** Timothy himself, however, fell among the forces of Dositheus and Sosipater, and with many pleas he begged to be released alive, because he held the parents and brothers of many of the Jews, who through his death might happen to be undone.

**12:25** And when he had given his word that he would restore them according to the agreement, they released him unharmed for the safety of their brothers.

**12:26** Then Judas marched out to Carnion, and twenty-five thousand were killed.

**12:27** After the flight and slaughter of these, he moved the army to Ephron, a fortified city, in which a multitude of diverse nations lived; and strong young men, taking their stand before the walls, fought back bravely; and in it there were many siege engines and a supply of missiles.

**12:28** But when they had called upon the Almighty, who by His power shatters the strength of enemies, they captured the city, and of those who were inside they struck down twenty-five thousand.

**12:29** From there they went away to the city of the Scythians, which was six hundred stadia away from Jerusalem.

**12:30** But as the Jews who were among the people of Scythopolis testified that they were treated kindly by them, and that even in times of misfortune they had dealt gently with them,

**12:31** giving thanks to them and urging them for the future also to be kind toward their nation, they came to Jerusalem as the solemn day of Weeks was approaching.

**12:32** And after Pentecost they went out against Gorgias, the governor of Idumea.

**12:33** And he came out with three thousand foot soldiers and four hundred horsemen.

**12:34** When they clashed, it happened that a few of the Jews fell.

**12:35** Now a certain Dositheus, a horseman of Bacenor's men, a brave man, had hold of Gorgias; and while he wanted to capture him alive, a certain horseman of the Thracians rushed upon him and severed his shoulder, and so Gorgias escaped to Maresha.

**12:36** But when those who were with Esdris had fought for a long time and were exhausted, Judas called upon the Lord to become helper and leader of the battle,

**12:37** and beginning in the ancestral language and raising a shout with hymns, he put the soldiers of Gorgias to flight.

**12:38** Then Judas, having gathered the army, came to the city of Adullam; and since the seventh day was arriving, having purified themselves according to custom, they kept the Sabbath in that same place.

**12:39** And on the following day Judas came with his men to take up the bodies of those who had been struck down and place them with their parents in the ancestral tombs.

**12:40** But under the tunics of those who had been killed they found some of the votive offerings of the idols that were at Jamnia, which the law forbids to the Jews; therefore it became evident to all that they had fallen for this reason.

**12:41** Therefore all blessed the righteous judgment of the Lord, who had made hidden things evident;

**12:42** and so, turning to prayer, they asked that the offense that had been committed be given over to oblivion. And the most valiant Judas urged the people to keep themselves without sin, seeing before their eyes what had happened because of the sins of those who were struck down.

**12:43** And having made a collection, he sent twelve thousand drachmas of silver to Jerusalem for a sacrifice to be offered for the sins of the dead, thinking rightly and devoutly about the resurrection

**12:44** (for if he were not hoping that those who had fallen would rise again, it would seem superfluous and vain to pray for the dead),

**12:45** and because he considered that those who had fallen asleep with godliness had the finest grace stored up. Therefore it is a holy and salutary thought to pray earnestly for the deceased, so that they may be released from sins.

## Translator Notes

- **12:11** — Latin pascua literally means 'pastures,' whereas the Greek reads boskemata ('livestock' or 'cattle').
- **12:15** — Latin Jesu (reflecting Greek Iesou) refers here to Joshua, son of Nun, under whom the walls of Jericho fell (Josh 6).
- **12:26** — The Vulgate reads simply 'to Carnion' without mentioning the temple of Atargatis (Atargateion) found in the Greek text.
- **12:41** — The Vulgate reads justum judicium Domini ('the righteous judgment of the Lord'), whereas the Greek reads 'the Lord, the righteous judge.'
- **12:43** — The Vulgate specifies duodecim millia ('twelve thousand') drachmas of silver and explicitly adds pro peccatis mortuorum ('for the sins of the dead'), whereas the Greek has 'two thousand' drachmas for a sin offering.
