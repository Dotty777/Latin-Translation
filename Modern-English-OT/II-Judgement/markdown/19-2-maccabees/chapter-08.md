## Text

**8:1** Now Judas Maccabeus and those who were with him were secretly entering the villages, and calling together their relatives and friends, and taking in those who remained in Judaism, they led out to themselves six thousand men.

**8:2** And they called upon the Lord that He would look upon the people who were being trampled down by all, and have mercy on the temple that was being defiled by the ungodly;

**8:3** that He would also have mercy on the destruction of the city that was about to be leveled at once, and hear the voice of the blood crying out to Him;

**8:4** and that He would also remember the most unjust deaths of the innocent little children, and the blasphemies directed against His name, and be indignant over these things.

**8:5** But Maccabeus, once a multitude had been gathered together, was becoming unbearable to the nations, for the wrath of the Lord was turned into mercy.

**8:6** And coming unexpectedly upon villages and cities, he set them on fire, and seizing advantageous places, he inflicted not a few slaughters upon the enemies.

**8:7** Moreover, he set out especially by night for raids of this kind, and the report of his valor was spreading everywhere.

**8:8** Now Philip, seeing that the man was gradually coming to advancement and that affairs were more frequently turning out swiftly for him, wrote to Ptolemy, the commander of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, that he should bring aid to the king's affairs.

**8:9** And he quickly sent Nicanor, son of Patroclus, one of the foremost friends, having given him no fewer than twenty thousand armed men from the mixed nations, to destroy the entire race of the Jews, with Gorgias joined to him, a military man and most experienced in matters of war.

**8:10** Now Nicanor resolved to make up for the king, from the captivity of the Jews, the tribute of two thousand talents that was to be given to the Romans.

**8:11** And immediately he sent to the coastal cities, calling them to the purchase of Jewish slaves, promising that he would sell off ninety slaves for a talent, having no regard for the vengeance that was about to overtake him from the Almighty.

**8:12** Now when Judas found out, he made known Nicanor's coming to the Jews who were with him.

**8:13** Some of them, being afraid and not believing in the justice of God, turned to flight.

**8:14** Others, however, sold whatever remained to them, and at the same time pleaded with the Lord that He would rescue them from the ungodly Nicanor, who had sold them before he came to close quarters,

**8:15** if not for their sake, yet for the sake of the covenant that was with their fathers, and for the sake of the invocation of His holy and magnificent name upon them.

**8:16** Now Maccabeus, having called together the seven thousand who were with him, urged them not to be reconciled to the enemies, nor to fear the multitude of enemies coming unjustly against them, but to fight bravely,

**8:17** keeping before their eyes the outrage that had been unjustly inflicted upon the holy place by them, and likewise the wrong done to the city held in mockery, and furthermore the overturned institutions of the ancients.

**8:18** "For they indeed trust in weapons," he said, "and in boldness at the same time; but we trust in the Almighty Lord, who is able with a single nod to destroy both those coming against us and the entire world."

**8:19** Moreover, he also reminded them of the acts of aid from God that were performed for their ancestors, and how under Sennacherib one hundred eighty-five thousand perished,

**8:20** and of the battle that they had against the Galatians in Babylonia: how when it came to the engagement, while their Macedonian allies hesitated, all of them, six thousand alone, killed one hundred twenty thousand because of the help given to them from heaven, and obtained very many rewards for these things.

**8:21** By these words they were made steadfast, and ready to die for the laws and their homeland.

**8:22** Therefore he appointed his brothers as commanders for each division—Simon, Joseph, and Jonathan—placing one thousand five hundred under each one.

**8:23** In addition to this, after the holy book had been read to them by Ezra, and after the watchword of God's help had been given, he himself, as commander in the front line, engaged in battle with Nicanor.

**8:24** And with the Almighty having become their helper, they killed more than nine thousand men, and they forced the greater part of Nicanor's army, disabled by wounds, to flee.

**8:25** And having carried off the money of those who had come to purchase them, they pursued them in every direction;

**8:26** but they turned back, constrained by the hour, for it was before the Sabbath; for this reason they did not persist in pursuing them.

**8:27** Now gathering their weapons and spoils, they observed the Sabbath, blessing the Lord, who delivered them on that day, distilling the beginning of mercy upon them.

**8:28** After the Sabbath, however, they divided the spoils among the disabled, the orphans, and the widows, and the remainder they themselves kept along with their own people.

**8:29** Therefore, when these things had been done, and a supplication had been made in common by all, they implored the merciful Lord that He would be completely reconciled to His servants.

**8:30** And of those who were with Timothy and Bacchides fighting against them, they killed more than twenty thousand, and they captured high strongholds, and divided abundant plunder, making an equal share for the disabled, the orphans, and the widows, and also for the elderly.

**8:31** And when they had carefully collected their weapons, they stored them all in advantageous places, while the remaining spoils they brought to Jerusalem.

**8:32** And they killed Philarches, who was with Timothy, a wicked man who had afflicted the Jews in many ways.

**8:33** And while they were holding victory celebrations in Jerusalem, they burned the one who had burned the sacred gates, that is, Callisthenes, when he had fled into a small house, a fitting reward having been repaid to him for his ungodly deeds.

**8:34** Now the most villainous Nicanor, who had brought a thousand merchants for the sale of the Jews,

**8:35** having been humbled by the help of the Lord through those whom he had regarded as nothing, having laid aside his garment of glory and fleeing through the inland regions, came alone to Antioch, having attained the utmost misfortune over the destruction of his army.

**8:36** And he who had promised the Romans that he would pay back the tribute from the captivity of Jerusalem now proclaimed that the Jews had God as their protector, and that on account of Him they were invulnerable, because they followed the laws established by Him.

## Translator Notes

- **8:8** — The Clementine Vulgate reads propere ("swiftly"), whereas some Latin witnesses read prospere ("prosperously"), matching the Greek.
- **8:16** — The Vulgate reads "seven thousand" (septem millibus), whereas the Greek text reads "six thousand," matching 8:1 and the four divisions of 1,500 in 8:22.
- **8:20** — The Vulgate gives the Jewish force as "six thousand" (sex millia) and does not specify the number of Macedonian allies, whereas the Greek reads "eight thousand" Jews alongside "four thousand" Macedonians.
- **8:23** — The Vulgate names "Ezra" (ab Esdra) as the reader of the holy book, whereas the Greek text names "Eleazar."
- **8:32** — The Vulgate treats the Greek title phylarches ("cavalry commander" or "tribal leader") as a proper name, Philarches (accusative Philarchen).
