## Text

**14:1** But after a period of three years, Judas and those who were with him learned that Demetrius, son of Seleucus, had gone up with a strong multitude and ships through the gate of Tripolis to advantageous places,

**14:2** and had occupied the regions against Antiochus and his commander Lysias.

**14:3** Now a certain Alcimus, who had been high priest, but had voluntarily defiled himself during the times of mingling, considering that in no way was there safety for him nor access to the altar,

**14:4** came to King Demetrius in the one hundred and fiftieth year, offering him a golden crown and a palm, and in addition to these also boughs that seemed to belong to the temple. And on that very day he kept silent.

**14:5** But having found an opportune time for his madness, when he was summoned by Demetrius to the council and asked on what things and plans the Jews relied,

**14:6** he answered, "Those very ones among the Jews who are called Hasideans, whom Judas Maccabeus leads, foster wars and stir up seditions, and do not allow the kingdom to be tranquil;

**14:7** for I myself also, defrauded of the glory of my ancestors (I mean the high priesthood), have come here:

**14:8** first indeed keeping faith with the interests of the king, and second also looking out for the citizens; for by their wickedness our entire nation is harassed in no small measure.

**14:9** But I beg you, king, once each of these matters is known, provide for both the region and the nation, according to your kindness that is widely known to all;

**14:10** for as long as Judas survives, it is impossible for affairs to be at peace."

**14:11** Now when such things had been spoken by him, the rest of the friends also, being hostilely disposed toward Judas, inflamed Demetrius.

**14:12** He immediately sent Nicanor, who was in charge of the elephants, into Judea as commander,

**14:13** having given orders that he should capture Judas himself, but scatter those who were with him, and appoint Alcimus as high priest of the greatest temple.

**14:14** Then the Gentiles who had fled from Judas out of Judea joined themselves to Nicanor in droves, considering the miseries and disasters of the Jews to be the prosperity of their own affairs.

**14:15** Therefore, when the Jews heard of the arrival of Nicanor and the assembly of the nations, sprinkled with earth, they pleaded with Him who established His people to guard them forever, and who protects His own portion with evident signs.

**14:16** Now when the commander gave the order, they immediately moved out from there and assembled at the stronghold of Dessau.

**14:17** Now Simon, the brother of Judas, had engaged in battle with Nicanor, but he was terrified by the sudden arrival of the adversaries.

**14:18** Yet Nicanor, hearing of the valor of the companions of Judas and the greatness of spirit that they had for the struggles of their homeland, was afraid to make the decision by blood.

**14:19** For this reason he sent ahead Posidonius, Theodotius, and Matthias, so that they might give and receive right hands.

**14:20** And when deliberation had gone on for a long time concerning these matters, and the commander himself had reported it to the multitude, the single decision of all was to agree to friendship.

**14:21** Therefore they appointed a day on which they might confer privately with one another; and seats were brought out and placed for each of them.

**14:22** Now Judas ordered armed men to be in advantageous places, in case any evil might suddenly arise from the enemies; and they held a fitting conference.

**14:23** Now Nicanor stayed in Jerusalem and did nothing unjustly; and he dismissed the throngs of the crowds that had been gathered together.

**14:24** Moreover, he always held Judas dear from his heart, and he was inclined toward the man.

**14:25** And he asked him to take a wife and father children. He married, lived quietly, and they shared a common life.

**14:26** But Alcimus, seeing their affection for one another and their agreements, came to Demetrius and kept saying that Nicanor was assenting to alien interests, and had appointed Judas, a plotter against the kingdom, as his own successor.

**14:27** Therefore the king, enraged and provoked by this man's most wicked accusations, wrote to Nicanor, saying that he was indeed gravely displeased over the agreement of friendship, yet that he commanded him to send Maccabeus bound to Antioch quickly.

**14:28** When he learned of these things, Nicanor was dismayed and was gravely distressed if he should make void the things that had been agreed upon, having been wronged in nothing by the man;

**14:29** but because he could not resist the king, he watched for an opportunity by which he might carry out the command.

**14:30** But Maccabeus, seeing that Nicanor was dealing more sternly with him and conducting their customary meetings more roughly, and understanding that this sternness came from no good, having gathered a few of his own men, hid himself from Nicanor.

**14:31** When he recognized this, that he had been boldly forestalled by the man, he came to the greatest and most holy temple; and while the priests were offering the customary sacrifices, he ordered the man to be handed over to him.

**14:32** When they declared with an oath that they did not know where the one who was being sought was, stretching out his hand toward the temple,

**14:33** he swore, saying, "Unless you hand Judas over to me bound, I will bring this sanctuary of God down to level ground, and I will dig up the altar, and I will consecrate this temple to Father Liber."

**14:34** And having said these things, he departed. But the priests, stretching out their hands toward heaven, called upon Him who was always the defender of their nation, saying these words:

**14:35** "You, Lord of all things, who have need of nothing, have willed that the temple of Your dwelling be made among us.

**14:36** And now, Holy One of holies, Lord of all, preserve forever undefiled this house that was recently cleansed."

**14:37** Now a certain Razis, one of the elders from Jerusalem, was denounced to Nicanor, a man who was a lover of the city and well spoken of, who because of his affection was called the father of the Jews.

**14:38** For a long time this man maintained a resolve of self-restraint in Judaism, and was content to surrender body and soul for perseverance.

**14:39** Now Nicanor, wanting to display the hatred that he had against the Jews, sent five hundred soldiers to seize him.

**14:40** For he thought that if he ensnared him, he would inflict the greatest disaster upon the Jews.

**14:41** Now while the crowds were eager to rush into his house, break down the door, and apply fire to it, since he was now about to be seized, he struck at himself with a sword,

**14:42** choosing to die nobly rather than become subject to sinners and be treated with unworthy outrages contrary to his birth.

**14:43** But because in his haste he had not dealt the wound with a sure stroke, and the crowds were bursting inside the doors, running back boldly to the wall, he threw himself headlong manfully into the crowds;

**14:44** and as they quickly gave way to his fall, he came down on the middle of his neck.

**14:45** And while he was still breathing, inflamed in spirit, he stood up; and although his blood was flowing down in a great stream and he was stricken with the most severe wounds, he passed through the crowd at a run;

**14:46** and standing upon a certain steep rock, and having now become drained of blood, grasping his own intestines, he threw them with both hands upon the crowds, calling upon the Sovereign of life and spirit that He would restore these to him again; and thus he departed from life.

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — The Vulgate reads per portam Tripolis ("through the gate of Tripolis"), whereas the Greek reads "into the harbor at Tripolis."
- **14:2** — The Vulgate reads tenuisse regiones adversus Antiochum ("had occupied the regions against Antiochus"), whereas the Greek reports that Demetrius had done away with Antiochus and Lysias.
- **14:19** — The Vulgate reads Theodotium ("Theodotius") and Matthiam ("Matthias"), where the Greek has "Theodotus" and "Mattathias"; giving and receiving right hands is an ancient idiom for pledging peace.
- **14:33** — The Vulgate reads Libero patri ("Father Liber," the Roman deity of wine), where the Greek has "Dionysus."
- **14:44** — The Vulgate reads venit per mediam cervicem ("he came down on the middle of his neck"), whereas the Greek has "in the middle of the empty space" (or "on his flank").
