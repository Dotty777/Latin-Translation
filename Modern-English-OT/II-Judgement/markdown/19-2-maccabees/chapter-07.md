## Text

**7:1** Now it also happened that seven brothers, together with their mother, were seized and compelled by the king to eat pork contrary to divine law, being tormented with whips and bull-hide thongs.

**7:2** And one of them, who was the first, spoke thus: "What are you asking, and what do you want to learn from us? We are ready to die rather than transgress the ancestral laws of God."

**7:3** Therefore the king, enraged, ordered bronze pans and cauldrons to be heated; and when these were immediately heated,

**7:4** he ordered the tongue of the one who had spoken first to be cut out, and after the skin of his head had been torn off, the extremities of his hands and feet also to be severed, while the rest of his brothers and his mother looked on.

**7:5** And when he had now been rendered helpless in every way, he ordered fire to be brought near and for him, while still breathing, to be roasted in the pan; and while he was tormented in it for a long time, the rest, together with their mother, encouraged one another to die courageously,

**7:6** saying, "The Lord God will look upon the truth and will take comfort in us, just as Moses declared in the testimony of the song: 'And in His servants He will take comfort.'"

**7:7** Therefore, when that first one had died in this way, they led the next one forward to be mocked; and after the skin of his head had been torn off along with the hair, they asked whether he would eat before he was punished throughout his whole body, limb by limb.

**7:8** But he, answering in the ancestral language, said, "I will not do it." Because of this, he too, next in order, underwent the tortures of the first;

**7:9** and at his last breath, he spoke thus: "You indeed, most wicked one, destroy us in the present life; but the King of the world will raise us who have died for His laws in the resurrection of eternal life."

**7:10** After him the third was mocked; and when asked for his tongue, he quickly put it out and steadfastly stretched out his hands,

**7:11** and with confidence he said, "From heaven I possess these, but because of the laws of God I now despise these very things, since I hope to receive them back from Him,"

**7:12** so that the king and those who were with him marveled at the spirit of the young man, because he regarded the torments as nothing.

**7:13** And when this one had died in this way, they mistreated the fourth, torturing him in the same manner.

**7:14** And when he was now at the point of death, he spoke thus: "It is better for those given over to death by men to await hope from God, to be raised up again by Him; for to you there will be no resurrection to life."

**7:15** And when they had brought the fifth forward, they mistreated him. But he, looking at him,

**7:16** said, "Having power among men, though you are perishable, you do what you want; yet do not think that our race has been abandoned by God;

**7:17** but you, wait patiently, and you will see His great power, how He will torture you and your seed."

**7:18** After him they led out the sixth, and as he was beginning to die, he spoke thus: "Do not be mistaken in vain; for we suffer these things because of ourselves, sinning against our God, and things worthy of wonder have happened among us;

**7:19** but do not suppose that it will go unpunished for you because you have attempted to fight against God."

**7:20** Now the mother was admirable beyond measure and worthy of the remembrance of the good, who, seeing her seven sons perishing within the span of a single day, bore it with good courage because of the hope that she had in God;

**7:21** she encouraged each of them courageously in the ancestral language, filled with wisdom; and infusing a manly spirit into her womanly thought,

**7:22** she said to them, "I do not know how you appeared in my womb; for neither did I grant you spirit and soul and life, nor did I myself fit together the limbs of each of you;

**7:23** rather, the Creator of the world, who formed the birth of man and who devised the origin of all things, will give back to you again with mercy both spirit and life, just as now you despise yourselves because of His laws."

**7:24** Now Antiochus, supposing that he was being held in contempt, and at the same time disdaining the voice of the one reproaching him, since the youngest still remained, not only urged him with words but also affirmed with an oath that he would make him rich and fortunate, would hold him as a friend once turned away from the ancestral laws, and would provide him with the necessary things.

**7:25** But when the young man was in no way inclined toward these things, the king called the mother and urged her to become the young man's deliverance.

**7:26** And when he had urged her with many words, she promised that she would persuade her son.

**7:27** Therefore, leaning toward him and mocking the cruel tyrant, she spoke in the ancestral language: "My son, have mercy on me, who carried you in the womb for nine months, and gave you milk for three years and nourished you, and brought you up to this age.

**7:28** I implore you, child, to look at heaven and earth, and at all things that are in them, and to understand that God made them and the human race from nothing;

**7:29** thus it will come about that you will not fear this executioner, but having become a partner worthy of your brothers, accept death, so that in that mercy I may receive you back with your brothers."

**7:30** While she was still saying these things, the young man said, "Whom are you waiting for? I do not obey the command of the king, but the command of the law that was given to us through Moses.

**7:31** But you, who have become the inventor of all wickedness against the Hebrews, will not escape the hand of God.

**7:32** For we suffer these things for our sins.

**7:33** And even if the Lord our God has been angry with us for a little while for the sake of rebuke and correction, yet He will again be reconciled to His servants.

**7:34** But you, wicked one and most vile of all men, do not be lifted up in vain with empty hopes, inflamed against His servants;

**7:35** for you have not yet escaped the judgment of Almighty God, who observes all things.

**7:36** For my brothers, having now endured a brief pain, have come under the covenant of eternal life; but you, by the judgment of God, will pay the just penalties of your arrogance.

**7:37** And I, like my brothers, surrender my soul and my body for the ancestral laws, calling upon God to become merciful to our nation quickly, and that with tortures and beatings you may confess that He alone is God.

**7:38** But in me and in my brothers, the wrath of the Almighty, which has been justly brought upon our entire race, will cease."

**7:39** Then the king, inflamed with anger against him, vented his rage more cruelly than against all the others, indignant that he had been mocked.

**7:40** And so this one also died pure, trusting in the Lord in all things.

**7:41** And last of all, after her sons, the mother also met her end.

**7:42** Therefore, concerning the sacrifices and the extreme cruelties, enough has been said.

## Translator Notes

- **7:5** — The Vulgate reads "he ordered fire to be brought near" (ignem admoveri) and "while he was tormented in it for a long time" (in qua cum diu cruciaretur), whereas the Greek Septuagint speaks of bringing him to the fire and of the vapor from the pan spreading widely.
- **7:6** — Latin consolabitur in nobis / in servis suis consolabitur ("will take comfort in us / in His servants") mirrors the Greek passive paraklethesetai ("will have compassion on" or "relent toward") in citing Deuteronomy 32:36; in protestatione cantici ("in the testimony of the song") also renders the Greek ("the song that confronts them face to face") distinctively.
- **7:20** — The Vulgate reads bonorum memoria digna ("worthy of the remembrance of the good"), using the genitive plural bonorum where the Greek Septuagint has "worthy of good remembrance."
- **7:22** — The Vulgate lists three elements, "spirit and soul and life" (spiritum et animam ... et vitam), where the Greek Septuagint mentions only "spirit and life."
- **7:30** — The Vulgate uses the masculine singular interrogative Quem sustinetis? ("Whom are you waiting for?"), resolving the ambiguous Greek tina ("whom" or "what"), and reads "given to us" (nobis) where the Greek has "given to our fathers."
