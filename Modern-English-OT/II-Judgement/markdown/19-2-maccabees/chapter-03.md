## Text

**3:1** Therefore, while the holy city was inhabited in complete peace and the laws also were still kept very well, because of the piety of Onias the high priest and minds that hated evil,

**3:2** it came about that even the kings and princes themselves considered the place worthy of the highest honor and glorified the temple with the greatest gifts,

**3:3** so that Seleucus, king of Asia, provided from his own revenues all the expenses pertaining to the service of the sacrifices.

**3:4** But Simon, of the tribe of Benjamin, who had been appointed overseer of the temple, was striving to contrive something unjust in the city, though the chief of the priests opposed him.

**3:5** But when he could not overcome Onias, he came to Apollonius, the son of Tharseas, who at that time was governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia,

**3:6** and reported to him that the treasury in Jerusalem was full of countless sums of money, and that the public resources were immense, which did not pertain to the account of the sacrifices, and that it was possible for all of it to fall under the power of the king.

**3:7** And when Apollonius had brought word to the king about the sums of money that had been reported, he summoned Heliodorus, who was over his affairs, and sent him with orders to transport the aforementioned money.

**3:8** And immediately Heliodorus set out on his journey, in appearance indeed as though he were about to travel through the cities across Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, but in reality to carry out the king's purpose.

**3:9** But when he had come to Jerusalem and had been kindly received by the high priest in the city, he told about the information that had been given concerning the money, and disclosed for what reason he was present; and he asked whether these things were truly so.

**3:10** Then the high priest showed that these were deposits and provisions for widows and orphans;

**3:11** that some indeed belonged to Hyrcanus, the son of Tobias, a very eminent man, among the things that the impious Simon had reported; and that in all there were four hundred talents of silver and two hundred of gold;

**3:12** and that it was altogether impossible for those who had trusted in the place and the temple, which is honored throughout the whole world for its reverence and holiness, to be deceived.

**3:13** But he, on account of what he had in his orders from the king, kept saying that by all means they had to be delivered to the king.

**3:14** Now on the appointed day, Heliodorus went in to make arrangements concerning these matters. And there was no small alarm throughout the whole city.

**3:15** And the priests threw themselves down before the altar in their priestly robes and called upon Him from heaven who established the law concerning deposits, that He would keep them safe for those who had deposited them.

**3:16** Indeed, whoever saw the expression of the high priest was wounded in mind, for his face and altered color revealed the inward grief of his soul.

**3:17** For a certain sadness had enveloped the man, and a shuddering of his body, through which the grief of his heart became evident to those looking on.

**3:18** Others also streamed together in throngs from their houses, imploring in public supplication, because the place was about to come into contempt.

**3:19** And women, wrapped with sackcloth around their chests, streamed together through the streets; and even the virgins who were kept shut in ran out to Onias, while others ran to the walls, and some looked out through the windows.

**3:20** And all of them, stretching out their hands toward heaven, pleaded in prayer;

**3:21** for the expectation of the mingled crowd and of the high priest, who stood in agony, was pitiful.

**3:22** And they indeed were calling upon Almighty God that the things entrusted to them might be preserved with complete integrity for those who had entrusted them.

**3:23** But Heliodorus was carrying out what he had resolved, himself present in that same place with his guards around the treasury.

**3:24** But the Spirit of Almighty God made a great display of His manifestation, so that all who had dared to obey him, collapsing by the power of God, were turned into faintness and terror.

**3:25** For there appeared to them a certain horse having a terrifying rider, adorned with the finest trappings; and with a violent rush it struck its front hooves against Heliodorus, and the one who sat upon it appeared to have golden armor.

**3:26** Two other young men also appeared, distinguished in strength, surpassing in glory, and splendid in attire, who stood around him and flogged him from both sides, beating him without ceasing with many blows.

**3:27** And suddenly Heliodorus collapsed to the ground, and they snatched him up, enveloped in thick darkness, and having placed him on a litter, they carried him out.

**3:28** And the one who had entered the aforementioned treasury with many runners and guards was being carried, with no one bringing him help, since the manifest power of God had been recognized;

**3:29** and he indeed, through divine power, lay mute and deprived of all hope and deliverance.

**3:30** But they were blessing the Lord, because He was magnifying His place; and the temple, which a little while before had been full of fear and uproar, was filled with joy and gladness at the appearing of the Almighty Lord.

**3:31** Then indeed some of the friends of Heliodorus immediately begged Onias to call upon the Most High, that He would grant life to the one who was at his very last breath.

**3:32** Now the high priest, considering that the king might perhaps suspect that some malice had been perpetrated against Heliodorus by the Jews, offered a saving sacrifice for the man's recovery.

**3:33** And while the high priest was praying earnestly, the same young men, clothed in the same garments, stood beside Heliodorus and said, "Give thanks to Onias the priest, for on his account the Lord has granted you life.

**3:34** And you, having been flogged by God, proclaim to all the great works of God and His power." And when these words had been spoken, they appeared no more.

**3:35** Then Heliodorus, having offered a sacrifice to God and promised great vows to Him who granted him to live, and giving thanks to Onias, took back his army and returned to the king.

**3:36** And he testified to everyone about the works of the great God that he had seen before his own eyes.

**3:37** Now when the king asked Heliodorus who would be suitable to be sent once more to Jerusalem, he said,

**3:38** "If you have any enemy or plotter against your kingdom, send him there, and you will receive him back flogged, if indeed he escapes; because there is truly a certain power of God in the place.

**3:39** For He Himself, who has His dwelling in the heavens, is the watcher and helper of that place, and He strikes and destroys those who come to do evil."

**3:40** Therefore, concerning Heliodorus and the safeguarding of the treasury, this is how the matter stands.

## Translator Notes

- **3:4** — The Vulgate states that Simon strove 'to contrive something unjust in the city' (iniquum aliquid in civitate moliri), whereas the Greek describes a dispute with the high priest over the administration of the city market.
- **3:5** — The Latin reads Tharsææ ('of Tharseas'), matching the Greek manuscripts' Tharseou; some modern editions of the Greek emend the name to 'Thraseas.'
- **3:11** — The Vulgate reads positively 'among the things that the impious Simon had reported' (in his quæ detulerat impius Simon), whereas the Greek Septuagint explicitly denies Simon's claim ('not at all as the impious Simon had slanderously represented').
- **3:19** — The Vulgate reads that the secluded virgins ran out 'to Onias' (ad Oniam), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads 'to the gates'.
- **3:24** — The Vulgate reads 'the Spirit of Almighty God' (spiritus omnipotentis Dei), whereas the Greek Septuagint reads 'the Sovereign of spirits and of all authority'.
