## Text

**15:1** Now Nicanor, when he learned that Judas was in the regions of Samaria, planned to engage in battle with all force on the Sabbath day.

**15:2** And when the Jews who were following him under compulsion said, "Do not act so savagely and barbarously, but pay honor to the day of sanctification, and honor Him who sees all things,"

**15:3** that wretched man asked whether there is a mighty one in heaven who commanded the Sabbath day to be observed.

**15:4** And when they answered, "There is the living Lord Himself, mighty in heaven, who ordered the seventh day to be observed,"

**15:5** he in turn said, "I also am mighty on the earth, I who command arms to be taken up and the king's affairs to be fulfilled." Yet he did not succeed in completing his plan.

**15:6** And Nicanor indeed, lifted up with the utmost pride, had planned to set up a public trophy over Judas.

**15:7** But Maccabeus was always confident, with every hope that help from God would come to him;

**15:8** and he exhorted his men not to be afraid at the approach of the nations, but to keep in mind the acts of help done for them from heaven, and now to hope that victory would come to them from the Almighty.

**15:9** And addressing them from the Law and the Prophets, reminding them also of the struggles that they had fought before, he made them more eager;

**15:10** and thus, with their spirits lifted up, he at the same time showed the deceit of the Gentiles and the violation of oaths.

**15:11** Now he armed each one of them, not with the protection of shield and spear, but with excellent words and exhortations, after relating a dream worthy of belief, through which he gladdened all of them.

**15:12** Now the vision was of this kind: Onias, who had been high priest, a good and kind man, modest in appearance, gentle in conduct, and dignified in speech, and one who from boyhood had been trained in virtues, was stretching out his hands, praying for the entire people of the Jews.

**15:13** After this, another man also appeared, remarkable in age and glory, and with a bearing of great dignity around him.

**15:14** And Onias answered and said, "This is the lover of the brothers and of the people of Israel; this is the one who prays much for the people and the entire holy city, Jeremiah, the prophet of God."

**15:15** Then Jeremiah stretched out his right hand and gave Judas a golden sword, saying,

**15:16** "Take the holy sword, a gift from God, with which you will cast down the adversaries of my people Israel."

**15:17** Encouraged therefore by the very good words of Judas, by which ardor could be raised up and the spirits of the young men strengthened, they resolved to fight and to engage bravely, so that valor might decide the affairs, because the holy city and the temple were in danger.

**15:18** For there was less anxiety for wives and children, and likewise for brothers and relatives; but the greatest and foremost fear was for the holiness of the temple.

**15:19** Moreover, no small anxiety gripped those also who were in the city for those who were about to engage in battle.

**15:20** And when all were now expecting the coming judgment, and the enemies were at hand and the army was drawn up in order, with the beasts and horsemen stationed in an advantageous place,

**15:21** Maccabeus, observing the approach of the multitude, and the varied array of arms, and the fierceness of the beasts, stretching out his hands toward heaven, called upon the Lord who works wonders, who, not according to the power of arms, but just as it pleases Him, gives victory to the worthy.

**15:22** And calling upon Him, he spoke in this manner: "You, Lord, who sent Your angel under Hezekiah king of Judah, and killed one hundred eighty-five thousand from the camp of Sennacherib,

**15:23** and now, Sovereign of the heavens, send Your good angel before us in fear and trembling at the greatness of Your arm,

**15:24** so that those who come with blasphemy against Your holy people may be afraid." And he indeed concluded his plea in this way.

**15:25** Now Nicanor and those who were with him were advancing with trumpets and songs.

**15:26** But Judas and those who were with him, having called upon God, engaged in battle through prayers:

**15:27** fighting indeed by hand, yet praying to the Lord in their hearts, they struck down no fewer than thirty-five thousand, greatly delighted by the presence of God.

**15:28** And when they had finished and were returning with joy, they discovered that Nicanor had fallen in his armor.

**15:29** Therefore, when a shout was raised and a commotion was stirred up, they blessed the Almighty Lord in their native tongue.

**15:30** Now Judas, who in every way was ready in body and soul to die for the citizens, commanded that the head of Nicanor, and his hand cut off with the shoulder, be carried to Jerusalem.

**15:31** When he arrived there, having called together his fellow tribesmen and the priests to the altar, he summoned also those who were in the citadel.

**15:32** And after showing the head of Nicanor, and the wicked hand which he had stretched out against the holy house of Almighty God as he boasted grandly,

**15:33** he also ordered the tongue of the impious Nicanor to be cut off and given piece by piece to the birds, and the hand of the madman to be hung opposite the temple.

**15:34** Therefore all the heavens blessed the Lord, saying, "Blessed is He who has preserved His own place undefiled."

**15:35** And he hung the head of Nicanor at the top of the citadel, so that it might be an evident and unmistakable sign of the help of God.

**15:36** Therefore all of them decreed by common consent in no way to let that day pass without celebration.

**15:37** Rather, they were to hold a celebration on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar, as it is called in the Syriac tongue, the day before Mordecai's day.

**15:38** So, with these matters concerning Nicanor accomplished, and the city having been possessed by the Hebrews from those times, I also will make an end of the account at this point.

**15:39** And if indeed it is well done, and as befits a history, this is what I myself would wish; but if less worthily, allowance must be made for me.

**15:40** For just as always drinking wine, or always water, is harmful, while using them in turn is delightful, so for readers, if the account is always exacting, it will not be pleasing. Here, therefore, it will be completed.

## Translator Notes

- **15:30** — The Vulgate uniquely describes Judas as "ready in body and soul to die for the citizens" (mori pro civibus paratus erat), whereas the Greek describes him as the chief champion in body and soul who kept his youthful goodwill toward his compatriots.
- **15:32** — The Latin verse has no main verb of its own: ostenso capite... is an ablative absolute and gloriatus est belongs to the relative clause, so the sentence continues with jussit ("ordered") in verse 33.
- **15:34** — Omnes igitur cæli can be construed "all the heavens" (as here) or, taking cæli as genitive with Dominum, "all blessed the Lord of heaven"; the Greek has "all [the people], looking toward heaven, blessed the manifest Lord."
- **15:36** — The Latin text here ends the sentence after "without celebration" and has no equivalent of the Greek specification of the date (the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, Adar, the day before Mordecai's day).
- **15:37** — Native Vulgate 2 Maccabees 15:37 maps to Saints.Bible 15:36b (shifting native 15:38-40 to Saints.Bible 15:37-39).
- **15:40** — The Vulgate epilogue differs markedly from the Greek: it contrasts always drinking wine or water with using them in turn (rather than mixing them), and notes that an account that is "always exacting" (semper exactus) will not please readers.
