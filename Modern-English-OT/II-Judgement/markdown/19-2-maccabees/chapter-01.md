## Text

**1:1** To the Jewish brothers who are throughout Egypt, the Jewish brothers who are in Jerusalem and those who are in the region of Judea send greetings and good peace.

**1:2** May God do good to you, and remember His covenant which He spoke to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, His faithful servants;

**1:3** and grant to all of you a heart to worship Him and carry out His will with a great heart and a willing spirit.

**1:4** May He open your heart in His law and in His precepts, and make peace.

**1:5** May He hear your prayers and be reconciled to you, and not abandon you in an evil time.

**1:6** And now we are here praying for you.

**1:7** While Demetrius was reigning, in the one hundred sixty-ninth year, we Jews wrote to you in the tribulation and onslaught that came upon us in these years, from the time Jason withdrew from the holy land and from the kingdom.

**1:8** They set fire to the gate and shed innocent blood; and we prayed to the Lord, and we were heard, and we offered sacrifice and fine flour, and we lit the lamps, and we set out the loaves.

**1:9** And now celebrate the days of Tabernacles of the month of Chislev.

**1:10** In the one hundred eighty-eighth year, the people who are in Jerusalem and in Judea, and the senate, and Judas, to Aristobulus, teacher of King Ptolemy, who is of the lineage of the anointed priests, and to the Jews who are in Egypt: greetings and health.

**1:11** Having been delivered by God from great dangers, we greatly give thanks to Him, as those who have fought against such a king.

**1:12** For He Himself caused those who fought against us and the holy city to pour forth from Persia.

**1:13** For when the leader himself was in Persia, and with him an immense army, he fell in the temple of Nanea, deceived by the counsel of the priests of Nanea.

**1:14** For Antiochus and his friends came to the place intending to live with her, and so that he might receive large sums of money by way of a dowry.

**1:15** And when the priests of Nanea had set them out, and he himself with a few others had entered inside the enclosure of the shrine, they shut the temple,

**1:16** once Antiochus had entered; and having opened a hidden entrance of the temple, throwing stones, they struck down the leader and those who were with him, and cut them apart limb by limb, and having cut off their heads, they threw them outside.

**1:17** In all things blessed be God, who has handed over the ungodly.

**1:18** Therefore, being about to perform the purification of the temple on the twenty-fifth day of the month of Chislev, we considered it necessary to inform you, so that you also may observe the day of Tabernacles and the day of the fire, which was given when Nehemiah, after the temple and the altar had been built, offered sacrifices.

**1:19** For when our fathers were being led into Persia, the priests who were then worshipers of God took fire from the altar and secretly hid it in a valley, where there was a deep and dry well, and in it they kept it safely, so that the place was unknown to everyone.

**1:20** Now when many years had passed, and it pleased God that Nehemiah should be sent by the king of Persia, he sent the descendants of those priests who had hidden it to search for the fire; and as they reported to us, they did not find fire, but thick water.

**1:21** And he commanded them to draw it and bring it to him; and the priest Nehemiah commanded the sacrifices that had been set in place to be sprinkled with that very water, both the wood and the things that were laid on top.

**1:22** And when this was done, and the time came when the sun shone forth, which previously was in a cloud, a great fire was kindled, so that all marveled.

**1:23** Now all the priests were offering prayer while the sacrifice was being completed, Jonathan beginning and the rest responding.

**1:24** And Nehemiah's prayer had this form: "Lord God, Creator of all things, awesome and strong, righteous and merciful, who alone is a good King,

**1:25** alone bountiful, alone righteous and almighty and eternal, who deliver Israel from every evil, who made the fathers chosen and sanctified them:

**1:26** accept the sacrifice for all Your people Israel, and guard Your portion, and sanctify it.

**1:27** Gather our dispersion, deliver those who serve the nations, and look upon the despised and detested, so that the nations may know that You are our God.

**1:28** Afflict those who oppress us and who inflict outrage in pride.

**1:29** Establish Your people in Your holy place, just as Moses said."

**1:30** Meanwhile the priests were singing hymns until the sacrifice was consumed.

**1:31** And when the sacrifice had been consumed, Nehemiah commanded that the larger stones be drenched with the remaining water.

**1:32** When this was done, a flame was kindled from them; but by the light that shone forth from the altar, it was consumed.

**1:33** Now when the matter became known, it was reported to the king of the Persians that in the place in which those priests who had been deported had hidden the fire, water appeared, with which Nehemiah and those who were with him purified the sacrifices.

**1:34** And the king, considering and carefully examining the matter, made a temple for it in order to verify what had happened;

**1:35** and when he had verified it, he bestowed on the priests many good things and one gift after another, and taking them with his own hand, he distributed them to them.

**1:36** Now Nehemiah called this place Nephthar, which is interpreted Purification; but among most people it is called Nephi.

## Translator Notes

- **1:12** — The Vulgate reads 'ebullire fecit de Perside' ("caused to pour forth from Persia") regarding those who fought against Jerusalem, whereas the Greek Septuagint reads "cast out those who arrayed themselves in the holy city" without mentioning Persia.
- **1:21** — The Vulgate reads nominative 'sacerdos Nehemias' ("the priest Nehemiah"), whereas the Greek Septuagint has Nehemiah commanding "the priests" to sprinkle the sacrifices.
- **1:24** — The Vulgate retains third-person 'qui solus est bonus rex' ("who alone is a good King") within second-person address, rendering the Greek substantival phrase for "the only good King."
- **1:34** — The Vulgate reads 'fecit ei templum, ut probaret quod factum erat' ("made a temple for it in order to verify what had happened"), whereas the Greek Septuagint states that the king enclosed the site and made it sacred.
- **1:36** — The Vulgate specifies 'hunc locum' ("this place") rather than the thick liquid itself, and preserves the distinct popular name 'Nephi' (Greek 'Nephthai').
