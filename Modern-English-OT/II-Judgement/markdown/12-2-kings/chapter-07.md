## Text

**7:1** Then Elisha said, "Hear the word of the Lord. This is what the Lord says: 'At this time tomorrow, a measure of fine flour will go for one stater, and two measures of barley for one stater, at the gate of Samaria.'"

**7:2** Then one of the commanders, on whose hand the king was leaning, answering the man of God, said, "Even if the Lord makes floodgates in heaven, could what you are saying possibly be?" And he said, "You will see it with your own eyes, and you will not eat from it."

**7:3** Now there were four leprous men beside the entrance of the gate, and they said to one another, "Why do we want to stay here until we die?

**7:4** If we choose to enter the city, we will die of famine; and if we remain here, we must die. Come then, and let us desert to the camp of Syria: if they spare us, we will live; but if they choose to kill us, we will die nonetheless."

**7:5** So they rose in the evening to come to the camp of Syria. And when they had come to the beginning of the camp of Syria, they found no one there.

**7:6** For the Lord had caused a sound to be heard in the camp of Syria, of chariots and of horses and of a very great army; and they said to one another, "Look, the king of Israel has hired against us the kings of the Hittites and of the Egyptians, and they have come upon us!"

**7:7** Therefore they rose and fled in the darkness, and abandoned their tents, and the horses and donkeys, in the camp, and fled, desiring only to save their own lives.

**7:8** So when those lepers had come to the beginning of the camp, they entered one tent and ate and drank, and took from there silver and gold and garments, and went away and hid them; and they returned again to another tent, and taking likewise from there, they hid them.

**7:9** And they said to one another, "We are not doing right, for this is a day of good news. If we remain silent and refuse to report it until morning, we will be charged with a crime. Come, let us go and report it in the king's court."

**7:10** And when they had come to the gate of the city, they told them, saying, "We went to the camp of Syria, and we found no person there, except horses and donkeys tied up, and the tents pitched."

**7:11** Therefore the gatekeepers went and reported it inside the king's palace.

**7:12** And he rose in the night and said to his servants, "I tell you what the Syrians have done to us: they know that we are suffering from famine, and for that reason they have gone out from the camp and are hiding in the fields, saying, 'When they have come out from the city, we will capture them alive, and then we will be able to enter the city.'"

**7:13** Then one of his servants answered, "Let us take the five horses that have remained in the city (because only they are left in the entire multitude of Israel, for the others have been consumed), and by sending them, we will be able to investigate."

**7:14** So they brought two horses, and the king sent them to the camp of the Syrians, saying, "Go and see."

**7:15** And they went after them all the way to the Jordan; and look, the entire road was full of garments and vessels that the Syrians had cast aside when they were thrown into confusion. And the messengers returned and told the king.

**7:16** And the people went out and plundered the camp of Syria; and a measure of fine flour went for one stater, and two measures of barley for one stater, according to the word of the Lord.

**7:17** Now the king stationed at the gate that commander on whose hand he was leaning; and the crowd trampled him in the entrance of the gate, and he died, according to what the man of God had spoken when the king had come down to him.

**7:18** And it happened according to the word of the man of God that he had spoken to the king, when he said, "Two measures of barley will go for one stater, and a measure of fine flour for one stater, at this same time tomorrow at the gate of Samaria,"

**7:19** when that commander had answered the man of God and said, "Even if the Lord makes floodgates in heaven, could what you are saying possibly happen?" And he said to him, "You will see it with your own eyes, and you will not eat from it."

**7:20** So it happened to him just as had been foretold, and the people trampled him in the gate, and he died.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — Latin modius ("measure") is a Roman dry measure of roughly nine liters (about a peck), and stater is a Greco-Roman silver coin; the Vulgate uses these to render the Hebrew seah and shekel.
- **7:11** — Vulgate reads "went" (Latin Ierunt) for the gatekeepers' action, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "called out."
- **7:13** — Vulgate streamlines a difficult, repetitive parenthetical clause in the Hebrew Masoretic Text into "(because only they are left in the entire multitude of Israel, for the others have been consumed)."
- **7:14** — Vulgate reads simply "two horses" (Latin duos equos), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "two chariots with horses."
