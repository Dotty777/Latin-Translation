## Text

**3:1** Now Joram the son of Ahab began to reign over Israel in Samaria in the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, and he reigned twelve years.

**3:2** And he did evil before the Lord, though not like his father and mother; for he removed the statues of Baal that his father had made.

**3:3** Nevertheless, he clung to the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel sin, and did not turn away from them.

**3:4** Now Mesha, king of Moab, raised many flocks, and he used to pay to the king of Israel one hundred thousand lambs, and one hundred thousand rams with their fleeces.

**3:5** And when Ahab had died, he broke the covenant that he had with the king of Israel.

**3:6** Therefore King Joram went out from Samaria on that day and mustered all Israel.

**3:7** And he sent to Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, saying, "The king of Moab has withdrawn from me; come with me against him to battle." And he answered, "I will go up. He who is mine is yours; my people are your people, and my horses are your horses."

**3:8** And he said, "By which way will we go up?" And he answered, "Through the wilderness of Idumea."

**3:9** So the king of Israel, and the king of Judah, and the king of Edom set out and went around on a seven-day journey, and there was no water for the army or for the beasts of burden that followed them.

**3:10** And the king of Israel said, "Alas! Alas! Alas! The Lord has gathered us three kings together to hand us over into the hands of Moab!"

**3:11** And Jehoshaphat said, "Is there a prophet of the Lord here, that we may pray to the Lord through him?" And one of the servants of the king of Israel answered, "Elisha the son of Shaphat is here, who used to pour water over the hands of Elijah."

**3:12** And Jehoshaphat said, "The word of the Lord is with him." And the king of Israel, and Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, and the king of Edom went down to him.

**3:13** But Elisha said to the king of Israel, "What is there between me and you? Go to the prophets of your father and of your mother." And the king of Israel said to him, "Why has the Lord gathered these three kings together to hand them over into the hands of Moab?"

**3:14** And Elisha said to him, "As the Lord of hosts lives, in whose sight I stand, if I did not respect the presence of Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, I would not even have paid attention to you, nor looked at you.

**3:15** But now bring me a harpist." And while the harpist was playing, the hand of the Lord came upon him, and he said,

**3:16** "This is what the Lord says: Make the channel of this stream trenches and trenches.

**3:17** For this is what the Lord says: You will not see wind, nor rain, and this channel will be filled with water, and you will drink, you and your households and your beasts of burden.

**3:18** And this is a small thing in the sight of the Lord; in addition, He will also hand Moab over into your hands.

**3:19** And you will strike every fortified city and every choice town, and you will cut down every fruit-bearing tree, and you will stop up all the springs of water, and you will cover every fine field with stones."

**3:20** So it happened in the morning, when the sacrifice is regularly offered, that, look, waters were coming by the way of Edom, and the land was filled with water.

**3:21** Now all the Moabites, hearing that the kings had come up to fight against them, summoned all who wore a sword belt from above, and they stood at the borders.

**3:22** And rising early in the morning, when the sun had already risen opposite the waters, the Moabites saw across from them the waters red like blood,

**3:23** and they said, "It is blood of the sword! The kings have fought against one another and have struck each other down; now go on to the plunder, Moab!"

**3:24** And they advanced into the camp of Israel; but rising up, Israel struck Moab, and they fled before them. Therefore those who had conquered came forward and struck Moab,

**3:25** and destroyed the cities; and they filled every finest field, each one throwing stones; and they stopped up all the springs of water; and they cut down all the fruit-bearing trees, so that only the clay walls remained; and the city was surrounded by slingers and struck in large part.

**3:26** And when the king of Moab saw this, namely, that the enemies had prevailed, he took with him seven hundred men drawing the sword to break through to the king of Edom, and they could not.

**3:27** And seizing his firstborn son, who was going to reign in his place, he offered him as a burnt offering on the wall. And great indignation arose in Israel, and immediately they withdrew from him and returned to their own land.

## Translator Notes

- **3:8** — The Vulgate uses the Greco-Latin regional name Idumea (Idumaeae) here, whereas it uses Edom in vv. 9, 12, 20, and 26.
- **3:21** — Latin accincti erant balteo desuper literally means "were belted with a sword belt from above" (a baldric worn over the shoulder), rendering the Hebrew idiom for everyone from the youngest age able to put on armor and upward.
- **3:23** — The Vulgate reads "It is blood of the sword" (Sanguis gladii est), taking the Hebrew verb form for "surely destroyed" as the similar-looking noun for "sword."
- **3:25** — Where the Hebrew names the Moabite stronghold Kir-hareseth, the Vulgate interprets the name etymologically as "the clay walls" (muri ... fictiles).
- **3:27** — Latin in Israel can mean either "in Israel" (outrage felt among the Israelites) or "against Israel" (wrath coming upon Israel); the exact cause and nature of this indignation remain debated.
