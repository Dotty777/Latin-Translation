## Text

**6:1** Now the sons of the prophets said to Elisha, "Look, the place where we live before you is too narrow for us.

**6:2** Let us go all the way to the Jordan, and let each one take a single piece of timber from the forest, so that we may build for ourselves a place there to live." And he said, "Go."

**6:3** And one of them said, "Come then, you too, with your servants." He answered, "I will come."

**6:4** And he went with them. And when they had come to the Jordan, they were cutting wood.

**6:5** Now it happened that when one had cut down a piece of timber, the iron of the axe fell into the water; and he cried out and said, "Alas! Alas! Alas, my lord! And I had borrowed this very thing."

**6:6** Then the man of God said, "Where did it fall?" And he showed him the place. So he cut off a piece of wood and threw it there, and the iron floated,

**6:7** and he said, "Take it." And he stretched out his hand and took it.

**6:8** Now the king of Syria was fighting against Israel, and he took counsel with his servants, saying, "In such and such a place let us set an ambush."

**6:9** Therefore the man of God sent to the king of Israel, saying, "Be careful not to pass into that place, because the Syrians are in ambush there."

**6:10** Therefore the king of Israel sent to the place of which the man of God had told him, and occupied it beforehand, and guarded himself there not once or twice.

**6:11** And the heart of the king of Syria was troubled over this matter; and having summoned his servants, he said, "Why do you not tell me who is a traitor against me with the king of Israel?"

**6:12** And one of his servants said, "Not at all, my lord the king, but Elisha the prophet, who is in Israel, tells the king of Israel all the words, whatever you speak in your private chamber."

**6:13** And he said to them, "Go and see where he is, so that I may send and capture him." And they reported to him, saying, "Look, he is in Dothan."

**6:14** Therefore he sent horses and chariots there, and the strength of the army; and when they had come by night, they surrounded the city.

**6:15** Now the attendant of the man of God, rising at dawn and going out, saw an army all around the city, and horses and chariots; and he reported to him, saying, "Alas! Alas! Alas, my lord! What shall we do?"

**6:16** But he answered, "Do not be afraid, for more are with us than with them."

**6:17** And when Elisha had prayed, he said, "Lord, open the eyes of this man, so that he may see." And the Lord opened the eyes of the young man, and he saw; and look, the mountain was full of horses and fiery chariots all around Elisha.

**6:18** Then the enemies came down to him; and Elisha prayed to the Lord, saying, "Please strike this people with blindness." And the Lord struck them so that they could not see, according to the word of Elisha.

**6:19** Then Elisha said to them, "This is not the way, nor is this the city. Follow me, and I will show you the man whom you are seeking." So he led them into Samaria.

**6:20** And when they had entered into Samaria, Elisha said, "Lord, open the eyes of these men, so that they may see." And the Lord opened their eyes, and they saw that they were in the middle of Samaria.

**6:21** And the king of Israel said to Elisha, when he had seen them, "Shall I strike them, my father?"

**6:22** But he said, "You shall not strike them, for you did not capture them with your sword and bow so that you might strike them; rather, set bread and water before them, so that they may eat and drink, and go to their master."

**6:23** And a great preparation of food was set before them, and they ate and drank; and he sent them away, and they departed to their master. And the raiders of Syria no longer came into the land of Israel.

**6:24** Now it happened after these things that Ben-hadad king of Syria gathered his entire army, and went up and was besieging Samaria.

**6:25** And a great famine arose in Samaria, and it was besieged so long that a donkey's head was sold for eighty pieces of silver, and a fourth part of a kab of doves' dung for five pieces of silver.

**6:26** And as the king of Israel was passing along the wall, a certain woman cried out to him, saying, "Save me, my lord the king!"

**6:27** And he said, "The Lord does not save you; from where can I save you? From the threshing floor, or from the winepress?" And the king said to her, "What do you want?" And she answered,

**6:28** "This woman said to me, 'Give your son, so that we may eat him today, and we will eat my son tomorrow.'

**6:29** So we cooked my son and ate him. And I said to her on the next day, 'Give your son, so that we may eat him.' But she hid her son."

**6:30** When the king had heard this, he tore his garments, and he was passing along the wall. And all the people saw the sackcloth with which he was clothed against his flesh underneath.

**6:31** And the king said, "May God do these things to me, and add these things as well, if the head of Elisha the son of Shaphat remains on him today."

**6:32** Now Elisha was sitting in his house, and the elders were sitting with him. So he sent a man ahead; and before that messenger arrived, he said to the elders, "Do you know that this son of a murderer has sent so that my head may be cut off? Watch therefore: when the messenger comes, shut the door and do not allow him to enter; for look, the sound of his master's feet is behind him."

**6:33** While he was still speaking to them, the messenger who was coming to him appeared. And he said, "Look, so great an evil is from the Lord; what more shall I expect from the Lord?"

## Translator Notes

- **6:2** — Vulgate specifies "from the forest" (Latin de silva) as the source of the timber, where the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "from there."
- **6:8** — Vulgate reads "let us set an ambush" (Latin ponamus insidias; similarly "in ambush" in 6:9), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text reads "my camp will be."
- **6:10** — Vulgate reads "occupied it beforehand" (Latin praeoccupavit eum) with the king of Israel as subject, whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text states that the man of God warned him.
- **6:22** — Vulgate renders this clause as a negative statement, "for you did not capture them with your sword and bow" (Latin neque enim cepisti eos), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text frames it as a rhetorical question.
- **6:27** — Vulgate gives an indicative statement, "The Lord does not save you" (Latin Non te salvat Dominus), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text is conditional ("If the Lord does not help you").
