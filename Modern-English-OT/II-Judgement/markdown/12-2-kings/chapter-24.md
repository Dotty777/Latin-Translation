## Text

**24:1** In his days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon went up, and Jehoiakim became his servant for three years; and again he rebelled against him.

**24:2** And the Lord sent against him raiders of the Chaldeans, and raiders of Syria, and raiders of Moab, and raiders of the sons of Ammon; and He sent them into Judah to destroy it, according to the word of the Lord that He had spoken through His servants the prophets.

**24:3** Now this happened through the word of the Lord against Judah, so that He might remove it from before Him because of all the sins of Manasseh that he committed,

**24:4** and because of the innocent blood that he shed, and he filled Jerusalem with the blood of the innocent; and on account of this the Lord was not willing to be appeased.

**24:5** Now the rest of the accounts of Jehoiakim, and all that he did, are these not written in the book of the words of the days of the kings of Judah? And Jehoiakim slept with his fathers,

**24:6** and Jehoiachin his son reigned in his place.

**24:7** And the king of Egypt did not go out again from his land, for the king of Babylon had taken, from the stream of Egypt to the Euphrates River, all that had belonged to the king of Egypt.

**24:8** Jehoiachin was eighteen years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem; his mother's name was Nehushta, the daughter of Elnathan of Jerusalem.

**24:9** And he did evil before the Lord, according to all that his father had done.

**24:10** At that time the servants of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon went up against Jerusalem, and the city was surrounded with siege works.

**24:11** And Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to the city with his servants so that they might attack it.

**24:12** And Jehoiachin king of Judah went out to the king of Babylon, he and his mother, and his servants, and his princes, and his eunuchs; and the king of Babylon received him in the eighth year of his reign.

**24:13** And he brought out from there all the treasures of the house of the Lord and the treasures of the royal house, and he cut to pieces all the golden vessels that Solomon king of Israel had made in the temple of the Lord, according to the word of the Lord.

**24:14** And he carried away all Jerusalem, and all the princes, and all the strong men of the army, ten thousand, into captivity, and every craftsman and locksmith; and nothing was left, except the poor of the people of the land.

**24:15** He also carried away Jehoiachin to Babylon, and the king's mother, and the king's wives, and his eunuchs; and the judges of the land he led into captivity from Jerusalem to Babylon.

**24:16** And all the stalwart men, seven thousand, and the craftsmen and locksmiths, one thousand, all strong men and warriors: the king of Babylon led them captive to Babylon.

**24:17** And he appointed Mattaniah his uncle in his place, and gave him the name Zedekiah.

**24:18** Zedekiah was twenty-one years of age when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem; his mother's name was Hamutal, the daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah.

**24:19** And he did evil before the Lord, according to all that Jehoiakim had done.

**24:20** For the Lord was angry against Jerusalem and against Judah, until He cast them away from His presence; and Zedekiah withdrew from the king of Babylon.

## Translator Notes

- **24:5** — In the Clementine Vulgate, "And Jehoiakim slept with his fathers" ends 24:5, leaving only "and Jehoiachin his son reigned in his place" as 24:6, whereas the Hebrew text places both clauses in 24:6.
- **24:14** — Vulgate "locksmith" (clusorem, also in v. 16) is a literal coinage from Latin claudere ("to shut/lock") for the Hebrew term masger, commonly rendered "smiths" or "metalworkers."
- **24:15** — Vulgate "judges of the land" (judices terrae) renders a Hebrew phrase usually understood as "chief men" or "mighty men of the land."
