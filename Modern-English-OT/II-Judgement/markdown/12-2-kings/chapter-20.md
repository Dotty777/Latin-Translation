## Text

**20:1** In those days Hezekiah became sick to the point of death; and Isaiah the prophet, the son of Amoz, came to him and said to him, "This is what the Lord God says: 'Give instructions to your household, for you will die and will not live.'"

**20:2** Then he turned his face to the wall and prayed to the Lord, saying,

**20:3** "I implore You, Lord, please remember how I have walked before You in truth and with a perfect heart, and have done what is pleasing before You." And so Hezekiah wept with great weeping.

**20:4** And before Isaiah had gone out from the middle part of the courtyard, the word of the Lord came to him, saying,

**20:5** "Go back and say to Hezekiah, the leader of My people: 'This is what the Lord, the God of David your father, says: "I have heard your prayer, and I have seen your tears, and look, I have healed you; on the third day you will go up to the temple of the Lord.

**20:6** And I will add fifteen years to your days; moreover, I will deliver you and this city from the hand of the king of the Assyrians, and I will protect this city for My own sake and for the sake of David My servant."'"

**20:7** And Isaiah said, "Bring a cake of figs." And when they had brought it and placed it on his boil, he was cured.

**20:8** Now Hezekiah had said to Isaiah, "What will be the sign that the Lord will heal me, and that I will go up to the temple of the Lord on the third day?"

**20:9** Isaiah said to him, "This will be the sign from the Lord that the Lord will carry out the word that He has spoken: do you want the shadow to go up ten lines, or to return as many degrees?"

**20:10** And Hezekiah said, "It is easy for the shadow to grow by ten lines; I do not want this to happen, but rather for it to return backward by ten degrees."

**20:11** Therefore Isaiah the prophet called upon the Lord, and he brought the shadow back along the lines by which it had already gone down on the sundial of Ahaz, backward by ten degrees.

**20:12** At that time Berodach-baladan, the son of Baladan, king of the Babylonians, sent letters and gifts to Hezekiah, for he had heard that Hezekiah had been sick.

**20:13** And Hezekiah rejoiced at their arrival, and he showed them the house of aromatics, and the gold and the silver, and various spices, and ointments as well, and the house of his vessels, and everything that he could have in his treasuries. There was nothing that Hezekiah did not show them in his house and in all his dominion.

**20:14** Then Isaiah the prophet came to King Hezekiah and said to him, "What did these men say? Or from where did they come to you?" Hezekiah said to him, "They came to me from a distant land, from Babylon."

**20:15** And he replied, "What have they seen in your house?" Hezekiah said, "They have seen everything that is in my house; there is nothing that I have not shown them in my treasuries."

**20:16** Therefore Isaiah said to Hezekiah, "Hear the word of the Lord:

**20:17** 'Look, days will come, and everything that is in your house, and what your fathers have stored up until this day, will be carried off to Babylon; nothing will remain,' says the Lord.

**20:18** 'Moreover, some of your sons who will come from you, whom you will father, will be taken away, and they will be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon.'"

**20:19** Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "The word of the Lord that you have spoken is good; let there be peace and truth in my days."

**20:20** Now the rest of the accounts of Hezekiah, and all his might, and how he made the pool and the aqueduct and brought the waters into the city, are these not written in the book of the words of the days of the kings of Judah?

**20:21** And Hezekiah slept with his fathers, and Manasseh his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **20:5** — The Vulgate uses the perfect tense sanavi te ("I have healed you") where the Hebrew has a participle ("I am going to heal you"), and renders "house of the Lord" as templum Domini ("temple of the Lord," also in v. 8).
- **20:11** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew "steps of Ahaz" as in horologio Achaz ("on the sundial of Ahaz"), distinguishing between lineæ ("lines") and gradus ("degrees") in vv. 9-11.
- **20:13** — The Vulgate reads Lætatus est autem in adventu eorum ("rejoiced at their arrival"), aligning with Isaiah 39:2 and the Septuagint where the Masoretic Text of 2 Kings has "he listened to them."
- **20:19** — The Vulgate renders Hezekiah's closing words as a direct wish, sit pax et veritas in diebus meis ("let there be peace and truth in my days"), without the rhetorical question "is it not [good] if..." found in the Hebrew.
