## Text

**18:1** In the third year of Hoshea, son of Elah, king of Israel, Hezekiah, son of Ahaz, king of Judah, became king.

**18:2** He was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned for twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. The name of his mother was Abi, the daughter of Zechariah.

**18:3** And he did what was good before the Lord, according to all that David his father had done.

**18:4** He himself destroyed the high places, and shattered the statues, and cut down the groves, and broke in pieces the bronze serpent that Moses had made, since up to that time the sons of Israel were burning incense to it; and he called its name Nehushtan.

**18:5** He hoped in the Lord God of Israel; therefore, after him there was no one like him among all the kings of Judah, nor even among those who were before him.

**18:6** And he clung to the Lord, and he did not depart from His footsteps, and he carried out His commandments, which the Lord had commanded Moses.

**18:7** For this reason the Lord was also with him, and in everything to which he went forth, he conducted himself wisely. He also rebelled against the king of the Assyrians and did not serve him.

**18:8** He himself struck the Philistines as far as Gaza and all their borders, from the tower of the watchmen to the fortified city.

**18:9** In the fourth year of King Hezekiah, which was the seventh year of Hoshea, son of Elah, king of Israel, Shalmaneser, king of the Assyrians, went up against Samaria and besieged it,

**18:10** and captured it. For after three years, in the sixth year of Hezekiah, that is, in the ninth year of Hoshea, king of Israel, Samaria was captured.

**18:11** And the king of the Assyrians carried Israel away to the Assyrians, and settled them in Halah and in Habor, the rivers of Gozan, in the cities of the Medes,

**18:12** because they did not listen to the voice of the Lord their God, but transgressed His covenant; all that Moses, the servant of the Lord, had commanded, they neither listened to nor did.

**18:13** In the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah, Sennacherib, king of the Assyrians, went up against all the fortified cities of Judah and captured them.

**18:14** Then Hezekiah, king of Judah, sent messengers to the king of the Assyrians at Lachish, saying, "I have sinned; depart from me, and everything that you impose on me, I will bear." Therefore the king of the Assyrians levied on Hezekiah, king of Judah, three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold.

**18:15** And Hezekiah gave all the silver that had been found in the house of the Lord and in the treasuries of the king.

**18:16** At that time Hezekiah broke off the doors of the temple of the Lord and the plates of gold that he himself had fastened, and he gave them to the king of the Assyrians.

**18:17** Now the king of the Assyrians sent Tartan, and Rabsaris, and Rabshakeh from Lachish to King Hezekiah at Jerusalem with a strong force. And when they had gone up, they came to Jerusalem, and they stood beside the aqueduct of the upper pool, which is on the road of the Fuller's Field.

**18:18** And they called for the king, but Eliakim, son of Hilkiah, overseer of the household, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah, son of Asaph, keeper of the records, went out to them.

**18:19** And Rabshakeh said to them, "Speak to Hezekiah, 'This is what the great king, the king of the Assyrians, says: What is this confidence on which you rely?

**18:20** Perhaps you have formed a plan to prepare yourself for battle. In whom do you trust, that you dare to rebel?

**18:21** Or do you hope in a broken reed staff, Egypt, on which, if a man leans, it will splinter and enter his hand and pierce it? So is Pharaoh, king of Egypt, to all who trust in him.'

**18:22** But if you say to me, 'We have confidence in the Lord our God,' is not He the one whose high places and altars Hezekiah removed and commanded Judah and Jerusalem, 'Before this altar you will worship in Jerusalem'?

**18:23** Now therefore, come over to my lord, the king of the Assyrians, and I will give you two thousand horses, and see whether you are able to have riders for them.

**18:24** And how can you resist before a single satrap of the least of my lord's servants? Or do you have confidence in Egypt because of chariots and horsemen?

**18:25** Have I indeed come up against this place without the will of the Lord to demolish it? The Lord said to me, 'Go up against this land and demolish it.'"

**18:26** Then Eliakim, son of Hilkiah, and Shebna, and Joah said to Rabshakeh, "We ask that you speak to us, your servants, in Syriac, since we understand this language, and do not speak to us in Judean while the people who are on the wall are listening."

**18:27** And Rabshakeh answered them, saying, "Was it really to your lord and to you that my lord sent me to speak these words, and not rather to the men who are sitting on the wall, so that they may eat their own dung and drink their own urine with you?"

**18:28** Therefore Rabshakeh stood and cried out with a loud voice in Judean, and said, "Hear the words of the great king, the king of the Assyrians.

**18:29** This is what the king says: Do not let Hezekiah mislead you, for he will not be able to rescue you from my hand,

**18:30** nor let him give you confidence in the Lord, saying, 'Rescuing us, the Lord will deliver us, and this city will not be handed over into the hand of the king of the Assyrians.'

**18:31** Do not listen to Hezekiah. For this is what the king of the Assyrians says: Do with me what is advantageous to you, and come out to me, and each one will eat from his own vine and from his own fig tree, and you will drink water from your own cisterns,

**18:32** until I come and carry you away to a land that is like your own land, to a fruitful land and fertile in wine, a land of bread and vineyards, a land of olive trees and oil and honey; and you will live and not die. Do not listen to Hezekiah, who deceives you, saying, 'The Lord will deliver us.'

**18:33** Have the gods of the nations delivered their land from the hand of the king of the Assyrians?

**18:34** Where is the god of Hamath and Arpad? Where is the god of Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivvah? Have they delivered Samaria from my hand?

**18:35** Who are they among all the gods of the lands who have rescued their region from my hand, that the Lord should be able to rescue Jerusalem from my hand?"

**18:36** Therefore the people kept silent and did not answer him anything, since they had received the king's command that they should not answer him.

**18:37** And Eliakim, son of Hilkiah, overseer of the household, and Shebna the scribe, and Joah, son of Asaph, keeper of the records, came to Hezekiah with their garments torn, and reported to him the words of Rabshakeh.

## Translator Notes

- **18:4** — Vulgate renders the Hebrew sacred pillars as statuas ("statues") and Asherah symbols as lucos ("groves"), and uses the active singular vocavit ("he called") with the Latin transliteration Nohestan (Nehushtan).
- **18:11** — Vulgate reads the plural fluviis Gozan ("the rivers of Gozan"), whereas the Hebrew text has the singular "the river of Gozan," and uses the ethnic plural in Assyrios ("to the Assyrians") for the land of Assyria.
- **18:16** — Vulgate reads confregit ... valvas ... et laminas auri quas ipse affixerat ("broke off the doors ... and the plates of gold that he himself had fastened"), whereas the Hebrew text speaks of stripping gold from the doors and doorposts Hezekiah had overlaid.
- **18:23** — Vulgate reads transite ad dominum meum ("come over to my lord"), diverging from the Hebrew idiom "make a wager with my master."
- **18:31** — Vulgate renders the Hebrew idiom "make a blessing [i.e., terms of peace] with me" as Facite mecum quod vobis est utile ("Do with me what is advantageous to you").
