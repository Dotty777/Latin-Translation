## Text

**1:1** Now Moab revolted against Israel after Ahab died.

**1:2** And Ahaziah fell through the lattice of his upper room that he had in Samaria and became sick; and he sent messengers, saying to them, "Go, consult Beelzebub, the god of Ekron, whether I can live through this illness of mine."

**1:3** But the angel of the Lord spoke to Elijah the Tishbite, saying, "Rise and go up to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria, and you will say to them, 'Is there no God in Israel, that you are going to consult Beelzebub, the god of Ekron?

**1:4** Therefore this is what the Lord says: From the bed onto which you have gone up, you will not come down, but you will surely die.'" And Elijah departed.

**1:5** And the messengers returned to Ahaziah. And he said to them, "Why have you returned?"

**1:6** And they answered him, "A man met us and said to us, 'Go and return to the king who sent you, and you will say to him, "This is what the Lord says: Is it because there was no God in Israel that you are sending to have Beelzebub, the god of Ekron, consulted? For that reason from the bed onto which you have gone up, you will not come down, but you will surely die."'"

**1:7** And he said to them, "What is the appearance and dress of that man who met you and spoke these words?"

**1:8** And they said, "A hairy man, and bound around his waist with a leather belt." And he said, "It is Elijah the Tishbite."

**1:9** And he sent to him a captain of fifty, and the fifty who were under him. And he went up to him, and said to him as he sat on the summit of the mountain, "Man of God, the king has commanded that you come down."

**1:10** And Elijah answered and said to the captain of fifty, "If I am a man of God, let fire come down from heaven and devour you and your fifty." And so fire came down from heaven and devoured him and the fifty who were with him.

**1:11** And again he sent to him a second captain of fifty, and fifty with him. And he spoke to him, "Man of God, this is what the king says: Hurry, come down."

**1:12** Elijah answered and said, "If I am a man of God, let fire come down from heaven and devour you and your fifty." Therefore fire came down from heaven and devoured him and his fifty.

**1:13** Again he sent a third captain of fifty, and the fifty who were with him. And when he had come, he bent his knees before Elijah, and pleaded with him, and said, "Man of God, do not despise my life and the lives of your servants who are with me.

**1:14** Look, fire came down from heaven and devoured the first two captains of fifty and the fifties who were with them; but now I implore you to have mercy on my life."

**1:15** Then the angel of the Lord spoke to Elijah, saying, "Go down with him; do not be afraid." Therefore he rose and went down with him to the king,

**1:16** and spoke to him, "This is what the Lord says: Because you sent messengers to consult Beelzebub, the god of Ekron, as if there were no God in Israel from whom you could inquire a word, therefore from the bed onto which you have gone up, you will not come down, but you will surely die."

**1:17** So he died according to the word of the Lord that Elijah had spoken, and Joram his brother reigned in his place, in the second year of Joram the son of Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, for he had no son.

**1:18** Now the rest of the acts of Ahaziah that he performed, are these not written in the book of the words of the days of the kings of Israel?

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — The Vulgate uses the form Beelzebub (as in the New Testament; compare Hebrew Baal-zebub), and reads literally "whether I can live from this illness of mine" (vivere queam de infirmitate mea hac), meaning survive or recover.
- **1:8** — Latin vir pilosus literally means "a hairy man," rendering a Hebrew idiom that can refer either to a hairy body or to a cloak of coarse hair.
- **1:12** — Unlike the Hebrew Masoretic Text, which reads "the fire of God," the Clementine Vulgate reads simply "fire" (ignis).
- **1:13** — The Vulgate renders the captain's plea negatively as "do not despise my life" (noli despicere animam meam) and in v. 14 as "have mercy on my life" (miserearis animae meae), where the Hebrew reads "let my life be precious in your sight."
- **1:17** — The Clementine Vulgate explicitly identifies Joram as "his brother" (frater ejus), a detail absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text.
