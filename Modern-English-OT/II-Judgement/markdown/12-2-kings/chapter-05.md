## Text

**5:1** Naaman, commander of the army of the king of Syria, was a great man with his master, and honored; for through him the Lord gave deliverance to Syria; and he was a mighty and wealthy man, yet a leper.

**5:2** Now raiders had gone out from Syria and had led away captive from the land of Israel a little girl, who was in the service of Naaman's wife;

**5:3** and she said to her mistress, "If only my master had been with the prophet who is in Samaria! Surely he would have cured him of the leprosy that he has."

**5:4** So Naaman went in to his master and reported to him, saying, "Thus and thus the girl from the land of Israel spoke."

**5:5** And the king of Syria said to him, "Go, and I will send a letter to the king of Israel." And when he had set out and had taken with him ten talents of silver, and six thousand gold pieces, and ten changes of garments,

**5:6** he delivered the letter to the king of Israel in these words: "When you receive this letter, know that I have sent my servant Naaman to you, so that you may cure him of his leprosy."

**5:7** And when the king of Israel had read the letter, he tore his garments and said, "Am I God, that I can kill and make alive, that this man has sent to me to cure a man of his leprosy? Take note and see that he is seeking pretexts against me."

**5:8** And when Elisha the man of God heard this, namely that the king of Israel had torn his garments, he sent to him, saying, "Why have you torn your garments? Let him come to me, and let him know that there is a prophet in Israel."

**5:9** So Naaman came with horses and chariots, and stood at the door of the house of Elisha;

**5:10** and Elisha sent a messenger to him, saying, "Go and wash seven times in the Jordan, and your flesh will recover health, and you will be cleansed."

**5:11** Naaman became angry and began leaving, saying, "I thought that he would come out to me, and standing there would call upon the name of the Lord his God, and would touch the place of the leprosy with his hand, and cure me.

**5:12** Are not Abana and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel, so that I might wash in them and be cleansed?" So when he had turned around and was going away in indignation,

**5:13** his servants approached him and spoke to him, "Father, even if the prophet had told you some great thing, surely you ought to have done it; how much more now that he has said to you, 'Wash, and you will be cleansed'?"

**5:14** He went down and washed in the Jordan seven times, according to the word of the man of God; and his flesh was restored like the flesh of a little boy, and he was cleansed.

**5:15** And returning to the man of God with his entire company, he came and stood before him, and said, "Truly I know that there is no other god in all the earth, except only in Israel. Therefore I beg you to accept a blessing from your servant."

**5:16** But he answered, "As the Lord lives, before whom I stand, I will not accept it." And though he pressed him hard, he would not consent at all.

**5:17** And Naaman said, "As you wish; yet, I beg you, grant to me, your servant, that I may take a two-mule load of earth; for your servant will no longer offer a burnt offering or a sacrifice to foreign gods, except to the Lord.

**5:18** Now this alone is the thing about which you should plead with the Lord for your servant: when my master enters the temple of Rimmon to bow down, and as he leans upon my hand, if I bow down in the temple of Rimmon while he bows down in that same place, that the Lord may forgive me, your servant, for this matter."

**5:19** And he said to him, "Go in peace." Therefore he departed from him in the choice season of the earth.

**5:20** And Gehazi, the servant of the man of God, said, "My master has spared this Naaman the Syrian by not accepting from him the things he brought; as the Lord lives, I will run after him and take something from him."

**5:21** And Gehazi followed behind Naaman; and when he saw him running toward him, he leaped down from the chariot to meet him and said, "Is everything well?"

**5:22** And he said, "All is well. My master has sent me to you, saying, 'Just now two young men have come to me from Mount Ephraim, from the sons of the prophets; give them a talent of silver and two changes of garments.'"

**5:23** And Naaman said, "It is better that you accept two talents." And he pressed him, and tied up two talents of silver in two bags, along with two sets of garments, and placed them upon two of his servants, who carried them before him.

**5:24** And when he had arrived, already in the evening, he took them from their hand and stored them in the house; and he dismissed the men, and they departed.

**5:25** Then he himself went in and stood before his master. And Elisha said, "Where are you coming from, Gehazi?" And he answered, "Your servant did not go anywhere."

**5:26** But he said, "Was not my heart present when the man turned back from his chariot to meet you? Now therefore you have accepted silver, and you have accepted garments so that you may buy olive groves, and vineyards, and sheep, and oxen, and servants, and maidservants.

**5:27** Yet the leprosy of Naaman will also cling to you and to your offspring forever." And he went out from him, leprous like snow.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew idiom for a mighty warrior with two adjectives, "mighty and wealthy" (fortis et dives).
- **5:18** — In the Vulgate, Naaman explicitly asks Elisha to intercede for him ("about which you should plead with the Lord for your servant"), whereas the Hebrew expresses a direct wish for the Lord's pardon.
- **5:19** — The Vulgate reads "in the choice season of the earth" (electo terræ tempore), interpreting the obscure Hebrew phrase for "some distance of land" as a reference to the prime season of the year.
- **5:24** — The Vulgate reads "already in the evening" (jam vesperi), taking the Hebrew word for "the hill" (Ophel) as a term for twilight or dusk.
- **5:26** — The Vulgate renders this clause as a declarative accusation ("Now therefore you have accepted silver, and you have accepted garments...") rather than the Hebrew rhetorical question ("Is it a time to accept money and garments...?").
