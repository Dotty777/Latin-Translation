## Text

**22:1** Josiah was eight years old when he began to reign; he reigned thirty-one years in Jerusalem; the name of his mother was Jedidah, daughter of Adaiah of Bozkath.

**22:2** And he did what was pleasing before the Lord, and walked through all the ways of David his father; he did not turn aside to the right or to the left.

**22:3** Now in the eighteenth year of King Josiah, the king sent Shaphan, son of Azaliah, son of Meshullam, the scribe of the temple of the Lord, saying to him,

**22:4** "Go to Hilkiah the high priest, so that the money that has been brought into the temple of the Lord may be melted down, which the doorkeepers of the temple have collected from the people,

**22:5** and may be given to the craftsmen through the overseers of the house of the Lord, who shall also distribute it to those who work in the temple of the Lord for restoring the structure of the temple,

**22:6** namely, to the carpenters and the masons, and to those who mend what is broken, and so that timber and stones from the quarries may be bought to restore the temple of the Lord.

**22:7** However, let the silver that they receive not be reckoned against them, but let them hold it in their power and in good faith."

**22:8** Now Hilkiah the high priest said to Shaphan the scribe, "I have found the book of the law in the house of the Lord." And Hilkiah gave the scroll to Shaphan, who also read it.

**22:9** Shaphan the scribe also came to the king and reported back to him what he had commanded, and said, "Your servants have melted down the money that was found in the house of the Lord, and have given it to be distributed to the craftsmen by the supervisors of the works of the temple of the Lord."

**22:10** Shaphan the scribe also told the king, saying, "Hilkiah the priest has given me a book." When Shaphan had read it before the king,

**22:11** and the king had heard the words of the book of the law of the Lord, he tore his garments.

**22:12** And he commanded Hilkiah the priest, and Ahikam son of Shaphan, and Achbor son of Micah, and Shaphan the scribe, and Asaiah the servant of the king, saying,

**22:13** "Go and consult the Lord concerning me, and concerning the people, and concerning all Judah, about the words of this scroll that has been found; for great wrath of the Lord has been kindled against us, because our fathers did not listen to the words of this book, so as to do everything that is written for us."

**22:14** Therefore Hilkiah the priest, and Ahikam, and Achbor, and Shaphan, and Asaiah went to Huldah the prophetess, the wife of Shallum, son of Tikvah, son of Harhas, keeper of the garments, who lived in Jerusalem in the Second Quarter; and they spoke to her.

**22:15** And she answered them, "This is what the Lord God of Israel says: Tell the man who sent you to me,

**22:16** 'This is what the Lord says: Look, I will bring evils upon this place and upon its inhabitants, all the words of the law that the king of Judah has read,

**22:17** because they have abandoned Me and have sacrificed to foreign gods, provoking Me in all the works of their hands; and My indignation will be kindled in this place and will not be extinguished.'

**22:18** But to the king of Judah, who sent you to consult the Lord, say this: 'This is what the Lord God of Israel says: Because you heard the words of the scroll,

**22:19** and your heart was terrified, and you humbled yourself before the Lord upon hearing the words against this place and its inhabitants, namely, that they would become an astonishment and a curse, and you tore your garments and wept before Me; I also have heard, says the Lord:

**22:20** therefore I will gather you to your fathers, and you will be gathered to your tomb in peace, so that your eyes will not see all the evils that I am about to bring upon this place.'"

## Translator Notes

- **22:3** — The Vulgate construes templi Domini as a genitive modifying scribam ("the scribe of the temple of the Lord"), whereas the Hebrew text takes "to the house of the Lord" as the destination to which Shaphan was sent.
- **22:4** — Both here (confletur) and in 22:9 (conflaverunt), the Vulgate speaks of melting down the temple money, whereas the Hebrew refers to counting or pouring out the silver; in 22:5, sartatecta is a classical Latin architectural term (literally "things patched and roofed") denoting the structural fabric and upkeep of a building.
- **22:12** — The Vulgate preserves the shorter proper name Micha ("Micah") where the Hebrew text reads "Micaiah."
- **22:19** — The Vulgate reads perterritum est ("was terrified") where the Hebrew text reads "was tender/soft"; similarly, the Vulgate reads verba legis ("words of the law") in 22:16 and verba voluminis ("words of the scroll") in 22:18.
- **22:20** — The Vulgate ends 22:20 here with Huldah's oracle, without the closing clause ("and they brought word back to the king") found at the end of this verse in the Hebrew text.
