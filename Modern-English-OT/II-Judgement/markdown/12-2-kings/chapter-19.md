## Text

**19:1** When King Hezekiah heard these things, he tore his garments and covered himself with sackcloth, and entered the house of the Lord.

**19:2** And he sent Eliakim, the overseer of the house, and Shebna the scribe, and the elders among the priests, covered with sackcloth, to Isaiah the prophet, the son of Amoz.

**19:3** They said, "This is what Hezekiah says: 'This day is a day of distress, of rebuke, and of blasphemy; children have come to the point of birth, and the woman in labor has no strength.

**19:4** Perhaps the Lord your God will hear all the words of Rabshakeh, whom the king of the Assyrians, his master, sent to reproach the living God and to reprove with words that the Lord your God has heard; and offer a prayer for the remnant that is found.'"

**19:5** So the servants of King Hezekiah came to Isaiah.

**19:6** And Isaiah said to them, "You will say this to your master: 'This is what the Lord says: "Do not be afraid in the face of the words that you have heard, with which the young men of the king of the Assyrians have blasphemed Me.

**19:7** Look, I will put a spirit in him, and he will hear a report, and he will return to his own land; and I will bring him down by the sword in his own land."'"

**19:8** So Rabshakeh returned and found the king of the Assyrians attacking Libnah, for he had heard that he had departed from Lachish.

**19:9** And when he had heard them saying concerning Tirhakah, king of Ethiopia, "Look, he has come out to fight against you," and was going against him, he sent messengers to Hezekiah, saying,

**19:10** "Say this to Hezekiah, king of Judah: 'Do not let your God, in whom you have confidence, deceive you, nor say, "Jerusalem will not be handed over into the hands of the king of the Assyrians."

**19:11** For you yourself have heard what the kings of the Assyrians have done to all the lands, how they have devastated them; will you alone, then, be able to be delivered?

**19:12** Have the gods of the nations delivered each of those whom my fathers devastated, namely Gozan, and Haran, and Rezeph, and the sons of Eden who were in Telassar?

**19:13** Where is the king of Hamath, and the king of Arpad, and the king of the city of Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivvah?'"

**19:14** Therefore, when Hezekiah had received the letters from the hand of the messengers and had read them, he went up into the house of the Lord and spread them out before the Lord,

**19:15** and prayed in His sight, saying, "Lord, God of Israel, who sit above the cherubim, You alone are God of all the kings of the earth; You have made heaven and earth.

**19:16** Incline Your ear and hear; open Your eyes, Lord, and see; hear all the words of Sennacherib, who has sent to reproach the living God to us.

**19:17** Truly, Lord, the kings of the Assyrians have laid waste the nations and the lands of them all.

**19:18** And they cast their gods into the fire, for they were not gods, but the works of the hands of men, of wood and stone; and they destroyed them.

**19:19** Now therefore, Lord our God, save us from his hand, so that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that You alone are the Lord God."

**19:20** Then Isaiah, the son of Amoz, sent to Hezekiah, saying, "This is what the Lord, the God of Israel, says: 'What you have prayed to Me concerning Sennacherib, king of the Assyrians, I have heard.'

**19:21** This is the word that the Lord has spoken concerning him: 'The virgin daughter of Zion has despised you and mocked you; the daughter of Jerusalem has shaken her head behind your back.

**19:22** Whom have you reproached, and whom have you blasphemed? Against whom have you raised your voice and lifted up your eyes on high? Against the Holy One of Israel.

**19:23** By the hand of your servants you have reproached the Lord, and you have said, "With the multitude of my chariots I have gone up to the heights of the mountains, to the summit of Lebanon, and I have cut down its tall cedars and its choice fir trees. And I have entered up to its borders, and the forest of its Carmel

**19:24** I myself have cut down. And I have drunk foreign waters, and with the soles of my feet I have dried up all the enclosed waters."

**19:25** Have you not heard what I have done from the beginning? From ancient days I formed it, and now I have brought it about; and fortified cities will become a ruin of fighting hills.

**19:26** And those who dwell in them, weak of hand, trembled and were put to shame; they became like the grass of the field and the green vegetation of the rooftops, which dried up before it came to maturity.

**19:27** Your dwelling place, and your going out, and your coming in, and your way I have known beforehand, and your fury against Me.

**19:28** You have raged against Me, and your arrogance has come up into My ears; therefore I will put a ring in your nostrils and a bit in your lips, and I will lead you back on the way by which you came.

**19:29** And to you, Hezekiah, this will be the sign: eat this year what you find; and in the second year, what grows of its own accord; moreover, in the third year, sow and reap, plant vineyards and eat their fruit.

**19:30** And whatever remains of the house of Judah will send root downward and bear fruit upward.

**19:31** For from Jerusalem a remnant will go out, and that which is saved from Mount Zion; the zeal of the Lord of hosts will do this.'

**19:32** Therefore this is what the Lord says concerning the king of the Assyrians: 'He will not enter this city, nor shoot an arrow into it, nor will a shield seize it, nor will a siegework surround it.

**19:33** By the way by which he came, he will return, and he will not enter this city,' says the Lord.

**19:34** 'And I will protect this city and save it for My own sake and for the sake of David My servant.'"

**19:35** So it happened on that night that the angel of the Lord came and struck down in the camp of the Assyrians one hundred eighty-five thousand. And when he had risen at daybreak, he saw all the bodies of the dead; and departing, he went away,

**19:36** and Sennacherib, king of the Assyrians, returned and remained in Nineveh.

**19:37** And while he was worshiping Nisroch his god in the temple, Adrammelech and Sharezer, his sons, struck him down with the sword and fled into the land of the Armenians; and Esarhaddon his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **19:4** — The Vulgate puts argueret ("to reprove") in the imperfect subjunctive coordinated with exprobraret ("to reproach") as part of Rabshakeh's mission, whereas the Hebrew shifts back to God as subject ("and will rebuke the words").
- **19:9** — The Vulgate renders Hebrew Cush as Æthiopia ("Ethiopia") and reads et iret contra eum ("and was going against him") in the temporal clause where the Hebrew has "he returned and sent" (i.e., "sent again").
- **19:24** — The Vulgate connects ego succidi ("I myself have cut down") across the verse break with saltum Carmeli ejus ("the forest of its Carmel") in v. 23, and renders Hebrew "streams of Egypt" literally as aquas clausas ("enclosed waters").
- **19:25** — The Vulgate reads in ruinam collium pugnantium ("a ruin of fighting hills"), translating the Hebrew phrase for "ruinous heaps" literally as "hills" (collium) and "fighting" (pugnantium).
- **19:35** — The Vulgate uses singular verbs (surrexisset, "he had risen"; vidit, "he saw") pointing forward to Sennacherib in v. 36, whereas the Hebrew uses plural ("when they arose early").
- **19:37** — The Vulgate reads in terram Armeniorum ("into the land of the Armenians") where the Hebrew has "into the land of Ararat."
