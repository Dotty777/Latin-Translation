## Text

**25:1** Now it happened in the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, on the tenth day of the month, that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came, he and all his army, against Jerusalem, and they surrounded it and erected fortifications all around it.

**25:2** And the city was shut in and encircled with ramparts until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah,

**25:3** on the ninth day of the month; and famine prevailed in the city, and there was no bread for the people of the land.

**25:4** And the city was breached, and all the fighting men fled at night by the way of the gate that is between the double wall near the king's garden. Meanwhile, the Chaldeans were besieging the city all around. So Zedekiah fled by the way that leads to the plains of the wilderness.

**25:5** And the army of the Chaldeans pursued the king and overtook him in the plain of Jericho; and all the warriors who were with him were scattered and left him.

**25:6** Therefore, having seized the king, they brought him to the king of Babylon at Riblah, who spoke judgment with him.

**25:7** And he killed the sons of Zedekiah in his presence, and gouged out his eyes, and bound him with chains, and brought him to Babylon.

**25:8** In the fifth month, on the seventh day of the month, which is the nineteenth year of the king of Babylon, Nebuzaradan, commander of the army, servant of the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem.

**25:9** And he set fire to the house of the Lord and the house of the king; and he burned up the houses of Jerusalem and every house with fire.

**25:10** And the entire army of the Chaldeans that was with the commander of the soldiers tore down the walls of Jerusalem all around.

**25:11** Now Nebuzaradan, commander of the forces, carried away the remaining part of the people that had stayed in the city, and the deserters who had defected to the king of Babylon, and the rest of the common people.

**25:12** And from the poor of the land he left vinedressers and farmers.

**25:13** Now the Chaldeans broke in pieces the bronze pillars that were in the temple of the Lord, and the bases, and the bronze sea that was in the house of the Lord, and carried away all the bronze to Babylon.

**25:14** They also took the bronze pots, and the scoops, and the three-pronged forks, and the cups, and the small mortars, and all the bronze vessels with which they ministered.

**25:15** Moreover, the commander of the forces took the censers and the bowls, those that were of gold as gold, and those that were of silver as silver,

**25:16** that is, the two pillars, the one sea, and the bases that Solomon had made in the temple of the Lord; the bronze of all the vessels was beyond weight.

**25:17** One pillar had a height of eighteen cubits, and a bronze capital upon it three cubits in height, and latticework and pomegranates upon the capital of the pillar, all of bronze; and the second pillar also had similar adornment.

**25:18** The commander of the forces also took Seraiah the first priest, and Zephaniah the second priest, and three doorkeepers.

**25:19** And from the city he took one eunuch who was set over the fighting men, and five men from those who had stood before the king, whom he found in the city, and Sopher, commander of the army, who examined the recruits from the people of the land, and sixty men from the common people who had been found in the city.

**25:20** Taking them, Nebuzaradan, commander of the soldiers, brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah.

**25:21** And the king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death at Riblah in the land of Hamath; and Judah was carried away from its land.

**25:22** Now over the people who had been left in the land of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had left behind, he set Gedaliah the son of Ahikam, the son of Shaphan.

**25:23** And when all the leaders of the soldiers heard this, they and the men who were with them, namely, that the king of Babylon had appointed Gedaliah, they came to Gedaliah at Mizpah: Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, and Johanan the son of Kareah, and Seraiah the son of Tanhumeth the Netophathite, and Jaazaniah the son of the Maacathite, they and their companions.

**25:24** And Gedaliah swore to them and to their companions, saying, "Do not be afraid to serve the Chaldeans; remain in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and it will be well with you."

**25:25** Now it happened in the seventh month that Ishmael the son of Nethaniah, the son of Elishama, of the royal seed, came, and ten men with him, and they struck down Gedaliah, so that he died, as well as the Jews and the Chaldeans who were with him at Mizpah.

**25:26** And all the people, from the small to the great, and the commanders of the soldiers rose up and came into Egypt, fearing the Chaldeans.

**25:27** And it happened in the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-seventh day of the month, that Evil-merodach king of Babylon, in the year in which he had begun to reign, lifted up the head of Jehoiachin king of Judah from prison.

**25:28** And he spoke kindly to him and set his throne above the throne of the kings who were with him in Babylon.

**25:29** And he changed his garments, which he had worn in prison, and he ate bread continually in his presence all the days of his life.

**25:30** He also appointed an allowance for him without interruption, which was indeed given to him by the king day by day, all the days of his life.

## Translator Notes

- **25:6** — "spoke judgment with him" renders the literal Latin idiom (locutus est cum eo judicium) for passing judicial sentence upon someone.
- **25:9** — The Vulgate reads omnemque domum ("and every house"), without the word "great" found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text ("every great house").
- **25:16** — The Vulgate connects this verse to v. 15 with id est ("that is"), making the bronze furnishings accusative objects of what the commander took; "was beyond weight" translates the literal Latin non erat pondus ("there was no weight").
- **25:19** — Where the Hebrew text has the title "the scribe" (hassopher) of the army commander who mustered the people, the Vulgate treats Sopher as a proper name in apposition to "commander of the army" and uses Roman military terminology (probabat tyrones) for examining recruits.
- **25:24** — The Vulgate reads servire Chaldaeis ("to serve the Chaldeans"), taking the Hebrew word as an infinitive ("from serving") rather than the Masoretic Text's noun "the servants/officials of the Chaldeans."
