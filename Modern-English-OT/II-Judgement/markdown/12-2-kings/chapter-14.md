## Text

**14:1** In the second year of Joash, son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel, Amaziah, son of Joash, king of Judah, began to reign.

**14:2** He was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned for twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Jehoaddan, from Jerusalem.

**14:3** And he did what was right before the Lord, yet not like his father David. He did according to all that his father Joash had done,

**14:4** except only this, that he did not remove the high places; for the people were still offering sacrifices and burning incense on the high places.

**14:5** And when he had secured the kingdom, he struck down his servants who had killed the king, his father.

**14:6** However, he did not kill the sons of those who had killed, according to what is written in the book of the law of Moses, just as the Lord commanded, saying, "Fathers will not die for sons, nor will sons die for fathers; rather, each one will die in his own sin."

**14:7** He himself struck down ten thousand of Edom in the Valley of the Salt Pits, and seized the rock in battle, and called its name Joktheel, to the present day.

**14:8** Then Amaziah sent messengers to Joash, son of Jehoahaz, son of Jehu, king of Israel, saying, "Come, and let us see one another."

**14:9** And Joash, king of Israel, sent back to Amaziah, king of Judah, saying, "The thistle of Lebanon sent to the cedar that is in Lebanon, saying, 'Give your daughter to my son as a wife.' And the beasts of the forest that are in Lebanon passed by and trampled the thistle.

**14:10** By striking, you have prevailed over Edom, and your heart has lifted you up. Be content with glory, and stay in your house; why do you provoke disaster, so that you fall, you and Judah with you?"

**14:11** And Amaziah did not consent. So Joash, king of Israel, went up, and they saw one another, he and Amaziah, king of Judah, at Beth-shemesh, a town of Judah.

**14:12** And Judah was struck down before Israel, and they fled, each one to his tents.

**14:13** Now Joash, king of Israel, captured Amaziah, king of Judah, son of Joash, son of Ahaziah, at Beth-shemesh, and brought him to Jerusalem; and he breached the wall of Jerusalem from the Gate of Ephraim to the Corner Gate, for four hundred cubits.

**14:14** And he took all the gold and silver, and all the vessels that were found in the house of the Lord and in the treasuries of the king, and hostages, and returned to Samaria.

**14:15** Now the rest of the accounts of Joash, what he did, and his might with which he fought against Amaziah, king of Judah, are these not written in the book of the words of the days of the kings of Israel?

**14:16** And Joash slept with his fathers, and was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel, and his son Jeroboam reigned in his place.

**14:17** Now Amaziah, son of Joash, king of Judah, lived for fifteen years after Joash, son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel, died.

**14:18** Now the rest of the accounts of Amaziah, are these not written in the book of the words of the days of the kings of Judah?

**14:19** And a conspiracy was formed against him in Jerusalem, but he fled to Lachish. And they sent after him to Lachish, and killed him there.

**14:20** And they carried him away on horses, and he was buried in Jerusalem with his fathers in the city of David.

**14:21** Then all the people of Judah took Azariah, who was sixteen years old, and appointed him king in place of his father Amaziah.

**14:22** He himself built Elath and restored it to Judah, after the king slept with his fathers.

**14:23** In the fifteenth year of Amaziah, son of Joash, king of Judah, Jeroboam, son of Joash, king of Israel, reigned in Samaria for forty-one years.

**14:24** And he did what is evil before the Lord; he did not depart from all the sins of Jeroboam, son of Nebat, who made Israel sin.

**14:25** He himself restored the borders of Israel from the entrance of Hamath to the Sea of the Wilderness, according to the word of the Lord God of Israel which He spoke through His servant Jonah, son of Amittai, the prophet, who was from Gath, which is in Opher.

**14:26** For the Lord saw the exceedingly bitter affliction of Israel, and that they had been consumed even to those shut up in prison and the lowest, and that there was no one to help Israel.

**14:27** Nor did the Lord say that He would blot out the name of Israel from under heaven, but He saved them by the hand of Jeroboam, son of Joash.

**14:28** Now the rest of the accounts of Jeroboam, and all that he did, and his might with which he waged war, and how he restored Damascus and Hamath to Judah in Israel, are these not written in the book of the words of the days of the kings of Israel?

**14:29** And Jeroboam slept with his fathers, the kings of Israel, and his son Zechariah reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **14:7** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew Valley of Salt as "valle Salinarum" ("Valley of the Salt Pits") and translates the place name Sela as the common noun "petram" ("the rock").
- **14:13** — The Vulgate reads transitively "adduxit eum in Jerusalem" ("brought him to Jerusalem"), whereas the Hebrew Masoretic Text states simply that Joash went to Jerusalem.
- **14:15** — The recurring Vulgate phrase "in libro sermonum [or verborum] dierum" ("in the book of the words of the days") literally renders the Hebrew idiom for royal annals or chronicles, while "verborum" and "sermonum" ("accounts") in this formula cover a king's deeds and affairs.
- **14:25** — The Vulgate splits the Hebrew town name Gath-hepher into two parts, "de Geth, quae est in Opher" ("from Gath, which is in Opher"), and translates the Sea of the Arabah as "mare solitudinis" ("Sea of the Wilderness").
- **14:26** — Latin "usque ad clausos carcere et extremos" ("even to those shut up in prison and the lowest") is Jerome's concrete rendering of a Hebrew idiom meaning "neither bond nor free," that is, no one left at all.
- **14:28** — Latin "Judae" in "restituit Damascum et Emath Judae in Israel" is read here as dative ("to Judah"), matching 14:22, though it can also be taken as genitive ("of Judah," referring to territory that had formerly belonged to Judah).
