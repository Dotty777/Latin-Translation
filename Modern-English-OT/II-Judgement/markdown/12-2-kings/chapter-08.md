## Text

**8:1** Now Elisha spoke to the woman whose son he had caused to live, saying, "Arise, go, you and your household, and live as a foreigner wherever you find a place; for the Lord has summoned a famine, and it will come upon the land for seven years."

**8:2** So she arose and did according to the word of the man of God; and going with her household, she lived as a foreigner in the land of the Philistines for many days.

**8:3** And when the seven years had ended, the woman returned from the land of the Philistines, and she went out to appeal to the king for her house and for her fields.

**8:4** Now the king was speaking with Gehazi, the attendant of the man of God, saying, "Tell me all the great deeds that Elisha has done."

**8:5** And while he was telling the king how he had raised one who was dead, the woman whose son he had made alive appeared, crying out to the king for her house and for her fields. And Gehazi said, "My lord the king, this is the woman, and this is her son whom Elisha raised."

**8:6** And the king questioned the woman, and she told him. And the king appointed one eunuch for her, saying, "Restore to her everything that is hers, and all the revenues of the fields, from the day she left the land until the present."

**8:7** Elisha also came to Damascus, and Ben-hadad king of Syria was sick. And they reported to him, saying, "The man of God has come here."

**8:8** And the king said to Hazael, "Take gifts with you, and go to meet the man of God, and consult the Lord through him, saying, 'Will I be able to escape from this illness of mine?'"

**8:9** So Hazael went to meet him, having with him gifts and all the good things of Damascus, the loads of forty camels. And when he stood before him, he said, "Your son Ben-hadad, king of Syria, has sent me to you, saying, 'Will I be able to be healed from this illness of mine?'"

**8:10** And Elisha said to him, "Go, say to him, 'You will be healed.' Yet the Lord has shown me that he will certainly die."

**8:11** And he stood with him, and was troubled to the flushing of his face; and the man of God wept.

**8:12** Hazael said to him, "Why is my lord weeping?" And he said, "Because I know what evils you are about to do to the sons of Israel. You will burn their fortified cities with fire, and you will kill their young men with the sword, and you will crush their little ones, and you will split open the pregnant women."

**8:13** And Hazael said, "For what am I, your servant, a dog, that I should do this great thing?" And Elisha said, "The Lord has shown me that you will be king of Syria."

**8:14** When he had departed from Elisha, he came to his master. And he said to him, "What did Elisha say to you?" And he answered, "He said to me, 'You will regain health.'"

**8:15** And when the next day had come, he took a blanket and poured water on it and spread it over his face; and when he had died, Hazael reigned in his place.

**8:16** In the fifth year of Joram son of Ahab, king of Israel, and of Jehoshaphat king of Judah, Joram son of Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, began to reign.

**8:17** He was thirty-two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem.

**8:18** And he walked in the ways of the kings of Israel, just as the house of Ahab had walked, for a daughter of Ahab was his wife; and he did what is evil in the sight of the Lord.

**8:19** Yet the Lord was not willing to destroy Judah, for the sake of David His servant, just as He had promised him, that He would give a lamp to him and to his sons for all days.

**8:20** In his days Edom broke away so as not to be under Judah, and appointed a king for itself.

**8:21** And Joram came to Zair, and all the chariots with him; and he arose by night and struck the Idumeans who had surrounded him, and the commanders of the chariots; but the people fled to their tents.

**8:22** Therefore Edom broke away so as not to be under Judah, until this day. Then Libnah also broke away at that time.

**8:23** Now the rest of the accounts of Joram, and everything that he did, are these not written in the book of the words of the days of the kings of Judah?

**8:24** And Joram slept with his fathers, and was buried with them in the city of David, and Ahaziah his son reigned in his place.

**8:25** In the twelfth year of Joram son of Ahab, king of Israel, Ahaziah, son of Joram king of Judah, began to reign.

**8:26** Ahaziah was twenty-two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned one year in Jerusalem; his mother's name was Athaliah, daughter of Omri king of Israel.

**8:27** And he walked in the ways of the house of Ahab, and did what is evil before the Lord, just like the house of Ahab; for he was a son-in-law of the house of Ahab.

**8:28** He also went with Joram son of Ahab to fight against Hazael king of Syria at Ramoth-gilead, and the Syrians wounded Joram.

**8:29** So he returned to be healed in Jezreel, because the Syrians had wounded him at Ramoth while he was fighting against Hazael king of Syria. Moreover, Ahaziah son of Joram, king of Judah, went down to visit Joram son of Ahab in Jezreel, because he was sick there.

## Translator Notes

- **8:2** — The Vulgate reads "for many days" (diebus multis), whereas the Masoretic Hebrew text reads "seven years."
- **8:10** — The Vulgate reads "You will be healed" (Sanaberis), aligning with the Hebrew Qere and Septuagint, whereas the written Hebrew text (Ketiv) reads "You will not live."
- **8:11** — The critical text prints Stetique (literally "and I stood"), evidently for Stetitque ("and he stood"), which the third-person narrative requires; the Vulgate also reads "was troubled to the flushing of his face" (conturbatus est usque ad suffusionem vultus), where the Hebrew reads "fixed his gaze until he was ashamed."
- **8:16** — The Vulgate coordinates both kings in the genitive ("of Joram... and of Jehoshaphat king of Judah"), reflecting a difficult chronological clause in the Hebrew.
- **8:23** — "The book of the words of the days" (libro verborum dierum) literally translates the Hebrew idiom for the royal chronicles or annals.
