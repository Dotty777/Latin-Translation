## Text

**12:1** In the seventh year of Jehu, Joash became king, and he reigned for forty years in Jerusalem. The name of his mother was Zibiah from Beersheba.

**12:2** And Joash did what was right before the Lord all the days in which Jehoiada the priest taught him.

**12:3** Nevertheless, he did not remove the high places, for the people were still sacrificing and burning incense on the high places.

**12:4** And Joash said to the priests, "All the money of the holy things that is brought into the temple of the Lord by those who pass by, that is offered for the price of a soul, and that they bring into the temple of the Lord willingly and by the choice of their heart,

**12:5** let the priests receive it according to their order, and let them carry out the repairs of the house, if they see anything in need of restoration."

**12:6** Now up to the twenty-third year of King Joash, the priests had not carried out the repairs of the temple.

**12:7** And King Joash called Jehoiada the high priest and the priests, saying to them, "Why are you not carrying out the repairs of the temple? Therefore, do not receive money any longer according to your order, but turn it over for the restoration of the temple."

**12:8** And the priests were prohibited from receiving money any further from the people and from carrying out the repairs of the house.

**12:9** And Jehoiada the high priest took one treasury chest, and opened a hole in the top, and placed it beside the altar on the right side of those entering the house of the Lord; and the priests who guarded the doors put into it all the money that was brought to the temple of the Lord.

**12:10** And when they saw that there was a very large amount of money in the treasury chest, the king's scribe and the high priest went up, and they poured out and counted the money that was found in the house of the Lord.

**12:11** And they gave it according to number and measure into the hand of those who were in charge of the masons of the house of the Lord, who spent it on the carpenters and on the masons, those who worked in the house of the Lord

**12:12** and made the repairs, and on those who cut stones, and to buy timber and stones that were quarried, so that the restoration of the house of the Lord was completed in all things that needed expense to strengthen the house.

**12:13** Nevertheless, there were not made from that same money water jars of the temple of the Lord, or forks, or censers, or trumpets, or any vessel of gold and silver, from the money that was brought into the temple of the Lord.

**12:14** For it was given to those who were doing the work, so that the temple of the Lord might be restored.

**12:15** And no accounting was made with those men who received the money to distribute it to the craftsmen, but they handled it in good faith.

**12:16** But they did not bring the money for trespass and the money for sins into the temple of the Lord, because it belonged to the priests.

**12:17** Then Hazael king of Syria went up and fought against Gath, and captured it; and he set his face to go up against Jerusalem.

**12:18** For this reason Joash king of Judah took all the sacred things that Jehoshaphat, and Joram, and Ahaziah, his fathers, kings of Judah, had consecrated, and those that he himself had offered, and all the silver that could be found in the treasuries of the temple of the Lord and in the king's palace, and sent them to Hazael king of Syria; and he withdrew from Jerusalem.

**12:19** Now the rest of the accounts of Joash, and all that he did, are these not written in the book of the words of the days of the kings of Judah?

**12:20** And his servants rose up and conspired among themselves, and struck Joash in the house of Millo, on the descent of Silla.

**12:21** For Jozacar the son of Shimeath and Jozabad the son of Shomer, his servants, struck him, and he died; and they buried him with his fathers in the city of David, and Amaziah his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **12:4** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew assessment phrase literally as "by those who pass by" (a praetereuntibus) and "for the price of a soul" (pro pretio animae), referring to temple offerings and redemption dues.
- **12:5** — Latin juxta ordinem suum ("according to their order," also in 12:7) differs from the Hebrew ("from his acquaintance"). The compound sartatecta (literally "mended and roofed," vv. 5-8, 12) is a classical Roman architectural term for the structural repairs and upkeep of a building.
- **12:8** — The Vulgate reads prohibiti sunt ("were prohibited"), whereas the Hebrew states that the priests "consented" or "agreed" to the arrangement.
- **12:10** — The Vulgate reads effundebant ("poured out"), depicting the officials emptying the chest to count the money, where the Hebrew verb means "tied up" (in bags).
- **12:13** — The Vulgate specifies hydriae ("water jars"), fuscinulae ("forks"), and thuribula ("censers") where the Hebrew lists basins, snuffers, and sprinkling bowls.
