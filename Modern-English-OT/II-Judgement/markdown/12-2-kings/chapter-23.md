## Text

**23:1** And they reported to the king what she had said. Then he sent, and all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem were gathered to him.

**23:2** And the king went up to the temple of the Lord, and all the men of Judah, and all who lived in Jerusalem with him, the priests and the prophets, and all the people from small to great; and with everyone listening, he read all the words of the book of the covenant that was found in the house of the Lord.

**23:3** And the king stood upon the step and made a covenant before the Lord, that they would walk after the Lord and keep His precepts and testimonies and ceremonies with all their heart and with their whole soul, and re-establish the words of this covenant that were written in that book; and the people agreed to the pact.

**23:4** And the king commanded Hilkiah the high priest, and the priests of the second order, and the doorkeepers, to throw out of the temple of the Lord all the vessels that had been made for Baal, and in the grove, and for all the host of heaven; and he burned them outside Jerusalem in the valley of Kidron, and carried their dust to Bethel.

**23:5** And he destroyed the soothsayers whom the kings of Judah had appointed to sacrifice at the high places throughout the cities of Judah and around Jerusalem, and those who burned incense to Baal, and to the sun, and to the moon, and to the twelve signs, and to all the host of heaven.

**23:6** And he had the grove carried out from the house of the Lord outside Jerusalem in the valley of Kidron, and burned it there, and reduced it to dust, and threw it upon the tombs of the common people.

**23:7** He also tore down the small chambers of the effeminate men that were in the house of the Lord, for which the women were weaving, as it were, little houses of the grove.

**23:8** And he gathered all the priests from the cities of Judah, and defiled the high places where the priests used to sacrifice, from Geba to Beersheba; and he tore down the altars of the gates at the entrance of the doorway of Joshua, the governor of the city, which was on the left side of the gate of the city.

**23:9** Nevertheless, the priests of the high places did not go up to the altar of the Lord in Jerusalem, but only ate unleavened bread among their brothers.

**23:10** He also defiled Topheth, which is in the valley of the son of Hinnom, so that no one would consecrate his son or daughter through fire to Molech.

**23:11** He also removed the horses that the kings of Judah had given to the sun at the entrance of the temple of the Lord, next to the chamber of Nathan-melech the eunuch, who was in Pharurim; and he burned the chariots of the sun with fire.

**23:12** The king also tore down the altars that were on the roof of the upper room of Ahaz, which the kings of Judah had made, and the altars that Manasseh had made in the two courts of the temple of the Lord; and he ran from there and scattered their ashes into the Kidron brook.

**23:13** The king also polluted the high places that were in Jerusalem, on the right side of the mountain of offense, which Solomon king of Israel had built for Ashtoreth, the idol of the Sidonians, and for Chemosh, the offense of Moab, and for Milcom, the abomination of the sons of Ammon.

**23:14** And he shattered the statues and cut down the groves, and filled their places with the bones of the dead.

**23:15** Moreover, as for the altar that was in Bethel and the high place that Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin, had made: both that altar and the high place he tore down, and burned, and crushed to dust, and he also set fire to the grove.

**23:16** And Josiah turned and saw there the tombs that were on the mountain; and he sent and took the bones from the tombs, and burned them upon the altar, and polluted it according to the word of the Lord that the man of God spoke, who had foretold these things.

**23:17** And he said, "What is that monument that I see?" And the citizens of that city answered him, "It is the tomb of the man of God who came from Judah and foretold these things that you have done upon the altar of Bethel."

**23:18** And he said, "Leave him alone; let no one disturb his bones." And his bones remained untouched, along with the bones of the prophet who had come from Samaria.

**23:19** Moreover, Josiah removed all the shrines of the high places that were in the cities of Samaria, which the kings of Israel had made to provoke the Lord, and he did to them according to all the deeds that he had done in Bethel.

**23:20** And he killed upon the altars all the priests of the high places who were there, and burned human bones upon them; and he returned to Jerusalem.

**23:21** And he commanded all the people, saying, "Keep the Passover to the Lord your God, according to what is written in the book of this covenant."

**23:22** For no such Passover was kept from the days of the judges who judged Israel, and in all the days of the kings of Israel and the kings of Judah,

**23:23** as this Passover was kept to the Lord in Jerusalem in the eighteenth year of King Josiah.

**23:24** Moreover, Josiah removed the mediums and the diviners, and the figures of idols, and the impurities, and the abominations that had been in the land of Judah and Jerusalem, so that he might establish the words of the law that were written in the book that Hilkiah the priest found in the temple of the Lord.

**23:25** Before him there was no king like him who returned to the Lord with all his heart and with his whole soul and with all his strength, according to all the law of Moses; nor did anyone like him arise after him.

**23:26** Nevertheless, the Lord did not turn away from the anger of His great fury, with which His fury was enraged against Judah because of the provocations with which Manasseh had provoked Him.

**23:27** Therefore the Lord said, "I will also remove Judah from My presence, just as I removed Israel, and I will cast away this city that I chose, Jerusalem, and the house of which I said, 'My name will be there.'"

**23:28** Now the rest of the accounts of Josiah, and all that he did, are these not written in the book of the words of the days of the kings of Judah?

**23:29** In his days Pharaoh Necho king of Egypt went up against the king of the Assyrians to the Euphrates River, and King Josiah went out to meet him; and he was killed at Megiddo when he had seen him.

**23:30** And his servants carried him dead from Megiddo, and brought him to Jerusalem, and buried him in his own tomb. And the people of the land took Jehoahaz son of Josiah, and anointed him, and appointed him king in place of his father.

**23:31** Jehoahaz was twenty-three years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem; his mother's name was Hamutal, the daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah.

**23:32** And he did evil before the Lord, according to all that his fathers had done.

**23:33** And Pharaoh Necho bound him at Riblah, which is in the land of Hamath, so that he would not reign in Jerusalem; and he imposed a fine on the land of one hundred talents of silver and a talent of gold.

**23:34** And Pharaoh Necho appointed Eliakim son of Josiah as king in place of Josiah his father, and changed his name to Jehoiakim. But he took Jehoahaz and led him into Egypt, and he died there.

**23:35** Now Jehoiakim gave the silver and the gold to Pharaoh, having assessed the land individually so that it would be contributed according to the command of Pharaoh; and he exacted from each one according to his means, both silver and gold, from the people of the land, so that he might give it to Pharaoh Necho.

**23:36** Jehoiakim was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem; his mother's name was Zebidah, the daughter of Pedaiah of Rumah.

**23:37** And he did evil before the Lord, according to all that his fathers had done.

## Translator Notes

- **23:1** — In the Clementine Vulgate, the opening sentence ("And they reported to the king what she had said") begins 23:1, whereas in the Hebrew Masoretic Text and most modern English Bibles it concludes 22:20.
- **23:4** — Here and throughout the chapter (vv. 6, 7, 14, 15), the Vulgate renders Hebrew Asherah (a wooden cultic image or pole) as lucus ("grove"), following the Septuagint; in v. 4 it also reads "in the grove" (in luco).
- **23:5** — Vulgate "soothsayers" (aruspices) renders a Hebrew term for idolatrous priests, and "the twelve signs" (duodecim signis) specifies the twelve signs of the zodiac where the Hebrew reads "constellations" (mazzalot).
- **23:11** — Vulgate "in Pharurim" treats the obscure Hebrew term parwarim ("precincts" or "colonnades") as a proper name, and its masculine relative pronoun (qui) attaches Pharurim to Nathan-melech rather than to the chamber.
- **23:12** — Vulgate "and he ran from there" (et cucurrit inde) reflects an alternate vocalization of the Hebrew verb usually understood as "and he crushed them there."
