## Text

**16:1** In the seventeenth year of Pekah son of Remaliah, Ahaz son of Jotham, king of Judah, became king.

**16:2** Ahaz was twenty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. He did not do what was pleasing in the sight of the Lord his God, as David his father did,

**16:3** but he walked in the way of the kings of Israel; moreover, he even consecrated his son, passing him through the fire according to the idols of the nations, which the Lord destroyed before the sons of Israel.

**16:4** He also sacrificed victims and burned incense on the high places, and on the hills, and under every leafy tree.

**16:5** Then Rezin, king of Syria, and Pekah, son of Remaliah, king of Israel, went up against Jerusalem to fight; and when they besieged Ahaz, they were not able to overcome him.

**16:6** At that time Rezin, king of Syria, restored Elath to Syria and drove out the Judeans from Elath; and the Edomites came into Elath and lived there to this day.

**16:7** Now Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser, king of the Assyrians, saying, "I am your servant and your son. Come up and save me from the hand of the king of Syria and from the hand of the king of Israel, who have risen up against me."

**16:8** And when he had gathered the silver and gold that could be found in the house of the Lord and in the treasuries of the king, he sent gifts to the king of the Assyrians.

**16:9** And he consented to his wish; for the king of the Assyrians went up against Damascus and laid it waste, and deported its inhabitants to Cyrene, and he put Rezin to death.

**16:10** And King Ahaz proceeded to Damascus to meet Tiglath-pileser, king of the Assyrians; and when he had seen the altar of Damascus, King Ahaz sent to Uriah the priest a pattern of it and a likeness according to all its workmanship.

**16:11** And Uriah the priest built an altar; according to all that King Ahaz had commanded from Damascus, so Uriah the priest did until King Ahaz came from Damascus.

**16:12** And when the king had come from Damascus, he saw the altar and venerated it; and he went up and offered burnt offerings and his sacrifice,

**16:13** and poured out drink offerings, and poured upon the altar the blood of the peace offerings that he had offered.

**16:14** Moreover, the bronze altar that was before the Lord he moved from the front of the temple, and from the place of the altar, and from the place of the temple of the Lord; and he placed it on the side of the altar toward the north.

**16:15** King Ahaz also commanded Uriah the priest, saying, "Upon the greater altar offer the morning burnt offering and the evening sacrifice, and the burnt offering of the king and his sacrifice, and the burnt offering of all the people of the land and their sacrifices and their drink offerings; and all the blood of the burnt offering and all the blood of the sacrificial victim you shall pour out upon it; but the bronze altar shall be ready according to my will."

**16:16** So Uriah the priest did according to everything that King Ahaz had commanded.

**16:17** Now King Ahaz removed the carved bases and the basin that was on top; and he took down the sea from the bronze oxen that supported it, and placed it upon a pavement laid with stone.

**16:18** Also the Musach of the Sabbath, which he had built in the temple, and the outer entrance of the king he turned into the temple of the Lord because of the king of the Assyrians.

**16:19** Now the rest of the acts of Ahaz that he did, are these not written in the book of the words of the days of the kings of Judah?

**16:20** And Ahaz slept with his fathers, and was buried with them in the city of David; and Hezekiah his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **16:3** — The Vulgate renders the Hebrew idiom for child sacrifice doubly as 'consecrated... passing him through the fire' (consecravit, transferens per ignem). It also reads 'idols' (idola) with neuter plural 'which' (quae) where the Hebrew has 'abominations' of the nations whom the Lord drove out.
- **16:9** — The Vulgate identifies the place of deportation as 'Cyrene' (Cyrenen), whereas the Hebrew text reads Kir.
- **16:12** — The Vulgate reads 'venerated it' (veneratus est illud), whereas the Hebrew states that Ahaz 'approached the altar.'
- **16:14** — The Vulgate reads 'from the place of the altar, and from the place of the temple of the Lord' (de loco altaris, et de loco templi Domini), where the Hebrew reads 'from between the altar and the house of the Lord.'
- **16:18** — The Vulgate retains the untranslated Hebrew term Musach (likely a covered canopy or portico used on the Sabbath) and reads 'turned into the temple of the Lord' (convertit in templum Domini), where the Hebrew is generally understood as removing it from the temple.
