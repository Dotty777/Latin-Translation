1 MACCABEES 1

1:1  LA: Et factum est, postquam percussit Alexander Philippi Macedo, qui primus regnavit in Græcia, egressus de terra Cethim, Darium regem Persarum et Medorum :
     EN: After Alexander the Macedonian, son of Philip, who first reigned in Greece, had come out of the land of Kittim and struck down Darius king of the Persians and Medes,

1:2  LA: constituit prælia multa, et obtinuit omnium munitiones, et interfecit reges terræ,
     EN: he fought many battles, seized everyone's strongholds, and killed the kings of the earth.

1:3  LA: et pertransiit usque ad fines terræ : et accepit spolia multitudinis gentium, et siluit terra in conspectu ejus.
     EN: He passed through to the ends of the earth and took the spoils of a multitude of nations, and the earth fell silent before him.

1:4  LA: Et congregavit virtutem, et exercitum fortem nimis : et exaltatum est, et elevatum cor ejus :
     EN: He gathered a powerful force and an exceedingly strong army, and his heart was exalted and lifted up.

1:5  LA: Et post hæc decidit in lectum, et cognovit quia moreretur.
     EN: After this he fell sick in bed and knew that he would die.

1:6  LA: Et vocavit pueros suos nobiles, qui secum erant nutriti a juventute : et divisit illis regnum suum, cum adhuc viveret.
     EN: He summoned his noble attendants, who had been brought up with him from youth, and divided his kingdom among them while he was still alive.

1:7  LA: Et regnavit Alexander annis duodecim, et mortuus est.
     EN: Alexander reigned twelve years and died.

1:8  LA: Et obtinuerunt pueri ejus regnum, unusquisque in loco suo :
     EN: His attendants took possession of the kingdom, each in his own place.

1:9  LA: et imposuerunt omnes sibi diademata post mortem ejus, et filii eorum post eos annis multis, et multiplicata sunt mala in terra.
     EN: They all put on diadems after his death, and their sons after them for many years; and evils multiplied on the earth.

1:10  LA: Et exiit ex eis radix peccatrix, Antiochus illustris, filius Antiochi regis, qui fuerat Romæ obses : et regnavit in anno centesimo trigesimo septimo regni Græcorum.
      EN: From them came a sinful root, Antiochus the Illustrious, son of King Antiochus, who had been a hostage in Rome. He became king in the one hundred thirty-seventh year of the kingdom of the Greeks.

1:11  LA: In diebus illis, exierunt ex Israël filii iniqui, et suaserunt multis, dicentes : Eamus, et disponamus testamentum cum gentibus, quæ circa nos sunt : quia ex quo recessimus ab eis, invenerunt nos multa mala.
      EN: In those days lawless sons came out of Israel and persuaded many, saying, “Let us go and make a covenant with the nations around us, because since we separated ourselves from them, many evils have come upon us.”

1:12  LA: Et bonus visus est sermo in oculis eorum.
      EN: The proposal seemed good in their eyes.

1:13  LA: Et destinaverunt aliqui de populo, et abierunt ad regem : et dedit illis potestatem ut facerent justitiam gentium.
      EN: Some of the people decided to do this and went to the king, who gave them authority to follow the customs of the nations.

1:14  LA: Et ædificaverunt gymnasium in Jerosolymis secundum leges nationum :
      EN: They built a gymnasium in Jerusalem according to the laws of the nations.

1:15  LA: et fecerunt sibi præputia, et recesserunt a testamento sancto, et juncti sunt nationibus, et venundati sunt ut facerent malum. · Translation note. The Latin explicitly says that they made foreskins for themselves. The wording is retained; the verse does not describe the procedure.
      EN: They made foreskins for themselves, departed from the holy covenant, joined themselves to the nations, and sold themselves to do evil.

1:16  LA: Et paratum est regnum in conspectu Antiochi, et cœpit regnare in terra Ægypti ut regnaret super duo regna.
      EN: The kingdom was established before Antiochus, and he set about reigning in the land of Egypt, so that he might reign over two kingdoms.

1:17  LA: Et intravit in Ægyptum in multitudine gravi, in curribus, et elephantis, et equitibus, et copiosa navium multitudine :
      EN: He entered Egypt with a mighty multitude, with chariots, elephants, horsemen, and a great multitude of ships.

1:18  LA: et constituit bellum adversus Ptolemæum regem Ægypti, et veritus est Ptolemæus a facie ejus, et fugit, et ceciderunt vulnerati multi.
      EN: He made war against Ptolemy king of Egypt. Ptolemy was afraid before him and fled, and many fell wounded.

1:19  LA: Et comprehendit civitates munitas in terra Ægypti, et accepit spolia terræ Ægypti.
      EN: He captured fortified cities in the land of Egypt and took the spoils of the land of Egypt.

1:20  LA: Et convertit Antiochus, postquam percussit Ægyptum in centesimo et quadragesimo tertio anno : et ascendit ad Israël,
      EN: After striking Egypt in the one hundred forty-third year, Antiochus returned and went up against Israel.

1:21  LA: Et intravit in sanctificationem cum superbia, et accepit altare aureum, et candelabrum luminis, et universa vasa ejus, et mensam propositionis, et libatoria, et phialas, et mortariola aurea, et velum, et coronas, et ornamentum aureum, quod in facie templi erat : et comminuit omnia. · Translation note. Mortariola aurea is rendered little golden mortars, preserving the named vessel rather than replacing it with an independently identified ritual implement. Its precise form and function need lexical review.
      EN: He entered the sanctuary in arrogance and took the golden altar, the lampstand for the light and all its vessels, the table of presentation, the vessels for pouring offerings, the bowls, the little golden mortars, the curtain, the crowns, and the golden ornament on the front of the temple. He broke everything to pieces.

1:22  LA: 
      EN: Corresponding Latin text: 1:23. The complete verse is displayed in its own card.

1:23  LA: Et accepit argentum, et aurum, et vasa concupiscibilia : et accepit thesauros occultos, quos invenit : et sublatis omnibus, abiit in terram suam.
      EN: He took the silver, the gold, and the precious vessels, and took the hidden treasures he found. Having taken everything away, he departed to his own land.

1:24  LA: Et fecit cædem hominum, et locutus est in superbia magna.
      EN: He slaughtered people and spoke with great arrogance.

1:25  LA: Et factus est planctus magnus in Israël, et in omni loco eorum :
      EN: Great mourning arose in Israel and in all their dwelling places.

1:26  LA: et ingemuerunt principes et seniores ; virgines et juvenes infirmati sunt : et speciositas mulierum immutata est.
      EN: Leaders and elders groaned; virgins and young men grew faint, and the beauty of the women was changed.

1:27  LA: Omnis maritus sumpsit lamentum, et quæ sedebant in thoro maritali, lugebant :
      EN: Every husband took up lamentation, and the women who sat on the marriage bed mourned.

1:28  LA: et commota est terra super habitantes in ea, et universa domus Jacob induit confusionem.
      EN: The earth shook over its inhabitants, and the whole house of Jacob clothed itself in shame.

1:29  LA: Et post duos annos dierum, misit rex principem tributorum in civitates Juda, et venit Jerusalem cum turba magna.
      EN: After two full years, the king sent the chief collector of tribute to the cities of Judah, and he came to Jerusalem with a great crowd.

1:30  LA: Et locutus est ad eos verba pacifica in dolo : et crediderunt ei.
      EN: He spoke peaceful words to them deceitfully, and they believed him.

1:31  LA: Et accepit spolia civitatis : et succendit eam igni, et destruxit domos ejus, et muros ejus in circuitu :
      EN: He took the city's spoils, burned it with fire, and tore down its houses and its surrounding walls.

1:32  LA: et captivas duxerunt mulieres, et natos et pecora possederunt.
      EN: They led the women away captive and took possession of the children and livestock.

1:33  LA: Et ædificaverunt civitatem David muro magno et firmo, et turribus firmis, et facta est illis in arcem :
      EN: They built up the city of David with a great, strong wall and strong towers, and it became a citadel for them.

1:34  LA: et posuerunt illic gentem peccatricem viros iniquos, et convaluerunt in ea : et posuerunt arma, et escas, et congregaverunt spolia Jerusalem :
      EN: They stationed there a sinful nation, lawless men, and they strengthened themselves in it. They stored weapons and food and gathered the spoils of Jerusalem.

1:35  LA: et reposuerunt illic : et facti sunt in laqueum magnum.
      EN: They deposited them there, and they became a great snare.

1:36  LA: Et factum est hoc ad insidias sanctificationi, et in diabolum malum in Israël : · Translation note. Diabolum malum is rendered evil adversary in the military context. The Latin noun need not identify the personal Devil here.
      EN: This became an ambush against the sanctuary and an evil adversary in Israel.

1:37  LA: et effuderunt sanguinem innocentem per circuitum sanctificationis, et contaminaverunt sanctificationem.
      EN: They poured out innocent blood around the sanctuary and defiled the sanctuary.

1:38  LA: Et fugerunt habitatores Jerusalem propter eos, et facta est habitatio exterorum, et facta est extera semini suo, et nati ejus reliquerunt eam.
      EN: The inhabitants of Jerusalem fled because of them. It became a dwelling of foreigners and became foreign to its own offspring, and its children abandoned it.

1:39  LA: Sanctificatio ejus desolata est sicut solitudo ; dies festi ejus conversi sunt in luctum, sabbata ejus in opprobrium, honores ejus in nihilum.
      EN: Its sanctuary was desolate like a wilderness; its feast days turned into mourning, its sabbaths into disgrace, and its honors into nothing.

1:40  LA: Secundum gloriam ejus multiplicata est ignominia ejus, et sublimitas ejus conversa est in luctum.
      EN: Its dishonor multiplied in proportion to its glory, and its exaltation turned into mourning.

1:41  LA: Et scripsit rex Antiochus omni regno suo ut esset omnis populus unus : et relinqueret unusquisque legem suam.
      EN: King Antiochus wrote to his whole kingdom that all the people should be one, and that each should abandon his own law.

1:42  LA: Et consenserunt omnes gentes secundum verbum regis Antiochi :
      EN: All the nations agreed to the word of King Antiochus.

1:43  LA: et multi ex Israël consenserunt servituti ejus, et sacrificaverunt idolis, et coinquinaverunt sabbatum.
      EN: Many from Israel also agreed to serve him, sacrificed to idols, and defiled the sabbath.

1:44  LA: Et misit rex libros per manus nuntiorum in Jerusalem, et in omnes civitates Juda, ut sequerentur leges gentium terræ,
      EN: Through the hands of messengers the king sent letters to Jerusalem and all the cities of Judah, ordering them to follow the laws of the nations of the earth,

1:45  LA: et prohiberent holocausta et sacrificia, et placationes fieri in templo Dei,
      EN: to forbid burnt offerings, sacrifices, and propitiatory offerings in the temple of God,

1:46  LA: et jussit coinquinari sancta, et sanctum populum Israël.
      EN: He ordered the holy things and the holy people of Israel to be defiled.

1:47  LA: Et jussit ædificari aras, et templa, et idola, et immolari carnes suillas, et pecora communia,
      EN: He ordered altars, temples, and idols to be built, and swine's flesh and unclean animals to be sacrificed,

1:48  LA: et relinquere filios suos incircumcisos, et coinquinari animas eorum in omnibus immundis, et abominationibus, ita ut obliviscerentur legem, et immutarent omnes justificationes Dei :
      EN: and ordered them to leave their sons uncircumcised and to defile their souls with every unclean thing and abomination, so that they would forget the law and change all God's ordinances.

1:49  LA: 
      EN: Corresponding Latin text: 1:51. The complete verse is displayed in its own card.

1:50  LA: et quicumque non fecissent secundum verbum regis Antiochi, morerentur.
      EN: Whoever did not act according to the word of King Antiochus was to die.

1:51  LA: Secundum omnia verba hæc scripsit omni regno suo : et præposuit principes populo, qui hæc fieri cogerent.
      EN: According to all these words he wrote to his whole kingdom, and he set rulers over the people to compel them to do these things.

1:52  LA: Et congregati sunt multi de populo ad eos qui dereliquerant legem Domini, et fecerunt mala super terram :
      EN: Many of the people gathered to those who had abandoned the law of the Lord, and they did evil in the land.

1:53  LA: et effugaverunt populum Israël in abditis, et in absconditis fugitivorum locis.
      EN: They drove the people of Israel into secret places and into the hidden refuges of fugitives.

1:54  LA: Die quintadecima mensis Casleu, quinto et quadragesimo et centesimo anno, ædificavit rex Antiochus abominandum idolum desolationis super altare Dei, et per universas civitates Juda in circuitu ædificaverunt aras :
      EN: On the fifteenth day of the month of Casleu, in the one hundred forty-fifth year, King Antiochus built the abominable idol of desolation upon the altar of God, and they built altars throughout the surrounding cities of Judah.

1:55  LA: et ante januas domorum et in plateis incendebant thura, et sacrificabant :
      EN: They burned incense and offered sacrifices before the doors of the houses and in the streets.

1:56  LA: et libros legis Dei combusserunt igni, scindentes eos :
      EN: They tore up the books of the law of God and burned them with fire.

1:57  LA: et apud quemcumque inveniebantur libri testamenti Domini, et quicumque observabat legem Domini, secundum edictum regis trucidabant eum.
      EN: Anyone with whom books of the Lord's covenant were found, and anyone who observed the law of the Lord, they slaughtered according to the king's edict.

1:58  LA: In virtute sua faciebant hæc populo Israël, qui inveniebatur in omni mense et mense in civitatibus.
      EN: In their power they did these things to the people of Israel who were found in the cities, month after month.

1:59  LA: Et quinta et vigesima die mensis sacrificabant super aram, quæ erat contra altare. · Translation note. Contra altare is rendered opposite the altar. The preposition is not silently replaced with upon or over from another textual tradition.
      EN: On the twenty-fifth day of the month they sacrificed on the altar that stood opposite the altar of God.

1:60  LA: Et mulieres, quæ circumcidebant filios suos, trucidabantur secundum jussum regis Antiochi,
      EN: Women who circumcised their sons were slaughtered according to the command of King Antiochus.

1:61  LA: et suspendebant pueros a cervicibus per universas domos eorum : et eos, qui circumciderant illos, trucidabant. · Translation note. The Latin has a cervicibus, from necks, without explicitly naming whose necks. The English pronoun does not settle that reference; the preceding verse names the women. A translation expressly naming the mothers would make a contextual interpretation explicit.
      EN: They hung the children from their necks throughout all their houses, and they slaughtered those who had circumcised them.

1:62  LA: Et multi de populo Israël definierunt apud se, ut non manducarent immunda : et elegerunt magis mori, quam cibis coinquinari immundis :
      EN: Many of the people of Israel resolved within themselves not to eat unclean things, and chose to die rather than be defiled by unclean foods.

1:63  LA: et noluerunt infringere legem Dei sanctam, et trucidati sunt :
      EN: They would not break the holy law of God, and they were slaughtered.

1:64  LA: et facta est ira magna super populum valde.
      EN: Exceedingly great wrath came upon the people.

1 MACCABEES 2

2:1  LA: In diebus illis surrexit Mathathias filius Joannis filii Simeonis, sacerdos ex filiis Joarib, ab Jerusalem, et consedit in monte Modin :
     EN: In those days Mattathias son of John, son of Simeon, a priest of the sons of Joarib, arose from Jerusalem and settled on Mount Modein.

2:2  LA: et habebat filios quinque, Joannem, qui cognominabatur Gaddis :
     EN: He had five sons: John, who was surnamed Gaddis;

2:3  LA: et Simonem, qui cognominabatur Thasi :
     EN: Simon, who was surnamed Thassi;

2:4  LA: et Judam, qui vocabatur Machabæus :
     EN: Judas, who was called Maccabeus;

2:5  LA: et Eleazarum, qui cognominabatur Abaron : et Jonathan, qui cognominabatur Apphus :
     EN: Eleazar, who was surnamed Abaron; and Jonathan, who was surnamed Apphus.

2:6  LA: hi viderunt mala, quæ fiebant in populo Juda, et in Jerusalem.
     EN: They saw the evils that were being done among the people of Judah and in Jerusalem.

2:7  LA: Et dixit Mathathias : Væ mihi ! ut quid natus sum videre contritionem populi mei, et contritionem civitatis sanctæ, et sedere illic, cum datur in manibus inimicorum ?
     EN: Mattathias said, “Woe to me! Why was I born to see the crushing of my people and the crushing of the holy city, and to sit there while it is given into the hands of enemies?

2:8  LA: Sancta in manu extraneorum facta sunt : templum ejus sicut homo ignobilis.
     EN: The holy things have come into the hands of strangers; her temple is like a dishonored man.

2:9  LA: Vasa gloriæ ejus captiva abducta sunt : trucidati sunt senes ejus in plateis, et juvenes ejus ceciderunt in gladio inimicorum.
     EN: Her glorious vessels have been carried away captive. Her elders have been slaughtered in the streets, and her young men have fallen by the enemies' sword.

2:10  LA: Quæ gens non hæreditavit regnum ejus et non obtinuit spolia ejus ?
      EN: What nation has not inherited her kingdom and taken possession of her spoils?

2:11  LA: Omnis compositio ejus ablata est. Quæ erat libera, facta est ancilla.
      EN: All her adornment has been taken away. She who was free has become a slave.

2:12  LA: Et ecce sancta nostra, et pulchritudo nostra, et claritas nostra desolata est, et coinquinaverunt ea gentes.
      EN: Look! Our holy things, our beauty, and our splendor have been laid waste, and the nations have defiled them.

2:13  LA: Quo ergo nobis adhuc vivere ?
      EN: Why, then, should we go on living?”

2:14  LA: Et scidit vestimenta sua Mathathias, et filii ejus : et operuerunt se ciliciis, et planxerunt valde.
      EN: Mattathias and his sons tore their clothes, covered themselves with sackcloth, and mourned deeply.

2:15  LA: Et venerunt illuc qui missi erant a rege Antiocho, ut cogerent eos, qui confugerant in civitatem Modin, immolare, et accendere thura, et a lege Dei discedere.
      EN: Those sent by King Antiochus came there to compel those who had taken refuge in the city of Modein to sacrifice, burn incense, and depart from the law of God.

2:16  LA: Et multi de populo Israël consentientes accesserunt ad eos : sed Mathathias et filii ejus constanter steterunt.
      EN: Many of the people of Israel agreed and approached them, but Mattathias and his sons stood firm.

2:17  LA: Et respondentes qui missi erant ab Antiocho, dixerunt Mathathiæ : Princeps, et clarissimus et magnus es in hac civitate, et ornatus filiis et fratribus :
      EN: Those sent by Antiochus addressed Mattathias and said, “You are a leader, illustrious and great in this city, and honored with sons and brothers.

2:18  LA: ergo accede prior, et fac jussum regis, sicut fecerunt omnes gentes, et viri Juda, et qui remanserunt in Jerusalem : et eris tu, et filii tui, inter amicos regis, et amplificatus auro, et argento, et muneribus multis.
      EN: Therefore come forward first and carry out the king's command, as all the nations have done, and the men of Judah and those who remain in Jerusalem. You and your sons will be among the king's friends, and you will be enriched with gold, silver, and many gifts.”

2:19  LA: Et respondit Mathathias, et dixit magna voce : Etsi omnes gentes regi Antiocho obediunt, ut discedat unusquisque a servitute legis patrum suorum, et consentiat mandatis ejus :
      EN: Mattathias answered in a loud voice, “Even if all the nations obey King Antiochus, so that each departs from serving the law of his fathers and agrees to his commands,

2:20  LA: ego et filii mei, et fratres mei, obediemus legi patrum nostrorum :
      EN: I, my sons, and my brothers will obey the law of our fathers.

2:21  LA: propitius sit nobis Deus : non est nobis utile relinquere legem, et justitias Dei :
      EN: May God be gracious to us! It is not good for us to abandon the law and the righteous decrees of God.

2:22  LA: non audiemus verba regis Antiochi, nec sacrificabimus transgredientes legis nostræ mandata, ut eamus altera via.
      EN: We will not listen to the words of King Antiochus, nor will we sacrifice and transgress the commands of our law by going another way.”

2:23  LA: Et ut cessavit loqui verba hæc, accessit quidam Judæus in omnium oculis sacrificare idolis super aram in civitate Modin, secundum jussum regis :
      EN: As he finished speaking these words, a certain Jew came forward in the sight of everyone to sacrifice to idols upon the altar in the city of Modein, according to the king's command.

2:24  LA: et vidit Mathathias, et doluit, et contremuerunt renes ejus, et accensus est furor ejus secundum judicium legis, et insiliens trucidavit eum super aram :
      EN: Mattathias saw him and was grieved; his loins trembled, and his fury was kindled according to the judgment of the law. He rushed forward and slaughtered him upon the altar.

2:25  LA: sed et virum, quem rex Antiochus miserat, qui cogebat immolare, occidit in ipso tempore, et aram destruxit :
      EN: At that very time he also killed the man whom King Antiochus had sent to compel them to sacrifice, and he tore down the altar.

2:26  LA: et zelatus est legem, sicut fecit Phinees Zamri filio Salomi.
      EN: He was zealous for the law, as Phinehas had been toward Zimri son of Salu.

2:27  LA: Et exclamavit Mathathias voce magna in civitate, dicens : Omnis qui zelum habet legis, statuens testamentum, exeat post me.
      EN: Mattathias cried out in the city with a loud voice, saying, “Everyone who is zealous for the law and upholds the covenant, come out after me!”

2:28  LA: Et fugit ipse, et filii ejus in montes, et reliquerunt quæcumque habebant in civitate.
      EN: He and his sons fled to the mountains and left everything they had in the city.

2:29  LA: Tunc descenderunt multi quærentes judicium, et justitiam, in desertum :
      EN: Then many who sought justice and righteousness went down into the wilderness.

2:30  LA: et sederunt ibi ipsi, et filii eorum, et mulieres eorum, et pecora eorum : quoniam inundaverunt super eos mala.
      EN: They settled there with their sons, their wives, and their livestock, because evils had flooded over them.

2:31  LA: Et renuntiatum est viris regis, et exercitui qui erat in Jerusalem civitate David, quoniam discessissent viri quidam, qui dissipaverunt mandatum regis, in loca occulta in deserto, et abiissent post illos multi.
      EN: It was reported to the king's men and to the army in Jerusalem, the city of David, that certain men who had defied the king's command had gone away to hidden places in the wilderness, and that many had gone after them.

2:32  LA: Et statim perrexerunt ad eos, et constituerunt adversus eos prælium in die sabbatorum,
      EN: They immediately went after them and drew up for battle against them on the sabbath day.

2:33  LA: et dixerunt ad eos : Resistitis et nunc adhuc ? exite, et facite secundum verbum regis Antiochi, et vivetis.
      EN: They said to them, “Are you still resisting even now? Come out and act according to the word of King Antiochus, and you will live.”

2:34  LA: Et dixerunt : Non exibimus, neque faciemus verbum regis, ut polluamus diem sabbatorum.
      EN: They said, “We will not come out, nor will we carry out the king's command and profane the sabbath day.”

2:35  LA: Et concitaverunt adversus eos prælium.
      EN: Then they stirred up battle against them.

2:36  LA: Et non responderunt eis, nec lapidem miserunt in eos, nec oppilaverunt loca occulta,
      EN: They did not answer them, throw a stone at them, or block the hidden places,

2:37  LA: dicentes : Moriamur omnes in simplicitate nostra : et testes erunt super nos cælum et terra, quod injuste perditis nos.
      EN: saying, “Let us all die in our integrity. Heaven and earth will bear witness concerning us that you are destroying us unjustly.”

2:38  LA: Et intulerunt illis bellum sabbatis : et mortui sunt ipsi, et uxores eorum, et filii eorum, et pecora eorum usque ad mille animas hominum.
      EN: They attacked them on the sabbath, and they died with their wives, their children, and their livestock—up to a thousand human lives.

2:39  LA: Et cognovit Mathathias et amici ejus, et luctum habuerunt super eos valde.
      EN: Mattathias and his friends learned of it and mourned deeply for them.

2:40  LA: Et dixit vir proximo suo : Si omnes fecerimus sicut fratres nostri fecerunt, et non pugnaverimus adversus gentes pro animabus nostris et justificationibus nostris, nunc citius disperdent nos a terra.
      EN: One man said to another, “If we all do as our brothers did and do not fight against the nations for our lives and our ordinances, they will quickly destroy us from the earth.”

2:41  LA: Et cogitaverunt in die illa, dicentes : Omnis homo, quicumque venerit ad nos in bello die sabbatorum, pugnemus adversus eum : et non moriemur omnes, sicut mortui sunt fratres nostri in occultis.
      EN: On that day they decided, saying, “Whoever comes against us in battle on the sabbath day, let us fight against him. We will not all die as our brothers died in the hidden places.”

2:42  LA: Tunc congregata est ad eos synagoga Assidæorum fortis viribus ex Israël, omnis voluntarius in lege :
      EN: Then an assembly of Hasideans joined them, mighty in strength from Israel, every one willingly devoted to the law.

2:43  LA: et omnes, qui fugiebant a malis, additi sunt ad eos, et facti sunt illis ad firmamentum.
      EN: All who were fleeing from the evils were added to them and became a support for them.

2:44  LA: Et collegerunt exercitum, et percusserunt peccatores in ira sua, et viros iniquos in indignatione sua : et ceteri fugerunt ad nationes, ut evaderent.
      EN: They gathered an army and struck down sinners in their anger and lawless men in their indignation, while the rest fled to the nations to escape.

7:37  LA: Tu, Domine, elegisti domum istam ad invocandum nomen tuum in ea, ut esset domus orationis et obsecrationis populo tuo :

      EN: Judas encamped at Adasa with three thousand men. Judas prayed and said,

7:41  LA: Qui missi erant a rege Sennacherib, Domine, quia blasphemaverunt te, exiit angelus, et percussit ex eis centum octoginta quinque millia :

      EN: The armies joined battle on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar. Nicanor's forces were crushed, and he himself fell first in the battle.

7:44  LA: Ut autem vidit exercitus ejus quia cecidisset Nicanor, projecerunt arma sua, et fugerunt :
      EN: When his army saw that Nicanor had fallen, they threw down their weapons and fled.

7:45  LA: et persecuti sunt eos viam unius diei ab Adazer usquequo veniatur in Gazara, et tubis cecinerunt post eos cum significationibus : · Translation note. Adazer is retained here, while Adarsa in 7:40 is given the familiar form Adasa. The precise relation between the source’s place-name forms is not decided in the translation.
      EN: They pursued them a day's journey from Adazer as far as Gazara, sounding signals on trumpets behind them.

7:46  LA: et exierunt de omnibus castellis Judææ in circuitu, et ventilabant eos cornibus, et convertebantur iterum ad eos, et ceciderunt omnes gladio, et non est relictus ex eis nec unus. · Translation note. Ventilabant eos cornibus literally presents a tossing or driving with horns. The image is kept. Cornibus may evoke military wings as well as the animal image; the verse’s surrounding pursuit gives the practical setting.
      EN: People came out from all the surrounding villages of Judea and tossed them with their horns, turning them back toward their pursuers. They all fell by the sword, and not even one of them was left.

7:47  LA: Et acceperunt spolia eorum in prædam : et caput Nicanoris amputaverunt, et dexteram ejus, quam extenderat superbe, et attulerunt, et suspenderunt contra Jerusalem.

7:48  LA: Et lætatus est populus valde, et egerunt diem illam in lætitia magna.
      EN: The people rejoiced greatly and spent that day in great gladness.

7:49  LA: Et constituit agi omnibus annis diem istam tertiadecima die mensis Adar.
      EN: He decreed that this day should be observed every year, on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar.

      EN: The land of Judah was quiet for a few days.

1 MACCABEES 8

8:1  LA: Et audivit Judas nomen Romanorum, quia sunt potentes viribus, et acquiescunt ad omnia quæ postulantur ab eis, et quicumque accesserunt ad eos, statuerunt cum eis amicitias : et quia sunt potentes viribus.
     EN: Judas heard the reputation of the Romans: that they were mighty in strength, consented to all that was asked of them, and established friendship with all who approached them; and that they were mighty in strength.

8:2  LA: Et audierunt prælia eorum, et virtutes bonas, quas fecerunt in Galatia, quia obtinuerunt eos, et duxerunt sub tributum :
     EN: They heard of their battles and the brave deeds they had done in Galatia, how they had conquered them and brought them under tribute;

8:3  LA: et quanta fecerunt in regione Hispaniæ, et quod in potestatem redegerunt metalla argenti et auri, quæ illic sunt, et possederunt omnem locum consilio suo, et patientia :
     EN: what great things they had done in the region of Spain, how they had brought its silver and gold mines under their power and had gained possession of the whole place through their planning and perseverance;

8:4  LA: locaque quæ longe erant valde ab eis, et reges, qui supervenerant eis ab extremis terræ, contriverunt, et percusserunt eos plaga magna : ceteri autem dant eis tributum omnibus annis.
     EN: and how they had crushed places very far from them and kings who had come against them from the ends of the earth, striking them with a great blow, while the others paid them tribute every year.

8:5  LA: Et Philippum et Persen Ceteorum regem, et ceteros qui adversum eos arma tulerant, contriverunt in bello, et obtinuerunt eos : · Translation note. Ceteorum is rendered of the Kittim as a conventional biblical designation in this setting, rather than replaced by the historical identification Macedonians. The underlying Latin form remains visible for review.
     EN: They had crushed Philip, Perseus king of the Kittim, and the others who had taken up arms against them in war, and had conquered them.

8:6  LA: et Antiochum magnum regem Asiæ, qui eis pugnam intulerat habens centum viginti elephantos, et equitatum, et currus, et exercitum magnum valde, contritum ab eis :
     EN: Antiochus the Great, king of Asia, who had made war upon them with a hundred twenty elephants, cavalry, chariots, and an exceedingly great army, had been crushed by them.

8:7  LA: et quia ceperunt eum vivum, et statuerunt ei ut daret ipse, et qui regnarent post ipsum, tributum magnum, et daret obsides, et constitutum,
     EN: They had captured him alive and imposed upon him and those who would reign after him the payment of great tribute, the giving of hostages and a fixed payment,

8:8  LA: et regionem Indorum, et Medos, et Lydos, de optimis regionibus eorum : et acceptas eas ab eis, dederunt Eumeni regi, · Translation note. The source names the Indians, Medes, and Lydians. These names are retained without historical or geographical emendation.
     EN: and the surrender of the region of the Indians, the Medes, and the Lydians, among their best regions. Having received these from them, they gave them to King Eumenes.

8:9  LA: et quia qui erant apud Helladam, voluerunt ire, et tollere eos : et innotuit sermo his,
     EN: Those in Hellas had wanted to go and destroy them, but the matter became known to the Romans.

8:10  LA: et miserunt ad eos ducem unum, et pugnaverunt contra illos, et ceciderunt ex eis multi, et captivas duxerunt uxores eorum et filios, et diripuerunt eos, et terram eorum possederunt, et destruxerunt muros eorum, et in servitutem illos redegerunt usque in hunc diem :
      EN: They sent a single commander against them and fought against them. Many of them fell; they took their wives and children captive, plundered them, possessed their land, tore down their walls, and reduced them to slavery to this day.

8:11  LA: et residua regna, et insulas, quæ aliquando restiterant illis, exterminaverunt, et in potestatem redegerunt.
      EN: They had devastated the remaining kingdoms and islands that had ever resisted them and brought them under their power.

8:12  LA: Cum amicis autem suis, et qui in ipsis requiem habebant, conservaverunt amicitiam, et obtinuerunt regna, quæ erant proxima, et quæ erant longe : quia quicumque audiebant nomen eorum, timebant eos :
      EN: But with their friends and those who found security in them, they maintained friendship. They conquered kingdoms near and far, for all who heard their name feared them.

8:13  LA: quibus vero vellent auxilio esse ut regnarent, regnabant : quos autem vellent, regno deturbabant : et exaltati sunt valde.
      EN: Those whom they wished to help become kings became kings, and those whom they wished they cast down from kingship. They were greatly exalted.

8:14  LA: Et in omnibus istis nemo portabat diadema, nec induebatur purpura, ut magnificaretur in ea.
      EN: Yet in all this no one wore a diadem or clothed himself in purple to be magnified by it.

8:15  LA: Et quia curiam fecerunt sibi, et quotidie consulebant trecentos viginti consilium agentes semper de multitudine, ut quæ digna sunt, gerant :
      EN: They had established a senate for themselves, and every day they consulted three hundred twenty men who were always taking counsel for the multitude, to carry out worthy measures.

8:16  LA: et committunt uni homini magistratum suum per singulos annos dominari universæ terræ suæ, et omnes obediunt uni, et non est invidia, neque zelus inter eos. · Translation note. The account explicitly speaks of one magistrate each year and universal obedience without rivalry. It is translated as the narrator’s account, not recast as a corrected description of Roman constitutional practice.
      EN: Each year they entrusted their governing authority to one man to rule their whole land. Everyone obeyed this one man, and there was no envy or jealousy among them.

8:17  LA: Et elegit Judas Eupolemum filium Joannis filii Jacob, et Jasonem filium Eleazari, et misit eos Romam constituere cum illis amicitiam et societatem :
      EN: Judas chose Eupolemus son of John, son of Jacob, and Jason son of Eleazar, and sent them to Rome to establish friendship and alliance with them,

8:18  LA: et ut auferrent ab eis jugum Græcorum, quia viderunt quod in servitutem premerent regnum Israël.

8:19  LA: Et abierunt Romam viam multam valde, et introierunt curiam, et dixerunt :
      EN: They traveled a very long journey to Rome, entered the senate, and said,

8:20  LA: Judas Machabæus, et fratres ejus, et populus Judæorum, miserunt nos ad vos statuere vobiscum societatem et pacem, et conscribere nos socios et amicos vestros.

8:21  LA: Et placuit sermo in conspectu eorum.
      EN: The proposal pleased them.

8:22  LA: Et hoc rescriptum est quod rescripserunt in tabulis æreis, et miserunt in Jerusalem, ut esset apud eos ibi memoriale pacis et societatis :
      EN: This is the reply they inscribed on bronze tablets and sent to Jerusalem, to remain with them there as a memorial of peace and alliance:

8:23  LA: Bene sit Romanis, et genti Judæorum, in mari et in terra in æternum : gladiusque et hostis procul sit ab eis.

8:24  LA: Quod si institerit bellum Romanis prius, aut omnibus sociis eorum in omni dominatione eorum,

8:25  LA: auxilium feret gens Judæorum, prout tempus dictaverit, corde pleno :

8:27  LA: Similiter autem et si genti Judæorum prius acciderit bellum, adjuvabunt Romani ex animo, prout eis tempus permiserit :

8:28  LA: et adjuvantibus non dabitur triticum, arma, pecunia, naves, sicut placuit Romanis : et custodient mandata eorum absque dolo : · Translation note. Adjuvantibus means those giving aid. The English follows that reading even though it makes the treaty clause difficult; it is not changed to those fighting against them to match the preceding clause or another tradition.
      EN: Those giving aid shall not be given grain, arms, money, or ships, as has seemed good to the Romans, and they shall keep their obligations without deceit.

The reflexive suum in an indirect request can make the ownership of the equipment uncertain. A familiar account in which baggage is entrusted for safekeeping has not been silently substituted.
      EN: Jonathan sent his brother, a leader of the people, and asked the Nabateans, his friends, to lend them their equipment, which was plentiful.

       EN: Athenobius, the king's friend, came to Jerusalem and saw Simon's glory, his splendor in gold and silver, and his abundant furnishings. He was astonished and reported the king's words to him.

15:33  LA: Et respondit ei Simon, et dixit ei : Neque alienam terram sumpsimus, neque aliena detinemus : sed hæreditatem patrum nostrorum, quæ injuste ab inimicis nostris aliquo tempore possessa est.
       EN: Simon answered him, saying, “We have neither taken another people's land nor held another people's possessions, but the inheritance of our fathers, which our enemies unjustly possessed for a time.

15:34  LA: Nos vero tempus habentes, vindicamus hæreditatem patrum nostrorum.
       EN: Now that we have the opportunity, we are reclaiming the inheritance of our fathers.

15:35  LA: Nam de Joppe et Gazara quæ expostulas, ipsi faciebant in populo plagam magnam, et in regione nostra : horum damus talenta centum. Et non respondit ei Athenobius verbum.
       EN: As for Joppa and Gazara, which you demand, they inflicted a great blow upon the people and our region. For these we offer a hundred talents.” Athenobius did not answer him a word.

15:36  LA: Reversus autem cum ira ad regem, renuntiavit ei verba ista, et gloriam Simonis, et universa quæ vidit, et iratus est rex ira magna.
       EN: Returning to the king in anger, he reported these words, Simon's glory, and everything he had seen, and the king became greatly enraged.

15:37  LA: Tryphon autem fugit navi in Orthosiada. · Translation note. Orthosiada is rendered by the conventional place-name Orthosias. This is spelling normalization of the same named place, not a substitution of another location.
       EN: But Trypho fled by ship to Orthosias.

15:38  LA: Et constituit rex Cendebæum ducem maritimum, et exercitum peditum et equitum dedit illi.
       EN: The king appointed Cendebaeus commander of the coast and gave him an army of infantry and cavalry.

15:39  LA: Et mandavit illi movere castra contra faciem Judææ : et mandavit ei ædificare Gedorem, et obstruere portas civitatis, et debellare populum. Rex autem persequebatur Typhonem. · Translation note. The source has Gedorem here and at 15:40, but Cedronem at 16:9. Gedor and Cedron are retained as different source forms; no silent name harmonization is made. Typhonem at the end of 15:39 is treated as a spelling variant of the same Trypho named in adjacent verses.
       EN: He commanded him to advance his camp toward Judea, to build Gedor, to block the city gates, and to wage war against the people. Meanwhile, the king pursued Trypho.

15:40  LA: Et pervenit Cendebæus Jamniam, et cœpit irritare plebem, et conculcare Judæam, et captivare populum, et interficere, et ædificare Gedorem.
       EN: Cendebaeus came to Jamnia and began to provoke the people, trample Judea, take people captive and kill them, and build Gedor.

15:41  LA: Et collocavit illic equites et exercitum, ut egressi perambularent viam Judææ, sicut constituit ei rex.
       EN: He stationed cavalry and an army there, so that they might go out and patrol the road to Judea, as the king had ordered him.

1 MACCABEES 16

16:1  LA: Et ascendit Joannes de Gazaris, et nuntiavit Simoni patri suo quæ fecit Cendebæus in populo ipsorum.
      EN: John went up from Gazara and reported to his father Simon what Cendebaeus had done among their people.

16:2  LA: Et vocavit Simon duos filios seniores, Judam et Joannem, et ait illis : Ego, et fratres mei, et domus patris mei expugnavimus hostes Israël ab adolescentia usque in hunc diem : et prosperatum est in manibus nostris liberare Israël aliquoties.
      EN: Simon summoned his two older sons, Judas and John, and said to them, “I, my brothers, and my father's house have fought against Israel's enemies from youth until this day, and we have succeeded in delivering Israel on several occasions.

16:3  LA: Nunc autem senui : sed estote loco meo, et fratres mei, et egressi pugnate pro gente nostra : auxilium vero de cælo vobiscum sit. · Translation note. The Latin reads et fratres mei. Fratres can be vocative or nominative. The running text adopts the vocative, my brothers, as Simon addresses his sons in their shared role of fighting for the nation. The predicate reading be my brothers is also grammatically possible. The familiar wording take my place and that of my brothers would require treating the construction as irregular or reading a genitive; it is not silently substituted. The clause remains a priority for specialist review.
      EN: But now I have grown old. Stand in my place, my brothers, and go out and fight for our nation. May help from heaven be with you.”

16:4  LA: Et elegit de regione viginti millia virorum belligeratorum, et equites : et profecti sunt ad Cendebæum, et dormierunt in Modin. · Translation note. The number twenty thousand qualifies fighting men; et equites adds horsemen without stating their number. The English keeps that distinction. The subject of elegit, he selected, is not explicitly named.
      EN: He selected twenty thousand fighting men from the region, as well as horsemen. They set out against Cendebaeus and spent the night in Modein.

16:5  LA: Et surrexerunt mane, et abierunt in campum : et ecce exercitus copiosus in obviam illis peditum et equitum : et fluvius torrens erat inter medium ipsorum.
      EN: They rose in the morning and went into the plain, and there was a large army of infantry and cavalry coming to meet them. A rushing stream lay between them.

16:6  LA: Et admovit castra contra faciem eorum ipse et populus ejus, et vidit populum trepidantem ad transfretandum torrentem : et transfretavit primus, et viderunt eum viri, et transierunt post eum. · Translation note. The leading individual is not named in the Latin verse. He is retained rather than silently assigning the act to one brother.
      EN: He and his people brought their camp opposite them, and he saw that the people were afraid to cross the stream. He crossed first, and when the men saw him, they crossed after him.

16:7  LA: Et divisit populum et equites in medio peditum : erat autem equitatus adversariorum copiosus nimis.
      EN: He divided the people and placed the cavalry among the infantry, for the adversaries' cavalry was exceedingly numerous.

16:8  LA: Et exclamaverunt sacris tubis, et in fugam conversus est Cendebæus et castra ejus : et ceciderunt ex eis multi vulnerati : residui autem in munitionem fugerunt.
      EN: They sounded the sacred trumpets, and Cendebaeus and his army were put to flight. Many of them fell wounded, and the rest fled into the stronghold.

16:9  LA: Tunc vulneratus est Judas frater Joannis : Joannes autem insecutus est eos, donec venit Cedronem, quam ædificavit : · Translation note. Cedron is retained against Gedor in 15:39–40. The subject of quam ædificavit, which he had built, is not explicitly restated. The narrative context can suggest Cendebaeus, but the immediately preceding subject is John. The running text preserves the pronoun and the uncertainty rather than silently naming the builder.
      EN: Then Judas, John's brother, was wounded. John pursued them until he came to Cedron, which he had built.

16:10  LA: et fugerunt usque ad turres, quæ erant in agris Azoti, et succendit eas igni. Et ceciderunt ex illis duo millia virorum, et reversus est in Judæam in pace.
       EN: They fled as far as the towers in the fields of Azotus, and he set them on fire. Two thousand of their men fell, and he returned to Judea in peace.

16:11  LA: Et Ptolemæus filius Abobi constitutus erat dux in campo Jericho, et habebat argentum et aurum multum :
       EN: Ptolemy son of Abubus had been appointed commander in the plain of Jericho, and he had much silver and gold,

16:12  LA: erat enim gener summi sacerdotis.
       EN: for he was the high priest's son-in-law.

16:13  LA: Et exaltatum est cor ejus, et volebat obtinere regionem, et cogitabat dolum adversus Simonem et filios ejus, ut tolleret eos.
       EN: His heart became proud, and he wanted to take possession of the region. He plotted deceit against Simon and his sons to do away with them.

16:14  LA: Simon autem, perambulans civitates quæ erant in regione Judææ, et sollicitudinem gerens earum, descendit in Jericho ipse, et Mathathias filius ejus, et Judas, anno centesimo septuagesimo septimo, mense undecimo : hic est mensis Sabath. · Translation note. The date is preserved in the source era without assigning a modern equivalent. Sabath is rendered Shebat, the conventional name of the eleventh month, rather than Sabbath.
       EN: Simon was visiting the cities in the region of Judea and attending to their needs. He went down to Jericho with his sons Mattathias and Judas in the one hundred seventy-seventh year, in the eleventh month, which is the month of Shebat.

16:15  LA: Et suscepit eos filius Abobi in munitiunculam, quæ vocatur Doch, cum dolo, quam ædificavit : et fecit eis convivium magnum, et abscondit illic viros.
       EN: The son of Abubus deceitfully received them in the small fortress called Doch, which he had built. He prepared a great banquet for them and concealed men there.

16:16  LA: Et cum inebriatus esset Simon et filii ejus, surrexit Ptolemæus cum suis, et sumpserunt arma sua, et intraverunt in convivium : et occiderunt eum, et duos filios ejus, et quosdam pueros ejus :
       EN: When Simon and his sons had become drunk, Ptolemy arose with his men. They took their weapons, entered the banquet, and killed him, his two sons, and some of his servants.

16:17  LA: et fecit deceptionem magnam in Israël, et reddidit mala pro bonis.
       EN: He committed a great treachery in Israel and repaid good with evil.

16:18  LA: Et scripsit hæc Ptolemæus, et misit regi ut mitteret ei exercitum in auxilium, et traderet ei regionem, et civitates eorum, et tributa. · Translation note. The Latin coordination ut mitteret ei … et traderet ei naturally allows the king to govern both verbs: he is asked to send help and grant the region, cities, and tribute to Ptolemy. That construal is translated here. The alternative involving Ptolemy offering these to the king would require an unexpressed subject shift; it is not silently substituted from another source tradition.
       EN: Ptolemy wrote these things and sent them to the king, asking him to send an army to help him and to hand over to him the region, their cities, and the tribute.

16:19  LA: Et misit alios in Gazaram tollere Joannem : et tribunis misit epistolas, ut venirent ad se, et daret eis argentum, et aurum, et dona. · Translation note. Tribunis is rendered military commanders. The Latin title does not establish the formal organization or size of a Roman legionary unit in this setting.
       EN: He sent others to Gazara to do away with John, and sent letters to the military commanders, asking them to come to him so that he might give them silver, gold, and gifts.

16:20  LA: Et alios misit occupare Jerusalem et montem templi.
       EN: He sent others to seize Jerusalem and the temple mount.

16:21  LA: Et præcurrens quidam, nuntiavit Joanni in Gazara quia periit pater ejus et fratres ejus, et quia misit te quoque interfici. · Translation note. The Latin moves from indirect report to te, you. The English marks the resulting direct address with quotation marks.
       EN: But someone ran ahead and reported to John in Gazara that his father and brothers had perished, and said, “He has sent men to kill you too.”

16:22  LA: Ut audivit autem, vehementer expavit : et comprehendit viros, qui venerant perdere eum, et occidit eos : cognovit enim quia quærebant eum perdere.
       EN: When he heard this, he was greatly alarmed. He seized the men who had come to destroy him and killed them, for he knew that they were seeking to destroy him.

16:23  LA: Et cetera sermonum Joannis, et bellorum ejus, et bonarum virtutum, quibus fortiter gessit, et ædificii murorum, quos exstruxit, et rerum gestarum ejus :
       EN: The rest of John's deeds, his wars, the noble exploits in which he acted courageously, the building of the walls he erected, and his achievements—

16:24  LA: ecce hæc scripta sunt in libro dierum sacerdotii ejus, ex quo factus est princeps sacerdotum post patrem suum. · Translation note. The book of the days of his priesthood is retained as the text’s own archival reference; no claim is made that such a book has survived or was consulted.
       EN: see, these are written in the book of the days of his priesthood, from the time he became high priest after his father.
