## Text

**22:1** A psalm of David. The Lord rules me, and nothing will be lacking to me;

**22:2** in a place of pasture, there He has settled me. Beside the water of refreshment He has nourished me;

**22:3** He has restored my soul. He has led me along paths of righteousness for the sake of His name.

**22:4** For even if I walk in the midst of the shadow of death, I will not fear evils, because You are with me. Your rod and Your staff, they themselves have comforted me.

**22:5** You have prepared a table in my sight against those who afflict me; You have richly anointed my head with oil, and my intoxicating cup, how splendid it is!

**22:6** And Your mercy will follow closely after me all the days of my life, and so that I may dwell in the house of the Lord for length of days.

## Translator Notes

- **22:1** — The Gallican Psalter reads Dominus regit me ("The Lord rules/guides me") rather than "is my shepherd."
- **22:2** — Latin educavit ("brought up / nourished / led forth") and collocavit ("placed / settled") are perfect-tense verbs throughout vv. 2-3.
- **22:3** — Latin convertit literally means "turned back" or "restored."
- **22:4** — Latin in medio umbræ mortis reads "in the midst of the shadow of death" with no word for "valley," and mala is plural ("evils").
- **22:5** — Latin impinguasti literally means "You have made fat / enriched" with oil; calix meus inebrians ("my intoxicating cup," following LXX methyskon) differs from Hebrew "my cup overflows."
- **22:6** — Latin et ut inhabitem uses a subjunctive purpose/result clause ("and so that I may dwell") following LXX, rather than indicative "and I will dwell."
