## Text

**7:53** And each one returned to his own house.

**8:1** But Jesus proceeded to the Mount of Olives.

**8:2** And at daybreak He came again into the temple, and all the people came to Him; and sitting down, He was teaching them.

**8:3** Then the scribes and the Pharisees bring a woman caught in adultery, and they stood her in the middle,

**8:4** and said to Him, "Teacher, this woman has just now been caught in adultery.

**8:5** Now in the Law Moses commanded us to stone such women. What therefore do You say?"

**8:6** Yet they were saying this to test Him, so that they might be able to accuse Him. But Jesus, stooping downward, was writing with His finger on the ground.

**8:7** So when they persisted in questioning Him, He stood upright and said to them, "Let the one among you who is without sin cast a stone at her first."

**8:8** And stooping down again, He was writing on the ground.

**8:9** Then hearing this, they went out one after another, beginning with the elders; and Jesus remained alone, with the woman standing in the middle.

**8:10** Then raising Himself up, Jesus said to her, "Woman, where are those who were accusing you? Has no one condemned you?"

**8:11** She said, "No one, Lord." Then Jesus said, "Neither will I condemn you; go, and from now on sin no more."

## Translator Notes

- **7:53** — Unlike the Saints Greek Critical Text, the Vulgate includes 7:53-8:11 without double text-critical brackets.
- **8:9** — The Vulgate reads simply Audientes autem ("Then hearing this") without the expansion "convicted by their conscience."
- **8:10** — The Vulgate reads qui te accusabant ("those who were accusing you"), whereas shorter Greek manuscripts read simply "where are they?"
- **8:11** — Latin condemnabo is future tense ("Neither will I condemn you"), differing from Greek present-tense katakrino ("Neither do I condemn you").
