SIRACH 1

1:1  LA: Omnis sapientia a Domino Deo est : et cum illo fuit semper, et est ante ævum.
     EN: All wisdom is from the Lord God; she has always been with Him and is before the age.

1:2  LA: Arenam maris, et pluviæ guttas, et dies sæculi, quis dinumeravit ? altitudinem cæli, et latitudinem terræ, et profundum abyssi, quis dimensus est ?
     EN: Who has counted the sand of the sea, the drops of rain, and the days of the age? Who has measured the height of heaven, the breadth of the earth, and the depth of the abyss?

1:3  LA: sapientiam Dei præcedentem omnia, quis investigavit ?
     EN: Who has searched out the wisdom of God that precedes all things?

1:4  LA: Prior omnium creata est sapientia, et intellectus prudentiæ ab ævo.
     EN: Wisdom was created before all things, and the understanding of prudence from of old.

1:6  LA: Radix sapientiæ cui revelata est ? et astutias illius quis agnovit ?
     EN: To whom has the root of wisdom been revealed? Who has come to know her subtle insights?

1:8  LA: Unus est altissimus, Creator omnipotens, et rex potens et metuendus nimis, sedens super thronum illius, et dominans Deus. · Translation note. The Latin illius does not specify gender. “His throne” takes God as its referent in late Latin usage; “her throne,” referring to wisdom, is another possible construal. The source is not altered.
     EN: One is the Most High, the almighty Creator, a powerful King greatly to be feared, seated upon His throne: God who rules.

1:9  LA: Ipse creavit illam in Spiritu Sancto, et vidit, et dinumeravit, et mensus est : · Translation note. The source explicitly reads in Spiritu Sancto. “In the Holy Spirit” preserves its presentation; the translation does not claim that this wording by itself establishes the full content of later Trinitarian doctrine.
     EN: He created her in the Holy Spirit; He saw her, counted her, and measured her.

1:10  LA: et effudit illam super omnia opera sua, et super omnem carnem, secundum datum suum, et præbuit illam diligentibus se.]
      EN: He poured her out upon all His works and upon all flesh according to His gift, and bestowed her upon those who love Him.

1:11  LA: [Timor Domini gloria, et gloriatio, et lætitia, et corona exsultationis.
      EN: The fear of the Lord is glory, boasting, gladness, and a crown of exultation.

1:12  LA: Timor Domini delectabit cor, et dabit lætitiam, et gaudium, et longitudinem dierum.
      EN: The fear of the Lord will delight the heart and give gladness, joy, and length of days.

1:13  LA: Timenti Dominum bene erit in extremis, et in die defunctionis suæ benedicetur.
      EN: It will be well at the end with the one who fears the Lord, and he will be blessed on the day of his death.

1:14  LA: Initium sapientiæ timor Domini : et cum fidelibus in vulva concreatus est : cum electis feminis graditur, et cum justis et fidelibus agnoscitur. · Translation note. The masculine concreatus is construed with timor, “fear,” rather than silently changed to a feminine adjective agreeing with wisdom. The wording about chosen women is retained.
      EN: The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord; it was created together with the faithful in the womb. It walks with chosen women and is recognized among the righteous and faithful.

1:15  LA: No aligned Latin source passage.
      EN: No corresponding passage is supplied in this Latin text.

1:16  LA: Plenitudo sapientiæ est timere Deum, et plenitudo a fructibus illius.
      EN: The fullness of wisdom is to fear God, and fullness comes from her fruits.

1:17  LA: Omnem domum illius implebit a generationibus, et receptacula a thesauris illius.
      EN: She will fill his whole house with her offspring, and his storehouses with her treasures.

1:18  LA: Corona sapientiæ timor Domini, replens pacem et salutis fructum :
      EN: The crown of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, filling up peace and the fruit of salvation.

1:19  LA: et vidit, et dinumeravit eam : utraque autem sunt dona Dei.
      EN: He saw her and counted her; both are gifts of God.

1:20  LA: Radix sapientiæ est timere Dominum, et rami illius longævi.
      EN: The root of wisdom is to fear the Lord, and her branches are long-lived.

1:22  LA: nam qui sine timore est non poterit justificari : iracundia enim animositatis illius subversio illius est.
      EN: For the one who is without fear cannot be made righteous: the anger of his fierce spirit is his downfall.

1:23  LA: Usque in tempus sustinebit patiens, et postea redditio jucunditatis.
      EN: A patient person will endure until the proper time, and afterward delight will be restored.

1:24  LA: Bonus sensus usque in tempus abscondet verba illius, et labia multorum enarrabunt sensum illius.
      EN: Good sense will conceal his words until the proper time, and the lips of many will recount his understanding.

1:25  LA: In thesauris sapientiæ significatio disciplinæ :
      EN: In the treasures of wisdom is the expression of instruction,

1:26  LA: Fili, concupiscens sapientiam, conserva justitiam, et Deus præbebit illam tibi.
      EN: Son, if you desire wisdom, keep righteousness, and God will bestow her upon you.

1:27  LA: Sapientia enim et disciplina timor Domini : et quod beneplacitum est illi,
      EN: For wisdom and instruction are the fear of the Lord; and what pleases Him

1:28  LA: Ne sis incredibilis timori Domini, et ne accesseris ad illum duplici corde.
      EN: Do not be distrustful toward the fear of the Lord, and do not approach Him with a divided heart.

1:29  LA: Ne fueris hypocrita in conspectu hominum, et non scandalizeris in labiis tuis.
      EN: Do not be a hypocrite in the sight of people, and do not stumble through your lips.

1:30  LA: Attende in illis, ne forte cadas, et adducas animæ tuæ inhonorationem :
      EN: Pay attention to them, lest you fall and bring dishonor upon your soul,

SIRACH 2

2:1  LA: Fili , accedens ad servitutem Dei sta in justitia et timore, et præpara animam tuam ad tentationem.
     EN: Son, when you come to serve God, stand in righteousness and fear, and prepare your soul for testing.

2:2  LA: Deprime cor tuum, et sustine : inclina aurem tuam, et suscipe verba intellectus : et ne festines in tempore obductionis.
     EN: Humble your heart and endure. Incline your ear and receive words of understanding, and do not act hastily in a time of oppression.

2:3  LA: Sustine sustentationes Dei : conjungere Deo, et sustine, ut crescat in novissimo vita tua.
     EN: Wait for God's support; cling to God and endure, so that your life may flourish at the end.

2:4  LA: Omne quod tibi applicitum fuerit accipe : et in dolore sustine, et in humilitate tua patientiam habe :
     EN: Accept whatever is brought upon you; endure in pain and be patient in your humiliation.

2:5  LA: quoniam in igne probatur aurum et argentum, homines vero receptibiles in camino humiliationis.
     EN: For gold and silver are tested in fire, but acceptable people in the furnace of humiliation.

2:6  LA: Crede Deo, et recuperabit te : et dirige viam tuam, et spera in illum : serva timorem illius, et in illo veterasce.]
     EN: Trust God, and He will restore you. Direct your way and hope in Him; keep the fear of Him and grow old in Him.

2:7  LA: [Metuentes Dominum, sustinete misericordiam ejus : et non deflectatis ab illo, ne cadatis.
     EN: You who fear the Lord, wait for His mercy, and do not turn aside from Him, lest you fall.

2:8  LA: Qui timetis Dominum, credite illi, et non evacuabitur merces vestra.
     EN: You who fear the Lord, trust Him, and your reward will not be made void.

2:9  LA: Qui timetis Dominum, sperate in illum, et in oblectationem veniet vobis misericordia.
     EN: You who fear the Lord, hope in Him, and mercy will come to you for your delight.

2:10  LA: Respicite, filii, nationes hominum : et scitote quia nullus speravit in Domino et confusus est.
      EN: Look upon the nations of humankind, children, and know that no one has hoped in the Lord and been put to shame.

2:11  LA: Quoniam pius et misericors est Deus, et remittet in die tribulationis peccata, et protector est omnibus exquirentibus se in veritate.]
      EN: For God is kind and merciful; He will forgive sins in the day of affliction, and He protects all who seek Him in truth.

2:12  LA: [Væ duplici corde, et labiis scelestis, et manibus malefacientibus, et peccatori terram ingredienti duabus viis !
      EN: Woe to the divided heart, to wicked lips, to hands that do evil, and to the sinner who walks the earth by two paths!

2:13  LA: Væ dissolutis corde, qui non credunt Deo, et ideo non protegentur ab eo !
      EN: Woe to those whose hearts have given way, who do not trust God and therefore will not be protected by Him!

2:14  LA: Væ his qui perdiderunt sustinentiam, et qui dereliquerunt vias rectas, et diverterunt in vias pravas !
      EN: Woe to those who have lost endurance, who have abandoned straight ways and turned aside into crooked ways!

2:15  LA: Qui timent Dominum non erunt incredibiles verbo illius : et qui diligunt illum conservabunt viam illius.
      EN: Those who fear the Lord will not distrust His word, and those who love Him will keep His way.

2:16  LA: Qui timent Dominum inquirent quæ beneplacita sunt ei, et qui diligunt eum replebuntur lege ipsius.
      EN: Those who fear the Lord will seek what pleases Him, and those who love Him will be filled with His law.

2:17  LA: Qui timent Dominum præparabunt corda sua, et in conspectu illius sanctificabunt animas suas.
      EN: Those who fear the Lord will prepare their hearts and sanctify their souls in His sight.

2:18  LA: dicentes : Si pœnitentiam non egerimus, incidemus in manus Domini, et non in manus hominum. · Translation note. The conditional “If we do not repent” is present in this Latin source and retained. The line is not harmonized to a different Greek or familiar English formulation.
      EN: saying, “If we do not repent, we will fall into the hands of the Lord, and not into the hands of people.

SIRACH 3

3:1  LA: Judicium patris audite, filii, et sic facite, ut salvi sitis.
     EN: Hear a father's judgment, children, and act accordingly, so that you may be saved.

3:2  LA: Deus enim honoravit patrem in filiis : et judicium matris exquirens, firmavit in filios.
     EN: For God has honored the father in his children, and, upholding the mother's judgment, He has established it over her children.

3:3  LA: Qui diligit Deum exorabit pro peccatis, et continebit se ab illis, et in oratione dierum exaudietur.
     EN: The one who loves God will pray for forgiveness of sins and restrain himself from them, and he will be heard in his daily prayer.

3:4  LA: Et sicut qui thesaurizat, ita et qui honorificat matrem suam.
     EN: The one who honors his mother is like someone storing up treasure.

3:5  LA: Qui honorat patrem suum jucundabitur in filiis, et in die orationis suæ exaudietur.
     EN: The one who honors his father will rejoice in his children, and he will be heard on the day of his prayer.

3:6  LA: Qui honorat patrem suum vita vivet longiore, et qui obedit patri refrigerabit matrem.
     EN: The one who honors his father will live a longer life, and the one who obeys his father will give relief to his mother.

3:7  LA: Qui timet Dominum honorat parentes, et quasi dominis serviet his qui se genuerunt.
     EN: The one who fears the Lord honors his parents and will serve those who begot him as masters.

3:8  LA: In opere, et sermone, et omni patientia, honora patrem tuum,
     EN: In deed and word and with all patience, honor your father,

3:9  LA: Benedictio patris firmat domos filiorum : maledictio autem matris eradicat fundamenta.
     EN: A father's blessing establishes the houses of his children, but a mother's curse uproots their foundations.

3:10  LA: Ne glorieris in contumelia patris tui : non enim est tibi gloria ejus confusio.
      EN: Do not glory in your father's disgrace, for his shame is no glory to you.

3:11  LA: Gloria enim hominis ex honore patris sui, et dedecus filii pater sine honore.
      EN: A person's glory comes from the honor of his father, and a father without honor is a son's disgrace.

3:12  LA: Fili, suscipe senectam patris tui, et non contristes eum in vita illius :
      EN: Son, support your father in his old age, and do not grieve him during his life.

3:13  LA: et si defecerit sensu, veniam da, et ne spernas eum in virtute tua : eleemosyna enim patris non erit in oblivione.
      EN: Even if his understanding fails, be forbearing, and do not despise him in your strength, for kindness to a father will not be forgotten.

3:14  LA: Nam pro peccato matris restituetur tibi bonum : · Translation note. The Latin says pro peccato matris, “in place of/for the mother’s sin.” This unexpected wording is retained rather than replaced from another textual tradition.
      EN: For good will be repaid to you in place of your mother's sin.

3:15  LA: et in justitia ædificabitur tibi, et in die tribulationis commemorabitur tui, et sicut in sereno glacies, solventur peccata tua.
      EN: In righteousness it will be built up for you, and in the day of affliction you will be remembered; your sins will dissolve like ice in clear weather.

3:16  LA: Quam malæ famæ est qui derelinquit patrem, et est maledictus a Deo qui exasperat matrem !]
      EN: How ill-reputed is the one who abandons his father! The one who provokes his mother is cursed by God.

3:17  LA: [Fili, in mansuetudine opera tua perfice, et super hominum gloriam diligeris.
      EN: Son, carry out your works with gentleness, and you will be loved beyond human glory.

3:18  LA: Quanto magnus es, humilia te in omnibus, et coram Deo invenies gratiam :
      EN: The greater you are, the more humble yourself in all things, and you will find favor before God,

3:20  LA: quoniam magna potentia Dei solius, et ab humilibus honoratur.
      EN: for great power belongs to God alone, and He is honored by the humble.

3:21  LA: Altiora te ne quæsieris, et fortiora te ne scrutatus fueris : sed quæ præcepit tibi Deus, illa cogita semper, et in pluribus operibus ejus ne fueris curiosus.
      EN: Do not seek what is too high for you or probe what is too strong for you. Instead, think always upon what God has commanded you, and do not be inquisitive about His many works.

3:22  LA: Non est enim tibi necessarium ea, quæ abscondita sunt, videre oculis tuis.
      EN: For it is not necessary for you to see with your own eyes the things that are hidden.

3:23  LA: In supervacuis rebus noli scrutari multipliciter, et in pluribus operibus ejus non eris curiosus.
      EN: Do not search excessively into unnecessary matters, and do not be inquisitive about His many works.

3:24  LA: multos quoque supplantavit suspicio illorum, et in vanitate detinuit sensus illorum.
      EN: Speculation about them has also brought many down and held their understanding captive in emptiness.

3:26  LA: Cor durum habebit male in novissimo, et qui amat periculum in illo peribit.
      EN: A hard heart will fare badly at the end, and the one who loves danger will perish in it.

3:27  LA: Cor nequam gravabitur in doloribus, et peccator adjiciet ad peccandum.
      EN: An evil heart will be weighed down with sorrows, and a sinner will add sin to sin.

3:28  LA: Synagogæ superborum non erit sanitas, frutex enim peccati radicabitur in illis, et non intelligetur.
      EN: There will be no healing for the assembly of the proud, for the shrub of sin will take root in them, and it will not be understood.

3:29  LA: Cor sapientis intelligitur in sapientia, et auris bona audiet cum omni concupiscentia sapientiam.
      EN: A wise person's heart is recognized by wisdom, and a good ear will listen to wisdom with complete eagerness.

3:30  LA: [Ignem ardentem exstinguit aqua, et eleemosyna resistit peccatis :
      EN: Water extinguishes a blazing fire, and almsgiving withstands sins.

3:31  LA: et Deus prospector est ejus qui reddit gratiam : meminit ejus in posterum, et in tempore casus sui inveniet firmamentum.]
      EN: God watches over the one who returns a kindness; He remembers him afterward, and in the time of his fall he will find support.

SIRACH 4

4:1  LA: Fili , eleemosynam pauperis ne defraudes, et oculos tuos ne transvertas a paupere.
     EN: Son, do not withhold the poor person's alms, and do not turn your eyes away from the poor.

4:2  LA: Animam esurientem ne despexeris, et non exasperes pauperem in inopia sua.
     EN: Do not despise a hungry soul or provoke a poor person in his need.

4:3  LA: Cor inopis ne afflixeris, et non protrahas datum angustianti.
     EN: Do not afflict the heart of the needy or delay your gift to someone in distress.

4:4  LA: Rogationem contribulati ne abjicias, et non avertas faciem tuam ab egeno.
     EN: Do not reject the plea of the afflicted or turn your face away from the destitute.

4:5  LA: Ab inope ne avertas oculos tuos propter iram : et non relinquas quærentibus tibi retro maledicere.
     EN: Do not turn your eyes away from the needy in anger, and do not leave those who seek your help to curse you behind your back.

4:6  LA: Maledicentis enim tibi in amaritudine animæ, exaudietur deprecatio illius : exaudiet autem eum qui fecit illum.
     EN: For the plea of one who curses you in bitterness of soul will be heard; the One who made him will hear him.

4:7  LA: Congregationi pauperum affabilem te facito : et presbytero humilia animam tuam, et magnato humilia caput tuum.
     EN: Make yourself approachable to the gathering of the poor; humble your soul before an elder and bow your head before a great man.

4:8  LA: Declina pauperi sine tristitia aurem tuam, et redde debitum tuum, et responde illi pacifica in mansuetudine.
     EN: Incline your ear to the poor without reluctance, pay what you owe, and answer him peaceably with gentleness.

4:9  LA: Libera eum qui injuriam patitur de manu superbi, et non acide feras in anima tua.
     EN: Rescue the one who suffers wrong from the hand of the proud, and do not bear it bitterly in your soul.

4:10  LA: In judicando esto pupillis misericors ut pater, et pro viro matri illorum :
      EN: In judgment be merciful to orphans as a father, and stand as a husband to their mother.

4:11  LA: [Sapientia filiis suis vitam inspirat : et suscipit inquirentes se, et præibit in via justitiæ.
      EN: Wisdom breathes life into her children, receives those who seek her, and goes before them in the way of righteousness.

4:12  LA: Et qui illam diligit, diligit vitam, et qui vigilaverint ad illam complectentur placorem ejus.
      EN: The one who loves her loves life, and those who keep watch for her will embrace her graciousness.

4:13  LA: Qui tenuerint illam, vitam hæreditabunt : et quo introibit benedicet Deus.
      EN: Those who hold her fast will inherit life, and wherever she enters, God will bless.

4:14  LA: Qui serviunt ei obsequentes erunt sancto : et eos qui diligunt illam, diligit Deus.
      EN: Those who serve her will obey the Holy One, and God loves those who love her.

4:15  LA: Qui audit illam judicabit gentes : et qui intuetur illam permanebit confidens.
      EN: The one who listens to her will judge nations, and the one who looks upon her will remain confident.

4:16  LA: Si crediderit ei, hæreditabit illam, et erunt in confirmatione creaturæ illius :
      EN: If he trusts her, he will inherit her, and his offspring will be firmly established,

4:17  LA: quoniam in tentatione ambulat cum eo, et in primis eligit eum. · Translation note. The source reads eligit eum, “chooses him,” and not an expression about tortuous or difficult paths. The draft follows the supplied Latin.
      EN: because she walks with him in testing and chooses him at the outset.

4:18  LA: Et firmabit illum, et iter adducet directum ad illum, et lætificabit illum :
      EN: She will strengthen him, bring a straight way to him, and gladden him.

4:19  LA: Si autem oberraverit, derelinquet eum, et tradet eum in manus inimici sui.]
      EN: But if he strays, she will abandon him and hand him over into the hands of his enemy.

4:20  LA: [Fili, conserva tempus, et devita a malo.
      EN: Son, observe the time, and avoid evil.

4:21  LA: est enim confusio adducens peccatum, et est confusio adducens gloriam et gratiam.
      EN: For there is a shame that brings sin, and there is a shame that brings glory and favor.

4:22  LA: Ne accipias faciem adversus faciem tuam, nec adversus animam tuam mendacium. · Translation note. The literal idiom repeats “face”: ne accipias faciem adversus faciem tuam. It is rendered as showing favor to someone to one’s own disadvantage; the distinct following clause about a lie against one’s soul remains.
      EN: Do not show anyone favor to your own disadvantage, or accept a lie against your soul.

4:23  LA: nec retineas verbum in tempore salutis. Non abscondas sapientiam tuam in decore suo :
      EN: or hold back a word when it can bring deliverance. Do not hide your wisdom in its beauty.

4:24  LA: in lingua enim sapientia dignoscitur : et sensus, et scientia, et doctrina in verbo sensati, et firmamentum in operibus justitiæ.
      EN: For wisdom is recognized by the tongue; understanding, knowledge, and teaching are in the word of a discerning person, and stability is in works of righteousness.

4:25  LA: Non contradicas verbo veritatis ullo modo, et de mendacio ineruditionis tuæ confundere.
      EN: Do not contradict a word of truth in any way, and be ashamed of the falsehood of your lack of instruction.

4:26  LA: Non confundaris confiteri peccata tua, et ne subjicias te omni homini pro peccato.
      EN: Do not be ashamed to confess your sins, and do not subject yourself to everyone on account of sin.

4:27  LA: Noli resistere contra faciem potentis, nec coneris contra ictum fluvii.
      EN: Do not stand against the face of the powerful or strive against the rush of a river.

4:28  LA: Pro justitia agonizare pro anima tua, et usque ad mortem certa pro justitia : et Deus expugnabit pro te inimicos tuos.
      EN: Struggle for righteousness for the sake of your soul, and contend for righteousness to the point of death; God will defeat your enemies for you.

4:29  LA: Noli citatus esse in lingua tua, et inutilis, et remissus in operibus tuis.
      EN: Do not be hasty with your tongue while useless and slack in your deeds.

4:30  LA: Noli esse sicut leo in domo tua, evertens domesticos tuos, et opprimens subjectos tibi.
      EN: Do not be like a lion in your house, destroying your household and oppressing those subject to you.

4:31  LA: Non sit porrecta manus tua ad accipiendum, et ad dandum collecta.]
      EN: Do not let your hand be stretched out to receive and drawn back to give.

SIRACH 5

5:1  LA: Noli attendere ad possessiones iniquas, et ne dixeris : Est mihi sufficiens vita : nihil enim proderit in tempore vindictæ et obductionis.
     EN: Do not rely on unjust possessions or say, “I have enough to live on,” for they will be of no use in the time of vengeance and oppression.

5:2  LA: Ne sequaris in fortitudine tua concupiscentiam cordis tui,
     EN: Do not follow the desire of your heart in your strength,

5:3  LA: et ne dixeris : Quomodo potui ? aut, Quis me subjiciet propter facta mea ? Deus enim vindicans vindicabit. · Translation note. Quomodo potui? is terse, literally “How have I been able?” The exclamation “How powerful I have been!” gives its boastful force in this context.
     EN: and do not say, “How powerful I have been!” or, “Who will bring me down for my deeds?” For God will surely exact vengeance.

5:4  LA: Ne dixeris : Peccavi : et quid mihi accidit triste ? Altissimus enim est patiens redditor.
     EN: Do not say, “I sinned, and what harm came to me?” For the Most High is patient in repaying.

5:5  LA: De propitiatio peccato noli esse sine metu, neque adjicias peccatum super peccatum. · Translation note. The online transcription reads propitiatio peccato, an irregular construction. The English construes it as the familiar participial sense “sin for which propitiation has been made,” while preserving the exact source string in the Latin field. This grammatical difficulty requires edition-level checking.
     EN: Do not be without fear concerning sin for which propitiation has been made, and do not add sin to sin.

5:6  LA: Et ne dicas : Miseratio Domini magna est, multitudinis peccatorum meorum miserebitur :
     EN: Do not say, “The Lord's compassion is great; He will have mercy on the multitude of my sins.”

5:7  LA: Non tardes converti ad Dominum, et ne differas de die in diem :
     EN: Do not delay turning to the Lord or put it off from day to day,

5:8  LA: Noli anxius esse in divitiis injustis : non enim proderunt tibi in die obductionis et vindictæ.]
     EN: Do not be anxious over unjust riches, for they will not help you on the day of oppression and vengeance.

5:9  LA: [Non ventiles te in omnem ventum, et non eas in omnem viam : sic enim omnis peccator probatur in duplici lingua.
     EN: Do not let yourself be blown about by every wind or walk in every path, for in this way every sinner is shown to have a double tongue.

5:10  LA: Esto firmus in via Domini, et in veritate sensus tui et scientia : et prosequatur te verbum pacis et justitiæ.
      EN: Stand firm in the way of the Lord and in the truth of your understanding and knowledge, and let a word of peace and righteousness accompany you.

5:11  LA: Esto mansuetus ad audiendum verbum, ut intelligas, et cum sapientia proferas responsum verum.
      EN: Be gentle in listening to a word, so that you may understand, and give a true answer with wisdom.

5:12  LA: Si est tibi intellectus, responde proximo : sin autem, sit manus tua super os tuum, ne capiaris in verbo indisciplinato, et confundaris.
      EN: If you have understanding, answer your neighbor; but if not, let your hand be over your mouth, lest you be caught by an undisciplined word and put to shame.

5:13  LA: Honor et gloria in sermone sensati : lingua vero imprudentis subversio est ipsius.
      EN: Honor and glory are in the speech of a discerning person, but the tongue of the imprudent is his downfall.

5:14  LA: Non appelleris susurro, et lingua tua ne capiaris et confundaris :

      EN: Evils will not come upon the one who fears the Lord; rather, in trial God will preserve him and deliver him from evils. A wise man does not hate commandments and acts of righteousness,

33:2  LA: et non illidetur quasi in procella navis.
      EN: and he will not be dashed about like a ship in a storm.

33:3  LA: Homo sensatus credit legi Dei, et lex illi fidelis.]
      EN: A man of understanding believes God's law, and the law is faithful to him.

33:4  LA: [Qui interrogationem manifestat parabit verbum, et sic deprecatus exaudietur : et conservabit disciplinam, et tunc respondebit.
      EN: The one who sets forth a question will prepare his word; then, when he has pleaded, he will be heard. He will keep instruction, and then answer.

33:5  LA: Præcordia fatui quasi rota carri, et quasi axis versatilis cogitatus illius.
      EN: A fool's inward parts are like a cartwheel, and his thought like a turning axle.

33:6  LA: Equus emissarius, sic et amicus subsannator : sub omni supra sedente hinnit.]
      EN: A mocking friend is like a breeding stallion; he neighs under everyone who sits upon him.

33:7  LA: [Quare dies diem superat, et iterum lux lucem, et annus annum a sole ?
      EN: Why does one day surpass another, and again one light another, and one year another, from the sun?

33:8  LA: A Domini scientia separati sunt, facto sole, et præceptum custodiente.
      EN: They were distinguished by the Lord's knowledge when the sun was made and kept His commandment.

33:9  LA: Ex ipsis exaltavit et magnificavit Deus, et ex ipsis posuit in numerum dierum : et omnes homines de solo et ex terra unde creatus est Adam.
      EN: Some of them God exalted and magnified, and some He placed among the ordinary days. All human beings are from the soil and from the earth from which Adam was created.

SIRACH 34

34:1  LA: Vana spes et mendacium viro insensato : et somnia extollunt imprudentes.
      EN: Vain hope and falsehood belong to a senseless man, and dreams lift up the imprudent.

34:2  LA: Quasi qui apprehendit umbram et persequitur ventum, sic et qui attendit ad visa mendacia.
      EN: Like someone grasping a shadow and chasing wind, so is one who attends to false visions.

34:3  LA: Hoc secundum hoc visio somniorum, ante faciem hominis similitudo hominis. · Translation note. The compressed hoc secundum hoc is retained as “this corresponding to that,” followed by the source’s image of a human likeness before a human face.
      EN: The vision of dreams is this corresponding to that: the likeness of a man before the man's face.

34:4  LA: Ab immundo, quid mundabitur ? et a mendace, quid verum dicetur ?
      EN: What will be cleansed by something unclean? And what truth will be spoken by a liar?

34:5  LA: Divinatio erroris, et auguria mendacia, et somnia malefacientium, vanitas est :
      EN: Misleading divination, lying auguries, and the dreams of evildoers are vanity.

34:6  LA: et sicut parturientis, cor tuum phantasias patitur. Nisi ab Altissimo fuerit emissa visitatio, ne dederis in illis cor tuum :
      EN: Like a woman in labor, your heart suffers fantasies. Unless a visitation has been sent from the Most High, do not set your heart upon them,

34:7  LA: multos enim errare fecerunt somnia, et exciderunt sperantes in illis.
      EN: for dreams have made many go astray, and those who hoped in them have fallen away.

34:8  LA: Sine mendacio consummabitur verbum legis, et sapientia in ore fidelis complanabitur.]
      EN: The word of the law will be fulfilled without falsehood, and wisdom will be made clear in the mouth of a faithful man.

34:9  LA: [Qui non est tentatus quid scit ? vir in multis expertus cogitabit multa : et qui multa didicit enarrabit intellectum.
      EN: What does one who has not been tested know? A man experienced in many things will ponder much, and the one who has learned much will recount understanding.

34:10  LA: Qui non est expertus pauca recognoscit : qui autem in multis factus est, multiplicat malitiam. · Translation note. Malitiam is retained as “malice,” despite the nearby praise of experience. The text is not harmonized to say merely knowledge or expertise.
       EN: The inexperienced man recognizes little, but the one who has been involved in many things multiplies malice.

34:11  LA: Multa vidi errando, et plurimas verborum consuetudines.
       EN: I have seen many things in my wandering and very many customs of speech.

34:12  LA: Aliquoties usque ad mortem periclitatus sum horum causa, et liberatus sum gratia Dei.]
       EN: At times I was endangered even to death because of these things, and I was delivered by God's grace.

34:13  LA: [Spiritus timentium Deum quæritur, et in respectu illius benedicetur.
       EN: The spirit of those who fear God is sought, and under His regard it will be blessed.

34:14  LA: Qui timet Dominum nihil trepidabit : et non pavebit, quoniam ipse est spes ejus.
       EN: The one who fears the Lord will tremble at nothing and will not be afraid, because He is his hope.

34:15  LA: Timentis Dominum, beata est anima ejus.
       EN: Blessed is the soul of the one who fears the Lord.

34:16  LA: Oculi Domini super timentes eum : protector potentiæ, firmamentum virtutis, tegimen ardoris, et umbraculum meridiani :
       EN: The eyes of the Lord are upon those who fear Him: a powerful protector, a support of strength, a covering against heat, and shade at midday,

34:17  LA: deprecatio offensionis, et adjutorium casus : exaltans animam, et illuminans oculos, dans sanitatem, et vitam, et benedictionem.]
       EN: a pleading against stumbling and help against falling, exalting the soul and enlightening the eyes, giving health, life, and blessing.

34:18  LA: [Immolantis ex iniquo oblatio est maculata, et non sunt beneplacitæ subsannationes injustorum.
       EN: The offering of one who sacrifices from unjust gain is stained, and the mockeries of the unjust are not pleasing.

34:19  LA: Dona iniquorum non probat Altissimus, nec respicit in oblationes iniquorum, nec in multitudine sacrificiorum eorum propitiabitur peccatis.
       EN: The Most High does not approve the gifts of the unjust or look upon their offerings, nor will He be propitiated for sins by the multitude of their sacrifices.

34:20  LA: Qui offert sacrificium ex substantia pauperum, quasi qui victimat filium in conspectu patris sui.
       EN: The one who offers a sacrifice from the property of the poor is like someone sacrificing a son before his father's eyes.

34:21  LA: Panis egentium vita pauperum est : qui defraudat illum homo sanguinis est.
       EN: The bread of the needy is the life of the poor; the one who cheats them of it is a man of blood.

34:22  LA: Qui aufert in sudore panem, quasi qui occidit proximum suum.
       EN: The one who takes away bread earned in sweat is like someone killing his neighbor.

34:23  LA: Unus ædificans, et unus destruens : quid prodest illis, nisi labor ?
       EN: One builds and another destroys; what does it profit them except labor?

34:24  LA: Unus orans, et unus maledicens : cujus vocem exaudiet Deus ?
       EN: One prays and another curses; whose voice will God hear?

34:25  LA: Qui baptizatur a mortuo, et iterum tangit eum, quid proficit lavatio illius ? · Translation note. Baptizatur here denotes washing after contact with a corpse, as shown by a mortuo and lavatio; it is not automatically identified with Christian baptism.
       EN: The one who washes after contact with a dead body and touches it again—what does his washing benefit him?

34:26  LA: Sic homo qui jejunat in peccatis suis, et iterum eadem faciens : quid proficit humiliando se ? orationem illius quis exaudiet ?]
       EN: So a man who fasts over his sins and does the same things again: what does he gain by humbling himself? Who will hear his prayer?

      EN: Whoever offers fine flour will return thanks, and whoever shows mercy offers a sacrifice.

      EN: Give to the Most High according to His gift, and with a generous eye make an offering from the gain of your hands,

       EN: for the Lord is One who repays, and He will return seven times as much to you.

       EN: Do not offer wicked gifts, for He will not receive them.

       EN: Do not a widow's tears run down her cheek, and her cry rise against the one who draws them forth?

35:16  LA: Qui adorat Deum in oblectatione suscipietur, et deprecatio illius usque ad nubes propinquabit.]
       EN: Whoever worships God will be received with delight, and his plea will approach the clouds.

35:17  LA: [Oratio humiliantis se nubes penetrabit, et donec propinquet non consolabitur, et non discedet donec Altissimus aspiciat.
       EN: The prayer of one who humbles himself will pierce the clouds. Until it draws near, it will not be comforted, and it will not depart until the Most High looks upon it.

35:18  LA: Et Dominus non elongabit : et judicabit justos, et faciet judicium : et Fortissimus non habebit in illis patientiam, ut contribulet dorsum ipsorum : · Translation note. SIRACH 37

37:1  LA: Omnis amicus dicet : Et ego amicitiam copulavi : sed est amicus solo nomine amicus. Nonne tristitia inest usque ad mortem ?
      EN: Every friend will say, “I too have formed a friendship,” but there is a friend who is a friend in name alone. Is there not sorrow in this, even to death?

37:2  LA: sodalis autem et amicus ad inimicitiam convertentur.
      EN: Yet a companion and a friend will turn to enmity.

37:3  LA: O præsumptio nequissima, unde creata es cooperire aridam malitia et dolositate illius ? · Translation note. The Latin addresses presumption in the second person but closes with illius, a third-person possessive. The shift is retained.
      EN: O most wicked presumption, from where were you created, to cover the dry land with its malice and deceit?

37:4  LA: Sodalis amico conjucundatur in oblectationibus, et in tempore tribulationis adversarius erit.
      EN: A companion rejoices with his friend in pleasures, but in a time of distress he will be an adversary.

37:5  LA: Sodalis amico condolet causa ventris, et contra hostem accipiet scutum.
      EN: A companion grieves with his friend for his belly's sake, and against an enemy he will take up a shield.

37:6  LA: Non obliviscaris amici tui in animo tuo, et non immemor sis illius in opibus tuis.
      EN: Do not forget your friend in your heart, and do not be unmindful of him in your prosperity.

37:7  LA: [Omnis consiliarius prodit consilium, sed est consiliarius in semetipso.
      EN: Every counselor brings forth counsel, but there is a counselor who counsels for himself.

37:8  LA: A consiliario serva animam tuam : prius scito quæ sit illius necessitas : et ipse enim animo suo cogitabit :
      EN: Guard your soul from a counselor; first know what his need is, for he too will be thinking within himself,

37:9  LA: ne forte mittat sudem in terram, et dicat tibi :
      EN: lest perhaps he plant a stake in the ground and say to you,

37:10  LA: Noli consiliari cum eo qui tibi insidiatur, et a zelantibus te absconde consilium.]
       EN: Do not take counsel with one who lies in wait for you, and hide your counsel from those who envy you.

37:11  LA: Cum viro irreligioso tracta de sanctitate, et cum injusto de justitia, et cum muliere de ea quæ æmulatur, cum timido de bello, cum negotiatore de trajectione, cum emptore de venditione, cum viro livido de gratiis agendis, · Translation note. The Latin says “discuss” positively, then rejects these counselors in 37:14. The sequence is retained as a rhetorical construction rather than inserting a negative into 37:12. Trajectio is rendered “transfer of goods”; its exact commercial sense needs specialist review.
       EN: Discuss holiness with an irreligious man, and righteousness with an unjust man; with a woman, discuss the woman of whom she is jealous; with a coward, war; with a merchant, the transfer of goods; with a buyer, selling; with an envious man, the giving of thanks;

37:12  LA: sed cum viro sancto assiduus esto, quemcumque cognoveris observantem timorem Dei :
       EN: But remain constantly with a holy man, whomever you have known to observe the fear of God,

SIRACH 51

51:1  LA: Oratio Jesu filii Sirach. [Confitebor tibi, Domine rex, et collaudabo te Deum salvatorem meum.
      EN: The prayer of Jesus son of Sirach. I will give thanks to You, Lord and King, and praise You, God my Savior.

51:2  LA: Confitebor nomini tuo, quoniam adjutor et protector factus es mihi,
      EN: I will give thanks to Your name, for You have become my Helper and Protector,

51:3  LA: et liberasti corpus meum a perditione : a laqueo linguæ iniquæ, et a labiis operantium mendacium : et in conspectu astantium factus es mihi adjutor.
      EN: and delivered my body from destruction, from the snare of an unjust tongue and the lips of those who work falsehood. And You became my Helper in the sight of those standing by.

51:4  LA: a pressura flammæ quæ circumdedit me, et in medio ignis non sum æstuatus ;
      EN: from the pressure of the flame that surrounded me, and in the midst of fire I was not scorched;

51:5  LA: de altitudine ventris inferi, et a lingua coinquinata, et a verbo mendacii, a rege iniquo, et a lingua injusta.
      EN: from the depth of the belly of the realm of the dead, from a polluted tongue and a word of falsehood, from an unjust king and an unjust tongue.

51:6  LA: Laudabit usque ad mortem anima mea Dominum,
      EN: My soul will praise the Lord until death,

51:7  LA: Circumdederunt me undique, et non erat qui adjuvaret : respiciens eram ad adjutorium hominum, et non erat.
      EN: They surrounded me on every side, and there was no one to help. I was looking for human help, and there was none.

51:8  LA: Memoratus sum misericordiæ tuæ Domine, et operationis tuæ, quæ a sæculo sunt :
      EN: I remembered Your mercy, Lord, and Your works, which are from of old,

51:9  LA: Exaltasti super terram habitationem meam, et pro morte defluente deprecatus sum. · Translation note. Pro morte defluente is difficult, literally concerning/for death flowing away. “For death’s passing away” preserves the motion and petition without reconstructing a different text.
      EN: You raised my dwelling above the earth, and I prayed for death’s passing away.

51:10  LA: Invocavi Dominum patrem Domini mei, ut non derelinquat me in die tribulationis meæ, et in tempore superborum, sine adjutorio. · Translation note. The Latin explicitly reads Dominum patrem Domini mei, “the Lord, the Father of my Lord.” This distinctive Latin wording is preserved without importing a harmonized reading.
       EN: I invoked the Lord, the Father of my Lord, so that He would not leave me without help in the day of my distress and in the time of the proud.

51:11  LA: Laudabo nomen tuum assidue, et collaudabo illud in confessione : et exaudita est oratio mea,
       EN: I will praise Your name continually and praise it with thanksgiving. And my prayer was heard,

51:12  LA: Propterea confitebor, et laudem dicam tibi, et benedicam nomini Domini.]
       EN: Therefore I will give thanks and speak praise to You, and bless the Lord's name.

51:13  LA: [Cum adhuc junior essem, priusquam oberrarem, quæsivi sapientiam palam in oratione mea.
       EN: When I was still younger, before I wandered abroad, I openly sought wisdom in my prayer.

51:14  LA: Ante templum postulabam pro illis, et usque in novissimis inquiram eam : et effloruit tamquam præcox uva. · Translation note. Pro illis is plural, “for them,” with no clear immediate antecedent. It is not silently changed to “for her.”
       EN: Before the temple I petitioned for them, and to the very last I will seek her; and she blossomed like an early grape.

51:15  LA: Lætatum est cor meum in ea : ambulavit pes meus iter rectum : a juventute mea investigabam eam.
       EN: My heart rejoiced in her; my foot walked a straight path; from my youth I searched for her.

51:16  LA: Inclinavi modico aurem meam, et excepi illam.
       EN: I bent my ear a little and received her.

51:17  LA: Multam inveni in meipso sapientiam, et multum profeci in ea.
       EN: I found much wisdom within myself and made great progress in her.

51:18  LA: consiliatus sum enim ut facerem illam. Zelatus sum bonum, et non confundar.
       EN: for I resolved to practice her. I was zealous for the good, and I will not be put to shame.

51:19  LA: Colluctata est anima mea in illa, et in faciendo eam confirmatus sum.
       EN: My soul wrestled with her, and I was strengthened in practicing her.

51:20  LA: animam meam direxi ad illam, et in agnitione inveni eam.
       EN: I directed my soul toward her, and in recognition I found her.

51:21  LA: Venter meus conturbatus est quærendo illam : propterea bonam possidebo possessionem.
       EN: My belly was troubled in seeking her; therefore I will possess a good possession.

51:22  LA: Dedit mihi Dominus linguam mercedem meam, et in ipsa laudabo eum.
       EN: The Lord gave me a tongue as my reward, and with it I will praise Him.

       EN: Draw near to me, you who are untaught, and gather yourselves into the house of instruction.
