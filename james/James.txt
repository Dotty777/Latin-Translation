JAMES 1

1:1  LA: Jacobus , Dei et Domini nostri Jesu Christi servus, duodecim tribubus, quæ sunt in dispersione, salutem.
     EN: James, the servant of God and of our Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes which are scattered abroad, greeting.

1:2  LA: Omne gaudium existimate fratres mei, cum in tentationes varias incideritis:
     EN: My brothers, count it all joy, when you shall fall into various temptations:

1:3  LA: scientes quod probatio fidei vestræ patientiam operatur.
     EN: Knowing that the trying of your faith works patience

1:4  LA: Patientia autem opus perfectum habet: ut sitis perfecti et integri in nullo deficientes.
     EN: And patience has a perfect work: that you may be perfect and entire, failing in nothing.

1:5  LA: Si quis autem vestrum indiget sapientia, postulet a Deo, qui dat omnibus affluenter, et non improperat: et dabitur ei.
     EN: If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach, and it will be given to him.

1:6  LA: Postulet autem in fide nihil hæsitans: qui enim hæsitat, similis est fluctui maris, qui a vento movetur et circumfertur:
     EN: But let him ask in faith, without wavering. For the one who wavers is like a wave of the sea, driven and tossed by the wind.

1:7  LA: non ergo æstimet homo ille quod accipiat aliquid a Domino.
     EN: Therefore let not that man think that he shall receive anything of the Lord.

1:8  LA: Vir duplex animo inconstans est in omnibus viis suis.
     EN: A double minded man is inconstant in all his ways.

1:9  LA: Glorietur autem frater humilis in exaltatione sua:
     EN: Let the lowly brother glory in being raised up,

1:10  LA: dives autem in humilitate sua, quoniam sicut flos fœni transibit;
      EN: and let the rich man glory in being brought low, for he will pass away like a flower in the grass.

1:11  LA: exortus est enim sol cum ardore, et arefecit fœnum, et flos ejus decidit, et decor vultus ejus deperiit: ita et dives in itineribus suis marcescet.
      EN: For the sun rose with its burning heat and dried up the grass. Its flower fell, and its beauty was gone. So too the rich man will wither in his ways.

1:12  LA: Beatus vir qui suffert tentationem: quoniam cum probatus fuerit, accipiet coronam vitæ, quam repromisit Deus diligentibus se.
      EN: Blessed is the man that endures temptation: for, when he has been proved, he shall receive the crown of life which God has promised to them that love him.

1:13  LA: Nemo cum tentatur, dicat quoniam a Deo tentatur: Deus enim intentator malorum est: ipse autem neminem tentat.
      EN: Let no one, when he is tempted, say that he is tempted by God. For God is not a tempter of evils: and he tempts no one.

1:14  LA: Unusquisque vero tentatur a concupiscentia sua abstractus, et illectus.
      EN: But every man is tempted by his own desire, being drawn away and allured.

1:15  LA: Deinde concupiscentia cum conceperit, parit peccatum: peccatum vero cum consummatum fuerit, generat mortem.
      EN: Then desire conceives and gives birth to sin, and sin, when it is full grown, gives birth to death.

1:16  LA: Nolite itaque errare, fratres mei dilectissimi.
      EN: Do not err, therefore, my dearest brothers.

1:17  LA: Omne datum optimum, et omne donum perfectum desursum est, descendens a Patre luminum, apud quem non est transmutatio, nec vicissitudinis obumbratio.
      EN: Every best gift and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no change nor shadow of alteration.

1:18  LA: Voluntarie enim genuit nos verbo veritatis, ut simus initium aliquod creaturæ ejus.
      EN: For he chose to give us birth by the word of truth, so that we might be a kind of beginning of his creation.

1:19  LA: Scitis, fratres mei dilectissimi. Sit autem omnis homo velox ad audiendum: tardus autem ad loquendum, et tardus ad iram.
      EN: You know, my dearest brothers. And let every man be swift to hear, but slow to speak and slow to anger.

1:20  LA: Ira enim viri justitiam Dei non operatur.
      EN: For the anger of man works not the justice of God.

1:21  LA: Propter quod abjicientes omnem immunditiam, et abundantiam malitiæ, in mansuetudine suscipite insitum verbum, quod potest salvare animas vestras.
      EN: So put away all uncleanness and the abundance of evil. Receive with meekness the implanted word, which can save your souls.

1:22  LA: Estote autem factores verbi, et non auditores tantum: fallentes vosmetipsos.
      EN: But be doers of the word and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.

1:23  LA: Quia si quis auditor est verbi, et non factor, hic comparabitur viro consideranti vultum nativitatis suæ in speculo:
      EN: For if anyone hears the word and does not do it, he is like a man looking at his own face in a mirror.

1:24  LA: consideravit enim se, et abiit, et statim oblitus est qualis fuerit.
      EN: He looked at himself, went away, and immediately forgot what he looked like.

1:25  LA: Qui autem perspexerit in legem perfectam libertatis, et permanserit in ea, non auditor obliviosus factus, sed factor operis: hic beatus in facto suo erit.
      EN: But whoever looks closely into the perfect law of liberty and abides in it, becoming a doer of the work instead of a forgetful hearer, will be blessed in what he does.

1:26  LA: Si quis autem putat se religiosum esse, non refrenans linguam suam, sed seducens cor suum, hujus vana est religio.
      EN: If anyone thinks he is religious but does not bridle his tongue and deceives his own heart, his religion is empty.

1:27  LA: Religio munda et immaculata apud Deum et Patrem, hæc est: visitare pupillos et viduas in tribulatione eorum, et immaculatum se custodire ab hoc sæculo.
      EN: Religion that is pure and undefiled before God the Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their distress and to keep oneself unstained by this world.

JAMES 2

2:1  LA: Fratres mei, nolite in personarum acceptione habere fidem Domini nostri Jesu Christi gloriæ.
     EN: My brothers, have not the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ of glory, with respect of persons.

2:2  LA: Etenim si introierit in conventum vestrum vir aureum annulum habens in veste candida, introierit autem et pauper in sordido habitu,
     EN: For if there shall come into your assembly a man having a golden ring, in fine apparel; and there shall come in also a poor man in mean attire:

2:3  LA: et intendatis in eum qui indutus est veste præclara, et dixeritis ei: Tu sede hic bene: pauperi autem dicatis: Tu sta illic; aut sede sub scabello pedum meorum:
     EN: And you have respect to him that is clothed with the fine apparel and shall say to him: Sit you here well: but say to the poor man: Stand you there, or: Sit under my footstool:

2:4  LA: nonne judicatis apud vosmetipsos, et facti estis judices cogitationum iniquarum?
     EN: Do you not judge within yourselves, and are become judges of unjust thoughts?

2:5  LA: Audite, fratres mei dilectissimi: nonne Deus elegit pauperes in hoc mundo, divites in fide, et hæredes regni, quod repromisit Deus diligentibus se?
     EN: Hearken, my dearest brothers: Has not God chosen the poor in this world, rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom which God has promised to them that love him?

2:6  LA: vos autem exhonorastis pauperem. Nonne divites per potentiam opprimunt vos, et ipsi trahunt vos ad judicia?
     EN: But you have dishonoured the poor man. Do not the rich oppress you by might? And do not they draw you before the judgment seats?

2:7  LA: nonne ipsi blasphemant bonum nomen, quod invocatum est super vos?
     EN: Do not they blaspheme the good name that is invoked upon you?

2:8  LA: Si tamen legem perficitis regalem secundum Scripturas: Diliges proximum tuum sicut teipsum: bene facitis:
     EN: If then you fulfill the royal law, according to the scriptures: You shall love your neighbour as yourself; you do well.

2:9  LA: si autem personas accipitis, peccatum operamini, redarguti a lege quasi transgressores.
     EN: But if you have respect to persons, you commit sin, being reproved by the law as transgressors.

2:10  LA: Quicumque autem totam legem servaverit, offendat autem in uno, factus est omnium reus.
      EN: And whoever shall keep the whole law, but offend in one point, has become guilty of all.

2:11  LA: Qui enim dixit: Non mœchaberis, dixit et: Non occides. Quod si non mœchaberis, occides autem, factus es transgressor legis.
      EN: For he that said: You shall not commit adultery, said also: You shall not kill. Now if you do not commit adultery, but shall kill, you are become a transgressor of the law.

2:12  LA: Sic loquimini, et sic facite sicut per legem libertatis incipientes judicari.
      EN: So speak you and so do, as being to be judged by the law of liberty.

2:13  LA: Judicium enim sine misericordia illi qui non fecit misericordiam: superexaltat autem misericordia judicium.
      EN: For judgment without mercy to him that has not done mercy. And mercy exalts itself above judgment.

2:14  LA: Quid proderit, fratres mei, si fidem quis dicat se habere, opera autem non habeat? numquid poterit fides salvare eum?
      EN: What good is it, my brothers, if someone says he has faith but does not have works? Can faith save him?

2:15  LA: Si autem frater et soror nudi sint, et indigeant victu quotidiano,
      EN: If a brother and sister are without clothing and in need of daily food,

2:16  LA: dicat autem aliquis ex vobis illis: Ite in pace, calefacimini et saturamini: non dederitis autem eis quæ necessaria sunt corpori, quid proderit?
      EN: and one of you says to them, “Go in peace; be warmed and filled,” but you do not give them what the body needs, what good is it?

2:17  LA: Sic et fides, si non habeat opera, mortua est in semetipsa.
      EN: So also faith, if it does not have works, is dead in itself.

2:18  LA: Sed dicet quis: Tu fidem habes, et ego opera habeo: ostende mihi fidem tuam sine operibus: et ego ostendam tibi ex operibus fidem meam.
      EN: But someone will say, “You have faith, and I have works.” Show me your faith without works, and I will show you my faith by my works.

2:19  LA: Tu credis quoniam unus est Deus: bene facis: et dæmones credunt, et contremiscunt.
      EN: You believe that God is one. You do well; the demons also believe—and tremble.

2:20  LA: Vis autem scire, o homo inanis, quoniam fides sine operibus mortua est?
      EN: But do you wish to know, empty-headed person, that faith without works is dead?

2:21  LA: Abraham pater noster nonne ex operibus justificatus est, offerens Isaac filium suum super altare?
      EN: Was not Abraham our father justified by works when he offered his son Isaac upon the altar?

2:22  LA: Vides quoniam fides cooperabatur operibus illius: et ex operibus fides consummata est?
      EN: Do you see that faith was working with his works, and through works faith was made complete?

2:23  LA: Et suppleta est Scriptura, dicens: Credidit Abraham Deo, et reputatum est illi ad justitiam, et amicus Dei appellatus est.
      EN: And the Scripture was fulfilled that says, “Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness,” and he was called the friend of God.

2:24  LA: Videtis quoniam ex operibus justificatur homo, et non ex fide tantum?
      EN: Do you see that a person is justified by works, and not by faith alone?

2:25  LA: Similiter et Rahab meretrix, nonne ex operibus justificata est, suscipiens nuntios, et alia via ejiciens?
      EN: Likewise, was not Rahab the prostitute justified by works when she received the messengers and sent them out by another way?

2:26  LA: Sicut enim corpus sine spiritu mortuum est, ita et fides sine operibus mortua est.
      EN: For just as the body without the spirit is dead, so also faith without works is dead.

JAMES 3

3:1  LA: Nolite plures magistri fieri fratres mei, scientes quoniam majus judicium sumitis.
     EN: My brothers, let not many of you become teachers, knowing that you will receive a stricter judgment.

3:2  LA: In multis enim offendimus omnes. Si quis in verbo non offendit, hic perfectus est vir: potest etiam freno circumducere totum corpus.
     EN: For we all stumble in many ways. If anyone does not stumble in what he says, he is a perfect man, able to bridle his whole body as well.

3:3  LA: Si autem equis frena in ora mittimus ad consentiendum nobis, et omne corpus illorum circumferimus.
     EN: When we put bits in horses’ mouths to make them obey us, we guide their whole bodies.

3:4  LA: Ecce et naves, cum magnæ sint, et a ventis validis minentur, circumferuntur a modico gubernaculo ubi impetus dirigentis voluerit.
     EN: Look at ships too. Though they are large and driven by strong winds, they are steered by a small rudder wherever the pilot wishes.

3:5  LA: Ita et lingua modicum quidem membrum est, et magna exaltat. Ecce quantus ignis quam magnam silvam incendit!
     EN: So also the tongue is a small member, yet it boasts of great things. See what a fire can set so great a forest ablaze!

3:6  LA: Et lingua ignis est, universitas iniquitatis. Lingua constituitur in membris nostris, quæ maculat totum corpus, et inflammat rotam nativitatis nostræ inflammata a gehenna.
     EN: The tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity among the parts of our body. It stains the whole body and sets the course of our life ablaze, itself set on fire by Gehenna.

3:7  LA: Omnis enim natura bestiarum, et volucrum, et serpentium, et ceterorum domantur, et domita sunt a natura humana:
     EN: Every kind of beast, bird, serpent, and other creature is tamed and has been tamed by humankind,

3:8  LA: linguam autem nullus hominum domare potest: inquietum malum, plena veneno mortifero.
     EN: but no one can tame the tongue. It is a restless evil, full of deadly poison.

3:9  LA: In ipsa benedicimus Deum et Patrem: et in ipsa maledicimus homines, qui ad similitudinem Dei facti sunt.
     EN: By it we bless God and the Father: and by it we curse men who are made after the likeness of God.

3:10  LA: Ex ipso ore procedit benedictio et maledictio. Non oportet, fratres mei, hæc ita fieri.
      EN: From the same mouth come blessing and cursing. My brothers, these things should not be so.

3:11  LA: Numquid fons de eodem foramine emanat dulcem et amaram aquam?
      EN: Does a fountain send forth, out of the same hole, sweet and bitter water?

3:12  LA: Numquid potest, fratres mei, ficus uvas facere, aut vitis ficus? Sic neque salsa dulcem potest facere aquam.
      EN: Can the fig tree, my brothers, bear grapes? Or the vine, figs? So neither can the salt water yield sweet.

3:13  LA: Quis sapiens et disciplinatus inter vos? Ostendat ex bona conversatione operationem suam in mansuetudine sapientiæ.
      EN: Who among you is wise and learned? Let him show his works by his good conduct, in the meekness that comes from wisdom.

3:14  LA: Quod si zelum amarum habetis, et contentiones sint in cordibus vestris: nolite gloriari, et mendaces esse adversus veritatem:
      EN: But if you have bitter zeal and rivalry in your hearts, do not boast and lie against the truth.

3:15  LA: non est enim ista sapientia desursum descendens: sed terrena, animalis, diabolica.
      EN: For this is not wisdom, descending from above: but earthly, sensual, devilish.

3:16  LA: Ubi enim zelus et contentio, ibi inconstantia et omne opus pravum.
      EN: For where envying and contention is: there is inconstancy and every evil work.

3:17  LA: Quæ autem desursum est sapientia, primum quidem pudica est, deinde pacifica, modesta, suadibilis, bonus consentiens, plena misericordia et fructibus bonis, non judicans, sine simulatione.
      EN: But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, modest, open to persuasion, willing to agree with what is good, full of mercy and good fruits, without judging and without pretense.

3:18  LA: Fructus autem justitiæ, in pace seminatur, facientibus pacem.
      EN: And the fruit of justice is sown in peace, to them that make peace.

JAMES 4

4:1  LA: Unde bella et lites in vobis? nonne hinc: ex concupiscentiis vestris, quæ militant in membris vestris?
     EN: From where are wars and quarrels among you? Are they not from here, from your desires, which war in your members?

4:2  LA: concupiscitis, et non habetis: occiditis, et zelatis: et non potestis adipisci: litigatis, et belligeratis, et non habetis, propter quod non postulatis.
     EN: You covet, and have not: you kill and envy and cannot obtain. You contend and war, and you have not: because you ask not.

4:3  LA: Petitis, et non accipitis: eo quod male petatis: ut in concupiscentiis vestris insumatis.
     EN: You ask and receive not: because you ask amiss, that you may consume it on your desires.

4:4  LA: Adulteri, nescitis quia amicitia hujus mundi inimica est Dei? quicumque ergo voluerit amicus esse sæculi hujus, inimicus Dei constituitur.
     EN: Adulterers, know you not that the friendship of this world is the enemy of God? Whoever therefore will be a friend of this world becomes an enemy of God.

4:5  LA: An putatis quia inaniter Scriptura dicat: Ad invidiam concupiscit spiritus qui habitat in vobis?
     EN: Or do you think that the scripture says in vain: To envy does the spirit covet which dwells in you?

4:6  LA: majorem autem dat gratiam. Propter quod dicit: Deus superbis resistit, humilibus autem dat gratiam.
     EN: But he gives greater grace. Therefore he says: God resists the proud and gives grace to the humble.

4:7  LA: Subditi ergo estote Deo, resistite autem diabolo, et fugiet a vobis.
     EN: Be subject therefore to God. But resist the devil: and he will fly from you.

4:8  LA: Appropinquate Deo, et appropinquabit vobis. Emundate manus, peccatores: et purificate corda, duplices animo.
     EN: Draw near to God: and he will draw near to you. Cleanse your hands, you sinners, and purify your hearts, you double minded.

4:9  LA: Miseri estote, et lugete, et plorate: risus vester in luctum convertatur, et gaudium in mœrorem.
     EN: Be afflicted and mourn and weep: let your laughter be turned into mourning and your joy into sorrow.

4:10  LA: Humiliamini in conspectu Domini, et exaltabit vos.
      EN: Be humbled in the sight of the Lord: and he will exalt you.

4:11  LA: Nolite detrahere alterutrum fratres. Qui detrahit fratri, aut qui judicat fratrem suum, detrahit legi, et judicat legem. Si autem judicas legem, non es factor legis, sed judex.
      EN: Detract not one another, my brothers. He that slanders his brother, or he that judges his brother, slanders the law and judges the law. But if you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law, but a judge.

4:12  LA: Unus est legislator et judex, qui potest perdere et liberare.
      EN: There is one lawgiver and judge, that is able to destroy and to deliver.

4:13  LA: Tu autem quis es, qui judicas proximum? Ecce nunc qui dicitis: Hodie, aut crastino ibimus in illam civitatem, et faciemus ibi quidem annum, et mercabimur, et lucrum faciemus:
      EN: But who are you that judge your neighbour? Behold, now you that say: To-day or to-morrow we will go into such a city, and there we will spend a year and will traffic and make our gain.

4:14  LA: qui ignoratis quid erit in crastino.
      EN: Whereas you do not know what shall be on the next day.

4:15  LA: Quæ est enim vita vestra? vapor est ad modicum parens, et deinceps exterminabitur; pro eo ut dicatis: Si Dominus voluerit. Et: Si vixerimus, faciemus hoc, aut illud.
      EN: For what is your life? It is a vapour which appears for a little while and afterwards shall vanish away. For that you should say: If the Lord will, and, If we shall live, we will do this or that.

4:16  LA: Nunc autem exsultatis in superbiis vestris. Omnis exsultatio talis, maligna est.
      EN: But now you rejoice in your arrogancies. All such rejoicing is wicked.

4:17  LA: Scienti igitur bonum facere, et non facienti, peccatum est illi.
      EN: To him therefore who knows to do good and does it not, to him it is sin.

JAMES 5

5:1  LA: Agite nunc divites, plorate ululantes in miseriis vestris, quæ advenient vobis.
     EN: Go to now, you rich men: weep and howl in your miseries, which shall come upon you.

5:2  LA: Divitiæ vestræ putrefactæ sunt, et vestimenta vestra a tineis comesta sunt.
     EN: Your riches are corrupted: and your garments are motheaten.

5:3  LA: Aurum et argentum vestrum æruginavit: et ærugo eorum in testimonium vobis erit, et manducabit carnes vestras sicut ignis. Thesaurizastis vobis iram in novissimis diebus.
     EN: Your gold and silver is cankered: and the rust of them shall be for a testimony against you and shall eat your flesh like fire. You have stored up to yourselves wrath against the last days.

5:4  LA: Ecce merces operariorum, qui messuerunt regiones vestras, quæ fraudata est a vobis, clamat: et clamor eorum in aures Domini sabbaoth introivit.
     EN: Behold the hire of the laborers who have reaped down your fields, which by fraud has been kept back by you, cries: and the cry of them has entered into the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth.

5:5  LA: Epulati estis super terram, et in luxuriis enutristis corda vestra in die occisionis.
     EN: You have feasted upon earth: and in riotousness you have nourished your hearts, in the day of slaughter.

5:6  LA: Addixistis, et occidistis justum, et non resistit vobis.
     EN: You have condemned and put to death the Just One: and he resisted you not.

5:7  LA: Patientes igitur estote, fratres, usque ad adventum Domini. Ecce agricola exspectat pretiosum fructum terræ, patienter ferens donec accipiat temporaneum et serotinum.
     EN: Be patient therefore, brothers, until the coming of the Lord. Behold, the husbandman waits for the precious fruit of the earth: patiently bearing until he receive the early and latter rain.

5:8  LA: Patientes igitur estote et vos, et confirmate corda vestra: quoniam adventus Domini appropinquavit.
     EN: Be therefore also patient and strengthen your hearts: for the coming of the Lord is near.

5:9  LA: Nolite ingemiscere, fratres, in alterutrum, ut non judicemini. Ecce judex ante januam assistit.
     EN: Grudge not, brothers, one against another, that you may not be judged. Behold the judge stands before the door.

5:10  LA: Exemplum accipite, fratres, exitus mali, laboris, et patientiæ, prophetas qui locuti sunt in nomine Domini.
      EN: Take, my brothers, for an example of suffering evil, of labor and patience, the prophets who spoke in the name of the Lord.

5:11  LA: Ecce beatificamus eos qui sustinuerunt. Sufferentiam Job audistis, et finem Domini vidistis, quoniam misericors Dominus est, et miserator.
      EN: Behold, we account them blessed who have endured. You have heard of the patience of Job and you have seen the end of the Lord, that the Lord is merciful and compassionate.

5:12  LA: Ante omnia autem, fratres mei, nolite jurare, neque per cælum, neque per terram, neque aliud quodcumque juramentum. Sit autem sermo vester: Est, est: Non, non: ut non sub judicio decidatis.
      EN: But above all things, my brothers, swear not, neither by heaven, nor by the earth, nor by any other oath. But let your speech be: Yes, Yes: No, No: that you fall not under judgment.

5:13  LA: Tristatur aliquis vestrum? oret. Æquo animo est? psallat.
      EN: Is any of you sad? Let him pray: Is he cheerful in mind? Let him sing.

5:14  LA: Infirmatur quis in vobis? inducat presbyteros ecclesiæ, et orent super eum, ungentes eum oleo in nomine Domini:
      EN: Is anyone among you sick? Let him call in the presbyters of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord.

5:15  LA: et oratio fidei salvabit infirmum, et alleviabit eum Dominus: et si in peccatis sit, remittentur ei.
      EN: And the prayer of faith will save the sick person, and the Lord will raise him up; and if he is in sins, they will be forgiven him.

5:16  LA: Confitemini ergo alterutrum peccata vestra, et orate pro invicem ut salvemini: multum enim valet deprecatio justi assidua.
      EN: Therefore confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, so that you may be healed; for the persistent prayer of a just person has great power.

5:17  LA: Elias homo erat similis nobis passibilis: et oratione oravit ut non plueret super terram, et non pluit annos tres, et menses sex.
      EN: Elijah was a man passible like to us: and with prayer he prayed that it might not rain upon the earth. And it rained not for three years and six months.

5:18  LA: Et rursum oravit: et cælum dedit pluviam, et terra dedit fructum suum.
      EN: And he prayed again. And the heaven gave rain: and the earth brought forth her fruit.

5:19  LA: Fratres mei, si quis ex vobis erraverit a veritate, et converterit quis eum:
      EN: My brothers, if any of you err from the truth and one convert him:

5:20  LA: scire debet quoniam qui converti fecerit peccatorem ab errore viæ suæ, salvabit animam ejus a morte, et operiet multitudinem peccatorum.
      EN: He must know that he who causes a sinner to be converted from the error of his way shall save his soul from death and shall cover a multitude of sins.
