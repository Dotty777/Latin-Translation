ECCLESIASTES 1

1:1  LA: Verba Ecclesiastæ, filii David, regis Jerusalem.
     EN: The words of Ecclesiastes, son of David, king of Jerusalem.

1:2  LA: [Vanitas vanitatum, dixit Ecclesiastes ; vanitas vanitatum, et omnia vanitas.
     EN: Futility of futilities, said Ecclesiastes; futility of futilities, and everything is futility.

1:3  LA: Quid habet amplius homo de universo labore suo quo laborat sub sole ?]
     EN: What more does a person have from all the labor at which he labors under the sun?

1:4  LA: [Generatio præterit, et generatio advenit ; terra autem in æternum stat.
     EN: A generation passes, and a generation comes, but the earth stands forever.

1:5  LA: Oritur sol et occidit, et ad locum suum revertitur ; ibique renascens,
     EN: The sun rises and sets and returns to its place; rising again there,

1:6  LA: gyrat per meridiem, et flectitur ad aquilonem. Lustrans universa in circuitu pergit spiritus, et in circulos suos revertitur.
     EN: it turns through the south and bends toward the north. Surveying everything, the wind goes on its circuit and returns to its circles.

1:7  LA: Omnia flumina intrant in mare, et mare non redundat ; ad locum unde exeunt flumina revertuntur ut iterum fluant.
     EN: All rivers enter the sea, and the sea does not overflow. The rivers return to the place from which they go out, to flow again.

1:8  LA: Cunctæ res difficiles ; non potest eas homo explicare sermone. Non saturatur oculus visu, nec auris auditu impletur.
     EN: All things are difficult; a person cannot explain them in speech. The eye is not satisfied with seeing, nor is the ear filled with hearing.

1:9  LA: Quid est quod fuit ? Ipsum quod futurum est. Quid est quod factum est ? Ipsum quod faciendum est.
     EN: What is it that has been? The very thing that will be. What is it that has been done? The very thing that is to be done.

1:10  LA: Nihil sub sole novum, nec valet quisquam dicere : Ecce hoc recens est : jam enim præcessit in sæculis quæ fuerunt ante nos.
      EN: There is nothing new under the sun, nor can anyone say, “Look, this is new!” For it has already gone before in the ages that were before us.

1:11  LA: Non est priorum memoria ; sed nec eorum quidem quæ postea futura sunt erit recordatio apud eos qui futuri sunt in novissimo.]
      EN: There is no memory of former things; neither will there be remembrance of the things that are yet to come among those who will come last.

1:12  LA: [Ego Ecclesiastes fui rex Israël in Jerusalem ;
      EN: I, Ecclesiastes, was king of Israel in Jerusalem,

1:13  LA: et proposui in animo meo quærere et investigare sapienter de omnibus quæ fiunt sub sole. Hanc occupationem pessimam dedit Deus filiis hominum, ut occuparentur in ea.
      EN: and I resolved in my mind to seek out and investigate wisely everything done under the sun. God has given this most wretched occupation to the children of men, to occupy them with it.

1:14  LA: Vidi cuncta quæ fiunt sub sole, et ecce universa vanitas et afflictio spiritus.
      EN: I saw everything done under the sun, and look, it is all futility and affliction of spirit.

1:15  LA: Perversi difficile corriguntur, et stultorum infinitus est numerus.
      EN: The perverse are corrected with difficulty, and the number of fools is infinite.

1:16  LA: Locutus sum in corde meo, dicens : Ecce magnus effectus sum, et præcessi omnes sapientia qui fuerunt ante me in Jerusalem ; et mens mea contemplata est multa sapienter, et didici.
      EN: I spoke in my heart, saying, “Look, I have become great and have surpassed in wisdom all who were before me in Jerusalem.” My mind has contemplated many things wisely, and I have learned.

1:17  LA: Dedique cor meum ut scirem prudentiam atque doctrinam, erroresque et stultitiam ; et agnovi quod in his quoque esset labor et afflictio spiritus :
      EN: I gave my heart to knowing prudence and learning, errors and foolishness, and I recognized that in these too there was labor and affliction of spirit,

1:18  LA: eo quod in multa sapientia multa sit indignatio ; et qui addit scientiam, addit et laborem.]
      EN: because in much wisdom there is much indignation, and whoever adds knowledge adds labor as well.

ECCLESIASTES 2

2:1  LA: Dixi ego in corde meo : Vadam, et affluam deliciis, et fruar bonis ; et vidi quod hoc quoque esset vanitas.
     EN: I said in my heart, “I will go and abound in delights and enjoy good things.” And I saw that this too was futility.

2:2  LA: Risum reputavi errorem, et gaudio dixi : Quid frustra deciperis ?
     EN: I reckoned laughter an error, and to joy I said, “Why are you deceived to no purpose?”

2:3  LA: Cogitavi in corde meo abstrahere a vino carnem meam, ut animam meam transferrem ad sapientiam, devitaremque stultitiam, donec viderem quid esset utile filiis hominum, quo facto opus est sub sole numero dierum vitæ suæ.
     EN: I considered in my heart withdrawing my flesh from wine, so that I might turn my soul toward wisdom and avoid foolishness, until I saw what was useful for the children of men, what they need to do under the sun during the number of the days of their lives.

2:4  LA: Magnificavi opera mea, ædificavi mihi domos, et plantavi vineas ;
     EN: I made my works great: I built houses for myself and planted vineyards.

2:5  LA: feci hortos et pomaria, et consevi ea cuncti generis arboribus ;
     EN: I made gardens and orchards and planted them with every kind of tree.

2:6  LA: et exstruxi mihi piscinas aquarum, ut irrigarem silvam lignorum germinantium.
     EN: I built pools of water for myself to irrigate a woodland of growing trees.

2:7  LA: Possedi servos et ancillas, multamque familiam habui : armenta quoque, et magnos ovium greges, ultra omnes qui fuerunt ante me in Jerusalem ;
     EN: I possessed male and female slaves and had a large household, as well as herds and great flocks of sheep, beyond all who had been before me in Jerusalem.

2:8  LA: coacervavi mihi argentum et aurum, et substantias regum ac provinciarum ; feci mihi cantores et cantatrices, et delicias filiorum hominum, scyphos, et urceos in ministerio ad vina fundenda ;
     EN: I heaped up silver and gold for myself and the wealth of kings and provinces. I acquired male and female singers and the delights of the children of men: cups and pitchers for service in pouring wine.

2:9  LA: et supergressus sum opibus omnes qui ante me fuerunt in Jerusalem : sapientia quoque perseveravit mecum.
     EN: I surpassed in wealth all who had been before me in Jerusalem, and wisdom also remained with me.

2:10  LA: Et omnia quæ desideraverunt oculi mei non negavi eis, nec prohibui cor meum quin omni voluptate frueretur, et oblectaret se in his quæ præparaveram ; et hanc ratus sum partem meam si uterer labore meo.
      EN: I denied my eyes nothing they desired, nor did I restrain my heart from enjoying every pleasure and delighting in the things I had prepared. I reckoned this my portion, if I could make use of my labor.

2:11  LA: Cumque me convertissem ad universa opera quæ fecerant manus meæ, et ad labores in quibus frustra sudaveram, vidi in omnibus vanitatem et afflictionem animi, et nihil permanere sub sole.]
      EN: When I turned to all the works my hands had made and to the labors at which I had sweated in vain, I saw futility and affliction of mind in them all, and that nothing endures under the sun.

2:12  LA: [Transivi ad contemplandam sapientiam, erroresque, et stultitiam. (Quid est, inquam, homo, ut sequi possit regem, factorem suum ?)
      EN: I turned to contemplate wisdom, errors, and foolishness. What is a man, I ask, that he can follow the king, his maker?

2:13  LA: Et vidi quod tantum præcederet sapientia stultitiam, quantum differt lux a tenebris.
      EN: I saw that wisdom surpasses foolishness as much as light differs from darkness.

2:14  LA: Sapientis oculi in capite ejus ; stultus in tenebris ambulat : et didici quod unus utriusque esset interitus.
      EN: The wise man's eyes are in his head; the fool walks in darkness. Yet I learned that the same destruction awaits them both.

2:15  LA: Et dixi in corde meo : Si unus et stulti et meus occasus erit, quid mihi prodest quod majorem sapientiæ dedi operam ? Locutusque cum mente mea, animadverti quod hoc quoque esset vanitas.
      EN: I said in my heart, “If the fool and I will meet the same end, what benefit is there to me in having applied myself more to wisdom?” Speaking with my mind, I perceived that this too was futility.

2:16  LA: Non enim erit memoria sapientis similiter ut stulti in perpetuum, et futura tempora oblivione cuncta pariter operient : moritur doctus similiter ut indoctus.
      EN: For there will be no everlasting memory of the wise man, just as there is none of the fool, and the times to come will cover everything alike with forgetfulness. The learned dies just like the unlearned.

2:17  LA: Et idcirco tæduit me vitæ meæ, videntem mala universa esse sub sole, et cuncta vanitatem et afflictionem spiritus.]
      EN: And so I grew weary of my life, seeing that everything under the sun is evil, and that everything is futility and affliction of spirit.

2:18  LA: [Rursus detestatus sum omnem industriam meam, qua sub sole studiosissime laboravi, habiturus hæredem post me,
      EN: Again I detested all my industry, at which I had labored most earnestly under the sun, since I would have an heir after me,

2:19  LA: quem ignoro utrum sapiens an stultus futurus sit, et dominabitur in laboribus meis, quibus desudavi et sollicitus fui : et est quidquam tam vanum ?
      EN: and I do not know whether he will be wise or foolish. He will rule over the fruits of my labors, at which I sweated and took pains. Is anything so futile?

2:20  LA: Unde cessavi, renuntiavitque cor meum ultra laborare sub sole.
      EN: So I stopped, and my heart renounced further labor under the sun.

2:21  LA: Nam cum alius laboret in sapientia, et doctrina, et sollicitudine, homini otioso quæsita dimittit ; et hoc ergo vanitas et magnum malum.
      EN: For when one person labors with wisdom, learning, and care, he leaves what he has gained to an idle man. This too, then, is futility and a great evil.

2:22  LA: Quid enim proderit homini de universo labore suo, et afflictione spiritus, qua sub sole cruciatus est ?
      EN: For what benefit will a person have from all his labor and the affliction of spirit with which he was tormented under the sun?

2:23  LA: Cuncti dies ejus doloribus et ærumnis pleni sunt, nec per noctem mente requiescit. Et hoc nonne vanitas est ?
      EN: All his days are full of pains and troubles, and even at night his mind does not rest. Is this not futility too?

2:24  LA: Nonne melius est comedere et bibere, et ostendere animæ suæ bona de laboribus suis ? et hoc de manu Dei est.
      EN: Is it not better to eat and drink and show one's soul good things from one's labors? This too is from the hand of God.

2:25  LA: Quis ita devorabit et deliciis affluet ut ego ?
      EN: Who will devour so much and abound in delights as I do?

2:26  LA: Homini bono in conspectu suo dedit Deus sapientiam, et scientiam, et lætitiam ; peccatori autem dedit afflictionem et curam superfluam, ut addat, et congreget, et tradat ei qui placuit Deo ; sed et hoc vanitas est, et cassa sollicitudo mentis.]
      EN: To the person who is good in His sight, God has given wisdom, knowledge, and joy; but to the sinner He has given affliction and needless care, to add and gather and hand over to the one who has pleased God. Yet this too is futility and empty anxiety of mind.

ECCLESIASTES 3

3:1  LA: Omnia tempus habent, et suis spatiis transeunt universa sub cælo.
     EN: Everything has its time, and all things under heaven pass through their own intervals.

3:2  LA: Tempus nascendi, et tempus moriendi ; tempus plantandi, et tempus evellendi quod plantatum est.
     EN: A time to be born and a time to die; a time to plant and a time to uproot what has been planted.

3:3  LA: Tempus occidendi, et tempus sanandi ; tempus destruendi, et tempus ædificandi.
     EN: A time to kill and a time to heal; a time to destroy and a time to build.

3:4  LA: Tempus flendi, et tempus ridendi ; tempus plangendi, et tempus saltandi.
     EN: A time to weep and a time to laugh; a time to mourn and a time to dance.

3:5  LA: Tempus spargendi lapides, et tempus colligendi, tempus amplexandi, et tempus longe fieri ab amplexibus.
     EN: A time to scatter stones and a time to gather them; a time to embrace and a time to remain far from embraces.

3:6  LA: Tempus acquirendi, et tempus perdendi ; tempus custodiendi, et tempus abjiciendi.
     EN: A time to acquire and a time to lose; a time to keep and a time to throw away.

3:7  LA: Tempus scindendi, et tempus consuendi ; tempus tacendi, et tempus loquendi.
     EN: A time to tear and a time to sew; a time to be silent and a time to speak.

3:8  LA: Tempus dilectionis, et tempus odii ; tempus belli, et tempus pacis.]
     EN: A time for love and a time for hatred; a time for war and a time for peace.

3:9  LA: [Quid habet amplius homo de labore suo ?
     EN: What more does a person have from his labor?

3:10  LA: Vidi afflictionem quam dedit Deus filiis hominum, ut distendantur in ea.
      EN: I have seen the affliction God has given the children of men, to keep them occupied with it.

3:11  LA: Cuncta fecit bona in tempore suo, et mundum tradidit disputationi eorum, ut non inveniat homo opus quod operatus est Deus ab initio usque ad finem.
      EN: He has made everything good in its time and has handed the world over to their inquiry, so that a person cannot discover the work God has done from beginning to end.

3:12  LA: Et cognovi quod non esset melius nisi lætari, et facere bene in vita sua ;
      EN: I recognized that there was nothing better than to rejoice and do good in one's life.

3:13  LA: omnis enim homo qui comedit et bibit, et videt bonum de labore suo, hoc donum Dei est.
      EN: For every person who eats and drinks and sees good from his labor—this is a gift of God.

3:14  LA: Didici quod omnia opera quæ fecit Deus perseverent in perpetuum ; non possumus eis quidquam addere, nec auferre, quæ fecit Deus ut timeatur.
      EN: I learned that all the works God has done endure forever. We cannot add anything to them or take anything away from the things God has done so that He may be feared.

3:15  LA: Quod factum est, ipsum permanet ; quæ futura sunt jam fuerunt, et Deus instaurat quod abiit.]
      EN: What has been done remains; the things to come have already been, and God restores what has passed away.

3:16  LA: [Vidi sub sole in loco judicii impietatem, et in loco justitiæ iniquitatem :
      EN: I saw wickedness under the sun in the place of judgment, and iniquity in the place of justice.

3:17  LA: et dixi in corde meo : Justum et impium judicabit Deus, et tempus omnis rei tunc erit.
      EN: I said in my heart, “God will judge the just and the wicked, and then there will be a time for everything.”

3:18  LA: Dixi in corde meo de filiis hominum, ut probaret eos Deus, et ostenderet similes esse bestiis.
      EN: I said in my heart concerning the children of men that God should test them and show that they are like beasts.

3:19  LA: Idcirco unus interitus est hominis et jumentorum, et æqua utriusque conditio. Sicut moritur homo, sic et illa moriuntur. Similiter spirant omnia, et nihil habet homo jumento amplius : cuncta subjacent vanitati,
      EN: For this reason the same destruction awaits humans and beasts of burden, and the condition of both is equal. As a human dies, so do they die. All breathe alike, and a human has nothing more than a beast of burden: everything is subject to futility,

3:20  LA: et omnia pergunt ad unum locum. De terra facta sunt, et in terram pariter revertuntur.
      EN: and everything goes to one place. They were made from the earth, and to the earth they return alike.

3:21  LA: Quis novit si spiritus filiorum Adam ascendat sursum, et si spiritus jumentorum descendat deorsum ?
      EN: Who knows whether the spirit of the children of Adam ascends above, and whether the spirit of the beasts of burden descends below?

3:22  LA: Et deprehendi nihil esse melius quam lætari hominem in opere suo, et hanc esse partem illius. Quis enim eum adducet ut post se futura cognoscat ?]
      EN: I found that there is nothing better than for a person to rejoice in his work, and that this is his portion. For who will bring him to know what will happen after him?

ECCLESIASTES 4

4:1  LA: Verti me ad alia, et vidi calumnias quæ sub sole geruntur, et lacrimas innocentium, et neminem consolatorem, nec posse resistere eorum violentiæ, cunctorum auxilio destitutos,
     EN: I turned to other things and saw the oppression carried on under the sun, the tears of the innocent, and no one to comfort them. They could not resist their oppressors' violence, abandoned by everyone who might help,

4:2  LA: et laudavi magis mortuos quam viventes ;
     EN: and I praised the dead more than the living.

4:3  LA: et feliciorem utroque judicavi qui necdum natus est, nec vidit mala quæ sub sole fiunt.
     EN: I judged happier than either the one who has not yet been born and has not seen the evils done under the sun.

4:4  LA: Rursum contemplatus sum omnes labores hominum, et industrias animadverti patere invidiæ proximi ; et in hoc ergo vanitas et cura superflua est.
     EN: Again I contemplated all people's labors and noticed that their industry is exposed to their neighbor's envy. In this too, then, there is futility and needless care.

4:5  LA: Stultus complicat manus suas, et comedit carnes suas, dicens :
     EN: The fool folds his hands and eats his own flesh, saying,

4:6  LA: Melior est pugillus cum requie, quam plena utraque manus cum labore et afflictione animi.
     EN: “One handful with rest is better than both hands full with labor and affliction of mind.”

4:7  LA: Considerans, reperi et aliam vanitatem sub sole.
     EN: As I considered, I found another futility under the sun.

4:8  LA: Unus est, et secundum non habet, non filium, non fratrem, et tamen laborare non cessat, nec satiantur oculi ejus divitiis ; nec recogitat, dicens : Cui laboro, et fraudo animam meam bonis ? In hoc quoque vanitas est et afflictio pessima.]
     EN: There is one person, and he has no second, no son or brother; yet he does not cease laboring, nor are his eyes satisfied with riches. He does not stop to think, saying, “For whom am I laboring and depriving my soul of good things?” In this too there is futility and most wretched affliction.

4:9  LA: [Melius est ergo duos esse simul quam unum ; habent enim emolumentum societatis suæ.
     EN: It is better, then, for two to be together than for one to be alone, for they have the benefit of their companionship.

4:10  LA: Si unus ceciderit, ab altero fulcietur. Væ soli, quia cum ceciderit, non habet sublevantem se.
      EN: If one falls, the other will support him. Woe to the one alone, because when he falls he has no one to lift him up.

4:11  LA: Et si dormierint duo, fovebuntur mutuo ; unus quomodo calefiet ?
      EN: If two sleep together, they will keep one another warm; how will one alone grow warm?

4:12  LA: Et si quispiam prævaluerit contra unum, duo resistunt ei ; funiculus triplex difficile rumpitur.
      EN: If someone overpowers one, two resist him. A threefold cord is broken with difficulty.

4:13  LA: Melior est puer pauper et sapiens, rege sene et stulto, qui nescit prævidere in posterum.
      EN: A poor and wise boy is better than an old and foolish king who does not know how to foresee the future.

4:14  LA: Quod de carcere catenisque interdum quis egrediatur ad regnum ; et alius, natus in regno, inopia consumatur.
      EN: For sometimes someone goes out from prison and chains to a kingdom, while another, born in a kingdom, is consumed by poverty.

4:15  LA: Vidi cunctos viventes qui ambulant sub sole cum adolescente secundo, qui consurget pro eo.
      EN: I saw all the living who walk under the sun with the second young man, who will rise in his place.

4:16  LA: Infinitus numerus est populi omnium qui fuerunt ante eum, et qui postea futuri sunt non lætabuntur in eo ; sed et hoc vanitas et afflictio spiritus.]
      EN: The number of the people, all who were before him, is infinite, and those who will come afterward will not rejoice in him. Yet this too is futility and affliction of spirit.

4:17  LA: [Custodi pedem tuum ingrediens domum Dei, et appropinqua ut audias. Multo enim melior est obedientia quam stultorum victimæ, qui nesciunt quid faciunt mali.]
      EN: Guard your foot as you enter the house of God, and draw near to listen. For obedience is much better than the sacrifices of fools, who do not know what evil they do.

ECCLESIASTES 5

5:1  LA: Ne temere quid loquaris, neque cor tuum sit velox ad proferendum sermonem coram Deo. Deus enim in cælo, et tu super terram ; idcirco sint pauci sermones tui.
     EN: Do not speak rashly, nor let your heart be quick to utter a word before God. For God is in heaven, and you are on earth; therefore let your words be few.

5:2  LA: Multas curas sequuntur somnia, et in multis sermonibus invenietur stultitia.
     EN: Dreams follow many cares, and foolishness will be found in many words.

5:3  LA: Si quid vovisti Deo, ne moreris reddere : displicet enim ei infidelis et stulta promissio, sed quodcumque voveris redde :
     EN: If you have vowed anything to God, do not delay in paying it, for an unfaithful and foolish promise displeases Him. Whatever you have vowed, pay it.

5:4  LA: multoque melius est non vovere, quam post votum promissa non reddere.
     EN: It is much better not to vow than to make a vow and not pay what was promised.

5:5  LA: Ne dederis os tuum ut peccare facias carnem tuam, neque dicas coram angelo : Non est providentia : ne forte iratus Deus contra sermones tuos dissipet cuncta opera manuum tuarum.
     EN: Do not yield your mouth so as to make your flesh sin, nor say before the angel, “There is no providence,” lest God become angry at your words and scatter all the works of your hands.

5:6  LA: Ubi multa sunt somnia, plurimæ sunt vanitates, et sermones innumeri ; tu vero Deum time.]
     EN: Where there are many dreams, there are very many futilities and countless words; but you, fear God.

5:7  LA: [Si videris calumnias egenorum, et violenta judicia, et subverti justitiam in provincia, non mireris super hoc negotio : quia excelso excelsior est alius, et super hos quoque eminentiores sunt alii ;
     EN: If you see the oppression of the needy, violent judgments, and justice overturned in a province, do not marvel at this affair, for there is another higher than the high one, and above these there are still others more exalted.

5:8  LA: et insuper universæ terræ rex imperat servienti.
     EN: And above all, the king commands the whole land that serves him.

5:9  LA: Avarus non implebitur pecunia, et qui amat divitias fructum non capiet ex eis ; et hoc ergo vanitas.
     EN: The greedy man will not be filled with money, and the one who loves riches will reap no fruit from them. This too, then, is futility.

5:10  LA: Ubi multæ sunt opes, multi et qui comedunt eas. Et quid prodest possessori, nisi quod cernit divitias oculis suis ?
      EN: Where there is much wealth, there are also many who eat it. What benefit does its possessor have, except that he sees the riches with his eyes?

5:11  LA: Dulcis est somnus operanti, sive parum sive multum comedat ; saturitas autem divitis non sinit eum dormire.]
      EN: Sweet is the sleep of the laborer, whether he eats little or much, but the rich man's fullness does not let him sleep.

5:12  LA: [Est et alia infirmitas pessima quam vidi sub sole : divitiæ conservatæ in malum domini sui.
      EN: There is another most wretched infirmity that I saw under the sun: riches kept to the harm of their owner.

5:13  LA: Pereunt enim in afflictione pessima : generavit filium qui in summa egestate erit.
      EN: For they perish in most wretched affliction; he has fathered a son who will be in utter poverty.

5:14  LA: Sicut egressus est nudus de utero matris suæ, sic revertetur, et nihil auferet secum de labore suo.
      EN: As he came naked from his mother's womb, so he will return, and he will carry nothing with him from his labor.

5:15  LA: Miserabilis prorsus infirmitas : quomodo venit, sic revertetur. Quid ergo prodest ei quod laboravit in ventum ?
      EN: An utterly miserable infirmity: as he came, so he will return. What benefit, then, does he have from laboring for the wind?

5:16  LA: cunctis diebus vitæ suæ comedit in tenebris, et in curis multis, et in ærumna atque tristitia.]
      EN: All the days of his life he eats in darkness, with many cares, and in trouble and sorrow.

5:17  LA: [Hoc itaque visum est mihi bonum, ut comedat quis et bibat, et fruatur lætitia ex labore suo quo laboravit ipse sub sole, numero dierum vitæ suæ quos dedit ei Deus ; et hæc est pars illius.
      EN: This, then, seemed good to me: that a person should eat and drink and enjoy gladness from the labor at which he himself has labored under the sun, during the number of days of his life that God has given him. This is his portion.

5:18  LA: Et omni homini cui dedit Deus divitias atque substantiam, potestatemque ei tribuit ut comedat ex eis, et fruatur parte sua, et lætetur de labore suo : hoc est donum Dei.
      EN: For every person to whom God has given riches and substance, granting him the power to eat from them, enjoy his portion, and rejoice in his labor—this is a gift of God.

5:19  LA: Non enim satis recordabitur dierum vitæ suæ, eo quod Deus occupet deliciis cor ejus.]
      EN: For he will scarcely remember the days of his life, because God occupies his heart with delights.

ECCLESIASTES 6

6:1  LA: Est et aliud malum quod vidi sub sole, et quidem frequens apud homines :
     EN: There is another evil that I saw under the sun, and indeed it is common among people:

6:2  LA: vir cui dedit Deus divitias, et substantiam, et honorem, et nihil deest animæ suæ ex omnibus quæ desiderat ; nec tribuit ei potestatem Deus ut comedat ex eo, sed homo extraneus vorabit illud : hoc vanitas et miseria magna est.
     EN: a man to whom God has given riches, substance, and honor, so that his soul lacks nothing of all it desires, yet God has not granted him the power to eat from it, and a stranger will devour it. This is futility and great misery.

6:3  LA: Si genuerit quispiam centum liberos, et vixerit multos annos, et plures dies ætatis habuerit, et anima illius non utatur bonis substantiæ suæ, sepulturaque careat : de hoc ergo pronuntio quod melior illo sit abortivus.
     EN: If someone fathers a hundred children, lives many years, and has many days in his lifetime, but his soul does not enjoy the good things of his substance and he lacks burial, then I declare that a stillborn child is better off than he is.

6:4  LA: Frustra enim venit, et pergit ad tenebras, et oblivione delebitur nomen ejus.
     EN: For it comes to no purpose and goes into darkness, and its name will be erased by forgetfulness.

6:5  LA: Non vidit solem, neque cognovit distantiam boni et mali.
     EN: It has not seen the sun or known the distinction between good and evil.

6:6  LA: Etiam si duobus millibus annis vixerit, et non fuerit perfruitus bonis, nonne ad unum locum properant omnia ?
     EN: Even if he lives two thousand years and has not fully enjoyed good things, do not all things hasten to one place?

6:7  LA: Omnis labor hominis in ore ejus ; sed anima ejus non implebitur.
     EN: All a person's labor is for his mouth, but his soul is not filled.

6:8  LA: Quid habet amplius sapiens a stulto ? et quid pauper, nisi ut pergat illuc ubi est vita ?
     EN: What more does the wise man have than the fool? And what does the poor man have, except to go where life is?

6:9  LA: Melius est videre quod cupias, quam desiderare quod nescias. Sed et hoc vanitas est, et præsumptio spiritus.
     EN: It is better to see what you desire than to desire what you do not know. Yet this too is futility and presumption of spirit.

6:10  LA: Qui futurus est, jam vocatum est nomen ejus ; et scitur quod homo sit, et non possit contra fortiorem se in judicio contendere.
      EN: Whoever is to come has already been named, and it is known that he is a man and cannot contend in judgment against one stronger than himself.

6:11  LA: Verba sunt plurima, multamque in disputando habentia vanitatem.]
      EN: There are very many words, with much futility in their disputing.

6:12  LA: Quid necesse est homini majora se quærere, cum ignoret quid conducat sibi in vita sua, numero dierum peregrinationis suæ, et tempore quod velut umbra præterit ? aut quis ei poterit indicare quod post eum futurum sub sole sit ?
      EN: What need is there for a person to seek things greater than himself, when he does not know what benefits him in his life, during the number of days of his pilgrimage and the time that passes like a shadow? Or who can tell him what will happen under the sun after him?

ECCLESIASTES 7

7:1  LA: Melius est nomen bonum quam unguenta pretiosa, et dies mortis die nativitatis.
     EN: A good name is better than precious ointments, and the day of death than the day of birth.

7:2  LA: Melius est ire ad domum luctus quam ad domum convivii ; in illa enim finis cunctorum admonetur hominum, et vivens cogitat quid futurum sit.
     EN: It is better to go to the house of mourning than to the house of feasting, for in the former the end of all people is called to mind, and the living person considers what is to come.

7:3  LA: Melior est ira risu, quia per tristitiam vultus corrigitur animus delinquentis.
     EN: Anger is better than laughter, because through a sorrowful face the mind of the offender is corrected.

7:4  LA: Cor sapientium ubi tristitia est, et cor stultorum ubi lætitia.
     EN: The heart of the wise is where there is sorrow, and the heart of fools where there is gladness.

7:5  LA: Melius est a sapiente corripi, quam stultorum adulatione decipi ;
     EN: It is better to be rebuked by a wise man than deceived by the flattery of fools,

7:6  LA: quia sicut sonitus spinarum ardentium sub olla, sic risus stulti. Sed et hoc vanitas.
     EN: for like the sound of thorns burning under a pot, so is the fool's laughter. Yet this too is futility.

7:7  LA: Calumnia conturbat sapientem, et perdet robur cordis illius.
     EN: Oppression disturbs the wise man and will destroy the strength of his heart.

7:8  LA: Melior est finis orationis quam principium. Melior est patiens arrogante.
     EN: The end of a speech is better than its beginning. The patient person is better than the arrogant.

7:9  LA: Ne sis velox ad irascendum, quia ira in sinu stulti requiescit.
     EN: Do not be quick to become angry, because anger rests in the fool's lap.

7:10  LA: Ne dicas : Quid putas causæ est quod priora tempora meliora fuere quam nunc sunt ? stulta enim est hujuscemodi interrogatio.
      EN: Do not say, “What do you think is the reason former times were better than they are now?” For a question of this kind is foolish.

7:11  LA: Utilior est sapientia cum divitiis, et magis prodest videntibus solem.
      EN: Wisdom with riches is more useful and benefits those who see the sun more.

7:12  LA: Sicut enim protegit sapientia, sic protegit pecunia ; hoc autem plus habet eruditio et sapientia, quod vitam tribuunt possessori suo.
      EN: For as wisdom protects, so does money; but learning and wisdom have this advantage, that they give life to their possessor.

7:13  LA: Considera opera Dei, quod nemo possit corrigere quem ille despexerit.
      EN: Consider the works of God: no one can correct someone whom He has looked down upon.

7:14  LA: In die bona fruere bonis, et malam diem præcave ; sicut enim hanc, sic et illam fecit Deus, ut non inveniat homo contra eum justas querimonias.
      EN: On a good day enjoy good things, and guard against an evil day; for God has made the one as well as the other, so that a person may find no just complaints against Him.

7:15  LA: Hæc quoque vidi in diebus vanitatis meæ : justus perit in justitia sua, et impius multo vivit tempore in malitia sua.
      EN: I have also seen these things in the days of my futility: the just man perishes in his justice, and the wicked man lives a long time in his malice.

7:16  LA: Noli esse justus multum, neque plus sapias quam necesse est, ne obstupescas.
      EN: Do not be excessively just, nor be wiser than necessary, lest you be left bewildered.

7:17  LA: Ne impie agas multum, et noli esse stultus, ne moriaris in tempore non tuo.
      EN: Do not act wickedly to excess, and do not be foolish, lest you die before your time.

7:18  LA: Bonum est te sustentare justum : sed et ab illo ne subtrahas manum tuam ; quia qui timet Deum nihil negligit.
      EN: It is good for you to support the just man, but do not withdraw your hand from him either, because the one who fears God neglects nothing.

7:19  LA: Sapientia confortavit sapientem super decem principes civitatis ;
      EN: Wisdom has strengthened the wise man beyond ten rulers of a city,

7:20  LA: non est enim homo justus in terra qui faciat bonum et non peccet.
      EN: for there is no just person on earth who does good and does not sin.

7:21  LA: Sed et cunctis sermonibus qui dicuntur ne accomodes cor tuum, ne forte audias servum tuum maledicentem tibi ;
      EN: Do not give your heart to every word that is spoken, lest you hear your slave cursing you,

7:22  LA: scit enim conscientia tua quia et tu crebro maledixisti aliis.
      EN: for your conscience knows that you too have often cursed others.

7:23  LA: Cuncta tentavi in sapientia. Dixi : Sapiens efficiar : et ipsa longius recessit a me,
      EN: I tested everything in wisdom. I said, “I will become wise,” and wisdom withdrew farther from me,

7:24  LA: multo magis quam erat. Et alta profunditas, quis inveniet eam ?]
      EN: much farther than it had been. And the deep depth—who will discover it?

7:25  LA: [Lustravi universa animo meo, ut scirem et considerarem, et quærerem sapientiam, et rationem, et ut cognoscerem impietatem stulti, et errorem imprudentium :
      EN: I surveyed everything in my mind, to know and consider and seek wisdom and reason, and to recognize the wickedness of the fool and the error of the imprudent.

7:26  LA: et inveni amariorem morte mulierem, quæ laqueus venatorum est, et sagena cor ejus ; vincula sunt manus illius. Qui placet Deo effugiet illam ; qui autem peccator est capietur ab illa.
      EN: I found more bitter than death the woman who is a hunters' snare and whose heart is a net; her hands are chains. Whoever pleases God will escape her, but the sinner will be caught by her.

7:27  LA: Ecce hoc inveni, dixit Ecclesiastes, unum et alterum ut invenirem rationem,
      EN: “Look, this I found,” said Ecclesiastes, “one thing and another, to find the reason,

7:28  LA: quam adhuc quærit anima mea, et non inveni. Virum de mille unum reperi ; mulierem ex omnibus non inveni.
      EN: which my soul still seeks, and I have not found. One man among a thousand I found; a woman among them all I did not find.

7:29  LA: Solummodo hoc inveni, quod fecerit Deus hominem rectum, et ipse se infinitis miscuerit quæstionibus. Quis talis ut sapiens est ? et quis cognovit solutionem verbi ?]
      EN: Only this have I found: that God made the human being upright, and he has entangled himself in endless questions. Who is like the wise man? And who knows the solution to a saying?”

ECCLESIASTES 8

8:1  LA: Sapientia hominis lucet in vultu ejus, et potentissimus faciem illius commutabit.
     EN: A person's wisdom shines in his face, and the most powerful one will change his face.

8:2  LA: Ego os regis observo, et præcepta juramenti Dei.
     EN: I observe the king's command and the precepts of the oath of God.

8:3  LA: Ne festines recedere a facie ejus, neque permaneas in opere malo : quia omne quod voluerit faciet.
     EN: Do not hurry to withdraw from his presence, nor persist in an evil work, because he will do whatever he wishes.

8:4  LA: Et sermo illius potestate plenus est, nec dicere ei quisquam potest : Quare ita facis ?
     EN: His word is full of power, and no one can say to him, “Why do you act this way?”

8:5  LA: Qui custodit præceptum non experietur quidquam mali. Tempus et responsionem cor sapientis intelligit.
     EN: Whoever keeps the commandment will experience no evil. The wise heart understands the time and the answer.

8:6  LA: Omni negotio tempus est, et opportunitas : et multa hominis afflictio,
     EN: For every affair there is a time and an opportunity, and a person's affliction is great,

8:7  LA: quia ignorat præterita, et futura nullo scire potest nuntio.
     EN: because he does not know the past and cannot learn the future from any messenger.

8:8  LA: Non est in hominis potestate prohibere spiritum, nec habet potestatem in die mortis : nec sinitur quiescere ingruente bello, neque salvabit impietas impium.]
     EN: It is not in a person's power to restrain the spirit, nor does he have power on the day of death. He is not allowed to rest when war presses in, nor will wickedness save the wicked.

8:9  LA: [Omnia hæc consideravi, et dedi cor meum in cunctis operibus quæ fiunt sub sole. Interdum dominatur homo homini in malum suum.
     EN: I considered all these things and gave my heart to all the works done under the sun. Sometimes one person rules another to his own harm.

8:10  LA: Vidi impios sepultos, qui etiam cum adhuc viverent in loco sancto erant, et laudabantur in civitate quasi justorum operum. Sed et hoc vanitas est.
      EN: I saw the wicked buried, who even while they were still living had been in the holy place and were praised in the city as though their works were just. Yet this too is futility.

8:11  LA: Etenim quia non profertur cito contra malos sententia, absque timore ullo filii hominum perpetrant mala.
      EN: For because sentence against the evil is not pronounced swiftly, the children of men commit evil without any fear.

8:12  LA: Attamen peccator ex eo quod centies facit malum, et per patientiam sustentatur ; ego cognovi quod erit bonum timentibus Deum, qui verentur faciem ejus.
      EN: Yet despite the sinner's doing evil a hundred times and being sustained through patience, I recognized that it will be well with those who fear God, who stand in awe before His face.

8:13  LA: Non sit bonum impio, nec prolongentur dies ejus, sed quasi umbra transeant qui non timent faciem Domini.
      EN: May it not be well with the wicked, nor may his days be prolonged; but let those who do not fear the face of the Lord pass like a shadow.

8:14  LA: Est et alia vanitas quæ fit super terram : sunt justi quibus mala proveniunt quasi opera egerint impiorum : et sunt impii qui ita securi sunt quasi justorum facta habeant. Sed et hoc vanissimum judico.
      EN: There is another futility that occurs on earth: there are just people to whom evils come as though they had performed the works of the wicked, and there are wicked people who are as secure as though they had the deeds of the just. Yet this too I judge utterly futile.

8:15  LA: Laudavi igitur lætitiam ; quod non esset homini bonum sub sole, nisi quod comederet, et biberet, atque gauderet, et hoc solum secum auferret de labore suo, in diebus vitæ suæ quos dedit ei Deus sub sole.
      EN: So I praised gladness: that there is no good for a person under the sun except to eat, drink, and rejoice, and that this alone he takes with him from his labor during the days of his life God has given him under the sun.

8:16  LA: Et apposui cor meum ut scirem sapientiam, et intelligerem distentionem quæ versatur in terra. Est homo qui diebus et noctibus somnum non capit oculis.
      EN: I applied my heart to knowing wisdom and understanding the activity that goes on upon the earth. There is a person whose eyes find no sleep by day or night.

8:17  LA: Et intellexi quod omnium operum Dei nullam possit homo invenire rationem eorum quæ fiunt sub sole ; et quanto plus laboraverit ad quærendum, tanto minus inveniat : etiam si dixerit sapiens se nosse, non poterit reperire.]
      EN: I understood that a person cannot discover the reason for any of God's works that are done under the sun. The more he labors to seek, the less he finds; even if the wise man says he knows, he will not be able to discover it.

ECCLESIASTES 9

9:1  LA: Omnia hæc tractavi in corde meo, ut curiose intelligerem. Sunt justi atque sapientes, et opera eorum in manu Dei ; et tamen nescit homo utrum amore an odio dignus sit.
     EN: I considered all these things in my heart, to understand them carefully. The just and the wise and their works are in the hand of God, yet a person does not know whether he is worthy of love or hatred.

9:2  LA: Sed omnia in futurum servantur incerta, eo quod universa æque eveniant justo et impio, bono et malo, mundo et immundo, immolanti victimas et sacrificia contemnenti. Sicut bonus, sic et peccator ; ut perjurus, ita et ille qui verum dejerat.]
     EN: But everything is kept uncertain for the future, because everything happens equally to the just and the wicked, the good and the evil, the clean and the unclean, the one offering victims and the one despising sacrifices. As it is for the good, so it is for the sinner; as for the perjurer, so for the one who swears truly.

9:3  LA: [Hoc est pessimum inter omnia quæ sub sole fiunt : quia eadem cunctis eveniunt. Unde et corda filiorum hominum implentur malitia et contemptu in vita sua, et post hæc ad inferos deducentur.
     EN: This is the worst of all the things done under the sun: that the same things happen to everyone. So the hearts of the children of men are filled with malice and contempt during their lives, and afterward they will be led down to the underworld.

9:4  LA: Nemo est qui semper vivat, et qui hujus rei habeat fiduciam ; melior est canis vivus leone mortuo.
     EN: There is no one who lives forever or can have confidence in this; a living dog is better than a dead lion.

9:5  LA: Viventes enim sciunt se esse morituros ; mortui vero nihil noverunt amplius, nec habent ultra mercedem, quia oblivioni tradita est memoria eorum.
     EN: For the living know that they will die, but the dead know nothing more and have no further reward, because their memory has been handed over to forgetfulness.

9:6  LA: Amor quoque, et odium, et invidiæ simul perierunt ; nec habent partem in hoc sæculo, et in opere quod sub sole geritur.
     EN: Their love, hatred, and envy have also perished together, and they have no portion in this age or in the work done under the sun.

9:7  LA: Vade ergo, et comede in lætitia panem tuum, et bibe cum gaudio vinum tuum, quia Deo placent opera tua.
     EN: Go, then, and eat your bread in gladness and drink your wine with joy, because your works please God.

9:8  LA: Omni tempore sint vestimenta tua candida, et oleum de capite tuo non deficiat.
     EN: Let your garments be white at all times, and let oil never be lacking on your head.

9:9  LA: Perfruere vita cum uxore quam diligis, cunctis diebus vitæ instabilitatis tuæ, qui dati sunt tibi sub sole omni tempore vanitatis tuæ : hæc est enim pars in vita et in labore tuo quo laboras sub sole.
     EN: Enjoy life fully with the wife you love, all the days of your unstable life that have been given you under the sun, throughout the time of your futility. For this is your portion in life and in the labor at which you labor under the sun.

9:10  LA: Quodcumque facere potest manus tua, instanter operare, quia nec opus, nec ratio, nec sapientia, nec scientia erunt apud inferos, quo tu properas.]
      EN: Whatever your hand can do, do it earnestly, because there will be neither work nor reason nor wisdom nor knowledge in the underworld, to which you are hastening.

9:11  LA: [Verti me ad aliud, et vidi sub sole nec velocium esse cursum, nec fortium bellum, nec sapientium panem, nec doctorum divitias, nec artificum gratiam ; sed tempus casumque in omnibus.
      EN: I turned to another thing and saw under the sun that the race does not belong to the swift, nor the battle to the strong, nor bread to the wise, nor riches to the learned, nor favor to the skilled, but time and chance are in everything.

9:12  LA: Nescit homo finem suum ; sed sicut pisces capiuntur hamo, et sicut aves laqueo comprehenduntur, sic capiuntur homines in tempore malo, cum eis extemplo supervenerit.
      EN: A person does not know his end; but as fish are caught by a hook and birds are taken in a snare, so people are caught at an evil time, when it comes upon them suddenly.

9:13  LA: Hanc quoque sub sole vidi sapientiam, et probavi maximam :
      EN: I also saw this wisdom under the sun and judged it very great:

9:14  LA: civitas parva, et pauci in ea viri ; venit contra eam rex magnus, et vallavit eam, exstruxitque munitiones per gyrum, et perfecta est obsidio.
      EN: there was a small city, with few men in it. A great king came against it, surrounded it, and built fortifications all around, and the siege was complete.

9:15  LA: Inventusque est in ea vir pauper et sapiens, et liberavit urbem per sapientiam suam ; et nullus deinceps recordatus est hominis illius pauperis.
      EN: In it a poor and wise man was found, and he delivered the city by his wisdom, yet afterward no one remembered that poor man.

9:16  LA: Et dicebam ego meliorem esse sapientiam fortitudine. Quomodo ergo sapientia pauperis contempta est, et verba ejus non sunt audita ?
      EN: I said that wisdom was better than strength. Why, then, was the poor man's wisdom despised, and why were his words not heard?

9:17  LA: Verba sapientium audiuntur in silentio, plus quam clamor principis inter stultos.
      EN: The words of the wise are heard in silence, more than the shouting of a ruler among fools.

9:18  LA: Melior est sapientia quam arma bellica ; et qui in uno peccaverit, multa bona perdet.]
      EN: Wisdom is better than weapons of war, and whoever sins in one thing will lose many good things.

ECCLESIASTES 10

10:1  LA: Muscæ morientes perdunt suavitatem unguenti. Pretiosior est sapientia et gloria, parva et ad tempus stultitia.
      EN: Dying flies spoil the sweetness of ointment. Wisdom and glory are more precious than a little, temporary foolishness.

10:2  LA: Cor sapientis in dextera ejus, et cor stulti in sinistra illius.
      EN: The wise man's heart is at his right hand, and the fool's heart at his left.

10:3  LA: Sed et in via stultus ambulans, cum ipse insipiens sit, omnes stultos æstimat.
      EN: Even when the fool walks along the road, though he himself is senseless, he considers everyone else foolish.

10:4  LA: Si spiritus potestatem habentis ascenderit super te, locum tuum ne demiseris, quia curatio faciet cessare peccata maxima.]
      EN: If the spirit of one who has power rises against you, do not abandon your place, because care will bring the greatest sins to an end.

10:5  LA: [Est malum quod vidi sub sole, quasi per errorem egrediens a facie principis :
      EN: There is an evil I saw under the sun, proceeding as though by an error from the ruler's presence:

10:6  LA: positum stultum in dignitate sublimi, et divites sedere deorsum.
      EN: a fool placed in high dignity, and the rich seated below.

10:7  LA: Vidi servos in equis, et principes ambulantes super terram quasi servos.
      EN: I saw slaves on horses and rulers walking on the ground like slaves.

10:8  LA: Qui fodit foveam incidet in eam, et qui dissipat sepem mordebit eum coluber.
      EN: Whoever digs a pit will fall into it, and whoever breaks down a hedge will be bitten by a snake.

10:9  LA: Qui transfert lapides affligetur in eis, et qui scindit ligna vulnerabitur ab eis.
      EN: Whoever moves stones will be hurt by them, and whoever splits wood will be wounded by it.

10:10  LA: Si retusum fuerit ferrum, et hoc non ut prius, sed hebetatum fuerit, multo labore exacuetur, et post industriam sequetur sapientia.
       EN: If the iron has been blunted and is no longer as before but dull, it will be sharpened with much labor, and wisdom will follow industry.

10:11  LA: Si mordeat serpens in silentio, nihil eo minus habet qui occulte detrahit.
       EN: If a serpent bites in silence, the one who secretly slanders is no better than it.

10:12  LA: Verba oris sapientis gratia, et labia insipientis præcipitabunt eum ;
       EN: The words of the wise man's mouth are grace, and the lips of the senseless will cast him down.

10:13  LA: initium verborum ejus stultitia, et novissimum oris illius error pessimus.
       EN: The beginning of his words is foolishness, and the end of his speech is most wretched error.

10:14  LA: Stultus verba multiplicat. Ignorat homo quid ante se fuerit ; et quid post se futurum sit, quis ei poterit indicare ?
       EN: The fool multiplies words. A person does not know what was before him; who can tell him what will be after him?

10:15  LA: Labor stultorum affliget eos, qui nesciunt in urbem pergere.]
       EN: The labor of fools will afflict them, for they do not know how to go into the city.

10:16  LA: [Væ tibi, terra, cujus rex puer est, et cujus principes mane comedunt.
       EN: Woe to you, land whose king is a boy and whose rulers eat in the morning.

10:17  LA: Beata terra cujus rex nobilis est, et cujus principes vescuntur in tempore suo, ad reficiendum, et non ad luxuriam.
       EN: Happy is the land whose king is noble and whose rulers eat at the proper time, to restore themselves and not for indulgence.

10:18  LA: In pigritiis humiliabitur contignatio, et in infirmitate manuum perstillabit domus.
       EN: Through laziness the roof beams will sag, and through weakness of hands the house will leak.

10:19  LA: In risum faciunt panem et vinum ut epulentur viventes ; et pecuniæ obediunt omnia.
       EN: They prepare bread and wine for laughter, so that the living may feast, and everything obeys money.

10:20  LA: In cogitatione tua regi ne detrahas, et in secreto cubiculi tui ne maledixeris diviti : quia et aves cæli portabunt vocem tuam, et qui habet pennas annuntiabit sententiam.]
       EN: Do not disparage the king in your thoughts, and do not curse the rich man in the privacy of your bedchamber, because even the birds of heaven will carry your voice, and whatever has wings will report the statement.

ECCLESIASTES 11

11:1  LA: Mitte panem tuum super transeuntes aquas, quia post tempora multa invenies illum.
      EN: Cast your bread upon the passing waters, because after a long time you will find it.

11:2  LA: Da partem septem necnon et octo, quia ignoras quid futurum sit mali super terram.
      EN: Give a portion to seven, and also to eight, because you do not know what evil will come upon the earth.

11:3  LA: Si repletæ fuerint nubes, imbrem super terram effundent. Si ceciderit lignum ad austrum aut ad aquilonem, in quocumque loco ceciderit, ibi erit.
      EN: If the clouds are full, they will pour rain upon the earth. If a tree falls toward the south or the north, in whatever place it falls, there it will be.

11:4  LA: Qui observat ventum non seminat ; et qui considerat nubes numquam metet.
      EN: Whoever watches the wind does not sow, and whoever studies the clouds will never reap.

11:5  LA: Quomodo ignoras quæ sit via spiritus, et qua ratione compingantur ossa in ventre prægnantis, sic nescis opera Dei, qui fabricator est omnium.
      EN: Just as you do not know the way of the spirit or how the bones are joined together in a pregnant woman's womb, so you do not know the works of God, who is the maker of all things.

11:6  LA: Mane semina semen tuum, et vespere ne cesset manus tua : quia nescis quid magis oriatur, hoc aut illud ; et si utrumque simul, melius erit.]
      EN: In the morning sow your seed, and in the evening do not let your hand cease, because you do not know which will grow better, this or that; and if both grow together, it will be better.

11:7  LA: [Dulce lumen, et delectabile est oculis videre solem.
      EN: Light is sweet, and it is delightful for the eyes to see the sun.

11:8  LA: Si annis multis vixerit homo, et in his omnibus lætatus fuerit, meminisse debet tenebrosi temporis, et dierum multorum, qui cum venerint, vanitatis arguentur præterita.
      EN: If a person lives many years and rejoices in them all, he should remember the dark time and the many days that, when they come, will reveal the futility of the past.

11:9  LA: Lætare ergo, juvenis, in adolescentia tua, et in bono sit cor tuum in diebus juventutis tuæ : et ambula in viis cordis tui, et in intuitu oculorum tuorum, et scito quod pro omnibus his adducet te Deus in judicium.
      EN: Rejoice, then, young man, in your adolescence, and let your heart be well in the days of your youth. Walk in the ways of your heart and in the sight of your eyes, and know that for all these things God will bring you into judgment.

11:10  LA: Aufer iram a corde tuo, et amove malitiam a carne tua : adolescentia enim et voluptas vana sunt.]
       EN: Remove anger from your heart and put evil away from your flesh, for adolescence and pleasure are futile.

ECCLESIASTES 12

12:1  LA: Memento Creatoris tui in diebus juventutis tuæ, antequam veniat tempus afflictionis, et appropinquent anni de quibus dicas : Non mihi placent ;
      EN: Remember your Creator in the days of your youth, before the time of affliction comes and the years approach of which you will say, “They do not please me”;

12:2  LA: antequam tenebrescat sol, et lumen, et luna, et stellæ, et revertantur nubes post pluviam ;
      EN: before the sun and light and moon and stars grow dark, and the clouds return after the rain;

12:3  LA: quando commovebuntur custodes domus, et nutabunt viri fortissimi, et otiosæ erunt molentes in minuto numero, et tenebrescent videntes per foramina ;
      EN: when the guardians of the house tremble, and the strongest men totter, and the women grinding are idle because they are few in number, and those looking through the openings grow dark;

12:4  LA: et claudent ostia in platea, in humilitate vocis molentis, et consurgent ad vocem volucris, et obsurdescent omnes filiæ carminis :
      EN: and they close the doors in the street, when the voice of the woman grinding is low, and they rise at a bird's voice, and all the daughters of song grow deaf.

12:5  LA: excelsa quoque timebunt, et formidabunt in via. Florebit amygdalus, impinguabitur locusta, et dissipabitur capparis, quoniam ibit homo in domum æternitatis suæ, et circuibunt in platea plangentes.
      EN: They will also fear heights and be afraid on the road. The almond tree will blossom, the locust will grow fat, and the caper will scatter, because the human being will go to the house of his eternity and the mourners will go around in the street.

12:6  LA: Antequam rumpatur funiculus argenteus, et recurrat vitta aurea, et conteratur hydria super fontem, et confringatur rota super cisternam,
      EN: Before the silver cord breaks and the golden band draws back, the pitcher is shattered at the spring, and the wheel is broken at the cistern,

12:7  LA: et revertatur pulvis in terram suam unde erat, et spiritus redeat ad Deum, qui dedit illum.
      EN: and the dust returns to its earth, from which it was, and the spirit returns to God, who gave it.

12:8  LA: Vanitas vanitatum, dixit Ecclesiastes, et omnia vanitas.]
      EN: Futility of futilities, said Ecclesiastes, and everything is futility.

12:9  LA: [Cumque esset sapientissimus Ecclesiastes, docuit populum, et enarravit quæ fecerat ; et investigans composuit parabolas multas.
      EN: Since Ecclesiastes was very wise, he taught the people and recounted what he had done; and through investigation he composed many proverbs.

12:10  LA: Quæsivit verba utilia, et conscripsit sermones rectissimos ac veritate plenos.
       EN: He sought useful words and wrote down sayings that were most upright and full of truth.

12:11  LA: Verba sapientium sicut stimuli, et quasi clavi in altum defixi, quæ per magistrorum consilium data sunt a pastore uno.
       EN: The words of the wise are like goads and like nails driven deep, which, through the counsel of teachers, have been given by one shepherd.

12:12  LA: His amplius, fili mi, ne requiras. Faciendi plures libros nullus est finis ; frequensque meditatio, carnis afflictio est.
       EN: Beyond these, my son, seek nothing more. There is no end to making many books, and frequent study is an affliction of the flesh.

12:13  LA: Finem loquendi pariter omnes audiamus. Deum time, et mandata ejus observa : hoc est enim omnis homo,
       EN: Let us all hear together the conclusion of the discourse: fear God and keep His commandments, for this is the whole human being,

12:14  LA: et cuncta quæ fiunt adducet Deus in judicium pro omni errato, sive bonum, sive malum illud sit.]
       EN: and God will bring everything that is done into judgment for every error, whether it is good or evil.
