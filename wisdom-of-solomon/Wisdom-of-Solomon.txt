WISDOM OF SOLOMON 1

1:1    LA: Diligite justitiam, qui judicatis terram. Sentite de Domino in bonitate, et in simplicitate cordis quærite illum :
       EN: Love righteousness, you who judge the earth. Think of the Lord with goodness, and seek Him in simplicity of heart,

1:2    LA: quoniam invenitur ab his qui non tentant illum, apparet autem eis qui fidem habent in illum.
       EN: because He is found by those who do not put Him to the test, and He appears to those who have faith in Him.

1:3    LA: Perversæ enim cogitationes separant a Deo ; probata autem virtus corripit insipientes.
       EN: For crooked thoughts separate people from God, and power, when put to the test, rebukes the foolish.

1:4    LA: Quoniam in malevolam animam non introibit sapientia, nec habitabit in corpore subdito peccatis.
       EN: For wisdom will not enter a malicious soul or dwell in a body subjected to sins.

1:5    LA: Spiritus enim sanctus disciplinæ effugiet fictum, et auferet se a cogitationibus quæ sunt sine intellectu, et corripietur a superveniente iniquitate.
       EN: For the holy spirit of instruction will flee deceit, withdraw from thoughts without understanding, and be checked by the arrival of injustice.

1:6    LA: Benignus est enim spiritus sapientiæ, et non liberabit maledicum a labiis suis : quoniam renum illius testis est Deus, et cordis illius scrutator est verus, et linguæ ejus auditor.
       EN: For the spirit of wisdom is kindly, yet will not release a slanderer from responsibility for his lips, because God is witness to his inward parts, a true examiner of his heart, and a hearer of his tongue.

1:7    LA: Quoniam spiritus Domini replevit orbem terrarum, et hoc quod continet omnia, scientiam habet vocis.
       EN: For the spirit of the Lord has filled the whole earth, and that which holds all things together has knowledge of every voice.

1:8    LA: Propter hoc qui loquitur iniqua non potest latere, nec præteriet illum corripiens judicium.
       EN: Therefore, someone who speaks unjust things cannot remain hidden, nor will reproving judgment pass him by.

1:9    LA: In cogitationibus enim impii interrogatio erit ; sermonum autem illius auditio ad Deum veniet, ad correptionem iniquitatum illius.
       EN: For the thoughts of the ungodly will be examined, and a report of his words will come to God, for the correction of his injustices.

1:10   LA: Quoniam auris zeli audit omnia, et tumultus murmurationum non abscondetur.
       EN: For the ear of jealousy hears everything, and the uproar of grumbling will not be hidden.

1:11   LA: Custodite ergo vos a murmuratione quæ nihil prodest, et a detractione parcite linguæ : quoniam sermo obscurus in vacuum non ibit, os autem quod mentitur occidit animam.]
       EN: Therefore guard yourselves against useless grumbling, and keep your tongue from slander, because a secret word will not pass into emptiness, and a lying mouth kills the soul.

1:12   LA: [Nolite zelare mortem in errore vitæ vestræ, neque acquiratis perditionem in operibus manuum vestrarum.
       EN: Do not eagerly seek death through the error of your life, or acquire destruction through the works of your hands.

1:13   LA: Quoniam Deus mortem non fecit, nec lætatur in perditione vivorum.
       EN: For God did not make death, nor does He delight in the destruction of the living.

1:14   LA: Creavit enim ut essent omnia, et sanabiles fecit nationes orbis terrarum : et non est in illis medicamentum exterminii, nec inferorum regnum in terra.
       EN: For He created all things to exist and made the nations of the world able to be healed. There is no deadly poison in them, nor does the realm of the dead reign on earth.

1:15   LA: Justitia enim perpetua est, et immortalis.
       EN: For righteousness is everlasting and immortal.

1:16   LA: Impii autem manibus et verbis accersierunt illam, et æstimantes illam amicam, defluxerunt ; et sponsiones posuerunt ad illam, quoniam digni sunt qui sint ex parte illius.]
       EN: But the ungodly summoned death with hands and words. Considering her a friend, they wasted away; they made pledges to her, because they deserve to belong to her company.

WISDOM OF SOLOMON 2

2:1    LA: Dixerunt enim cogitantes apud se non recte : Exiguum et cum tædio est tempus vitæ nostræ, et non est refrigerium in fine hominis, et non est qui agnitus sit reversus ab inferis.
       EN: For they said to themselves, reasoning wrongly: “The time of our life is short and wearisome. There is no relief at a person's end, and no one is known to have returned from the realm of the dead.

2:2    LA: Quia ex nihilo nati sumus, et post hoc erimus tamquam non fuerimus. Quoniam fumus flatus est in naribus nostris, et sermo scintilla ad commovendum cor nostrum :
       EN: For we were born from nothing, and afterward we will be as though we had never existed. For the breath in our nostrils is smoke, and speech is a spark that stirs our heart.

2:3    LA: qua extincta, cinis erit corpus nostrum, et spiritus diffundetur tamquam mollis aër ; et transibit vita nostra tamquam vestigium nubis, et sicut nebula dissolvetur quæ fugata est a radiis solis, et a calore illius aggravata.
       EN: When it is extinguished, our body will be ashes, and our spirit will disperse like soft air. Our life will pass like the trace of a cloud and dissolve like mist driven away by the sun’s rays and weighed down by its heat.

2:4    LA: Et nomen nostrum oblivionem accipiet per tempus, et nemo memoriam habebit operum nostrorum.
       EN: Our name will fall into oblivion in time, and no one will remember our works.

2:5    LA: Umbræ enim transitus est tempus nostrum, et non est reversio finis nostri : quoniam consignata est, et nemo revertitur.
       EN: For our time is the passing of a shadow, and there is no reversal of our end, because it is sealed, and no one returns.

2:6    LA: Venite ergo, et fruamur bonis quæ sunt, et utamur creatura tamquam in juventute celeriter.
       EN: Come, then, let us enjoy the good things that exist, and make swift use of creation, as in youth.

2:7    LA: Vino pretioso et unguentis nos impleamus, et non prætereat nos flos temporis.
       EN: Let us fill ourselves with costly wine and perfumes, and let the flower of the season not pass us by.

2:8    LA: Coronemus nos rosis antequam marcescant ; nullum pratum sit quod non pertranseat luxuria nostra :
       EN: Let us crown ourselves with roses before they wither. Let there be no meadow through which our revelry does not pass.

2:9    LA: nemo nostrum exsors sit luxuriæ nostræ. Ubique relinquamus signa lætitiæ, quoniam hæc est pars nostra, et hæc est sors.
       EN: Let none of us be excluded from our revelry. Everywhere let us leave signs of merriment, because this is our portion and this our lot.

2:10   LA: Opprimamus pauperem justum, et non parcamus viduæ, nec veterani revereamur canos multi temporis :
       EN: Let us oppress the righteous poor man, spare no widow, and show no respect for the gray hair of an old man of many years.

2:11   LA: sit autem fortitudo nostra lex justitiæ ; quod enim infirmum est, inutile invenitur.
       EN: Let our strength be the law of righteousness, for what is weak proves useless.

2:12   LA: Circumveniamus ergo justum, quoniam inutilis est nobis, et contrarius est operibus nostris, et improperat nobis peccata legis, et diffamat in nos peccata disciplinæ nostræ.
       EN: So let us lay a trap for the righteous man, because he is no use to us and opposes our works. He reproaches us for sins against the law and publicizes the sins against our instruction.

2:13   LA: Promittit se scientiam Dei habere, et filium Dei se nominat.
       EN: He professes to have knowledge of God and calls himself a son of God.

2:14   LA: Factus est nobis in traductionem cogitationum nostrarum.
       EN: He has become an exposure of our thoughts.

2:15   LA: Gravis est nobis etiam ad videndum, quoniam dissimilis est aliis vita illius, et immutatæ sunt viæ ejus.
       EN: Even seeing him is a burden to us, because his life is unlike other people’s, and his ways are different.

2:16   LA: Tamquam nugaces æstimati sumus ab illo, et abstinet se a viis nostris tamquam ab immunditiis, et præfert novissima justorum, et gloriatur patrem se habere Deum.
       EN: He considers us triflers and keeps away from our ways as from uncleanness. He declares the final end of the righteous preferable, and boasts that he has God as his Father.

2:17   LA: Videamus ergo si sermones illius veri sint, et tentemus quæ ventura sunt illi, et sciemus quæ erunt novissima illius.
       EN: So let us see whether his words are true, test what will happen to him, and learn what his final end will be.

2:18   LA: Si enim est verus filius Dei, suscipiet illum, et liberabit eum de manibus contrariorum.
       EN: For if he is a true son of God, God will take him up and rescue him from the hands of his opponents.

2:19   LA: Contumelia et tormento interrogemus eum, ut sciamus reverentiam ejus, et probemus patientiam illius.
       EN: Let us question him with insult and torture, so that we may learn his reverence and put his patience to the test.

2:20   LA: Morte turpissima condemnemus eum ; erit enim ei respectus ex sermonibus illius.
       EN: Let us condemn him to a most shameful death, for, according to his own words, he will receive care.”

2:21   LA: Hæc cogitaverunt, et erraverunt : excæcavit enim illos malitia eorum.
       EN: They thought these things and went astray, for their malice blinded them.

2:22   LA: Et nescierunt sacramenta Dei : neque mercedem speraverunt justitiæ, nec judicaverunt honorem animarum sanctarum.
       EN: They did not know God's mysteries; they neither hoped for the reward of righteousness nor judged the honor of holy souls rightly.

2:23   LA: Quoniam Deus creavit hominem inexterminabilem, et ad imaginem similitudinis suæ fecit illum.
       EN: For God created the human being indestructible and made him in the image of His own likeness.

2:24   LA: Invidia autem diaboli mors introivit in orbem terrarum : imitantur autem illum qui sunt ex parte illius.]
       EN: But through the devil’s envy, death entered the whole world, and those who belong to his company imitate him.

WISDOM OF SOLOMON 3

3:1    LA: Justorum autem animæ in manu Dei sunt, et non tanget illos tormentum mortis.
       EN: But the souls of the righteous are in God's hand, and the torment of death will not touch them.

3:2    LA: Visi sunt oculis insipientium mori, et æstimata est afflictio exitus illorum,
       EN: In the eyes of the foolish they seemed to die, and their departure was considered an affliction,

3:3    LA: et quod a nobis est iter exterminium ; illi autem sunt in pace :
       EN: and their journey away from us, destruction. But they are at peace.

3:4    LA: etsi coram hominibus tormenta passi sunt, spes illorum immortalitate plena est.
       EN: Even if they suffered torments in human sight, their hope is full of immortality.

3:5    LA: In paucis vexati sunt, in multis bene disponentur, quoniam Deus tentavit eos, et invenit illos dignos se.
       EN: After being troubled in a few things, they will be well provided for in many, because God tested them and found them worthy of Himself.

3:6    LA: Tamquam aurum in fornace probavit illos, et quasi holocausti hostiam accepit illos, et in tempore erit respectus illorum.
       EN: He tested them like gold in a furnace and accepted them like the offering of a whole burnt sacrifice; their visitation will come in due time.

3:7    LA: Fulgebunt justi et tamquam scintillæ in arundineto discurrent.
       EN: The righteous will shine and run about like sparks through a reed bed.

3:8    LA: Judicabunt nationes, et dominabuntur populis, et regnabit Dominus illorum in perpetuum.
       EN: They will judge nations and rule over peoples, and their Lord will reign forever.

3:9    LA: Qui confidunt in illo intelligent veritatem, et fideles in dilectione acquiescent illi, quoniam donum et pax est electis ejus.
       EN: Those who trust in Him will understand truth, and the faithful will rest in Him in love, because gift and peace belong to His chosen ones.

3:10   LA: Impii autem secundum quæ cogitaverunt correptionem habebunt : qui neglexerunt justum, et a Domino recesserunt.
       EN: But the ungodly will receive correction according to their thoughts, those who disregarded the righteous and withdrew from the Lord.

3:11   LA: Sapientiam enim et disciplinam qui abjicit infelix est : et vacua est spes illorum, et labores sine fructu, et inutilia opera eorum.
       EN: For whoever rejects wisdom and instruction is miserable. Their hope is empty, their labors fruitless, and their works useless.

3:12   LA: Mulieres eorum insensatæ sunt, et nequissimi filii eorum.
       EN: Their wives are senseless, and their children utterly wicked.

3:13   LA: Maledicta creatura eorum, quoniam felix est sterilis ; et incoinquinata, quæ nescivit thorum in delicto, habebit fructum in respectione animarum sanctarum ;
       EN: Their offspring is accursed, for blessed is the barren woman; she who is undefiled and has not known a bed in transgression will have fruit at the visitation of holy souls.

3:14   LA: et spado qui non operatus est per manus suas iniquitatem, nec cogitavit adversus Deum nequissima : dabitur enim illi fidei donum electum, et sors in templo Dei acceptissima.
       EN: So too will the eunuch who has done no injustice with his hands and has not thought utterly wicked things against God. For an excellent gift of faithfulness will be given him, and a most welcome portion in God's temple.

3:15   LA: Bonorum enim laborum gloriosus est fructus, et quæ non concidat radix sapientiæ.
       EN: For the fruit of good labors is glorious, and wisdom's root does not fail.

3:16   LA: Filii autem adulterorum in inconsummatione erunt, et ab iniquo thoro semen exterminabitur.
       EN: But the children of adulterers will remain unfulfilled, and the seed from an unlawful bed will be destroyed.

3:17   LA: Et si quidem longæ vitæ erunt, in nihilum computabuntur, et sine honore erit novissima senectus illorum :
       EN: Even if they live long, they will be counted as nothing, and their final old age will be without honor.

3:18   LA: et si celerius defuncti fuerint, non habebunt spem, nec in die agnitionis allocutionem.
       EN: If they die sooner, they will have no hope, nor a word of comfort on the day of examination.

3:19   LA: Nationis enim iniquæ diræ sunt consummationes.]
       EN: For the endings of an unrighteous generation are dreadful.

WISDOM OF SOLOMON 4

4:1    LA: O quam pulchra est casta generatio, cum claritate ! immortalis est enim memoria illius, quoniam et apud Deum nota est, et apud homines.
       EN: Oh, how beautiful is a chaste generation with its radiance! For its memory is immortal, because it is known both to God and to human beings.

4:2    LA: Cum præsens est, imitantur illam, et desiderant eam cum se eduxerit ; et in perpetuum coronata triumphat, incoinquinatorum certaminum præmium vincens.
       EN: When it is present, people imitate it, and they long for it when it has withdrawn. Crowned, it triumphs forever, winning the prize of undefiled contests.

4:3    LA: Multigena autem impiorum multitudo non erit utilis, et spuria vitulamina non dabunt radices altas, nec stabile firmamentum collocabunt.
       EN: But the prolific multitude of the ungodly will be of no use. Illegitimate shoots will not send down deep roots or establish a firm foundation.

4:4    LA: Etsi in ramis in tempore germinaverint, infirmiter posita, a vento commovebuntur, et a nimietate ventorum eradicabuntur.
       EN: Even if for a time they sprout into branches, weakly established, they will be shaken by the wind and uprooted by violent winds.

4:5    LA: Confringentur enim rami inconsummati ; et fructus illorum inutiles et acerbi ad manducandum, et ad nihilum apti.
       EN: For the immature branches will be broken, and their fruit will be useless, unripe for eating, and fit for nothing.

4:6    LA: Ex iniquis enim somnis filii qui nascuntur, testes sunt nequitiæ adversus parentes in interrogatione sua.
       EN: For children born from unlawful sleeping together are witnesses of wickedness against their parents when they are examined.

4:7    LA: Justus autem si morte præoccupatus fuerit, in refrigerio erit ;
       EN: But the righteous person, even if overtaken by death, will be at rest.

4:8    LA: senectus enim venerabilis est non diuturna, neque annorum numero computata : cani autem sunt sensus hominis,
       EN: For honorable old age is not a matter of long duration or a count of years. A person’s understanding is gray hair,

4:9    LA: et ætas senectutis vita immaculata.
       EN: and an unstained life is the age of old age.

4:10   LA: Placens Deo factus est dilectus, et vivens inter peccatores translatus est.
       EN: Pleasing to God, he became beloved, and while living among sinners he was taken away.

4:11   LA: Raptus est, ne malitia mutaret intellectum ejus, aut ne fictio deciperet animam illius.
       EN: He was snatched away so that malice would not alter his understanding or deceit mislead his soul.

4:12   LA: Fascinatio enim nugacitatis obscurat bona, et inconstantia concupiscentiæ transvertit sensum sine malitia.
       EN: For the fascination of trifling obscures good things, and the restlessness of desire perverts an innocent mind.

4:13   LA: Consummatus in brevi, explevit tempora multa ;
       EN: Brought to completion in a short time, he fulfilled many years.

4:14   LA: placita enim erat Deo anima illius : propter hoc properavit educere illum de medio iniquitatum. Populi autem videntes, et non intelligentes, nec ponentes in præcordiis talia,
       EN: For his soul was pleasing to God; therefore He hastened to lead him out from the midst of injustices. Yet the peoples saw and did not understand, nor take such things to heart:

4:15   LA: quoniam gratia Dei et misericordia est in sanctos ejus, et respectus in electos illius.
       EN: that God's grace and mercy are upon His holy ones, and His care upon His chosen ones.

4:16   LA: Condemnat autem justus mortuus vivos impios, et juventus celerius consummata longam vitam injusti.
       EN: But the righteous man who has died condemns the ungodly who live, and youth brought swiftly to completion condemns the long life of the unjust.

4:17   LA: Videbunt enim finem sapientis, et non intelligent quid cogitaverit de illo Deus, et quare munierit illum Dominus.
       EN: For they will see the wise person's end and will not understand what God intended for him or why the Lord protected him.

4:18   LA: Videbunt, et contemnent eum ; illos autem Dominus irridebit.
       EN: They will see and despise him, but the Lord will laugh at them.

4:19   LA: Et erunt post hæc decidentes sine honore, et in contumelia inter mortuos in perpetuum : quoniam disrumpet illos inflatos sine voce, et commovebit illos a fundamentis, et usque ad supremum desolabuntur, et erunt gementes, et memoria illorum peribit.
       EN: After this they will fall without honor and remain in disgrace among the dead forever. For He will break them in their swollen pride, leaving them speechless, and shake them from their foundations. They will be utterly laid waste and will groan, and their memory will perish.

4:20   LA: Venient in cogitatione peccatorum suorum timidi, et traducent illos ex adverso iniquitates ipsorum.]
       EN: They will come fearfully at the thought of their sins, and their own injustices will confront and expose them.

WISDOM OF SOLOMON 5

5:1    LA: Tunc stabunt justi in magna constantia adversus eos qui se angustiaverunt, et qui abstulerunt labores eorum.
       EN: Then the righteous will stand with great steadfastness against those who afflicted them and took away the fruits of their labors.

5:2    LA: Videntes turbabuntur timore horribili, et mirabuntur in subitatione insperatæ salutis ;
       EN: At the sight, these people will be shaken with dreadful fear and astonished at the suddenness of the salvation they had not expected.

5:3    LA: dicentes intra se, pœnitentiam agentes, et præ angustia spiritus gementes : Hi sunt quos habuimus aliquando in derisum, et in similitudinem improperii.
       EN: Repenting and groaning in anguish of spirit, they will say within themselves: “These are the ones we once held in derision and made a byword of reproach.

5:4    LA: Nos insensati, vitam illorum æstimabamus insaniam, et finem illorum sine honore ;
       EN: Fools that we were, we considered their life madness and their end dishonorable.

5:5    LA: ecce quomodo computati sunt inter filios Dei, et inter sanctos sors illorum est.
       EN: See how they are counted among God's children, and their portion is among the holy ones!

5:6    LA: Ergo erravimus a via veritatis, et justitiæ lumen non luxit nobis, et sol intelligentiæ non est ortus nobis.
       EN: So we wandered from the way of truth; the light of righteousness did not shine for us, and the sun of understanding did not rise for us.

5:7    LA: Lassati sumus in via iniquitatis et perditionis, et ambulavimus vias difficiles : viam autem Domini ignoravimus.
       EN: We wore ourselves out on the way of injustice and destruction, and traveled difficult roads; but we did not know the Lord's way.

5:8    LA: Quid nobis profuit superbia ? aut divitiarum jactantia quid contulit nobis ?
       EN: What did pride profit us? What did boasting of riches bring us?

5:9    LA: Transierunt omnia illa tamquam umbra, et tamquam nuntius percurrens,
       EN: All those things passed like a shadow and like a messenger running by,

5:10   LA: et tamquam navis quæ pertransit fluctuantem aquam, cujus cum præterierit non est vestigium invenire, neque semitam carinæ illius in fluctibus ;
       EN: like a ship crossing surging water, whose trace cannot be found once it has passed, nor the path of its keel in the waves;

5:11   LA: aut tamquam avis quæ transvolat in aëre, cujus nullum invenitur argumentum itineris, sed tantum sonitus alarum verberans levem ventum, et scindens per vim itineris aërem : commotis alis transvolavit, et post hoc nullum signum invenitur itineris illius ;
       EN: or like a bird flying through the air, of whose journey no evidence is found—only the sound of wings beating the light wind and cutting through the air with the force of its flight. With moving wings it flew across, and afterward no sign of its journey is found;

5:12   LA: aut tamquam sagitta emissa in locum destinatum, divisus aër continuo in se reclusus est, ut ignoretur transitus illius :
       EN: or like an arrow shot at its intended target: the divided air immediately closes upon itself, so that its passage cannot be known.

5:13   LA: sic et nos nati continuo desivimus esse ; et virtutis quidem nullum signum valuimus ostendere, in malignitate autem nostra consumpti sumus. Talia dixerunt in inferno hi qui peccaverunt :
       EN: So we too, once born, immediately ceased to be. We managed to show no sign of virtue, but were consumed in our wickedness.” Such things were said in the underworld by those who sinned.

5:14   LA: quoniam spes impii tamquam lanugo est quæ a vento tollitur, et tamquam spuma gracilis quæ a procella dispergitur, et tamquam fumus qui a vento diffusus est, et tamquam memoria hospitis unius diei prætereuntis.
       EN: For the hope of the ungodly is like down carried off by the wind, like thin foam scattered by a storm, like smoke dispersed by the wind, and like the memory of a guest who stays one day and passes on.

5:15   LA: Justi autem in perpetuum vivent, et apud Dominum est merces eorum, et cogitatio illorum apud Altissimum.
       EN: But the righteous will live forever, and their reward is with the Lord; care for them is with the Most High.

5:16   LA: Ideo accipient regnum decoris, et diadema speciei de manu Domini : quoniam dextera sua teget eos, et brachio sancto suo defendet illos.
       EN: Therefore they will receive a beautiful kingdom and a splendid diadem from the Lord's hand, because He will shelter them with His right hand and defend them with His holy arm.

5:17   LA: Accipiet armaturam zelus illius, et armabit creaturam ad ultionem inimicorum.
       EN: His zeal will take up armor, and He will arm creation to take vengeance on His enemies.

5:18   LA: Induet pro thorace justitiam, et accipiet pro galea judicium certum ;
       EN: He will put on righteousness as a breastplate and take sure judgment as a helmet.

5:19   LA: sumet scutum inexpugnabile æquitatem.
       EN: He will take fairness as an unassailable shield.

5:20   LA: Acuet autem duram iram in lanceam, et pugnabit cum illo orbis terrarum contra insensatos.
       EN: He will sharpen stern wrath into a spear, and the whole earth will fight with Him against the senseless.

5:21   LA: Ibunt directe emissiones fulgurum, et tamquam a bene curvato arcu nubium exterminabuntur, et ad certum locum insilient.
       EN: Bolts of lightning will go straight ahead, hurled as from a well-bent bow of clouds, and will leap upon a definite target.

5:22   LA: Et a petrosa ira plenæ mittentur grandines ; excandescet in illos aqua maris, et flumina concurrent duriter.
       EN: From rocklike wrath, hailstones will be hurled in full force. The water of the sea will rage against them, and rivers will rush together fiercely.

5:23   LA: Contra illos stabit spiritus virtutis, et tamquam turbo venti dividet illos ; et ad eremum perducet omnem terram iniquitas illorum, et malignitas evertet sedes potentium.]
       EN: A spirit of power will rise against them and scatter them like a whirlwind. Their injustice will turn the whole earth into a wilderness, and wickedness will overthrow the thrones of the powerful.

WISDOM OF SOLOMON 6

6:1    LA: Melior est sapientia quam vires, et vir prudens quam fortis. Audite ergo, reges, et intelligite ; discite, judices finium terræ.
       EN: Wisdom is better than strength, and a prudent man better than a strong one. Listen, therefore, kings, and understand; learn, judges of the ends of the earth.

6:2    LA: Præbete aures, vos qui continetis multitudines, et placetis vobis in turbis nationum.
       EN: Give ear, you who hold multitudes in your power and take pleasure in crowds of nations.

6:3    LA: Quoniam data est a Domino potestas vobis, et virtus ab Altissimo : qui interrogabit opera vestra, et cogitationes scrutabitur.
       EN: For authority was given you by the Lord, and power by the Most High, who will examine your works and search your thoughts.

6:4    LA: Quoniam cum essetis ministri regni illius, non recte judicastis, nec custodistis legem justitiæ, neque secundum voluntatem Dei ambulastis.
       EN: For although you were ministers of His kingdom, you did not judge rightly, keep the law of righteousness, or walk according to God's will.

6:5    LA: Horrende et cito apparebit vobis, quoniam judicium durissimum his qui præsunt fiet.

       EN: For it is not the power of the things by which oaths are sworn, but the punishment of sinners, that always pursues the transgression of the unjust.

WISDOM OF SOLOMON 15

15:1   LA: Tu autem, Deus noster, suavis et verus es, patiens, et in misericordia disponens omnia.
       EN: But You, our God, are gentle and true, patient, ordering all things in mercy.

15:2   LA: Etenim si peccaverimus, tui sumus, scientes magnitudinem tuam ; et si non peccaverimus, scimus quoniam apud te sumus computati.
       EN: For even if we sin, we are Yours, knowing Your greatness; and if we do not sin, we know that we are counted as Yours.

15:3   LA: Nosse enim te, consummata justitia est ; et scire justitiam et virtutem tuam, radix est immortalitatis.
       EN: For to know You is perfect righteousness, and to know Your righteousness and power is the root of immortality.

15:4   LA: Non enim in errorem induxit nos hominum malæ artis excogitatio, nec umbra picturæ labor sine fructu, effigies sculpta per varios colores :
       EN: For the invention of human evil craft did not lead us astray, nor the illusion of a painting, a fruitless labor, a sculpted figure with varied colors.

15:5   LA: cujus aspectus insensato dat concupiscentiam, et diligit mortuæ imaginis effigiem sine anima.
       EN: Its appearance awakens desire in a senseless person, and he loves the lifeless likeness of a dead image.

15:6   LA: Malorum amatores digni sunt qui spem habeant in talibus, et qui faciunt illos, et qui diligunt, et qui colunt.]
       EN: Lovers of evil deserve to place their hope in such things, both those who make them, those who love them, and those who worship them.

15:7   LA: [Sed et figulus mollem terram premens, laboriose fingit ad usus nostros unumquodque vas ; et de eodem luto fingit quæ munda sunt in usum vasa, et similiter quæ his sunt contraria : horum autem vasorum quis sit usus, judex est figulus.
       EN: A potter too, kneading soft earth, laboriously shapes each vessel for our use. From the same clay he shapes vessels for clean uses and likewise for the opposite uses; the potter judges what the use of these vessels will be.

15:8   LA: Et cum labore vano deum fingit de eodem luto ille qui paulo ante de terra factus fuerat, et post pusillum reducit se unde acceptus est, repetitus animæ debitum quam habebat.
       EN: With vain labor, he also shapes a god from the same clay, although he himself was made from earth a little earlier and shortly returns to the place from which he was taken, when the soul he possessed is demanded back as a debt.

15:9   LA: Sed cura est illi non quia laboraturus est, nec quoniam brevis illi vita est : sed concertatur aurificibus et argentariis ; sed et ærarios imitatur, et gloriam præfert, quoniam res supervacuas fingit.
       EN: Yet his concern is not that he will suffer, or that his life is short. Instead he competes with goldsmiths and silversmiths, imitates workers in bronze, and makes a boast of shaping worthless things.

15:10  LA: Cinis est enim cor ejus, et terra supervacua spes illius, et luto vilior vita ejus :
       EN: For his heart is ashes, his hope worthless earth, and his life cheaper than mud,

15:11  LA: quoniam ignoravit qui se finxit, et qui inspiravit illi animam quæ operatur, et qui insufflavit ei spiritum vitalem.
       EN: because he did not know the One who shaped him, breathed into him an active soul, and breathed into him a living spirit.

15:12  LA: Sed et æstimaverunt ludum esse vitam nostram, et conversationem vitæ compositam ad lucrum, et oportere undecumque etiam ex malo acquirere.
       EN: They also considered our life a game and our way of living arranged for profit, and thought one ought to gain from any source, even from evil.

15:13  LA: Hic enim scit se super omnes delinquere, qui ex terræ materia fragilia vasa et sculptilia fingit.
       EN: For this man knows that he transgresses more than all others, when he fashions both fragile vessels and carved images from the substance of earth.

15:14  LA: Omnes enim insipientes, et infelices supra modum animæ superbi, sunt inimici populi tui, et imperantes illi :
       EN: All the enemies of Your people and those who rule over them are foolish, exceedingly miserable, and proud of soul,

15:15  LA: quoniam omnia idola nationum deos æstimaverunt, quibus neque oculorum usus est ad videndum, neque nares ad percipiendum spiritum, neque aures ad audiendum, neque digiti manuum ad tractandum, sed et pedes eorum pigri ad ambulandum.
       EN: because they considered all the idols of the nations gods, though they have no use of eyes to see, no nostrils to draw breath, no ears to hear, and no fingers on their hands to touch; their feet too are sluggish for walking.

15:16  LA: Homo enim fecit illos ; et qui spiritum mutuatus est, is finxit illos. Nemo enim sibi similem homo poterit deum fingere.
       EN: For a human being made them; one who borrowed his spirit shaped them. For no human being can shape a god like himself.

15:17  LA: Cum enim sit mortalis, mortuum fingit manibus iniquis. Melior enim est ipse his quos colit, quia ipse quidem vixit, cum esset mortalis, illi autem numquam.]
       EN: For, being mortal, he shapes a dead thing with unjust hands. He himself is better than those he worships, because, though mortal, he at least has lived, but they never have.

15:18  LA: [Sed et animalia miserrima colunt ; insensata enim comparata his, illis sunt deteriora.
       EN: They also worship the most wretched animals, for compared with the others, these are worse in their senselessness.

15:19  LA: Sed nec aspectu aliquis ex his animalibus bona potest conspicere : effugerunt autem Dei laudem et benedictionem ejus.]
       EN: Nor can anyone discern good things in the appearance of these animals; they have escaped God's praise and His blessing.

WISDOM OF SOLOMON 16

16:1   LA: Propter hæc et per his similia passi sunt digne tormenta, et per multitudinem bestiarum exterminati sunt.
       EN: For these reasons they deservedly suffered torments through similar things and were destroyed by a multitude of beasts.

16:2   LA: Pro quibus tormentis bene disposuisti populum tuum, quibus dedisti concupiscentiam delectamenti sui novum saporem, escam parans eis ortygometram :
       EN: In place of those torments, You provided well for Your people: You gave them a new taste to satisfy their delightful desire, preparing quail as food for them.

16:3   LA: ut illi quidem, concupiscentes escam propter ea quæ illis ostensa et missa sunt, etiam a necessaria concupiscentia averterentur. Hi autem in brevi inopes facti, novam gustaverunt escam.
       EN: Thus the former, though desiring food, were turned away even from their necessary appetite because of what was shown and sent to them; but the latter, after a brief period of want, tasted a new food.

16:4   LA: Oportebat enim illis sine excusatione quidem supervenire interitum exercentibus tyrannidem ; his autem tantum ostendere quemadmodum inimici eorum exterminabantur.
       EN: For unavoidable destruction had to come upon those who practiced tyranny, while these needed only to be shown how their enemies were being destroyed.

16:5   LA: Etenim cum illis supervenit sæva bestiarum ira, morsibus perversorum colubrorum exterminabantur.
       EN: For when the savage rage of beasts came upon them, they were being destroyed by the bites of vicious snakes.

16:6   LA: Sed non in perpetuum ira tua permansit, sed ad correptionem in brevi turbati sunt, signum habentes salutis ad commemorationem mandati legis tuæ.
       EN: But Your wrath did not remain forever. They were troubled briefly for correction, having a sign of salvation as a reminder of the commandment of Your law.

16:7   LA: Qui enim conversus est, non per hoc quod videbat sanabatur, sed per te, omnium salvatorem.
       EN: For whoever turned toward it was healed, not through what he saw, but through You, the Savior of all.

16:8   LA: In hoc autem ostendisti inimicis nostris quia tu es qui liberas ab omni malo.
       EN: By this You showed our enemies that You are the One who delivers from every evil.

16:9   LA: Illos enim locustarum et muscarum occiderunt morsus, et non est inventa sanitas animæ illorum, quia digni erant ab hujuscemodi exterminari.
       EN: For the bites of locusts and flies killed them, and no healing was found for their souls, because they deserved to be destroyed by such things.

16:10  LA: Filios autem tuos nec draconum venenatorum vicerunt dentes : misericordia enim tua adveniens sanabat illos.
       EN: But not even the teeth of venomous dragons overcame Your children, for Your mercy came and healed them.

16:11  LA: In memoria enim sermonum tuorum examinabantur, et velociter salvabantur : ne in altam incidentes oblivionem non possent tuo uti adjutorio.
       EN: For they were tested as a reminder of Your words and swiftly saved, so that they would not fall into deep forgetfulness and become unable to use Your help.

16:12  LA: Etenim neque herba, neque malagma sanavit eos : sed tuus, Domine, sermo, qui sanat omnia.
       EN: For neither herb nor poultice healed them, but Your word, Lord, which heals all things.

16:13  LA: Tu es enim, Domine, qui vitæ et mortis habes potestatem, et deducis ad portas mortis, et reducis.
       EN: For You, Lord, have authority over life and death; You lead down to the gates of death and bring back again.

16:14  LA: Homo autem occidit quidem per malitiam ; et cum exierit spiritus, non revertetur, nec revocabit animam quæ recepta est.
       EN: A human being does kill through malice; but when the spirit has departed, it will not return, nor will he call back the soul that has been received away.

16:15  LA: Sed tuam manum effugere impossibile est.
       EN: But it is impossible to escape Your hand.

16:16  LA: Negantes enim te nosse impii, per fortitudinem brachii tui flagellati sunt : novis aquis, et grandinibus, et pluviis persecutionem passi, et per ignem consumpti.
       EN: For the ungodly who denied knowing You were scourged by the strength of Your arm, pursued by strange waters, hail, and rains, and consumed by fire.

16:17  LA: Quod enim mirabile erat, in aqua, quæ omnia extinguit, plus ignis valebat : vindex est enim orbis justorum.
       EN: What was marvelous was that fire grew stronger in water, which extinguishes everything, for the world avenges the righteous.

16:18  LA: Quodam enim tempore mansuetabatur ignis, ne comburerentur quæ ad impios missa erant animalia, sed ut ipsi videntes scirent quoniam Dei judicio patiuntur persecutionem.
       EN: At one time the fire was restrained, so that the animals sent against the ungodly would not be burned up, and those people, seeing this, would know that they were being pursued by God's judgment.

16:19  LA: Et quodam tempore in aqua supra virtutem ignis exardescebat undique, ut iniquæ terræ nationem exterminaret.
       EN: At another time it blazed everywhere in water beyond fire's own strength, so that it might destroy the nation of an unjust land.

16:20  LA: Pro quibus angelorum esca nutrivisti populum tuum, et paratum panem de cælo præstitisti illis sine labore, omne delectamentum in se habentem, et omnis saporis suavitatem.
       EN: In place of these things, You nourished Your people with the food of angels and supplied them with bread from heaven, ready without labor, containing every delight and the sweetness of every flavor.

16:21  LA: Substantia enim tua dulcedinem tuam, quam in filios habes, ostendebat ; et deserviens uniuscujusque voluntati, ad quod quisque volebat convertebatur.
       EN: For Your provision showed the sweetness You have toward Your children, and, serving each person's wish, it changed into whatever each desired.

16:22  LA: Nix autem et glacies sustinebant vim ignis, et non tabescebant : ut scirent quoniam fructus inimicorum exterminabat ignis ardens in grandine et pluvia coruscans ;
       EN: But snow and ice endured the force of fire and did not melt, so that they might know that fire, burning in hail and flashing in rain, destroyed their enemies' crops;

16:23  LA: hic autem iterum ut nutrirentur justi, etiam suæ virtutis oblitus est.
       EN: yet this same fire again forgot even its own strength so that the righteous might be fed.

16:24  LA: Creatura enim tibi factori deserviens, exardescit in tormentum adversus injustos, et lenior fit ad benefaciendum pro his qui in te confidunt.
       EN: For creation, serving You its Maker, flares up in torment against the unjust and becomes gentler to do good to those who trust in You.

16:25  LA: Propter hoc et tunc in omnia transfigurata, omnium nutrici gratiæ tuæ deserviebat, ad voluntatem eorum qui a te desiderabant :
       EN: Therefore at that time too, transformed into all things, it served Your grace, the nurse of all, according to the wish of those who desired things from You,

16:26  LA: ut scirent filii tui quos dilexisti, Domine, quoniam non nativitatis fructus pascunt homines, sed sermo tuus hos qui in te crediderint conservat.
       EN: so that Your children whom You loved, Lord, might know that the fruits of natural growth do not nourish human beings, but Your word preserves those who believe in You.

16:27  LA: Quod enim ab igne non poterat exterminari, statim ab exiguo radio solis calefactum tabescebat :
       EN: For what could not be destroyed by fire immediately melted when warmed by a slight ray of the sun,

16:28  LA: ut notum omnibus esset quoniam oportet prævenire solem ad benedictionem tuam, et ad ortum lucis te adorare.
       EN: so that all might know that one must rise before the sun to bless You and worship You at the coming of light.

16:29  LA: Ingrati enim spes tamquam hibernalis glacies tabescet, et disperiet tamquam aqua supervacua.]
       EN: For the hope of the ungrateful will melt like winter ice and disappear like useless water.

WISDOM OF SOLOMON 17

17:1   LA: Magna sunt enim judicia tua, Domine, et inenarrabilia verba tua : propter hoc indisciplinatæ animæ erraverunt.
       EN: For great are Your judgments, Lord, and beyond telling are Your words; therefore uninstructed souls went astray.

17:2   LA: Dum enim persuasum habent iniqui posse dominari nationi sanctæ, vinculis tenebrarum et longæ noctis compediti, inclusi sub tectis, fugitivi perpetuæ providentiæ jacuerunt.
       EN: For while the unjust were convinced that they could rule over the holy nation, they lay bound in the chains of darkness and long night, shut beneath their roofs, fugitives from everlasting providence.

17:3   LA: Et dum putant se latere in obscuris peccatis, tenebroso oblivionis velamento dispersi sunt, paventes horrende, et cum admiratione nimia perturbati.
       EN: While they thought they lay hidden in their secret sins, they were scattered beneath a dark covering of forgetfulness, horribly afraid and troubled with overwhelming astonishment.

17:4   LA: Neque enim quæ continebat illos spelunca sine timore custodiebat, quoniam sonitus descendens perturbabat illos, et personæ tristes illis apparentes pavorem illis præstabant.
       EN: For even the cavern that enclosed them did not keep them free from fear, because a descending noise disturbed them, and gloomy figures appearing to them brought terror.

17:5   LA: Et ignis quidem nulla vis poterat illis lumen præbere, nec siderum limpidæ flammæ illuminare poterant illam noctem horrendam.
       EN: No force of fire could give them light, nor could the bright flames of the stars illuminate that dreadful night.

17:6   LA: Apparebat autem illis subitaneus ignis, timore plenus ; et timore perculsi illius quæ non videbatur faciei, æstimabant deteriora esse quæ videbantur.
       EN: Yet a sudden fire appeared to them, full of terror; struck with fear of the appearance they could not see, they considered what they did see still worse.

17:7   LA: Et magicæ artis appositi erant derisus, et sapientiæ gloriæ correptio cum contumelia.
       EN: Mockeries were brought against their magical craft, and a humiliating rebuke against their boast of wisdom.

17:8   LA: Illi enim qui promittebant timores et perturbationes expellere se ab anima languente, hi cum derisu pleni timore languebant.
       EN: For those who promised to drive fears and disturbances from a sick soul themselves languished in ridicule, full of fear.

17:9   LA: Nam etsi nihil illos ex monstris perturbabat, transitu animalium et serpentium sibilatione commoti, tremebundi peribant, et aërem quem nulla ratione quis effugere posset, negantes se videre.
       EN: Even if no monstrous thing disturbed them, they were shaken by passing animals and the hissing of serpents, and perished trembling, refusing to look upon the air that no one could possibly escape.

17:10  LA: Cum sit enim timida nequitia, dat testimonium condemnationis : semper enim præsumit sæva, perturbata conscientia :
       EN: For wickedness, being cowardly, bears witness to its own condemnation; troubled by conscience, it always anticipates terrible things.

17:11  LA: nihil enim est timor nisi proditio cogitationis auxiliorum.
       EN: For fear is nothing other than the abandonment of reason's resources for help.

17:12  LA: Et dum ab intus minor est exspectatio, majorem computat inscientiam ejus causæ, de qua tormentum præstat.
       EN: When inward expectation is weaker, it reckons its ignorance of the cause that brings the torment to be greater.

17:13  LA: Illi autem qui impotentem vere noctem, et ab infimis et ab altissimis inferis supervenientem, eumdem somnum dormientes,
       EN: But they, during a truly overpowering night that came upon them from the lowest and deepest regions of the dead, slept the same sleep,

17:14  LA: aliquando monstrorum exagitabantur timore, aliquando animæ deficiebant traductione : subitaneus enim illis et insperatus timor supervenerat.
       EN: sometimes harried by fear of monstrous things, sometimes fainting from their souls' exposure; for a sudden and unexpected fear had come upon them.

17:15  LA: Deinde si quisquam ex illis decidisset, custodiebatur in carcere sine ferro reclusus.
       EN: Then if any one of them fell down, he was kept shut in a prison without iron.

17:16  LA: Si enim rusticus quis erat, aut pastor, aut agri laborum operarius præoccupatus esset, ineffugibilem sustinebat necessitatem ;
       EN: For whether a farmer, a shepherd, or a worker caught in his labors in the field, he endured an inescapable constraint,

17:17  LA: una enim catena tenebrarum omnes erant colligati. Sive spiritus sibilans, aut inter spissos arborum ramos avium sonus suavis, aut vis aquæ decurrentis nimium,
       EN: for all were bound together by one chain of darkness. Whether it was a whistling wind, the sweet sound of birds among thick branches of trees, the force of water rushing violently,

17:18  LA: aut sonus validus præcipitatarum petrarum, aut ludentium animalium cursus invisus, aut mugientium valida bestiarum vox, aut resonans de altissimis montibus echo : deficientes faciebant illos præ timore.
       EN: the loud sound of rocks tumbling down, the unseen running of playful animals, the mighty voice of roaring beasts, or an echo resounding from the highest mountains, these made them faint with fear.

17:19  LA: Omnis enim orbis terrarum limpido illuminabatur lumine, et non impeditis operibus continebatur.
       EN: For the whole world was illuminated by clear light and engaged in unhindered works.

17:20  LA: Solis autem illis superposita erat gravis nox, imago tenebrarum quæ superventura illis erat : ipsi ergo sibi erant graviores tenebris.]
       EN: Only over them lay heavy night, an image of the darkness that was to come upon them. Thus they were a greater burden to themselves than the darkness.

WISDOM OF SOLOMON 18

18:1   LA: Sanctis autem tuis maxima erat lux, et horum quidem vocem audiebant, sed figuram non videbant. Et quia non et ipsi eadem passi erant, magnificabant te ;
       EN: But for Your holy ones there was the greatest light. The others heard their voices but did not see their form, and because they themselves had not suffered the same things, they magnified You.

18:2   LA: et qui ante læsi erant, quia non lædebantur, gratias agebant, et ut esset differentia, donum petebant.
       EN: Those who had previously been harmed, because they were no longer being harmed, gave thanks and asked for the gift of a distinction.

18:3   LA: Propter quod ignis ardentem columnam ducem habuerunt ignotæ viæ, et solem sine læsura boni hospitii præstitisti.
       EN: For this reason they had a burning pillar of fire as guide on an unknown way, and You provided a harmless sun for their good sojourn.

18:4   LA: Digni quidem illi carere luce, et pati carcerem tenebrarum, qui inclusos custodiebant filios tuos, per quos incipiebat incorruptum legis lumen sæculo dari.]
       EN: Those deserved to lose the light and suffer imprisonment in darkness who kept Your children shut in, through whom the incorruptible light of the law was beginning to be given to the world.

18:5   LA: [Cum cogitarent justorum occidere infantes, et uno exposito filio et liberato, in traductionem illorum, multitudinem filiorum abstulisti, et pariter illos perdidisti in aqua valida.
       EN: When they planned to kill the infants of the righteous, and one exposed child had been delivered, You took away a multitude of their children to expose them, and destroyed them together in mighty water.

18:6   LA: Illa enim nox ante cognita est a patribus nostris, ut vere scientes quibus juramentis crediderunt, animæquiores essent.
       EN: For that night was made known beforehand to our fathers, so that, knowing truly what oaths they had trusted, they might be more assured.

18:7   LA: Suscepta est autem a populo tuo sanitas quidem justorum, injustorum autem exterminatio.
       EN: Your people received the deliverance of the righteous and the destruction of the unjust.

18:8   LA: Sicut enim læsisti adversarios, sic et nos provocans magnificasti.
       EN: For just as You harmed our adversaries, so You magnified us by calling us to Yourself.

18:9   LA: Absconse enim sacrificabant justi pueri bonorum, et justitiæ legem in concordia disposuerunt ; similiter et bona et mala recepturos justos, patrum jam decantantes laudes.
       EN: For the righteous children of good people offered sacrifice in secret and agreed together upon the law of righteousness: that the righteous would share good things and bad alike, already singing the praises of their fathers.

18:10  LA: Resonabat autem inconveniens inimicorum vox, et flebilis audiebatur planctus ploratorum infantium.
       EN: But the discordant cry of their enemies resounded, and the sorrowful lament of people mourning their children was heard.

18:11  LA: Simili autem pœna servus cum domino afflictus est, et popularis homo regi similia passus.
       EN: Slave and master were afflicted with the same punishment, and a common person suffered the same things as a king.

18:12  LA: Similiter ergo omnes, uno nomine mortis, mortuos habebant innumerabiles : nec enim ad sepeliendum vivi sufficiebant, quoniam uno momento quæ erat præclarior natio illorum exterminata est.
       EN: Thus all alike, under one name of death, had countless dead. The living were not enough to bury them, because in a single moment their more illustrious offspring had been destroyed.

18:13  LA: De omnibus enim non credentes, propter veneficia ; tunc vero primum cum fuit exterminium primogenitorum, spoponderunt populum Dei esse.
       EN: For, disbelieving everything because of their sorceries, only then, when the firstborn were destroyed, did they acknowledge that the people belonged to God.

18:14  LA: Cum enim quietum silentium contineret omnia, et nox in suo cursu medium iter haberet,
       EN: For while a still silence held all things, and night had reached the middle of its course,

18:15  LA: omnipotens sermo tuus de cælo, a regalibus sedibus, durus debellator in mediam exterminii terram prosilivit,
       EN: Your almighty word leaped from heaven, from the royal thrones, a fierce warrior, into the midst of the land of destruction,

18:16  LA: gladius acutus insimulatum imperium tuum portans : et stans, replevit omnia morte, et usque ad cælum attingebat stans in terra.
       EN: a sharp sword bearing Your unfeigned command. Standing there, it filled all things with death, and, while standing on earth, reached as far as heaven.

18:17  LA: Tunc continuo visus somniorum malorum turbaverunt illos, et timores supervenerunt insperati.
       EN: Immediately the sights of evil dreams disturbed them, and unexpected terrors came upon them.

18:18  LA: Et alius alibi projectus semivivus, propter quam moriebatur causam demonstrabat mortis.
       EN: One here, another there, cast down half-alive, made known the cause of the death by which he was dying.

18:19  LA: Visiones enim quæ illos turbaverunt hæc præmonebant, ne inscii quare mala patiebantur perirent.
       EN: For the visions that troubled them forewarned them of these things, so that they would not perish unaware of why they were suffering evils.

18:20  LA: Tetigit autem tunc et justos tentatio mortis, et commotio in eremo facta est multitudinis : sed non diu permansit ira tua.
       EN: Then the trial of death touched even the righteous, and a disturbance of the multitude occurred in the wilderness; but Your wrath did not remain long.

18:21  LA: Prosperans enim homo sine querela deprecari pro populis, proferens servitutis suæ scutum, orationem et per incensum deprecationem allegans, restitit iræ, et finem imposuit necessitati, ostendens quoniam tuus est famulus.
       EN: For a blameless man hastened to intercede for the peoples, bringing forward the shield of his ministry, presenting prayer and supplication through incense. He resisted the wrath and put an end to the calamity, showing that he was Your servant.

18:22  LA: Vicit autem turbas non in virtute corporis, nec armaturæ potentia : sed verbo illum qui se vexabat subjecit, juramenta parentum et testamentum commemorans.
       EN: He overcame the multitudes, not by bodily strength or the power of armor, but by a word he subdued the one afflicting him, recalling the oaths to the fathers and the covenant.

18:23  LA: Cum enim jam acervatim cecidissent super alterutrum mortui, interstitit, et amputavit impetum, et divisit illam quæ ad vivos ducebat viam.
       EN: For when the dead had already fallen in heaps upon one another, he stood between them, cut off the onslaught, and blocked the path leading to the living.

18:24  LA: In veste enim poderis quam habebat, totus erat orbis terrarum ; et parentum magnalia in quatuor ordinibus lapidum erant sculpta, et magnificentia tua in diademate capitis illius sculpta erat.
       EN: For upon the robe reaching to his feet was the whole world. The glories of the fathers were engraved in four rows of stones, and Your magnificence was engraved upon the diadem on his head.

18:25  LA: His autem cessit qui exterminabat, et hæc extimuit : erat enim sola tentatio iræ sufficiens.]
       EN: Before these the destroyer gave way and feared them, for the mere trial of wrath was sufficient.

WISDOM OF SOLOMON 19

19:1   LA: Impiis autem usque in novissimum sine misericordia ira supervenit. Præsciebat enim et futura illorum :
       EN: But wrath without mercy came upon the ungodly to the very end, for He also knew beforehand what lay in their future:

19:2   LA: quoniam cum ipsi permisissent ut se educerent, et cum magna sollicitudine præmisissent illos, consequebantur illos, pœnitentia acti.
