# Wisdom of Solomon — Latin–English (Clementine Vulgate)

19 chapters, 435 readings (439 Latin verses; two of them, 19:13 and 19:20, appear in two reader rows), captured from the Saints.Bible staging Parallel Reader on 1 November 2026. One of the 70 books of the [Latin–English column](../README.md); audit results in [AUDIT.md](../AUDIT.md).

Until 1 November 2026 the Parallel Reader had no Latin column for this book, although the site already carried a complete [Wisdom of Solomon Latin–Greek comparison edition](https://staging-8e0d-waywardnightjars.wpcomstaging.com/comparison-library/wisdom-comparison-edition/) in the Comparison Library (Latin, English rendering, classification and translation notes for every Clementine verse). That edition lives under the Comparison Library rather than under the book page, so the reader never found it. Its 439 cards were reproduced in the reader's card format as the book's [vulgate-comparison page](https://staging-8e0d-waywardnightjars.wpcomstaging.com/bible/wisdom/wisdom-of-solomon/vulgate-comparison/), each card linking back to its full five-layer entry, and the reader now shows the column.

- **Latin:** matches the Clementine Vulgate reference text in 439 of 439 Latin verses (100.0%).
- **English origin:** 392 fresh renderings; 3 Douay-Rheims verbatim (short verses such as 12:26 whose literal rendering coincides with Douay); 44 Douay-Rheims lightly modernised, that is, close to Douay because both render the Latin literally; 0 with no Latin text. The rendering is the Comparison Library's own: literal, in modern English ("righteousness", "the realm of the dead"), with translation notes on difficult words (1:5 *corripietur*, 2:25 *imitantur*, 9:19 *sanati sunt*).
- **Site review labels:** none pending — every Latin verse carries a classification: 297 Similar, 94 Different, 43 Divergent, 4 Same, 1 Disparate.
- **Verse numbering:** 69 readings carry a number that differs from the Vulgate's own (recorded as `vulgate_reference` in verses.json). The Clementine has 439 verses to the Greek's 435: six Latin verses have no separate Greek counterpart (2:25, 5:14, 6:1, 6:23, 9:19, 11:6) and are shown with a neighbouring Saints verse; chapters 5, 6, 11 and 19 run one or two numbers apart; Latin 19:13 and 19:20 each stand beside two Saints verses.

**Audit.** The 439 renderings were compared with Douay-Rheims and with an independent literal rendering made from the same Latin for this audit (median content-word overlap 0.53 — the site prefers modern vocabulary, so overlap is low where the meaning is the same: "dire are the ends" / "the endings … are dreadful"). The 25 lowest-overlap verses and 24 random verses were read against the Latin: no meaning error was found. Four verses have brackets left in the Latin (7:22, 10:14, 13:19, 14:31), the same paragraph markers seen in other books.
