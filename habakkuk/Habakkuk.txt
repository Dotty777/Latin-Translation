HABAKKUK — LATIN–ENGLISH (CLEMENTINE VULGATE)

HABAKKUK 1

1:1  LA: Onus quod vidit Habacuc propheta.
     EN: The burden that Habakkuk the prophet saw.

1:2  LA: [Usquequo, Domine, clamabo, et non exaudies ? vociferabor ad te, vim patiens, et non salvabis ?
     EN: How long, Lord, shall I cry out and You will not hear? Shall I shout to You as I suffer violence, and You will not save?

1:3  LA: Quare ostendisti mihi iniquitatem et laborem, videre prædam et injustitiam contra me ? Et factum est judicium, et contradictio potentior.
     EN: Why have You shown me iniquity and misery, making me see plunder and injustice against me? Judgment has taken place, and opposition has grown stronger.

1:4  LA: Propter hoc lacerata est lex, et non pervenit usque ad finem judicium ; quia impius prævalet adversus justum, propterea egreditur judicium perversum.
     EN: Because of this the law is torn apart, and judgment never reaches its end. Because the wicked prevail over the just, judgment therefore comes forth distorted.

1:5  LA: Aspicite in gentibus, et videte ; admiramini, et obstupescite : quia opus factum est in diebus vestris, quod nemo credet cum narrabitur.
     EN: Look among the nations and see; marvel and be astounded. For a work has been done in your days that no one will believe when it is told.

1:6  LA: Quia ecce ego suscitabo Chaldæos, gentem amaram et velocem, ambulantem super latitudinem terræ, ut possideat tabernacula non sua.
     EN: For look, I will raise up the Chaldeans, a bitter and swift nation, marching across the breadth of the earth to possess dwellings that are not theirs.

1:7  LA: Horribilis et terribilis est : ex semetipsa judicium et onus ejus egredietur.
     EN: It is dreadful and terrifying; its judgment and its burden will proceed from itself.

1:8  LA: Leviores pardis equi ejus, et velociores lupis vespertinis : et diffundentur equites ejus : equites namque ejus de longe venient ; volabunt quasi aquila festinans ad comedendum.
     EN: Its horses are swifter than leopards and faster than evening wolves. Its horsemen will spread out; indeed, its horsemen will come from far away. They will fly like an eagle hurrying to devour.

1:9  LA: Omnes ad prædam venient, facies eorum ventus urens ; et congregabit quasi arenam captivitatem.
     EN: They will all come for plunder; their faces are a scorching wind. It will gather captives like sand.

1:10  LA: Et ipse de regibus triumphabit, et tyranni ridiculi ejus erunt ; ipse super omnem munitionem ridebit, et comportabit aggerem, et capiet eam.
      EN: It will triumph over kings, and rulers will be its laughingstock. It will laugh at every fortress, heap up a ramp, and capture it.

1:11  LA: Tunc mutabitur spiritus, et pertransibit, et corruet : hæc est fortitudo ejus dei sui.]
      EN: Then its spirit will change; it will pass through and fall. This is its strength, belonging to its god.

1:12  LA: [Numquid non tu a principio, Domine, Deus meus, sancte meus, et non moriemur ? Domine, in judicium posuisti eum, et fortem, ut corriperes, fundasti eum.
      EN: Are You not from the beginning, Lord, my God, my Holy One? We shall not die. Lord, You have appointed him for judgment, and made him strong in order to correct us.

1:13  LA: Mundi sunt oculi tui, ne videas malum, et respicere ad iniquitatem non poteris. Quare respicis super iniqua agentes, et taces devorante impio justiorem se ?
      EN: Your eyes are pure, so that You do not look upon evil, and You cannot look approvingly on iniquity. Why do You look upon those who act wickedly and remain silent while the wicked devours one more just than himself?

1:14  LA: Et facies homines quasi pisces maris, et quasi reptile non habens principem.
      EN: You will make human beings like the fish of the sea, like a creeping thing that has no ruler.

1:15  LA: Totum in hamo sublevavit, traxit illud in sagena sua, et congregavit in rete suum. Super hoc lætabitur, et exsultabit.
      EN: He has lifted up all of them with a hook, dragged them in his seine, and gathered them in his net. Over this he will rejoice and exult.

1:16  LA: Propterea immolabit sagenæ suæ, et sacrificabit reti suo, quia in ipsis incrassata est pars ejus, et cibus ejus electus.
      EN: Therefore he will offer victims to his seine and sacrifice to his net, because through them his portion has become rich and his food choice.

1:17  LA: Propter hoc ergo expandit sagenam suam, et semper interficere gentes non parcet.]
      EN: For this reason, then, he spreads his seine, and he will never spare the nations from slaughter.

HABAKKUK 2

2:1  LA: Super custodiam meam stabo, et figam gradum super munitionem : et contemplabor ut videam quid dicatur mihi, et quid respondeam ad arguentem me.]
     EN: I will stand at my watch and take my position on the fortification. I will keep watch to see what is said to me and what I should answer the one who reproves me.

2:2  LA: Et respondit mihi Dominus, et dixit : [Scribe visum, et explana eum super tabulas, ut percurrat qui legerit eum.
     EN: The Lord answered me and said: Write the vision and make it plain upon tablets, so that whoever reads it may run through it.

2:3  LA: Quia adhuc visus procul ; et apparebit in finem, et non mentietur : si moram fecerit, exspecta illum, quia veniens veniet, et non tardabit.
     EN: For the vision is still far off; it will appear at the end and will not deceive. If it delays, wait for it, for it will surely come and will not be late.

2:4  LA: Ecce qui incredulus est, non erit recta anima ejus in semetipso ; justus autem in fide sua vivet.
     EN: Look, the soul of the unbelieving person will not be upright within him; but the just person will live by his faith.

2:5  LA: Et quomodo vinum potantem decipit, sic erit vir superbus, et non decorabitur : qui dilatavit quasi infernus animam suam, et ipse quasi mors, et non adimpletur : et congregabit ad se omnes gentes, et coacervabit ad se omnes populos.
     EN: As wine deceives the drinker, so it will be with the proud man, and he will receive no honor. He has enlarged his appetite like the underworld; he is like death and is never filled. He will gather all nations to himself and heap up all peoples for himself.

2:6  LA: Numquid non omnes isti super eum parabolam sument, et loquelam ænigmatum ejus, et dicetur : Væ ei qui multiplicat non sua ? usquequo et aggravat contra se densum lutum ?
     EN: Will not all these take up a parable against him and a riddling saying about him? They will say: Woe to the one who multiplies what is not his own! How long will he heap thick mud upon himself?

2:7  LA: Numquid non repente consurgent qui mordeant te, et suscitabuntur lacerantes te, et eris in rapinam eis ?
     EN: Will those who bite you not rise suddenly, and those who tear you to pieces awaken? You will become their plunder.

2:8  LA: Quia tu spoliasti gentes multas, spoliabunt te omnes qui reliqui fuerint de populis, propter sanguinem hominis, et iniquitatem terræ, civitatis, et omnium habitantium in ea.
     EN: Because you have plundered many nations, all the peoples who remain will plunder you, because of human bloodshed and the violence done to the land, the city, and all who dwell in it.

2:9  LA: Væ qui congregat avaritiam malam domui suæ, ut sit in excelso nidus ejus, et liberari se putat de manu mali !
     EN: Woe to the one who gathers evil gain for his house, so that his nest may be on high, and thinks he can escape the hand of evil!

2:10  LA: Cogitasti confusionem domui tuæ ; concidisti populos multos, et peccavit anima tua.
      EN: You have planned shame for your house; you have cut down many peoples, and your soul has sinned.

2:11  LA: Quia lapis de pariete clamabit, et lignum, quod inter juncturas ædificiorum est, respondebit.
      EN: For the stone will cry out from the wall, and the timber among the building's joints will answer.

2:12  LA: Væ qui ædificat civitatem in sanguinibus, et præparat urbem in iniquitate !
      EN: Woe to the one who builds a city with bloodshed and establishes a town through iniquity!

2:13  LA: Numquid non hæc sunt a Domino exercituum ? laborabunt enim populi in multo igne, et gentes in vacuum, et deficient.
      EN: Do these things not come from the Lord of hosts? For peoples will labor amid great fire, and nations for nothing, and they will fail.

2:14  LA: Quia replebitur terra, ut cognoscant gloriam Domini, quasi aquæ operientes mare.
      EN: For the earth will be filled with knowledge of the Lord's glory, like waters covering the sea.

2:15  LA: Væ qui potum dat amico suo mittens fel suum, et inebrians ut aspiciat nuditatem ejus !
      EN: Woe to the one who gives his friend a drink, adding his own gall and making him drunk so that he may look upon his nakedness!

2:16  LA: Repletus es ignominia pro gloria ; bibe tu quoque, et consopire. Circumdabit te calix dexteræ Domini, et vomitus ignominiæ super gloriam tuam.
      EN: You are filled with disgrace instead of glory. You too must drink and fall asleep. The cup of the Lord's right hand will come around to you, and shameful vomit will cover your glory.

2:17  LA: Quia iniquitas Libani operiet te, et vastitas animalium deterrebit eos de sanguinibus hominum, et iniquitate terræ, et civitatis, et omnium habitantium in ea.
      EN: For the violence done to Lebanon will cover you, and the devastation of animals will terrify them, because of human bloodshed and the violence done to the land, the city, and all who dwell in it.

2:18  LA: Quid prodest sculptile, quia sculpsit illud fictor suus, conflatile, et imaginem falsam ? quia speravit in figmento fictor ejus, ut faceret simulacra muta.
      EN: What use is a carved image because its maker has carved it—a cast image and a lying likeness? Its maker has trusted in his own handiwork, making mute idols.

2:19  LA: Væ qui dicit ligno : Expergiscere ; Surge, lapidi tacenti ! Numquid ipse docere poterit ? ecce iste coopertus est auro et argento, et omnis spiritus non est in visceribus ejus.
      EN: Woe to the one who says to wood, “Awake!” and to silent stone, “Rise!” Can it teach? Look, it is covered in gold and silver, but there is no breath at all within it.

2:20  LA: Dominus autem in templo sancto suo : sileat a facie ejus omnis terra !]
      EN: But the Lord is in His holy temple; let all the earth be silent before Him!

HABAKKUK 3

3:1  LA: Oratio Habacuc prophetæ, pro ignorantiis.
     EN: The prayer of Habakkuk the prophet, for sins of ignorance.

3:2  LA: [Domine, audivi auditionem tuam, et timui. Domine, opus tuum, in medio annorum vivifica illud ; in medio annorum notum facies : cum iratus fueris, misericordiæ recordaberis.
     EN: Lord, I have heard the report of You and have been afraid. Lord, revive Your work in the midst of the years; in the midst of the years You will make it known. When You are angry, You will remember mercy.

3:3  LA: Deus ab austro veniet, et Sanctus de monte Pharan : operuit cælos gloria ejus, et laudis ejus plena est terra.
     EN: God will come from the south, and the Holy One from Mount Paran. His glory has covered the heavens, and the earth is full of His praise.

3:4  LA: Splendor ejus ut lux erit, cornua in manibus ejus : ibi abscondita est fortitudo ejus.
     EN: His brightness will be like light; horns are in His hands. There His strength is hidden.

3:5  LA: Ante faciem ejus ibit mors, et egredietur diabolus ante pedes ejus.
     EN: Death will go before His face, and the devil will go forth before His feet.

3:6  LA: Stetit, et mensus est terram ; aspexit, et dissolvit gentes, et contriti sunt montes sæculi : incurvati sunt colles mundi ab itineribus æternitatis ejus.
     EN: He stood and measured the earth; He looked and scattered the nations. The everlasting mountains were crushed; the hills of the world bowed beneath His eternal paths.

3:7  LA: Pro iniquitate vidi tentoria Æthiopiæ ; turbabuntur pelles terræ Madian.
     EN: I saw Ethiopia's tents suffering for iniquity; the tent skins of the land of Midian will tremble.

3:8  LA: Numquid in fluminibus iratus es, Domine ? aut in fluminibus furor tuus ? vel in mari indignatio tua ? Qui ascendes super equos tuos, et quadrigæ tuæ salvatio.
     EN: Were You angry with the rivers, Lord? Was Your fury against the rivers, or Your indignation against the sea? You will mount Your horses, and Your chariots are salvation.

3:9  LA: Suscitans suscitabis arcum tuum, juramenta tribubus quæ locutus es ; fluvios scindes terræ.
     EN: You will surely rouse Your bow, in keeping with the oaths You spoke to the tribes. You will cleave rivers through the earth.

3:10  LA: Viderunt te, et doluerunt montes ; gurges aquarum transiit : dedit abyssus vocem suam ; altitudo manus suas levavit.
      EN: The mountains saw You and writhed; a torrent of waters passed by. The deep gave forth its voice; the height lifted its hands.

3:11  LA: Sol et luna steterunt in habitaculo suo : in luce sagittarum tuarum ibunt, in splendore fulgurantis hastæ tuæ.
      EN: Sun and moon stood still in their dwelling. They will move in the light of Your arrows, in the brightness of Your flashing spear.

3:12  LA: In fremitu conculcabis terram ; in furore obstupefacies gentes.
      EN: In wrath You will trample the earth; in fury You will stun the nations.

3:13  LA: Egressus es in salutem populi tui, in salutem cum christo tuo : percussisti caput de domo impii, denudasti fundamentum ejus usque ad collum.
      EN: You went forth for the salvation of Your people, for salvation with Your anointed. You struck the head from the house of the wicked; You laid bare its foundation up to the neck.

3:14  LA: Maledixisti sceptris ejus, capiti bellatorum ejus, venientibus ut turbo ad dispergendum me : exsultatio eorum, sicut ejus qui devorat pauperem in abscondito.
      EN: You cursed his scepters, the head of his warriors, who came like a whirlwind to scatter me. Their exultation was like that of one who devours the poor in secret.

3:15  LA: Viam fecisti in mari equis tuis, in luto aquarum multarum.
      EN: You made a path in the sea for Your horses, in the mud of mighty waters.

3:16  LA: Audivi, et conturbatus est venter meus ; a voce contremuerunt labia mea. Ingrediatur putredo in ossibus meis, et subter me scateat : ut requiescam in die tribulationis, ut ascendam ad populum accinctum nostrum.
      EN: I heard, and my belly was troubled; at the sound my lips trembled. Let rottenness enter my bones and well up beneath me, so that I may rest on the day of distress, so that I may go up to our people girded for battle.

3:17  LA: Ficus enim non florebit, et non erit germen in vineis ; mentietur opus olivæ, et arva non afferent cibum : abscindetur de ovili pecus, et non erit armentum in præsepibus.
      EN: For the fig tree will not blossom, and there will be no growth on the vines. The labor spent on the olive will disappoint, and the fields will yield no food. The flock will be cut off from the fold, and there will be no herd in the stalls.

3:18  LA: Ego autem in Domino gaudebo ; et exsultabo in Deo Jesu meo.
      EN: Yet I will rejoice in the Lord; I will exult in God, my Jesus.

3:19  LA: Deus Dominus fortitudo mea, et ponet pedes meos quasi cervorum : et super excelsa mea deducet me victor in psalmis canentem.]
      EN: The Lord God is my strength. He will make my feet like those of deer, and He, the conqueror, will lead me upon my heights as I sing psalms.
