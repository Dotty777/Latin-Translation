1:2  LA: Osculetur me osculo oris sui ; quia meliora sunt ubera tua vino,
     EN: [Bride] Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth, for your breasts are better than wine,

1:3  LA: fragrantia unguentis optimis. Oleum effusum nomen tuum ; ideo adolescentulæ dilexerunt te.
     EN: fragrant with the finest ointments. Your name is oil poured out; therefore the young women have loved you.

1:4  LA: <Chorus Adolescentularum>Trahe me, post te curremus in odorem unguentorum tuorum. Introduxit me rex in cellaria sua ; exsultabimus et lætabimur in te, memores uberum tuorum super vinum. Recti diligunt te.
     EN: [Chorus of Young Women] Draw me; we will run after you in the fragrance of your ointments. The king has brought me into his storerooms. We will exult and rejoice in you, remembering your breasts more than wine. The upright love you.

1:5  LA: Nigra sum, sed formosa, filiæ Jerusalem, sicut tabernacula Cedar, sicut pelles Salomonis.
     EN: [Bride] I am black, but beautiful, daughters of Jerusalem, like the tents of Kedar, like the hides of Solomon.

1:6  LA: Nolite me considerare quod fusca sim, quia decoloravit me sol. Filii matris meæ pugnaverunt contra me ; posuerunt me custodem in vineis : vineam meam non custodivi.
     EN: Do not gaze at me because I am dark, for the sun has changed my color. My mother's sons fought against me; they made me keeper of the vineyards, but I have not kept my own vineyard.

1:7  LA: Indica mihi, quem diligit anima mea, ubi pascas, ubi cubes in meridie, ne vagari incipiam post greges sodalium tuorum.
     EN: Tell me, you whom my soul loves, where you pasture your flock, where you lie down at noon, lest I begin to wander after the flocks of your companions.

1:8  LA: Si ignoras te, o pulcherrima inter mulieres, egredere, et abi post vestigia gregum, et pasce hædos tuos juxta tabernacula pastorum.
     EN: [Bridegroom] If you do not know yourself, most beautiful among women, go out and follow the tracks of the flocks, and pasture your young goats beside the shepherds' tents.

1:9  LA: Equitatui meo in curribus Pharaonis assimilavi te, amica mea.
     EN: I have likened you, my friend, to my cavalry among Pharaoh's chariots.

1:10  LA: Pulchræ sunt genæ tuæ sicut turturis ; collum tuum sicut monilia.
      EN: Your cheeks are beautiful like a turtledove's; your neck is like necklaces.

1:11  LA: Murenulas aureas faciemus tibi, vermiculatas argento.
      EN: We will make you little chains of gold, worked with silver.

1:12  LA: Dum esset rex in accubitu suo, nardus mea dedit odorem suum.
      EN: [Bride] While the king was reclining at his table, my nard gave forth its fragrance.

1:13  LA: Fasciculus myrrhæ dilectus meus mihi ; inter ubera mea commorabitur.
      EN: My beloved is a bundle of myrrh to me; he will rest between my breasts.

1:14  LA: Botrus cypri dilectus meus mihi in vineis Engaddi.
      EN: My beloved is a cluster of henna to me in the vineyards of En-gedi.

1:15  LA: Ecce tu pulchra es, amica mea ! ecce tu pulchra es ! Oculi tui columbarum.
      EN: [Bridegroom] Behold, you are beautiful, my friend! Behold, you are beautiful! Your eyes are doves' eyes.

1:16  LA: Ecce tu pulcher es, dilecte mi, et decorus ! Lectulus noster floridus.
      EN: [Bride] Behold, you are beautiful, my beloved, and lovely! Our bed is in bloom.

1:17  LA: Tigna domorum nostrarum cedrina, laquearia nostra cypressina.]
      EN: The beams of our houses are cedar, our paneled ceilings cypress.

SONG OF SONGS 2

2:1  LA: Ego flos campi, et lilium convallium.
     EN: I am a flower of the field and a lily of the valleys.

2:2  LA: Sicut lilium inter spinas, sic amica mea inter filias.
     EN: [Bridegroom] Like a lily among thorns, so is my friend among the daughters.

2:3  LA: Sicut malus inter ligna silvarum, sic dilectus meus inter filios. Sub umbra illius quem desideraveram sedi, et fructus ejus dulcis gutturi meo.
     EN: [Bride] Like an apple tree among the trees of the woods, so is my beloved among the sons. I sat beneath the shade of him whom I had desired, and his fruit was sweet to my throat.

2:4  LA: Introduxit me in cellam vinariam ; ordinavit in me caritatem.
     EN: He brought me into the wine cellar; he set love in order within me.

2:5  LA: Fulcite me floribus, stipate me malis, quia amore langueo.
     EN: Support me with flowers, surround me with apples, for I languish with love.

2:6  LA: Læva ejus sub capite meo, et dextera illius amplexabitur me.
     EN: His left hand is beneath my head, and his right hand will embrace me.

2:7  LA: Adjuro vos, filiæ Jerusalem, per capreas cervosque camporum, ne suscitetis, neque evigilare faciatis dilectam, quoadusque ipsa velit.
     EN: [Bridegroom] I charge you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the gazelles and the deer of the fields, do not rouse or awaken the beloved until she herself wishes.

2:8  LA: Vox dilecti mei ; ecce iste venit, saliens in montibus, transiliens colles.
     EN: [Bride] The voice of my beloved! Behold, he comes, leaping upon the mountains, bounding over the hills.

2:9  LA: Similis est dilectus meus capreæ, hinnuloque cervorum. En ipse stat post parietem nostrum, respiciens per fenestras, prospiciens per cancellos.
     EN: My beloved is like a gazelle or a young stag. Behold, he stands behind our wall, looking through the windows, peering through the lattice.

2:10  LA: En dilectus meus loquitur mihi. <Sponsus>Surge, propera, amica mea, columba mea, formosa mea, et veni :
      EN: Behold, my beloved speaks to me. [Bridegroom] Rise, hurry, my friend, my dove, my beautiful one, and come,

2:11  LA: jam enim hiems transiit ; imber abiit, et recessit.
      EN: for now winter has passed; the rain has gone and departed.

2:12  LA: Flores apparuerunt in terra nostra ; tempus putationis advenit : vox turturis audita est in terra nostra ;
      EN: Flowers have appeared in our land; the time of pruning has arrived. The turtledove's voice has been heard in our land.

2:13  LA: ficus protulit grossos suos ; vineæ florentes dederunt odorem suum. Surge, amica mea, speciosa mea, et veni :
      EN: The fig tree has brought forth its first figs; the flowering vines have given their fragrance. Rise, my friend, my lovely one, and come.

2:14  LA: columba mea, in foraminibus petræ, in caverna maceriæ, ostende mihi faciem tuam, sonet vox tua in auribus meis : vox enim tua dulcis, et facies tua decora.
      EN: My dove in the clefts of the rock, in the hollow of the wall, show me your face; let your voice sound in my ears, for your voice is sweet and your face is lovely.

2:15  LA: Capite nobis vulpes parvulas quæ demoliuntur vineas : nam vinea nostra floruit.
      EN: [Bride] Catch for us the little foxes that ruin the vineyards, for our vineyard is in bloom.

2:16  LA: Dilectus meus mihi, et ego illi, qui pascitur inter lilia,
      EN: My beloved is mine, and I am his, who feeds among the lilies,

2:17  LA: donec aspiret dies, et inclinentur umbræ. Revertere ; similis esto, dilecte mi, capreæ, hinnuloque cervorum super montes Bether.]
      EN: until the day breathes and the shadows incline. Return, my beloved; be like a gazelle or a young stag upon the mountains of Bether.

SONG OF SONGS 3

3:1  LA: In lectulo meo, per noctes, quæsivi quem diligit anima mea : quæsivi illum, et non inveni.
     EN: On my bed, night after night, I sought the one my soul loves; I sought him and did not find him.

3:2  LA: Surgam, et circuibo civitatem : per vicos et plateas quæram quem diligit anima mea : quæsivi illum, et non inveni.
     EN: I will rise and go about the city; through its streets and squares I will seek the one my soul loves. I sought him and did not find him.

3:3  LA: Invenerunt me vigiles qui custodiunt civitatem : Num quem diligit anima mea vidistis ?
     EN: The watchmen who guard the city found me. Have you seen the one my soul loves?

3:4  LA: Paululum cum pertransissem eos, inveni quem diligit anima mea : tenui eum, nec dimittam, donec introducam illum in domum matris meæ, et in cubiculum genetricis meæ.
     EN: I had passed them only a little when I found the one my soul loves. I held him and will not let him go until I bring him into my mother's house, into the chamber of the one who bore me.

3:5  LA: Adjuro vos, filiæ Jerusalem, per capreas cervosque camporum, ne suscitetis, neque evigilare faciatis dilectam, donec ipsa velit.
     EN: [Bridegroom] I charge you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the gazelles and the deer of the fields, do not rouse or awaken the beloved until she herself wishes.

3:6  LA: Quæ est ista quæ ascendit per desertum sicut virgula fumi ex aromatibus myrrhæ, et thuris, et universi pulveris pigmentarii ?
     EN: [Chorus] Who is this who comes up through the wilderness like a plume of smoke from fragrant myrrh, frankincense, and every powder of the perfumer?

3:7  LA: En lectulum Salomonis sexaginta fortes ambiunt ex fortissimis Israël,
     EN: Behold, sixty mighty men surround Solomon's couch, from the mightiest of Israel,

3:8  LA: omnes tenentes gladios, et ad bella doctissimi : uniuscujusque ensis super femur suum propter timores nocturnos.
     EN: all holding swords and thoroughly trained for war, each man's sword at his thigh against the terrors of the night.

3:9  LA: Ferculum fecit sibi rex Salomon de lignis Libani ;
     EN: King Solomon made himself a litter from the wood of Lebanon.

3:10  LA: columnas ejus fecit argenteas, reclinatorium aureum, ascensum purpureum ; media caritate constravit, propter filias Jerusalem.
      EN: He made its columns silver, its reclining place gold, its steps purple; he spread its interior with love for the daughters of Jerusalem.

3:11  LA: Egredimini et videte, filiæ Sion, regem Salomonem in diademate quo coronavit illum mater sua in die desponsationis illius, et in die lætitiæ cordis ejus.]
      EN: Go out and see, daughters of Zion, King Solomon in the diadem with which his mother crowned him on the day of his wedding, the day of his heart's gladness.

SONG OF SONGS 4

4:1  LA: Quam pulchra es, amica mea ! quam pulchra es ! Oculi tui columbarum, absque eo quod intrinsecus latet. Capilli tui sicut greges caprarum quæ ascenderunt de monte Galaad.
     EN: [Bridegroom] How beautiful you are, my friend! How beautiful you are! Your eyes are doves' eyes, apart from what lies hidden within. Your hair is like flocks of goats that have come up from Mount Gilead.

4:2  LA: Dentes tui sicut greges tonsarum quæ ascenderunt de lavacro ; omnes gemellis fœtibus, et sterilis non est inter eas.
     EN: Your teeth are like flocks of shorn sheep that have come up from washing, all bearing twins, with none barren among them.

4:3  LA: Sicut vitta coccinea labia tua, et eloquium tuum dulce. Sicut fragmen mali punici, ita genæ tuæ, absque eo quod intrinsecus latet.
     EN: Your lips are like a scarlet ribbon, and your speech is sweet. Your cheeks are like a piece of pomegranate, apart from what lies hidden within.

4:4  LA: Sicut turris David collum tuum, quæ ædificata est cum propugnaculis ; mille clypei pendent ex ea, omnis armatura fortium.
     EN: Your neck is like David's tower, built with battlements. A thousand shields hang from it, all the armor of mighty men.

4:5  LA: Duo ubera tua sicut duo hinnuli, capreæ gemelli, qui pascuntur in liliis.
     EN: Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle, that feed among the lilies.

4:6  LA: Donec aspiret dies, et inclinentur umbræ, vadam ad montem myrrhæ, et ad collem thuris.
     EN: Until the day breathes and the shadows incline, I will go to the mountain of myrrh and the hill of frankincense.

4:7  LA: Tota pulchra es, amica mea, et macula non est in te.
     EN: You are altogether beautiful, my friend, and there is no blemish in you.

4:8  LA: Veni de Libano, sponsa mea : veni de Libano, veni, coronaberis : de capite Amana, de vertice Sanir et Hermon, de cubilibus leonum, de montibus pardorum.
     EN: Come from Lebanon, my bride; come from Lebanon, come, you shall be crowned: from the summit of Amana, from the peak of Senir and Hermon, from the lions' dens, from the leopards' mountains.

4:9  LA: Vulnerasti cor meum, soror mea, sponsa ; vulnerasti cor meum in uno oculorum tuorum, et in uno crine colli tui.
     EN: You have wounded my heart, my sister, my bride; you have wounded my heart with one of your eyes and with one lock of hair at your neck.

4:10  LA: Quam pulchræ sunt mammæ tuæ, soror mea sponsa ! pulchriora sunt ubera tua vino, et odor unguentorum tuorum super omnia aromata.
      EN: How beautiful are your breasts, my sister, my bride! Your breasts are more beautiful than wine, and the fragrance of your ointments surpasses all spices.

4:11  LA: Favus distillans labia tua, sponsa ; mel et lac sub lingua tua : et odor vestimentorum tuorum sicut odor thuris.
      EN: Your lips are a dripping honeycomb, my bride; honey and milk are beneath your tongue, and the fragrance of your garments is like the fragrance of frankincense.

4:12  LA: Hortus conclusus soror mea, sponsa, hortus conclusus, fons signatus.
      EN: My sister, my bride, is an enclosed garden, an enclosed garden, a sealed spring.

4:13  LA: Emissiones tuæ paradisus malorum punicorum, cum pomorum fructibus, cypri cum nardo.
      EN: Your shoots are a paradise of pomegranates, with orchard fruits, henna with nard,

4:14  LA: Nardus et crocus, fistula et cinnamomum, cum universis lignis Libani ; myrrha et aloë, cum omnibus primis unguentis.
      EN: nard and saffron, cane and cinnamon, with all the trees of Lebanon, myrrh and aloe, with all the finest ointments.

4:15  LA: Fons hortorum, puteus aquarum viventium, quæ fluunt impetu de Libano.
      EN: A spring of gardens, a well of living waters flowing swiftly from Lebanon.

4:16  LA: Surge, aquilo, et veni, auster : perfla hortum meum, et fluant aromata illius.]
      EN: [Bride] Rise, north wind, and come, south wind; blow through my garden, and let its spices flow.

SONG OF SONGS 5

5:1  LA: Veniat dilectus meus in hortum suum, et comedat fructum pomorum suorum. <Sponsus>Veni in hortum meum, soror mea, sponsa ; messui myrrham meam cum aromatibus meis ; comedi favum cum melle meo ; bibi vinum meum cum lacte meo ; comedite, amici, et bibite, et inebriamini, carissimi.
     EN: Let my beloved come into his garden and eat the fruit of his fruit trees. [Bridegroom] I have come into my garden, my sister, my bride; I have gathered my myrrh with my spices, eaten my honeycomb with my honey, and drunk my wine with my milk. Eat, friends, and drink; become drunk, dearest ones.

5:2  LA: Ego dormio, et cor meum vigilat. Vox dilecti mei pulsantis : <Sponsus>Aperi mihi, soror mea, amica mea, columba mea, immaculata mea, quia caput meum plenum est rore, et cincinni mei guttis noctium.
     EN: [Bride] I sleep, but my heart keeps watch. The voice of my beloved knocking! [Bridegroom] Open to me, my sister, my friend, my dove, my unblemished one, for my head is full of dew and my curls of the drops of the night.

5:3  LA: Expoliavi me tunica mea : quomodo induar illa ? lavi pedes meos : quomodo inquinabo illos ?
     EN: [Bride] I have taken off my tunic; how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet; how shall I soil them?

5:4  LA: Dilectus meus misit manum suam per foramen, et venter meus intremuit ad tactum ejus.
     EN: My beloved put his hand through the opening, and my belly trembled at his touch.

5:5  LA: Surrexi ut aperirem dilecto meo ; manus meæ stillaverunt myrrham, et digiti mei pleni myrrha probatissima.
     EN: I rose to open to my beloved; my hands dripped myrrh, and my fingers were full of the finest myrrh.

5:6  LA: Pessulum ostii mei aperui dilecto meo, at ille declinaverat, atque transierat. Anima mea liquefacta est, ut locutus est ; quæsivi, et non inveni illum ; vocavi, et non respondit mihi.
     EN: I opened the bolt of my door to my beloved, but he had turned aside and gone away. My soul melted when he spoke. I sought him and did not find him; I called him, and he did not answer me.

5:7  LA: Invenerunt me custodes qui circumeunt civitatem ; percusserunt me, et vulneraverunt me. Tulerunt pallium meum mihi custodes murorum.
     EN: The guards who go about the city found me; they struck me and wounded me. The guards of the walls took my mantle from me.

5:8  LA: Adjuro vos, filiæ Jerusalem, si inveneritis dilectum meum, ut nuntietis ei quia amore langueo.
     EN: I charge you, daughters of Jerusalem, if you find my beloved, tell him that I languish with love.

5:9  LA: Qualis est dilectus tuus ex dilecto, o pulcherrima mulierum ? qualis est dilectus tuus ex dilecto, quia sic adjurasti nos ?
     EN: [Chorus] How is your beloved different from another beloved, most beautiful among women? How is your beloved different from another beloved, that you have charged us so?

5:10  LA: Dilectus meus candidus et rubicundus ; electus ex millibus.
      EN: [Bride] My beloved is white and ruddy, chosen from thousands.

5:11  LA: Caput ejus aurum optimum ; comæ ejus sicut elatæ palmarum, nigræ quasi corvus.
      EN: His head is the finest gold; his hair is like the lofty fronds of palms, black as a raven.

5:12  LA: Oculi ejus sicut columbæ super rivulos aquarum, quæ lacte sunt lotæ, et resident juxta fluenta plenissima.
      EN: His eyes are like doves beside streams of water, washed in milk and resting beside overflowing currents.

5:13  LA: Genæ illius sicut areolæ aromatum, consitæ a pigmentariis. Labia ejus lilia, distillantia myrrham primam.
      EN: His cheeks are like little beds of spices planted by perfumers. His lips are lilies, dripping the finest myrrh.

5:14  LA: Manus illius tornatiles, aureæ, plenæ hyacinthis. Venter ejus eburneus, distinctus sapphiris.
      EN: His hands are finely rounded, golden, set with jacinths. His belly is ivory, adorned with sapphires.

5:15  LA: Crura illius columnæ marmoreæ quæ fundatæ sunt super bases aureas. Species ejus ut Libani, electus ut cedri.
      EN: His legs are marble columns founded upon golden bases. His appearance is like Lebanon, choice as the cedars.

5:16  LA: Guttur illius suavissimum, et totus desiderabilis. Talis est dilectus meus, et ipse est amicus meus, filiæ Jerusalem.
      EN: His throat is most sweet, and he is altogether desirable. Such is my beloved, and he is my friend, daughters of Jerusalem.

SONG OF SONGS 6

6:1  LA: Quo abiit dilectus tuus, o pulcherrima mulierum ? quo declinavit dilectus tuus ? et quæremus eum tecum.]
     EN: [Chorus] Where has your beloved gone, most beautiful among women? Where has your beloved turned aside, so that we may seek him with you?

6:2  LA: Dilectus meus descendit in hortum suum ad areolam aromatum, ut pascatur in hortis, et lilia colligat.
     EN: [Bride] My beloved has gone down to his garden, to the little bed of spices, to feed in the gardens and gather lilies.

6:3  LA: Ego dilecto meo, et dilectus meus mihi, qui pascitur inter lilia.
     EN: I am my beloved's, and my beloved is mine, who feeds among the lilies.

6:4  LA: Pulchra es, amica mea ; suavis, et decora sicut Jerusalem ; terribilis ut castrorum acies ordinata.
     EN: [Bridegroom] You are beautiful, my friend, sweet and lovely as Jerusalem, fearsome as an army drawn up in battle order.

6:5  LA: Averte oculos tuos a me, quia ipsi me avolare fecerunt. Capilli tui sicut grex caprarum quæ apparuerunt de Galaad.
     EN: Turn your eyes away from me, for they have made me fly away. Your hair is like a flock of goats that have appeared from Gilead.

6:6  LA: Dentes tui sicut grex ovium quæ ascenderunt de lavacro : omnes gemellis fœtibus, et sterilis non est in eis.
     EN: Your teeth are like a flock of sheep that have come up from washing, all bearing twins, with none barren among them.

6:7  LA: Sicut cortex mali punici, sic genæ tuæ, absque occultis tuis.
     EN: Your cheeks are like the rind of a pomegranate, apart from what is hidden in you.

6:8  LA: Sexaginta sunt reginæ, et octoginta concubinæ, et adolescentularum non est numerus.
     EN: There are sixty queens and eighty concubines, and young women beyond number.

6:9  LA: Una est columba mea, perfecta mea, una est matris suæ, electa genetrici suæ. Viderunt eam filiæ, et beatissimam prædicaverunt ; reginæ et concubinæ, et laudaverunt eam.
     EN: My dove, my perfect one, is one alone, her mother's only one, the chosen one of her who bore her. The daughters saw her and proclaimed her most blessed; the queens and concubines praised her.

6:10  LA: Quæ est ista quæ progreditur quasi aurora consurgens, pulchra ut luna, electa ut sol, terribilis ut castrorum acies ordinata ?
      EN: Who is this who comes forth like the rising dawn, beautiful as the moon, choice as the sun, fearsome as an army drawn up in battle order?

6:11  LA: Descendi in hortum nucum, ut viderem poma convallium, et inspicerem si floruisset vinea, et germinassent mala punica.
      EN: [Bride] I went down to the garden of nuts to see the fruits of the valleys and examine whether the vine had flowered and the pomegranates had put forth shoots.

6:12  LA: Nescivi : anima mea conturbavit me, propter quadrigas Aminadab.
      EN: I did not know; my soul troubled me because of Amminadab's four-horse chariots.

SONG OF SONGS 7

7:1  LA: Revertere, revertere, Sulamitis ! revertere, revertere ut intueamur te.]
     EN: [Chorus] Return, return, Shulammite! Return, return, that we may look upon you. [Bride] What will you see in the Shulammite, except the dances of the camps? [Chorus] How beautiful are your steps in sandals, prince's daughter! The joints of your thighs are like necklaces made by the hand of an artisan.

7:2  LA: Revertere, revertere, Sulamitis ! revertere, revertere ut intueamur te.]
     EN: [Chorus] Return, return, Shulammite! Return, return, that we may look upon you. [Bride] What will you see in the Shulammite, except the dances of the camps? [Chorus] How beautiful are your steps in sandals, prince's daughter! The joints of your thighs are like necklaces made by the hand of an artisan.

7:3  LA: Umbilicus tuus crater tornatilis, numquam indigens poculis. Venter tuus sicut acervus tritici vallatus liliis.
     EN: Your navel is a rounded bowl, never lacking drink. Your belly is like a heap of wheat surrounded by lilies.

7:4  LA: Duo ubera tua sicut duo hinnuli, gemelli capreæ.
     EN: Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle.

7:5  LA: Collum tuum sicut turris eburnea ; oculi tui sicut piscinæ in Hesebon quæ sunt in porta filiæ multitudinis. Nasus tuus sicut turris Libani, quæ respicit contra Damascum.
     EN: Your neck is like an ivory tower; your eyes are like the pools in Heshbon at the gate of the daughter of the multitude. Your nose is like the tower of Lebanon that looks toward Damascus.

7:6  LA: Caput tuum ut Carmelus ; et comæ capitis tui sicut purpura regis vincta canalibus.
     EN: Your head is like Carmel, and the hair of your head is like the king's purple bound in channels.

7:7  LA: Quam pulchra es, et quam decora, carissima, in deliciis !
     EN: [Bridegroom] How beautiful you are, how lovely, dearest one, in your delights!

7:8  LA: Statura tua assimilata est palmæ, et ubera tua botris.
     EN: Your stature is like a palm tree, and your breasts like clusters of fruit.

7:9  LA: Dixi : Ascendam in palmam, et apprehendam fructus ejus ; et erunt ubera tua sicut botri vineæ, et odor oris tui sicut malorum.
     EN: I said: I will climb the palm tree and take hold of its fruits. Your breasts will be like clusters on the vine, and the fragrance of your mouth like apples.

7:10  LA: Guttur tuum sicut vinum optimum, dignum dilecto meo ad potandum, labiisque et dentibus illius ad ruminandum.
      EN: Your throat is like the best wine, worthy for my beloved to drink and for his lips and teeth to savor.

7:11  LA: Ego dilecto meo, et ad me conversio ejus.
      EN: [Bride] I am my beloved's, and his turning is toward me.

7:12  LA: Veni, dilecte mi, egrediamur in agrum, commoremur in villis.
      EN: Come, my beloved, let us go out into the field; let us stay in the villages.

7:13  LA: Mane surgamus ad vineas : videamus si floruit vinea, si flores fructus parturiunt, si floruerunt mala punica ; ibi dabo tibi ubera mea.
      EN: Let us rise early to go to the vineyards and see whether the vine has flowered, whether the flowers are bringing forth fruit, whether the pomegranates have blossomed. There I will give you my breasts.

7:14  LA: Mandragoræ dederunt odorem in portis nostris omnia poma : nova et vetera, dilecte mi, servavi tibi.]
      EN: The mandrakes have given their fragrance; at our gates are all kinds of fruit. New and old, my beloved, I have kept them for you.

SONG OF SONGS 8

8:1  LA: Quis mihi det te fratrem meum, sugentem ubera matris meæ, ut inveniam te foris, et deosculer te, et jam me nemo despiciat ?
     EN: Who would give you to me as my brother, nursing at my mother's breasts, so that I might find you outside and kiss you, and no one would despise me any longer?

8:2  LA: Apprehendam te, et ducam in domum matris meæ : ibi me docebis, et dabo tibi poculum ex vino condito, et mustum malorum granatorum meorum.
     EN: I will take hold of you and lead you into my mother's house. There you will teach me, and I will give you a cup of spiced wine and the fresh juice of my pomegranates.

8:3  LA: Læva ejus sub capite meo, et dextera illius amplexabitur me.
     EN: His left hand is beneath my head, and his right hand will embrace me.

8:4  LA: Adjuro vos, filiæ Jerusalem, ne suscitetis, neque evigilare faciatis dilectam, donec ipsa velit.
     EN: [Bridegroom] I charge you, daughters of Jerusalem, do not rouse or awaken the beloved until she herself wishes.

8:5  LA: Quæ est ista quæ ascendit de deserto, deliciis affluens, innixa super dilectum suum ? <Sponsus>Sub arbore malo suscitavi te ; ibi corrupta est mater tua, ibi violata est genitrix tua.
     EN: [Chorus] Who is this who comes up from the wilderness, abounding in delights, leaning upon her beloved? [Bridegroom] Beneath the apple tree I awakened you; there your mother was corrupted, there she who bore you was violated.

8:6  LA: Pone me ut signaculum super cor tuum, ut signaculum super brachium tuum, quia fortis est ut mors dilectio, dura sicut infernus æmulatio : lampades ejus lampades ignis atque flammarum.
     EN: [Bride] Set me as a seal upon your heart, as a seal upon your arm, for love is strong as death, jealousy hard as the underworld. Its torches are torches of fire and flames.

8:7  LA: Aquæ multæ non potuerunt extinguere caritatem, nec flumina obruent illam. Si dederit homo omnem substantiam domus suæ pro dilectione, quasi nihil despiciet eam.
     EN: Many waters have not been able to extinguish love, nor will rivers overwhelm it. If a man gave all the wealth of his house for love, he would despise it as nothing.

8:8  LA: <Chorus Fratrum>Soror nostra parva, et ubera non habet ; quid faciemus sorori nostræ in die quando alloquenda est ?
     EN: [Chorus of Brothers] Our sister is little and has no breasts; what shall we do for our sister on the day when she is to be addressed?

8:9  LA: Si murus est, ædificemus super eum propugnacula argentea ; si ostium est, compingamus illud tabulis cedrinis.
     EN: If she is a wall, let us build silver battlements upon it; if she is a door, let us join cedar boards around it.

8:10  LA: Ego murus, et ubera mea sicut turris, ex quo facta sum coram eo, quasi pacem reperiens.
      EN: [Bride] I am a wall, and my breasts are like a tower, since I have become before him like one who finds peace.

8:11  LA: <Chorus Fratrum>Vinea fuit pacifico in ea quæ habet populos : tradidit eam custodibus ; vir affert pro fructu ejus mille argenteos.
      EN: [Chorus of Brothers] The peaceful one had a vineyard in the place that has peoples. He entrusted it to keepers; a man brings a thousand silver pieces for its fruit.

8:12  LA: Vinea mea coram me est. Mille tui pacifici, et ducenti his qui custodiunt fructus ejus.
      EN: [Bride] My vineyard is before me. The thousand belong to your peaceful one, and two hundred to those who guard its fruit.

8:13  LA: Quæ habitas in hortis, amici auscultant ; fac me audire vocem tuam.
      EN: [Bridegroom] You who dwell in the gardens, friends are listening; let me hear your voice.

8:14  LA: Fuge, dilecte mi, et assimilare capreæ, hinnuloque cervorum super montes aromatum.]
      EN: [Bride] Flee, my beloved, and be like a gazelle or a young stag upon the mountains of spices.
