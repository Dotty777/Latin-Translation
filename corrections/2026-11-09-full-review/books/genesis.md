## Proposed English corrections (17)

**Genesis 1:20** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Dixit etiam Deus : Producant aquæ reptile animæ viventis, et volatile super terram sub firmamento cæli.
- Now: God also said, “Let the waters bring forth swarming living creatures, and winged creatures above the earth beneath the firmament of heaven.”
- Proposed: God also said, “Let the waters bring forth creeping living creatures, and winged creatures above the earth beneath the firmament of heaven.”
- Why: "reptile animæ viventis" is the creeping creature having life (reptile, from repere, to creep; rendered "creeping creatures" in 1:24-26). "Swarming" follows the Hebrew, not the Latin.

**Genesis 1:27** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et creavit Deus hominem ad imaginem suam : ad imaginem Dei creavit illum, masculum et feminam creavit eos.
- Now: God created humankind in His image; in the image of God He created them; male and female He created them.
- Proposed: God created humankind in His image; in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them.
- Why: "creavit illum" is singular (him), set against the plural "creavit eos"; the English makes both "them", losing the Latin's change of number (cf. 5:1 "fecit illum", rendered "him").

**Genesis 4:23** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Dixitque Lamech uxoribus suis Adæ et Sellæ : [Audite vocem meam, uxores Lamech ; auscultate sermonem meum : quoniam occidi virum in vulnus meum, et adolescentulum in livorem meum.
- Now: Lamech said to his wives Adah and Zillah, “Hear my voice, wives of Lamech; listen to my words. For I have killed a man for wounding me, and a young man for bruising me.
- Proposed: Lamech said to his wives Adah and Zillah, “Hear my voice, wives of Lamech; listen to my words. For I have killed a man to my own wounding, and a young man to my own bruising.
- Why: "in vulnus meum ... in livorem meum" = to my (own) wounding / bruising (in + accusative of result). "For wounding me / for bruising me" gives the motive as in modern Hebrew-based versions, which the Latin does not say.

**Genesis 15:16** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Generatione autem quarta revertentur huc : necdum enim completæ sunt iniquitates Amorrhæorum usque ad præsens tempus.
- Now: In the fourth generation they shall return here, for the iniquities of the Amorites have not yet reached their fullness.”
- Proposed: In the fourth generation they shall return here, for the iniquities of the Amorites have not yet reached their fullness up to the present time.”
- Why: The closing phrase "usque ad præsens tempus" (until the present time) is left untranslated.

**Genesis 17:14** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Masculus, cujus præputii caro circumcisa non fuerit, delebitur anima illa de populo suo : quia pactum meum irritum fecit.
- Now: An uncircumcised male, whose foreskin's flesh has not been circumcised—that person shall be cut off from his people, because he has broken My covenant.”
- Proposed: A male whose foreskin's flesh has not been circumcised—that person shall be cut off from his people, because he has broken My covenant.”
- Why: The Latin has only "Masculus, cujus præputii caro circumcisa non fuerit"; "uncircumcised" before "male" is not in the Latin and matches the Hebrew/KJV wording ("the uncircumcised male").

**Genesis 21:7** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Rursumque ait : Quis auditurus crederet Abraham quod Sara lactaret filium, quem peperit ei jam seni ?
- Now: Again she said, “Who would have believed, on hearing it, that Abraham's Sarah would nurse a son whom she bore him in his old age?”
- Proposed: Again she said, “Who would have believed Abraham, on hearing it, that Sarah would nurse a son whom she bore him in his old age?”
- Why: "Abraham" stands in the main clause before "quod" (with "crederet"/"auditurus"), not as a genitive of Sara; "Abraham's Sarah" misconstrues "Quis auditurus crederet Abraham quod Sara lactaret filium".

**Genesis 21:17** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Exaudivit autem Deus vocem pueri : vocavitque angelus Dei Agar de cælo, dicens : Quid agis Agar ? noli timere : exaudivit enim Deus vocem pueri de loco in quo est.
- Now: God heard the boy's voice. The angel of God called to Hagar from heaven, saying, “What troubles you, Hagar? Do not fear, for God has heard the boy's voice from the place where he is.
- Proposed: God heard the boy's voice. The angel of God called to Hagar from heaven, saying, “What are you doing, Hagar? Do not fear, for God has heard the boy's voice from the place where he is.
- Why: "Quid agis Agar ?" = What are you doing, Hagar? "What troubles you" is the modern Hebrew-based wording ("What ails thee"), not the Latin.

**Genesis 22:6** · other-text · minor

- Now: He took the wood for the whole burnt offering and laid it upon his son Isaac, while he himself carried the fire and the knife in his hands. As the two walked together,
- Proposed: He took the wood for the whole burnt offering and laid it upon his son Isaac, while he himself carried the fire and the sword in his hands. As the two walked together,
- Why: "gladium" is a sword; "knife" follows the Hebrew-based versions rather than the Latin word.

- Now: He stretched out his hand and grasped the knife to sacrifice his son.
- Proposed: He stretched out his hand and grasped the sword to sacrifice his son.

**Genesis 24:65** · other-text · minor

- Latin: et ait ad puerum : Quis est ille homo qui venit per agrum in occursum nobis ? Dixitque ei : Ipse est dominus meus. At illa tollens cito pallium, operuit se.
- Now: She said to the servant, “Who is that man coming through the field to meet us?” He said, “He is my master.” She quickly took her veil and covered herself.
- Proposed: She said to the servant, “Who is that man coming through the field to meet us?” He said, “He is my master.” She quickly took her cloak and covered herself.
- Why: "tollens cito pallium": pallium is a cloak or mantle; "veil" is the wording of the Hebrew-based versions, not of the Latin.

**Genesis 25:27** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Quibus adultis, factus est Esau vir gnarus venandi, et homo agricola : Jacob autem vir simplex habitabat in tabernaculis.
- Now: When they grew up, Esau became a skilled hunter and a man of the fields, while Jacob was a simple man who lived in tents.
- Proposed: When they grew up, Esau became a skilled hunter and a farmer, while Jacob was a simple man who lived in tents.
- Why: "homo agricola" = a husbandman, a tiller of the soil (so rendered at 4:2 and 9:20); "a man of the fields" reproduces the Hebrew idiom, not the Latin.

**Genesis 26:17** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et ille discedens, ut veniret ad torrentem Geraræ, habitaretque ibi,
- Now: He departed, coming to the valley of Gerar to live there,
- Proposed: He departed, coming to the torrent of Gerar to live there,
- Why: "ad torrentem Geraræ": torrens is a torrent or stream; "valley of Gerar" is the KJV/Hebrew-based wording.

**Genesis 26:19** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Foderuntque in torrente, et repererunt aquam vivam.
- Now: They dug in the valley and found flowing water.
- Proposed: They dug in the torrent and found flowing water.
- Why: "in torrente" = in the torrent (stream bed); "valley" is the KJV/Hebrew-based wording, as in 26:17.

**Genesis 31:29** · meaning · minor

- Latin: valet manus mea reddere tibi malum : sed Deus patris vestri heri dixit mihi : Cave ne loquaris contra Jacob quidquam durius.
- Now: my hand has power to repay you with harm, but yesterday the God of your fathers said to me, ‘Take care not to speak anything harsh against Jacob.’
- Proposed: my hand has power to repay you with harm, but yesterday the God of your father said to me, ‘Take care not to speak anything harsh against Jacob.’
- Why: "Deus patris vestri" has the singular patris: the God of your father. The English plural "your fathers" changes the number.

**Genesis 33:1** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Elevans autem Jacob oculos suos, vidit venientem Esau, et cum eo quadringentos viros : divisitque filios Liæ et Rachel, ambarumque famularum :
- Now: Jacob raised his eyes and saw Esau coming, and with him four hundred men. He divided the children among Leah, Rachel, and the two maidservants.
- Proposed: Jacob raised his eyes and saw Esau coming, and with him four hundred men. He divided the children of Leah, of Rachel, and of the two maidservants.
- Why: "filios Liæ et Rachel, ambarumque famularum" are genitives: he divided the children of Leah, Rachel and both handmaids. "Among Leah, Rachel..." is the Hebrew construction, not this Latin.

**Genesis 33:11** · meaning · minor

- Latin: et suscipe benedictionem quam attuli tibi, et quam donavit mihi Deus tribuens omnia. Vix fratre compellente, suscipiens,
- Now: and accept the blessing I have brought you, which God gave me by granting me everything.” Only at his brother's urging did he accept it,
- Proposed: and accept the blessing I have brought you, which God, who grants all things, gave me.” Only at his brother's urging did he accept it,
- Why: "Deus tribuens omnia" describes God as the one who bestows all things; the Latin has no "me" and no instrumental sense ("by granting me everything").

**Genesis 36:37** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Hoc quoque mortuo regnavit pro eo Saul de fluvio Rohoboth.
- Now: When he too died, Saul from Rehoboth by the river reigned in his place.
- Proposed: When he too died, Saul from the river Rehoboth reigned in his place.
- Why: "de fluvio Rohoboth" is "from the river Rohoboth"; "Rehoboth by the river" is the Hebrew phrasing, not the Latin's.

## Notes on the recorded Latin (7)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **Genesis 6:6** · suspected-typo · same as reference: "quod hominum fecisset" is ungrammatical (genitive plural with no governing noun); the Clementine reads "pœnituit eum quod hominem fecisset in terra". The English already renders the correct sense.
- **Genesis 20:2** · suspected-typo · same as reference: "de Sara uxore suo" has the wrong gender; the Clementine reads "Dixitque de Sara uxore sua : Soror mea est." English unaffected.
- **Genesis 43:10** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin reads "jam vice alter venissemus"; "alter" cannot agree with feminine ablative "vice". Clementine reads "jam vice altera venissemus" (a second time). The English already renders the correct reading.
- **Genesis 46:29** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin reads "juncto Joseph curro suo"; "currus" is fourth declension and the Clementine has "juncto Joseph curru suo" (his chariot being harnessed). The English is unaffected.
- **Genesis 49:2** · bracket: Latin begins with a stray opening bracket, "[Congregamini" (source edition's verse-section markup, closed at 49:27). Corrected Latin: "Congregamini, et audite, filii Jacob, audite Israël patrem vestrum :"
- **Genesis 49:27** · bracket: Latin ends with a stray closing bracket, "spolia.]" (verse-section markup opened at 49:2). Corrected Latin: "Benjamin lupus rapax, mane comedat prædam, et vespere dividet spolia."
- **Genesis 49:32 (Vulgate 49:30)** · coverage · same as reference: Duplicated verse: this row repeats Vg 49:30 ("contra Mambre in terra Chanaan, quam emit Abraham...") already shown in full at 49:30 (#52); the Vulgate has no separate text for this site verse.
