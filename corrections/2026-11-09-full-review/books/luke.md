## Proposed English corrections (12)

**Luke 1:19** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Et respondens angelus dixit ei: Ego sum Gabriel, qui asto ante Deum: et missus sum loqui ad te, et hæc tibi evangelizare.
- Now: And the angel answered him: I am Gabriel, who stand before God and am sent to speak to you and to bring you these good news.
- Proposed: And the angel answered him: I am Gabriel, who stand before God and am sent to speak to you and to bring you this good news.
- Why: Grammatical slip from modernising: 'these good news' (for 'hæc tibi evangelizare'); 'news' is singular in modern English, so 'this good news'.

**Luke 2:24** · meaning · minor

- Latin: et ut darent hostiam secundum quod dictum est in lege Domini, par turturum, aut duos pullos columbarum.
- Now: And to offer a sacrifice, just as it is written in the law of the Lord, a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons:
- Proposed: And to offer a sacrifice, just as it is said in the law of the Lord, a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons:
- Why: 'secundum quod dictum est' means 'as it is said', not 'written' (contrast 'scriptum est' in v. 23).

**Luke 5:15** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Perambulabat autem magis sermo de illo: et conveniebant turbæ multæ ut audirent, et curarentur ab infirmitatibus suis.
- Now: But the fame of him went abroad the more: and great multitudes came together to hear and to be healed by him of their infirmities.
- Proposed: But the fame of him went abroad the more: and great multitudes came together to hear and to be healed of their infirmities.
- Why: 'by him' has no Latin behind it: 'ut audirent, et curarentur ab infirmitatibus suis' has no 'ab eo'; the Douay wording reflects the Greek received text.

**Luke 8:22** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Factum est autem in una dierum: et ipse ascendit in naviculam, et discipuli ejus, et ait ad illos: Transfretemus trans stagnum. Et ascenderunt.
- Now: And it happened on a certain day that he went into a little boat with his disciples. And he said to them: Let us go over to the other side of the lake. And they launched forth.
- Proposed: And it happened on a certain day that he went into a little boat with his disciples. And he said to them: Let us go over to the other side of the lake. And they went aboard.
- Why: 'Et ascenderunt' means 'they went up/embarked' (same verb as 'ascendit in naviculam'); 'launched forth' renders the Greek verb, not the Latin.

**Luke 9:55** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Et conversus increpavit illos, dicens: Nescitis cujus spiritus estis.
- Now: And turning, he rebuked them, saying: you do not know of what spirit you are.
- Proposed: And turning, he rebuked them, saying: You do not know of what spirit you are.
- Why: Lower-case 'you' begins the quoted speech ('Nescitis cujus spiritus estis'); elsewhere speech after a colon is capitalised.

**Luke 11:35** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Vide ergo ne lumen quod in te est, tenebræ sint.
- Now: Take care therefore that the light which is in you do not be darkness.
- Proposed: Take care therefore that the light which is in you be not darkness.
- Why: Ungrammatical 'do not be darkness' for 'ne ... tenebræ sint'; the subjunctive needs 'be not darkness'.

**Luke 12:26** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Si ergo neque quod minimum est potestis, quid de ceteris solliciti estis?
- Now: If then you do not be able to do so much as the least thing, why are you anxious for the rest?
- Proposed: If then you are not able to do so much as the least thing, why are you anxious for the rest?
- Why: Ungrammatical 'you do not be able' for 'neque ... potestis' ('you are not able'); a slip from modernising 'ye be not able'.

**Luke 13:4** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Sicut illi decem et octo, supra quos cecidit turris in Siloë, et occidit eos: putatis quia et ipsi debitores fuerint præter omnes homines habitantes in Jerusalem?
- Now: Or those eighteen upon whom the tower fell in Siloam and slew them: think you that they also were debtors above all the men that dwelt in Jerusalem?
- Proposed: Like those eighteen upon whom the tower fell in Siloam and slew them: think you that they also were debtors above all the men that dwelt in Jerusalem?
- Why: 'Sicut illi decem et octo' is 'Like/as those eighteen'; the opening 'Or' follows the Greek conjunction, not the Latin 'Sicut'.

**Luke 13:10** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Erat autem docens in synagoga eorum sabbatis.
- Now: And he was teaching in their synagogue on their sabbath.
- Proposed: And he was teaching in their synagogue on the sabbath.
- Why: 'sabbatis' has no possessive; 'eorum' belongs only to 'synagoga', so 'on their sabbath' adds a word the Latin lacks.

**Luke 16:8** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et laudavit dominus villicum iniquitatis, quia prudenter fecisset: quia filii hujus sæculi prudentiores filiis lucis in generatione sua sunt.
- Now: The master praised the unjust steward because he had acted prudently. For the children of this age are more prudent toward their own generation than the children of light.
- Proposed: The master praised the unjust steward because he had acted prudently. For the children of this age are more prudent in their generation than the children of light.
- Why: 'in generatione sua' (ablative) is 'in their generation'; 'toward their own generation' renders the Greek preposition, not the Latin.

**Luke 16:15** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Et ait illis: Vos estis qui justificatis vos coram hominibus: Deus autem novit corda vestra: quia quod hominibus altum est, abominatio est ante Deum.
- Now: And he said to them: you are they who justify yourselves before men, but God knows your hearts. For that which is high to men is an abomination before God.
- Proposed: And he said to them: You are they who justify yourselves before men, but God knows your hearts. For that which is high to men is an abomination before God.
- Why: Lower-case 'you' begins the quoted speech ('Vos estis qui justificatis vos'); elsewhere speech after a colon is capitalised.

**Luke 20:5** · meaning · minor

- Latin: At illi cogitabant intra se, dicentes: Quia si dixerimus: De cælo, dicet: Quare ergo non credidistis illi?
- Now: But they thought within themselves, saying: If we shall say, From heaven: he will say: Why then did you not believe in him?
- Proposed: But they thought within themselves, saying: If we shall say, From heaven: he will say: Why then did you not believe him?
- Why: 'credidistis illi' (dative) is 'believed him', i.e. gave credence to John; 'believe in him' adds a sense the Latin lacks.

## Notes on the recorded Latin (8)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **Luke 6:22** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has 'ejicerint', not a valid form; the future perfect parallel to 'separaverint' and 'exprobraverint' is 'ejecerint'. English 'cast out' is correct.
- **Luke 6:37** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has singular 'Dimitte, et dimittemini' amid plural imperatives and a plural passive; the Clementine reads 'Dimittite, et dimittemini'. English 'Forgive: and you shall be forgiven' is correct.
- **Luke 8:2** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin typo 'infirmatibus'; read 'infirmitatibus' ('a spiritibus malignis et infirmitatibus'). English 'infirmities' is correct.
- **Luke 8:40** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin typo 'erunt enim omnes exspectantes eum' (future); read 'erant enim omnes exspectantes eum'. English 'they were all waiting' is correct.
- **Luke 17:37** · coverage · same as reference: Vg 17:36 'Respondentes dicunt illi: Ubi Domine?' is missing: no unit between 17:35 and 17:37 carries it, so 'Qui dixit illis' lacks the disciples' question in both Latin and English.
- **Luke 19:47** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Recorded 'princeps plebis' (singular) appears to be a slip for the Clementine 'principes plebis' (plural, with 'quærebant'); if corrected, English 'the ruler of the people' should read 'the rulers of the people'. Verify against a printed Clementine.
- **Luke 20:36** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Recorded 'mori potuerunt' (perfect) appears to be a typo for the Clementine 'mori poterunt' (future: 'neither shall they be able to die any more'), parallel to 'nubent', 'ducent'. English is unaffected.
- **Luke 23:18** · coverage · same as reference: Clementine Luke 23:17 ('Necesse autem habebat dimittere eis per diem festum unum') is absent from the record: units pass from 23:16 to 23:18 with neither Latin nor English for it.
