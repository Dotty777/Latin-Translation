## Proposed English corrections (43)

**Isaiah 1:18** · meaning · minor

- Latin: [Et venite, et arguite me, dicit Dominus. Si fuerint peccata vestra ut coccinum, quasi nix dealbabuntur ; et si fuerint rubra quasi vermiculus, velut lana alba erunt.
- Now: Come, then, and argue your case with Me, says the Lord. If your sins are like scarlet, they will be made white as snow; if they are red as crimson, they will be white as wool.
- Proposed: Come, then, and accuse Me, says the Lord. If your sins are like scarlet, they will be made white as snow; if they are red as crimson, they will be white as wool.
- Why: "arguite me": me is the direct object of arguere (accuse, reprove me). "Argue your case with Me" follows the Hebrew-style "reason together", not the Latin.

- Why: "arguet populos multos" means He will rebuke or reprove many peoples; "settle disputes for many peoples" is a modern rendering of the Hebrew, not of arguet.

**Isaiah 64:1 (Vulgate 64:2)** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: sicut exustio ignis tabescerent, aquæ arderent igni : ut notum fieret nomen tuum inimicis tuis ; a facie tua gentes turbarentur.
- Now: melt as at fire's burning, and waters would boil in the fire, so that Your name might become known to Your enemies and nations tremble before You!
- Proposed: They would melt as at fire's burning, and waters would boil in the fire, so that Your name might become known to Your enemies and nations tremble before You!
- Why: English opens with a subjectless fragment, 'melt as at fire's burning', because the preceding Vg 64:1 is missing; 'tabescerent' is 'they would melt'.

**Isaiah 66:8** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Quis audivit umquam tale ? et quis vidit huic simile ? numquid parturiet terra in die una, aut parietur gens simul, quia parturivit et peperit Sion filios suos ?
- Now: Who has ever heard such a thing, or seen anything like it? Will the earth bring forth in one day, or a nation be born at once? Yet Zion was in labor and bore her children.
- Proposed: Who has ever heard such a thing, or seen anything like it? Will the earth bring forth in one day, or a nation be born at once? For Zion was in labor and bore her children.
- Why: 'quia parturivit et peperit Sion' is causal, 'because/for Zion has been in labor and borne'; adversative 'Yet' is not a sense of quia.

## Notes on the recorded Latin (19)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **Isaiah 1:19** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin shows present "comeditis" after future-perfect "volueritis ... audieritis"; the English "you will eat" and the parallel "devorabit" (1:20) presuppose future "comedetis". Probable typo; check against the Clementine.
- **Isaiah 2:6** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin typo: "augeres habuerunt" should read "augures habuerunt" (they have had soothsayers); the English already renders augures.
- **Isaiah 2:21** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin shows present "ingreditur"; context (2:19 introibunt, 2:20 projiciet) and the English "will enter" require future "ingredietur". Probable typo; check against the Clementine.
- **Isaiah 3:1** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin spelling slip: "omne robor aquæ" should read "omne robur aquæ", matching "omne robur panis" just before.
- **Isaiah 3:18** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin typo: "ornamentum calceamentum" should read "ornamentum calceamentorum" (the adornment of the shoes), as the English renders.
- **Isaiah 5:25** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin typo: "non est adversus furor ejus" should read "non est aversus furor ejus" (His fury is not turned away), as in the refrain at 9:12, 9:17, 9:21, 10:4 and as the English renders.
- **Isaiah 8:21** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin typo: "suscipiet sursum" (will receive) should read "suspiciet sursum" (will look upward), as the English renders.
- **Isaiah 9:4 (Vulgate 9:5)** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin typo: "omnis violentia prædatio" should read "omnis violenta prædatio" (every violent plundering), adjective agreeing with prædatio, as the English renders.
- **Isaiah 14:29** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin typo: "Ne lætaris" (indicative) should read "Ne læteris" (prohibitive subjunctive, do not rejoice), as the English renders.
- **Isaiah 16:9** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin typo: "inebriabo de lacrima mea" should read "inebriabo te lacrima mea" (I will drench you with my tears); the English "you" renders te.
- **Isaiah 20:1** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin typo in the name: "Thathan" should read "Tharthan" (Tartan), as the English renders.
- **Isaiah 22:18** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin typo: "Coronas coronabit te" should read "Coronans coronabit te tribulatione" (participle with finite verb, crowning He will crown you).
- **Isaiah 23:5** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin spelling slip: "de Tiro" should read "de Tyro" (of Tyre), as elsewhere in the chapter (Tyri, Tyrum, Tyro).
- **Isaiah 24:11** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin typo: "deserta est omnia lætitia" should read "deserta est omnis lætitia" (all gladness), nominative feminine singular agreeing with lætitia.
- **Isaiah 40:18** · suspected-typo · same as reference: 'fecisti' (singular) stands beside plural 'ponetis'; the Clementine reads 'Cui ergo similem fecistis Deum ?'. Probable dropped final -s in the recorded Latin.
- **Isaiah 43:3** · suspected-typo · same as reference: 'Æthopiam' is a misspelling in the recorded Latin; read 'Æthiopiam'.
- **Isaiah 56:11** · suspected-typo · same as reference: 'imprudentissimi' is a misprint; the Clementine reads 'canes impudentissimi' (most shameless dogs). The English 'utterly senseless' renders the misprint and would need 'utterly shameless' once the Latin is corrected.
- **Isaiah 56:11** · coverage · same as reference: Isaiah 56:12 ('Venite, sumamus vinum, et impleamur ebrietate ; et erit sicut hodie, sic et cras, et multo amplius.]') has no unit: the record passes from 56:11 to 57:1, so the verse is missing in Latin and English.
- **Isaiah 63:19** · coverage · same as reference: Vg 64:1 is absent from the record: after 63:19 the Latin should continue 'Utinam dirumperes cælos, et descenderes ; a facie tua montes defluerent.' Neither this unit nor the next (Vg 64:2) carries it, in Latin or English.

## Clementine verses with no Latin in the record (3)

Vulgate numbering: 2:22, 56:12, 64:1.

## Withdrawn on second reading (2)

- **Isaiah 25:4**: 'spes a turbine', hope as protection from the whirlwind, is idiomatically 'refuge', as Douay also reads; 'a hope from the whirlwind' is poorer English with no gain.
- **Isaiah 66:11**: 'ab omnimoda gloria ejus' (glory of every kind) is fairly paraphrased as 'abundance of her glory', as Douay also reads; the difference is stylistic.
