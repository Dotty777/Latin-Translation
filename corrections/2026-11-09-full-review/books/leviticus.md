## Proposed English corrections (4)

**Leviticus 6:2 (Vulgate 6:9)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Præcipe Aaron et filiis ejus : Hæc est lex holocausti : cremabitur in altari tota nocte usque mane : ignis ex eodem altari erit.
- Now: Command Aaron and his sons: This is the law of the whole burnt offering. It shall burn upon the altar all night until morning; the fire shall remain on that same altar.
- Proposed: Command Aaron and his sons: This is the law of the whole burnt offering. It shall burn upon the altar all night until morning; the fire shall be from that same altar.
- Why: "ignis ex eodem altari erit" says the fire shall be of/from that same altar (its source); "shall remain on that same altar" misrenders "ex ... erit" and echoes the Hebrew sense instead.

**Leviticus 7:35** · meaning · major

- Latin: Hæc est unctio Aaron et filiorum ejus in cæremoniis Domini die qua obtulit eos Moyses, ut sacerdotio fungerentur,
- Now: This is the portion belonging to Aaron and his sons by their anointing in the Lord's sacred rites, from the day Moses presented them to exercise the priesthood.
- Proposed: This is the anointing of Aaron and his sons in the Lord's sacred rites, on the day Moses presented them to exercise the priesthood.
- Why: "Hæc est unctio Aaron et filiorum ejus ... die qua" = this is the anointing ... on the day; English adds "the portion belonging to ... by" (Hebrew-based sense) and turns "die qua" into "from the day".

**Leviticus 11:42** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Quidquid super pectus quadrupes graditur, et multos habet pedes, sive per humum trahitur, non comedetis, quia abominabile est.
- Now: Whatever moves upon its belly, goes on four feet, has many feet, or drags itself along the ground, you shall not eat, because it is abhorrent.
- Proposed: Whatever goes upon its breast on four feet, has many feet, or drags itself along the ground, you shall not eat, because it is abhorrent.
- Why: "super pectus quadrupes graditur" is one class (a four-footed thing going on its breast); English splits it into "moves upon its belly, goes on four feet" as in the Hebrew, and "pectus" is breast, not belly.

**Leviticus 24:3** · meaning · minor

- Latin: extra velum testimonii in tabernaculo fœderis. Ponetque eas Aaron a vespere usque ad mane coram Domino, cultu rituque perpetuo in generationibus vestris.
- Now: outside the veil of the testimony in the tabernacle of the covenant. Aaron shall tend them before the Lord from evening until morning, as a perpetual observance and rite throughout your generations.
- Proposed: outside the veil of the testimony in the tabernacle of the covenant. Aaron shall set them before the Lord from evening until morning, as a perpetual observance and rite throughout your generations.
- Why: "Ponetque eas Aaron" = Aaron shall set/place them (the lamps); "tend" is not a sense of "ponere" (compare "ponentur" rendered "set" in the next verse).

## Notes on the recorded Latin (4)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **Leviticus 7:38** · suspected-typo · same as reference: "quando mandabit" (future) is an apparent typo for the perfect "quando mandavit filiis Israël" (when He commanded), as the past context and the English require.
- **Leviticus 15:15** · suspected-typo · same as reference: "a fluxi seminis sui" is an apparent typo; the ablative required after "a" is "a fluxu seminis sui" (from his flow of semen), as the English already renders.
- **Leviticus 22:11** · suspected-typo · same as reference: "his comedent ex eis" is an apparent typo; the subject pronoun is required: "hi comedent ex eis" (these shall eat of them), as the English renders.
- **Leviticus 26:44** · suspected-typo · same as reference: "et irritum facerent pactum meum" is probably a slip for "facerem" (and I should make void My covenant), God being the subject as in "abjeci ... despexi"; the English follows the recorded "facerent". Verify against a printed Clementine.
