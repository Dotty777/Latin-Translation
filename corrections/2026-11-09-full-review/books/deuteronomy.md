# Deuteronomy · Latin–English review, 9 November 2026

957 distinct readings read against the Clementine Latin (957 reader rows). [Method and limits](../README.md). Nothing below has been applied to the staging site.

## Proposed English corrections (8)

**Deuteronomy 3:4** · meaning · minor

- Latin: vastantes cunctas civitates illius uno tempore. Non fuit oppidum, quod nos effugeret : sexaginta urbes, omnem regionem Argob regni Og in Basan.
- Now: laying waste all his cities at that time. No town escaped us: sixty cities, the entire region of Argob, the kingdom of Og in Bashan.
- Proposed: laying waste all his cities at one time. No town escaped us: sixty cities, the entire region of Argob, the kingdom of Og in Bashan.
- Why: "uno tempore" means at one time, all at once; "at that time" renders the Hebrew phrase (illo tempore), not this Latin.

**Deuteronomy 3:17** · other-text · minor

- Latin: et planitiem solitudinis, atque Jordanem, et terminos Cenereth usque ad mare deserti, quod est salsissimum, ad radices montis Phasga contra orientem.
- Now: also the wilderness plain, the Jordan and its territory, from Chinnereth to the sea of the wilderness, which is exceedingly salty, at the foot of Mount Pisgah to the east.
- Proposed: also the wilderness plain, and the Jordan, and the borders of Chinnereth as far as the sea of the wilderness, which is exceedingly salty, at the foot of Mount Pisgah to the east.
- Why: "atque Jordanem, et terminos Cenereth usque ad mare deserti": the Jordan and the borders of Chinnereth as far as the sea; "the Jordan and its territory, from Chinnereth" follows the Hebrew.

**Deuteronomy 24:5** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Cum acceperit homo nuper uxorem, non procedet ad bellum, nec ei quippiam necessitatis injungetur publicæ, sed vacabit absque culpa domi suæ, ut uno anno lætetur cum uxore sua.
- Now: When a man has recently married a wife, he shall not go out to war or be assigned any public duty. He shall remain at home free of obligation, to rejoice with his wife for one year.
- Proposed: When a man has recently married a wife, he shall not go out to war or be assigned any public duty. He shall be free at home without blame, to rejoice with his wife for one year.
- Why: 'vacabit absque culpa domi suae' = he shall be free at home without blame; 'absque culpa' (without fault) is not rendered, 'free of obligation' only covers 'vacabit'.

**Deuteronomy 24:7** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Si deprehensus fuerit homo sollicitans fratrem suum de filiis Israël, et vendito eo acceperit pretium, interficietur, et auferes malum de medio tui.
- Now: If a man is caught kidnapping his brother from among the children of Israel and receiving money from selling him, he shall be killed. Remove the evil from your midst.
- Proposed: If a man is caught enticing away his brother from among the children of Israel and receiving money from selling him, he shall be killed. Remove the evil from your midst.
- Why: 'sollicitans fratrem suum' means soliciting, enticing or luring away, not seizing; 'kidnapping' reflects the Hebrew 'stealing' rather than the Latin participle.

**Deuteronomy 25:10** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et vocabitur nomen illius in Israël, Domus discalceati.
- Now: His household shall be called in Israel: The House of the Unsandaled Man.
- Proposed: His name shall be called in Israel: The House of the Unsandaled Man.
- Why: 'vocabitur nomen illius' = his name shall be called; the English substitutes 'His household' for 'nomen illius' (his name), which the Latin does not say.

**Deuteronomy 29:18 (Vulgate 29:19)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Cumque audierit verba juramenti hujus, benedicat sibi in corde suo, dicens : Pax erit mihi, et ambulabo in pravitate cordis mei : et absumat ebria sitientem,
- Now: Let no one who hears the words of this oath bless himself in his heart, saying: I will have peace though I walk in the perversity of my heart, so that the drunken one consumes the thirsty.
- Proposed: Let no one who hears the words of this oath bless himself in his heart, saying: I will have peace, and I will walk in the perversity of my heart, so that the drunken one consumes the thirsty.
- Why: 'Pax erit mihi, et ambulabo in pravitate cordis mei' is two coordinate futures (I shall have peace, and I will walk); concessive 'though I walk' is not in the Latin and echoes the Hebrew/KJV.

**Deuteronomy 29:20 (Vulgate 29:21)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: et consumat eum in perditionem ex omnibus tribubus Israël, juxta maledictiones, quæ in libro legis hujus ac fœderis continentur.
- Now: and single him out for destruction from all the tribes of Israel, according to the curses contained in this book of the law and covenant.
- Proposed: and consume him to destruction out of all the tribes of Israel, according to the curses contained in this book of the law and covenant.
- Why: 'consumat eum in perditionem' = consume him unto destruction; 'single him out' renders the Hebrew (separate him for evil), not the Latin verb 'consumat'.

**Deuteronomy 34:2** · other-text · minor

- Latin: et universum Nephthali, terramque Ephraim et Manasse, et omnem terram Juda usque ad mare novissimum,
- Now: all Naphtali, the land of Ephraim and Manasseh, and all the land of Judah as far as the western sea,
- Proposed: all Naphtali, the land of Ephraim and Manasseh, and all the land of Judah as far as the farthest sea,
- Why: 'usque ad mare novissimum' = as far as the last/farthest sea; 'western sea' is the interpretation of the Hebrew term, not what 'novissimum' says.

## Notes on the recorded Latin (7)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **Deuteronomy 7:25** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin as recorded ends "abominatio est Domini tui"; the Vulgate reads "abominatio est Domini Dei tui" ("Dei" apparently dropped). English "your Lord" follows the defective text; could not verify against a printed Clementine here.
- **Deuteronomy 9:1** · spacing: Stray space before the comma in the Latin: "Audi , Israël" should read "Audi, Israël".
- **Deuteronomy 10:2** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has "in his qui ante confregisti"; the relative refers to the tablets (tabulis, feminine) and should read "in his quas ante confregisti". English is correct.
- **Deuteronomy 17:3** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has "ut vadant et servant diis alienis"; the verb should be the subjunctive of servire: "ut vadant et serviant diis alienis". English ("to serve foreign gods") is correct.
- **Deuteronomy 23:2 (Vulgate 23:1)** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has perfect 'Non intravit eunuchus' (has not entered); the Clementine reads future 'Non intrabit eunuchus', as the parallel 'ingredietur'/'intrabunt' and the English 'shall not enter' confirm. Correct to 'intrabit'.
- **Deuteronomy 26:13** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Typo in the Latin: 'in conspectu Domini Deo tui' should read 'in conspectu Domini Dei tui' (genitive, as in 26:5 and 26:10). English is correct.
- **Deuteronomy 32:1** · bracket: Stray space before the comma: 'Audite , caeli' should read 'Audite, caeli'. The song's opening poetry bracket is also missing here although its closing ']' stands at the end of 32:3.

## Withdrawn on second reading (3)

- **Deuteronomy 6:8**: 'eruntque et movebuntur inter oculos tuos': 'hang' is an idiomatic English verb for locative 'erunt' beside 'movebuntur'; sense is unchanged.
- **Deuteronomy 19:13**: 'auferes innoxium sanguinem': blood stands for bloodguilt; 'the guilt of' only spells out that metonymy, as Douay also does, without changing the sense.
- **Deuteronomy 33:4**: 'multitudinis Jacob': in the Vulgate multitudo regularly denotes the whole body or community of the people, so 'assembly' is within its range.
