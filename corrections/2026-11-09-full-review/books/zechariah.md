# Zechariah · Latin–English review, 9 November 2026

211 distinct readings read against the Clementine Latin (211 reader rows). [Method and limits](../README.md). Nothing below has been applied to the staging site.

## Proposed English corrections (15)

**Zechariah 1:16 (Vulgate 1:17)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Propterea hæc dicit Dominus : Revertar ad Jerusalem in misericordiis, et domus mea ædificabitur in ea, ait Dominus exercituum, et perpendiculum extendetur super Jerusalem.]
- Now: Therefore thus says the Lord: I will return to Jerusalem with mercies, and My house will be built in her, says the Lord of hosts, and the measuring line will be stretched over Jerusalem.
- Proposed: Therefore thus says the Lord: I will return to Jerusalem with mercies, and My house will be built in her, says the Lord of hosts, and the plumb line will be stretched over Jerusalem.
- Why: "perpendiculum" is a plumb line (builder's line), not a measuring line; "measuring line" reflects the Hebrew, not this Latin word.

**Zechariah 2:9 (Vulgate 2:6)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et ego ero ei, ait Dominus, murus ignis in circuitu, et in gloria ero in medio ejus.
- Now: And I, says the Lord, will be a wall of fire all around her, and I will be glory in her midst.”
- Proposed: And I, says the Lord, will be a wall of fire all around her, and I will be in glory in her midst.”
- Why: "in gloria ero in medio ejus" = I will be in glory in her midst; "I will be glory" drops "in" and follows the Hebrew.

**Zechariah 4:2 (Vulgate 4:3)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et dixit ad me : Quid tu vides ? Et dixi : Vidi, et ecce candelabrum aureum totum, et lampas ejus super caput ipsius, et septem lucernæ ejus super illud, et septem infusoria lucernis quæ erant super caput ejus.
- Now: He said to me, “What do you see?” I said, “I saw—and look, a lampstand all of gold, with its reservoir on top of it, its seven lamps upon it, and seven channels for the lamps on its top.
- Proposed: He said to me, “What do you see?” I said, “I saw—and look, a lampstand all of gold, with its lamp on top of it, its seven lamps upon it, and seven channels for the lamps on its top.
- Why: "lampas ejus" means its lamp (torch), not a reservoir; "reservoir" reflects the Hebrew bowl, not the Latin word.

**Zechariah 4:3 (Vulgate 4:4)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et duæ olivæ super illud : una a dextris lampadis, et una a sinistris ejus.
- Now: And there are two olive trees beside it, one to the right of the reservoir and one to its left.”
- Proposed: And there are two olive trees over it, one to the right of the lamp and one to its left.”
- Why: "super illud" = over/above it, not beside it; "a dextris lampadis" = to the right of the lamp, not of a reservoir. English follows the Hebrew.

**Zechariah 5:7 (Vulgate 5:8)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et ecce talentum plumbi portabatur, et ecce mulier una sedens in medio amphoræ.
- Now: And look, a talent of lead was being lifted up, and there was a woman sitting inside the amphora.
- Proposed: And look, a talent of lead was being carried, and there was a woman sitting inside the amphora.
- Why: "portabatur" = was being carried; "lifted up" follows the Hebrew, not this Latin verb.

**Zechariah 6:12 (Vulgate 6:13)** · other-text · minor · amended on second reading

- Latin: et loqueris ad eum, dicens : Hæc ait Dominus exercituum, dicens : Ecce vir, Oriens nomen ejus, et subter eum orietur, et ædificabit templum Domino.
- Now: Speak to him, saying: Thus says the Lord of hosts: Look, a man whose name is the Rising One! He will rise from his own place, and he will build a temple for the Lord.
- Proposed: Speak to him, saying: Thus says the Lord of hosts: Look, a man whose name is the Rising One! Under him he will rise, and he will build a temple for the Lord.
- Why: "subter eum orietur" = under him he (it) shall spring up; "from his own place" is the usual interpretation of the Hebrew, not a rendering of "subter eum".

**Zechariah 7:2 (Vulgate 7:3)** · meaning · major

- Latin: Et miserunt ad domum Dei Sarasar et Rogommelech, et viri qui erant cum eo, ad deprecandam faciem Domini :
- Now: They sent Sharezer and Regem-melech, and the men who were with him, to the house of God to entreat the Lord's favor,
- Proposed: Sharezer and Regem-melech, and the men who were with him, sent to the house of God to entreat the Lord's favor,
- Why: "viri qui erant cum eo" is nominative: Sarasar, Rogommelech and the men are the senders ("miserunt ad domum Dei"), not persons sent by an unnamed "they".

**Zechariah 7:11 (Vulgate 7:12)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et noluerunt attendere, et averterunt scapulam recedentem, et aures suas aggravaverunt ne audirent.
- Now: But they refused to pay attention; they turned a stubborn shoulder and made their ears heavy so that they would not hear.
- Proposed: But they refused to pay attention; they turned away a withdrawing shoulder and made their ears heavy so that they would not hear.
- Why: "scapulam recedentem" = a shoulder drawing back/departing; "stubborn" renders the Hebrew adjective, not "recedentem".

**Zechariah 9:13 (Vulgate 9:14)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Quoniam extendi mihi Judam quasi arcum : implevi Ephraim : et suscitabo filios tuos, Sion, super filios tuos, Græcia : et ponam te quasi gladium fortium.
- Now: For I have bent Judah for Myself as a bow and fitted Ephraim into it. I will rouse your sons, Zion, against your sons, Greece, and make you like a warrior's sword.
- Proposed: For I have bent Judah for Myself as a bow; I have filled Ephraim. I will rouse your sons, Zion, against your sons, Greece, and make you like a warrior's sword.
- Why: "implevi Ephraim" = I have filled Ephraim; "fitted Ephraim into it" makes Ephraim the arrow set in the bow, an interpretation of the Hebrew not stated by the Latin.

**Zechariah 11:2 (Vulgate 11:3)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Ulula, abies, quia cecidit cedrus, quoniam magnifici vastati sunt : ululate, quercus Basan, quoniam succisus est saltus munitus.
- Now: Wail, cypress, for the cedar has fallen, because the magnificent trees have been laid waste. Wail, oaks of Bashan, for the fortified forest has been cut down.
- Proposed: Wail, fir tree, for the cedar has fallen, because the magnificent trees have been laid waste. Wail, oaks of Bashan, for the fortified forest has been cut down.
- Why: "abies" is the fir tree, not the cypress; "cypress" reflects modern renderings of the Hebrew.

**Zechariah 12:2 (Vulgate 12:3)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Ecce ego ponam Jerusalem superliminare crapulæ omnibus populis in circuitu : sed et Juda erit in obsidione contra Jerusalem.
- Now: Look, I will make Jerusalem a threshold of drunkenness to all the surrounding peoples. Judah too will be involved in the siege against Jerusalem.
- Proposed: Look, I will make Jerusalem a lintel of drunkenness to all the surrounding peoples. Judah too will be involved in the siege against Jerusalem.
- Why: "superliminare" is the lintel (the beam above the door), not the threshold (limen).

**Zechariah 14:10 (Vulgate 14:11)** · other-text · major

- Latin: Et revertetur omnis terra usque ad desertum, de colle Remmon ad austrum Jerusalem : et exaltabitur, et habitabit in loco suo, a porta Benjamin usque ad locum portæ prioris, et usque ad portam angulorum, et a turre Hananeel usque ad torcularia regis.
- Now: All the land will become like the desert, from the hill of Rimmon south of Jerusalem. Jerusalem will be raised up and remain in her place, from the Benjamin Gate to the site of the former gate, as far as the Corner Gate, and from the Tower of Hananel to the king's winepresses.
- Proposed: All the land will return as far as the desert, from the hill of Rimmon south of Jerusalem. Jerusalem will be raised up and remain in her place, from the Benjamin Gate to the site of the former gate, as far as the Corner Gate, and from the Tower of Hananel to the king's winepresses.
- Why: "revertetur omnis terra usque ad desertum" = all the land shall return as far as the desert; "will become like the desert" follows the Hebrew (turned into a plain), not the Latin.

**Zechariah 14:15 (Vulgate 14:16)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et sic erit ruina equi, et muli, et cameli, et asini, et omnium jumentorum quæ fuerint in castris illis, sicut ruina hæc.
- Now: A plague like this will fall upon the horse, mule, camel, donkey, and all the beasts in those camps.
- Proposed: A ruin like this will fall upon the horse, mule, camel, donkey, and all the beasts in those camps.
- Why: "ruina ... sicut ruina hæc" = ruin, downfall, destruction; "plague" renders the Hebrew word (Latin "plaga" stands only in v. 12).

**Zechariah 14:18 (Vulgate 14:19)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Quod etsi familia Ægypti non ascenderit et non venerit, nec super eos erit : sed erit ruina, qua percutiet Dominus omnes gentes quæ non ascenderint ad celebrandam festivitatem tabernaculorum.
- Now: If the family of Egypt does not go up and does not come, there will be none upon them either; instead there will be the plague with which the Lord strikes all the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Festival of Tabernacles.
- Proposed: If the family of Egypt does not go up and does not come, there will be none upon them either; instead there will be the ruin with which the Lord strikes all the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Festival of Tabernacles.
- Why: "erit ruina, qua percutiet Dominus" = there will be the ruin/destruction with which the Lord strikes; "plague" renders the Hebrew, not "ruina".

**Zechariah 14:19 (Vulgate 14:20)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Hoc erit peccatum Ægypti, et hoc peccatum omnium gentium quæ non ascenderint ad celebrandam festivitatem tabernaculorum.
- Now: This will be Egypt's punishment for sin and the punishment for sin of all the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Festival of Tabernacles.
- Proposed: This will be Egypt's sin and the sin of all the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Festival of Tabernacles.
- Why: "Hoc erit peccatum Ægypti, et hoc peccatum omnium gentium" says sin; "punishment for sin" (twice) adds an interpretation the Latin word does not carry.

## Withdrawn on second reading (1)

- **Zechariah 13:6 (Vulgate 13:7)**: 'in medio' with a genitive plural regularly means between/among; 'between your hands' is within the range of 'in medio manuum tuarum'.
