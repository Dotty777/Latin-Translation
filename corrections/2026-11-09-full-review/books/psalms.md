## Proposed English corrections (8)

**Psalms 17:17** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Misit de summo, et accepit me ; et assumpsit me de aquis multis.
- Now: He reached down from on high and took me; He drew me out of many waters.
- Proposed: He sent from on high and took me; He drew me out of many waters.
- Why: "Misit de summo" is "He sent from on high"; "reached down" is not what misit says and follows modern versions of the Hebrew.

**Psalms 17:51** · meaning · minor

- Latin: magnificans salutes regis ejus, et faciens misericordiam christo suo David, et semini ejus usque in sæculum.]
- Now: He magnifies the victories of His king and shows mercy to His anointed, David, and to his descendants forever.
- Proposed: He magnifies the deliverances of His king and shows mercy to His anointed, David, and to his descendants forever.
- Why: "salutes regis ejus" means the salvations/deliverances of His king; "victories" is not a sense of salus and follows modern versions of the Hebrew.

**Psalms 34:27** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Exsultent et lætentur qui volunt justitiam meam ; et dicant semper : Magnificetur Dominus, qui volunt pacem servi ejus.
- Now: Let those who desire my vindication rejoice and be glad, and let those who desire His servant's peace always say, “May the Lord be magnified.”
- Proposed: Let those who desire my righteousness rejoice and be glad, and let those who desire His servant's peace always say, “May the Lord be magnified.”
- Why: "qui volunt justitiam meam": justitia is righteousness/justice (so rendered elsewhere in these psalms); "vindication" is not a sense of the word and follows modern versions of the Hebrew.

**Psalms 57:12** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et dicet homo : Si utique est fructus justo, utique est Deus judicans eos in terra.]
- Now: A person will say, “Surely there is fruit for the just; surely there is a God who judges them upon the earth.”
- Proposed: A person will say, “If indeed there is fruit for the just, surely there is a God who judges them upon the earth.”
- Why: "Si utique est fructus justo" is conditional ("If indeed there is fruit for the just"); the English drops "Si" and makes it a flat assertion, as in versions of the Hebrew.

**Psalms 79:10** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Dux itineris fuisti in conspectu ejus ; plantasti radices ejus, et implevit terram.
- Now: You were its guide along the way. You planted its roots, and it filled the land.
- Proposed: You were the guide of the journey before it. You planted its roots, and it filled the land.
- Why: "in conspectu ejus" (in its sight, before it) is not rendered; "Dux itineris fuisti in conspectu ejus" = You were guide of the journey before it.

**Psalms 86:4** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Memor ero Rahab et Babylonis, scientium me ; ecce alienigenæ, et Tyrus, et populus Æthiopum, hi fuerunt illic.
- Now: I will remember Rahab and Babylon among those who know Me. Look, foreigners and Tyre and the people of Ethiopia—these were there.
- Proposed: I will remember Rahab and Babylon, who know Me. Look, foreigners and Tyre and the people of Ethiopia—these were there.
- Why: "scientium me" is a genitive agreeing with Rahab and Babylon ("who know me"); "among those who know Me" follows the Hebrew construction, not the Latin.

**Psalms 106:32** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et exaltent eum in ecclesia plebis, et in cathedra seniorum laudent eum.
- Now: Let them exalt Him in the assembly of the people and praise Him in the council of elders.
- Proposed: Let them exalt Him in the assembly of the people and praise Him in the seat of the elders.
- Why: "in cathedra seniorum" is "in the seat (chair) of the elders"; "cathedra" does not mean "council".

**Psalms 113:8** · other-text · minor

- Latin: qui convertit petram in stagna aquarum, et rupem in fontes aquarum.
- Now: who turned the rock into pools of water and the flint into springs of water.
- Proposed: who turned the rock into pools of water and the crag into springs of water.
- Why: "rupem" is a crag or cliff of rock; "flint" is the Hebrew/KJV wording, not the Latin.

## Withdrawn on second reading (4)

- **Psalms 3:6**: "dormivi, et soporatus sum": Vulgate dormire covers lying down to sleep and soporatus the sleep itself; current is sound and the proposal is tautological.
- **Psalms 44:5**: "intende": intendere (arcum) is the standard verb for bending a bow, and arrows follow; a legitimate reading, no worse than "set out".
- **Psalms 78:7**: 'locum ejus' is the place where Jacob lives; 'his dwelling' is a sound contextual rendering of locus and changes nothing asserted.
- **Psalms 148:10**: 'serpentes' is the participle of serpere used as a noun, 'creeping things' as well as 'serpents'; the current English is within its range.
