## Proposed English corrections (14)

**2 Chronicles 1:13** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Venit ergo Salomon ab excelso Gabaon in Jerusalem coram tabernaculo fœderis, et regnavit super Israël.
- Now: Solomon came from the high place at Gibeon, from before the tabernacle of the covenant, to Jerusalem, and reigned over Israel.
- Proposed: Solomon came from the high place at Gibeon to Jerusalem, before the tabernacle of the covenant, and reigned over Israel.
- Why: Latin 'ab excelso Gabaon in Jerusalem coram tabernaculo fœderis' has only 'coram' (before); 'from before the tabernacle' re-attaches the phrase to Gibeon, following the Hebrew/modern versions.

**2 Chronicles 6:5** · other-text · minor · amended on second reading

- Latin: A die qua eduxi populum meum de terra Ægypti, non elegi civitatem de cunctis tribubus Israël ut ædificaretur in ea domus nomini meo, neque elegi quemquam alium virum ut esset dux in populo Israël :
- Now: ‘From the day I brought My people out of Egypt, I chose no city from all the tribes of Israel for a house to be built there for My name, nor did I choose any other man to lead My people Israel.
- Proposed: ‘From the day I brought My people out of Egypt, I chose no city from all the tribes of Israel for a house to be built there for My name, nor did I choose any other man to lead the people of Israel.
- Why: Latin "ut esset dux in populo Israël" has no "meo"; "My people Israel" follows the Hebrew wording, not this Latin.

**2 Chronicles 6:22** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Si peccaverit quispiam in proximum suum, et jurare contra eum paratus venerit, seque maledicto constrinxerit coram altari in domo ista :
- Now: If someone sins against his neighbor and comes prepared to swear concerning him, binding himself with a curse before Your altar in this house,
- Proposed: If someone sins against his neighbor and comes prepared to swear against him, binding himself with a curse before the altar in this house,
- Why: "jurare contra eum" is to swear against him, not "concerning him"; "coram altari" has no "tuo" ("Your" added).

**2 Chronicles 7:9** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Fecitque die octavo collectam, eo quod dedicasset altare septem diebus, et solemnitatem celebrasset diebus septem.
- Now: On the eighth day he held an assembly, for they had dedicated the altar for seven days and celebrated the festival for seven days.
- Proposed: On the eighth day he held an assembly, for he had dedicated the altar for seven days and celebrated the festival for seven days.
- Why: "dedicasset ... celebrasset" are singular, continuing the subject of "fecit" (Solomon); "they had dedicated" follows the Hebrew plural.

**2 Chronicles 9:20** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Omnia quoque vasa convivii regis erant aurea, et vasa domus saltus Libani ex auro purissimo. Argentum enim in diebus illis pro nihilo reputabatur.
- Now: All the king's drinking vessels were gold, and the vessels of the House of the Forest of Lebanon were purest gold. Silver was considered nothing in those days.
- Proposed: All the vessels of the king's table were gold, and the vessels of the House of the Forest of Lebanon were purest gold. Silver was considered nothing in those days.
- Why: "vasa convivii regis" are the vessels of the king's banquet/table; "drinking vessels" reflects the Hebrew, not the Latin "convivii".

**2 Chronicles 9:29** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Reliqua autem operum Salomonis priorum et novissimorum scripta sunt in verbis Nathan prophetæ, et in libris Ahiæ Silonitis, in visione quoque Addo videntis contra Jeroboam filium Nabat.
- Now: The rest of Solomon's deeds, first and last, are written in the words of Nathan the prophet, the books of Ahijah the Shilonite, and the vision of Iddo the seer concerning Jeroboam son of Nebat.
- Proposed: The rest of Solomon's deeds, first and last, are written in the words of Nathan the prophet, the books of Ahijah the Shilonite, and the vision of Iddo the seer against Jeroboam son of Nebat.

**2 Chronicles 11:11** · meaning · minor · amended on second reading

- Latin: Cumque clausisset eas muris, posuit in eis principes, ciborumque horrea, hoc est, olei, et vini.
- Now: He enclosed them with walls and placed commanders and stores of food, oil, and wine in them.
- Proposed: He enclosed them with walls and placed commanders and stores of food, that is, of oil and wine, in them.
- Why: "ciborumque horrea, hoc est, olei, et vini": the oil and wine explain the provisions ("hoc est"); the English turns them into a list of three separate things and drops "hoc est".

**2 Chronicles 18:7** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et ait rex Israël ad Josaphat : Est vir unus a quo possumus quærere Domini voluntatem : sed ego odi eum, quia non prophetat mihi bonum, sed malum omni tempore : est autem Michæas filius Jemla. Dixitque Josaphat : Ne loquaris, rex, hoc modo.
- Now: The king of Israel told Jehoshaphat, “There is one man through whom we can seek the Lord's will, but I hate him, because he never prophesies good for me, but always evil: Micaiah son of Imlah.” Jehoshaphat said, “The king should not speak like that.”
- Proposed: The king of Israel told Jehoshaphat, “There is one man through whom we can seek the Lord's will, but I hate him, because he never prophesies good for me, but always evil: Micaiah son of Imlah.” Jehoshaphat said, “Do not speak like that, O king.”
- Why: "Ne loquaris, rex, hoc modo" is second-person address with vocative "rex" (Do not speak so, O king); the third-person "The king should not speak" follows the Hebrew form.

**2 Chronicles 20:11** · other-text · minor

- Latin: e contrario agunt, et nituntur ejicere nos de possessione quam tradidisti nobis.
- Now: Yet these repay us by trying to drive us from the possession You gave us.
- Proposed: Yet these do the contrary and strive to drive us from the possession You gave us.
- Why: "e contrario agunt, et nituntur ejicere nos" is "they act to the contrary and strive to cast us out"; "repay us" reflects the Hebrew verb, which the Latin does not have.

**2 Chronicles 24:7** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Athalia enim impiissima, et filii ejus, destruxerunt domum Dei, et de universis quæ sanctificata fuerant in templo Domini, ornaverunt fanum Baalim.
- Now: For wicked Athaliah and her sons damaged God's house and adorned the shrine of the Baals with all the things consecrated in the Lord's temple.”
- Proposed: For the most wicked Athaliah and her sons destroyed God's house and adorned the shrine of the Baals with all the things consecrated in the Lord's temple.”
- Why: "destruxerunt domum Dei" is destroyed/pulled down, softened to "damaged"; the superlative "impiissima" (most wicked) is reduced to "wicked".

**2 Chronicles 29:31** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Ezechias autem etiam hæc addidit : Implestis manus vestras Domino : accedite, et offerte victimas et laudes in domo Domini. Obtulit ergo universa multitudo hostias, et laudes, et holocausta, mente devota.
- Now: Hezekiah added, “You have filled your hands for the Lord. Come and offer sacrifices and thank offerings in the Lord's house.” The whole multitude offered sacrifices, thank offerings, and burnt offerings with devoted hearts.
- Proposed: Hezekiah added, “You have filled your hands for the Lord. Come and offer sacrifices and praises in the Lord's house.” The whole multitude offered sacrifices, praises, and burnt offerings with devoted hearts.
- Why: "victimas et laudes ... hostias, et laudes": Latin "laudes" is praises; "thank offerings" (twice) reflects the Hebrew term rather than the Latin word.

- Why: The Latin's second name is "Azarias" (Azariah); the English "Azaziah" is the Hebrew text's name, not a modern form of the Latin one.

**2 Chronicles 33:16** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Porro instauravit altare Domini, et immolavit super illud victimas, et pacifica, et laudem : præcepitque Judæ ut serviret Domino Deo Israël.
- Now: He restored the Lord's altar and sacrificed victims, peace offerings, and thank offerings upon it. He commanded Judah to serve the Lord God of Israel.
- Proposed: He restored the Lord's altar and sacrificed victims, peace offerings, and praise upon it. He commanded Judah to serve the Lord God of Israel.
- Why: "victimas, et pacifica, et laudem": Latin "laudem" is praise (a sacrifice of praise); "thank offerings" reflects the Hebrew term rather than the Latin word.

**2 Chronicles 33:23** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et non est reveritus faciem Domini, sicut reveritus est Manasses pater ejus, et multo majora deliquit.
- Now: He did not humble himself before the Lord as his father Manasseh had done, and committed far greater offenses.
