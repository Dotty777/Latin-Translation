## Proposed English corrections (26)

**Jeremiah 2:36** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Quam vilis facta es nimis, iterans vias tuas ! et ab Ægypto confunderis, sicut confusa es ab Assur.
- Now: How utterly contemptible you have become, changing your ways again and again! You will be put to shame by Egypt, just as you were put to shame by Assyria.
- Proposed: How utterly contemptible you have become, repeating your ways again and again! You will be put to shame by Egypt, just as you were put to shame by Assyria.
- Why: 'iterans vias tuas' is 'repeating, going over your ways again'; 'changing your ways' is the Hebrew/KJV sense ('to change thy way'), not that of iterare.

**Jeremiah 4:1** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Si reverteris, Israël, ait Dominus, ad me convertere : si abstuleris offendicula tua a facie mea, non commoveberis.
- Now: “If you return, Israel, says the Lord, return to Me. If you remove your abominations from My presence, you will not be shaken.
- Proposed: “If you return, Israel, says the Lord, return to Me. If you remove your stumbling blocks from My presence, you will not be shaken.
- Why: 'offendicula tua' is 'your stumbling blocks' (so rendered at Isaiah 57:14); 'abominations' is the Hebrew/KJV word.

**Jeremiah 4:16** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Dicite gentibus : Ecce auditum est in Jerusalem custodes venire de terra longinqua, et dare super civitates Juda vocem suam :
- Now: Tell the nations: Look, it has been heard in Jerusalem that besiegers are coming from a distant land and raising their voices against the cities of Judah.
- Proposed: Tell the nations: Look, it has been heard in Jerusalem that guards are coming from a distant land and raising their voices against the cities of Judah.
- Why: 'custodes' is 'guards, keepers', the same word as 'custodes agrorum' ('keepers of fields') in 4:17; 'besiegers' follows modern renderings of the Hebrew and loses the Latin link.

**Jeremiah 5:13** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Prophetæ fuerunt in ventum locuti, et responsum non fuit in eis : hæc ergo evenient illis.
- Now: The prophets have spoken into the wind, and no divine answer was in them. So let these things happen to them!’
- Proposed: The prophets have spoken into the wind, and no divine answer was in them. So these things will happen to them!’
- Why: "hæc ergo evenient illis" is future indicative, 'these things therefore will befall them'; the English turns the statement into a wish ('let these things happen').

- Now: Will you not fear Me, then, says the Lord, and tremble before Me? I set the sand as the sea's boundary, an everlasting decree that it cannot cross. Its waves will churn but be powerless; they will swell but not cross it.
- Proposed: Will you not fear Me, then, says the Lord, and grieve before Me? I set the sand as the sea's boundary, an everlasting decree that it cannot cross. Its waves will churn but be powerless; they will swell but not cross it.
- Why: "a facie mea non dolebitis" = 'will you not be pained/grieve before Me'; 'tremble' renders the Hebrew verb, not Latin 'dolere'.

**Jeremiah 5:27** · other-text · minor

- **Jeremiah 2:2** · coverage · same as reference: Jeremiah 2:1 ('Et factum est verbum Domini ad me, dicens :') has no unit: the record passes from 1:19 straight to 2:2, so the verse is missing in Latin and English. Unit 2:2 itself is sound.

- **Jeremiah 30:18 (Vulgate 49:2)** · suspected-typo · same as reference: "erit in tumultum dissipata" is probably a slip for "in tumulum dissipata" ('destroyed into a heap', as Douay renders); the English 'scattered in tumult' follows the recorded form. Verify against a printed Clementine.
- **Jeremiah 52:8** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Typo in the Latin: 'Chadæorum' should read 'Chaldæorum' ('Persecutus est autem Chaldæorum exercitus regem'). The English is correct.

## Withdrawn on second reading (5)

- **Jeremiah 7:18**: In Vulgate usage 'conspergere' is to moisten or knead meal (cf. conspersio, dough) and 'adeps' the rich flour; 'sprinkle the fat' would misrender 'conspergunt adipem'.
- **Jeremiah 37:3 (Vulgate 30:3)**: 'convertam conversionem' is an opaque calque (turn the turning); 'restore the fortunes' is a fair idiomatic rendering, and 'bring about the return' is no closer.
- **Jeremiah 37:18 (Vulgate 30:18)**: 'convertam conversionem tabernaculorum Jacob' is the same calque; 'restore the fortunes' is fair idiom, and 'the return of Jacob's tents' is no more exact.
- **Jeremiah 40:7 (Vulgate 33:7)**: 'convertam conversionem Juda et conversionem Jerusalem' is the same calque; 'restore the fortunes' is an acceptable idiomatic rendering of it.
- **Jeremiah 40:11 (Vulgate 33:11)**: 'reducam conversionem terræ' is the same calque; 'restore the fortunes of the land' is fair idiom, and 'bring back the return' is no clearer or closer.
