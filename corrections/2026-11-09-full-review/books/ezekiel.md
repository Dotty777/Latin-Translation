# Ezekiel · Latin–English review, 9 November 2026

1,265 distinct readings read against the Clementine Latin (1,265 reader rows). [Method and limits](../README.md). Nothing below has been applied to the staging site.

## Proposed English corrections (25)

**Ezekiel 3:25** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et tu, fili hominis, ecce data sunt super te vincula, et ligabunt te in eis, et non egredieris de medio eorum.
- Now: As for you, son of man, behold, bonds have been placed upon you, and they will bind you with them, and you will not go out among them.
- Proposed: As for you, son of man, behold, bonds have been placed upon you, and they will bind you with them, and you will not go out from the midst of them.
- Why: 'non egredieris de medio eorum' is 'you will not go out from the midst of them'; 'go out among them' follows the Hebrew sense, not the Latin 'de medio'.

**Ezekiel 11:16** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Propterea hæc dicit Dominus Deus : Quia longe feci eos in gentibus, et quia dispersi eos in terris : ero eis in sanctificationem modicam in terris ad quas venerunt.
- Now: Therefore thus says the Lord God: Though I have sent them far away among the nations and scattered them through the lands, I will be a little sanctuary for them in the lands to which they have come.
- Proposed: Therefore thus says the Lord God: Because I have sent them far away among the nations and because I have scattered them through the lands, I will be a little sanctuary for them in the lands to which they have come.
- Why: Latin 'Quia longe feci eos ... et quia dispersi eos' is causal ('because ... and because'); concessive 'Though' follows modern versions of the Hebrew, not the Latin.

**Ezekiel 14:14** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et si fuerint tres viri isti in medio ejus, Noë, Daniel, et Job, ipsi justitia sua liberabunt animas suas, ait Dominus exercituum.
- Now: Even if these three men, Noah, Daniel, and Job, were within it, they would deliver only their own souls by their righteousness, says the Lord of hosts.
- Proposed: Even if these three men, Noah, Daniel, and Job, were within it, they themselves would deliver their own souls by their righteousness, says the Lord of hosts.
- Why: "only" is added: the Latin has "ipsi justitia sua liberabunt animas suas" (they themselves shall deliver their souls), with no "soli" here (contrast 14:16, 18); 14:20 renders the same phrase without "only".

**Ezekiel 16:10** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et vestivi te discoloribus, et calceavi te janthino, et cinxi te bysso, et indui te subtilibus :
- Now: I clothed you in embroidered garments, put violet-colored shoes on you, girded you with fine linen, and dressed you in delicate fabrics.
- Proposed: I clothed you in many-colored garments, put violet-colored shoes on you, girded you with fine linen, and dressed you in delicate fabrics.
- Why: "discoloribus" means garments of varied colours (cf. "multicoloribus" 16:13, rendered "multicolored"); "embroidered" is the Hebrew/KJV sense, not what the Latin word says.

**Ezekiel 16:32** · other-text · minor

- Latin: sed quasi mulier adultera, quæ super virum suum inducit alienos.
- Now: but like an adulterous wife who brings in strangers in place of her husband.
- Proposed: but like an adulterous wife who brings in strangers over and above her husband.
- Why: "super virum suum inducit alienos": super = over, in addition to her husband; "in place of" follows the Hebrew/KJV ("instead of"), a sense "super" does not have.

**Ezekiel 17:7** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et facta est aquila altera grandis, magnis alis, multisque plumis : et ecce vinea ista quasi mittens radices suas ad eam, palmites suos extendit ad illam, ut irrigaret eam de areolis germinis sui.
- Now: There was another great eagle, with great wings and many feathers; and behold, this vine, as though sending its roots toward him, stretched out its shoots toward him from the beds in which it grew, so that he might water it.
- Proposed: There was another great eagle, with great wings and many feathers; and behold, this vine, as though sending its roots toward him, stretched out its shoots toward him, so that he might water it from the beds in which it grew.
- Why: "ut irrigaret eam de areolis germinis sui": "de areolis" belongs to the purpose clause (water it from the beds of its growth); the English attaches it to "extendit" instead.

**Ezekiel 33:22** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Manus autem Domini facta fuerat ad me vespere, antequam veniret qui fugerat : aperuitque os meum donec veniret ad me mane : et aperto ore meo, non silui amplius.
- Now: The Lord's hand had come upon me in the evening before the escaped man arrived, and He opened my mouth before he came to me in the morning. My mouth was opened, and I was silent no longer.
- Proposed: The Lord's hand had come upon me in the evening before the escaped man arrived, and He opened my mouth until he came to me in the morning. My mouth was opened, and I was silent no longer.

**Ezekiel 40:9** · other-text · minor

## Withdrawn on second reading (3)

- **Ezekiel 30:24**: 'interfecti' can equally be genitive singular with 'gemitibus' (groans of a slain man), which the current renders; the nominative parse is not required.
- **Ezekiel 38:10**: 'ascendent sermones super cor tuum': words rising upon the heart are idiomatically thoughts; Douay likewise paraphrases ('projects'). Acceptable idiom.
- **Ezekiel 43:8**: 'et murus erat inter me et eos': 'only' brings out the implied point of the complaint, as Douay's 'but a wall' also does; acceptable idiom.
