# Mark · Latin–English review, 9 November 2026

677 distinct readings read against the Clementine Latin (677 reader rows). [Method and limits](../README.md). Nothing below has been applied to the staging site.

## Proposed English corrections (3)

**Mark 5:1** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Et venerunt trans fretum maris in regionem Gerasenorum.
- Now: And they came over the narrow of the sea, into the country of the Gerasens.
- Proposed: And they came over the strait of the sea, into the country of the Gerasens.
- Why: "the narrow of the sea" is broken English: Douay "strait" (noun, for "fretum") has been wrongly modernised to the adjective "narrow".

**Mark 5:21** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Et cum transcendisset Jesus in navi rursum trans fretum, convenit turba multa ad eum, et erat circa mare.
- Now: And when Jesus had passed again in the boat over the narrow, a great multitude assembled together to him, and he was near to the sea.
- Proposed: And when Jesus had passed again in the boat over the strait, a great multitude assembled together to him, and he was near to the sea.
- Why: "over the narrow" is broken English: "trans fretum" is "over the strait"; the noun "strait" was wrongly replaced by the adjective "narrow".

**Mark 10:27** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Et intuens illos Jesus, ait: Apud homines impossibile est, sed non apud Deum: omnia enim possibilia sunt apud Deum.
- Now: And Jesus looking on them, says with men it is impossible; but not with God. For all things are possible with God.
- Proposed: And Jesus looking on them, says: With men it is impossible; but not with God. For all things are possible with God.
- Why: Colon and capital lost after "says" ("ait: Apud homines impossibile est"), so the text reads as "says with men"; the speech is not marked off.

## Notes on the recorded Latin (8)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **Mark 2:23** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has lost "cum": the subjunctive "ambularet" has no conjunction. Clementine reads "Et factum est iterum cum Dominus sabbatis ambularet per sata"; the English ("as the Lord walked") renders the full text.
- **Mark 7:19** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Typo in the Latin: "in ventrum vadit" should be "in ventrem vadit" (accusative of venter). English "into the belly" is right.
- **Mark 7:34** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Typo in the Latin: "suscipiens in cælum" (receiving) should be "suspiciens in cælum" (looking up), as the Clementine reads and as the English "looking up to heaven" renders.
- **Mark 8:4** · suspected-typo · same as reference: English "here" has no counterpart in the Latin shown; the Clementine appears to read "quis poterit hic saturare panibus", so "hic" seems dropped from the record. If the Latin stands as recorded, delete "here".
- **Mark 8:17** · suspected-typo · same as reference: "nondum cognoscetis" (future) looks like a typo for the Clementine's present "nondum cognoscitis nec intelligitis"; the English "Do you not yet know" renders the present.
- **Mark 12:15** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Typo in the Latin: "ait illos" should be "ait illis" (dative), as the Clementine reads; English "says to them" is right.
- **Mark 13:5** · suspected-typo · same as reference: "ne quid vos seducat" (lest anything deceive you) looks like a typo for the Clementine's "ne quis vos seducat", which the English "lest any man deceive you" renders.
- **Mark 15:7** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has 'Barrabas', against 'Barabbam' in 15:11 and 15:15; apparently a misspelling of 'Barabbas' (possibly inherited from the source edition). English 'Barabbas' is correct.
