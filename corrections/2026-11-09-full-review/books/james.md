## Proposed English corrections (1)

**James 4:13** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Tu autem quis es, qui judicas proximum? Ecce nunc qui dicitis: Hodie, aut crastino ibimus in illam civitatem, et faciemus ibi quidem annum, et mercabimur, et lucrum faciemus:
- Now: But who are you that judge your neighbour? Behold, now you that say: To-day or to-next day we will go into such a city, and there we will spend a year and will traffic and make our gain.
- Proposed: But who are you that judge your neighbour? Behold, now you that say: To-day or to-morrow we will go into such a city, and there we will spend a year and will traffic and make our gain.
- Why: "to-next day" is a garbled modernisation of "to-morrow" for "Hodie, aut crastino"; it is not English.

## Notes on the recorded Latin (1)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **James 3:17** · suspected-typo · same as reference: "bonus consentiens" is a typo for the Clementine "bonis consentiens" (dative plural, "consenting to the good"), which the English already renders.

## Withdrawn on second reading (1)

- **James 5:16**: "salvemini" in a context of sickness ("salvabit infirmum") can mean be made whole, healed, as "salvus fieri" does in the Gospels; within range.
