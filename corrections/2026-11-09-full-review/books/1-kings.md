844 distinct readings read against the Clementine Latin (845 reader rows). [Method and limits](../README.md). Nothing below has been applied to the staging site.

## Proposed English corrections (9)

**1 Kings 2:3** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et observa custodias Domini Dei tui, ut ambules in viis ejus : ut custodias cæremonias ejus, et præcepta ejus, et judicia, et testimonia, sicut scriptum est in lege Moysi : ut intelligas universa quæ facis, et quocumque te verteris :
- Now: Keep the observances of the Lord your God, walking in His ways and keeping His ceremonies, commandments, judgments, and testimonies, as written in the law of Moses, so that you may act wisely in everything you do and wherever you turn,
- Proposed: Keep the observances of the Lord your God, walking in His ways and keeping His ceremonies, commandments, judgments, and testimonies, as written in the law of Moses, so that you may understand everything you do and wherever you turn,
- Why: "ut intelligas universa quæ facis" means that you may understand all you do; "act wisely in" replaces understanding with acting, nearer the Hebrew than the Latin.

**1 Kings 5:14 (Vulgate 4:34)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et veniebant de cunctis populis ad audiendam sapientiam Salomonis, et ab universis regibus terræ qui audiebant sapientiam ejus.
- Now: People came from all nations to hear Solomon's wisdom, sent by all the kings of the earth who heard of his wisdom.
- Proposed: People came from all nations to hear Solomon's wisdom, and from all the kings of the earth who heard of his wisdom.
- Why: "et ab universis regibus terræ" says only "and from all the kings of the earth"; "sent by" adds a verb the Latin lacks.

**1 Kings 7:49 (Vulgate 7:12)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et atrium majus rotundum trium ordinum de lapidibus sectis, et unius ordinis de dolata cedro : necnon et in atrio domus Domini interiori, et in porticu domus.
- Now: The great surrounding court had three courses of cut stones and one course of hewn cedar, as did the inner court of the Lord's house and the vestibule of the house.
- Proposed: The great round court had three courses of cut stones and one course of hewn cedar, as did the inner court of the Lord's house and the vestibule of the house.
- Why: 'atrium majus rotundum' says the greater court was round; 'surrounding' is not a sense of 'rotundum' and reflects the Hebrew 'round about'.

**1 Kings 8:30** · meaning · minor

- Latin: ut exaudias deprecationem servi tui et populi tui Israël, quodcumque oraverint in loco isto, et exaudies in loco habitaculi tui in cælo : et cum exaudieris, propitius eris.
- Now: Hear the supplication of Your servant and Your people Israel whenever they pray in this place. Hear in Your dwelling place in heaven, and when You hear, be merciful.
- Proposed: Hear the supplication of Your servant and Your people Israel, whatever they pray for in this place. Hear in Your dwelling place in heaven, and when You hear, be merciful.
- Why: 'quodcumque oraverint' means 'whatsoever they shall pray for', the object of the prayer, not the temporal 'whenever they pray'.

**1 Kings 8:33** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Si fugerit populus tuus Israël inimicos suos (quia peccaturus est tibi), et agentes pœnitentiam, et confitentes nomini tuo, venerint, et oraverint, et deprecati te fuerint in domo hac :
- Now: If Your people Israel flee before their enemies because they have sinned against You, and they repent, acknowledge Your name, and come to pray and plead with You in this house,
- Proposed: If Your people Israel flee before their enemies because they will sin against You, and they repent, acknowledge Your name, and come to pray and plead with You in this house,
- Why: 'quia peccaturus est tibi' is a future participle, 'because they will sin against You'; the English perfect 'have sinned' follows the Hebrew rather than this Latin.

**1 Kings 10:8** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Beati viri tui, et beati servi tui, qui stant coram te semper, et audiunt sapientiam tuam.
- Now: Happy are your men, and happy these servants of yours who stand before you continually and hear your wisdom.
- Proposed: Happy are your men, and happy your servants who stand before you continually and hear your wisdom.
- Why: Latin has simply 'beati servi tui'; the demonstrative 'these servants of yours' is not in the Latin and reflects the Hebrew ('these your servants').

**1 Kings 16:12** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Delevitque Zambri omnem domum Baasa, juxta verbum Domini quod locutus fuerat ad Baasa in manu Jehu prophetæ,
- Now: Zimri destroyed the whole house of Baasha according to the word the Lord had spoken against Baasha through Jehu the prophet,
- Proposed: Zimri destroyed the whole house of Baasha according to the word the Lord had spoken to Baasha through Jehu the prophet,
- Why: 'locutus fuerat ad Baasa' is 'had spoken to Baasha'; 'against Baasha' renders the Hebrew preposition, not the Latin 'ad' with a verb of speaking.

**1 Kings 18:40** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Dixitque Elias ad eos : Apprehendite prophetas Baal, et ne unus quidem effugiat ex eis. Quos cum apprehendissent, duxit eos Elias ad torrentem Cison, et interfecit eos ibi.
- Now: Elijah said to them, “Seize Baal's prophets, and do not let even one of them escape.” When they seized them, Elijah led them down to the Kishon stream and killed them there.
- Proposed: Elijah said to them, “Seize Baal's prophets, and do not let even one of them escape.” When they seized them, Elijah led them to the Kishon stream and killed them there.
- Why: 'duxit eos Elias ad torrentem Cison' is simply 'led them to the torrent'; 'down' is not in the Latin and comes from the Hebrew ('brought them down').

**1 Kings 20:29 (Vulgate 21:29)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Nonne vidisti humiliatum Achab coram me ? quia igitur humiliatus est mei causa, non inducam malum in diebus ejus, sed in diebus filii sui inferam malum domui ejus.
- Now: “Have you seen Ahab humbled before Me? Because he has humbled himself for My sake, I will not bring the calamity in his days; in his son's days I will bring calamity upon his house.”
- Proposed: “Have you not seen Ahab humbled before Me? Because he has humbled himself for My sake, I will not bring the calamity in his days; in his son's days I will bring calamity upon his house.”
- Why: 'Nonne vidisti' is a negative question expecting yes, 'Have you not seen'; the English 'Have you seen' drops the negative, following the Hebrew form.

## Notes on the recorded Latin (9)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **1 Kings 7:2 (Vulgate 7:14)** · coverage · same as reference: Latin begins mid-sentence ('filium mulieris viduæ'): Vg 7:13 'Misit quoque rex Salomon, et tulit Hiram de Tyro,' is absent from the record; unit #1 (site 7:1) carries Vg 7:1 instead, and site 7:38 is missing.

- **1 Kings 7:31** · coverage · same as reference: Latin shown is Vg 7:31 (mouth of the laver), out of sequence between Vg 7:44 and 7:47; Vg 7:45 ('Et lebetes, et scutras, et hamulas: omnia vasa quæ fecit Hiram... de aurichalco erant') is absent.

## Withdrawn on second reading (1)

- **1 Kings 16:26**: 'in vanitatibus suis': Vulgate 'suus' is often loosely used and can refer to 'Israel' just named; Douay also reads 'their vanities', so the current English is defensible.
