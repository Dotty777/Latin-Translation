# 1 Chronicles · Latin–English review, 9 November 2026

926 distinct readings read against the Clementine Latin (930 reader rows). [Method and limits](../README.md). Nothing below has been applied to the staging site.

## Proposed English corrections (10)

**1 Chronicles 4:11** · other-text · major

- Latin: Caleb autem frater Sua genuit Mahir, qui fuit pater Esthon.
- Now: Chelub, Shuhah's brother, fathered Mehir, who was Eshton's father.
- Proposed: Caleb, Shuhah's brother, fathered Mehir, who was Eshton's father.
- Why: Latin names him "Caleb autem frater Sua"; English 'Chelub' is the Hebrew name form of modern versions, not the Vulgate's Caleb (rendered 'Caleb' elsewhere in the chapter).

**1 Chronicles 6:33 (Vulgate 6:48)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Fratres quoque eorum Levitæ, qui ordinati sunt in cunctum ministerium tabernaculi domus Domini.
- Now: Their brothers the Levites were appointed to all the service of the tabernacle of God's house.
- Proposed: Their brothers the Levites were appointed to all the service of the tabernacle of the Lord's house.
- Why: Latin has 'tabernaculi domus Domini' (of the house of the Lord); 'God's house' follows the Hebrew wording, not this Latin.

**1 Chronicles 12:33 (Vulgate 12:32)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: De filiis quoque Issachar viri eruditi, qui noverant singula tempora ad præcipiendum quid facere deberet Israël, principes ducenti : omnis autem reliqua tribus eorum consilium sequebatur.
- Now: Of the sons of Issachar, learned men who understood the times and knew what Israel should do: two hundred leaders. All the rest of their tribe followed their counsel.
- Proposed: Of the sons of Issachar, learned men who understood the times so as to direct what Israel should do: two hundred leaders. All the rest of their tribe followed their counsel.
- Why: 'ad præcipiendum quid facere deberet Israël' means 'to direct/instruct what Israel should do'; English 'and knew what Israel should do' drops 'præcipiendum' (follows the Hebrew 'to know').

**1 Chronicles 13:11** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Contristatusque est David, eo quod divisisset Dominus Ozam : vocavitque locum illum Divisio Ozæ, usque in præsentem diem.
- Now: David was grieved because the Lord had broken out against Uzzah. He called that place the Breach of Uzzah, as it is to the present day.
- Proposed: David was grieved because the Lord had divided Uzzah. He called that place the Breach of Uzzah, as it is to the present day.
- Why: 'eo quod divisisset Dominus Ozam' = because the Lord had divided Oza (cf. 'Divisio Ozæ'); 'broken out against' renders the Hebrew idiom, not the Latin verb.

**1 Chronicles 14:11** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Cumque illi ascendissent in Baalpharasim, percussit eos ibi David, et dixit : Divisit Deus inimicos meos per manum meam, sicut dividuntur aquæ : et idcirco vocatum est nomen illius loci Baalpharasim.
- Now: They went up to Baal-perazim, and David struck them there. He said, “God has broken through my enemies by my hand, as waters break through.” Therefore that place was named Baal-perazim.
- Proposed: They went up to Baal-perazim, and David struck them there. He said, “God has divided my enemies by my hand, as waters are divided.” Therefore that place was named Baal-perazim.
- Why: 'Divisit Deus inimicos meos ... sicut dividuntur aquæ' = God has divided my enemies as waters are divided (passive); 'broken through ... break through' follows the Hebrew sense.

**1 Chronicles 15:1** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Fecit quoque sibi domos in civitate David : et ædificavit locum arcæ Dei, tetenditque ei tabernaculum.
- Now: David made houses for himself in the city of David. He prepared a place for the ark of God and pitched a tent for it.
- Proposed: David made houses for himself in the city of David. He built a place for the ark of God and pitched a tent for it.
- Why: 'ædificavit locum arcæ Dei' = he built a place for the ark; 'prepared' follows the Hebrew, not the Latin 'ædificavit'.

**1 Chronicles 15:13** · meaning · major

- Latin: ne ut a principio, quia non eratis præsentes, percussit nos Dominus ; sic et nunc fiat, illicitum quid nobis agentibus.
- Now: lest what happened at first happen again: because you were not present, the Lord struck us, for we acted unlawfully.”
- Proposed: lest, as at first the Lord struck us because you were not present, so it happen now also, should we do anything unlawful.”
- Why: 'sic et nunc fiat, illicitum quid nobis agentibus' is conditional on the present ('so it happen now too, if we do anything unlawful'); English turns it into a past cause, 'for we acted unlawfully'.

**1 Chronicles 23:5** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Porro quatuor millia janitores, et totidem psaltæ, canentes Domino in organis quæ fecerat ad canendum.
- Now: Four thousand were gatekeepers, and as many were musicians, making music to the Lord on the instruments he had made for praise.
- Proposed: Four thousand were gatekeepers, and as many were musicians, making music to the Lord on the instruments he had made for playing.
- Why: 'in organis quæ fecerat ad canendum' = instruments he had made for singing/playing; 'for praise' reflects the Hebrew ('to praise'), not 'ad canendum'.

**1 Chronicles 25:2** · other-text · minor

- Latin: De filiis Asaph : Zachur, et Joseph, et Nathania, et Asarela, filii Asaph : sub manu Asaph prophetantis juxta regem.
- Now: Of the sons of Asaph: Zaccur, Joseph, Nethaniah, and Asarelah, the sons of Asaph, under Asaph, who prophesied under the king's direction.
- Proposed: Of the sons of Asaph: Zaccur, Joseph, Nethaniah, and Asarelah, the sons of Asaph, under Asaph, who prophesied beside the king.
- Why: 'prophetantis juxta regem' = prophesying near/beside the king; 'under the king's direction' renders the Hebrew idiom, not the Latin 'juxta regem'.

**1 Chronicles 25:6** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Universi sub manu patris sui ad cantandum in templo Domini distributi erant, in cymbalis, et psalteriis, et citharis, in ministeria domus Domini juxta regem : Asaph videlicet, et Idithun, et Heman.
- Now: All were assigned under their father to sing in the Lord's temple with cymbals, harps, and lyres, for the services of the Lord's house, under the king's direction: Asaph, Jeduthun, and Heman.
- Proposed: All were assigned under their father to sing in the Lord's temple with cymbals, harps, and lyres, for the services of the Lord's house, beside the king: Asaph, Jeduthun, and Heman.
- Why: 'in ministeria domus Domini juxta regem' = near/beside the king; 'under the king's direction' follows the Hebrew idiom rather than the Latin 'juxta regem'.

## English and Latin cover different amounts of text (1)

- **1 Chronicles 11:35 (Vulgate 11:34)**: Latin shown is only Vg 11:34 'Ahiam filius Sachar Ararites'; the English adds 'Eliphal son of Ur', i.e. Vg 11:35 'Eliphal filius Ur', which appears in no Latin unit here.

## Notes on the recorded Latin (5)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **1 Chronicles 1:1** · spacing: Stray space before the first comma ("Adam , Seth"), probably left by stripped markup. Correct Latin: Adam, Seth, Enos,
- **1 Chronicles 1:4** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Typo "Japtheth"; the Clementine reads "Japheth" (as in 1:5, "Filii Japheth"). Correct Latin: Noë, Sem, Cham, et Japheth.
- **1 Chronicles 6:57 (Vulgate 6:72)** · suspected-typo · same as reference: 'Cedes et suburbanis suis' is ungrammatical (ablative without 'cum'); the Clementine reads 'Cedes et suburbana ejus, et Dabereth cum suburbanis suis'.
- **1 Chronicles 6:62 (Vulgate 6:77)** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Typo 'cum suburbanis sus'; should read 'et Thabor cum suburbanis suis'.
- **1 Chronicles 16:36** · suspected-typo · same as reference: 'Et dicat omnis populo' is a typo; the Clementine reads 'Et dicat omnis populus : Amen, et hymnum Domino.'
