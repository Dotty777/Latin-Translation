# 2 Samuel · Latin–English review, 9 November 2026

695 distinct readings read against the Clementine Latin (695 reader rows). [Method and limits](../README.md). Nothing below has been applied to the staging site.

## Proposed English corrections (8)

**2 Samuel 2:6** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et nunc retribuet vobis quidem Dominus misericordiam et veritatem : sed et ego reddam gratiam, eo quod fecistis verbum istud.
- Now: Now may the Lord repay you with mercy and faithfulness. I too will repay your kindness, because you have done this.
- Proposed: Now the Lord will indeed repay you with mercy and faithfulness. I too will repay your kindness, because you have done this.
- Why: Latin 'retribuet vobis quidem Dominus' is future indicative ('the Lord will indeed repay you'), parallel to 'et ego reddam'; the English turns it into a wish ('may the Lord repay'), as in the Hebrew.

**2 Samuel 3:38** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Dixit quoque rex ad servos suos : Num ignoratis quoniam princeps et maximus cecidit hodie in Israël ?
- Now: The king also said to his servants, “Do you not know that a prince and a great man have fallen today in Israel?
- Proposed: The king also said to his servants, “Do you not know that a prince and a great man has fallen today in Israel?
- Why: Latin 'princeps et maximus cecidit' is singular, one man (Abner); plural 'have fallen' reads as two persons. Should be 'has fallen'.

**2 Samuel 4:10** · meaning · minor

- Latin: quoniam eum qui annuntiaverat mihi, et dixerat : Mortuus est Saul : qui putabat se prospera nuntiare, tenui, et occidi eum in Siceleg, cui oportebat mercedem dare pro nuntio.
- Now: the man who brought me news, saying, ‘Saul is dead,’ thought he was bringing good news. I seized and killed him at Ziklag, though he expected a reward for his report.
- Proposed: the man who brought me news, saying, ‘Saul is dead,’ thought he was bringing good news. I seized and killed him at Ziklag, though he ought to have been given a reward for his report.
- Why: Latin 'cui oportebat mercedem dare pro nuntio' = 'to whom a reward ought to have been given for the news'; the English substitutes 'he expected a reward', which the Latin does not say.

**2 Samuel 12:21** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Dixerunt autem ei servi sui : Quis est sermo quem fecisti ? propter infantem, cum adhuc viveret, jejunasti et flebas : mortuo autem puero, surrexisti, et comedisti panem.
- Now: His servants said to him, “What is this you have done? While the infant was still alive, you fasted and wept, but when the child died, you arose and ate bread.”
- Proposed: His servants said to him, “What is this you have done? For the infant's sake, while he was still alive, you fasted and wept, but when the child died, you arose and ate bread.”
- Why: "propter infantem" (on account of / for the infant) is not rendered; the English keeps only the time clause "cum adhuc viveret".

**2 Samuel 12:22** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Qui ait : Propter infantem, dum adhuc viveret, jejunavi et flevi : dicebam enim : Quis scit si forte donet eum mihi Dominus, et vivat infans ?
- Now: He said, “While the infant was still alive, I fasted and wept, for I said, ‘Who knows whether the Lord may grant him to me and the infant may live?’
- Proposed: He said, “For the infant's sake, while he was still alive, I fasted and wept, for I said, ‘Who knows whether the Lord may grant him to me and the infant may live?’
- Why: "Propter infantem" (for the infant's sake) is omitted; only "dum adhuc viveret" is rendered.

**2 Samuel 13:34** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Fugit autem Absalom. Et elevavit puer speculator oculos suos, et aspexit : et ecce populus multus veniebat per iter devium ex latere montis.
- Now: Absalom fled. The young watchman raised his eyes and looked, and a great crowd was coming by a winding road along the mountainside.
- Proposed: Absalom fled. The young watchman raised his eyes and looked, and a great crowd was coming by an out-of-the-way road along the mountainside.
- Why: "per iter devium" means by a by-way, a road off the main route (devius = out of the way), not a "winding" road.

**2 Samuel 15:20** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Heri venisti, et hodie compelleris nobiscum egredi ? ego autem vadam quo iturus sum : revertere, et reduc tecum fratres tuos, et Dominus faciet tecum misericordiam et veritatem, quia ostendisti gratiam et fidem.
- Now: You came yesterday, and today are you to be compelled to leave with us? I am going wherever I must go. Return and take your brothers back with you. May the Lord show you mercy and faithfulness, because you have shown kindness and fidelity.”
- Proposed: You came yesterday, and today are you to be compelled to leave with us? I am going wherever I must go. Return and take your brothers back with you. The Lord will show you mercy and faithfulness, because you have shown kindness and fidelity.”
- Why: "Dominus faciet tecum misericordiam et veritatem" is future indicative, an assurance (the Lord will show), not the wish "May the Lord show".

**2 Samuel 22:28** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et populum pauperem salvum facies : oculisque tuis excelsos humiliabis.
- Now: You will save an afflicted people and humble the lofty with Your eyes.
- Proposed: You will save a poor people and humble the lofty with Your eyes.
- Why: "populum pauperem" is a poor people; "afflicted" is the Hebrew/KJV wording, not a sense of pauper.

## Notes on the recorded Latin (1)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **2 Samuel 24:13** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin typo: "et ille te persequentur" has singular pronoun with plural verb; the Clementine reads "et illi te persequentur". The English (they pursue you) already follows the correct text.

## Withdrawn on second reading (3)

- **2 Samuel 22:36**: 'mansuetudo tua multiplicavit me': multiplicare also means increase, enlarge, so 'made me great' is within range; 'multiplied me' is poorer English.
- **2 Samuel 23:10**: 'Cumque ascendissent viri Israel': with 'populus qui fugerat' following, the going up is a departure (Douay 'were gone away'); 'withdrawn' is a sound contextual rendering.
- **2 Samuel 23:31**: 'Azmaveth de Beromi' is 'of' a place; 'of Bahurim' is the standard modern form for it, while proposed 'the Barhumite' swaps the Latin 'de' for a KJV gentilic.
