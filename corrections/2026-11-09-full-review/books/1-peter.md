# 1 Peter · Latin–English review, 9 November 2026

105 distinct readings read against the Clementine Latin (105 reader rows). [Method and limits](../README.md). Nothing below has been applied to the staging site.

## Proposed English corrections (4)

**1 Peter 2:3** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: si tamen gustastis quoniam dulcis est Dominus.
- Now: If so be have tasted that the Lord is sweet.
- Proposed: If so be you have tasted that the Lord is sweet.
- Why: The subject "you" is missing ("si tamen gustastis"), leaving "If so be have tasted" broken.

**1 Peter 2:8** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: et lapis offensionis, et petra scandali, his qui offendunt verbo, nec credunt in quo et positi sunt.
- Now: And a stone of stumbling and a rock of scandal, to them who stumble at the word, neither do believe, to what also they are set.
- Proposed: And a stone of stumbling and a rock of scandal, to them who stumble at the word, neither do believe, to which also they are set.
- Why: "to what also they are set" is a faulty modernisation of "whereunto"; the relative "in quo et positi sunt" needs "to which".

**1 Peter 3:7** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Viri similiter cohabitantes secundum scientiam, quasi infirmiori vasculo muliebri impartientes honorem, tamquam et cohæredibus gratiæ vitæ: ut non impediantur orationes vestræ.
- Now: You husbands, likewise dwelling with them according to knowledge, giving honor to the female as to the weaker vessel and as to the co-heirs of the grace of life: that your prayers do not be hindered.
- Proposed: You husbands, likewise dwelling with them according to knowledge, giving honor to the female as to the weaker vessel and as to the co-heirs of the grace of life: that your prayers be not hindered.
- Why: "that your prayers do not be hindered" is ungrammatical, a slip in modernising "be not hindered" for "ut non impediantur".

**1 Peter 3:20** · meaning · minor

- Latin: qui increduli fuerant aliquando, quando exspectabant Dei patientiam in diebus Noë, cum fabricaretur arca: in qua pauci, id est octo animæ, salvæ factæ sunt per aquam.
- Now: who had once been unbelieving, when they waited during God’s patience in the days of Noah, while the ark was being built. In it a few, that is, eight souls, were saved through water.
- Proposed: who had once been unbelieving, when they waited for God’s patience in the days of Noah, while the ark was being built. In it a few, that is, eight souls, were saved through water.
- Why: "exspectabant Dei patientiam": "patientiam" is the direct object, they waited for the patience of God; "waited during God’s patience" turns it into a circumstance.

## Notes on the recorded Latin (3)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **1 Peter 1:6** · coverage · same as reference: "exsultabis" (2nd singular) is a truncation of the Clementine "exsultabitis" (2nd plural), which the English "you shall greatly rejoice" renders.
- **1 Peter 3:1** · suspected-typo · same as reference: "per mulierem conversationem" is a typo for the Clementine "per mulierum conversationem" (genitive plural, "by the conversation of the wives"), as the English has it.
- **1 Peter 3:21** · suspected-typo · same as reference: "salvos fecit" appears to be a typo for the Clementine "salvos facit" (present, "now saves you"); the English "has now saved" follows the perfect and would need "now saves you also".

## Withdrawn on second reading (1)

- **1 Peter 3:18**: "offerret" (ob + ferre) properly means bring to, present; Douay itself renders "offerebant illi parvulos" as brought, so "bring us to God" is sound.
