## Proposed English corrections (10)

**Matthew 10:13** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Et siquidem fuerit domus illa digna, veniet pax vestra super eam: si autem non fuerit digna, pax vestra revertetur ad vos.
- Now: And if that house be worthy, your peace shall come upon it; but if it do not be worthy, your peace shall return to you.
- Proposed: And if that house be worthy, your peace shall come upon it; but if it be not worthy, your peace shall return to you.
- Why: "if it do not be worthy" is ungrammatical English for "si autem non fuerit digna" (if it be not worthy); a slip of the modernisation.

**Matthew 12:23** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et stupebant omnes turbæ, et dicebant: Numquid hic est filius David?
- Now: And all the multitudes were amazed, and said: Is not this the son of David?
- Proposed: And all the multitudes were amazed, and said: Is this the son of David?
- Why: "Numquid hic est filius David?" asks doubtfully (Can this be...?), expecting no; "Is not this" renders "nonne" and reverses the expected answer.

**Matthew 13:28** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Et ait illis: Inimicus homo hoc fecit. Servi autem dixerunt ei: Vis, imus, et colligimus ea?
- Now: And he said to them: An enemy has done this. And the servants said to him: Will you that we go and gather it up?
- Proposed: And he said to them: An enemy has done this. And the servants said to him: Will you that we go and gather them up?
- Why: "colligimus ea" (plural, the zizania); after the change of "cockle" to "weeds" (v. 27) the singular "it" no longer agrees.

**Matthew 13:29** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Et ait: Non: ne forte colligentes zizania, eradicetis simul cum eis et triticum.
- Now: And he said: No, lest gathering up the weeds, you root up the wheat also together with it.
- Proposed: And he said: No, lest gathering up the weeds, you root up the wheat also together with them.
- Why: "simul cum eis" = together with them; "it" is left over from the Douay singular "cockle" and does not agree with "weeds".

**Matthew 13:30** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Sinite utraque crescere usque ad messem, et in tempore messis dicam messoribus: Colligite primum zizania, et alligate ea in fasciculos ad comburendum: triticum autem congregate in horreum meum.
- Now: Suffer both to grow until the harvest, and in the time of the harvest I will say to the reapers: Gather up first the weeds, and bind it into bundles to burn, but the wheat gather you into my barn.
- Proposed: Suffer both to grow until the harvest, and in the time of the harvest I will say to the reapers: Gather up first the weeds, and bind them into bundles to burn, but the wheat gather you into my barn.
- Why: "alligate ea" = bind them; "bind it" does not agree with "the weeds" (leftover of the Douay singular "cockle").

**Matthew 13:40** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Sicut ergo colliguntur zizania, et igni comburuntur: sic erit in consummatione sæculi.
- Now: Even as weeds therefore is gathered up, and burnt with fire: so shall it be at the end of the world.
- Proposed: Even as weeds therefore are gathered up, and burnt with fire: so shall it be at the end of the world.
- Why: "colliguntur zizania" is plural; "weeds ... is gathered up" is a broken agreement left by replacing the Douay singular "cockle".

**Matthew 25:22** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Accessit autem et qui duo talenta acceperat, et ait: Domine, duo talenta tradidisti mihi, ecce alia duo lucratus sum.
- Now: And he also that had received the two talents came and said: Lord, you deliveredst two talents to me. Behold I have gained other two.
- Proposed: And he also that had received the two talents came and said: Lord, you delivered two talents to me. Behold I have gained other two.
- Why: "you deliveredst" keeps the archaic second-person singular ending after modernised "you" (Latin "tradidisti"); ungrammatical leftover from "thou deliveredst".

**Matthew 27:4** · other-text · minor

- Latin: dicens: Peccavi, tradens sanguinem justum. At illi dixerunt: Quid ad nos? tu videris.
- Now: Saying: I have sinned in betraying innocent blood. But they said: What is that to us? Look you to it.
- Proposed: Saying: I have sinned in betraying just blood. But they said: What is that to us? Look you to it.
- Why: "innocent blood" is Challoner's wording after the Greek/KJV; the Latin has "sanguinem justum", just blood (cf. "sanguis justus" 23:35, "justi hujus" 27:24, both rendered "just").

**Matthew 27:43** · meaning · minor

- Latin: confidit in Deo: liberet nunc, si vult eum: dixit enim: Quia Filius Dei sum.
- Now: He trusted in God: let him now deliver him if he will have him. For he said: I am the Son of God.
- Proposed: He trusts in God: let him now deliver him if he will have him. For he said: I am the Son of God.
- Why: "confidit" is present tense (the perfect of confido is "confisus est"): he trusts in God, not "trusted".

**Matthew 28:11** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Quæ cum abiissent, ecce quidam de custodibus venerunt in civitatem, et nuntiaverunt principibus sacerdotum omnia quæ facta fuerant.
- Now: While they were going, behold, some of the guards came into the city and reported to the chief priests everything that had happened.
- Proposed: When they had gone, behold, some of the guards came into the city and reported to the chief priests everything that had happened.
- Why: "Quæ cum abiissent" is pluperfect: when they (the women) had departed. "While they were going" follows the Greek present participle, not the Latin.
