# Joshua · Latin–English review, 9 November 2026

647 distinct readings read against the Clementine Latin (659 reader rows). [Method and limits](../README.md). Nothing below has been applied to the staging site.

## Proposed English corrections (3)

**Joshua 6:5** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Cumque insonuerit vox tubæ longior atque concisior, et in auribus vestris increpuerit, conclamabit omnis populus vociferatione maxima, et muri funditus corruent civitatis, ingredienturque singuli per locum contra quem steterint.
- Now: When the trumpet's longer, sharper blast sounds and rings in your ears, all the people must shout with a very loud cry. The city's walls will collapse to their foundations, and each person will enter at the place opposite where he stands.”
- Proposed: When the trumpet's longer, more broken blast sounds and rings in your ears, all the people must shout with a very loud cry. The city's walls will collapse to their foundations, and each person will enter at the place opposite where he stands.”
- Why: 'concisior' means more broken, cut short (a blast in short bursts), not 'sharper'.

**Joshua 15:6** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Ascenditque terminus in Beth Hagla, et transit ab aquilone in Beth Araba, ascendens ad lapidem Boën filii Ruben :
- Now: The boundary rises to Beth-hoglah and passes northward to Beth-arabah, going up to the stone of Bohan son of Reuben.
- Proposed: The boundary rises to Beth-hoglah and passes on the north to Beth-arabah, going up to the stone of Bohan son of Reuben.
- Why: 'transit ab aquilone in Beth Araba': 'ab aquilone' gives position (on/from the north side), not a direction of travel; 'northward' turns it into movement toward the north.

**Joshua 19:28** · other-text · minor

- Latin: et Abran et Rohob et Hamon et Cana, usque ad Sidonem magnam.
- Now: Abdon, Rehob, Hammon, and Kanah, as far as Great Sidon.
- Proposed: Ebron, Rehob, Hammon, and Kanah, as far as Great Sidon.
- Why: Latin 'Abran' is the Hebrew Ebron; 'Abdon' is the emended reading of some manuscripts and modern versions (cf. 'Abdon' at 21:30), not the name in this Latin.

## English and Latin cover different amounts of text (2)

- **Joshua 4:23**: Latin shown ends at 'donec transiretis' (Vg 4:23); English runs on into Vg 4:24 ('sicut fecerat prius in mari Rubro, quod siccavit donec transiremus'), which is absent from the Latin.
- **Joshua 5:14**: Latin shown ends at 'et nunc venio' (Vg 5:14); English adds 'Joshua fell facedown ... to his servant?', i.e. Vg 5:15 ('Cecidit Josue pronus in terram...'), absent from the Latin.

## Notes on the recorded Latin (3)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **Joshua 6:15** · suspected-typo · same as reference: 'Dies autem septimo' is ungrammatical; the Clementine reads 'Die autem septimo, diluculo consurgentes, circuierunt urbem...'. English is correct.
- **Joshua 9:5** · suspected-typo · same as reference: 'in frustra comminuti' should be 'in frusta comminuti' (frusta, pieces; 'frustra' means in vain). English 'broken into pieces' rightly renders 'frusta'.
- **Joshua 21:6** · suspected-typo · same as reference: 'Porro filii Gerson egressa est sors' should read 'Porro filiis Gerson egressa est sors' (dative, as in 'filiis Merari' in 21:7). English is correct.
