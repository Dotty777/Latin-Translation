# Titus · Latin–English review, 9 November 2026

46 distinct readings read against the Clementine Latin (46 reader rows). [Method and limits](../README.md). Nothing below has been applied to the staging site.

## Proposed English corrections (6)

**Titus 1:16 (Vulgate 2:1)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Confitentur se nosse Deum, factis autem negant: cum sint abominati, et incredibiles, et ad omne opus bonum reprobi.
- Now: They profess to know God, but deny him by their deeds. They are detestable, disobedient, and unfit for any good work.
- Proposed: They profess to know God, but deny him by their deeds. They are detestable, unbelieving, and unfit for any good work.
- Why: "incredibiles" here means unbelieving (compare "increduli" rendered so at 3:3); "disobedient" renders the Greek word, not the Latin.

**Titus 2:2 (Vulgate 2:3)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: senes ut sobrii sint, pudici, prudentes, sani in fide, in dilectione, in patientia:
- Now: older men are to be sober, dignified, prudent, and sound in faith, love, and endurance;
- Proposed: older men are to be sober, chaste, prudent, and sound in faith, love, and endurance;
- Why: "pudici" means chaste or modest (so rendered in 1 Timothy 3); "dignified" renders the Greek term, not this Latin word.

**Titus 2:3 (Vulgate 2:4)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: anus similiter in habitu sancto, non criminatrices, non multo vino servientes, bene docentes:
- Now: older women likewise are to be holy in their conduct, not slanderers or enslaved to much wine, but teachers of what is good,
- Proposed: older women likewise are to be holy in their conduct, not slanderers or enslaved to much wine, but teaching well,
- Why: "bene docentes" is "teaching well" (adverb of manner); "teachers of what is good" makes the good the object taught, as in the Greek compound.

**Titus 2:8 (Vulgate 2:9)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: verbum sanum, irreprehensibile: ut is qui ex adverso est, vereatur, nihil habens malum dicere de nobis.
- Now: and sound speech beyond reproach, so that an opponent may be put to shame, having nothing evil to say about us.
- Proposed: and sound speech beyond reproach, so that an opponent may be afraid, having nothing evil to say about us.
- Why: "vereatur" means may fear or stand in awe; "be put to shame" renders the Greek verb, not the Latin.

**Titus 3:8 (Vulgate 3:9)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Fidelis sermo est: et de his volo te confirmare: ut curent bonis operibus præesse qui credunt Deo. Hæc sunt bona, et utilia hominibus.
- Now: This saying is trustworthy, and I want you to insist on these things, so that those who believe God may take care to devote themselves to good works. These things are good and useful to people.
- Proposed: This saying is trustworthy, and I want you to insist on these things, so that those who believe God may take care to excel in good works. These things are good and useful to people.
- Why: "bonis operibus præesse" means to be foremost or take the lead in good works; "devote themselves to" loses the sense of "præesse".

**Titus 3:14 (Vulgate 3:15)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Discant autem et nostri bonis operibus præesse ad usus necessarios: ut non sint infructuosi.
- Now: Let our people also learn to devote themselves to good works to meet necessary needs, so that they may not be unfruitful.
- Proposed: Let our people also learn to excel in good works to meet necessary needs, so that they may not be unfruitful.
- Why: "bonis operibus præesse" means to be foremost or take the lead in good works; "devote themselves to" loses the sense of "præesse".
