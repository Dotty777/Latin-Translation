# 2 Kings · Latin–English review, 9 November 2026

719 distinct readings read against the Clementine Latin (719 reader rows). [Method and limits](../README.md). Nothing below has been applied to the staging site.

## Proposed English corrections (9)

**2 Kings 3:11** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et ait Josaphat : Estne hic propheta Domini, ut deprecemur Dominum per eum ? Et respondit unus de servis regis Israël : Est hic Eliseus filius Saphat, qui fundebat aquam super manus Eliæ.
- Now: Jehoshaphat said, “Is there no prophet of the Lord here, through whom we may entreat the Lord?” One of the servants of the king of Israel answered, “Elisha son of Shaphat is here, who used to pour water over Elijah's hands.”
- Proposed: Jehoshaphat said, “Is there a prophet of the Lord here, through whom we may entreat the Lord?” One of the servants of the king of Israel answered, “Elisha son of Shaphat is here, who used to pour water over Elijah's hands.”
- Why: 'Estne hic propheta Domini' is a plain positive question, 'Is there here a prophet of the Lord'; the negative 'Is there no prophet' follows the Hebrew form, not the Latin.

**2 Kings 3:21** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Universi autem Moabitæ audientes quod ascendissent reges ut pugnarent adversum eos, convocaverunt omnes qui accincti erant balteo desuper, et steterunt in terminis.
- Now: All the Moabites heard that the kings had come up to fight against them. They summoned everyone old enough to wear a sword belt and took their stand at the borders.
- Proposed: All the Moabites heard that the kings had come up to fight against them. They summoned everyone who was girded with a sword belt and took their stand at the borders.
- Why: 'omnes qui accincti erant balteo desuper' is 'all who were girded with a belt upon them'; 'old enough to wear' adds an age criterion the Latin does not state.

**2 Kings 4:25** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Profecta est igitur, et venit ad virum Dei in montem Carmeli : cumque vidisset eam vir Dei e contra, ait ad Giezi puerum suum : Ecce Sunamitis illa.
- Now: She set out and came to the man of God on Mount Carmel. When the man of God saw her from a distance, he said to his servant Gehazi, “Look, there is that Shunammite woman.
- Proposed: She set out and came to the man of God on Mount Carmel. When the man of God saw her opposite him, he said to his servant Gehazi, “Look, there is that Shunammite woman.
- Why: 'cumque vidisset eam vir Dei e contra' is 'saw her over against him / coming toward him'; 'from a distance' renders the Hebrew ('afar off'), not 'e contra'.

**2 Kings 4:38** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et Eliseus reversus est in Galgala. Erat autem fames in terra, et filii prophetarum habitabant coram eo. Dixitque uni de pueris suis : Pone ollam grandem, et coque pulmentum filiis prophetarum.
- Now: Elisha returned to Gilgal. There was a famine in the land, and the sons of the prophets were sitting before him. He said to one of his servants, “Set out the large pot and cook a stew for the sons of the prophets.”
- Proposed: Elisha returned to Gilgal. There was a famine in the land, and the sons of the prophets were dwelling before him. He said to one of his servants, “Set out the large pot and cook a stew for the sons of the prophets.”
- Why: 'filii prophetarum habitabant coram eo' is 'dwelt before him'; 'were sitting before him' follows the Hebrew verb, not the Latin 'habitabant'.

**2 Kings 9:16** · meaning · minor · amended on second reading

- Latin: Et ascendit, et profectus est in Jezrahel : Joram enim ægrotabat ibi, et Ochozias rex Juda descenderat ad visitandum Joram.
- Now: He mounted his chariot and went to Jezreel, for Joram was ill there, and Ahaziah king of Judah had gone down to visit him.
- Proposed: He mounted and went to Jezreel, for Joram was ill there, and Ahaziah king of Judah had gone down to visit him.
- Why: "Et ascendit, et profectus est" is simply 'he went up and set out'; "his chariot" is not in the Latin (it reflects the Hebrew 'rode').

**2 Kings 9:26** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Si non pro sanguine Naboth, et pro sanguine filiorum ejus, quem vidi heri, ait Dominus, reddam tibi in agro isto, dicit Dominus. Nunc ergo tolle, et projice eum in agrum juxta verbum Domini.
- Now: ‘As surely as I saw the blood of Naboth and the blood of his sons yesterday, says the Lord, I will repay you in this field, says the Lord.’ Now take him and throw him into the field, according to the Lord's word.”
- Proposed: ‘Surely for the blood of Naboth and for the blood of his sons, which I saw yesterday, says the Lord, I will repay you in this field, says the Lord.’ Now take him and throw him into the field, according to the Lord's word.”
- Why: Latin oath is "Si non pro sanguine Naboth... quem vidi heri... reddam tibi": 'surely I will repay you FOR the blood... which I saw'. English follows the Hebrew syntax ('As surely as I saw') and drops "pro".

**2 Kings 10:25** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Factum est autem, cum completum esset holocaustum, præcepit Jehu militibus et ducibus suis : Ingredimini, et percutite eos : nullus evadat. Percusseruntque eos in ore gladii, et projecerunt milites et duces : et ierunt in civitatem templi Baal,
- Now: When the burnt offering was completed, Jehu commanded his soldiers and officers, “Go in and strike them down; let none escape.” They struck them with the edge of the sword. The soldiers and officers threw them out and went into the citadel of Baal's temple.
- Proposed: When the burnt offering was completed, Jehu commanded his soldiers and officers, “Go in and strike them down; let none escape.” They struck them with the edge of the sword. The soldiers and officers threw them out and went into the city of Baal's temple.
- Why: "in civitatem templi Baal" is 'into the city of the temple of Baal'; civitas does not mean 'citadel' (a modern rendering of the Hebrew).

**2 Kings 15:5** · meaning · minor · amended on second reading

- Latin: Percussit autem Dominus regem, et fuit leprosus usque in diem mortis suæ, et habitabat in domo libera seorsum : Joatham vero filius regis gubernabat palatium, et judicabit populum terræ.
- Now: The Lord struck the king, and he was a leper until the day of his death. He lived apart in a separate house, while Jotham the king's son governed the palace and was to judge the people of the land.
- Proposed: The Lord struck the king, and he was a leper until the day of his death. He lived apart in a free house, while Jotham the king's son governed the palace and was to judge the people of the land.
- Why: "in domo libera seorsum" is 'in a free house apart'; "libera" is not 'separate' (that reflects the Hebrew). "judicabat" (Clementine) is 'judged', not 'was to judge'.

**2 Kings 19:25** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Numquid non audisti quid ab initio fecerim ? ex diebus antiquis plasmavi illud, et nunc adduxi : eruntque in ruinam collium pugnantium civitates munitæ.
- Now: Have you not heard what I did from the beginning? From ancient days I shaped it, and now I have brought it about: fortified cities will become ruined heaps for those who fight.
- Proposed: Have you not heard what I did from the beginning? From ancient days I shaped it, and now I have brought it about: fortified cities of fighting men will become ruined heaps.
- Why: "pugnantium civitates munitæ": pugnantium is genitive, 'fortified cities of fighting men', not ruins 'for those who fight'.

## Notes on the recorded Latin (10)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **2 Kings 8:11** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has "Stetique" (which would be first person, 'and I stood'); the Clementine reads "Stetitque cum eo" ('and he stood with him'), as the English correctly renders. Correct Latin: Stetitque.
- **2 Kings 9:12** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has singular "At ille responderunt" with a plural verb; the Clementine reads "At illi responderunt" ('But they answered'). Correct Latin: At illi responderunt.
- **2 Kings 10:6** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has "apud optimates civitates nutriebantur"; the Clementine reads genitive "civitatis" ('with the nobles of the city'), as the English renders. Correct Latin: apud optimates civitatis.
- **2 Kings 10:7** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has accusative "Cumque venissent litteras ad eos"; the subject must be nominative: Clementine "Cumque venissent litteræ ad eos" ('when the letter reached them').
- **2 Kings 15:5** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has future "judicabit populum terræ" beside imperfect "gubernabat"; the Clementine reads "judicabat" ('judged'). Correct Latin: et judicabat populum terræ. The English 'was to judge' appears to follow the slip.
- **2 Kings 15:13** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has "trigesimo novo anno" ('thirty-new year'); the Clementine reads "trigesimo nono anno" ('thirty-ninth year'), as the English renders. Correct Latin: trigesimo nono anno.
- **2 Kings 19:21** · bracket: Stray markup: an opening square bracket before "Sprevit" ("[Sprevit te"), with its closing bracket after "servum meum." in 19:34 (#34). Source-edition verse brackets, not Latin text. Correct Latin: Sprevit te ... servum meum.
- **2 Kings 21:17** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has "Manasse, ut universa quæ fecit"; the Clementine reads "et universa quæ fecit" ('and everything he did'), as the English renders. Correct Latin: et universa quæ fecit.
- **2 Kings 22:14** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has "et Sapham" for the scribe named "Saphan" in 22:3, 8-10, 12; apparent typo. Correct Latin: et Saphan. English 'Shaphan' is right.
- **2 Kings 23:18** · coverage · same as reference: Latin has singular "Dimitte eum", though Josiah addresses the citizens (plural "Responderunt... cives"); the Clementine reads plural "Dimittite eum". Apparent truncation. Correct Latin: Dimittite eum. English is unaffected.
