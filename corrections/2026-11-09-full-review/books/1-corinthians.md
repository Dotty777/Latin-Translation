## Proposed English corrections (16)

**1 Corinthians 6:5** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Ad verecundiam vestram dico. Sic non est inter vos sapiens quisquam, qui possit judicare inter fratrem suum?
- Now: I speak to your shame. Is it so that there is not among you anyone wise man that is able to judge between his brothers?
- Proposed: I speak to your shame. Is it so that there is not among you any one wise man that is able to judge between his brothers?
- Why: "anyone wise man" is ungrammatical; "sapiens quisquam" is "any one wise man".

**1 Corinthians 6:15** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Nescitis quoniam corpora vestra membra sunt Christi? Tollens ergo membra Christi, faciam membra meretricis? Absit.
- Now: Do you not know that your bodies are the members of Christ? Shall I then take the members of Christ and make them the members of an prostitute? God forbid!
- Proposed: Do you not know that your bodies are the members of Christ? Shall I then take the members of Christ and make them the members of a prostitute? God forbid!
- Why: Wrong article: "an prostitute" ("membra meretricis") should be "a prostitute".

**1 Corinthians 7:7** · other-text · major

- Latin: Volo enim omnes vos esse sicut meipsum: sed unusquisque proprium donum habet ex Deo: alius quidem sic, alius vero sic.
- Now: For I would that all men were even as myself. But everyone has his proper gift from God: one after this manner, and another after that.
- Proposed: For I would that all of you were even as myself. But everyone has his proper gift from God: one after this manner, and another after that.
- Why: "Volo enim omnes vos esse" is "all of you"; "all men" follows the Greek reading (all men), not the Latin "vos".

**1 Corinthians 7:35** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Porro hoc ad utilitatem vestram dico: non ut laqueum vobis injiciam, sed ad id, quod honestum est, et quod facultatem præbeat sine impedimento Dominum obsecrandi.
- Now: And this I speak for your profit, not to cast a snare upon you, but for that which is decent and which may give you power to attend upon the Lord, without impediment.
- Proposed: And this I speak for your profit, not to cast a snare upon you, but for that which is decent and which may give you power to entreat the Lord, without impediment.
- Why: "Dominum obsecrandi" means of beseeching, praying to the Lord; "attend upon the Lord" does not render "obsecrare".

**1 Corinthians 9:2** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Et si aliis non sum Apostolus, sed tamen vobis sum: nam signaculum apostolatus mei vos estis in Domino.
- Now: And if to others I do not be an apostle, but yet to you I am. For you are the seal of my apostleship in the Lord.
- Proposed: And if to others I am not an apostle, but yet to you I am. For you are the seal of my apostleship in the Lord.
- Why: "I do not be an apostle" is ungrammatical (garbled modernisation of "I be not") for "non sum Apostolus".

**1 Corinthians 9:8** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Numquid secundum hominem hæc dico? an et lex hæc non dicit?
- Now: Speak I these things according to man? Or does not the law also say; these things?
- Proposed: Speak I these things according to man? Or does not the law also say these things?
- Why: Stray semicolon breaks the clause: "say; these things" ("an et lex hæc non dicit?").

**1 Corinthians 10:32** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Sine offensione estote Judæis, et gentibus, et ecclesiæ Dei:
- Now: Be without offence to the Jew, and to the Gentiles and to the church of God:
- Proposed: Be without offence to the Jews, and to the Gentiles and to the church of God:
- Why: "Judæis" is plural, to the Jews, parallel with "gentibus"; the English has the singular "the Jew".

**1 Corinthians 11:26** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Quotiescumque enim manducabitis panem hunc, et calicem bibetis, mortem Domini annuntiabitis donec veniat.
- Now: For whenever you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord’s death until he comes.
- Proposed: For whenever you eat this bread and drink the cup, you will proclaim the Lord’s death until he comes.
- Why: "annuntiabitis" is future (you shall proclaim); the present "you proclaim" follows the Greek, not the Vulgate's future.

**1 Corinthians 13:12** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Videmus nunc per speculum in ænigmate: tunc autem facie ad faciem. Nunc cognosco ex parte: tunc autem cognoscam sicut et cognitus sum.
- Now: For now we see through a mirror, in a riddle, but then face to face. Now I know in part, but then I shall know just as I have been known.
- Proposed: Now we see through a mirror, in a riddle, but then face to face. Now I know in part, but then I shall know just as I have been known.
- Why: 'For' is added from the Greek/KJV (gar); the Latin begins simply 'Videmus nunc' with no 'enim'.

**1 Corinthians 14:4** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Qui loquitur lingua, semetipsum ædificat: qui autem prophetat, ecclesiam Dei ædificat.
- Now: He that speaks in a tongue builds up himself: but he that prophesies, builds up the church.
- Proposed: He that speaks in a tongue builds up himself: but he that prophesies, builds up the church of God.
- Why: 'ecclesiam Dei ædificat' = builds up the church of God; 'Dei' is left untranslated.

**1 Corinthians 15:14** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Si autem Christus non resurrexit, inanis est ergo prædicatio nostra, inanis est et fides vestra:
- Now: And if Christ do not be risen again, then is our preaching vain: and your faith is also vain.
- Proposed: And if Christ is not risen again, then is our preaching vain: and your faith is also vain.
- Why: 'do not be risen' is broken English (faulty modernisation of 'be not risen') for 'non resurrexit'.

**1 Corinthians 15:32** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Si secundum hominem ad bestias pugnavi Ephesi, quid mihi prodest, si mortui non resurgunt? Manducemus, et bibamus, cras enim moriemur.
- Now: If (according to man) I fought with beasts at Ephesus, what does it profit me, if the dead rise not again? Let us eat and drink, for to next day we shall die.
- Proposed: If (according to man) I fought with beasts at Ephesus, what does it profit me, if the dead rise not again? Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we shall die.
- Why: 'for to next day we shall die' is a broken modernisation of 'to morrow'; 'cras enim moriemur' = for tomorrow we shall die.

**1 Corinthians 16:2** · english-typo · minor

- Latin: Per unam sabbati unusquisque vestrum apud se seponat, recondens quod ei bene placuerit: ut non, cum venero, tunc collectæ fiant.
- Now: On the first day of the week, let each of you put apart with himself, laying up what it shall well please him: that when I come, the collections do not be then to be made.
- Proposed: On the first day of the week, let each of you put apart with himself, laying up what it shall well please him: that when I come, the collections be not then to be made.
- Why: 'the collections do not be then to be made' is broken English (faulty modernisation of 'be not then to be made') for 'ut non ... tunc collectæ fiant'.

**1 Corinthians 16:15** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Obsecro autem vos fratres, nostis domum Stephanæ, et Fortunati, et Achaici: quoniam sunt primitiæ Achaiæ, et in ministerium sanctorum ordinaverunt seipsos:
- Now: And I implore you, brothers, you know the house of Stephanus, and of Fortunatus, and of Achaicus, that they are the firstfruits of Achaia, and have dedicated themselves to the ministry of the saints:
- Proposed: And I implore you, brothers, you know the house of Stephanas, and of Fortunatus, and of Achaicus, that they are the firstfruits of Achaia, and have dedicated themselves to the ministry of the saints:
- Why: 'Stephanæ' is the genitive of Stephanas; 'Stephanus' is a different name (Stephen).
