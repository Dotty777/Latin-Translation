## Proposed English corrections (7)

**Sirach 7:27 (Vulgate 7:29)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: honora patrem tuum, et gemitus matris tuæ ne obliviscaris :
- Now: honor your father, and do not forget your mother's birth pangs.
- Proposed: honor your father, and do not forget your mother's groans.
- Why: Latin 'gemitus matris tuæ' means your mother's groans/groanings; 'birth pangs' renders the Greek (odinas), not the Latin word.

**Sirach 19:3** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et qui se jungit fornicariis erit nequam : putredo et vermes hæreditabunt illum : et extolletur in exemplum majus, et tolletur de numero anima ejus.]
- Now: The one who joins himself to prostitutes will become worthless. Rot and worms will inherit him; he will be held up as a great example, and his soul will be removed from the number of the living.
- Proposed: The one who joins himself to prostitutes will become worthless. Rot and worms will inherit him; he will be held up as a great example, and his soul will be removed from the number.
- Why: Latin 'tolletur de numero anima ejus' says only 'his soul will be taken from the number'; 'of the living' is an explanatory addition with no Latin behind it.

**Sirach 28:15 (Vulgate 28:19)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Lingua tertia mulieres viratas ejecit, et privavit illas laboribus suis.
- Now: A third tongue has driven out married women and deprived them of their labors.
- Proposed: A third tongue has driven out valiant women and deprived them of their labors.
- Why: Latin 'mulieres viratas': viratus means manly, valiant (Douay 'valiant women'), not 'married'; the English narrows it to marital status.

**Sirach 39:1** · meaning · major

- Latin: Sapientiam omnium antiquorum exquiret sapiens, et in prophetis vacabit.
- Now: This passage continues Latin 38:39 in the preceding comparison chapter. A wise man will seek out the wisdom of all the ancients and devote his leisure to the prophets.
- Proposed: A wise man will seek out the wisdom of all the ancients and devote his leisure to the prophets.
- Why: The English opens with an editorial sentence, 'This passage continues Latin 38:39 in the preceding comparison chapter.', which renders nothing in the Latin 'Sapientiam omnium antiquorum exquiret sapiens, et in prophetis vacabit'.

**Sirach 41:19 (Vulgate 41:23)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: a socio et amico de injustitia, et de loco in quo habitas :
- Now: before a companion and a friend, of injustice, and before the place where you dwell,
- Proposed: before a companion and a friend, of injustice, and of the place where you dwell,
- Why: Latin 'de loco in quo habitas' uses 'de', the list's marker for the thing one is ashamed of; 'before the place' renders it as if 'a loco', the Greek sense, making the place a witness.

**Sirach 41:20 (Vulgate 41:24)** · other-text · minor

- Latin: de furto, de veritate Dei, et testamento : de discubitu in panibus, et ab obfuscatione dati et accepti :
- Now: of theft; before God's truth and covenant; of reclining upon the bread, and of obscuring what is given and received;
- Proposed: of theft; of God's truth and covenant; of reclining upon the bread, and of obscuring what is given and received;
- Why: Latin 'de veritate Dei, et testamento' is 'of/concerning God's truth and the covenant' (the matter of shame); 'before God's truth and covenant' turns them into witnesses, as in the Greek, against the Latin 'de'.

**Sirach 50:16 (Vulgate 50:18)** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Tunc exclamaverunt filii Aaron, in tubis productilibus sonuerunt : et auditam fecerunt vocem magnam in memoriam coram Deo.
- Now: Then Aaron's sons cried aloud and sounded long trumpets, and made a great sound heard as a remembrance before God.
- Proposed: Then Aaron's sons cried aloud and sounded beaten trumpets, and made a great sound heard as a remembrance before God.
- Why: "in tubis productilibus": productilis means beaten or hammered out (of metal), not long; the trumpets are of beaten work.
