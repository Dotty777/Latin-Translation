## Proposed English corrections (15)

**Job 3:9** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Obtenebrentur stellæ caligine ejus ; expectet lucem, et non videat, nec ortum surgentis auroræ.
- Now: Let its stars be darkened with gloom. Let it await light and not see it, nor the rising of the dawn,
- Proposed: Let the stars be darkened with its gloom. Let it await light and not see it, nor the rising of the dawn,
- Why: "stellæ caligine ejus": ejus qualifies caligine ("with its gloom"), not stellæ; the English moves the possessive to the stars ("its stars ... with gloom").

**Job 5:23** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Sed cum lapidibus regionum pactum tuum, et bestiæ terræ pacificæ erunt tibi.
- Now: For your covenant shall be with the stones of the fields, and the beasts of the earth shall be at peace with you.
- Proposed: But your covenant shall be with the stones of the lands, and the beasts of the earth shall be at peace with you.
- Why: "Sed cum lapidibus regionum" = "But ... with the stones of the lands"; "For ... stones of the fields" follows the Hebrew wording, not the Latin Sed / regionum.

**Job 5:24** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et scies quod pacem habeat tabernaculum tuum ; et visitans speciem tuam, non peccabis.
- Now: You shall know that your tent is at peace; when you inspect your property, you shall not go wrong.
- Proposed: You shall know that your tent is at peace; when you visit your beauty, you shall not sin.
- Why: "visitans speciem tuam, non peccabis" = "visiting your beauty, you shall not sin"; "inspect your property ... not go wrong" reflects the Hebrew (fold/miss nothing), not speciem / peccabis.

**Job 8:2** · other-text · minor

- Latin: [Usquequo loqueris talia, et spiritus multiplex sermones oris tui ?
- Now: How long will you speak such things, the words of your mouth a mighty wind?
- Proposed: How long will you speak such things, the words of your mouth a manifold wind?
- Why: "spiritus multiplex" = a manifold (many-sided) wind/spirit; "mighty wind" follows the Hebrew (and DR "strong wind"), not multiplex.

**Job 8:18** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Si absorbuerit eum de loco suo, negabit eum, et dicet : Non novi te.
- Now: If his place swallows him up, it shall deny him and say: I do not know you.
- Proposed: If one swallows him up from his place, it shall deny him and say: I do not know you.
- Why: "Si absorbuerit eum de loco suo": he is swallowed up out of his place (de loco suo, separation); the English makes the place the swallower and drops "from".

**Job 10:7** · meaning · minor

- Latin: et scias quia nihil impium fecerim, cum sit nemo qui de manu tua possit eruere ?
- Now: though You know that I have done nothing wicked and there is no one who can rescue me from Your hand?
- Proposed: and know that I have done nothing wicked, while there is no one who can rescue me from Your hand?
- Why: "et scias quia nihil impium fecerim" continues ut quæras ... scruteris ("that You should seek ... and know"); the English turns it into a concession ("though You know"), as in the Hebrew.

**Job 12:24** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Qui immutat cor principum populi terræ, et decipit eos ut frustra incedant per invium :
- Now: He changes the heart of the earth's rulers and misleads them to walk in vain through a trackless place.
- Proposed: He changes the heart of the rulers of the people of the earth and misleads them to walk in vain through a trackless place.
- Why: "principum populi terræ" = the princes of the people of the earth; "populi" is left untranslated ("the earth's rulers").

**Job 14:4** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Quis potest facere mundum de immundo conceptum semine ? nonne tu qui solus es ?
- Now: Who can make clean one conceived from unclean seed? Is it not You, who alone can do so?
- Proposed: Who can make clean one conceived from unclean seed? Is it not You, who alone are?
- Why: "nonne tu qui solus es?" = "is it not You who alone are?"; the English adds "can do so", which the Latin lacks, changing es (are) into an ability.

**Job 16:3** · meaning · major

- Latin: Numquid habebunt finem verba ventosa ? aut aliquid tibi molestum est, si loquaris ?
- Now: Will windy words have an end? Or does something trouble you, that you must speak?
- Proposed: Will windy words have an end? Or is it any trouble to you to speak?
- Why: "aut aliquid tibi molestum est, si loquaris?" = "or is anything burdensome to you if you speak?"; the English reverses it (a trouble that makes him speak), as in the Hebrew.

**Job 22:14** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Nubes latibulum ejus, nec nostra considerat, et circa cardines cæli perambulat.
- Now: Clouds are His hiding place; He does not consider our affairs, but walks around the vault of heaven.
- Proposed: Clouds are His hiding place; He does not consider our affairs, but walks around the poles of heaven.
- Why: "circa cardines cæli" = about the hinges/poles of heaven; "vault of heaven" reflects the Hebrew (circle/vault), not cardines.

**Job 22:22** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Suscipe ex ore illius legem, et pone sermones ejus in corde tuo.
- Now: Receive instruction from His mouth and place His words in your heart.
- Proposed: Receive the law from His mouth and place His words in your heart.
- Why: "Suscipe ex ore illius legem": legem = law; "instruction" follows the Hebrew torah sense rather than the Latin lex.

**Job 23:4** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Ponam coram eo judicium, et os meum replebo increpationibus :
- Now: I would set my case before Him and fill my mouth with arguments,
- Proposed: I would set my case before Him and fill my mouth with reproofs,
- Why: "os meum replebo increpationibus": increpatio = rebuke, reproof; "arguments" follows the Hebrew/KJV wording, not increpationibus.

**Job 30:19** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Comparatus sum luto, et assimilatus sum favillæ et cineri.
- Now: I have been likened to mud and made like dust and ashes.
- Proposed: I have been likened to mud and made like embers and ashes.
- Why: "favillæ et cineri" = embers (hot ash) and ashes; "dust and ashes" follows the Hebrew pairing, not favillæ.

**Job 31:18** · meaning · minor

- Latin: (quia ab infantia mea crevit mecum miseratio, et de utero matris meæ egressa est mecum) ;
- Now: though compassion grew with me from my infancy and came out with me from my mother's womb,
- Proposed: for compassion grew with me from my infancy and came out with me from my mother's womb,
- Why: "quia ab infantia mea crevit mecum miseratio": quia = for/because, giving the reason he did not eat alone; the English makes it concessive ("though").

**Job 36:30** · other-text · minor

- Latin: et fulgurare lumine suo desuper, cardines quoque maris operiet.
- Now: and flash with His light from above, He will also cover the depths of the sea.
- Proposed: and flash with His light from above, He will also cover the ends of the sea.
- Why: "cardines quoque maris operiet": cardines = hinges, poles, extremities ("the ends of the sea"); "depths" reflects the Hebrew (roots/bottom of the sea), not cardines.

## English and Latin cover different amounts of text (1)

- **Job 16:4**: Latin ends at "pro anima mea"; the English adds "I too would comfort you with speeches and shake my head over you" = Vg 16:5 "consolarer et ego vos sermonibus, et moverem caput meum super vos", absent.
