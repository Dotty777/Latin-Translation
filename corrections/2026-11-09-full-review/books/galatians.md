## Proposed English corrections (4)

**Galatians 1:16** · other-text · minor

- Latin: ut revelaret Filium suum in me, ut evangelizarem illum in gentibus: continuo non acquievi carni et sanguini,
- Now: to reveal his Son in me so that I might proclaim him among the Gentiles, I did not immediately confer with flesh and blood,
- Proposed: to reveal his Son in me so that I might proclaim him among the Gentiles, I did not immediately yield to flesh and blood,
- Why: 'non acquievi carni et sanguini' = I did not acquiesce in / yield to flesh and blood (DR 'condescended not'); 'confer with' renders the Greek prosanethemen, not the Latin verb.

**Galatians 4:4** · other-text · minor

- Latin: At ubi venit plenitudo temporis, misit Deus Filium suum factum ex muliere, factum sub lege,
- Now: But when the fullness of time came, God sent his Son, born of a woman, born under the law,
- Proposed: But when the fullness of time came, God sent his Son, made of a woman, made under the law,
- Why: 'factum ex muliere, factum sub lege' = made of a woman, made under the law; 'born' is the modern rendering of the Greek and would answer to 'natum', not the Vulgate's distinctive 'factum'.

**Galatians 4:24** · other-text · minor

- Latin: quæ sunt per allegoriam dicta. Hæc enim sunt duo testamenta. Unum quidem in monte Sina, in servitutem generans, quæ est Agar:
- Now: These things are spoken as an allegory. For these are two covenants. One is from Mount Sinai, bearing children for slavery. This is Hagar.
- Proposed: These things are spoken as an allegory. For these are two covenants. One is on Mount Sinai, bearing children for slavery. This is Hagar.
- Why: 'in monte Sina' = on Mount Sinai; 'from Mount Sinai' follows the Greek (apo), not the Latin preposition.

**Galatians 6:11** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Videte qualibus litteris scripsi vobis mea manu.
- Now: See how I have written to you with my own hand.
- Proposed: See with what letters I have written to you with my own hand.
- Why: 'qualibus litteris scripsi' = with what (kind of) letters I have written; the English 'how' drops 'litteris'.
