# Judges · Latin–English review, 9 November 2026

## Proposed English corrections (3)

**Judges 7:17** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et dixit ad eos : Quod me facere videritis, hoc facite : ingrediar partem castrorum, et quod fecero, sectamini.
- Now: He said to them, “Do what you see me do. I will enter the edge of the camp; follow what I do.
- Proposed: He said to them, “Do what you see me do. I will enter a part of the camp; follow what I do.
- Why: "ingrediar partem castrorum" = I will enter a part of the camp; "the edge of the camp" follows the Hebrew, not the Latin "partem".

**Judges 7:19** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Ingressusque est Gedeon, et trecenti viri qui erant cum eo, in partem castrorum, incipientibus vigiliis noctis mediæ : et custodibus suscitatis, cœperunt buccinis clangere, et complodere inter se lagenas.
- Now: Gideon and the three hundred men with him entered the edge of the camp at the beginning of the middle watch of the night, when the guards had been roused. They began to sound their trumpets and smash their jars together.
- Proposed: Gideon and the three hundred men with him entered a part of the camp at the beginning of the middle watch of the night, when the guards had been roused. They began to sound their trumpets and smash their jars together.
- Why: "in partem castrorum" = into a part of the camp; "the edge of the camp" follows the Hebrew, not the Latin "partem".

**Judges 18:15** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et cum paululum declinassent, ingressi sunt domum adolescentis Levitæ, qui erat in domo Michæ : salutaveruntque eum verbis pacificis.
- Now: They turned aside a little and entered the house of the young Levite, which was in Micah's household, and greeted him with words of peace.
- Proposed: They turned aside a little and entered the house of the young Levite, who was in Micah's household, and greeted him with words of peace.
- Why: "adolescentis Levitæ, qui erat in domo Michæ": masculine "qui" refers to the Levite, not to "domum" (feminine); it is the Levite who was in Micah's house, not the house "which was".

## English and Latin cover different amounts of text (1)

- **Judges 5:31**: English ends with "The land had rest for forty years", which is Vg 5:32 (Quievitque terra per quadraginta annos); the Latin shown stops at "ita rutilent" and has no such sentence.

## Notes on the recorded Latin (6)

"Same as reference" means the recorded Latin has the same words as the reference Clementine transcription, so a suspected typo was not introduced by the site.

- **Judges 14:6** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has "in frustra discerpens"; "frustra" (in vain) is a slip for "frusta" (pieces): read "in frusta discerpens". Same spelling recurs at Judges 19:29 and 20:6. The English "into pieces" is correct.
- **Judges 14:16** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has "et quærebatur, dicens" (was being sought); the sense requires the deponent "querebatur" (complained): read "et querebatur, dicens". The English "complained" already renders the correct word.
- **Judges 16:3** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has "usque ad medium noctem", which is ungrammatical (neuter "medium" with feminine accusative); read "usque ad medium noctis". The English "until midnight" is correct.
- **Judges 16:8** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has "quibus vinxit eam" (bound her); the sense, and the English "she bound him", require the masculine: read "quibus vinxit eum".
- **Judges 19:29** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has "in duodecim partes ac frustra concidens"; "frustra" (in vain) stands for "frusta" (pieces): read "partes ac frusta". Same spelling recurs at Judges 14:6 and 20:6. The English "pieces" is correct.
- **Judges 20:6** · suspected-typo · same as reference: Latin has "in frustra concidi"; "frustra" (in vain) stands for "frusta" (pieces): read "in frusta concidi". Same spelling recurs at Judges 14:6 and 19:29. The English "into pieces" is correct.
