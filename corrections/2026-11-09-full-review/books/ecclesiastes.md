## Proposed English corrections (2)

**Ecclesiastes 9:1** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Omnia hæc tractavi in corde meo, ut curiose intelligerem. Sunt justi atque sapientes, et opera eorum in manu Dei ; et tamen nescit homo utrum amore an odio dignus sit.
- Now: I considered all these things in my heart, to understand them carefully. The just and the wise and their works are in the hand of God, yet a person does not know whether he is worthy of love or hatred.
- Proposed: I considered all these things in my heart, to understand them carefully. There are just and wise people, and their works are in the hand of God, yet a person does not know whether he is worthy of love or hatred.
- Why: Latin "Sunt justi atque sapientes, et opera eorum in manu Dei": there are just and wise men, and their works are in God's hand. English puts the just and wise themselves in God's hand, as Hebrew/KJV.

**Ecclesiastes 12:5** · meaning · minor

- Latin: excelsa quoque timebunt, et formidabunt in via. Florebit amygdalus, impinguabitur locusta, et dissipabitur capparis, quoniam ibit homo in domum æternitatis suæ, et circuibunt in platea plangentes.
- Now: They will also fear heights and be afraid on the road. The almond tree will blossom, the locust will grow fat, and the caper will scatter, because the human being will go to the house of his eternity and the mourners will go around in the street.
- Proposed: They will also fear heights and be afraid on the road. The almond tree will blossom, the locust will grow fat, and the caper will be scattered, because the human being will go to the house of his eternity and the mourners will go around in the street.
- Why: Latin "dissipabitur capparis" is passive: the caper will be scattered/destroyed. English "will scatter" makes it active.
