## Proposed English corrections (11)

**1 Samuel 3:21** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et addidit Dominus ut appareret in Silo, quoniam revelatus fuerat Dominus Samueli in Silo juxta verbum Domini. Et evenit sermo Samuelis universo Israëli.
- Now: The Lord continued to appear at Shiloh, for the Lord had revealed Himself to Samuel at Shiloh through the word of the Lord. And Samuel's word came to all Israel.
- Proposed: The Lord continued to appear at Shiloh, for the Lord had revealed Himself to Samuel at Shiloh according to the word of the Lord. And Samuel's word came to all Israel.
- Why: "juxta verbum Domini" = according to the word of the Lord; "through the word" gives an instrumental sense that "juxta" does not have.

**1 Samuel 4:8** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Væ nobis : non enim fuit tanta exultatio heri et nudiustertius : væ nobis. Quis nos salvabit de manu deorum sublimium istorum ? hi sunt dii, qui percusserunt Ægyptum omni plaga in deserto.
- Now: “Woe to us! There was no such rejoicing yesterday or the day before. Woe to us! Who will save us from the hand of these mighty gods? These are the gods who struck Egypt with every plague in the wilderness.
- Proposed: “Woe to us! There was no such rejoicing yesterday or the day before. Woe to us! Who will save us from the hand of these exalted gods? These are the gods who struck Egypt with every plague in the wilderness.
- Why: "deorum sublimium istorum" = these lofty/exalted gods; "mighty gods" reflects the Hebrew (and KJV) wording rather than the Latin "sublimium".

**1 Samuel 6:21** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Miseruntque nuntios ad habitatores Cariathiarim, dicentes : Reduxerunt Philisthiim arcam Domini : descendite, et reducite eam ad vos.
- Now: They sent messengers to the inhabitants of Kiriath-jearim, saying, “The Philistines have brought back the ark of the Lord. Come down and take it up to yourselves.”
- Proposed: They sent messengers to the inhabitants of Kiriath-jearim, saying, “The Philistines have brought back the ark of the Lord. Come down and bring it back to yourselves.”
- Why: Latin 'reducite eam ad vos' = 'bring it back to you' (same verb as 'reduxerunt' just before); 'take it up' follows the Hebrew/KJV 'bring it up'.

**1 Samuel 7:1** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Venerunt ergo viri Cariathiarim, et reduxerunt arcam Domini, et intulerunt eam in domum Abinadab in Gabaa : Eleazarum autem filium ejus sanctificaverunt, ut custodiret arcam Domini.
- Now: The men of Kiriath-jearim came and took up the ark of the Lord. They brought it into Abinadab's house at Gibeah and consecrated his son Eleazar to guard the ark of the Lord.
- Proposed: The men of Kiriath-jearim came and brought back the ark of the Lord. They brought it into Abinadab's house at Gibeah and consecrated his son Eleazar to guard the ark of the Lord.
- Why: Latin 'reduxerunt arcam Domini' = 'brought back the ark'; 'took up' reflects the Hebrew 'brought up', not the Vulgate's verb.

**1 Samuel 13:17** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Et egressi sunt ad prædandum de castris Philisthinorum tres cunei. Unus cuneus pergebat contra viam Ephra ad terram Sual :
- Now: Three companies went out from the Philistine camp to plunder. One company took the road toward Ophrah, in the land of Shual.
- Proposed: Three companies went out from the Philistine camp to plunder. One company took the road toward Ophrah, to the land of Shual.
- Why: Latin 'ad terram Sual' gives the direction of the march ('to the land of Shual'); 'in the land of Shual' makes it the location of Ophrah.

**1 Samuel 13:23** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Egressa est autem statio Philisthiim, ut transcenderet in Machmas.
- Now: A Philistine outpost went out to the crossing at Michmash.
- Proposed: A Philistine outpost went out to cross over into Michmash.
- Why: Latin 'ut transcenderet in Machmas' is a purpose clause, 'to cross over into Michmash'; 'to the crossing at Michmash' renders the Hebrew noun ('pass of Michmash').

**1 Samuel 14:15** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et factum est miraculum in castris per agros : sed et omnis populus stationis eorum qui ierant ad prædandum, obstupuit, et conturbata est terra : et accidit quasi miraculum a Deo.
- Now: A wonder occurred in the camp throughout the fields. All the people of the outpost and those who had gone out to plunder were stunned, and the earth was shaken. It happened like a wonder from God.
- Proposed: A wonder occurred in the camp throughout the fields. All the people of their outpost who had gone out to plunder were stunned, and the earth was shaken. It happened like a wonder from God.
- Why: Latin 'omnis populus stationis eorum qui ierant ad prædandum' is one group (no 'et'); 'the outpost and those who had gone out to plunder' makes two groups, as in the Hebrew.

**1 Samuel 16:19** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Misit ergo Saul nuntios ad Isai, dicens : Mitte ad me David filium tuum, qui est in pascuis.
- Now: Saul sent messengers to Jesse, saying, “Send me your son David, who is with the flocks.”
- Proposed: Saul sent messengers to Jesse, saying, “Send me your son David, who is in the pastures.”
- Why: Latin 'qui est in pascuis' = 'who is in the pastures'; 'with the flocks' renders the Hebrew ('with the sheep'), not the Vulgate.

**1 Samuel 17:53** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Et revertentes filii Israël postquam persecuti fuerant Philisthæos, invaserunt castra eorum.
- Now: The Israelites returned from pursuing the Philistines and plundered their camp.
- Proposed: The Israelites returned from pursuing the Philistines and fell upon their camp.
- Why: Latin 'invaserunt castra eorum' = 'fell upon/invaded their camp'; 'plundered' renders the Hebrew ('spoiled their tents'), not 'invaserunt'.

**1 Samuel 23:19** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Ascenderunt autem Ziphæi ad Saul in Gabaa, dicentes : Nonne ecce David latitat apud nos in locis tutissimis silvæ, in colle Hachila, quæ est ad dexteram deserti ?
- Now: The Ziphites went up to Saul at Gibeah, saying, “Is David not hiding among us in the secure places of the forest, on the hill of Hachilah, south of the wilderness?
- Proposed: The Ziphites went up to Saul at Gibeah, saying, “Is David not hiding among us in the secure places of the forest, on the hill of Hachilah, on the right of the wilderness?
- Why: Latin 'ad dexteram deserti' = 'on the right hand of the wilderness'; 'south of' is the modern rendering of the Hebrew idiom, not what the Latin says.

**1 Samuel 23:24** · other-text · minor

- Latin: At illi surgentes abierunt in Ziph ante Saul : David autem et viri ejus erant in deserto Maon, in campestribus ad dexteram Jesimon.
- Now: They rose and went to Ziph ahead of Saul. David and his men were in the wilderness of Maon, in the plains south of Jeshimon.
- Proposed: They rose and went to Ziph ahead of Saul. David and his men were in the wilderness of Maon, in the plains on the right of Jeshimon.
- Why: Latin 'ad dexteram Jesimon' = 'on the right hand of Jeshimon'; 'south of' interprets the Hebrew idiom rather than rendering the Latin.

## English and Latin cover different amounts of text (3)

- **1 Samuel 20:21 (card shown on 2 rows, to 1 Samuel 20:22)**: Latin shown stops at 'affer mihi sagittas' (first sentence of v.21); English continues through all of Vg 20:22 ('Si dixero puero: Ecce sagittæ intra te sunt … quia dimisit te Dominus'), whose Latin is not displayed.
- **1 Samuel 20:42**: English ends with 'David rose and went away, while Jonathan went into the city', i.e. Vg 20:43 ('Et surrexit David, et abiit: sed et Jonathas ingressus est civitatem'), absent from the Latin shown, which ends at 'usque in sempiternum'.
- **1 Samuel 21:1 (Vulgate 20:42)**: Latin shown is Vg 20:42 only (same card as the 20:42 row); English adds 'David rose and went away, while Jonathan went into the city' = Vg 20:43 'Et surrexit David, et abiit: sed et Jonathas ingressus est civitatem', not displayed.
