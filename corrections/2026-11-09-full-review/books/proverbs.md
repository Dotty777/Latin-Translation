## Proposed English corrections (4)

**Proverbs 1:23** · other-text · minor

- Latin: convertimini ad correptionem meam. En proferam vobis spiritum meum, et ostendam vobis verba mea.
- Now: Turn at my correction. Look, I will pour out my spirit for you and make my words known to you.
- Proposed: Turn at my correction. Look, I will bring forth my spirit for you and make my words known to you.
- Why: Latin "proferam vobis spiritum meum" = I will bring forth/utter my spirit to you (cf. "profert verba sua" in 1:21). "pour out" is the Hebrew/KJV wording, not a sense of proferre.

**Proverbs 4:22** · other-text · minor

- Latin: vita enim sunt invenientibus ea, et universæ carni sanitas.
- Now: for they are life to those who find them and health to all their flesh.
- Proposed: for they are life to those who find them and health to all flesh.
- Why: Latin "universæ carni sanitas" has no possessive: "health to all flesh". "their flesh" follows the Hebrew/KJV wording, not the Latin.

**Proverbs 10:14** · other-text · minor

- Latin: Sapientes abscondunt scientiam ; os autem stulti confusioni proximum est.
- Now: The wise store up knowledge, but the mouth of a fool is close to ruin.
- Proposed: The wise store up knowledge, but the mouth of a fool is close to shame.
- Why: Latin "confusioni proximum est" = next to confusion/shame (cf. "filius confusionis" rendered "son of shame" at 10:5). "ruin" follows the Hebrew, not the Latin.

**Proverbs 20:29** · meaning · minor

- Latin: Exsultatio juvenum fortitudo eorum, et dignitas senum canities.
- Now: The pride of the young is their strength, and the dignity of the old is gray hair.
- Proposed: The joy of the young is their strength, and the dignity of the old is gray hair.
- Why: Latin "Exsultatio juvenum" = the exultation/joy of the young; "pride" reflects the Hebrew/modern-version "glory" rather than exsultatio.
