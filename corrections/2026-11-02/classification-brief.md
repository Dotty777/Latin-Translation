# Classifying Latin–Greek comparison cards (Saints.Bible, Genesis–Deuteronomy)

Each card compares one Latin reading (the Clementine Vulgate, with the site's English rendering of it) with the Greek reading (the site's Saints Translation English of the Septuagint) at the same place. The card is labelled "Assessment open"; your job is to assign the textual category and write the one-paragraph "Direct word comparison and meaning" note, judging what the LATIN says against what the GREEK says.

Categories (the site's own definitions; choose exactly one):
- Same: the readings correspond in meaning; differences are only English wording/register (begat/fathered, the Lord/YHWH-style conventions, word order).
- Similar: a modest difference of expression or emphasis (synonym with a slightly different nuance, singular/plural, a pronoun vs a name, a small particle).
- Different: a meaningful detail differs (a different image, verb, object, number, name, place, or condition) while the passage is otherwise parallel.
- Divergent: material is added, omitted or rearranged (a clause, phrase or sentence present in one and not the other; a verse split or merged differently so that content is missing on one side).
- Disparate: a substantial difference in meaning or consequence (contradictory facts, a different legal outcome, a different subject doing the action).

Notes: 1–2 sentences, at most about 35 words, plain text, no HTML. Name the specific difference in the site's style, e.g. "Created / made differs; both speak of God bringing heaven and earth into being." or "Latin dates completion to the seventh day; this Greek reading dates completion to the sixth." For Same, say what agrees in a few words (e.g. "Both give the same instruction; command / charge is only English wording."). You may cite a Latin word in plain text (e.g. Latin "ipsa"). Do not cite Greek words unless you are certain. Do not speculate about origins. Base the judgment on the Latin text where its English rendering is ambiguous.

Where the card's Latin and Greek cover visibly different amounts of text because of verse division, judge what each card shows and use Divergent only if content is genuinely missing on one side.
