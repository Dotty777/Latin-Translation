2 SAMUEL 1

1:1  LA: Factum est autem, postquam mortuus est Saul, ut David reverteretur a cæde Amalec, et maneret in Siceleg duos dies.
     EN: After Saul died, David returned from the slaughter of Amalek and stayed in Ziklag for two days.

1:2  LA: In die autem tertia apparuit homo veniens de castris Saul veste conscissa, et pulvere conspersus caput : et ut venit ad David, cecidit super faciem suam, et adoravit.
     EN: On the third day a man appeared, coming from Saul's camp with his clothes torn and dust scattered on his head. When he came to David, he fell on his face and bowed down.

1:3  LA: Dixitque ad eum David : Unde venis ? Qui ait ad eum : De castris Israël fugi.
     EN: David said to him, “Where have you come from?” He answered him, “I have escaped from Israel's camp.”

1:4  LA: Et dixit ad eum David : Quod est verbum quod factum est ? indica mihi. Qui ait : Fugit populus ex prælio, et multi corruentes e populo mortui sunt : sed et Saul et Jonathas filius ejus interierunt.
     EN: David said to him, “What has happened? Tell me.” He said, “The people fled from the battle, and many of the people fell and died. Saul and his son Jonathan have also perished.”

1:5  LA: Dixitque David ad adolescentem qui nuntiabat ei : Unde scis quia mortuus est Saul, et Jonathas filius ejus ?
     EN: David said to the young man bringing him the news, “How do you know that Saul and his son Jonathan are dead?”

1:6  LA: Et ait adolescens qui nuntiabat ei : Casu veni in montem Gelboë, et Saul incumbebat super hastam suam : porro currus et equites appropinquabant ei,
     EN: The young man bringing him the news said, “I happened to come to Mount Gilboa, and Saul was leaning on his spear. Chariots and horsemen were approaching him.

1:7  LA: et conversus post tergum suum, vidensque me, vocavit. Cui cum respondissem : Adsum :
     EN: He turned around, saw me, and called. When I answered, ‘Here I am,’

1:8  LA: dixit mihi : Quisnam es tu ? Et aio ad eum : Amalecites ego sum.
     EN: he said to me, ‘Who are you?’ I told him, ‘I am an Amalekite.’

1:9  LA: Et locutus est mihi : Sta super me, et interfice me : quoniam tenent me angustiæ, et adhuc tota anima mea in me est.
     EN: He said to me, ‘Stand over me and kill me, for anguish grips me, and all my life is still within me.’

1:10  LA: Stansque super eum, occidi illum : sciebam enim quod vivere non poterat post ruinam : et tuli diadema quod erat in capite ejus, et armillam de brachio illius, et attuli ad te dominum meum huc.
      EN: I stood over him and killed him, for I knew he could not live after his fall. I took the crown from his head and the bracelet from his arm and brought them here to you, my lord.”

1:11  LA: Apprehendens autem David, vestimenta sua scidit, omnesque viri qui erant cum eo,
      EN: David took hold of his clothes and tore them, and so did all the men with him.

1:12  LA: et planxerunt, et fleverunt, et jejunaverunt usque ad vesperam super Saul, et super Jonathan filium ejus, et super populum Domini, et super domum Israël, eo quod corruissent gladio.
      EN: They mourned, wept, and fasted until evening for Saul, for his son Jonathan, for the Lord's people, and for the house of Israel, because they had fallen by the sword.

1:13  LA: Dixitque David ad juvenem qui nuntiaverat ei : Unde es tu ? Qui respondit : Filius hominis advenæ Amalecitæ ego sum.
      EN: David said to the young man who had brought him the news, “Where are you from?” He answered, “I am the son of an Amalekite resident foreigner.”

1:14  LA: Et ait ad eum David : Quare non timuisti mittere manum tuam ut occideres christum Domini ?
      EN: David said to him, “Why were you not afraid to stretch out your hand to kill the Lord's anointed?”

1:15  LA: Vocansque David unum de pueris suis, ait : Accedens irrue in eum. Qui percussit illum, et mortuus est.
      EN: David called one of his servants and said, “Go and strike him down.” He struck him, and he died.

1:16  LA: Et ait ad eum David : Sanguis tuus super caput tuum : os enim tuum locutum est adversum te, dicens : Ego interfeci christum Domini.
      EN: David said to him, “Your blood is on your own head, for your mouth has testified against you, saying, ‘I killed the Lord's anointed.’”

1:17  LA: Planxit autem David planctum hujuscemodi super Saul, et super Jonathan filium ejus
      EN: David lamented with this lament over Saul and his son Jonathan

1:18  LA: (et præcepit ut docerent filios Juda arcum, sicut scriptum est in libro justorum), et ait : [Considera, Israël, pro his qui mortui sunt, super excelsa tua vulnerati.
      EN: and commanded that the children of Judah be taught the bow, as it is written in the Book of the Upright. He said, “Consider, Israel, those who died, wounded upon your heights.

1:19  LA: Inclyti Israël super montes tuos interfecti sunt : quomodo ceciderunt fortes ?
      EN: The renowned of Israel have been killed upon your mountains. How have the mighty fallen!

1:20  LA: Nolite annuntiare in Geth, neque annuntietis in compitis Ascalonis : ne forte lætentur filiæ Philisthiim ; ne exultent filiæ incircumcisorum.]
      EN: Do not announce it in Gath or proclaim it in the streets of Ashkelon, lest the daughters of the Philistines rejoice, lest the daughters of the uncircumcised exult.

1:21  LA: [Montes Gelboë, nec ros, nec pluvia veniant super vos, neque sint agri primitiarum : quia ibi abjectus est clypeus fortium : clypeus Saul, quasi non esset unctus oleo.]
      EN: Mountains of Gilboa, may neither dew nor rain come upon you, and may there be no fields of firstfruits! For there the shield of the mighty was cast away, Saul's shield, as though it had not been anointed with oil.

1:22  LA: [A sanguine interfectorum, ab adipe fortium, sagitta Jonathæ numquam rediit retrorsum, et gladius Saul non est reversus inanis.
      EN: From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty, Jonathan's arrow never turned back, and Saul's sword did not return empty.

1:23  LA: Saul et Jonathas amabiles, et decori in vita sua, in morte quoque non sunt divisi : aquilis velociores, leonibus fortiores.]
      EN: Saul and Jonathan, lovable and fair in their lives, were not divided even in death: swifter than eagles, stronger than lions.

1:24  LA: [Filiæ Israël, super Saul flete, qui vestiebat vos coccino in deliciis, qui præbebat ornamenta aurea cultui vestro.]
      EN: Daughters of Israel, weep over Saul, who clothed you luxuriously in scarlet, who provided golden ornaments for your attire.

1:25  LA: [Quomodo ceciderunt fortes in prælio ? Jonathas in excelsis tuis occisus est ?
      EN: How have the mighty fallen in battle! Has Jonathan been killed upon your heights?

1:26  LA: Doleo super te, frater mi Jonatha, decore nimis, et amabilis super amorem mulierum. Sicut mater unicum amat filium suum, ita ego te diligebam.
      EN: I grieve over you, my brother Jonathan, exceedingly fair and lovable beyond the love of women. As a mother loves her only son, so I loved you.

1:27  LA: Quomodo ceciderunt robusti, et perierunt arma bellica ?]
      EN: How have the strong fallen, and the weapons of war perished!”

2 SAMUEL 2

2:1  LA: Igitur post hæc consuluit David Dominum, dicens : Num ascendam in unam de civitatibus Juda ? Et ait Dominus ad eum : Ascende. Dixitque David : Quo ascendam ? Et respondit ei : In Hebron.
     EN: After this David consulted the Lord, saying, “Shall I go up to one of the cities of Judah?” The Lord said to him, “Go up.” David said, “Where shall I go up?” He answered him, “To Hebron.”

2:2  LA: Ascendit ergo David, et duæ uxores ejus, Achinoam Jezraëlites, et Abigail uxor Nabal Carmeli :
     EN: David went up, with his two wives, Ahinoam of Jezreel and Abigail, the wife of Nabal of Carmel.

2:3  LA: sed et viros, qui erant cum eo, duxit David singulos cum domo sua : et manserunt in oppidis Hebron.
     EN: David also brought the men who were with him, each with his household, and they lived in the towns of Hebron.

2:4  LA: Veneruntque viri Juda, et unxerunt ibi David ut regnaret super domum Juda. Et nuntiatum est David quod viri Jabes Galaad sepelissent Saul.
     EN: The men of Judah came and anointed David there to reign over the house of Judah. David was told that the men of Jabesh-gilead had buried Saul.

2:5  LA: Misit ergo David nuntios ad viros Jabes Galaad, dixitque ad eos : Benedicti vos Domino, qui fecistis misericordiam hanc cum domino vestro Saul, et sepelistis eum.
     EN: David sent messengers to the men of Jabesh-gilead and said to them, “May you be blessed by the Lord, for you have shown this mercy to your lord Saul and buried him.

2:6  LA: Et nunc retribuet vobis quidem Dominus misericordiam et veritatem : sed et ego reddam gratiam, eo quod fecistis verbum istud.
     EN: Now the Lord will indeed repay you with mercy and faithfulness. I too will repay your kindness, because you have done this.

2:7  LA: Confortentur manus vestræ, et estote filii fortitudinis : licet enim mortuus sit dominus vester Saul, tamen me unxit domus Juda in regem sibi.
     EN: Let your hands be strengthened, and be valiant men. Though your lord Saul is dead, the house of Judah has anointed me as its king.”

2:8  LA: Abner autem filius Ner, princeps exercitus Saul, tulit Isboseth filium Saul, et circumduxit eum per castra,
     EN: But Abner son of Ner, the commander of Saul's army, took Saul's son Ish-bosheth and led him around the camp,

2:9  LA: regemque constituit super Galaad, et super Gessuri, et super Jezraël, et super Ephraim, et super Benjamin, et super Israël universum.
     EN: and made him king over Gilead, the Geshurites, Jezreel, Ephraim, Benjamin, and all Israel.

2:10  LA: Quadraginta annorum erat Isboseth filius Saul cum regnare cœpisset super Israël, et duobus annis regnavit : sola autem domus Juda sequebatur David.
      EN: Ish-bosheth son of Saul was forty years old when he began to reign over Israel, and he reigned for two years. Only the house of Judah followed David.

2:11  LA: Et fuit numerus dierum quos commoratus est David imperans in Hebron super domum Juda, septem annorum et sex mensium.
      EN: The time David spent reigning in Hebron over the house of Judah was seven years and six months.

2:12  LA: Egressusque est Abner filius Ner, et pueri Isboseth filii Saul, de castris in Gabaon.
      EN: Abner son of Ner and the servants of Ish-bosheth son of Saul went out from the camp to Gibeon.

2:13  LA: Porro Joab filius Sarviæ, et pueri David, egressi sunt, et occurrerunt eis juxta piscinam Gabaon. Et cum in unum convenissent, e regione sederunt : hi ex una parte piscinæ, et illi ex altera.
      EN: Joab son of Zeruiah and David's servants also went out and met them beside the pool of Gibeon. When they came together, they sat opposite each other, one group on one side of the pool and the other on the other side.

2:14  LA: Dixitque Abner ad Joab : Surgant pueri, et ludant coram nobis. Et respondit Joab : Surgant.
      EN: Abner said to Joab, “Let the young men arise and compete before us.” Joab answered, “Let them arise.”

2:15  LA: Surrexerunt ergo, et transierunt numero duodecim de Benjamin, ex parte Isboseth filii Saul, et duodecim de pueris David.
      EN: They arose and crossed over, twelve men of Benjamin on the side of Ish-bosheth son of Saul, and twelve of David's servants.

2:16  LA: Apprehensoque unusquisque capite comparis sui, defixit gladium in latus contrarii, et ceciderunt simul : vocatumque est nomen loci illius : Ager robustorum, in Gabaon.
      EN: Each seized his opponent's head and thrust his sword into his adversary's side, and they fell together. That place in Gibeon was called Field of the Strong.

2:17  LA: Et ortum est bellum durum satis in die illa : fugatusque est Abner et viri Israël a pueris David.
      EN: A very fierce battle arose that day, and Abner and the men of Israel were put to flight by David's servants.

2:18  LA: Erant autem ibi tres filii Sarviæ, Joab, et Abisai, et Asaël : porro Asaël cursor velocissimus fuit, quasi unus de capreis quæ morantur in silvis.
      EN: Zeruiah's three sons, Joab, Abishai, and Asahel, were there. Asahel was an exceedingly swift runner, like one of the gazelles living in the woods.

2:19  LA: Persequebatur autem Asaël Abner, et non declinavit ad dextram neque ad sinistram omittens persequi Abner.
      EN: Asahel pursued Abner and did not turn to the right or left to give up pursuing him.

2:20  LA: Respexit itaque Abner post tergum suum, et ait : Tune es Asaël ? Qui respondit : Ego sum.
      EN: Abner looked behind him and said, “Is that you, Asahel?” He answered, “It is.”

2:21  LA: Dixitque ei Abner : Vade ad dexteram, sive ad sinistram, et apprehende unum de adolescentibus, et tolle tibi spolia ejus. Noluit autem Asaël omittere quin urgeret eum.
      EN: Abner said to him, “Turn to your right or left, seize one of the young men, and take his armor for yourself.” But Asahel was unwilling to stop pressing him.

2:22  LA: Rursumque locutus est Abner ad Asaël : Recede, noli me sequi, ne compellar confodere te in terram, et levare non potero faciem meam ad Joab fratrem tuum.
      EN: Abner spoke to Asahel again: “Withdraw; do not follow me, lest I be compelled to strike you to the ground, and then I could not lift my face to your brother Joab.”

2:23  LA: Qui audire contempsit, et noluit declinare : percussit ergo eum Abner aversa hasta in inguine, et transfodit, et mortuus est in eodem loco : omnesque qui transibant per locum illum, in quo ceciderat Asaël et mortuus erat, subsistebant.
      EN: He refused to listen and would not turn aside. So Abner struck him in the groin with the rear end of his spear and pierced him through. He died in that place, and everyone passing the place where Asahel had fallen and died stopped there.

2:24  LA: Persequentibus autem Joab et Abisai fugientem Abner, sol occubuit : et venerunt usque ad collem aquæductus, qui est ex adverso vallis itineris deserti in Gabaon.
      EN: As Joab and Abishai pursued the fleeing Abner, the sun set. They came as far as the hill of the aqueduct, opposite the valley of the wilderness road in Gibeon.

2:25  LA: Congregatique sunt filii Benjamin ad Abner : et conglobati in unum cuneum, steterunt in summitate tumuli unius.
      EN: The children of Benjamin gathered around Abner, massed into one company, and stood on the summit of a hill.

2:26  LA: Et exclamavit Abner ad Joab, et ait : Num usque ad internecionem tuus mucro desæviet ? an ignoras quod periculosa sit desperatio ? usquequo non dicis populo ut omittat persequi fratres suos ?
      EN: Abner cried to Joab and said, “Will your blade rage until utter destruction? Do you not know that desperation is dangerous? How long before you tell the people to stop pursuing their brothers?”

2:27  LA: Et ait Joab : Vivit Dominus, si locutus fuisses, mane recessisset populus persequens fratrem suum.
      EN: Joab said, “As the Lord lives, if you had spoken, the people pursuing their brothers would have withdrawn in the morning.”

2:28  LA: Insonuit ergo Joab buccina, et stetit omnis exercitus, nec persecuti sunt ultra Israël, neque iniere certamen.
      EN: Joab sounded the trumpet, and the whole army halted. They no longer pursued Israel or entered battle.

2:29  LA: Abner autem et viri ejus abierunt per campestria, tota nocte illa : et transierunt Jordanem, et lustrata omni Beth-horon, venerunt ad castra.
      EN: Abner and his men traveled through the plains all that night, crossed the Jordan, traversed all Beth-horon, and came to the camp.

2:30  LA: Porro Joab reversus, omisso Abner, congregavit omnem populum : et defuerunt de pueris David decem et novem viri, excepto Asaële.
      EN: Joab returned from pursuing Abner and gathered all the people. Nineteen of David's servants were missing, besides Asahel.

2:31  LA: Servi autem David percusserunt de Benjamin, et de viris qui erant cum Abner, trecentos sexaginta, qui et mortui sunt.
      EN: David's servants had struck down three hundred and sixty men of Benjamin and those who were with Abner, and they died.

2:32  LA: Tuleruntque Asaël, et sepelierunt eum in sepulchro patris sui in Bethlehem : et ambulaverunt tota nocte Joab et viri qui erant cum eo, et in ipso crepusculo pervenerunt in Hebron.
      EN: They took Asahel and buried him in his father's tomb at Bethlehem. Joab and the men with him traveled all night and reached Hebron at dawn.

2 SAMUEL 3

3:1  LA: Facta est ergo longa concertatio inter domum Saul et inter domum David : David proficiscens, et semper seipso robustior, domus autem Saul decrescens quotidie.
     EN: There was a long conflict between Saul's house and David's house. David advanced and continually grew stronger, while Saul's house declined day by day.

3:2  LA: Natique sunt filii David in Hebron : fuitque primogenitus ejus Amnon, de Achinoam Jezraëlitide.
     EN: Sons were born to David in Hebron. His firstborn was Amnon, by Ahinoam of Jezreel.

3:3  LA: Et post eum Cheleab, de Abigail uxore Nabal Carmeli : porro tertius Absalom, filius Maacha filiæ Tholmai regis Gessur.
     EN: After him came Chileab, by Abigail, the wife of Nabal of Carmel. The third was Absalom, son of Maacah, the daughter of Talmai king of Geshur.

3:4  LA: Quartus autem Adonias, filius Haggith : et quintus Saphathia, filius Abital.
     EN: The fourth was Adonijah, son of Haggith, and the fifth Shephatiah, son of Abital.

3:5  LA: Sextus quoque Jethraam, de Egla uxore David : hi nati sunt David in Hebron.
     EN: The sixth was Ithream, by David's wife Eglah. These were born to David in Hebron.

3:6  LA: Cum ergo esset prælium inter domum Saul et domum David, Abner filius Ner regebat domum Saul.
     EN: During the war between Saul's house and David's house, Abner son of Ner governed Saul's house.

3:7  LA: Fuerat autem Sauli concubina nomine Respha, filia Aja. Dixitque Isboseth ad Abner :
     EN: Saul had had a concubine named Rizpah, daughter of Aiah. Ish-bosheth said to Abner,

3:8  LA: Quare ingressus es ad concubinam patris mei ? Qui iratus nimis propter verba Isboseth, ait : Numquid caput canis ego sum adversum Judam hodie, qui fecerim misericordiam super domum Saul patris tui, et super fratres et proximos ejus, et non tradidi te in manus David, et tu requisisti in me quod argueres pro muliere hodie ?
     EN: “Why have you gone in to my father's concubine?” Extremely angry at Ish-bosheth's words, he said, “Am I a dog's head against Judah today? I have shown mercy to the house of your father Saul, to his brothers and relatives, and have not delivered you into David's hands, yet today you have sought a charge against me concerning a woman!

3:9  LA: Hæc faciat Deus Abner, et hæc addat ei, nisi quomodo juravit Dominus David, sic faciam cum eo,
     EN: May God do these things to Abner and add still more, unless I deal with David as the Lord swore to him,

3:10  LA: ut transferatur regnum de domo Saul, et elevetur thronus David super Israël et super Judam, a Dan usque Bersabee.
      EN: transferring the kingdom from Saul's house and establishing David's throne over Israel and Judah, from Dan to Beersheba.”

3:11  LA: Et non potuit respondere ei quidquam, quia metuebat illum.
      EN: Ish-bosheth could not answer him a word, because he feared him.

3:12  LA: Misit ergo Abner nuntios ad David pro se dicentes : Cujus est terra ? et ut loquerentur : Fac mecum amicitias, et erit manus mea tecum, et reducam ad te universum Israël.
      EN: Abner sent messengers to David on his behalf, saying, “Whose is the land?” He also instructed them to say, “Make an alliance with me, and my hand will be with you, and I will bring all Israel back to you.”

3:13  LA: Qui ait : Optime : ego faciam tecum amicitias : sed unam rem peto a te, dicens : Non videbis faciem meam antequam adduxeris Michol filiam Saul : et sic venies, et videbis me.
      EN: He said, “Very well. I will make an alliance with you, but I ask one thing of you: you will not see my face before you bring Michal, Saul's daughter. Then you may come and see me.”

3:14  LA: Misit autem David nuntios ad Isboseth filium Saul, dicens : Redde uxorem meam Michol, quam despondi mihi centum præputiis Philisthiim.
      EN: David sent messengers to Ish-bosheth son of Saul, saying, “Restore my wife Michal, whom I betrothed to myself with a hundred Philistine foreskins.”

3:15  LA: Misit ergo Isboseth, et tulit eam a viro suo Phaltiel filio Lais.
      EN: Ish-bosheth sent and took her from her husband Paltiel son of Laish.

3:16  LA: Sequebaturque eam vir suus, plorans usque Bahurim : et dixit ad eum Abner : Vade, et revertere. Qui reversus est.
      EN: Her husband followed her, weeping, as far as Bahurim. Abner said to him, “Go, return.” And he returned.

3:17  LA: Sermonem quoque intulit Abner ad seniores Israël, dicens : Tam heri quam nudiustertius quærebatis David ut regnaret super vos.
      EN: Abner also spoke to the elders of Israel, saying, “For some time you have wanted David to reign over you.

3:18  LA: Nunc ergo facite : quoniam Dominus locutus est ad David, dicens : In manu servi mei David salvabo populum meum Israël de manu Philisthiim, et omnium inimicorum ejus.
      EN: Now do it, for the Lord has spoken to David, saying, ‘By the hand of My servant David I will save My people Israel from the hand of the Philistines and all their enemies.’”

3:19  LA: Locutus est autem Abner etiam ad Benjamin. Et abiit ut loqueretur ad David in Hebron omnia quæ placuerant Israëli et universo Benjamin.
      EN: Abner also spoke to Benjamin. He went to tell David in Hebron everything that had pleased Israel and all Benjamin.

3:20  LA: Venitque ad David in Hebron cum viginti viris : et fecit David Abner, et viris ejus qui venerant cum eo, convivium.
      EN: He came to David in Hebron with twenty men, and David made a feast for Abner and the men who had come with him.

3:21  LA: Et dixit Abner ad David : Surgam, ut congregem ad te dominum meum regem omnem Israël, et ineam tecum fœdus, et imperes omnibus, sicut desiderat anima tua. Cum ergo deduxisset David Abner, et ille isset in pace,
      EN: Abner said to David, “I will arise and gather all Israel to you, my lord the king, and enter into a covenant with you, so that you may rule over all, as your soul desires.” David then escorted Abner away, and he went in peace.

3:22  LA: statim pueri David et Joab venerunt, cæsis latronibus, cum præda magna nimis : Abner autem non erat cum David in Hebron, quia jam dimiserat eum, et profectus fuerat in pace.
      EN: Just then David's servants and Joab returned from striking down raiders, bringing very great plunder. Abner was no longer with David in Hebron, because David had already dismissed him and he had departed in peace.

3:23  LA: Et Joab, et omnis exercitus qui erat cum eo, postea venerunt : nuntiatum est itaque Joab a narrantibus : Venit Abner filius Ner ad regem, et dimisit eum, et abiit in pace.
      EN: Joab and the whole army with him came afterward. Those reporting the news told Joab, “Abner son of Ner came to the king, who dismissed him, and he went in peace.”

3:24  LA: Et ingressus est Joab ad regem, et ait : Quid fecisti ? Ecce venit Abner ad te : quare dimisisti eum, et abiit et recessit ?
      EN: Joab entered before the king and said, “What have you done? Abner came to you. Why have you dismissed him, so that he has gone away and departed?

3:25  LA: ignoras Abner filium Ner, quoniam ad hoc venit ad te ut deciperet te, et sciret exitum tuum et introitum tuum, et nosset omnia quæ agis ?
      EN: Do you not know Abner son of Ner? He came to deceive you, to learn your going out and coming in, and to discover everything you do.”

3:26  LA: Egressus itaque Joab a David, misit nuntios post Abner, et reduxit eum a cisterna Sira, ignorante David.
      EN: Joab went out from David and sent messengers after Abner. He brought him back from the cistern of Sirah, without David's knowledge.

3:27  LA: Cumque rediisset Abner in Hebron, seorsum adduxit eum Joab ad medium portæ ut loqueretur ei, in dolo : et percussit illum ibi in inguine, et mortuus est in ultionem sanguinis Asaël fratris ejus.
      EN: When Abner returned to Hebron, Joab took him aside into the middle of the gate, pretending to speak to him privately. There he struck him in the groin, and he died, in vengeance for the blood of Joab's brother Asahel.

3:28  LA: Quod cum audisset David rem jam gestam, ait : Mundus ego sum, et regnum meum apud Dominum usque in sempiternum, a sanguine Abner filii Ner :
      EN: When David heard of the deed already done, he said, “I and my kingdom are innocent before the Lord forever of the blood of Abner son of Ner.

3:29  LA: et veniat super caput Joab, et super omnem domum patris ejus : nec deficiat de domo Joab fluxum seminis sustinens, et leprosus, et tenens fusum, et cadens gladio, et indigens pane.
      EN: May it fall upon Joab's head and all his father's house. May Joab's house never lack someone suffering a discharge of semen, someone with leprosy, someone holding a spindle, someone falling by the sword, or someone lacking bread.”

      EN: Joab and his brother Abishai killed Abner because he had killed their brother Asahel in battle at Gibeon.

      EN: David said to Joab and all the people with him, “Tear your clothes, gird yourselves with sackcloth, and mourn before Abner's funeral procession.” King David followed the bier.

3:32  LA: Cumque sepelissent Abner in Hebron, levavit rex David vocem suam, et flevit super tumulum Abner : flevit autem et omnis populus.
      EN: When they had buried Abner in Hebron, King David raised his voice and wept over Abner's tomb, and all the people also wept.

3:33  LA: Plangensque rex, et lugens Abner, ait : [Nequaquam ut mori solent ignavi, mortuus est Abner.
      EN: The king lamented and mourned Abner, saying, “Abner has by no means died as cowards usually die.

3:34  LA: Manus tuæ ligatæ non sunt, et pedes tui non sunt compedibus aggravati : sed sicut solent cadere coram filiis iniquitatis, sic corruisti.] Congeminansque omnis populus flevit super eum.
      EN: Your hands were not bound, nor your feet weighed down with fetters. But as people fall before the sons of wickedness, so you have fallen.” All the people redoubled their weeping over him.

3:35  LA: Cumque venisset universa multitudo cibum capere cum David, clara adhuc die juravit David, dicens : Hæc faciat mihi Deus, et hæc addat, si ante occasum solis gustavero panem vel aliud quidquam.
      EN: When the entire multitude came to eat with David while it was still daylight, David swore, saying, “May God do these things to me and add still more, if I taste bread or anything else before sunset.”

3:36  LA: Omnisque populus audivit, et placuerunt eis cuncta quæ fecit rex in conspectu totius populi.
      EN: All the people heard, and everything the king did in the sight of all the people pleased them.

3:37  LA: Et cognovit omne vulgus et universus Israël in die illa, quoniam non actum fuisset a rege ut occideretur Abner filius Ner.
      EN: All the populace and all Israel knew that day that the king had not brought about the killing of Abner son of Ner.

3:38  LA: Dixit quoque rex ad servos suos : Num ignoratis quoniam princeps et maximus cecidit hodie in Israël ?
      EN: The king also said to his servants, “Do you not know that a prince and a very great man has fallen today in Israel?

3:39  LA: Ego autem adhuc delicatus, et unctus rex : porro viri isti filii Sarviæ duri sunt mihi : retribuat Dominus facienti malum juxta malitiam suam.
      EN: I am still weak, though anointed king, and these men, Zeruiah's sons, are too harsh for me. May the Lord repay the evildoer according to his wickedness.”

2 SAMUEL 4

4:1  LA: Audivit autem Isboseth filius Saul quod cecidisset Abner in Hebron : et dissolutæ sunt manus ejus, omnisque Israël perturbatus est.
     EN: Ish-bosheth son of Saul heard that Abner had fallen in Hebron. His hands grew weak, and all Israel was disturbed.

4:2  LA: Duo autem viri principes latronum erant filio Saul, nomen uni Baana, et nomen alteri Rechab, filii Remmon Berothitæ de filiis Benjamin : siquidem et Beroth reputata est in Benjamin.
     EN: Saul's son had two men who led raiding bands, one named Baanah and the other Rechab, sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, from the children of Benjamin, for Beeroth too was reckoned to Benjamin.

4:3  LA: Et fugerunt Berothitæ in Gethaim, fueruntque ibi advenæ usque ad tempus illud.
     EN: The Beerothites had fled to Gittaim and lived there as resident foreigners until that time.

4:4  LA: Erat autem Jonathæ filio Saul filius debilis pedibus : quinquennis enim fuit, quando venit nuntius de Saul et Jonatha ex Jezrahel. Tollens itaque eum nutrix sua, fugit : cumque festinaret ut fugeret, cecidit, et claudus effectus est : habuitque vocabulum Miphiboseth.
     EN: Jonathan son of Saul had a son disabled in his feet. He was five years old when the news concerning Saul and Jonathan came from Jezreel. His nurse picked him up and fled, but as she hurried to escape, he fell and became lame. His name was Mephibosheth.

4:5  LA: Venientes igitur filii Remmon Berothitæ, Rechab et Baana, ingressi sunt fervente die domum Isboseth : qui dormiebat super stratum suum meridie. Et ostiaria domus purgans triticum, obdormivit.
     EN: The sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, Rechab and Baanah, came in the heat of the day and entered Ish-bosheth's house. He was sleeping on his bed at midday, and the woman guarding the door had fallen asleep while cleaning wheat.

4:6  LA: Ingressi sunt autem domum latenter assumentes spicas tritici, et percusserunt eum in inguine Rechab, et Baana frater ejus, et fugerunt.
     EN: Rechab and his brother Baanah entered the house secretly, taking ears of wheat, struck him in the groin, and fled.

4:7  LA: Cum autem ingressi fuissent domum, ille dormiebat super lectum suum in conclavi, et percutientes interfecerunt eum : sublatoque capite ejus, abierunt per viam deserti tota nocte,
     EN: When they entered the house, he was sleeping on his bed in the chamber. They struck and killed him, took his head, and traveled by the wilderness road all night.

4:8  LA: et attulerunt caput Isboseth ad David in Hebron : dixeruntque ad regem : Ecce caput Isboseth filii Saul inimici tui, qui quærebat animam tuam : et dedit Dominus domino meo regi ultionem hodie de Saul, et de semine ejus.
     EN: They brought Ish-bosheth's head to David at Hebron and said to the king, “Here is the head of Ish-bosheth son of Saul, your enemy who sought your life. Today the Lord has given my lord the king vengeance on Saul and his offspring.”

4:9  LA: Respondens autem David Rechab, et Baana fratri ejus, filiis Remmon Berothitæ, dixit ad eos : Vivit Dominus, qui eruit animam meam de omni angustia,
     EN: David answered Rechab and his brother Baanah, the sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, saying, “As the Lord lives, who has rescued my life from every distress,

4:10  LA: quoniam eum qui annuntiaverat mihi, et dixerat : Mortuus est Saul : qui putabat se prospera nuntiare, tenui, et occidi eum in Siceleg, cui oportebat mercedem dare pro nuntio.
      EN: the man who brought me news, saying, ‘Saul is dead,’ thought he was bringing good news. I seized and killed him at Ziklag, though he ought to have been given a reward for his report.

4:11  LA: Quanto magis nunc cum homines impii interfecerunt virum innoxium in domo sua, super lectum suum, non quæram sanguinem ejus de manu vestra, et auferam vos de terra ?
      EN: How much more now, when wicked men have killed an innocent man in his own house, on his bed, shall I require his blood from your hands and remove you from the earth!”

4:12  LA: Præcepit itaque David pueris suis, et interfecerunt eos : præcidentesque manus et pedes eorum, suspenderunt eos super piscinam in Hebron : caput autem Isboseth tulerunt, et sepelierunt in sepulchro Abner in Hebron.
      EN: David commanded his servants, and they killed them. They cut off their hands and feet and hung them above the pool in Hebron. But they took Ish-bosheth's head and buried it in Abner's tomb in Hebron.

2 SAMUEL 5

5:1  LA: Et venerunt universæ tribus Israël ad David in Hebron, dicentes : Ecce nos os tuum et caro tua sumus.
     EN: All the tribes of Israel came to David at Hebron, saying, “We are your bone and your flesh.

5:2  LA: Sed et heri et nudiustertius cum esset Saul rex super nos, tu eras educens et reducens Israël : dixit autem Dominus ad te : Tu pasces populum meum Israël, et tu eris dux super Israël.
     EN: Even previously, when Saul was king over us, you were the one who led Israel out and brought them back. The Lord said to you, ‘You will shepherd My people Israel, and you will be ruler over Israel.’”

5:3  LA: Venerunt quoque et seniores Israël ad regem in Hebron, et percussit cum eis rex David fœdus in Hebron coram Domino : unxeruntque David in regem super Israël.
     EN: The elders of Israel also came to the king at Hebron. King David made a covenant with them at Hebron before the Lord, and they anointed David king over Israel.

5:4  LA: Filius triginta annorum erat David cum regnare cœpisset, et quadraginta annis regnavit.
     EN: David was thirty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned for forty years.

5:5  LA: In Hebron regnavit super Judam septem annis et sex mensibus : in Jerusalem autem regnavit triginta tribus annis super omnem Israël et Judam.
     EN: He reigned over Judah in Hebron for seven years and six months, and in Jerusalem he reigned over all Israel and Judah for thirty-three years.

5:6  LA: Et abiit rex, et omnes viri qui erant cum eo, in Jerusalem, ad Jebusæum habitatorem terræ : dictumque est David ab eis : Non ingredieris huc, nisi abstuleris cæcos et claudos dicentes : Non ingredietur David huc.
     EN: The king and all the men with him went to Jerusalem against the Jebusites who inhabited the land. They said to David, “You will not enter here unless you remove the blind and the lame who say, ‘David will not enter here.’”

5:7  LA: Cepit autem David arcem Sion : hæc est civitas David.
     EN: But David captured the stronghold of Zion, which is the City of David.

5:8  LA: Proposuerat enim David in die illa præmium, qui percussisset Jebusæum, et tetigisset domatum fistulas, et abstulisset cæcos et claudos odientes animam David. Idcirco dicitur in proverbio : Cæcus et claudus non intrabunt in templum.
     EN: That day David had offered a reward to whoever struck the Jebusites, reached the conduits of the roofs, and removed the blind and lame who hated David's life. Therefore it is said in a proverb, “The blind and the lame shall not enter the temple.”

5:9  LA: Habitavit autem David in arce, et vocavit eam civitatem David : et ædificavit per gyrum a Mello et intrinsecus.
     EN: David lived in the stronghold and called it the City of David. He built all around, from the Millo inward.

5:10  LA: Et ingrediebatur proficiens atque succrescens, et Dominus Deus exercituum erat cum eo.
      EN: He continued to advance and grow stronger, and the Lord God of hosts was with him.

5:11  LA: Misit quoque Hiram rex Tyri nuntios ad David, et ligna cedrina, et artifices lignorum, artificesque lapidum ad parietes : et ædificaverunt domum David.
      EN: Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, with cedar timber, carpenters, and stoneworkers for walls, and they built David a house.

5:12  LA: Et cognovit David quoniam confirmasset eum Dominus regem super Israël, et quoniam exaltasset regnum ejus super populum suum Israël.
      EN: David knew that the Lord had established him as king over Israel and had exalted his kingdom over His people Israel.

5:13  LA: Accepit ergo David adhuc concubinas et uxores de Jerusalem, postquam venerat de Hebron : natique sunt David et alii filii et filiæ :
      EN: After coming from Hebron, David took more concubines and wives from Jerusalem, and more sons and daughters were born to him.

5:14  LA: et hæc nomina eorum, qui nati sunt ei in Jerusalem : Samua, et Sobab, et Nathan, et Salomon,

2 SAMUEL 6

6:1  LA: Congregavit autem rursum David omnes electos ex Israël, triginta millia.
     EN: David again gathered all the chosen men of Israel, thirty thousand.

6:2  LA: Surrexitque David, et abiit, et universus populus qui erat cum eo de viris Juda, ut adducerent arcam Dei, super quam invocatum est nomen Domini exercituum, sedentis in cherubim super eam.
     EN: David arose and went with all the men of Judah who were with him to bring up the ark of God, over which the name of the Lord of hosts is invoked, who sits above it upon the cherubim.

6:3  LA: Et imposuerunt arcam Dei super plaustrum novum : tuleruntque eam de domo Abinadab, qui erat in Gabaa : Oza autem et Ahio, filii Abinadab, minabant plaustrum novum.
     EN: They placed the ark of God on a new cart and took it from Abinadab's house in Gibeah. Abinadab's sons Uzzah and Ahio drove the new cart.

6:4  LA: Cumque tulissent eam de domo Abinadab, qui erat in Gabaa, custodiens arcam Dei Ahio præcedebat arcam.
     EN: When they took it from Abinadab's house in Gibeah, Ahio went ahead of the ark, guarding the ark of God.

6:5  LA: David autem et omnis Israël ludebant coram Domino in omnibus lignis fabrefactis, et citharis et lyris et tympanis et sistris et cymbalis.
     EN: David and all Israel played before the Lord on all kinds of crafted wooden instruments, on harps, lyres, tambourines, rattles, and cymbals.

6:6  LA: Postquam autem venerunt ad aream Nachon, extendit Oza manum ad arcam Dei, et tenuit eam : quoniam calcitrabant boves, et declinaverunt eam.
     EN: When they reached Nacon's threshing floor, Uzzah stretched out his hand to the ark of God and held it, because the oxen were kicking and had tilted it.

6:7  LA: Iratusque est indignatione Dominus contra Ozam, et percussit eum super temeritate : qui mortuus est ibi juxta arcam Dei.
     EN: The Lord blazed with indignation against Uzzah and struck him for his rashness. He died there beside the ark of God.

6:8  LA: Contristatus est autem David, eo quod percussisset Dominus Ozam, et vocatum est nomen loci illius : Percussio Ozæ, usque in diem hanc.
     EN: David was distressed because the Lord had struck Uzzah. That place has been called the Striking of Uzzah to this day.

6:9  LA: Et extimuit David Dominum in die illa, dicens : Quomodo ingredietur ad me arca Domini ?
     EN: David feared the Lord that day, saying, “How can the ark of the Lord come to me?”

6:10  LA: Et noluit divertere ad se arcam Domini in civitatem David : sed divertit eam in domum Obededom Gethæi.
      EN: He was unwilling to bring the ark of the Lord to himself in the City of David, but diverted it to the house of Obed-edom the Gittite.

6:11  LA: Et habitavit arca Domini in domo Obededom Gethæi tribus mensibus : et benedixit Dominus Obededom, et omnem domum ejus.
      EN: The ark of the Lord remained in the house of Obed-edom the Gittite for three months, and the Lord blessed Obed-edom and his whole household.

6:12  LA: Nuntiatumque est regi David quod benedixisset Dominus Obededom, et omnia ejus, propter arcam Dei. Abiit ergo David, et adduxit arcam Dei de domo Obededom in civitatem David cum gaudio : et erant cum David septem chori, et victima vituli.
      EN: King David was told that the Lord had blessed Obed-edom and all that belonged to him because of the ark of God. David went and brought the ark of God from Obed-edom's house to the City of David with joy. With David were seven choirs and a calf for sacrifice.

6:13  LA: Cumque transcendissent qui portabant arcam Domini sex passus, immolabat bovem et arietem,
      EN: When those carrying the ark of the Lord had advanced six paces, he sacrificed an ox and a ram.

6:14  LA: et David saltabat totis viribus ante Dominum : porro David erat accinctus ephod lineo.
      EN: David danced with all his strength before the Lord, girded with a linen ephod.

6:15  LA: Et David et omnis domus Israël ducebant arcam testamenti Domini in jubilo, et in clangore buccinæ.
      EN: David and all the house of Israel brought up the ark of the Lord's covenant with joyful shouting and the sound of the trumpet.

6:16  LA: Cumque intrasset arca Domini in civitatem David, Michol filia Saul, prospiciens per fenestram, vidit regem David subsilientem atque saltantem coram Domino : et despexit eum in corde suo.
      EN: When the ark of the Lord entered the City of David, Saul's daughter Michal looked through the window and saw King David leaping and dancing before the Lord. She despised him in her heart.

6:17  LA: Et introduxerunt arcam Domini, et imposuerunt eam in loco suo in medio tabernaculi, quod tetenderat ei David : et obtulit David holocausta et pacifica coram Domino.
      EN: They brought the ark of the Lord in and placed it in its position inside the tent David had pitched for it. David offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before the Lord.

6:18  LA: Cumque complesset offerens holocausta et pacifica, benedixit populo in nomine Domini exercituum.
      EN: When he finished offering the burnt offerings and peace offerings, he blessed the people in the name of the Lord of hosts.

6:19  LA: Et partitus est universæ multitudini Israël tam viro quam mulieri singulis collyridam panis unam, et assaturam bubulæ carnis unam, et similam frixam oleo : et abiit omnis populus, unusquisque in domum suam.
      EN: He distributed to the whole multitude of Israel, to each man and woman, one cake of bread, one portion of roast beef, and fine flour fried in oil. All the people went away, each to his own house.

6:20  LA: Reversusque est David ut benediceret domui suæ : et egressa Michol filia Saul in occursum David, ait : Quam gloriosus fuit hodie rex Israël discooperiens se ante ancillas servorum suorum, et nudatus est, quasi si nudetur unus de scurris.
      EN: David returned to bless his household. Saul's daughter Michal came out to meet him and said, “How glorious the king of Israel was today, uncovering himself before his servants' slave women, baring himself like one of the buffoons!”

6:21  LA: Dixitque David ad Michol : Ante Dominum, qui elegit me potius quam patrem tuum et quam omnem domum ejus, et præcepit mihi ut essem dux super populum Domini in Israël,
      EN: David said to Michal, “Before the Lord, who chose me rather than your father and his entire house and commanded me to be ruler over the Lord's people in Israel,

6:22  LA: et ludam, et vilior fiam plus quam factus sum : et ero humilis in oculis meis, et cum ancillis de quibus locuta es, gloriosior apparebo.
      EN: I will play, and I will become still more contemptible than I have been. I will be lowly in my own eyes, and among the slave women of whom you spoke I will appear more glorious.”

6:23  LA: Igitur Michol filiæ Saul non est natus filius usque in diem mortis suæ.
      EN: Michal, Saul's daughter, had no child born to her until the day of her death.

2 SAMUEL 7

7:1  LA: Factum est autem cum sedisset rex in domo sua, et Dominus dedisset ei requiem undique ab universis inimicis suis,
     EN: When the king was settled in his house and the Lord had given him rest on every side from all his enemies,

7:2  LA: dixit ad Nathan prophetam : Videsne quod ego habitem in domo cedrina, et arca Dei posita sit in medio pellium ?
     EN: he said to the prophet Nathan, “Do you see that I live in a cedar house, while the ark of God is placed amid skins?”

7:3  LA: Dixitque Nathan ad regem : Omne quod est in corde tuo, vade, fac : quia Dominus tecum est.
     EN: Nathan said to the king, “Go and do everything in your heart, for the Lord is with you.”

7:4  LA: Factum est autem in illa nocte : et ecce sermo Domini ad Nathan, dicens :
     EN: But that night the word of the Lord came to Nathan, saying,

7:5  LA: Vade, et loquere ad servum meum David : Hæc dicit Dominus : Numquid tu ædificabis mihi domum ad habitandum ?
     EN: “Go and speak to My servant David: Thus says the Lord: Will you build Me a house to live in?

7:6  LA: Neque enim habitavi in domo ex die illa, qua eduxi filios Israël de terra Ægypti, usque in diem hanc : sed ambulabam in tabernaculo, et in tentorio.
     EN: I have not lived in a house from the day I brought the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt until this day, but have traveled in a tabernacle and a tent.

7:7  LA: Per cuncta loca quæ transivi cum omnibus filiis Israël, numquid loquens locutus sum ad unam de tribubus Israël, cui præcepi ut pasceret populum meum Israël, dicens : Quare non ædificastis mihi domum cedrinam ?
     EN: In all the places I passed through with all the children of Israel, did I ever say to any of the tribes of Israel whom I commanded to shepherd My people Israel, ‘Why have you not built Me a cedar house?’

7:8  LA: Et nunc hæc dices servo meo David : Hæc dicit Dominus exercituum : Ego tuli te de pascuis sequentem greges, ut esses dux super populum meum Israël :
     EN: Now say this to My servant David: Thus says the Lord of hosts: I took you from the pastures, from following the flocks, to be ruler over My people Israel.

7:9  LA: et fui tecum in omnibus ubicumque ambulasti, et interfeci universos inimicos tuos a facie tua : fecique tibi nomen grande, juxta nomen magnorum qui sunt in terra.
     EN: I have been with you everywhere you went and killed all your enemies before you. I have made you a great name, like the names of the great ones on earth.

7:10  LA: Et ponam locum populo meo Israël, et plantabo eum, et habitabit sub eo, et non turbabitur amplius : nec addent filii iniquitatis ut affligant eum sicut prius,
      EN: I will appoint a place for My people Israel and plant them there. They will live in it and will no longer be disturbed, nor will the sons of wickedness continue to afflict them as before,

7:11  LA: ex die qua constitui judices super populum meum Israël : et requiem dabo tibi ab omnibus inimicis tuis : prædicitque tibi Dominus quod domum faciat tibi Dominus.
      EN: from the day I appointed judges over My people Israel. I will give you rest from all your enemies. The Lord declares to you that the Lord will make you a house.

7:12  LA: Cumque completi fuerint dies tui, et dormieris cum patribus tuis, suscitabo semen tuum post te, quod egredietur de utero tuo, et firmabo regnum ejus.
      EN: When your days are fulfilled and you sleep with your fathers, I will raise up your offspring after you, who will come from your own body, and I will establish his kingdom.

7:13  LA: Ipse ædificabit domum nomini meo, et stabiliam thronum regni ejus usque in sempiternum.
      EN: He will build a house for My name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever.

7:14  LA: Ego ero ei in patrem, et ipse erit mihi in filium : qui si inique aliquid gesserit, arguam eum in virga virorum, et in plagis filiorum hominum.
      EN: I will be a father to him, and he will be a son to Me. If he does anything wicked, I will rebuke him with the rod of men and the blows of the sons of men.

7:15  LA: Misericordiam autem meam non auferam ab eo, sicut abstuli a Saul, quem amovi a facie mea.
      EN: But I will not take My mercy from him as I took it from Saul, whom I removed from My presence.

      EN: When David was told, he sent to meet them, for the men had been deeply humiliated. David instructed them, “Stay in Jericho until your beards grow, and then return.”

10:6  LA: Videntes autem filii Ammon quod injuriam fecissent David, miserunt, et conduxerunt mercede Syrum Rohob, et Syrum Soba, viginti millia peditum, et a rege Maacha mille viros, et ab Istob duodecim millia virorum.
      EN: The children of Ammon saw that they had insulted David. They sent and hired twenty thousand Syrian foot soldiers from Rehob and Zobah, a thousand men from the king of Maacah, and twelve thousand men from Ish-tob.

10:7  LA: Quod cum audisset David, misit Joab et omnem exercitum bellatorum.
      EN: When David heard this, he sent Joab and the entire army of warriors.

      EN: Joab saw that battle was prepared against him both in front and behind. He chose men from all the chosen men of Israel and drew up a battle line against the Syrians.

10:10  LA: reliquam autem partem populi tradidit Abisai fratri suo, qui direxit aciem adversus filios Ammon.
       EN: He gave the rest of the people to his brother Abishai, who drew up a battle line against the children of Ammon.

       EN: Joab said, “If the Syrians prevail against me, you must help me. If the children of Ammon prevail against you, I will help you.

10:12  LA: Esto vir fortis, et pugnemus pro populo nostro et civitate Dei nostri : Dominus autem faciet quod bonum est in conspectu suo.

10:13  LA: Iniit itaque Joab, et populus qui erat cum eo, certamen contra Syros : qui statim fugerunt a facie ejus.
       EN: Joab and the people with him entered battle against the Syrians, who immediately fled before him.

10:14  LA: Filii autem Ammon videntes quia fugissent Syri, fugerunt et ipsi a facie Abisai, et ingressi sunt civitatem : reversusque est Joab a filiis Ammon, et venit Jerusalem.
       EN: When the children of Ammon saw that the Syrians had fled, they too fled before Abishai and entered the city. Joab returned from the children of Ammon and came to Jerusalem.

       EN: The Syrians saw that they had fallen before Israel and gathered together.

       EN: The Syrians fled before Israel, and David destroyed seven hundred Syrian chariot crews and forty thousand horsemen. He struck Shobach, the army commander, who died at once.

2 SAMUEL 11

11:1  LA: Factum est autem, vertente anno, eo tempore quo solent reges ad bella procedere, misit David Joab, et servos suos cum eo, et universum Israël, et vastaverunt filios Ammon, et obsederunt Rabba : David autem remansit in Jerusalem.

      EN: While this was happening, David arose from his bed after midday and walked on the roof terrace of the royal house. From his terrace he saw a woman bathing opposite him. The woman was very beautiful.

      EN: The king sent to inquire who the woman was. He was told that she was Bathsheba daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite.

11:5  LA: et reversa est domum suam concepto fœtu. Mittensque nuntiavit David, et ait : Concepi.
      EN: and returned to her house, having conceived a child. She sent word to David, saying, “I have conceived.”

11:6  LA: Misit autem David ad Joab, dicens : Mitte ad me Uriam Hethæum. Misitque Joab Uriam ad David.
      EN: David sent to Joab, saying, “Send Uriah the Hittite to me.” Joab sent Uriah to David.

11:7  LA: Et venit Urias ad David. Quæsivitque David quam recte ageret Joab et populus, et quomodo administraretur bellum.
      EN: Uriah came to David, and David asked how Joab and the people were doing and how the war was being conducted.

11:8  LA: Et dixit David ad Uriam : Vade in domum tuam, et lava pedes tuos. Et egressus est Urias de domo regis, secutusque est eum cibus regius.
      EN: David said to Uriah, “Go to your house and wash your feet.” Uriah left the king's house, and royal food was sent after him.

11:9  LA: Dormivit autem Urias ante portam domus regiæ cum aliis servis domini sui, et non descendit ad domum suam.
      EN: But Uriah slept before the gate of the royal house with his lord's other servants and did not go down to his own house.

11:10  LA: Nuntiatumque est David a dicentibus : Non ivit Urias in domum suam. Et ait David ad Uriam : Numquid non de via venisti ? quare non descendisti in domum tuam ?
       EN: David was told, “Uriah did not go to his house.” David said to Uriah, “Have you not come from a journey? Why did you not go down to your house?”

11:11  LA: Et ait Urias ad David : Arca Dei et Israël et Juda habitant in papilionibus, et dominus meus Joab et servi domini mei super faciem terræ manent : et ego ingrediar domum meam, ut comedam et bibam, et dormiam cum uxore mea ? Per salutem tuam, et per salutem animæ tuæ, non faciam rem hanc.
       EN: Uriah said to David, “The ark of God, Israel, and Judah live in tents, and my lord Joab and my lord's servants stay on the open ground. Shall I go into my house to eat and drink and sleep with my wife? By your welfare and by the life of your soul, I will not do this.”

11:12  LA: Ait ergo David ad Uriam : Mane hic etiam hodie, et cras dimittam te. Mansit Urias in Jerusalem in die illa et altera :
       EN: David said to Uriah, “Stay here today also, and tomorrow I will send you away.” Uriah stayed in Jerusalem that day and the next.

11:13  LA: et vocavit eum David ut comederet coram se et biberet, et inebriavit eum : qui egressus vespere, dormivit in strato suo cum servis domini sui, et in domum suam non descendit.
       EN: David invited him to eat and drink before him and made him drunk. But he went out in the evening and slept on his bed with his lord's servants, and did not go down to his house.

11:14  LA: Factum est ergo mane, et scripsit David epistolam ad Joab : misitque per manum Uriæ,
       EN: In the morning David wrote a letter to Joab and sent it by Uriah's hand,

11:15  LA: scribens in epistola : Ponite Uriam ex adverso belli, ubi fortissimum est prælium : et derelinquite eum, ut percussus intereat.
       EN: writing in the letter, “Place Uriah at the battlefront, where the fighting is fiercest, and abandon him, so that he may be struck down and die.”

       EN: As Joab besieged the city, he stationed Uriah where he knew the strongest men were.

       EN: The men came out of the city and fought against Joab. Some of David's servants fell, and Uriah the Hittite also died.

11:18  LA: Misit itaque Joab, et nuntiavit David omnia verba prælii :
       EN: Joab sent and reported all the events of the battle to David.

11:19  LA: præcepitque nuntio, dicens : Cum compleveris universos sermones belli ad regem,
       EN: He instructed the messenger, saying, “When you have finished giving the king the full account of the war,

11:20  LA: si eum videris indignari, et dixerit : Quare accessistis ad murum, ut præliaremini ? an ignorabatis quod multa desuper ex muro tela mittantur ?
       EN: if you see him grow angry and he says, ‘Why did you approach the wall to fight? Did you not know that many missiles would be hurled from above, from the wall?

11:21  LA: Quis percussit Abimelech filium Jerobaal ? nonne mulier misit super eum fragmen molæ de muro, et interfecit eum in Thebes ? quare juxta murum accessistis ? dices : Etiam servus tuus Urias Hethæus occubuit.
       EN: Who struck Abimelech son of Jerubbaal? Did not a woman throw a fragment of a millstone on him from the wall and kill him at Thebez? Why did you approach the wall?’ then say, ‘Your servant Uriah the Hittite has also fallen.’”

      EN: The rich man had a great many sheep and cattle.

      EN: Why, then, have you scorned the Lord's word by doing evil in My sight? You struck Uriah the Hittite with the sword and took his wife as your own wife, killing him with the sword of the children of Ammon.

12:10  LA: Quam ob rem non recedet gladius de domo tua usque in sempiternum, eo quod despexeris me, et tuleris uxorem Uriæ Hethæi ut esset uxor tua.
       EN: Therefore the sword will never depart from your house, because you despised Me and took Uriah the Hittite's wife to be your wife.

       EN: Thus says the Lord: I will raise up evil against you from your own house. I will take your wives before your eyes and give them to your neighbor, and he will sleep with your wives in the sight of this sun.

12:12  LA: Tu enim fecisti abscondite : ego autem faciam verbum istud in conspectu omnis Israël, et in conspectu solis.

       EN: David said to Nathan, “I have sinned against the Lord.” Nathan said to David, “The Lord has also taken away your sin; you will not die.

12:21  LA: Dixerunt autem ei servi sui : Quis est sermo quem fecisti ? propter infantem, cum adhuc viveret, jejunasti et flebas : mortuo autem puero, surrexisti, et comedisti panem.
       EN: His servants said to him, “What is this you have done? For the infant's sake, while he was still alive, you fasted and wept, but when the child died, you arose and ate bread.”

12:23  LA: Nunc autem quia mortuus est, quare jejunem ? numquid potero revocare eum amplius ? ego vadam magis ad eum : ille vero non revertetur ad me.
       EN: But now that he is dead, why should I fast? Can I bring him back again? Rather, I will go to him, but he will not return to me.”

       EN: David comforted his wife Bathsheba. He went in to her and slept with her, and she bore a son and named him Solomon. The Lord loved him.

12:25  LA: Misitque in manu Nathan prophetæ, et vocavit nomen ejus, Amabilis Domino, eo quod diligeret eum Dominus.
       EN: He sent word through the prophet Nathan and named him Beloved of the Lord, because the Lord loved him.

12:26  LA: Igitur pugnabat Joab contra Rabbath filiorum Ammon, et expugnabat urbem regiam.
       EN: Joab was fighting against Rabbah of the children of Ammon and attacking the royal city.

12:27  LA: Misitque Joab nuntios ad David, dicens : Dimicavi adversum Rabbath, et capienda est Urbs aquarum.
       EN: Joab sent messengers to David, saying, “I have fought against Rabbah, and the City of Waters is ready to be taken.

       EN: He brought out its people and sawed them apart, drove iron-shod wagons over them, divided them with blades, and made them pass through brick molds. He did this to all the cities of the children of Ammon. David and the whole army returned to Jerusalem.

2 SAMUEL 13

13:1  LA: Factum est autem post hæc ut Absalom filii David sororem speciosissimam, vocabulo Thamar, adamaret Amnon filius David,
      EN: After this, Amnon son of David fell in love with Tamar, the very beautiful sister of David's son Absalom.

13:2  LA: et deperiret eam valde, ita ut propter amorem ejus ægrotaret : quia cum esset virgo, difficile ei videbatur ut quippiam inhoneste ageret cum ea.
      EN: He pined for her so greatly that he became ill with love for her. Because she was a virgin, it seemed difficult to him to do anything dishonorable with her.

13:3  LA: Erat autem Amnon amicus nomine Jonadab, filius Semmaa fratris David, vir prudens valde.
      EN: Amnon had a friend named Jonadab, the son of David's brother Shimeah, a very shrewd man.

13:4  LA: Qui dixit ad eum : Quare sic attenuaris macie, fili regis, per singulos dies ? cur non indicas mihi ? Dixitque ei Amnon : Thamar sororem fratris mei Absalom amo.

13:5  LA: Cui respondit Jonadab : Cuba super lectum tuum, et languorem simula : cumque venerit pater tuus ut visitet te, dic ei : Veniat, oro, Thamar soror mea, ut det mihi cibum, et faciat pulmentum, ut comedam de manu ejus.
      EN: Jonadab answered him, “Lie down on your bed and pretend to be ill. When your father comes to visit you, say to him, ‘Please let my sister Tamar come and give me food and prepare a dish, so that I may eat from her hand.’”

13:6  LA: Accubuit itaque Amnon, et quasi ægrotare cœpit : cumque venisset rex ad visitandum eum, ait Amnon ad regem : Veniat, obsecro, Thamar soror mea, ut faciat in oculis meis duas sorbitiunculas, et cibum capiam de manu ejus.
      EN: Amnon lay down and began to pretend to be ill. When the king came to visit him, Amnon said to the king, “Please let my sister Tamar come and make two small servings of broth before my eyes, so that I may take food from her hand.”

13:7  LA: Misit ergo David ad Thamar domum, dicens : Veni in domum Amnon fratris tui, et fac ei pulmentum.
      EN: David sent word to Tamar at home, saying, “Come to your brother Amnon's house and prepare a dish for him.”

13:8  LA: Venitque Thamar in domum Amnon fratris sui : ille autem jacebat. Quæ tollens farinam commiscuit, et liquefaciens, in oculis ejus coxit sorbitiunculas.
      EN: Tamar came to the house of her brother Amnon, who was lying down. She took flour, mixed it, thinned it, and cooked the small servings of broth before his eyes.

13:9  LA: Tollensque quod coxerat, effudit, et posuit coram eo, et noluit comedere : dixitque Amnon : Ejicite universos a me. Cumque ejecissent omnes,

13:10  LA: dixit Amnon ad Thamar : Infer cibum in conclave, ut vescar de manu tua. Tulit ergo Thamar sorbitiunculas quas fecerat, et intulit ad Amnon fratrem suum in conclave.
       EN: Amnon said to Tamar, “Bring the food into the chamber, so that I may eat from your hand.” Tamar took the broth she had made and brought it to her brother Amnon in the chamber.

13:11  LA: Cumque obtulisset ei cibum, apprehendit eam, et ait : Veni, cuba mecum, soror mea.
       EN: When she offered him the food, he seized her and said, “Come, lie with me, my sister.”

13:12  LA: Quæ respondit ei : Noli frater mi, noli opprimere me : neque enim hoc fas est in Israël : noli facere stultitiam hanc.
       EN: She answered him, “No, my brother, do not force me. Such a thing is not lawful in Israel. Do not commit this folly.

13:13  LA: Ego enim ferre non potero opprobrium meum, et tu eris quasi unus de insipientibus in Israël : quin potius loquere ad regem, et non negabit me tibi.
       EN: I could not bear my disgrace, and you would become like one of the fools in Israel. Rather, speak to the king, and he will not refuse me to you.”

13:14  LA: Noluit autem acquiescere precibus ejus, sed prævalens viribus oppressit eam, et cubavit cum ea.
       EN: He was unwilling to heed her pleas. Overpowering her by force, he violated her and lay with her.

13:15  LA: Et exosam eam habuit Amnon odio magno nimis : ita ut majus esset odium quo oderat eam, amore quo ante dilexerat. Dixitque ei Amnon : Surge, et vade.
       EN: Then Amnon hated her with an exceedingly great hatred. The hatred with which he hated her was greater than the love with which he had loved her before. Amnon said to her, “Get up and go.”

13:16  LA: Quæ respondit ei : Majus est hoc malum quod nunc agis adversum me, quam quod ante fecisti, expellens me. Et noluit audire eam :
       EN: She answered him, “This evil you are now doing to me by driving me out is greater than what you did before.” But he would not listen to her.

13:17  LA: sed vocato puero qui ministrabat ei, dixit : Ejice hanc a me foras, et claude ostium post eam.
       EN: He called the servant who attended him and said, “Put this woman out from me and shut the door behind her.”

13:18  LA: Quæ induta erat talari tunica : hujuscemodi enim filiæ regis virgines vestibus utebantur. Ejecit itaque eam minister illius foras : clausitque fores post eam.
       EN: She wore an ankle-length tunic, for the king's virgin daughters wore garments of this kind. His servant put her outside and shut the doors behind her.

13:19  LA: Quæ aspergens cinerem capiti suo, scissa talari tunica, impositisque manibus super caput suum, ibat ingrediens, et clamans.
       EN: She scattered ashes on her head, tore her ankle-length tunic, put her hands on her head, and went on her way crying aloud.

13:20  LA: Dixit autem ei Absalom frater suus : Numquid Amnon frater tuus concubuit tecum ? sed nunc soror, tace : frater tuus est : neque affligas cor tuum pro hac re. Mansit itaque Thamar contabescens in domo Absalom fratris sui.
       EN: Her brother Absalom said to her, “Has your brother Amnon lain with you? But now be silent, sister; he is your brother. Do not afflict your heart over this.” Tamar remained wasting away in her brother Absalom's house.

13:21  LA: Cum autem audisset rex David verba hæc, contristatus est valde : et noluit contristare spiritum Amnon filii sui, quoniam diligebat eum, quia primogenitus erat ei.
       EN: When King David heard these things, he was deeply distressed. But he was unwilling to grieve the spirit of his son Amnon, because he loved him, for he was his firstborn.

13:22  LA: Porro non est locutus Absalom ad Amnon nec malum nec bonum : oderat enim Absalom Amnon, eo quod violasset Thamar sororem suam.
       EN: Absalom spoke neither evil nor good to Amnon, for Absalom hated Amnon because he had violated his sister Tamar.

13:33  LA: Nunc ergo ne ponat dominus meus rex super cor suum verbum istud, dicens : Omnes filii regis occisi sunt : quoniam Amnon solus mortuus est.
       EN: Now let not my lord the king take this report to heart, saying, ‘All the king's sons have been killed,’ for Amnon alone is dead.”

       EN: Absalom fled. The young watchman raised his eyes and looked, and a great crowd was coming by an out-of-the-way road along the mountainside.

13:35  LA: Dixit autem Jonadab ad regem : Ecce filii regis adsunt : juxta verbum servi tui, sic factum est.
       EN: Jonadab said to the king, “Here are the king's sons. It has happened as your servant said.”

13:36  LA: Cumque cessasset loqui, apparuerunt et filii regis : et intrantes levaverunt vocem suam, et fleverunt : sed et rex et omnes servi ejus fleverunt ploratu magno nimis.

13:37  LA: Porro Absalom fugiens abiit ad Tholomai filium Ammiud regem Gessur. Luxit ergo David filium suum cunctis diebus.

13:38  LA: Absalom autem cum fugisset, et venisset in Gessur, fuit ibi tribus annis.
       EN: After Absalom fled and came to Geshur, he remained there for three years.

13:39  LA: Cessavitque rex David persequi Absalom, eo quod consolatus esset super Amnon interitu.
       EN: King David stopped pursuing Absalom, because he had been comforted over Amnon's death.

2 SAMUEL 14

14:1  LA: Intelligens autem Joab filius Sarviæ quod cor regis versum esset ad Absalom,
      EN: Joab son of Zeruiah understood that the king's heart had turned toward Absalom.

14:3  LA: et ingredieris ad regem, et loqueris ad eum sermones hujuscemodi. Posuit autem Joab verba in ore ejus.
      EN: Go in to the king and speak to him in this manner.” Joab put the words in her mouth.

      EN: The woman of Tekoa entered before the king, fell to the ground before him, bowed down, and said, “Save me, O king.”

14:5  LA: Et ait ad eam rex : Quid causæ habes ? Quæ respondit : Heu, mulier vidua ego sum : mortuus est enim vir meus.
      EN: The king said to her, “What is your case?” She answered, “Alas, I am a widow, for my husband is dead.

14:6  LA: Et ancillæ tuæ erant duo filii : qui rixati sunt adversum se in agro, nullusque erat qui eos prohibere posset : et percussit alter alterum, et interfecit eum.

       EN: When Absalom's servants came to the house, they said to the woman, “Where are Ahimaaz and Jonathan?” The woman answered them, “They passed on quickly after tasting a little water.” Those searching for them did not find them and returned to Jerusalem.

17:21  LA: Cumque abiissent, ascenderunt illi de puteo, et pergentes nuntiaverunt regi David, et dixerunt : Surgite, et transite cito fluvium : quoniam hujuscemodi dedit consilium contra vos Achitophel.
       EN: After they left, the two came up from the well and went to tell King David. They said, “Arise and cross the river quickly, for Ahithophel has given this counsel against you.”

       EN: Ahithophel saw that his counsel had not been followed. He saddled his donkey, arose, and went to his house in his own city. He put his household in order and died by hanging, and was buried in his father's tomb.

17:27  LA: Cumque venisset David in castra, Sobi filius Naas de Rabbath filiorum Ammon, et Machir filius Ammihel de Lodabar, et Berzellai Galaadites de Rogelim,

       EN: as well as honey, butter, sheep, and fattened calves. They gave these to David and the people with him to eat, for they supposed the people were weary with hunger and thirst in the wilderness.

2 SAMUEL 18

18:1  LA: Igitur considerato David populo suo, constituit super eos tribunos et centuriones,
      EN: David reviewed his people and appointed commanders of thousands and hundreds over them.

18:2  LA: et dedit populi tertiam partem sub manu Joab, et tertiam partem sub manu Abisai filii Sarviæ fratris Joab, et tertiam partem sub manu Ethai, qui erat de Geth. Dixitque rex ad populum : Egrediar et ego vobiscum.
      EN: He placed a third of the people under Joab, a third under Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab's brother, and a third under Ittai of Gath. The king said to the people, “I too will go out with you.”

18:3  LA: Et respondit populus : Non exibis : sive enim fugerimus, non magnopere ad eos de nobis pertinebit : sive media pars ceciderit e nobis, non satis curabunt, quia tu unus pro decem millibus computaris : melius est igitur ut sis nobis in urbe præsidio.
      EN: The people answered, “You must not go out. If we flee, they will not care greatly about us. If half of us fall, they will not care much, for you alone count as ten thousand. It is better for you to support us from the city.”

      EN: The king said to them, “I will do what seems right to you.” The king stood beside the gate, and the people went out in their companies by hundreds and thousands.

18:5  LA: Et præcepit rex Joab, et Abisai, et Ethai, dicens : Servate mihi puerum Absalom. Et omnis populus audiebat præcipientem regem cunctis principibus pro Absalom.
      EN: The king commanded Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, saying, “Spare the young man Absalom for me.” All the people heard the king giving orders to all the commanders concerning Absalom.

18:6  LA: Itaque egressus est populus in campum contra Israël, et factum est prælium in saltu Ephraim.
      EN: The people went out into the field against Israel, and battle took place in the forest of Ephraim.

18:7  LA: Et cæsus est ibi populus Israël ab exercitu David, factaque est plaga magna in die illa, viginti millium.
      EN: The people of Israel were struck down there by David's army, and a great slaughter of twenty thousand occurred that day.

18:8  LA: Fuit autem ibi prælium dispersum super faciem omnis terræ, et multo plures erant quos saltus consumpserat de populo, quam hi quos voraverat gladius in die illa.
      EN: The battle spread across the whole countryside, and the forest consumed far more of the people that day than the sword devoured.

18:9  LA: Accidit autem ut occurreret Absalom servis David, sedens mulo : cumque ingressus fuisset mulus subter condensam quercum et magnam, adhæsit caput ejus quercui : et illo suspenso inter cælum et terram, mulus cui insederat, pertransivit.
      EN: Absalom happened to meet David's servants while riding a mule. The mule went under a great, dense oak, and his head caught in the oak. He hung between heaven and earth, while the mule beneath him went on.

18:10  LA: Vidit autem hoc quispiam, et nuntiavit Joab, dicens : Vidi Absalom pendere de quercu.
       EN: Someone saw this and told Joab, saying, “I saw Absalom hanging from an oak.”

       EN: Joab said to the man who told him, “If you saw him, why did you not strike him to the ground? I would have given you ten silver shekels and a belt.”

18:12  LA: Qui dixit ad Joab : Si appenderes in manibus meis mille argenteos, nequaquam mitterem manum meam in filium regis : audientibus enim nobis præcepit rex tibi, et Abisai, et Ethai, dicens : Custodite mihi puerum Absalom.
       EN: He said to Joab, “Even if you weighed a thousand pieces of silver into my hands, I would not stretch out my hand against the king's son. For in our hearing the king commanded you, Abishai, and Ittai, saying, ‘Guard the young man Absalom for me.’

18:13  LA: Sed etsi fecissem contra animam meam audacter, nequaquam hoc regem latere potuisset, et tu stares ex adverso ?
       EN: Even if I had acted rashly against my own life, the matter could not have remained hidden from the king, and you would have stood against me.”

18:14  LA: Et ait Joab : Non sicut tu vis, sed aggrediar eum coram te. Tulit ergo tres lanceas in manu sua, et infixit eas in corde Absalom : cumque adhuc palpitaret hærens in quercu,

18:15  LA: cucurrerunt decem juvenes armigeri Joab, et percutientes interfecerunt eum.
       EN: ten young men, Joab's armor-bearers, ran up, struck him, and killed him.

18:16  LA: Cecinit autem Joab buccina, et retinuit populum, ne persequeretur fugientem Israël, volens parcere multitudini.
       EN: Joab sounded the trumpet and restrained the people from pursuing the fleeing Israelites, wishing to spare the multitude.

18:17  LA: Et tulerunt Absalom, et projecerunt eum in saltu, in foveam grandem, et comportaverunt super eum acervum lapidum magnum nimis : omnis autem Israël fugit in tabernacula sua.
       EN: They took Absalom, threw him into a great pit in the forest, and piled an exceedingly large heap of stones over him. All Israel fled to their tents.

       EN: While still alive, Absalom had erected for himself a monument in the King's Valley, for he had said, “I have no son, and this will preserve the memory of my name.” He named the monument after himself, and it is called Absalom's Hand to this day.

18:19  LA: Achimaas autem filius Sadoc, ait : Curram, et nuntiabo regi quia judicium fecerit ei Dominus de manu inimicorum ejus.
       EN: Ahimaaz son of Zadok said, “Let me run and tell the king that the Lord has vindicated him against his enemies.”

       EN: Joab said to him, “You will not be a messenger today; you may bring news another day. I do not want you to bring news today, for the king's son is dead.”

       EN: Joab said to Cushi, “Go and tell the king what you have seen.” Cushi bowed to Joab and ran.

       EN: Ahimaaz son of Zadok again said to Joab, “What prevents me from running after Cushi too?” Joab said to him, “Why do you want to run, my son? You will not be a bearer of good news.”

       EN: David was sitting between the two gates. The watchman on the top of the gate above the wall raised his eyes and saw a man running alone.

       EN: the watchman saw another man running. He shouted from the top, “I see another man running alone.” The king said, “He too is a bearer of good news.”

       EN: The watchman said, “I recognize the first man's running as the running of Ahimaaz son of Zadok.” The king said, “He is a good man and comes bringing good news.”

18:28  LA: Clamans autem Achimaas, dixit ad regem : Salve rex. Et adorans regem coram eo pronus in terram, ait : Benedictus Dominus Deus tuus, qui conclusit homines qui levaverunt manus suas contra dominum meum regem.
       EN: Ahimaaz called out to the king, “Hail, king!” Bowing face down to the ground before the king, he said, “Blessed be the Lord your God, who has shut up the men who raised their hands against my lord the king.”

18:29  LA: Et ait rex : Estne pax puero Absalom ? Dixitque Achimaas : Vidi tumultum magnum cum mitteret Joab servus tuus, o rex, me servum tuum : nescio aliud.
       EN: The king said, “Is the young man Absalom safe?” Ahimaaz said, “I saw a great commotion when Joab, your servant, O king, sent me, your servant. I know nothing more.”

18:30  LA: Ad quem rex : Transi, ait, et sta hic. Cumque ille transisset, et staret,
       EN: The king said to him, “Step aside and stand here.” He stepped aside and stood there.

       EN: Cushi appeared and came forward, saying, “I bring good news, my lord the king, for today the Lord has vindicated you against all who rose against you.”

       EN: The king said to Cushi, “Is the young man Absalom safe?” Cushi answered, “May the enemies of my lord the king and all who rise against him for evil become like that young man.”

2 SAMUEL 19

19:1  LA: Contristatus itaque rex, ascendit cœnaculum portæ, et flevit. Et sic loquebatur, vadens : Fili mi Absalom, Absalom fili mi : quis mihi tribuat ut ego moriar pro te, Absalom fili mi, fili mi Absalom ?
      EN: The king was deeply distressed. He went up to the chamber over the gate and wept, saying as he went, “My son Absalom! Absalom, my son! Who would grant that I might die for you, Absalom, my son, my son Absalom!”

      EN: Joab was told that the king was weeping and mourning his son.

      EN: That day's victory turned to mourning for all the people, for they heard it said that day, “The king grieves over his son.”

      EN: The people avoided entering the city that day, as people do when they have been routed and are fleeing from battle.

19:5  LA: Porro rex operuit caput suum, et clamabat voce magna : Fili mi Absalom, Absalom fili mi, fili mi.
      EN: The king covered his head and cried aloud, “My son Absalom! Absalom, my son, my son!”

19:6  LA: Ingressus ergo Joab ad regem in domum, dixit : Confudisti hodie vultus omnium servorum tuorum, qui salvam fecerunt animam tuam, et animam filiorum tuorum et filiarum tuarum, et animam uxorum tuarum, et animam concubinarum tuarum.
      EN: Joab went in to the king in the house and said, “Today you have put to shame the faces of all your servants who saved your life and the lives of your sons, daughters, wives, and concubines.

19:7  LA: Diligis odientes te, et odio habes diligentes te : et ostendisti hodie quia non curas de ducibus tuis et de servis tuis : et vere cognovi modo, quia si Absalom viveret, et omnes nos occubuissemus, tunc placeret tibi.
      EN: You love those who hate you and hate those who love you. Today you have shown that you do not care about your commanders and servants. I now truly know that if Absalom were alive and all of us had fallen, you would be pleased.

19:8  LA: Nunc igitur surge, et procede, et alloquens satisfac servis tuis : juro enim tibi per Dominum quod si non exieris, ne unus quidem remansurus sit tecum nocte hac : et pejus erit hoc tibi quam omnia mala quæ venerunt super te ab adolescentia tua usque in præsens.
      EN: Now arise, go out, and speak to reassure your servants. For I swear to you by the Lord that if you do not go out, not even one man will remain with you tonight. This will be worse for you than all the evils that have come upon you from your youth until now.”

19:9  LA: Surrexit ergo rex et sedit in porta : et omni populo nuntiatum est quod rex sederet in porta. Venitque universa multitudo coram rege : Israël autem fugit in tabernacula sua.
      EN: The king arose and sat in the gate. All the people were told that the king was sitting in the gate, and the entire multitude came before the king. Israel had fled to their tents.

19:10  LA: Omnis quoque populus certabat in cunctis tribubus Israël, dicens : Rex liberavit nos de manu inimicorum nostrorum ; ipse salvavit nos de manu Philisthinorum : et nunc fugit de terra propter Absalom.
       EN: All the people were arguing throughout the tribes of Israel, saying, “The king rescued us from the hands of our enemies; he saved us from the hands of the Philistines. Now he has fled the land because of Absalom.

24:16  LA: Cumque extendisset manum suam angelus Domini super Jerusalem ut disperderet eam, misertus est Dominus super afflictione, et ait angelo percutienti populum : Sufficit : nunc contine manum tuam. Erat autem angelus Domini juxta aream Areuna Jebusæi.
       EN: When the angel of the Lord stretched out his hand over Jerusalem to destroy it, the Lord had pity over the affliction and said to the angel striking the people, “It is enough. Now restrain your hand.” The angel of the Lord was beside the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite.

24:17  LA: Dixitque David ad Dominum cum vidisset angelum cædentem populum : Ego sum qui peccavi, ego inique egi : isti qui oves sunt, quid fecerunt ? vertatur, obsecro, manus tua contra me, et contra domum patris mei.
       EN: When David saw the angel striking down the people, he said to the Lord, “I am the one who sinned; I acted wickedly. What have these sheep done? Please turn Your hand against me and my father's house.”

24:18  LA: Venit autem Gad ad David in die illa, et dixit ei : Ascende, et constitue altare Domino in area Areuna Jebusæi.
       EN: Gad came to David that day and said to him, “Go up and establish an altar to the Lord on the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite.”

24:19  LA: Et ascendit David juxta sermonem Gad, quem præceperat ei Dominus.
       EN: David went up according to Gad's word, as the Lord had commanded him.

24:20  LA: Conspiciensque Areuna, animadvertit regem et servos ejus transire ad se :
       EN: Araunah looked and saw the king and his servants coming toward him.

24:21  LA: et egressus adoravit regem prono vultu in terram, et ait : Quid causæ est ut veniat dominus meus rex ad servum suum ? Cui David ait : Ut emam a te aream, et ædificem altare Domino, et cesset interfectio quæ grassatur in populo.
       EN: He went out and bowed before the king with his face to the ground, saying, “Why has my lord the king come to his servant?” David said to him, “To buy the threshing floor from you and build an altar to the Lord, so that the slaughter raging among the people may cease.”

24:22  LA: Et ait Areuna ad David : Accipiat, et offerat dominus meus rex sicut placet ei : habes boves in holocaustum, et plaustrum, et juga boum in usum lignorum.
       EN: Araunah said to David, “Let my lord the king take and offer what pleases him. Here are oxen for the burnt offering, and the cart and the oxen's yokes to use as wood.”

24:23  LA: Omnia dedit Areuna rex regi : dixitque Areuna ad regem : Dominus Deus tuus suscipiat votum tuum.
       EN: Araunah the king gave everything to the king. Araunah said to the king, “May the Lord your God accept your offering.”

24:24  LA: Cui respondens rex, ait : Nequaquam ut vis, sed emam pretio a te, et non offeram Domino Deo meo holocausta gratuita. Emit ergo David aream, et boves, argenti siclis quinquaginta :
       EN: The king answered him, “It will not be as you wish. I will buy it from you for a price, and I will not offer the Lord my God burnt offerings that cost me nothing.” David bought the threshing floor and the oxen for fifty silver shekels.

24:25  LA: et ædificavit ibi David altare Domino, et obtulit holocausta et pacifica : et propitiatus est Dominus terræ, et cohibita est plaga ab Israël.
       EN: David built an altar to the Lord there and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings. The Lord became favorable to the land, and the plague was restrained from Israel.
