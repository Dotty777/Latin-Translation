1:1  LA: Anno secundo, regnante Artaxerxe maximo, prima die mensis Nisan, vidit somnium Mardochæus filius Jairi, filii Semei, filii Cis, de tribu Benjamin :
     EN: In the second year of the reign of the great Artaxerxes, on the first day of the month Nisan, Mordecai son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, of the tribe of Benjamin, saw a dream. He was a Jewish man who lived in the city of Susa, a great man and among the foremost in the royal court. He was one of the captives whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had deported from Jerusalem with Jeconiah king of Judah. This was his dream: voices, tumult, thunder, an earthquake, and turmoil appeared upon the earth. Behold, there were two great dragons, ready to fight against each other. At their cry all the nations were stirred up to fight against the nation of the just. That day was a day of darkness and peril, tribulation and distress, and great fear upon the earth. The nation of the just was troubled, fearing its calamities, and prepared for death. They cried out to God, and as they raised their voices, a little spring grew into a very great river and overflowed in abundant waters. Light and the sun arose, and the lowly were exalted and devoured the mighty. When Mordecai had seen this and risen from his bed, he pondered what God intended to do. He kept it fixed in his mind, wishing to know what the dream meant. At that time he was staying in the king's court with Bagatha and Thara, the king's eunuchs, who were the palace doorkeepers. When he understood their thoughts and examined their concerns more closely, he learned that they were attempting to lay hands on King Artaxerxes, and he reported the matter to the king. The king questioned both men and, when they confessed, ordered them led away to death. The king recorded what had happened in the records, and Mordecai too committed an account of the event to writing. The king commanded him to remain in the palace court and gave him gifts for his report. But Haman son of Hammedatha, the Bugaean, was held in very great honor before the king, and he wished to harm Mordecai and his people because of the king's two eunuchs who had been killed. And they plundered their goods, or possessions. This was the copy of the letter.

     EN: The king's attendants who guarded the palace doors said to him: Why, unlike the others, do you not observe the king's command?

     EN: In the first month, called Nisan, in the twelfth year of Ahasuerus's reign, a lot, called phur in Hebrew, was cast into an urn before Haman to determine the day and month on which the Jewish nation should be killed. The twelfth month, called Adar, came out.

     EN: Haman said to King Ahasuerus: There is a people scattered throughout all the provinces of your kingdom, separated from one another, using strange laws and ceremonies and, moreover, despising the king's decrees. You know very well that it does not benefit your kingdom to let them grow insolent through unchecked freedom.

3:9  LA: Si tibi placet, decerne, ut pereat, et decem millia talentorum appendam arcariis gazæ tuæ.
     EN: If it pleases you, decree their destruction, and I will weigh out ten thousand talents to the keepers of your treasury.

3:10  LA: Tulit ergo rex annulum, quo utebatur, de manu sua, et dedit eum Aman filio Amadathi de progenie Agag, hosti Judæorum,

7:10  LA: Suspensus est itaque Aman in patibulo quod paraverat Mardochæo : et regis ira quievit.
      EN: So Haman was hanged on the gibbet he had prepared for Mordecai, and the king's anger subsided.

ESTHER 8

8:1  LA: Die illo dedit rex Assuerus Esther reginæ domum Aman adversarii Judæorum, et Mardochæus ingressus est ante faciem regis. Confessa est enim ei Esther quod esset patruus suus.
     EN: That day King Ahasuerus gave Queen Esther the house of Haman, the Jews' adversary, and Mordecai came before the king. For Esther had disclosed to him that Mordecai was her uncle.

8:2  LA: Tulitque rex annulum, quem ab Aman recipi jusserat, et tradidit Mardochæo. Esther autem constituit Mardochæum super domum suam.
     EN: The king took the ring he had ordered recovered from Haman and handed it to Mordecai. Esther appointed Mordecai over her house.

8:3  LA: Nec his contenta, procidit ad pedes regis, flevitque, et locuta ad eum oravit ut malitiam Aman Agagitæ, et machinationes ejus pessimas quas excogitaverat contra Judæos, juberet irritas fieri.
     EN: Not content with this, she fell at the king's feet and wept, speaking to him and begging him to order the malice of Haman the Agagite and the wicked schemes he had devised against the Jews to be brought to nothing.

8:4  LA: At ille ex more sceptrum aureum protendit manu, quo signum clementiæ monstrabatur : illaque consurgens stetit ante eum,
     EN: As was customary, he extended the golden scepter in his hand as a sign of mercy. She rose, stood before him,

8:5  LA: et ait : Si placet regi, et si inveni gratiam in oculis ejus, et deprecatio mea non ei videtur esse contraria, obsecro ut novis epistolis, veteres Aman litteræ, insidiatoris et hostis Judæorum, quibus eos in cunctis regis provinciis perire præceperat, corrigantur.
     EN: and said: If it pleases the king, if I have found favor in his eyes, and if my plea does not seem objectionable to him, I ask that new letters correct the earlier letters of Haman, the plotter and enemy of the Jews, in which he commanded their destruction in all the king's provinces.

8:6  LA: Quomodo enim potero sustinere necem et interfectionem populi mei ?
     EN: For how could I endure the killing and slaughter of my people?

8:7  LA: Responditque rex Assuerus Esther reginæ, et Mardochæo Judæo : Domum Aman concessi Esther, et ipsum jussi affigi cruci, quia ausus est manum mittere in Judæos.
     EN: King Ahasuerus answered Queen Esther and Mordecai the Jew: I have granted Haman's house to Esther and ordered him fastened to a cross because he dared to lay hands on the Jews.

8:8  LA: Scribite ergo Judæis, sicut vobis placet, regis nomine, signantes litteras annulo meo. Hæc enim consuetudo erat, ut epistolis, quæ ex regis nomine mittebantur et illius annulo signatæ erant, nemo auderet contradicere.
     EN: Therefore write to the Jews as you please in the king's name, sealing the letters with my ring. For it was the custom that no one dared contradict letters sent in the king's name and sealed with his ring.

8:9  LA: Accitisque scribis et librariis regis (erat autem tempus tertii mensis, qui appellatur Siban) vigesima et tertia die illius scriptæ sunt epistolæ, ut Mardochæus voluerat, ad Judæos, et ad principes, procuratoresque et judices, qui centum viginti septem provinciis ab India usque ad Æthiopiam præsidebant : provinciæ atque provinciæ, populo et populo juxta linguas et litteras suas, et Judæis, prout legere poterant et audire.
     EN: The king's scribes and recorders were summoned. It was the twenty-third day of the third month, called Sivan. Letters were written as Mordecai wished to the Jews and to the princes, administrators, and governors who presided over the hundred and twenty-seven provinces from India to Ethiopia: to each province and each people in its own language and script, and to the Jews as they could read and hear them.

8:10  LA: Ipsæque epistolæ, quæ regis nomine mittebantur, annulo ipsius obsignatæ sunt, et missæ per veredarios : qui per omnes provincias discurrentes, veteres litteras novis nuntiis prævenirent.
      EN: These letters, sent in the king's name, were sealed with his ring and sent by mounted couriers. They were to ride through all the provinces and forestall the earlier letters with the new message.

8:11  LA: Quibus imperavit rex, ut convenirent Judæos per singulas civitates, et in unum præciperent congregari ut starent pro animabus suis, et omnes inimicos suos cum conjugibus ac liberis et universis domibus, interficerent atque delerent, et spolia eorum diriperent.
      EN: Through these letters the king commanded that the Jews in every city be approached and ordered to gather together, to stand for their lives, kill and destroy all their enemies with their wives, children, and entire households, and plunder their spoils.

8:12  LA: Et constituta est per omnes provincias una ultionis dies, id est tertiadecima mensis duodecimi Adar.
      EN: Throughout all the provinces a single day of vengeance was appointed, the thirteenth of the twelfth month, Adar.

8:12a  LA: Rex magnus Artaxerxes ab India usque Æthiopiam, centum viginti septem provinciarum ducibus ac principibus qui nostræ jussioni obediunt, salutem dicit.
       EN: The great King Artaxerxes sends greetings to the rulers and princes of the hundred and twenty-seven provinces from India to Ethiopia who obey our command. Many have abused the goodness of rulers and the honor bestowed upon them, turning to pride. They endeavor not only to oppress kings' subjects but, unable to bear the glory given them, plot against the very men who gave it. They are not content to withhold thanks for benefits and violate the obligations arising from the kindness shown them; they also suppose that they can escape the judgment of God, who sees everything. They have broken out into such madness that they attempt, through the underground workings of lies, to overthrow those who diligently fulfill their entrusted duties and do everything in a manner worthy of everyone's praise. With cunning deceit they mislead the ears of rulers, who are trusting and judge others by their own character. Both ancient histories and daily events prove how the evil suggestions of certain people corrupt kings' intentions. Therefore provision must be made for the peace of all the provinces. You must not suppose that, if we issue different commands, it comes from fickleness of mind. Rather, we give judgment according to the character and necessity of the times, as the welfare of the state requires. To make our meaning clearer to you: Haman son of Hammedatha, a Macedonian in both spirit and nationality, foreign to Persian blood and staining our goodness with his cruelty, was received by us as a foreigner. He experienced such kindness from us that he was called our father and received homage from everyone, second after the king. He swelled to such a height of arrogance that he attempted to deprive us of both kingdom and life. For he sought the death of Mordecai, through whose loyalty and services we are alive, and of Esther, the partner of our royal power, together with her entire nation, through strange and unheard-of schemes. He intended, once they were killed, to plot against us in our isolation and transfer the Persian kingdom to the Macedonians. But we have found that the Jews, whom this worst of mortals had marked for death, are entirely without fault and, on the contrary, follow just laws. They are children of the Most High, the greatest and ever-living God, through whose kindness the kingdom was handed down to our fathers and to us and is preserved to this day. Therefore know that the letters he sent under our name are void. For this crime, both the plotter and his entire family hang on gibbets before the gates of this city, Susa. It is God, not we, who has repaid him what he deserved. Let this edict that we now send be posted in every city, so that the Jews may be permitted to follow their own laws. You must assist them, so that on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, called Adar, they may kill those who had prepared to put them to death. For almighty God has changed this day of grief and mourning into joy for them. Therefore keep this day among your other festivals and celebrate it with every gladness, so that in the future too it may be known that all who faithfully obey the Persians receive a reward worthy of their loyalty, while those who plot against their kingdom perish for their crime. Let every province and city unwilling to share in this solemn festival perish by sword and fire and be so utterly destroyed that it remains forever inaccessible not only to people but even to beasts, as an example of contempt and disobedience.

The two dragons are Haman and me. The nations that assembled are those who attempted to destroy the name of the Jews. My nation is Israel, which cried out to the Lord, and the Lord saved His people. He delivered us from every evil and performed great signs and wonders among the nations. He ordained that there be two lots, one for God's people and the other for all the nations. Both lots came before God for all the nations on the day appointed from that time. The Lord remembered His people and had mercy on His inheritance. These days shall be observed in the month Adar, on the fourteenth and fifteenth days of that month, with every care and with joy, the people assembled in a single gathering, throughout all succeeding generations of the people of Israel.
