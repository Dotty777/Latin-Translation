JOB 1

1:1  LA: Vir erat in terra Hus, nomine Job : et erat vir ille simplex, et rectus, ac timens Deum, et recedens a malo.
     EN: There was a man in the land of Uz named Job. That man was blameless and upright, fearing God and turning away from evil.

1:2  LA: Natique sunt ei septem filii, et tres filiæ.
     EN: Seven sons and three daughters were born to him.

1:3  LA: Et fuit possessio ejus septem millia ovium, et tria millia camelorum, quingenta quoque juga boum, et quingentæ asinæ, ac familia multa nimis : eratque vir ille magnus inter omnes orientales.
     EN: His possessions were seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen, five hundred female donkeys, and a very large household. This man was great among all the people of the East.

1:4  LA: Et ibant filii ejus, et faciebant convivium per domos, unusquisque in die suo. Et mittentes vocabant tres sorores suas, ut comederent et biberent cum eis.
     EN: His sons used to go and hold a feast in their houses, each on his own day. They would send for their three sisters to eat and drink with them.

1:5  LA: Cumque in orbem transissent dies convivii, mittebat ad eos Job, et sanctificabat illos : consurgensque diluculo, offerebat holocausta pro singulis. Dicebat enim : Ne forte peccaverint filii mei, et benedixerint Deo in cordibus suis. Sic faciebat Job cunctis diebus.
     EN: When the days of feasting had completed their round, Job would send for them and sanctify them. Rising at dawn, he offered whole burnt offerings for each of them, for he said: Perhaps my children have sinned and blessed God in their hearts. Job did this continually.

1:6  LA: Quadam autem die, cum venissent filii Dei ut assisterent coram Domino, affuit inter eos etiam Satan.
     EN: One day, when the sons of God came to stand before the Lord, Satan also appeared among them.

1:7  LA: Cui dixit Dominus : Unde venis ? Qui respondens, ait : Circuivi terram, et perambulavi eam.
     EN: The Lord said to him: Where have you come from? He answered: I have gone around the earth and walked through it.

1:8  LA: Dixitque Dominus ad eum : Numquid considerasti servum meum Job, quod non sit ei similis in terra, homo simplex et rectus, ac timens Deum, et recedens a malo ?
     EN: The Lord said to him: Have you considered My servant Job? There is no one like him on the earth, a blameless and upright man, fearing God and turning away from evil.

1:9  LA: Cui respondens Satan, ait : Numquid Job frustra timet Deum ?
     EN: Satan answered Him: Does Job fear God for nothing?

1:10  LA: nonne tu vallasti eum, ac domum ejus, universamque substantiam per circuitum ; operibus manuum ejus benedixisti, et possessio ejus crevit in terra ?
      EN: Have You not surrounded him, his house, and all his possessions with a protective wall? You have blessed the work of his hands, and his possessions have increased in the land.

1:11  LA: sed extende paululum manum tuam et tange cuncta quæ possidet, nisi in faciem benedixerit tibi.
      EN: But stretch out Your hand a little and touch everything he possesses, and see whether he does not bless You to Your face.

1:12  LA: Dixit ergo Dominus ad Satan : Ecce universa quæ habet in manu tua sunt : tantum in eum ne extendas manum tuam. Egressusque est Satan a facie Domini.
      EN: The Lord therefore said to Satan: Behold, everything he has is in your hand; only do not stretch out your hand against him. Satan went out from the Lord's presence.

1:13  LA: Cum autem quadam die filii et filiæ ejus comederent et biberent vinum in domo fratris sui primogeniti,
      EN: One day, when Job's sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their eldest brother,

1:14  LA: nuntius venit ad Job, qui diceret : Boves arabant, et asinæ pascebantur juxta eos :
      EN: a messenger came to Job and said: The oxen were plowing, and the female donkeys were grazing beside them,

1:15  LA: et irruerunt Sabæi, tuleruntque omnia, et pueros percusserunt gladio : et evasi ego solus, ut nuntiarem tibi.
      EN: when the Sabeans rushed in and took everything. They struck down the servants with the sword, and I alone escaped to tell you.

1:16  LA: Cumque adhuc ille loqueretur, venit alter, et dixit : Ignis Dei cecidit e cælo, et tactas oves puerosque consumpsit : et effugi ego solus, ut nuntiarem tibi.
      EN: While he was still speaking, another came and said: The fire of God fell from heaven and consumed the sheep and servants it struck, and I alone escaped to tell you.

1:17  LA: Sed et illo adhuc loquente, venit alius, et dixit : Chaldæi fecerunt tres turmas, et invaserunt camelos, et tulerunt eos, necnon et pueros percusserunt gladio : et ego fugi solus, ut nuntiarem tibi.
      EN: While he too was still speaking, another came and said: The Chaldeans formed three bands, attacked the camels, and took them. They also struck down the servants with the sword, and I alone fled to tell you.

1:18  LA: Adhuc loquebatur ille, et ecce alius intravit, et dixit : Filiis tuis et filiabus vescentibus et bibentibus vinum in domo fratris sui primogeniti,
      EN: He was still speaking when another entered and said: Your sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their eldest brother,

1:19  LA: repente ventus vehemens irruit a regione deserti, et concussit quatuor angulos domus : quæ corruens oppressit liberos tuos, et mortui sunt : et effugi ego solus, ut nuntiarem tibi.
      EN: when suddenly a violent wind rushed in from the wilderness and shook the four corners of the house. It collapsed upon your children, and they died, and I alone escaped to tell you.

1:20  LA: Tunc surrexit Job, et scidit vestimenta sua : et tonso capite corruens in terram, adoravit,
      EN: Then Job rose, tore his garments, shaved his head, and fell to the ground in worship.

1:21  LA: et dixit : Nudus egressus sum de utero matris meæ, et nudus revertar illuc. Dominus dedit, Dominus abstulit ; sicut Domino placuit, ita factum est. Sit nomen Domini benedictum.
      EN: He said: Naked I came from my mother's womb, and naked I shall return there. The Lord gave, and the Lord took away; as it pleased the Lord, so it has happened. Blessed be the Lord's name.

1:22  LA: In omnibus his non peccavit Job labiis suis, neque stultum quid contra Deum locutus est.
      EN: In all this Job did not sin with his lips or speak anything foolish against God.

JOB 2

2:1  LA: Factum est autem, cum quadam die venissent filii Dei, et starent coram Domino, venisset quoque Satan inter eos, et staret in conspectu ejus,
     EN: One day, when the sons of God came and stood before the Lord, Satan also came among them and stood in His sight.

2:2  LA: ut diceret Dominus ad Satan : Unde venis ? Qui respondens ait : Circuivi terram, et perambulavi eam.
     EN: The Lord said to Satan: Where have you come from? He answered: I have gone around the earth and walked through it.

2:3  LA: Et dixit Dominus ad Satan : Numquid considerasti servum meum Job, quod non sit ei similis in terra, vir simplex et rectus, ac timens Deum, et recedens a malo, et adhuc retinens innocentiam ? tu autem commovisti me adversus eum, ut affligerem eum frustra.
     EN: The Lord said to Satan: Have you considered My servant Job? There is no one like him on the earth, a blameless and upright man, fearing God and turning away from evil, still holding fast to his innocence. Yet you stirred Me against him to afflict him without cause.

2:4  LA: Cui respondens Satan, ait : Pellem pro pelle, et cuncta quæ habet homo dabit pro anima sua ;
     EN: Satan answered Him: Skin for skin! A man will give everything he has for his life.

2:5  LA: alioquin mitte manum tuam, et tange os ejus et carnem, et tunc videbis quod in faciem benedicat tibi.
     EN: But stretch out Your hand and touch his bone and flesh, and then You will see that he blesses You to Your face.

2:6  LA: Dixit ergo Dominus ad Satan : Ecce in manu tua est : verumtamen animam illius serva.
     EN: The Lord therefore said to Satan: Behold, he is in your hand; only preserve his life.

2:7  LA: Egressus igitur Satan a facie Domini, percussit Job ulcere pessimo, a planta pedis usque ad verticem ejus ;
     EN: Satan went out from the Lord's presence and struck Job with a grievous ulcer from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head.

2:8  LA: qui testa saniem radebat, sedens in sterquilinio.
     EN: Job scraped away the discharge with a potsherd, sitting on a dung heap.

2:9  LA: Dixit autem illi uxor sua : Adhuc tu permanes in simplicitate tua ? Benedic Deo, et morere.
     EN: His wife said to him: Do you still hold fast to your blamelessness? Bless God and die.

2:9b  LA: Dixit autem illi uxor sua : Adhuc tu permanes in simplicitate tua ? Benedic Deo, et morere.
      EN: His wife said to him: Do you still hold fast to your blamelessness? Bless God and die.

2:9c  LA: Dixit autem illi uxor sua : Adhuc tu permanes in simplicitate tua ? Benedic Deo, et morere.
      EN: His wife said to him: Do you still hold fast to your blamelessness? Bless God and die.

2:9d  LA: Dixit autem illi uxor sua : Adhuc tu permanes in simplicitate tua ? Benedic Deo, et morere.
      EN: His wife said to him: Do you still hold fast to your blamelessness? Bless God and die.

2:9e  LA: Dixit autem illi uxor sua : Adhuc tu permanes in simplicitate tua ? Benedic Deo, et morere.
      EN: His wife said to him: Do you still hold fast to your blamelessness? Bless God and die.

2:10  LA: Qui ait ad illam : Quasi una de stultis mulieribus locuta es : si bona suscepimus de manu Dei, mala quare non suscipiamus ? In omnibus his non peccavit Job labiis suis.
      EN: He said to her: You have spoken like one of the foolish women. If we have received good things from God's hand, why should we not receive bad things? In all this Job did not sin with his lips.

2:11  LA: Igitur audientes tres amici Job omne malum quod accidisset ei, venerunt singuli de loco suo, Eliphaz Themanites, et Baldad Suhites, et Sophar Naamathites. Condixerant enim ut pariter venientes visitarent eum, et consolarentur.
      EN: When Job's three friends heard of all the evil that had happened to him, each came from his own place: Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite. They had agreed to come together to visit and comfort him.

2:12  LA: Cumque elevassent procul oculos suos, non cognoverunt eum, et exclamantes ploraverunt, scissisque vestibus sparserunt pulverem super caput suum in cælum.
      EN: When they lifted their eyes from afar, they did not recognize him. They cried aloud and wept, tore their garments, and scattered dust toward heaven over their heads.

2:13  LA: Et sederunt cum eo in terra septem diebus et septem noctibus : et nemo loquebatur ei verbum : videbant enim dolorem esse vehementem.
      EN: They sat with him on the ground for seven days and seven nights, and no one spoke a word to him, for they saw that his suffering was very great.

JOB 3

3:1  LA: Post hæc aperuit Job os suum, et maledixit diei suo,
     EN: After this Job opened his mouth and cursed his day.

3:2  LA: et locutus est :
     EN: He said:

3:3  LA: [Pereat dies in qua natus sum, et nox in qua dictum est : Conceptus est homo.
     EN: Let the day on which I was born perish, and the night in which it was said: A human being has been conceived.

3:4  LA: Dies ille vertatur in tenebras : non requirat eum Deus desuper, et non illustretur lumine.
     EN: Let that day turn to darkness; let God above not seek it, and let no light shine upon it.

3:5  LA: Obscurent eum tenebræ et umbra mortis ; occupet eum caligo, et involvatur amaritudine.
     EN: Let darkness and the shadow of death obscure it; let gloom seize it, and let it be wrapped in bitterness.

3:6  LA: Noctem illam tenebrosus turbo possideat ; non computetur in diebus anni, nec numeretur in mensibus.
     EN: Let a dark whirlwind take possession of that night. Let it not be counted among the days of the year or numbered among the months.

3:7  LA: Sit nox illa solitaria, nec laude digna.
     EN: Let that night be solitary and unworthy of praise.

3:8  LA: Maledicant ei qui maledicunt diei, qui parati sunt suscitare Leviathan.
     EN: Let those who curse the day curse it, those prepared to rouse Leviathan.

3:9  LA: Obtenebrentur stellæ caligine ejus ; expectet lucem, et non videat, nec ortum surgentis auroræ.
     EN: Let its stars be darkened with gloom. Let it await light and not see it, nor the rising of the dawn,

3:10  LA: Quia non conclusit ostia ventris qui portavit me, nec abstulit mala ab oculis meis.
      EN: because it did not close the doors of the womb that bore me, or remove evil from my eyes.

3:11  LA: Quare non in vulva mortuus sum ? egressus ex utero non statim perii ?
      EN: Why did I not die in the womb? Why did I not perish as soon as I came from it?

3:12  LA: Quare exceptus genibus ? cur lactatus uberibus ?
      EN: Why was I received upon the knees? Why was I nursed at the breasts?

3:13  LA: Nunc enim dormiens silerem, et somno meo requiescerem
      EN: For now I would be sleeping in silence and resting in my sleep

3:14  LA: cum regibus et consulibus terræ, qui ædificant sibi solitudines ;
      EN: with the kings and rulers of the earth who build desolate places for themselves,

3:15  LA: aut cum principibus qui possident aurum, et replent domos suas argento ;
      EN: or with princes who possess gold and fill their houses with silver.

3:16  LA: aut sicut abortivum absconditum non subsisterem, vel qui concepti non viderunt lucem.
      EN: Or I would have no existence, like a hidden miscarriage, like those conceived who never saw the light.

3:17  LA: Ibi impii cessaverunt a tumultu, et ibi requieverunt fessi robore.
      EN: There the wicked have ceased their tumult, and there those weary of strength have found rest.

3:18  LA: Et quondam vincti pariter sine molestia, non audierunt vocem exactoris.
      EN: There those once imprisoned are together without distress; they do not hear the taskmaster's voice.

3:19  LA: Parvus et magnus ibi sunt, et servus liber a domino suo.
      EN: The small and the great are there, and the slave is free from his master.

3:20  LA: Quare misero data est lux, et vita his qui in amaritudine animæ sunt :
      EN: Why is light given to the miserable, and life to those bitter in soul,

3:21  LA: qui expectant mortem, et non venit, quasi effodientes thesaurum ;
      EN: who await death and it does not come, as though digging for treasure,

3:22  LA: gaudentque vehementer cum invenerint sepulchrum ?
      EN: and rejoice exceedingly when they find the grave?

3:23  LA: viro cujus abscondita est via et circumdedit eum Deus tenebris ?
      EN: Why is it given to a man whose way is hidden, whom God has surrounded with darkness?

3:24  LA: Antequam comedam, suspiro ; et tamquam inundantes aquæ, sic rugitus meus :
      EN: Before I eat, I sigh, and my groaning is like overflowing waters.

3:25  LA: quia timor quem timebam evenit mihi, et quod verebar accidit.
      EN: For the fear I feared has come upon me, and what I dreaded has happened.

3:26  LA: Nonne dissimulavi ? nonne silui ? nonne quievi ? et venit super me indignatio.]
      EN: Did I not hold back? Did I not remain silent? Did I not keep quiet? Yet wrath came upon me.

JOB 4

4:1  LA: Respondens autem Eliphaz Themanites, dixit :
     EN: Eliphaz the Temanite answered:

4:2  LA: [Si cœperimus loqui tibi, forsitan moleste accipies ; sed conceptum sermonem tenere quis poterit ?
     EN: If we begin to speak to you, perhaps you will take it badly; but who can hold back words already conceived?

4:3  LA: Ecce docuisti multos, et manus lassas roborasti ;
     EN: Behold, you have instructed many and strengthened weary hands.

4:4  LA: vacillantes confirmaverunt sermones tui, et genua trementia confortasti.
     EN: Your words have steadied those who stumbled, and you have strengthened trembling knees.

4:5  LA: Nunc autem venit super te plaga, et defecisti ; tetigit te, et conturbatus es.
     EN: But now the blow has come upon you, and you have failed; it has touched you, and you are troubled.

4:6  LA: Ubi est timor tuus, fortitudo tua, patientia tua, et perfectio viarum tuarum ?
     EN: Where are your reverence, your strength, your patience, and the integrity of your ways?

4:7  LA: Recordare, obsecro te, quis umquam innocens periit ? aut quando recti deleti sunt ?
     EN: Remember, I ask you: Who ever perished while innocent? Or when were the upright destroyed?

4:8  LA: Quin potius vidi eos qui operantur iniquitatem, et seminant dolores, et metunt eos,
     EN: Rather, I have seen those who work iniquity and sow sorrows and reap them

4:9  LA: flante Deo perisse, et spiritu iræ ejus esse consumptos.
     EN: perish at God's breath and be consumed by the blast of His anger.

4:10  LA: Rugitus leonis, et vox leænæ, et dentes catulorum leonum contriti sunt.
      EN: The lion's roar, the lioness's voice, and the teeth of the young lions have been broken.

4:11  LA: Tigris periit, eo quod non haberet prædam, et catuli leonis dissipati sunt.
      EN: The tiger perished because it had no prey, and the lion's cubs were scattered.

4:12  LA: Porro ad me dictum est verbum absconditum, et quasi furtive suscepit auris mea venas susurri ejus.
      EN: A hidden word was spoken to me, and my ear received, as if by stealth, the faint strains of its whisper.

4:13  LA: In horrore visionis nocturnæ, quando solet sopor occupare homines,
      EN: In the dread of a night vision, when deep sleep usually seizes people,

4:14  LA: pavor tenuit me, et tremor, et omnia ossa mea perterrita sunt ;
      EN: fear and trembling took hold of me, and all my bones were terrified.

4:15  LA: et cum spiritus, me præsente, transiret, inhorruerunt pili carnis meæ.
      EN: When a spirit passed before me, the hair of my flesh stood on end.

4:16  LA: Stetit quidam, cujus non agnoscebam vultum, imago coram oculis meis, et vocem quasi auræ lenis audivi.
      EN: Someone stood there whose face I did not recognize, an image before my eyes, and I heard a voice like a gentle breeze:

4:17  LA: Numquid homo, Dei comparatione, justificabitur ? aut factore suo purior erit vir ?
      EN: Can a human being be justified in comparison with God? Can a man be purer than his Maker?

4:18  LA: Ecce qui serviunt ei, non sunt stabiles, et in angelis suis reperit pravitatem ;
      EN: Behold, those who serve Him are not steadfast, and He finds fault in His angels.

4:19  LA: quanto magis hi qui habitant domos luteas, qui terrenum habent fundamentum, consumentur velut a tinea ?
      EN: How much more shall those who dwell in houses of clay, whose foundation is earthly, be consumed as by a moth?

4:20  LA: De mane usque ad vesperam succidentur ; et quia nullus intelligit, in æternum peribunt.
      EN: From morning to evening they shall be cut down; because no one understands, they shall perish forever.

4:21  LA: Qui autem reliqui fuerint, auferentur ex eis ; morientur, et non in sapientia.]
      EN: Those who remain shall be taken away from among them. They shall die, and not in wisdom.

JOB 5

5:1  LA: Voca ergo, si est qui tibi respondeat, et ad aliquem sanctorum convertere.
     EN: Call, then, if there is anyone to answer you, and turn to one of the holy ones.

5:2  LA: Vere stultum interficit iracundia, et parvulum occidit invidia.
     EN: Truly, anger kills the fool, and envy puts the simpleton to death.

5:3  LA: Ego vidi stultum firma radice, et maledixi pulchritudini ejus statim.
     EN: I saw a fool firmly rooted, and at once I cursed his flourishing state.

5:4  LA: Longe fient filii ejus a salute, et conterentur in porta, et non erit qui eruat.
     EN: His children shall be far from safety; they shall be crushed at the gate, and there shall be no one to rescue them.

5:5  LA: Cujus messem famelicus comedet, et ipsum rapiet armatus, et bibent sitientes divitias ejus.
     EN: The hungry shall eat his harvest; an armed man shall seize him, and the thirsty shall drink up his wealth.

5:6  LA: Nihil in terra sine causa fit, et de humo non oritur dolor.
     EN: Nothing on earth happens without a cause, and sorrow does not sprout from the soil.

5:7  LA: Homo nascitur ad laborem, et avis ad volatum.
     EN: A human being is born for toil, and a bird for flight.

5:8  LA: Quam ob rem ego deprecabor Dominum, et ad Deum ponam eloquium meum :
     EN: Therefore I will implore the Lord and lay my speech before God,

5:9  LA: qui facit magna et inscrutabilia, et mirabilia absque numero ;
     EN: who does great and unsearchable things, wonders without number,

5:10  LA: qui dat pluviam super faciem terræ, et irrigat aquis universa ;
      EN: who gives rain upon the face of the earth and waters all things,

5:11  LA: qui ponit humiles in sublime, et mœrentes erigit sospitate ;
      EN: who sets the lowly on high and raises mourners to safety,

5:12  LA: qui dissipat cogitationes malignorum, ne possint implere manus eorum quod cœperant ;
      EN: who scatters the plans of the malicious, so that their hands cannot complete what they began,

5:13  LA: qui apprehendit sapientes in astutia eorum, et consilium pravorum dissipat.
      EN: who catches the wise in their own cunning and frustrates the counsel of the perverse.

5:14  LA: Per diem incurrent tenebras, et quasi in nocte, sic palpabunt in meridie.
      EN: By day they shall encounter darkness; at noon they shall grope as in the night.

5:15  LA: Porro salvum faciet egenum a gladio oris eorum, et de manu violenti pauperem.
      EN: But He will save the needy from the sword of their mouth, and the poor from the violent man's hand.

5:16  LA: Et erit egeno spes ; iniquitas autem contrahet os suum.
      EN: The needy shall have hope, and iniquity shall close its mouth.

5:17  LA: Beatus homo qui corripitur a Deo : increpationem ergo Domini ne reprobes :
      EN: Blessed is the person whom God corrects; therefore do not reject the Lord's rebuke.

5:18  LA: quia ipse vulnerat, et medetur ; percutit, et manus ejus sanabunt.
      EN: For He wounds and heals; He strikes, and His hands will restore.

5:19  LA: In sex tribulationibus liberabit te, et in septima non tanget te malum.
      EN: From six troubles He will deliver you, and in the seventh evil shall not touch you.

5:20  LA: In fame eruet te de morte, et in bello de manu gladii.
      EN: In famine He will rescue you from death, and in war from the power of the sword.

5:21  LA: A flagello linguæ absconderis, et non timebis calamitatem cum venerit.
      EN: You shall be hidden from the scourge of the tongue, and you shall not fear calamity when it comes.

5:22  LA: In vastitate et fame ridebis, et bestias terræ non formidabis.
      EN: At devastation and famine you shall laugh, and you shall not fear the beasts of the earth.

5:23  LA: Sed cum lapidibus regionum pactum tuum, et bestiæ terræ pacificæ erunt tibi.
      EN: For your covenant shall be with the stones of the fields, and the beasts of the earth shall be at peace with you.

5:24  LA: Et scies quod pacem habeat tabernaculum tuum ; et visitans speciem tuam, non peccabis.
      EN: You shall know that your tent is at peace; when you inspect your property, you shall not go wrong.

5:25  LA: Scies quoque quoniam multiplex erit semen tuum, et progenies tua quasi herba terræ.
      EN: You shall also know that your offspring will be many, and your descendants like the grass of the earth.

5:26  LA: Ingredieris in abundantia sepulchrum, sicut infertur acervus tritici in tempore suo.
      EN: You shall enter the grave in fullness, as a heap of wheat is brought in at its proper time.

5:27  LA: Ecce hoc, ut investigavimus, ita est : quod auditum, mente pertracta.]
      EN: Behold, this is what we have investigated, and so it is. Hear it and consider it in your mind.

JOB 6

6:1  LA: Respondens autem Job, dixit :
     EN: Job answered:

6:2  LA: [Utinam appenderentur peccata mea quibus iram merui, et calamitas quam patior, in statera !
     EN: If only my sins, by which I have deserved anger, and the calamity I suffer were weighed in a balance!

6:3  LA: Quasi arena maris hæc gravior appareret ; unde et verba mea dolore sunt plena :
     EN: The latter would appear heavier than the sand of the sea; therefore my words are full of grief.

6:4  LA: quia sagittæ Domini in me sunt, quarum indignatio ebibit spiritum meum ; et terrores Domini militant contra me.
     EN: For the Lord's arrows are within me; their fury drinks up my spirit, and the Lord's terrors wage war against me.

6:5  LA: Numquid rugiet onager cum habuerit herbam ? aut mugiet bos cum ante præsepe plenum steterit ?
     EN: Does a wild donkey bray when it has grass? Or does an ox low when it stands before a full manger?

6:6  LA: aut poterit comedi insulsum, quod non est sale conditum ? aut potest aliquis gustare quod gustatum affert mortem ?
     EN: Can something tasteless be eaten without salt? Or can anyone taste what brings death when tasted?

     EN: What my soul once refused to touch has now, in my anguish, become my food.
