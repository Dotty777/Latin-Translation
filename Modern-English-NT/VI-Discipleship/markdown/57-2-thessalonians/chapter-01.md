## Text

**1:1** Paul, Silvanus, and Timothy, to the church of the Thessalonians in God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:2** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:3** We must always give thanks to God for you, brothers, just as is fitting, because your faith is growing exceedingly, and the love of each one of you toward one another is abounding,

**1:4** so that we ourselves also boast of you in the churches of God, for your patience and faith, and in all your persecutions and afflictions that you are enduring,

**1:5** as an example of the just judgment of God, so that you may be considered worthy in the kingdom of God, for which you also suffer;

**1:6** if indeed it is just with God to repay affliction to those who afflict you,

**1:7** and to you who are afflicted, rest with us at the revelation of the Lord Jesus from heaven with the angels of His power,

**1:8** in a flame of fire, rendering vengeance to those who do not know God and who do not obey the gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ,

**1:9** who will pay eternal penalties in destruction, away from the face of the Lord and from the glory of His power,

**1:10** when He comes to be glorified in His saints, and to become wondrous in all who have believed, because our testimony to you was believed, on that day.

**1:11** In this also we always pray for you, that our God may deem you worthy of His calling, and fulfill every desire of goodness and the work of faith in power,

**1:12** so that the name of our Lord Jesus Christ may be glorified in you, and you in Him, according to the grace of our God and the Lord Jesus Christ.

## Translator Notes

- **1:5** — Vulgate reads in exemplum ('as an example', where Greek has endeigma, 'evidence/proof') and in regno Dei ('in the kingdom of God', where Greek has the genitive 'of the kingdom of God').
- **1:9** — Vulgate reads poenas ... in interitu aeternas ('eternal penalties in destruction'), attaching 'eternal' to 'penalties' and placing 'in destruction' in a prepositional phrase, whereas Greek has 'the penalty of eternal destruction'.
- **1:10** — Vulgate renders Greek thaumasthenai ('to be marveled at') with the adjective-plus-verb construction admirabilis fieri ('to become wondrous').
