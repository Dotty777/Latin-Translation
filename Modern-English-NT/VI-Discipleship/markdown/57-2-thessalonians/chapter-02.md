## Text

**2:1** Now we ask you, brothers, by the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ and of our gathering together to Him,

**2:2** that you not be quickly moved from your mind, nor be frightened, either by a spirit, or by a word, or by a letter as though sent through us, as if the day of the Lord were imminent.

**2:3** Let no one deceive you in any way, because unless the falling away comes first, and the man of sin is revealed, the son of perdition,

**2:4** who opposes and lifts himself up above everything that is called God or that is worshiped, so that he sits in the temple of God, showing himself as though he were God.

**2:5** Do you not recall that when I was still with you, I used to tell you these things?

**2:6** And now you know what restrains, so that he may be revealed in his own time.

**2:7** For the mystery of wickedness is already at work; only that the one who now restrains must keep restraining until he is removed from the midst.

**2:8** And then that wicked one will be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus will kill by the breath of His mouth, and by the brilliance of His coming He will destroy him,

**2:9** whose coming is according to Satan's working, in all power and signs and lying wonders,

**2:10** and in every deception of wickedness for those who are perishing, because they did not accept the love of the truth so that they might be saved.

**2:11** For this reason God will send them a working of error, so that they may believe a lie,

**2:12** in order that all may be judged who did not believe the truth, but consented to wickedness.

**2:13** But we must always give thanks to God for you, brothers loved by God, because God chose you as firstfruits for salvation in the sanctification of the Spirit and in faith in the truth,

**2:14** in which He also called you through our gospel, for the obtaining of the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**2:15** So then, brothers, stand firm and hold fast to the traditions that you learned, whether by word or by our letter.

**2:16** Now may our Lord Jesus Christ Himself, and God and our Father, who loved us and gave eternal comfort and good hope in grace,

**2:17** encourage your hearts and strengthen them in every good work and word.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — Vulgate uses the preposition of appeal per adventum ('by the coming') rather than Greek hyper ('concerning'), while retaining the genitive nostrae congregationis ('and of our gathering together').
- **2:2** — Vulgate explicitly adds missam ('sent') to tamquam per nos and renders Greek enesteken ('has already come') with instet ('were imminent' or 'at hand').
- **2:3** — Vulgate reads discessio ('the falling away' or 'departure/defection') and homo peccati ('the man of sin'), matching the Byzantine Greek reading (hamartias) rather than critical Greek editions (anomias, 'of lawlessness').
- **2:7** — Vulgate uses iniquitas ('wickedness/iniquity') across 2:7, 10, 12 and ille iniquus ('that wicked one') in 2:8, uniting Greek anomia/anomos and adikia; it also supplies the explicit subjunctive tantum ut ... teneat ('only that ... must keep restraining') and the idiom de medio fiat ('be removed from the midst').
- **2:13** — Vulgate reads a Deo ('by God', where Greek has 'by the Lord') and primitias ('as firstfruits', reflecting the Greek variant aparchen rather than ap' arches, 'from the beginning').
