## Text

**6:1** Children, obey your parents in the Lord, because this is right.

**6:2** "Honor your father and your mother," which is the first commandment with a promise:

**6:3** "so that it may go well with you, and you may be long-lived on the earth."

**6:4** And you, fathers, do not provoke your children to anger, but raise them in the discipline and correction of the Lord.

**6:5** Slaves, obey your masters in the flesh with fear and trembling, in the sincerity of your heart, as to Christ,

**6:6** not serving to the eye, as though pleasing people, but as slaves of Christ, doing the will of God from the soul,

**6:7** serving with good will, as to the Lord and not to people,

**6:8** knowing that each one, whatever good he has done, will receive this from the Lord, whether slave or free.

**6:9** And you, masters, do the same things to them, giving up threats, knowing that both their Lord and yours is in heaven, and there is no partiality with Him.

**6:10** Finally, brothers, be strengthened in the Lord and in the power of His strength.

**6:11** Put on the armor of God, so that you may be able to stand against the plots of the devil,

**6:12** for our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against rulers and authorities, against the world rulers of this darkness, against the spiritual forces of wickedness in the heavenly places.

**6:13** Therefore, take up the armor of God, so that you may be able to resist in the evil day and to stand perfected in all things.

**6:14** Stand, therefore, with your waist belted in truth, and having put on the breastplate of righteousness,

**6:15** and with your feet fitted in the preparation of the gospel of peace,

**6:16** in all things taking up the shield of faith, with which you may be able to extinguish all the flaming arrows of the most wicked one;

**6:17** and receive the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit (which is the word of God),

**6:18** through all prayer and petition, praying at all times in the Spirit, and keeping watch in it with all persistence and petition for all the saints,

**6:19** and for me, that speech may be given to me in the opening of my mouth with boldness, to make known the mystery of the gospel,

**6:20** for which I serve as an ambassador in a chain, so that in it I may be bold, just as I ought to speak.

**6:21** Now so that you also may know the things concerning me, what I am doing, Tychicus, dearest brother and faithful minister in the Lord, will make all things known to you.

**6:22** I have sent him to you for this very reason, so that you may come to know the things concerning us, and that he may comfort your hearts.

**6:23** Peace to the brothers, and love with faith, from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**6:24** Grace be with all who love our Lord Jesus Christ in incorruptibility. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **6:10** — The Clementine Vulgate includes fratres ('brothers'), which is omitted in many modern critical Greek editions (while the Textus Receptus reads 'my brothers').
- **6:13** — The Vulgate reading in omnibus perfecti stare ('to stand perfected in all things') differs from the Greek text, which reads 'having done everything, to stand.'
- **6:16** — Latin nequissimi is superlative ('the most wicked one'), rendering the positive Greek phrase 'the evil one'; in omnibus ('in all things') reflects the Greek reading en pasin rather than epi pasin ('above all').
- **6:18** — Latin in ipso ('in it') refers to prayer, though grammatically in Latin it can also mean 'in Him,' referring back to the Spirit.
- **6:24** — Latin in incorruptione ('in incorruptibility') is often understood as referring to unfailing or imperishable love; the Clementine Vulgate also retains 'Amen,' which is omitted in many modern Greek editions.
