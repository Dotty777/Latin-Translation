## Text

**4:1** Therefore I, a prisoner in the Lord, implore you to walk worthily of the calling by which you were called,

**4:2** with all humility and meekness, with patience, bearing with one another in love,

**4:3** careful to preserve the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.

**4:4** One body and one Spirit, just as you were called in one hope of your calling.

**4:5** One Lord, one faith, one baptism.

**4:6** One God and Father of all, who is above all, and through all things, and in all of us.

**4:7** Now to each one of us grace was given according to the measure of the gift of Christ.

**4:8** For this reason it says: "Ascending on high, He led captivity captive; He gave gifts to people."

**4:9** Now what does it mean that He ascended, except that He also first descended into the lower parts of the earth?

**4:10** He who descended is Himself also He who ascended above all the heavens, so that He might fill all things.

**4:11** And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, others evangelists, and still others pastors and teachers,

**4:12** for the completion of the saints, for the work of ministry, for the building up of the body of Christ,

**4:13** until we all arrive at the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the age of the fullness of Christ,

**4:14** so that we may no longer be little children tossed by waves and carried about by every wind of teaching, in the wickedness of people, in craftiness for the scheming of error.

**4:15** Instead, doing the truth in love, let us grow in Him in all things, who is the head, Christ,

**4:16** from whom the whole body, fitted and joined together through every joint of supply, according to the working in the measure of each individual member, produces the growth of the body for the building up of itself in love.

**4:17** Therefore I say this and testify in the Lord, that you should no longer walk just as the Gentiles also walk, in the futility of their thinking,

**4:18** having their understanding obscured by darkness, alienated from the life of God through the ignorance that is in them, because of the blindness of their heart,

**4:19** who, despairing, handed themselves over to shamelessness, for the working of all impurity in greed.

**4:20** You, however, did not learn Christ in such a way,

**4:21** if indeed you heard Him and were instructed in Him, just as truth is in Jesus:

**4:22** that you put off, according to the former way of life, the old man, who is being corrupted according to the desires of error.

**4:23** But be renewed in the spirit of your mind,

**4:24** and put on the new man, who was created according to God in righteousness and holiness of truth.

**4:25** For this reason, putting off lying, speak truth, each one with his neighbor, because we are members of one another.

**4:26** Be angry, and do not sin; let the sun not set upon your anger.

**4:27** Do not give room to the devil.

**4:28** Let the one who used to steal steal no longer; rather, let him labor, doing with his own hands what is good, so that he may have the means to give to the one suffering need.

**4:29** Let no evil speech proceed from your mouth, but whatever is good for the building up of faith, so that it may give grace to those who hear.

**4:30** And do not sadden the Holy Spirit of God, in whom you were sealed for the day of redemption.

**4:31** Let all bitterness, and wrath, and indignation, and shouting, and slander be removed from you, along with all malice.

**4:32** But be kind to one another, merciful, forgiving one another, just as God in Christ also forgave you.

## Translator Notes

- **4:6** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "in all of us" (in omnibus nobis), whereas the critical Greek text has simply "in all" (and some later Greek manuscripts read "in you all").
- **4:14** — The Vulgate renders the Greek term for "trickery" or "dice-playing" more broadly as "wickedness" (nequitia).
- **4:15** — Where the Greek reads "speaking/maintaining the truth" (aletheuontes), the Vulgate literally has "doing the truth" (veritatem facientes).
- **4:19** — The Vulgate renders Greek "having become callous" as "despairing" (desperantes, reflecting a reading meaning "having lost hope"); similarly, in 4:18 it translates Greek "hardness" as "blindness" (caecitatem).
- **4:23** — Latin bare ablative 'spiritu mentis vestræ' ("in the spirit of your mind") has no preposition and can be understood either locatively of the renewed human inner spirit ("in the spirit of your mind") or instrumentally of the divine Spirit ("by the Spirit of your mind").
- **4:29** — The Vulgate reads "for the building up of faith" (ad aedificationem fidei), following an ancient textual variant where the critical Greek text reads "of the need" (as fits the occasion).
