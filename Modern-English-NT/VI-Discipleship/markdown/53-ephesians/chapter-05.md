## Text

**5:1** Be imitators of God, therefore, as most beloved children,

**5:2** and walk in love, just as Christ also loved us and delivered Himself up for us, an offering and a sacrifice to God for an aroma of sweetness.

**5:3** But fornication and all impurity or greed must not even be named among you, as is fitting for saints,

**5:4** nor obscenity, nor foolish talk, nor vulgar joking, which is out of place, but rather thanksgiving.

**5:5** For know this, understanding that no fornicator, or impure, or greedy person, which is the service of idols, has an inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and God.

**5:6** Let no one mislead you with empty words, for because of these things the wrath of God comes upon the sons of unbelief.

**5:7** Therefore do not become sharers with them.

**5:8** For once you were darkness, but now light in the Lord. Walk as children of light,

**5:9** for the fruit of light is in all goodness, and righteousness, and truth,

**5:10** discerning what is well-pleasing to God,

**5:11** and do not share in the fruitless works of darkness, but rather expose them.

**5:12** For the things done in secret by them are shameful even to speak of.

**5:13** But all things that are exposed are revealed by the light; for everything that is revealed is light.

**5:14** For this reason it says: "Rise, you who sleep, and rise up from the dead, and Christ will give you light."

**5:15** See therefore, brothers, how carefully you walk, not as unwise,

**5:16** but as wise, redeeming the time, since the days are evil.

**5:17** Because of this, do not become foolish, but understand what the will of God is.

**5:18** And do not become drunk with wine, in which there is debauchery, but be filled with the Holy Spirit,

**5:19** speaking to yourselves in psalms, and hymns, and spiritual songs, singing and making music in your hearts to the Lord,

**5:20** giving thanks always for all things in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ to God and the Father,

**5:21** subject to one another in the fear of Christ.

**5:22** Let wives be subject to their own husbands, as to the Lord,

**5:23** because the husband is the head of the wife, just as Christ is the head of the church; He Himself is the Savior of His body.

**5:24** But just as the church is subject to Christ, so also let wives be to their husbands in everything.

**5:25** Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also loved the church and delivered Himself up for her,

**5:26** so that He might sanctify her, cleansing her by the washing of water in the word of life,

**5:27** so that He Himself might present to Himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or anything of this kind, but so that she may be holy and spotless.

**5:28** So also husbands ought to love their wives as their own bodies. The one who loves his own wife loves himself.

**5:29** For no one has ever hated his own flesh, but feeds and cherishes it, just as Christ also does the church,

**5:30** because we are members of His body, of His flesh and of His bones.

**5:31** Because of this, a man will leave his father and his mother and will cling to his wife, and the two will be in one flesh.

**5:32** This mystery is great; I, however, speak in Christ and in the church.

**5:33** Nevertheless, you also individually, let each one love his own wife as himself; and let the wife fear her husband.

## Translator Notes

- **5:5** — The Vulgate reads "which is the service of idols" (quod est idolorum servitus, i.e., idolatry), where the critical Greek text has masculine "who is an idolater."
- **5:15** — The Clementine Vulgate adds the address "brothers" (fratres), absent from standard Greek texts; similarly, it specifies "to God" (5:10) and "the will of God" (5:17) where Greek has "the Lord," and "the Holy Spirit" (5:18) where Greek has simply "the Spirit."
- **5:26** — The Clementine Vulgate adds "of life" (in verbo vitae, "in the word of life"), whereas the Greek text reads simply "in the word."
- **5:30** — The Vulgate includes "of His flesh and of His bones" (de carne ejus et de ossibus ejus), a reading found in many ancient witnesses but omitted in the critical Greek text.
- **5:32** — Latin sacramentum, the Vulgate's usual equivalent of Greek mysterion, is rendered "mystery" here as in 1:9, 3:3 and 3:9; "in Christ and in the church" literally renders in Christo et in Ecclesia, meaning with reference to Christ and the church.
