## Text

**6:1** Let all who are slaves under a yoke consider their own masters worthy of complete honor, so that the name of the Lord and the teaching may not be blasphemed.

**6:2** Now those who have believing masters should not look down on them because they are brothers; rather, they should serve all the more, because those who share in the benefit are believers and beloved. Teach and exhort these things.

**6:3** If anyone teaches otherwise and does not consent to the wholesome words of our Lord Jesus Christ and to the teaching that is according to godliness,

**6:4** he is arrogant, knowing nothing, but ailing over controversies and battles of words, from which spring jealousies, quarrels, slanders, evil suspicions,

**6:5** wranglings of people who are corrupted in mind and deprived of the truth, supposing godliness to be gain.

**6:6** Godliness with contentment, however, is great gain.

**6:7** For we brought nothing into this world, and there is no doubt that we cannot carry anything out either.

**6:8** Now having nourishment and covering, let us be content with these.

**6:9** For those who want to become rich fall into temptation, and into the snare of the devil, and into many useless and harmful desires that sink people into ruin and perdition.

**6:10** For greed is the root of all evils; craving it, some have wandered from the faith and have entangled themselves in many griefs.

**6:11** But you, man of God, flee these things; instead, pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, gentleness.

**6:12** Contend in the good contest of the faith; take hold of eternal life, in which you were called and confessed the good confession before many witnesses.

**6:13** I charge you before God, who makes all things alive, and before Christ Jesus, who rendered testimony under Pontius Pilate, the good confession,

**6:14** that you keep the commandment spotless, above reproach, until the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ,

**6:15** which the blessed and only Mighty One will display at His own times, the King of kings and Lord of those who rule,

**6:16** who alone has immortality and inhabits inaccessible light, whom no one among mankind has seen, nor indeed can see; to whom be honor and everlasting sovereignty. Amen.

**6:17** Command the rich of this age not to be haughty, nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches, but in the living God (who supplies us with all things abundantly to enjoy),

**6:18** to do good, to become rich in good works, to give readily, to share,

**6:19** to store up for themselves a good foundation for the future, so that they may take hold of true life.

**6:20** Timothy, guard the deposit, avoiding profane novelties of speech and contradictions of falsely named knowledge,

**6:21** by professing which, some have fallen away concerning the faith. Grace be with you. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — The Vulgate reads nomen Domini ("the name of the Lord"), whereas the Greek text reads "the name of God."
- **6:7** — The Vulgate reads haud dubium quod ("there is no doubt that") and hunc mundum ("this world"), reflecting textual variants where many modern Greek editions omit "it is certain/doubtless" and "this."
- **6:9** — The Vulgate adds diaboli ("of the devil") to "the snare," whereas critical Greek editions read simply "into a snare."
- **6:10** — Latin cupiditas ("greed" or "covetousness") is broader than the Greek philargyria ("love of money"), and inseruerunt se literally means "entangled/involved themselves in" rather than the Greek "pierced themselves."
- **6:20** — The Vulgate reads profanas vocum novitates ("profane novelties of speech"), reflecting the Greek variant kainophonias ("new-fangled speech") rather than kenophonias ("empty chatter").
