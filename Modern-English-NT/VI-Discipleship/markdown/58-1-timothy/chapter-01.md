## Text

**1:1** Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ, according to the command of God our Savior and Christ Jesus our hope,

**1:2** to Timothy, beloved son in the faith. Grace, mercy, and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord.

**1:3** Just as I asked you to remain in Ephesus when I was going into Macedonia, so that you might charge certain people not to teach differently,

**1:4** nor pay attention to myths and endless genealogies, which produce disputes rather than the edification of God that is in faith.

**1:5** Now the goal of the charge is love from a pure heart, a good conscience, and a faith without pretense.

**1:6** Straying from these things, some have turned aside into empty talk,

**1:7** wanting to be teachers of the law, understanding neither what they say nor the things about which they make assertions.

**1:8** Now we know that the law is good, if anyone uses it lawfully,

**1:9** knowing this, that the law is not laid down for a righteous person, but for the unrighteous and insubordinate, the ungodly and sinners, the wicked and defiled, killers of fathers and killers of mothers, murderers,

**1:10** fornicators, those who lie with males, kidnappers, liars and perjurers, and anything else that opposes sound teaching,

**1:11** which is according to the gospel of the glory of the blessed God, which was entrusted to me.

**1:12** I give thanks to Him who strengthened me, Christ Jesus our Lord, because He considered me faithful, placing me in ministry,

**1:13** though previously I was a blasphemer, a persecutor, and an insolent man. Yet I obtained the mercy of God, because I acted ignorantly in unbelief.

**1:14** And the grace of our Lord overflowed exceedingly, with faith and the love that is in Christ Jesus.

**1:15** A trustworthy saying and worthy of complete acceptance: that Christ Jesus came into this world to save sinners, of whom I am the first.

**1:16** Yet for this reason I obtained mercy, so that in me first Christ Jesus might show all patience, for the instruction of those who are going to believe in Him for eternal life.

**1:17** Now to the King of the ages, immortal, invisible, the only God, honor and glory for the ages of ages. Amen.

**1:18** This charge I entrust to you, son Timothy, according to the preceding prophecies concerning you, so that in them you may wage good warfare,

**1:19** having faith and a good conscience; rejecting this, some have suffered shipwreck regarding the faith,

**1:20** among whom are Hymenaeus and Alexander, whom I delivered to Satan so that they may learn not to blaspheme.

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — Vulgate reads 'dilecto' ('beloved'), whereas the Greek text has 'gnesio' ('true' or 'genuine').
- **1:4** — Vulgate reads 'aedificationem Dei' ('the edification of God'), reflecting a Greek textual variant ('oikodomen') rather than the standard Greek reading 'oikonomian' ('stewardship' or 'administration').
- **1:13** — Clementine Vulgate explicitly includes 'Dei' ('of God') after 'mercy', which is absent from the Greek text.
- **1:15** — Vulgate specifies 'in hunc mundum' ('into this world'), whereas the Greek text reads simply 'into the world'.
- **1:16** — Latin 'ad informationem' conveys instruction, formation, or modeling, rendering Greek 'hypotyposis' ('outline' or 'pattern').
