## Text

**2:1** I urge, therefore, first of all, that supplications, prayers, petitions, and thanksgivings be made for all people,

**2:2** for kings and all who are in high positions, so that we may live a quiet and tranquil life in all godliness and chastity.

**2:3** For this is good and acceptable before God our Savior,

**2:4** who wills that all people be saved and come to the knowledge of the truth.

**2:5** For there is one God, and also one mediator between God and mankind, the man Christ Jesus,

**2:6** who gave Himself as a redemption for all, a testimony in its own times,

**2:7** for which I myself was appointed a preacher and an apostle (I speak the truth; I do not lie), a teacher of the Gentiles in faith and truth.

**2:8** Therefore I want the men to pray in every place, lifting up pure hands without anger and dispute.

**2:9** Likewise also, women should be in respectable attire, adorning themselves with modesty and sobriety, and not with braided hair, or gold, or pearls, or costly clothing,

**2:10** but with what is fitting for women, professing godliness through good works.

**2:11** Let a woman learn in silence, with all submission.

**2:12** But I do not permit a woman to teach, nor to exercise dominion over a man, but to be in silence.

**2:13** For Adam was formed first, and then Eve;

**2:14** and Adam was not deceived, but the woman, having been deceived, was in transgression.

**2:15** Yet she will be saved through the bearing of children, if she remains in faith, love, and sanctification, with sobriety.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — Vulgate uses 'castitate' ('chastity') here and in 3:4 where the Greek text has 'semnoteti' ('dignity' or 'reverence').
- **2:8** — Vulgate reads 'puras manus' ('pure hands'), whereas the Greek text has 'hosious' ('holy' or 'devout').
- **2:11** — Vulgate renders Greek 'hesychia' ('quietness' or 'stillness') specifically as 'silentium' ('silence') here and in 2:12.
- **2:15** — Clementine Vulgate keeps the singular verb 'permanserit' ('if she remains'), whereas the Greek text shifts to the plural ('if they continue').
