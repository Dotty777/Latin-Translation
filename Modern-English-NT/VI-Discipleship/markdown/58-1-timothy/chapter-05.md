## Text

**5:1** Do not reprimand an older man, but appeal to him as a father, young men as brothers,

**5:2** elderly women as mothers, young women as sisters, in complete purity.

**5:3** Honor widows who are genuinely widows.

**5:4** But if any widow has children or grandchildren, let her first learn to govern her own household and to make a return in kind to her parents, for this is pleasing before God.

**5:5** Now let the one who is genuinely a widow and left alone hope in God and persist in supplications and prayers night and day.

**5:6** For she who lives in pleasure is dead while living.

**5:7** And command this, so that they may be above reproach.

**5:8** Yet if anyone does not care for his own, and especially for his household members, he has disowned the faith and is worse than an unbeliever.

**5:9** Let a widow be chosen at not less than sixty years old, who has been the wife of one husband,

**5:10** having testimony in good works: if she raised children, if she extended hospitality, if she washed the feet of the saints, if she provided for those suffering affliction, if she followed after every good work.

**5:11** But avoid younger widows; for when they grow self-indulgent in Christ, they want to marry,

**5:12** having condemnation because they have nullified their first faith.

**5:13** At the same time, being idle, they also learn to make the rounds of houses; not only idle, but also talkative and meddlesome, speaking what is not proper.

**5:14** Therefore I want the younger women to marry, to bear children, to run a household, to give no opportunity to the adversary for reviling.

**5:15** For some have already turned back after Satan.

**5:16** If any believer has widows, let him provide for them, and let the church not be burdened, so that it may have enough for those who are genuinely widows.

**5:17** Let the elders who preside well be regarded as worthy of double honor, especially those who toil in word and teaching.

**5:18** For Scripture says, "You shall not bind the mouth of an ox that is threshing," and, "The worker is worthy of his pay."

**5:19** Do not accept an accusation against an elder unless supported by two or three witnesses.

**5:20** As for those who sin, reprove them before everyone, so that the rest may also have fear.

**5:21** I solemnly urge you before God and Christ Jesus and the chosen angels to observe these things without prejudgment, doing nothing by leaning to one side.

**5:22** Do not hastily lay hands on anyone, and do not share in the sins of others. Keep yourself pure.

**5:23** Do not keep drinking water, but take a little wine because of your stomach and your frequent weaknesses.

**5:24** The sins of some people are evident, going ahead to judgment, while they also follow after others.

**5:25** Likewise good deeds are also evident, and those that are otherwise cannot be hidden.

## Translator Notes

- **5:4** — The Vulgate uses the singular verb discat ("let her learn") and regere ("to govern"), referring to the widow herself, whereas the Greek has the plural manthanetosan ... eusebein ("let them [the children or grandchildren] learn to show godliness").
- **5:5** — The Vulgate uses jussive subjunctives (speret ... et instet, "let her hope ... and persist"), whereas the Greek text uses indicative verbs ("has set her hope ... and continues").
- **5:11** — The Vulgate reads luxuriatae fuerint in Christo ("grow self-indulgent/wanton in Christ"), whereas the Greek means "grow wanton against Christ." In the context of 5:9-11, devita ("avoid") means refraining from enrolling younger widows on the church's list.
- **5:12** — Latin primam fidem literally means "first faith" and is widely understood here as an initial pledge or vow of devotion.
- **5:16** — The Vulgate reads the masculine/generic Si quis fidelis ("If any believer"), whereas the critical Greek text has the feminine ei tis piste ("if any believing woman").
