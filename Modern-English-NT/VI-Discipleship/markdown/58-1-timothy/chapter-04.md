## Text

**4:1** Now the Spirit clearly says that in the last times some will leave the faith, giving attention to spirits of error and to teachings of demons,

**4:2** in the hypocrisy of those who speak falsehood and have their own conscience seared,

**4:3** who forbid marrying and require abstaining from foods, which God created to be received with thanksgiving by believers and by those who have come to know the truth.

**4:4** For every created thing of God is good, and nothing that is received with thanksgiving is to be rejected;

**4:5** for it is made holy through the word of God and prayer.

**4:6** By setting these things before the brothers, you will be a good servant of Christ Jesus, nourished by the words of the faith and of the good teaching that you have followed.

**4:7** But avoid silly and old wives' tales; instead, exercise yourself for godliness.

**4:8** For physical exercise is useful to a small extent, whereas godliness is useful for all things, having the promise of the life that now is and of the life to come.

**4:9** Trustworthy is the saying, and worthy of complete acceptance.

**4:10** For to this end we toil and are reviled, because we hope in the living God, who is the Savior of all people, especially of believers.

**4:11** Command these things and teach them.

**4:12** Let no one look down on your youth; instead, be an example for the believers in word, in conduct, in love, in faith, in purity.

**4:13** Until I come, give attention to reading, to exhortation, and to teaching.

**4:14** Do not neglect the grace that is in you, which was given to you through prophecy, with the laying on of the hands of the presbytery.

**4:15** Meditate on these things; devote yourself to them, so that your progress may be evident to everyone.

**4:16** Pay attention to yourself and to the teaching; persist in them. For in doing this, you will save both yourself and those who listen to you.

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — The Latin syntax is elliptical (zeugma), literally "forbidding to marry, to abstain from foods," with a positive verb such as "requiring" implied before "to abstain." The relative pronoun quod ("which") is neuter singular after plural cibis ("foods"), treating food collectively.
- **4:7** — The Vulgate reads ineptas ("silly" or "foolish"), giving a distinct nuance compared with the Greek bebelous ("profane" or "irreverent").
- **4:10** — The Vulgate reads maledicimur ("we are reviled"), matching the Greek textual variant oneidizometha, whereas many modern editions read agonizometha ("we strive").
- **4:14** — The Vulgate reads gratiam ("grace"), where the Greek text has charismatos ("gift").
