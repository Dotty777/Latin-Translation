## Text

**3:1** A trustworthy saying: if anyone desires the office of bishop, he desires a good work.

**3:2** Therefore a bishop must be irreproachable, the husband of one wife, sober, prudent, respectable, chaste, hospitable, a teacher,

**3:3** not given to wine, not violent, but forbearing, not quarrelsome, not greedy, but

**3:4** governing his own household well, having children in submission with all chastity.

**3:5** But if anyone does not know how to govern his own household, how will he care for the church of God?

**3:6** Not a new convert, so that, lifted up into pride, he may not fall into the judgment of the devil.

**3:7** Moreover, he must also have a good testimony from those who are outside, so that he may not fall into reproach and into the snare of the devil.

**3:8** Deacons likewise must be chaste, not double-tongued, not addicted to much wine, not pursuing shameful gain,

**3:9** holding the mystery of the faith in a pure conscience.

**3:10** And let these also be tested first, and then let them serve, having no charge against them.

**3:11** Women likewise must be chaste, not slanderous, sober, faithful in all things.

**3:12** Let deacons be husbands of one wife, who govern their own children and their own households well.

**3:13** For those who have served well will acquire for themselves a good standing and great confidence in the faith that is in Christ Jesus.

**3:14** I am writing these things to you, hoping to come to you soon;

**3:15** but if I am delayed, so that you may know how you ought to conduct yourself in the house of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and support of the truth.

**3:16** And manifestly great is the mystery of godliness, which was manifested in the flesh, was justified in the spirit, appeared to angels, was preached to the Gentiles, was believed in the world, was taken up in glory.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — Vulgate repeats the same verb 'desiderat' ('desires') in both clauses, whereas the Greek text uses two different verbs ('aspires to' and 'desires').
- **3:2** — Clementine Vulgate includes 'pudicum' ('chaste') alongside 'ornatum' ('respectable') where the Greek has a single adjective ('kosmion'), and renders Greek 'didaktikon' ('able to teach') with the noun 'doctorem' ('a teacher').
- **3:11** — Latin 'mulieres' can denote either women serving alongside deacons or the wives of deacons.
- **3:15** — Vulgate specifies the second-person singular 'oporteat te' ('how you ought'), whereas the Greek clause is impersonal ('how one ought').
- **3:16** — Vulgate renders Greek 'mysterion' with 'sacramentum' here (in contrast to 'mysterium' in 3:9) and follows it with the neuter relative 'quod' ('which') agreeing with 'sacramentum', rather than the Greek masculine 'hos' ('he who') or the variant 'theos' ('God').
