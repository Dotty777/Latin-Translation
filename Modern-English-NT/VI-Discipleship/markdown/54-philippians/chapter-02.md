## Text

**2:1** If therefore there is any consolation in Christ, if any comfort of love, if any fellowship of the Spirit, if any depths of compassion,

**2:2** make my joy full so that you share the same mind, having the same love, united in soul, thinking the very same thing,

**2:3** nothing through rivalry nor through empty glory, but in humility considering one another superior to yourselves,

**2:4** each one looking not to the things that are his own, but to those of others.

**2:5** For have this mindset in yourselves, which was also in Christ Jesus,

**2:6** who, although He was in the form of God, did not consider it robbery for Himself to be equal to God,

**2:7** but emptied Himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in outward appearance as a man.

**2:8** He humbled Himself, becoming obedient all the way to death; indeed, death on a cross.

**2:9** For this reason God also exalted Him and granted to Him the name that is above every name,

**2:10** so that in the name of Jesus every knee may bend, of those in heaven, on earth, and under the earth,

**2:11** and every tongue may confess that the Lord Jesus Christ is in the glory of God the Father.

**2:12** Therefore, my dearest ones, just as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but much more now in my absence, work out your salvation with fear and trembling.

**2:13** For it is God who works in you both to will and to accomplish for good will.

**2:14** Now do all things without grumblings and hesitations,

**2:15** so that you may be blameless and guileless children of God, without reproach in the midst of a depraved and perverse nation, among whom you shine like lights in the world,

**2:16** holding fast the word of life for my glory on the day of Christ, because I did not run in vain nor toil in vain.

**2:17** Yet even if I am offered in sacrifice upon the sacrifice and service of your faith, I rejoice and rejoice together with all of you.

**2:18** And in the very same way, you also rejoice, and rejoice together with me.

**2:19** Now I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you soon, so that I also may be in good spirits, once I have learned the things concerning you.

**2:20** For I have no one so kindred in soul who will be concerned for you with sincere affection.

**2:21** For all seek the things that are their own, not those of Jesus Christ.

**2:22** But know his proven worth, that just as a son serves a father, he served with me in the gospel.

**2:23** Therefore I hope to send him to you as soon as I see the things concerning me.

**2:24** And I am confident in the Lord that I myself will also come to you soon.

**2:25** Yet I deemed it necessary to send to you Epaphroditus, my brother and coworker and fellow soldier, and your apostle and minister to my need,

**2:26** since indeed he was longing for all of you and was distressed because you had heard that he had fallen ill.

**2:27** For indeed he fell ill to the point of death, but God had mercy on him, and not on him alone, but also on me, so that I would not have sorrow upon sorrow.

**2:28** Therefore I have sent him all the more promptly, so that upon seeing him you may rejoice again, and I may be without sorrow.

**2:29** Welcome him, therefore, with all joy in the Lord, and hold such men in honor,

**2:30** because for the work of Christ he drew near even to death, handing over his life so that he might fill up what was lacking on your part toward service to me.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The Vulgate joins 'depths of compassion' as a genitive phrase (viscera miserationis, literally 'inmost depths of compassion'), whereas the Greek has two coordinate nouns ('affection and sympathy').
- **2:6** — Latin 'rapinam' denotes 'robbery,' 'plunder,' or 'something forcibly seized'; the clause is understood in Latin tradition either as Christ not regarding equality with God as usurpation or not clinging to it as a prize.
- **2:11** — In a notable Vulgate-distinct reading, the Latin renders the confession as 'that the Lord Jesus Christ is in the glory of God the Father' (quia Dominus Jesus Christus in gloria est Dei Patris), rather than the Greek 'that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father'.
- **2:17** — The Vulgate uses 'immolor' ('am offered in sacrifice'), whereas the Greek verb refers specifically to being poured out as a drink offering.
- **2:30** — The Vulgate reads 'handing over his life' (tradens animam suam), whereas Greek manuscripts read 'risking' or 'disregarding his life'.
