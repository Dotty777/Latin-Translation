## Text

**4:1** Therefore, my brothers, most beloved and most longed for, my joy and my crown: in this way stand firm in the Lord, most beloved.

**4:2** I urge Euodia and I implore Syntyche to be of the very same mind in the Lord.

**4:3** Yes, I urge you also, genuine companion, help those women who labored with me in the gospel, along with Clement and the rest of my helpers, whose names are in the book of life.

**4:4** Rejoice in the Lord always; once more I say, rejoice.

**4:5** Let your moderation be known to all people; the Lord is near.

**4:6** Be anxious about nothing, but in every prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests become known before God.

**4:7** And may the peace of God, which exceeds all understanding, guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.

**4:8** For the rest, brothers, whatever things are true, whatever things are modest, whatever things are just, whatever things are holy, whatever things are lovely, whatever things are of good reputation, if there is any virtue, if there is any praise of discipline, reflect on these things.

**4:9** The things that you have both learned and received and heard and seen in me, do these things; and the God of peace will be with you.

**4:10** Now I rejoiced greatly in the Lord because at long last you have flourished again in being concerned for me, just as indeed you were concerned, but you were occupied.

**4:11** Not as though I speak on account of want, for I have learned, in whatever condition I am, to be content.

**4:12** I know how to be humbled, and I know how to abound (everywhere and in all things I have been instructed): both to be filled and to be hungry, both to abound and to endure want.

**4:13** I am able to do all things in Him who strengthens me.

**4:14** Nevertheless, you have done well by sharing in my tribulation.

**4:15** Now you also know, Philippians, that at the beginning of the gospel, when I departed from Macedonia, no church shared with me in an account of giving and receiving, except you alone;

**4:16** for even to Thessalonica, once and twice, you sent for my use.

**4:17** Not that I seek the gift, but I look for the fruit abounding in your account.

**4:18** Now I have all things, and I abound; I am filled, having received from Epaphroditus the things you sent: a fragrance of sweetness, an accepted sacrifice, pleasing to God.

**4:19** And may my God fill every desire of yours according to His riches in glory in Christ Jesus.

**4:20** Now to God and our Father be glory forever and ever. Amen.

**4:21** Greet every saint in Christ Jesus.

**4:22** The brothers who are with me greet you. All the saints greet you, and especially those who are from Caesar's household.

**4:23** The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **4:6** — The Vulgate construes omni ("every") with oratione ("in every prayer and supplication"), rather than taking Greek en panti independently as "in everything."
- **4:7** — The Vulgate uses the subjunctive custodiat ("may ... guard"), framing the clause as a blessing rather than the Greek future indicative ("will guard").
- **4:8** — The Vulgate renders Greek semna ("honorable") as pudica ("modest") and hagna ("pure") as sancta ("holy"), and adds disciplinae ("of discipline") after laus ("praise").
- **4:10** — The Vulgate renders Greek ekaireisthe ("you lacked opportunity") as occupati eratis ("you were occupied").
- **4:19** — The Vulgate uses the subjunctive impleat ("may ... fill") rather than the Greek future indicative ("will fill") and reads desiderium ("desire") where the Greek has chreian ("need").
