## Text

**1:1** Paul and Timothy, servants of Jesus Christ, to all the saints in Christ Jesus who are at Philippi, together with the bishops and deacons.

**1:2** Grace to you, and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:3** I thank my God in every remembrance of you,

**1:4** always in all my prayers for all of you, making petition with joy,

**1:5** because of your participation in the gospel of Christ from the first day until now,

**1:6** having confidence in this very thing, that He who began a good work in you will complete it until the day of Christ Jesus,

**1:7** just as it is right for me to think this concerning all of you, because I hold you in my heart, and because both in my chains and in the defense and confirmation of the gospel, all of you are sharers of my joy.

**1:8** For God is my witness how I yearn for all of you in the deep affection of Jesus Christ.

**1:9** And I pray this, that your love may abound more and more in knowledge and in all perception,

**1:10** so that you may approve what is superior, that you may be sincere and without offense for the day of Christ,

**1:11** filled with the fruit of righteousness through Jesus Christ, for the glory and praise of God.

**1:12** Now I want you to know, brothers, that the things concerning me have rather led to the progress of the gospel,

**1:13** so that my chains became evident in Christ throughout the whole praetorium and among all the rest,

**1:14** and most of the brothers, confident in the Lord because of my chains, dared all the more abundantly to speak the word of God without fear.

**1:15** Some indeed preach Christ even because of envy and rivalry, and some also because of good will;

**1:16** some out of love, knowing that I have been placed for the defense of the gospel.

**1:17** But some out of rivalry proclaim Christ without sincerity, supposing that they stir up distress for my chains.

**1:18** What then? Provided that in every way, whether through pretext or through truth, Christ is proclaimed, in this too I rejoice, and indeed I will rejoice.

**1:19** For I know that this will result in salvation for me through your prayer and the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ,

**1:20** according to my expectation and hope that in nothing I will be put to shame, but in all confidence, as always, now also Christ will be magnified in my body, whether through life or through death.

**1:21** For to me, living is Christ, and dying is gain.

**1:22** But if I am to live in the flesh, this is the fruit of work for me, and what I should choose I do not know.

**1:23** But I am hard-pressed between the two, having the desire to be released and to be with Christ, which is far, far better;

**1:24** yet to remain in the flesh is necessary because of you.

**1:25** And confident of this, I know that I will remain and continue with all of you for your progress and joy of faith,

**1:26** so that your rejoicing may abound in Christ Jesus in me through my coming again to you.

**1:27** Only live worthily of the gospel of Christ, so that whether when I come and see you or while absent, I may hear about you that you stand in one spirit, united in soul, laboring together for the faith of the gospel,

**1:28** and are in nothing terrified by adversaries, which to them is a cause of destruction, but to you of salvation, and this from God;

**1:29** because it has been granted to you for Christ, not only to believe in Him, but also to suffer for Him,

**1:30** having the same struggle that you both saw in me and have now heard about me.

## Translator Notes

- **1:5** — The Clementine Vulgate reads 'in the gospel of Christ' (in Evangelio Christi), adding 'of Christ' where Greek manuscripts read simply 'in the gospel'.
- **1:7** — The Vulgate reads 'sharers of my joy' (socios gaudii mei), differing from the Greek reading 'partakers of grace with me'.
- **1:8** — Latin 'in visceribus' literally means 'in the inmost organs,' a biblical idiom for the deepest seat of affection and compassion.
- **1:28** — The Vulgate reads 'a cause of destruction' (causa perditionis), whereas the Greek reads 'a sign' or 'proof of destruction'.
- **1:30** — The Vulgate uses the perfect tense 'audistis' ('have heard'), whereas the Greek uses the present tense ('hear').
