## Text

**3:1** For the rest, my brothers, rejoice in the Lord. To write the same things to you is indeed not tedious for me, but for you it is necessary.

**3:2** Watch out for the dogs, watch out for the evil workers, watch out for the mutilation.

**3:3** For we are the circumcision, who serve God in spirit, and boast in Christ Jesus, and have no trust in the flesh,

**3:4** although I myself have confidence even in the flesh. If anyone else seems to have confidence in the flesh, I even more:

**3:5** circumcised on the eighth day, from the lineage of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews; according to the law, a Pharisee;

**3:6** according to zeal, persecuting the church of God; according to the righteousness that is in the law, having lived without reproach.

**3:7** But the things that were gains for me, these I have considered losses on account of Christ.

**3:8** Yet indeed, I regard all things to be a loss on account of the surpassing knowledge of Jesus Christ my Lord, on account of whom I have incurred the loss of all things and consider them as dung, so that I may gain Christ,

**3:9** and be found in Him, not having my own righteousness, which is from the law, but that which is from the faith of Christ Jesus, the righteousness that is from God in faith,

**3:10** so as to know Him, and the power of His resurrection, and the sharing of His sufferings, being conformed to His death,

**3:11** if somehow I may arrive at the resurrection that is from the dead;

**3:12** not that I have already received it or am already perfected; yet I pursue, if somehow I may take hold of that in which I have also been taken hold of by Christ Jesus.

**3:13** Brothers, I do not consider myself to have taken hold of it. One thing, however: forgetting indeed the things that are behind, and stretching myself out toward the things that are ahead,

**3:14** I press onward toward the appointed goal, toward the prize of the heavenly calling of God in Christ Jesus.

**3:15** Therefore, as many of us as are perfect, let us think this way; and if in anything you are otherwise minded, God will reveal even this to you.

**3:16** Nevertheless, as far as we have reached, let us be of the same mind and remain in the same rule.

**3:17** Be imitators of me, brothers, and observe those who walk in this way, just as you have our pattern.

**3:18** For many walk, of whom I often told you (and now tell you even weeping) that they are enemies of the cross of Christ:

**3:19** whose end is destruction, whose god is their belly, and whose glory is in their own shame, who have their mind on earthly things.

**3:20** Our way of life, however, is in the heavens, from where we also await the Savior, our Lord Jesus Christ,

**3:21** who will reshape the body of our lowliness, conformed to the body of His splendor, according to the working by which He is able even to subject all things to Himself.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The Vulgate reads necessarium ("necessary"), whereas the Greek text has asphales ("safe" or "a safeguard").
- **3:3** — The Vulgate reads spiritu servimus Deo ("serve God in spirit"), matching the Greek dative reading Theo ("to God") rather than the genitive Theou ("by the Spirit of God") followed in most modern critical Greek editions.
- **3:16** — The Vulgate preserves the longer traditional reading ut idem sapiamus, et in eadem permaneamus regula ("let us be of the same mind and remain in the same rule"), which modern critical Greek editions shorten.
- **3:20** — The Vulgate renders Greek politeuma ("citizenship" or "commonwealth") with conversatio ("way of life" or "conduct," echoing conversatus in 3:6) and includes nostrum ("our") before Jesum Christum.
