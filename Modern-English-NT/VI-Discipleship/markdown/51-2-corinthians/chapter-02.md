## Text

**2:1** But I resolved this very thing within myself, not to come to you again in sorrow.

**2:2** For if I make you sorrowful, then who is there to make me glad, except the one who is made sorrowful by me?

**2:3** And I wrote this very thing to you, so that when I come I may not have sorrow upon sorrow from those over whom I ought to have rejoiced, having confidence in all of you that my joy belongs to all of you.

**2:4** For out of much affliction and anguish of heart I wrote to you through many tears, not that you might be made sorrowful, but that you might know what love I have more abundantly toward you.

**2:5** Now if anyone has caused sorrow, he has not caused me sorrow, but in part, so that I may not burden all of you.

**2:6** Sufficient for such a one is this rebuke that comes from the majority,

**2:7** so that on the contrary you should rather forgive and console him, in order that such a one may not perhaps be swallowed up by excessive sorrow.

**2:8** For this reason I implore you to confirm love toward him.

**2:9** For to this end I also wrote, so that I might know the proof of you, whether you are obedient in all things.

**2:10** Now whomever you have forgiven anything, I also do; for what I too have forgiven, if I have forgiven anything, has been for your sake in the person of Christ,

**2:11** so that we may not be outmaneuvered by Satan; for we are not unaware of his thoughts.

**2:12** Now when I had come to Troas for the gospel of Christ, and a door had been opened for me in the Lord,

**2:13** I had no rest for my spirit, because I did not find Titus my brother, but bidding them farewell, I set out for Macedonia.

**2:14** Now thanks be to God, who always leads us in triumph in Christ Jesus and manifests the aroma of His knowledge through us in every place;

**2:15** for we are a pleasing aroma of Christ to God among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing:

**2:16** to some indeed an aroma of death to death, but to others an aroma of life to life. And for these things, who is so qualified?

**2:17** For we are not like very many, adulterating the word of God, but out of sincerity, but as from God, before God, we speak in Christ.

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — The Clementine Vulgate reads tristitiam super tristitiam ("sorrow upon sorrow"), whereas most Greek manuscripts read simply "sorrow."
- **2:5** — In the Vulgate punctuation, omnes vos ("all of you") serves as the direct object of onerem ("so that I may not burden all of you"), whereas Greek editions often treat "not to be too severe" as parenthetical.
- **2:10** — Latin in persona Christi ("in the person of Christ") renders Greek en prosopo Christou ("in the presence/face of Christ").
- **2:16** — The Vulgate adds tam ("so") before idoneus ("qualified"), where the Greek asks simply "who is sufficient?" In 2:17, Latin adulterantes ("adulterating") renders Greek kapeleuontes ("peddling/huckstering").
