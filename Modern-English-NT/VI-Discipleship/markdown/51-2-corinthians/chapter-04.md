## Text

**4:1** Therefore, having the ministry, just as we have obtained mercy, we do not grow faint,

**4:2** but we renounce the hidden things of disgrace, not walking in cunning, nor adulterating the word of God, but by the manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every human conscience before God.

**4:3** And even if our gospel is covered, it is covered among those who are perishing,

**4:4** among whom the god of this age has blinded the minds of unbelievers, so that the illumination of the gospel of the glory of Christ, who is the image of God, may not shine upon them.

**4:5** For we do not proclaim ourselves, but Jesus Christ our Lord, and ourselves as your servants through Jesus;

**4:6** because God, who said that light should shine out of darkness, has Himself shone in our hearts for the illumination of the knowledge of the radiance of God, in the face of Christ Jesus.

**4:7** But we have this treasure in clay vessels, so that the preeminence may be of the power of God, and not from us.

**4:8** In all things we suffer affliction, but we are not hemmed in; we are perplexed, but we are not left destitute;

**4:9** we suffer persecution, but we are not abandoned; we are thrown down, but we do not perish,

**4:10** always carrying around the dying of Jesus in our body, so that the life of Jesus also may be manifested in our bodies.

**4:11** For we who live are always handed over to death on account of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus also may be manifested in our mortal flesh.

**4:12** Thus death is at work in us, but life in you.

**4:13** Now having the same spirit of faith, just as it is written, "I believed, for which reason I spoke," we also believe, for which reason we also speak,

**4:14** knowing that He who raised Jesus will raise us also with Jesus, and will set us with you.

**4:15** For all things are on account of you, so that grace, abounding through many in thanksgiving, may abound to the glory of God.

**4:16** For this reason we do not grow faint; but even though our outward man is decaying, yet the one within is being renewed from day to day.

**4:17** For what is presently momentary and light in our affliction produces in us, beyond measure in sublimity, an eternal weight of glory,

**4:18** while we do not gaze at the things that are seen, but at the things that are not seen. For the things that are seen are temporary, whereas the things that are not seen are eternal.

## Translator Notes

- **4:4** — The critical text capitalizes Deus; some ancient Latin interpreters joined hujus sæculi to infidelium ("God has blinded the minds of the unbelievers of this age"), while others read Deus hujus sæculi as "the god of this age."
- **4:5** — The Vulgate explicitly reads Dominum nostrum ("our Lord") where critical Greek texts have simply "Lord," and uses per Jesum ("through Jesus") rather than "for Jesus' sake" (contrast propter Jesum in 4:11).
- **4:10** — The Vulgate shifts from singular in corpore nostro ("in our body") to plural in corporibus nostris ("in our bodies"), whereas the Greek keeps the singular in both clauses.
- **4:15** — The Vulgate makes gratia abundans ("abounding grace") the subject of abundet ("may abound") with in gratiarum actione ("in thanksgiving") as a prepositional modifier, whereas the Greek makes thanksgiving the object or subject of abounding.
- **4:17** — The Vulgate reads in nobis ("in us") rather than the Greek "for us," and renders the emphatic Greek expression as supra modum in sublimitate ("beyond measure in sublimity").
