## Text

**11:1** If only you would bear with a little of my foolishness; indeed, bear with me as well.

**11:2** For I am jealous for you with the jealousy of God. For I have betrothed you to one husband, to present a chaste virgin to Christ.

**11:3** Yet I fear that, just as the serpent deceived Eve by his craftiness, so your minds may be corrupted and fall away from the simplicity that is in Christ.

**11:4** For if the one who comes preaches another Christ, whom we did not preach, or you receive another spirit, which you did not receive, or another gospel, which you did not accept, you would rightly endure it.

**11:5** For I consider that I have done nothing less than the great apostles.

**11:6** For even if I am unskilled in speech, yet not in knowledge, still in all things we have been made manifest to you.

**11:7** Or did I commit a sin, humbling myself so that you might be exalted, because I preached the gospel of God to you free of charge?

**11:8** I plundered other churches, receiving wages for your service.

**11:9** And when I was with you and was in need, I was burdensome to no one; for what was lacking to me, the brothers who came from Macedonia supplied; and in all things I kept myself without being a burden to you, and I will keep myself so.

**11:10** The truth of Christ is in me, that this boasting will not be broken in me in the regions of Achaia.

**11:11** Why? Because I do not love you? God knows.

**11:12** Yet what I am doing, I will also do, so that I may cut off the opportunity of those who desire an opportunity, so that in what they boast about, they may be found just as we also are.

**11:13** For such false apostles are deceitful workers, transforming themselves into apostles of Christ.

**11:14** And it is no wonder, for Satan himself transforms himself into an angel of light.

**11:15** Therefore it is no great thing if his servants are transformed as servants of righteousness, whose end will be according to their own works.

**11:16** Again I say (let no one think me to be foolish; otherwise receive me as a foolish person, so that I too may boast a little),

**11:17** what I speak, I do not speak according to God, but as if in foolishness, in this basis of boasting.

**11:18** Since many boast according to the flesh, I also will boast.

**11:19** For you gladly put up with foolish people, since you yourselves are wise.

**11:20** For you bear with it if anyone brings you into slavery, if anyone devours, if anyone takes, if anyone exalts himself, if anyone strikes you in the face.

**11:21** I speak by way of disgrace, as though we have been weak in this respect. In whatever anyone dares (I speak in foolishness), I dare as well.

**11:22** They are Hebrews; so am I. They are Israelites; so am I. They are the seed of Abraham; so am I.

**11:23** They are servants of Christ (I speak as one less wise); I am more: in very many labors, in prisons more abundantly, in beatings beyond measure, in deaths frequently.

**11:24** From the Jews five times I received forty lashes minus one.

**11:25** Three times I was beaten with rods, once I was stoned, three times I suffered shipwreck, a night and a day I was in the depth of the sea,

**11:26** on journeys often, in dangers from rivers, dangers from bandits, dangers from my own people, dangers from the Gentiles, dangers in the city, dangers in the wilderness, dangers at sea, dangers among false brothers,

**11:27** in labor and hardship, in many vigils, in hunger and thirst, in many fastings, in cold and nakedness,

**11:28** besides those things that are external, my daily pressure, the anxiety for all the churches.

**11:29** Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is made to stumble, and I do not burn?

**11:30** If it is necessary to boast, I will boast of the things that belong to my weakness.

**11:31** The God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who is blessed forever, knows that I am not lying.

**11:32** In Damascus, the governor of the people of King Aretas was guarding the city of the Damascenes in order to arrest me,

**11:33** and through a window I was let down in a basket down the wall, and thus I escaped his hands.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — Clementine Vulgate prints the infinitive supportare rather than the imperative supportate, translated here according to its hortatory sense ("bear with me as well").
- **11:3** — Vulgate adds et excidant ("and fall away") and reads in Christo ("in Christ"), without the Greek addition "and purity" found in some manuscripts.
- **11:4** — Vulgate reads alium Christum ("another Christ"), whereas the Greek reads allon Iesoun ("another Jesus").
- **11:17** — Vulgate reads secundum Deum ("according to God"), whereas the Greek reads kata Kyrion ("according to the Lord").
- **11:23** — Latin renders Greek paraphronon ("speaking like a madman") more softly as ut minus sapiens ("as one less wise").
