## Text

**12:1** If it is necessary to boast (it is indeed not profitable), yet I will come to visions and revelations of the Lord.

**12:2** I know a man in Christ fourteen years ago (whether in the body I do not know, or outside the body I do not know; God knows), such a one caught up all the way to the third heaven.

**12:3** And I know such a man (whether in the body or outside the body I do not know; God knows):

**12:4** that he was caught up into paradise and heard secret words, which it is not permitted for a man to speak.

**12:5** On behalf of such a one I will boast, yet on behalf of myself I will boast of nothing except in my weaknesses.

**12:6** For even if I should wish to boast, I will not be foolish, for I will speak the truth; yet I hold back, so that no one may reckon me above what he sees in me or anything he hears from me.

**12:7** And so that the greatness of the revelations may not exalt me, there was given to me a goad of my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me.

**12:8** For this reason I begged the Lord three times that it might depart from me.

**12:9** And He said to me, "My grace suffices for you, for power is brought to perfection in weakness." Gladly therefore I will boast in my weaknesses, so that the power of Christ may dwell in me.

**12:10** For this reason I am well pleased in my weaknesses, in insults, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses, for Christ; for when I am weak, then I am powerful.

**12:11** I have become foolish; you compelled me. For I ought to have been commended by you, for I was nothing less than those who are apostles beyond measure, even though I am nothing.

**12:12** Yet the signs of my apostleship were performed upon you in all patience, in signs and wonders and mighty deeds.

**12:13** For what is it in which you had less than the rest of the churches, except that I myself did not burden you? Forgive me this wrong.

**12:14** Look, this third time I am ready to come to you, and I will not be burdensome to you. For I do not seek the things that are yours, but you. For children ought not to store up treasure for parents, but parents for children.

**12:15** Yet I will most gladly spend, and myself be spent beyond measure for your souls, even though, loving you more, I am loved less.

**12:16** Yet let it be so: I did not burden you; still, being crafty, I caught you by deceit.

**12:17** Did I take advantage of you through any of those whom I sent to you?

**12:18** I asked Titus, and I sent the brother with him. Did Titus take advantage of you? Did we not walk in the same spirit? Not in the same footsteps?

**12:19** Have you been thinking all this time that we are defending ourselves before you? Before God in Christ we speak; yet all things, most beloved, are for your building up.

**12:20** For I fear that perhaps when I come, I may not find you such as I wish, and I may be found by you such as you do not wish; that perhaps quarrels, jealousies, animosities, dissensions, slanders, whisperings, conceits, seditions may be among you;

**12:21** that again when I come, God may humble me before you, and I may mourn over many of those who sinned previously and have not repented over the impurity and fornication and shamelessness that they have practiced.

## Translator Notes

- **12:4** — Latin translates Greek arreta ("unutterable" or "inexpressible") as arcana ("secret" or "hidden").
- **12:7** — Latin renders the Greek "thorn in the flesh" with a genitive phrase, stimulus carnis meae ("a goad of my flesh").
- **12:9** — Latin nam virtus in infirmitate perficitur omits the possessive "My" before "power" (matching older Greek witnesses) and renders dynamis as virtus ("power").
- **12:17** — Clementine Vulgate prints quod (for expected quos, "whom") referring back to eorum ("of those").
- **12:21** — Latin non egerunt poenitentiam literally means "have not done repentance," the standard Vulgate idiom for Greek metanoeo ("repent").
