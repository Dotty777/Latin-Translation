## Text

**9:1** For regarding the ministry that is carried out for the saints, it is superfluous for me to write to you.

**9:2** For I know your ready mind, on account of which I boast about you before the Macedonians that Achaia too has been ready since the past year; and your zeal has stirred up very many.

**9:3** But I have sent the brothers so that what we boast about you may not be made empty in this respect, so that (just as I said) you may be ready;

**9:4** otherwise, when Macedonians come with me and find you unprepared, we (not to say you) would be put to shame in this substance.

**9:5** Therefore I considered it necessary to ask the brothers to go ahead to you and prepare beforehand for this previously promised blessing to be ready in this way, as a blessing, not as greed.

**9:6** Now I say this: the one who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and the one who sows in blessings will also reap from blessings.

**9:7** Each one, just as he has resolved in his heart, not out of sorrow or out of necessity; for God loves a cheerful giver.

**9:8** Moreover, God is able to make every grace abound in you, so that in all things, always having all sufficiency, you may abound in every good work,

**9:9** just as it is written: "He scattered, he gave to the poor; his righteousness remains forever and ever."

**9:10** Now He who supplies seed to the sower will also provide bread to eat, and will multiply your seed, and will increase the growth of the fruits of your righteousness,

**9:11** so that, enriched in all things, you may abound in all simplicity, which produces through us thanksgiving to God.

**9:12** For the ministry of this service not only supplies the things that are lacking to the saints, but also abounds through many thanksgivings in the Lord,

**9:13** through the proof of this ministry, glorifying God in the obedience of your confession to the Gospel of Christ, and in the simplicity of sharing with them and with all,

**9:14** and in their supplication for you, as they long for you because of the surpassing grace of God in you.

**9:15** Thanks to God for His inexpressible gift.

## Translator Notes

- **9:4** — Vulgate in hac substantia ("in this substance") literally translates Greek en te hypostasei taute ("in this confidence / undertaking"); in Latin, substantia can denote either a basis of confidence or material means.
- **9:6** — Vulgate in benedictionibus ... de benedictionibus ("in blessings ... from blessings") preserves the literal biblical idiom for sowing and reaping bountifully.
- **9:10** — The Vulgate treats et panem ad manducandum præstabit ("will also provide bread to eat") as the first future main verb rather than part of the opening participle phrase, and expands Greek genemata ("harvest") into the double noun incrementa frugum ("the growth of the fruits").
- **9:12** — The Vulgate reads in Domino ("in the Lord"), whereas most Greek manuscripts read "to God" (to theo).
