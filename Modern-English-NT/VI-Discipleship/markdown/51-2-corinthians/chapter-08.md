## Text

**8:1** Now, brothers, we make known to you the grace of God that was given in the churches of Macedonia:

**8:2** that in a great test of affliction there was an abundance of their joy, and their deepest poverty abounded into the riches of their simplicity;

**8:3** for according to their ability, I bear witness for them, and beyond their ability, they were willing of their own accord,

**8:4** with much urging imploring us for the grace and sharing of the ministry that is carried out for the saints.

**8:5** And not as we had hoped, but they gave their very selves first to the Lord, and then to us through the will of God,

**8:6** so that we asked Titus that, just as he began, so he would also complete in you this grace as well.

**8:7** But just as you abound in everything, in faith, and speech, and knowledge, and all earnestness, and moreover in your love toward us, may you also abound in this grace.

**8:8** I do not speak as one commanding, but through the earnestness of others proving also the good character of your love.

**8:9** For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that on account of you He became poor, although He was rich, so that through His poverty you might be rich.

**8:10** And I give advice in this matter, for this is beneficial to you, who began not only to act, but also to be willing, from the previous year;

**8:11** now indeed complete it in action as well, so that just as the willing mind is ready, so it may also be ready to complete it out of what you have.

**8:12** For if the willingness is ready, it is acceptable according to what one has, not according to what one does not have.

**8:13** For it is not that there should be relief for others and affliction for you, but out of equality.

**8:14** At the present time may your abundance supply their need, so that their abundance too may be a supply for your need, so that there may be equality, just as it is written:

**8:15** "The one who had much did not have too much, and the one who had little did not fall short."

**8:16** Now thanks to God, who put the same earnestness for you in the heart of Titus,

**8:17** for he indeed accepted the urging, yet being even more earnest, he set out to you of his own free will.

**8:18** We also sent along with him the brother whose praise is in the Gospel throughout all the churches;

**8:19** and not only that, but he was also appointed by the churches as the companion of our travels in this grace that is being administered by us for the glory of the Lord and our resolved will,

**8:20** avoiding this, so that no one may blame us in this fullness that is being administered by us.

**8:21** For we take care for what is good not only before God, but also before people.

**8:22** Now we also sent with them our brother, whom we have often proved in many things to be earnest, and now much more earnest through great confidence in you,

**8:23** whether regarding Titus, who is my partner and helper toward you, or our brothers, apostles of the churches, the glory of Christ.

**8:24** Therefore, show toward them the demonstration of your love and of our glory on your behalf, before the face of the churches.

## Translator Notes

- **8:2** — The Vulgate supplies a separate verb fuit ("there was") for the first clause and renders Greek haplotes ("generosity / single-heartedness") literally as simplicitas ("simplicity"; likewise in 9:11 and 9:13), denoting ungrudging liberality.
- **8:8** — Vulgate ingenium bonum literally means "good character" or "good disposition," translating Greek gnesion ("genuineness").
- **8:9** — The Vulgate uses essetis ("might be") rather than "might become" (Greek ploutesete).
- **8:21** — The Vulgate reads coram Deo ("before God"), whereas most Greek manuscripts read "before the Lord" (enopion kyriou).
- **8:24** — Vulgate gloriæ ("glory") renders Greek kaucheseos ("boasting"), and in faciem ecclesiarum preserves the literal idiom "before the face of the churches."
