## Text

**7:1** Therefore, having these promises, beloved, let us purify ourselves from every defilement of flesh and spirit, completing sanctification in the fear of God.

**7:2** Make room for us. We have harmed no one, we have corrupted no one, we have defrauded no one.

**7:3** I do not speak for your condemnation, for we have said before that you are in our hearts to die together and to live together.

**7:4** I have great confidence toward you; I have great boasting on your behalf. I have been filled with comfort; I abound exceedingly with joy in all our affliction.

**7:5** For even when we had come into Macedonia, our flesh had no rest, but we suffered every affliction: outside, conflicts; inside, fears.

**7:6** But God, who comforts the humble, comforted us by the arrival of Titus.

**7:7** And not only by his arrival, but also by the comfort with which he was comforted in you, reporting to us your longing, your weeping, your zeal for me, so that I rejoiced even more.

**7:8** For although I grieved you in the letter, I do not regret it; even if I did regret it, seeing that (though for an hour) that letter grieved you,

**7:9** now I rejoice, not because you were grieved, but because you were grieved to repentance. For you were grieved toward God, so that you might suffer loss in nothing from us.

**7:10** For the sorrow that is according to God produces steadfast repentance to salvation, but the sorrow of the world produces death.

**7:11** For look at this very thing, your being grieved according to God: how much earnestness it produces in you; yes, defense; yes, indignation; yes, fear; yes, longing; yes, zeal; yes, avenging! In all things you have shown yourselves to be undefiled in the matter.

**7:12** Therefore, even though I wrote to you, it was not on account of the one who did the wrong, nor on account of the one who suffered it, but to make manifest our earnestness that we have for you

**7:13** before God; for this reason we have been comforted. Moreover, in our comfort we rejoiced all the more abundantly over the joy of Titus, because his spirit has been refreshed by all of you;

**7:14** and if I have boasted of anything before him about you, I have not been put to shame; rather, just as we spoke all things to you in truth, so also our boasting, which was to Titus, has become truth,

**7:15** and his inward affection is all the more abundant toward you as he recalls the obedience of all of you, how you received him with fear and trembling.

**7:16** I rejoice that in all things I am confident in you.

## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — The Vulgate reads ad condemnationem vestram ("for your condemnation") and plural prædiximus ("we have said before"), whereas the Greek omits "your" and uses the singular "I have said before."
- **7:8** — The Vulgate uses the participle videns ("seeing") rather than a finite clause ("for I see"), linking this verse directly to nunc gaudeo ("now I rejoice") in 7:9.
- **7:10** — Vulgate stabilem ("steadfast" or "lasting") renders Greek ametameleton ("without regret"); grammatically it can modify either pœnitentiam ("steadfast repentance") or salutem ("lasting salvation").
- **7:12** — The Vulgate reads sollicitudinem nostram, quam habemus pro vobis ("our earnestness that we have for you"), whereas most Greek manuscripts read "your earnestness for us." The Clementine Vulgate also places coram Deo ("before God") at the start of 7:13 rather than the end of 7:12.
