## Text

**1:1** Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ through the will of God, and Timothy the brother, to the church of God that is in Corinth, with all the saints who are in all of Achaia.

**1:2** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:3** Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and the God of all consolation,

**1:4** who consoles us in all our affliction, so that we ourselves may also be able to console those who are in any distress, through the encouragement with which we ourselves are also encouraged by God.

**1:5** For just as the sufferings of Christ abound in us, so also through Christ our consolation abounds.

**1:6** Now whether we are afflicted, it is for your encouragement and salvation; or whether we are consoled, it is for your consolation; or whether we are encouraged, it is for your encouragement and salvation, which produces endurance of the same sufferings that we also suffer,

**1:7** so that our hope for you may be firm, knowing that just as you are sharers in the sufferings, so you will also be in the consolation.

**1:8** For we do not want you to be unaware, brothers, concerning our affliction that happened in Asia, that we were weighed down beyond measure, beyond strength, so that we grew weary even of living.

**1:9** But we ourselves within ourselves had the verdict of death, so that we would not be trusting in ourselves, but in God who raises the dead,

**1:10** who rescued us from such great perils and delivers us, in whom we hope that He will yet rescue us,

**1:11** as you also help in prayer for us, so that from the persons of many, thanks may be given through many on our behalf for that gift which is in us.

**1:12** For our glory is this: the testimony of our conscience that in simplicity of heart and sincerity of God, and not in fleshly wisdom, but in the grace of God, we have conducted ourselves in this world, and more abundantly toward you.

**1:13** For we do not write other things to you than what you have read and understood. And I hope that you will understand even to the end,

**1:14** just as you have also understood us in part, that we are your glory, just as you also are ours, on the day of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:15** And with this confidence I wanted to come to you first, so that you might have a second grace,

**1:16** and to pass through you into Macedonia, and again from Macedonia to come to you, and to be escorted by you into Judea.

**1:17** Therefore, when I had wanted this, did I display fickleness? Or the things that I plan, do I plan according to the flesh, so that with me there should be "Yes" and "No"?

**1:18** But as God is faithful, our word, which was with you, does not have "Yes" and "No" in it.

**1:19** For the Son of God, Jesus Christ, who was preached among you through us, through me and Silvanus and Timothy, was not "Yes" and "No," but "Yes" was in Him.

**1:20** For however many promises of God there are, in Him is "Yes"; therefore also through Him is "Amen" to God for our glory.

**1:21** Now He who confirms us with you in Christ, and who anointed us, is God,

**1:22** who also sealed us and gave the pledge of the Spirit in our hearts.

**1:24** Now I call God as witness upon my soul that, sparing you, I did not come again to Corinth; not that we exercise dominion over your faith, but we are helpers of your joy, for by faith you stand.

## Translator Notes

- **1:6** — The Clementine Vulgate includes a third clause (sive exhortamur pro vestra exhortatione et salute) not found in most Greek manuscripts, and continues in 1:7 with a purpose clause (ut spes nostra firma sit) where the Greek has a coordinate statement ("and our hope is firm"). Throughout 1:3-7, the Vulgate alternates between consolatio/consolari ("consolation"/"console") and exhortatio/exhortari ("encouragement"/"encourage") for a single Greek word family.
- **1:10** — The Vulgate reads de tantis periculis ("from such great perils") and adds the present verb et eruit ("and delivers [us]"), whereas most Greek witnesses read "from so great a death" without the present verb.
- **1:12** — The Vulgate adds cordis ("of heart") to simplicitate ("simplicity") and uses gloria ("glory") here and in 1:14 where the Greek has kauchesis ("boast").
- **1:20** — The Clementine Vulgate reads ad gloriam nostram ("for our glory"), whereas the Greek reads "for glory through us." Earlier in 1:17, Latin Est et Non (literally "It is and No," the Vulgate idiom for "Yes and No") appears singly rather than the doubled Greek "Yes, yes and No, no."
- **1:24** — In this Clementine Vulgate numbering, what modern Bibles divide into verses 23 and 24 is combined into verse 24.
