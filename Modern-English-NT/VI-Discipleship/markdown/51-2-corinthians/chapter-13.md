## Text

**13:1** Look, this third time I am coming to you: by the mouth of two or three witnesses every word will stand.

**13:2** I have said beforehand, and I say beforehand, as present, and now absent, to those who sinned before and to all the rest, that if I come again, I will not spare.

**13:3** Or do you seek proof of Him who speaks in me, Christ, who is not weak in you, but is powerful in you?

**13:4** For even though He was crucified out of weakness, yet He lives by the power of God. For we also are weak in Him, yet we will live with Him by the power of God in you.

**13:5** Test yourselves whether you are in the faith; prove yourselves. Or do you not know yourselves, that Christ Jesus is in you, unless perhaps you are unapproved?

**13:6** But I hope that you will know that we are not unapproved.

**13:7** Now we pray to God that you may do nothing evil; not that we may appear approved, but that you may do what is good, while we may be as unapproved.

**13:8** For we cannot do anything against the truth, but for the truth.

**13:9** For we rejoice because we are weak, while you are powerful. This we also pray for: your completion.

**13:10** For this reason I am writing these things while absent, so that when present I may not act more severely, according to the authority that the Lord gave me for building up and not for destruction.

**13:11** Finally, brothers, rejoice, be perfect, be encouraged, be of the same mind, have peace, and the God of peace and love will be with you.

**13:12** Greet one another with a holy kiss. All the saints greet you.

**13:13** The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Spirit be with all of you. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **13:2** — Unlike the Greek text, which adds 'the second time' (to deuteron), the Clementine Vulgate reads simply 'as present, and now absent' (ut praesens, et nunc absens).
- **13:3** — The Vulgate opens a direct question with An ('Or do you seek...?') where the Greek has a causal clause (epei, 'since'), and uses in vobis ('in you') both here and in 13:4 where the Greek distinguishes eis hymas ('toward you') from en hymin ('in you').
- **13:5** — Verses 5-7 sustain a Latin wordplay on the root prob- (probate, 'prove'; probati, 'approved'; reprobi, 'unapproved'). The Vulgate also reads Christus Jesus ('Christ Jesus') here rather than 'Jesus Christ.'
- **13:11** — The Vulgate reverses the Greek order ('love and peace') to pacis et dilectionis ('of peace and love'). The imperative exhortamini renders Greek parakaleisthe in a passive sense ('be encouraged' or 'receive exhortation').
- **13:13** — Following the Vulgate verse division (which combines both greetings in 13:12, yielding 13 verses instead of 14), the benediction includes nostri ('our') and Amen, and translates Greek koinonia with communicatio ('communion' or 'sharing').
