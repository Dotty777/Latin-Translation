## Text

**3:1** Now Peter and John were going up into the temple at the ninth hour of prayer.

**3:2** And a certain man who was lame from his mother's womb was being carried; they placed him daily at the gate of the temple that is called Beautiful, so that he might ask for alms from those entering the temple.

**3:3** When he saw Peter and John beginning to enter the temple, he asked to receive alms.

**3:4** Now looking intently at him, Peter, with John, said, "Look at us."

**3:5** And he paid close attention to them, hoping to receive something from them.

**3:6** But Peter said, "Silver and gold I do not have; yet what I have, this I give to you: in the name of Jesus Christ the Nazarene, rise and walk."

**3:7** And taking hold of his right hand, he lifted him up, and at once his feet and soles were made firm.

**3:8** And leaping up, he stood and began walking; and he entered with them into the temple, walking and leaping and praising God.

**3:9** And all the people saw him walking and praising God.

**3:10** And they recognized him, that he was the very one who sat for alms at the Beautiful gate of the temple; and they were filled with astonishment and amazement at what had happened to him.

**3:11** Now while he was holding onto Peter and John, all the people ran to them at the portico that is called Solomon's, astonished.

**3:12** But when Peter saw this, he answered the people, "Men of Israel, why do you marvel at this, or why do you look intently at us, as if by our own power or authority we had made this man walk?

**3:13** The God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, the God of our fathers, has glorified His Son Jesus, whom you indeed handed over and denied before the face of Pilate, when he had decided that He should be released.

**3:14** But you denied the Holy and Righteous One, and asked for a man who was a murderer to be granted to you;

**3:15** yet you killed the Author of life, whom God raised from the dead, of which we are witnesses.

**3:16** And by faith in His name, His name has strengthened this man whom you have seen and know; and the faith that is through Him has given this complete health in the sight of all of you.

**3:17** And now, brothers, I know that you acted through ignorance, just as your rulers also did.

**3:18** But God has fulfilled in this way the things He foretold through the mouth of all the prophets, that His Christ would suffer.

**3:19** Repent, therefore, and turn back, so that your sins may be wiped out,

**3:20** so that when the times of refreshment come from the presence of the Lord, and He sends Him who has been preached to you, Jesus Christ,

**3:21** whom heaven must indeed receive until the times of the restoration of all things that God has spoken through the mouth of His holy prophets from ancient times.

**3:22** Moses indeed said, 'The Lord your God will raise up a prophet for you from among your brothers, like me; you shall listen to Him according to everything that He speaks to you.

**3:23** And it will come about that every soul that does not listen to that prophet will be utterly destroyed from among the people.'

**3:24** And all the prophets, from Samuel onward, who have spoken, have announced these days.

**3:25** You are the sons of the prophets and of the covenant that God established with our fathers, saying to Abraham, 'And in your seed all the families of the earth will be blessed.'

**3:26** To you first, God, raising up His Son, sent Him blessing you, so that each one may turn himself away from his wickedness."

## Translator Notes

- **3:6** — The Vulgate reads surge, et ambula ("rise and walk"), aligning with the Byzantine Greek tradition rather than the shorter Alexandrian Greek reading ("walk").
- **3:7** — The Vulgate translates the healed parts as bases ejus et plantae ("his feet and soles"), whereas the Greek text reads "feet and ankles."
- **3:12** — The Clementine Vulgate reads virtute aut potestate ("power or authority"), in contrast to the Greek text's "power or piety."
- **3:13** — Here and in 3:26, the Vulgate renders the Greek title pais ("servant/child") as filius ("Son"), emphasizing Jesus' divine sonship.
- **3:20** — The Vulgate literally reproduces ut cum ("so that when") and reads qui praedicatus est ("who has been preached"), differing from the Alexandrian Greek reading "who has been appointed."
