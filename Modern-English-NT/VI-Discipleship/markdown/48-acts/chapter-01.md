## Text

**1:1** I indeed produced the first account, Theophilus, concerning all the things that Jesus began to do and to teach,

**1:2** up to the day on which, giving commands through the Holy Spirit to the apostles whom He chose, He was taken up.

**1:3** To them He also showed Himself alive after His suffering with many proofs, appearing to them throughout forty days and speaking concerning the kingdom of God.

**1:4** And while eating with them, He commanded them not to depart from Jerusalem, but to wait for the promise of the Father, "which you heard," He said, "through My mouth;

**1:5** because John indeed baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit not many days after this."

**1:6** Therefore those who had gathered together were questioning Him, saying, "Lord, will You restore the kingdom to Israel at this time?"

**1:7** But He said to them, "It is not yours to know the times or moments that the Father has set in His own authority;

**1:8** but you will receive the power of the Holy Spirit coming upon you, and you will be witnesses for Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and all the way to the farthest point of the earth."

**1:9** And when He had said these things, as they were watching, He was lifted up, and a cloud took Him away from their eyes.

**1:10** And as they were gazing intently at Him going into heaven, look, two men stood beside them in white garments,

**1:11** who also said, "Men of Galilee, why are you standing looking into heaven? This Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will thus come, just as you saw Him going into heaven."

**1:12** Then they returned to Jerusalem from the mountain that is called Olivet, which is close to Jerusalem, a Sabbath's journey away.

**1:13** And when they had entered into the upper room, they went up to where Peter and John, James and Andrew, Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew, James son of Alphaeus, and Simon the Zealot, and Judas son of James were staying.

**1:14** All of these were continuing steadfastly, of one mind, in prayer with the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and His brothers.

**1:15** In those days, standing up in the midst of the brothers, Peter said (now the crowd of people together was about one hundred twenty):

**1:16** "Men, brothers, it is necessary for the Scripture to be fulfilled that the Holy Spirit foretold through the mouth of David concerning Judas, who was the guide of those who seized Jesus.

**1:17** He had been counted among us and obtained the allotted share of this ministry."

**1:18** (And this man indeed took possession of a field from the wages of iniquity, and being hanged, he burst open in the middle, and all his entrails poured out.

**1:19** And it became known to all those living in Jerusalem, so that in their language that field was called Hakeldama, that is, Field of Blood.)

**1:20** "For it is written in the book of Psalms: 'Let their dwelling become desolate, and let there be no one who dwells in it; and let another receive his office.'

**1:21** Therefore it is necessary that, from these men who have gathered with us throughout all the time in which the Lord Jesus went in and went out among us,

**1:22** beginning from the baptism of John up to the day on which He was taken up from us, one of these become a witness with us of His resurrection."

**1:23** And they presented two: Joseph, who was called Barsabbas, who was surnamed Justus; and Matthias.

**1:24** And praying, they said, "You, Lord, who know the hearts of all, show which one of these two You have chosen

**1:25** to receive the place of this ministry and apostleship, from which Judas turned aside so that he might depart to his own place."

**1:26** And they cast lots for them, and the lot fell upon Matthias, and he was counted with the eleven apostles.

## Translator Notes

- **1:4** — Latin convescens translates Greek synalizomenos specifically as "eating with them" (rather than "staying/gathering with them"), and the Vulgate explicitly includes inquit ("He said") and per os meum ("through My mouth"), whereas the Greek has simply "which you heard from me."
- **1:8** — Latin virtutem supervenientis Spiritus Sancti in vos renders the Greek genitive absolute ("power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you") as a genitive phrase modifying "power" ("the power of the Holy Spirit coming upon you").
- **1:16** — The Clementine Vulgate uses the present tense oportet ("it is necessary"), whereas the Greek has the imperfect edei ("it was necessary" / "had to be").
- **1:18** — The Vulgate translates Greek prenes genomenos ("falling headlong") as suspensus ("being hanged" / "suspended"), harmonizing the passage with Judas hanging himself in Matthew 27:5.
- **1:20** — The Clementine Vulgate reads the plural eorum ("their dwelling"), agreeing with Psalm 68:26 (69:25), where the Greek text of Acts has the singular autou ("his"); Latin episcopatum literally denotes a "bishopric" or "office of oversight."
