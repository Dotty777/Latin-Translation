## Text

**14:1** Now it happened in Iconium that they entered together into the synagogue of the Jews and spoke in such a way that a large multitude of Jews and Greeks believed.

**14:2** But the Jews who were unbelieving stirred up and provoked to anger the souls of the Gentiles against the brothers.

**14:3** Therefore they stayed for a long time, acting boldly in the Lord, who bore witness to the word of His grace, granting signs and wonders to be done through their hands.

**14:4** But the multitude of the city was divided; and some indeed were with the Jews, while others were with the apostles.

**14:5** Now when an attack arose from the Gentiles and the Jews with their rulers to mistreat and stone them,

**14:6** becoming aware of it, they fled to the cities of Lycaonia, Lystra and Derbe, and to the entire surrounding region, and there they were preaching the gospel.

**14:8** And a certain man was sitting in Lystra, weak in his feet, lame from his mother's womb, who had never walked.

**14:9** This man heard Paul speaking; and Paul, gazing intently at him and seeing that he had faith to be made well,

**14:10** said with a loud voice, "Rise upright on your feet." And he sprang up and began walking.

**14:11** Now when the crowds saw what Paul had done, they raised their voice in the Lycaonian language, saying, "The gods, having become like men, have come down to us."

**14:12** And they were calling Barnabas Jupiter, and Paul Mercury, because he was the leader in speaking.

**14:13** Also the priest of Jupiter, who was before the city, bringing bulls and garlands before the gates, wanted to offer sacrifice with the people.

**14:14** When the apostles Barnabas and Paul heard this, tearing their tunics, they rushed out into the crowds, shouting

**14:15** and saying, "Men, why are you doing these things? We also are mortals, men like you, proclaiming to you to turn from these vain things to the living God, who made heaven and earth and the sea and all things that are in them,

**14:16** who in past generations permitted all the nations to walk in their own ways.

**14:17** And indeed He did not leave Himself without witness, doing good from heaven, giving rains and fruitful seasons, filling our hearts with food and gladness."

**14:18** And saying these things, they barely restrained the crowds from sacrificing to them.

**14:19** But certain Jews arrived from Antioch and Iconium; and having persuaded the crowds, and stoning Paul, they dragged him outside the city, thinking that he was dead.

**14:20** But while the disciples surrounded him, rising up, he entered the city; and on the following day he departed with Barnabas for Derbe.

**14:21** And when they had preached the gospel to that city and had taught many, they returned to Lystra, and Iconium, and Antioch,

**14:22** strengthening the souls of the disciples, and exhorting them to remain in the faith, and saying that through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God.

**14:23** And when they had appointed elders for them in each church and had prayed with fastings, they commended them to the Lord in whom they had believed.

**14:24** And passing through Pisidia, they came into Pamphylia,

**14:25** and speaking the word of the Lord in Perga, they went down to Attalia;

**14:26** and from there they sailed to Antioch, from where they had been committed to the grace of God for the work that they had completed.

**14:27** Now when they had arrived and gathered the church together, they reported what great things God had done with them, and that He had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles.

**14:28** And they stayed for no short time with the disciples.

## Translator Notes

- **14:3** — The Vulgate reads fiducialiter agentes ("acting boldly/confidently"), whereas the Greek has παρρησιαζόμενοι ("speaking boldly").
- **14:6** — The Clementine Vulgate includes the closing clause et ibi evangelizantes erant ("and there they were preaching the gospel") inside verse 6, so there is no separate verse 7.
- **14:12** — Here and in verse 13, the Vulgate renders the Greek deity names Zeus and Hermes with their Roman equivalents, Jupiter (Jovem/Jovis) and Mercury (Mercurium).
- **14:15** — The Vulgate renders the Greek adjective ὁμοιοπαθεῖς ("of like nature") with the fuller phrase mortales sumus, similes vobis homines ("we also are mortals, men like you").
- **14:17** — The Vulgate connects de cælo ("from heaven") to benefaciens ("doing good") and reads corda nostra ("our hearts") rather than "your hearts".
