## Text

**27:1** Now when it was decided that he should sail to Italy, and that Paul with the rest of the prisoners should be handed over to a centurion named Julius, of the Augustan cohort,

**27:2** we boarded a ship of Adramyttium and, beginning to sail around the places of Asia, weighed anchor, with Aristarchus, a Macedonian of Thessalonica, remaining with us.

**27:3** And on the following day we arrived at Sidon. And Julius, treating Paul kindly, permitted him to go to friends and take care of himself.

**27:4** And after we had weighed anchor from there, we sailed in the lee of Cyprus, because the winds were contrary.

**27:5** And sailing across the open sea of Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came to Lystra, which is in Lycia.

**27:6** And there the centurion, finding an Alexandrian ship sailing to Italy, transferred us onto it.

**27:7** And when we had sailed slowly for many days and had with difficulty arrived opposite Cnidus, as the wind hindered us, we sailed toward Crete near Salmone.

**27:8** And sailing alongside it with difficulty, we came to a certain place that is called Good Harbors, near which was the city of Thalassa.

**27:9** Now when much time had elapsed, and since sailing was no longer safe because even the Fast had already passed, Paul was consoling them,

**27:10** saying to them, "Men, I see that the voyage is going to be with harm and much loss, not only of the cargo and the ship, but also of our lives."

**27:11** But the centurion believed the helmsman and the shipowner more than the things that were being spoken by Paul.

**27:12** And since the harbor was not suitable for wintering, the majority decided on a plan to sail from there, if somehow they could arrive at and winter in Phoenix, a harbor of Crete facing toward the southwest and toward the northwest.

**27:13** And when a south wind blew gently, supposing that they had secured their purpose, after they had weighed anchor from Asson, they coasted along Crete.

**27:14** Not long afterward, however, a tempestuous wind, which is called Euroaquilo, hurled itself against it.

**27:15** And when the ship was seized and could not press into the wind, having given the ship over to the gusts, we were driven along.

**27:16** And running down toward a certain island that is called Cauda, we were able with difficulty to secure the skiff.

**27:17** When it had been hoisted up, they used supports, girding the ship; and fearing that they might run into the Syrtis, having lowered the gear, they were driven along in this way.

**27:18** And as we were tossed by a violent storm, on the following day they jettisoned cargo.

**27:19** And on the third day, with their own hands, they threw out the tackle of the ship.

**27:20** And with neither sun nor stars appearing for many days, and no small storm bearing down, all hope of our deliverance was now taken away.

**27:21** And when there had been much fasting, then Paul, standing in the midst of them, said, "Men, you indeed ought to have listened to me, not to have weighed anchor from Crete, and to have spared yourselves this harm and loss.

**27:22** And now I advise you to be of good courage, for there will be the loss of not a single life from among you, except of the ship.

**27:23** For an angel of God, whose I am and whom I serve, stood beside me this night,

**27:24** saying, 'Do not be afraid, Paul; you must stand before Caesar; and look, God has granted to you all who are sailing with you.'

**27:25** For this reason, be of good courage, men; for I believe God that it will be just as it has been told to me.

**27:26** However, we must arrive at a certain island."

**27:27** But after the fourteenth night had come, as we were sailing in the Adriatic about midnight, the sailors suspected that some region was appearing to them.

**27:28** And they, letting down the sounding lead, found twenty fathoms; and after moving a little distance from there, they found fifteen fathoms.

**27:29** And fearing that we might run onto rough places, casting four anchors from the stern, they wished for day to come.

**27:30** But as the sailors were seeking to flee from the ship, after they had lowered the skiff into the sea under the pretext as if they were about to lay out anchors from the bow,

**27:31** Paul said to the centurion and to the soldiers, "Unless these men remain in the ship, you cannot be saved."

**27:32** Then the soldiers cut away the ropes of the skiff and let it fall away.

**27:33** And as daylight was beginning to come, Paul was urging all of them to take food, saying, "Today, the fourteenth day, you remain waiting and fasting, taking nothing.

**27:34** For this reason I urge you to take food for your preservation; for not a hair from the head of any of you will perish."

**27:35** And when he had said these things, taking bread, he gave thanks to God in the sight of all; and when he had broken it, he began to eat.

**27:36** And having all become more encouraged, they themselves also took food.

**27:37** Now in all we were two hundred seventy-six souls in the ship.

**27:38** And when they were satisfied with food, they lightened the ship, throwing the wheat into the sea.

**27:39** And when day had come, they did not recognize the land; but they observed a certain bay having a beach, into which they planned, if they could, to drive the ship.

**27:40** And after they had weighed the anchors, they committed themselves to the sea, at the same time loosening the fastenings of the rudders; and having hoisted the foresail to the blowing of the breeze, they headed toward the beach.

**27:41** And when we had run onto a place between two seas, they drove the ship aground; and the bow, stuck fast, remained immovable, while the stern was being broken up by the force of the sea.

**27:42** Now the plan of the soldiers was to kill the prisoners, so that no one might swim away and escape.

**27:43** But the centurion, wishing to save Paul, prevented it from being done; and he commanded those who could swim to cast themselves out first, and to escape, and to come out onto the land.

**27:44** And the rest, some they carried on planks, and some upon the things that were from the ship. And so it came about that all the souls escaped to the land.

## Translator Notes

- **27:1** — The Clementine Vulgate reads navigare eum ("that he should sail"), referring to Paul, whereas the Greek text has "that we should sail."
- **27:5** — The Clementine Vulgate reads Lystram ("Lystra") in Lycia, a Vulgate-distinct place name where the Greek text has Myra.
- **27:8** — The Clementine Vulgate names the harbor Boniportus ("Good Harbors") and the nearby city Thalassa, where most Greek manuscripts read Lasea.
- **27:13** — The Clementine Vulgate reads de Asson ("from Asson"), taking the Greek comparative adverb asson ("closer") as a proper place name.
- **27:40** — The Clementine Vulgate reads committebant se mari ("they committed themselves to the sea") after weighing the anchors, whereas the Greek text says they cast off the anchors and left them in the sea.
- **27:44** — The Clementine Vulgate adds the active verb ferebant ("they carried") for the rest of the passengers brought ashore on planks and ship debris.
