## Text

**28:1** And when we had escaped, we then learned that the island was called Malta. Now the native inhabitants showed us no ordinary kindness.

**28:2** For after lighting a fire, they refreshed all of us because of the threatening rain and the cold.

**28:3** Now when Paul had gathered a sizable quantity of brushwood and placed it on the fire, a viper came out because of the heat and seized his hand.

**28:4** When the native inhabitants saw the creature hanging from his hand, they began saying to one another, "Surely this man is a murderer; though he has escaped from the sea, vengeance does not allow him to live."

**28:5** And he, shaking the creature off into the fire, suffered no harm.

**28:6** But they were expecting him to swell up and suddenly fall down and die. However, after waiting a long time and seeing no harm happening to him, they changed their minds and began saying that he was a god.

**28:7** Now in those parts were the estates of the leading man of the island, named Publius, who welcomed us and hosted us kindly for three days.

**28:8** And it happened that the father of Publius lay afflicted with fevers and dysentery. Paul went in to him, and when he had prayed and laid hands on him, he healed him.

**28:9** When this had happened, all on the island who had illnesses kept coming and were cured.

**28:10** They also honored us with many honors, and as we set sail, they put on board for us the things that were necessary.

**28:11** After three months, we set sail on an Alexandrian ship that had wintered at the island, whose figurehead was Castor and Pollux.

**28:12** And when we had come to Syracuse, we stayed there for three days.

**28:13** From there, coasting along, we reached Rhegium; and after one day, with a south wind blowing, on the second day we came to Puteoli,

**28:14** where, having found brothers, we were asked to stay with them seven days; and so we came to Rome.

**28:15** And when the brothers from there had heard, they came to meet us as far as the Forum of Appius and Three Taverns. When Paul saw them, giving thanks to God, he took courage.

**28:16** Now when we had come to Rome, Paul was permitted to stay by himself with the soldier guarding him.

**28:17** After the third day, however, he called together the foremost men of the Jews. And when they had gathered together, he said to them, "Men, brothers, having done nothing against the people or ancestral custom, I was handed over as a prisoner from Jerusalem into the hands of the Romans,

**28:18** who, when they had held an examination of me, wanted to release me, because there was no basis for death in me.

**28:19** But when the Jews spoke against it, I was compelled to appeal to Caesar, not as though I had anything of which to accuse my nation.

**28:20** For this reason, therefore, I have asked to see you and to speak with you. For on account of the hope of Israel, I am bound with this chain."

**28:21** But they said to him, "We have neither received letters concerning you from Judea, nor has any of the brothers coming here reported or spoken anything evil about you.

**28:22** However, we ask to hear from you what you think; for concerning this sect, it is known to us that it is spoken against everywhere."

**28:23** When they had appointed a day for him, very many came to him at his lodging; to them he expounded, bearing witness to the kingdom of God and seeking to persuade them concerning Jesus from the law of Moses and the prophets, from morning until evening.

**28:24** And some believed the things that were being said, while others did not believe.

**28:25** And since they were not in agreement with one another, they began to depart as Paul spoke one word: "The Holy Spirit spoke well through Isaiah the prophet to our fathers,

**28:26** saying, 'Go to this people and say to them, "You will hear with the ear and not understand, and seeing, you will see and not discern."

**28:27** For the heart of this people has grown dull, and with their ears they have heard with difficulty, and they have pressed their eyes shut; otherwise they might see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and turn back, and I would heal them.'

**28:28** Let it be known to you, therefore, that this salvation of God has been sent to the Gentiles, and they themselves will listen."

**28:29** And when he had said these things, the Jews departed from him, having much debate among themselves.

**28:30** Now he stayed for two full years in his own rented quarters, and he welcomed all who came in to him,

**28:31** proclaiming the kingdom of God and teaching the things concerning the Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness, without restriction.

## Translator Notes

- **28:1** — Latin barbari (literally 'barbarians') denotes non-Greek- and non-Latin-speaking local inhabitants rather than uncivilized people; the Vulgate also places this second sentence in verse 1 rather than verse 2.
- **28:4** — Latin ultio ('vengeance' or 'retribution') corresponds to Greek Dike ('Justice'), reflecting a pagan notion of personified divine retribution, and the Vulgate uses the present tense non sinit ('does not allow').
- **28:11** — Latin Castorum (literally 'of the Castors') is the idiomatic Latin plural for the twin deities Castor and Pollux, patron gods of sailors (corresponding to Greek Dioskouroi).
- **28:25** — The Vulgate reads patres nostros ('our fathers'), whereas many critical Greek editions read 'your fathers.'
- **28:29** — Native Clementine Vulgate includes Acts 28:29 (absent from Saints.Bible Greek numbering and some early Vulgate manuscripts).
