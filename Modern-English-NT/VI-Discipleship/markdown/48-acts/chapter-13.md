## Text

**13:1** Now there were in the church that was at Antioch prophets and teachers, among whom were Barnabas, and Simon who was called Niger, and Lucius of Cyrene, and Manaen, who was a foster brother of Herod the tetrarch, and Saul.

**13:2** And while they were ministering to the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said to them, "Set apart for Me Saul and Barnabas for the work to which I have taken them."

**13:3** Then, fasting and praying, and laying hands on them, they sent them away.

**13:4** And they indeed, sent by the Holy Spirit, departed to Seleucia; and from there they sailed to Cyprus.

**13:5** And when they had come to Salamis, they were preaching the word of God in the synagogues of the Jews. And they also had John in the ministry.

**13:6** And when they had walked through the whole island as far as Paphos, they found a certain man, a magician, a false prophet, a Jew, whose name was Bar-Jesus,

**13:7** who was with the proconsul Sergius Paulus, a prudent man. This man, having summoned Barnabas and Saul, desired to hear the word of God.

**13:8** But Elymas the magician was resisting them (for so his name is interpreted), seeking to turn the proconsul away from the faith.

**13:9** But Saul, who is also Paul, filled with the Holy Spirit, looking intently at him,

**13:10** said, "You who are full of all deceit and all trickery, son of the devil, enemy of all righteousness, you do not stop subverting the straight ways of the Lord.

**13:11** And now, look, the hand of the Lord is upon you, and you will be blind, not seeing the sun for a time." And immediately a mist and darkness fell upon him; and going around, he was seeking someone who would give him a hand.

**13:12** Then the proconsul, when he had seen what had happened, believed, marveling at the teaching of the Lord.

**13:13** And when Paul and those who were with him had sailed from Paphos, they came to Perga of Pamphylia. But John, departing from them, returned to Jerusalem.

**13:14** They, however, passing through Perga, came to Antioch of Pisidia; and having entered the synagogue on the Sabbath day, they sat down.

**13:15** And after the reading of the law and the prophets, the rulers of the synagogue sent to them, saying, "Men, brothers, if there is any word of exhortation among you for the people, speak."

**13:16** And Paul, rising up and signaling for silence with his hand, said, "Men of Israel, and you who fear God, listen:

**13:17** The God of the people of Israel chose our fathers, and exalted the people when they were sojourners in the land of Egypt, and with an uplifted arm brought them out of it,

**13:18** and for a period of forty years endured their conduct in the wilderness.

**13:19** And destroying seven nations in the land of Canaan, He distributed their land to them by lot,

**13:20** after about four hundred and fifty years; and after these things He gave judges, until Samuel the prophet.

**13:21** And afterward they asked for a king; and God gave them Saul the son of Kish, a man of the tribe of Benjamin, for forty years;

**13:22** and when he had been removed, He raised up David for them as king; and bearing witness to him, He said, 'I have found David the son of Jesse, a man according to My heart, who will do all My desires.'

**13:23** From the seed of this man, according to promise, God brought forth for Israel a Savior, Jesus,

**13:24** with John preaching before the face of His coming a baptism of repentance to all the people of Israel.

**13:25** And as John was fulfilling his course, he was saying, 'I am not the one whom you suppose me to be; but look, One is coming after me, the sandals of whose feet I am not worthy to untie.'

**13:26** Men, brothers, sons of the lineage of Abraham, and those among you who fear God, to you the word of this salvation has been sent.

**13:27** For those who lived in Jerusalem and its rulers, being ignorant of Him and of the voices of the prophets which are read every Sabbath, fulfilled them in passing judgment,

**13:28** and finding no ground for death in Him, they asked Pilate that they might put Him to death.

**13:29** And when they had completed all things that were written about Him, taking Him down from the tree, they laid Him in a tomb.

**13:30** But God raised Him from the dead on the third day; and He was seen for many days by those

**13:31** who had gone up together with Him from Galilee to Jerusalem; and until now they are His witnesses to the people.

**13:32** And we proclaim to you that promise which was made to our fathers:

**13:33** that God has fulfilled this for our children, raising up Jesus, as it is also written in the second psalm, 'You are My Son; today I have fathered You.'

**13:34** And as for the fact that He raised Him from the dead, no longer to return to decay, He spoke thus, 'I will give you the faithful holy things of David.'

**13:35** And therefore in another place He also says, 'You will not permit Your Holy One to see decay.'

**13:36** For David, when he had served in his own generation, fell asleep by the will of God, and was laid with his fathers, and saw decay.

**13:37** But He whom God raised from the dead did not see decay.

**13:38** Therefore let it be known to you, men, brothers, that through Him forgiveness of sins is proclaimed to you, and from all things from which you could not be justified in the law of Moses,

**13:39** in Him everyone who believes is justified.

**13:40** See to it, therefore, that what was spoken in the prophets does not come upon you:

**13:41** 'Look, despisers, and marvel, and perish; for I am working a work in your days, a work which you will not believe if anyone recounts it to you.'"

**13:42** And as they were going out, they were asking that on the following Sabbath they would speak these words to them.

**13:43** And when the synagogue had been dismissed, many of the Jews and of the devout proselytes followed Paul and Barnabas, who, speaking, were persuading them to remain in the grace of God.

**13:44** And on the following Sabbath almost the whole city came together to hear the word of God.

**13:45** But the Jews, seeing the crowds, were filled with jealousy, and were contradicting the things that were being spoken by Paul, blaspheming.

**13:46** Then Paul and Barnabas resolutely said, "It was necessary to speak the word of God to you first; but since you thrust it away and judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life, look, we are turning to the Gentiles.

**13:47** For so the Lord has commanded us, 'I have set you as a light of the Gentiles, that you may be for salvation to the end of the earth.'"

**13:48** And when the Gentiles heard this, they rejoiced and were glorifying the word of the Lord; and as many as were preordained to eternal life believed.

**13:49** And the word of the Lord was being spread throughout the whole region.

**13:50** But the Jews incited the religious and honorable women and the leading men of the city, and aroused persecution against Paul and Barnabas, and drove them out of their borders.

**13:51** But they, having shaken off the dust of their feet against them, came to Iconium.

**13:52** And the disciples also were being filled with joy and with the Holy Spirit.

## Translator Notes

- **13:1** — The Vulgate reads Simon rather than the Greek form Symeon found in modern critical editions.
- **13:20** — Following the Vulgate wording (quasi post quadringentos et quinquaginta annos: et post hæc dedit judices), the four hundred and fifty years precede the period of the judges, whereas modern Greek critical editions attach the duration to the judges themselves.
- **13:30** — The Vulgate explicitly adds tertia die ("on the third day"), a reading absent from the standard Greek text, and includes the clause "and He was seen for many days by those" at the end of verse 30 rather than verse 31.
- **13:36** — The Clementine Vulgate punctuates after administrasset, joining voluntati Dei with dormivit ("fell asleep by the will of God"); the dative voluntati Dei can also be construed with administrasset ("when he had served the will of God in his own generation").
- **13:48** — The Vulgate renders the Greek participle tetagmenoi ("appointed" or "disposed") with the specific compound præordinati ("preordained").
