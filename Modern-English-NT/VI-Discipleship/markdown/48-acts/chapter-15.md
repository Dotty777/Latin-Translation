## Text

**15:1** And certain men coming down from Judea were teaching the brothers, "Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved."

**15:2** Therefore, when no small dissension had arisen for Paul and Barnabas against them, they decided that Paul and Barnabas, along with certain others from among the others, should go up to the apostles and elders in Jerusalem concerning this question.

**15:3** So they, being escorted on their way by the church, passed through Phoenicia and Samaria, recounting the conversion of the Gentiles, and caused great joy to all the brothers.

**15:4** Now when they had come to Jerusalem, they were received by the church, and by the apostles and elders, declaring what great things God had done with them.

**15:5** But certain ones from the sect of the Pharisees who had believed rose up, saying that it is necessary for them to be circumcised, and also to command them to keep the law of Moses.

**15:6** And the apostles and elders came together to look into this matter.

**15:7** Now when much debate was taking place, Peter rose up and said to them, "Men, brothers, you know that from early days God chose among us that by my mouth the Gentiles should hear the word of the gospel and believe.

**15:8** And God, who knows the hearts, bore witness, giving them the Holy Spirit, just as also to us,

**15:9** and made no distinction between us and them, purifying their hearts by faith.

**15:10** Now therefore, why do you test God, to place a yoke upon the necks of the disciples which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear?

**15:11** But we believe that through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ we will be saved, in the same way as they also."

**15:12** And the whole multitude fell silent; and they listened to Barnabas and Paul recounting what great signs and wonders God had done among the Gentiles through them.

**15:13** And after they became silent, James answered, saying, "Men, brothers, listen to me.

**15:14** Simon has recounted how God first visited to take from among the Gentiles a people for His name.

**15:15** And with this the words of the prophets agree, just as it is written:

**15:16** 'After these things I will return and rebuild the tabernacle of David that has fallen, and I will rebuild its ruins and raise it up,

**15:17** so that the rest of men may seek the Lord, and all the Gentiles upon whom My name has been called, says the Lord who does these things.'

**15:18** Known from eternity to the Lord is His work.

**15:19** For this reason I judge that those who turn to God from among the Gentiles should not be troubled,

**15:20** but to write to them that they should abstain from the pollutions of idols, and from fornication, and from things strangled, and from blood.

**15:21** For from ancient times Moses has in each city those who preach him in the synagogues, where he is read every Sabbath."

**15:22** Then it pleased the apostles and the elders, with the whole church, to choose men from among them and send them to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas: Judas, who was called Barsabbas, and Silas, leading men among the brothers,

**15:23** writing by their hands: "The apostles and the elders, brothers, to those who are in Antioch, and Syria, and Cilicia, brothers from among the Gentiles, greetings.

**15:24** Since we have heard that certain ones going out from us have troubled you with words, subverting your souls, to whom we gave no command,

**15:25** it pleased us, having gathered together as one, to choose men and send them to you with our most beloved Barnabas and Paul,

**15:26** men who have delivered up their lives for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**15:27** Therefore we have sent Judas and Silas, who will themselves also report the same things to you by word.

**15:28** For it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us to place no further burden upon you than these necessary things:

**15:29** that you abstain from things sacrificed to idols, and from blood, and from what has been strangled, and from fornication; keeping yourselves from these, you will do well. Farewell."

**15:30** So they, being sent off, went down to Antioch; and having gathered the multitude together, they delivered the letter.

**15:31** And when they had read it, they rejoiced over the consolation.

**15:32** Now Judas and Silas, since they themselves were also prophets, consoled the brothers with many words and strengthened them.

**15:33** And when some time had passed there, they were sent off with peace by the brothers to those who had sent them.

**15:35** But Paul and Barnabas stayed in Antioch, teaching and proclaiming as good news, with many others, the word of the Lord.

**15:36** Now after some days, Paul said to Barnabas, "Let us return and visit the brothers throughout all the cities in which we preached the word of the Lord, to see how they are doing."

**15:37** Now Barnabas wanted to take John also with them, who was called Mark.

**15:38** But Paul urged that he, as one who had departed from them out of Pamphylia and had not gone with them to the work, ought not to be received.

**15:39** And a dissension arose, so that they separated from one another, and Barnabas indeed, taking Mark along, sailed to Cyprus.

**15:40** But Paul, having chosen Silas, departed, having been committed to the grace of God by the brothers.

**15:41** And he traveled through Syria and Cilicia, strengthening the churches, commanding them to keep the precepts of the apostles and the elders.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**15:34** *(after `15:33`)* However, it seemed good to Silas to remain there, while Judas alone went away to Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **15:2** — The Clementine Vulgate reads quidam alii ex aliis ("certain others from among the others" or "from the other side") rather than ex illis ("from among them"), and has only seditione ("dissension") without a second noun for "debate".
- **15:14** — The Vulgate uses the standard form Simon where the Greek text uses the Semitic form Symeon.
- **15:18** — The Vulgate reads this as a complete sentence with singular opus suum ("His work"), differing from both the shorter critical Greek text attached to verse 17 and the Byzantine plural ("all His works").
- **15:33** — The Latin text omits verse 34 (present in some manuscripts and editions, stating that Silas decided to remain in Antioch), proceeding directly to verse 35.
- **15:41** — The closing clause præcipiens custodire præcepta Apostolorum et seniorum ("commanding them to keep the precepts of the apostles and the elders") is a distinctive Western/Vulgate expansion absent from standard Greek editions; in verse 40 the Vulgate also reads gratiæ Dei ("grace of God") rather than "grace of the Lord".
