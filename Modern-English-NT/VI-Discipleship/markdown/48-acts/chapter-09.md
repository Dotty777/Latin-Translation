## Text

**9:1** Now Saul, still breathing threats and slaughter against the disciples of the Lord, approached the high priest

**9:2** and requested letters from him for Damascus, to the synagogues, so that if he found any men and women of this Way, he might lead them bound to Jerusalem.

**9:3** And while he was journeying, it happened that he was drawing near to Damascus, and suddenly a light from heaven shone all around him.

**9:4** And as he fell to the ground, he heard a voice saying to him, "Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me?"

**9:5** He said, "Who are You, Lord?" And He replied, "I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting; it is hard for you to kick against the goad."

**9:6** And the Lord said to him, "Get up and go into the city, and there you will be told what you must do." Now those men who were traveling with him stood astounded, hearing the voice indeed, yet seeing no one.

**9:8** Then Saul got up from the ground, and with his eyes opened, he saw nothing; and leading him by the hands, they brought him into Damascus.

**9:9** And he was there for three days without seeing, and he did not eat or drink.

**9:10** Now there was a certain disciple in Damascus by the name of Ananias; and the Lord said to him in a vision, "Ananias." He answered, "Here I am, Lord."

**9:11** And the Lord said to him, "Get up and go into the street that is called Straight, and search in the house of Judas for one named Saul, from Tarsus; for look, he is praying.

**9:12** And he has seen a man named Ananias entering and laying hands on him so that he may recover his sight."

**9:13** Then Ananias replied, "Lord, I have heard from many concerning this man, how many evil things he has done to Your saints in Jerusalem,

**9:14** and here he holds authority from the chief priests to bind all who invoke Your name."

**9:15** But the Lord said to him, "Go, because this man is a chosen vessel for Me, to bear My name before the Gentiles, and kings, and the sons of Israel.

**9:16** For I Myself will show him how much he must suffer for My name."

**9:17** And Ananias left and entered the house, and laying hands on him, he said, "Brother Saul, the Lord has sent me, Jesus, who appeared to you on the way by which you were coming, so that you may see and be filled with the Holy Spirit."

**9:18** And at once things like scales fell from his eyes, and he recovered his sight; and getting up, he was baptized.

**9:19** And when he had taken food, he was strengthened. Now he was with the disciples who were in Damascus for several days.

**9:20** And immediately he was preaching Jesus in the synagogues, that He is the Son of God.

**9:21** Now all who were listening were astounded and were saying, "Is this not the man who in Jerusalem was attacking those who invoked this name, and came here for this purpose, so that he might lead them bound to the chief priests?"

**9:22** But Saul kept growing much stronger and confounded the Jews who lived in Damascus, affirming that He is the Christ.

**9:23** Now when many days were completed, the Jews conspired together to kill him.

**9:24** Yet their ambush was made known to Saul. Moreover, they were also guarding the gates day and night so that they might kill him.

**9:25** But the disciples, taking him by night, let him out through the wall, lowering him in a basket.

**9:26** Now when he had arrived in Jerusalem, he was trying to join himself to the disciples, and all of them feared him, not believing that he was a disciple.

**9:27** But Barnabas took hold of him and led him to the apostles, and recounted to them how on the way he had seen the Lord, and that He had spoken to him, and how in Damascus he had acted boldly in the name of Jesus.

**9:28** And he was with them, going in and going out in Jerusalem, and acting boldly in the name of the Lord.

**9:29** He was also speaking to the Gentiles and disputing with the Greeks, but they were seeking to kill him.

**9:30** When the brothers learned of this, they led him down to Caesarea and sent him away to Tarsus.

**9:31** Now the church throughout all Judea, Galilee, and Samaria had peace and was being built up, walking in the fear of the Lord, and was being filled with the consolation of the Holy Spirit.

**9:32** Now it happened that while Peter was passing among all of them, he came down to the saints who lived in Lydda.

**9:33** And there he found a certain man named Aeneas, who had been lying on a cot for eight years and was paralyzed.

**9:34** And Peter said to him, "Aeneas, the Lord Jesus Christ heals you. Get up and make your own bed." And immediately he got up.

**9:35** And all who resided in Lydda and Sharon saw him, and they turned to the Lord.

**9:36** Now in Joppa there was a certain female disciple named Tabitha, which translated is called Dorcas. This woman was full of good works and acts of charity that she did.

**9:37** Now it happened in those days that she fell ill and died. When they had washed her, they placed her in an upper room.

**9:38** Now since Lydda was near Joppa, the disciples, on hearing that Peter was there, sent two men to him, pleading, "Do not hesitate to come to us."

**9:39** So Peter got up and came with them. And when he had arrived, they led him into the upper room, and all the widows stood around him weeping and showing him the tunics and garments that Dorcas used to make for them.

**9:40** Then, after sending everyone outside, Peter knelt down and prayed; and turning toward the body, he said, "Tabitha, get up." And she opened her eyes, and seeing Peter, she sat up.

**9:41** Then giving her his hand, he raised her up. And when he had summoned the saints and the widows, he presented her alive.

**9:42** Now it became known throughout the whole of Joppa, and many believed in the Lord.

**9:43** And it happened that he stayed for many days in Joppa with a certain Simon, a tanner.

## Translator Notes

- **9:5** — The Vulgate includes the clause "it is hard for you to kick against the goad" (with singular Latin "stimulum"), which is absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts here and appears instead in Acts 26:14.
- **9:6** — This Latin critical text omits the "trembling and astonished" expansion found in some Clementine printings and combines the material traditionally numbered as verses 6 and 7; in addition, Latin "stabant stupefacti" means "stood astounded" rather than the Greek "stood speechless."
- **9:12** — The Clementine Vulgate encloses this verse in parentheses and omits "in a vision" (found in many Greek manuscripts). The Latin present subjunctive "recipiat" follows primary direct-speech sequence after "orat" in verse 11, indicating that the Lord's speech continues through this verse.
- **9:29** — Where the Greek text names a single group ("the Hellenists"), the Vulgate uniquely distinguishes two groups: "speaking to the Gentiles" ("gentibus") and "disputing with the Greeks" ("Græcis").
- **9:31** — The Vulgate reads "replebatur" ("was being filled") with the consolation of the Holy Spirit, whereas the Greek text reads "was multiplied" or "increased."
