## Text

**12:1** Now at that same time King Herod stretched out his hands to afflict some from the church.

**12:2** And he killed James, the brother of John, with the sword.

**12:3** And seeing that this pleased the Jews, he proceeded to seize Peter also. Now those were the days of Unleavened Bread.

**12:4** When he had seized him, he put him into prison, handing him over to four squads of four soldiers to be guarded, intending after the Passover to bring him out to the people.

**12:5** And Peter indeed was being kept in prison. But prayer was being made without ceasing by the church to God for him.

**12:6** Now when Herod was about to bring him out, on that very night Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains, and guards before the door were guarding the prison.

**12:7** And look, an angel of the Lord stood there, and a light shone brightly in the room; and striking Peter's side, he woke him up, saying, "Get up quickly." And the chains fell from his hands.

**12:8** And the angel said to him, "Fasten your belt and put on your sandals." And he did so. And he said to him, "Wrap your cloak around yourself and follow me."

**12:9** And going out, he followed him, and he did not know that what was happening through the angel was real, but he supposed he was seeing a vision.

**12:10** And passing through the first and second guard, they came to the iron gate that leads to the city, which opened for them of its own accord. And going out, they went forward along one street, and immediately the angel departed from him.

**12:11** And Peter, coming back to himself, said, "Now I truly know that the Lord has sent His angel and rescued me from the hand of Herod and from all the expectation of the people of the Jews."

**12:12** And reflecting on this, he came to the house of Mary, the mother of John, who was called Mark, where many were gathered together and praying.

**12:13** And as he knocked at the door of the gate, a girl named Rhoda came forward to listen.

**12:14** And when she recognized Peter's voice, out of joy she did not open the gate, but running inside, she reported that Peter was standing before the gate.

**12:15** But they said to her, "You are out of your mind." Yet she kept insisting that it was so. And they were saying, "It is his angel."

**12:16** But Peter continued knocking. And when they had opened it, they saw him and were astonished.

**12:17** Motioning to them with his hand to be silent, he recounted how the Lord had led him out of prison, and he said, "Report these things to James and to the brothers." And going out, he departed to another place.

**12:18** Now when day came, there was no small commotion among the soldiers over what had become of Peter.

**12:19** And when Herod had searched for him and not found him, after conducting an inquiry concerning the guards, he ordered them to be led away; and going down from Judea to Caesarea, he stayed there.

**12:20** Now he was angry with the Tyrians and Sidonians. But they came to him with one mind, and having persuaded Blastus, who was over the king's bedchamber, they were asking for peace, because their regions were fed by him.

**12:21** And on an appointed day, Herod, clothed in a royal robe, sat on the judgment seat and delivered an address to them.

**12:22** And the people were shouting out, "The voices of a god, and not of a man!"

**12:23** And immediately an angel of the Lord struck him, because he had not given honor to God; and consumed by worms, he breathed his last.

**12:24** But the word of the Lord was growing and multiplying.

**12:25** And Barnabas and Saul returned from Jerusalem when they had completed the service, taking along John, who was called Mark.

## Translator Notes

- **12:5** — Latin sine intermissione ("without ceasing") differs in nuance from the Greek ektenos ("earnestly" or "fervently").
- **12:19** — Latin jussit eos duci ("he ordered them to be led away") preserves the concise idiom for being led off to execution.
- **12:20** — Latin ab illo ("by him") refers directly to Herod, whereas the Greek apo tes basilikes means "from the king's country."
- **12:22** — The Clementine Vulgate reads the plural Dei voces ("The voices of a god"), whereas the Greek has the singular "the voice of a god."
- **12:25** — The Clementine Vulgate reads ab Jerosolymis ("from Jerusalem"), whereas many modern critical editions of the Greek text read "to Jerusalem."
