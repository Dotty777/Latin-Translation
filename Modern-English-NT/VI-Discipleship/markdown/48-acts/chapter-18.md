## Text

**18:1** After these things, departing from Athens, he came to Corinth.

**18:2** And finding a certain Jew named Aquila, a native of Pontus, who had recently come from Italy, and Priscilla his wife (because Claudius had commanded all Jews to depart from Rome), he went to them.

**18:3** And because he was of the same trade, he stayed with them and worked. (Now they were of the tentmaking trade.)

**18:4** And he debated in the synagogue every Sabbath, introducing the name of the Lord Jesus, and was persuading Jews and Greeks.

**18:5** Now when Silas and Timothy had come from Macedonia, Paul applied himself intently to the word, testifying to the Jews that Jesus is the Christ.

**18:6** But as they contradicted and blasphemed, shaking out his garments, he said to them, "Your blood be on your head; I am clean; from now on I will go to the Gentiles."

**18:7** And moving from there, he entered the house of a certain man named Titus Justus, a worshiper of God, whose house adjoined the synagogue.

**18:8** Now Crispus, the ruler of the synagogue, believed in the Lord together with his entire household; and many of the Corinthians, as they heard, believed and were baptized.

**18:9** And the Lord said to Paul at night through a vision, "Do not be afraid, but speak, and do not stay silent,

**18:10** because I am with you, and no one will set upon you to harm you, for I have many people in this city."

**18:11** And he stayed there a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them.

**18:12** Now while Gallio was proconsul of Achaia, the Jews rose up with one accord against Paul and brought him to the tribunal,

**18:13** saying, "This man is persuading people to worship God contrary to the law."

**18:14** But just as Paul was beginning to open his mouth, Gallio said to the Jews, "If indeed it were some wrongdoing or a heinous crime, Jewish men, I would rightly bear with you.

**18:15** But if they are questions about a word, and names, and your law, see to it yourselves; I do not wish to be a judge of these matters."

**18:16** And he drove them from the tribunal.

**18:17** Then they all seized Sosthenes, the ruler of the synagogue, and were beating him in front of the tribunal; and none of those things was of concern to Gallio.

**18:18** Paul, however, after staying many days longer, took leave of the brothers and sailed for Syria (and Priscilla and Aquila were with him), having shaved his head in Cenchreae, for he had a vow.

**18:19** And he arrived at Ephesus and left them there. But he himself entered the synagogue and debated with the Jews.

**18:20** And when they asked him to stay for a longer time, he did not consent,

**18:21** but taking leave and saying, "I will return to you again, God willing," he departed from Ephesus.

**18:22** And going down to Caesarea, he went up and greeted the church, and went down to Antioch.

**18:23** And after spending some time there, he departed, traveling in order through the Galatian region and Phrygia, strengthening all the disciples.

**18:24** Now a certain Jew named Apollos, a native of Alexandria, an eloquent man, arrived at Ephesus, being mighty in the Scriptures.

**18:25** This man had been instructed in the way of the Lord; and being fervent in spirit, he spoke and taught accurately the things concerning Jesus, knowing only the baptism of John.

**18:26** This man, therefore, began to act boldly in the synagogue. When Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and explained to him the way of the Lord more accurately.

**18:27** Now when he wanted to go to Achaia, the brothers encouraged him and wrote to the disciples to receive him. And when he had arrived, he contributed much to those who had believed;

**18:28** for he vigorously refuted the Jews in public, showing through the Scriptures that Jesus is the Christ.

## Translator Notes

- **18:4** — The Vulgate includes interponens nomen Domini Jesu ("introducing the name of the Lord Jesus"), a Western-text addition absent from most Greek manuscripts.
- **18:7** — The Vulgate reads nomine Titi Justi ("named Titus Justus"), preserving the name Titus where Greek manuscripts vary between "Titius Justus" and "Justus."
- **18:18** — The subject of qui sibi totonderat ("having shaved his head") is grammatically ambiguous between Paul and Aquila, though the Clementine punctuation places Priscilla and Aquila in parentheses to link the clause back to Paul.
- **18:26** — The Vulgate reads fiducialiter agere (literally "to act boldly") and viam Domini ("the way of the Lord"), where Greek manuscripts read "to speak boldly" and "the way of God."
- **18:27** — The Vulgate ends with his qui crediderant ("to those who had believed"), omitting the phrase "through grace" present in Greek manuscripts.
