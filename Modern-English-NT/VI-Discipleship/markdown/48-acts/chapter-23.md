## Text

**23:1** Now looking intently at the council, Paul said, "Men, brothers, I have conducted myself with all good conscience before God up to this very day."

**23:2** And the chief priest Ananias commanded those standing beside him to strike his mouth.

**23:3** Then Paul said to him, "God will strike you, whitewashed wall. And you sit judging me according to the law, and contrary to the law you order me to be struck?"

**23:4** And those who were standing nearby said, "You are cursing the high priest of God."

**23:5** And Paul said, "I did not know, brothers, that he is the chief priest. For it is written, 'You shall not curse the ruler of your people.'"

**23:6** Now Paul, knowing that one part was Sadducees and the other Pharisees, cried out in the council, "Men, brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees; concerning the hope and resurrection of the dead I am being judged."

**23:7** And when he had said these things, a dissension arose between the Pharisees and the Sadducees, and the assembly was split apart.

**23:8** For the Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, nor angel, nor spirit, but the Pharisees confess both.

**23:9** Then a great outcry arose. And some of the Pharisees stood up and contended, saying, "We find nothing wrong in this man; what if a spirit spoke to him, or an angel?"

**23:10** And when a great dissension had arisen, the tribune, fearing that Paul might be torn to pieces by them, ordered the soldiers to go down and snatch him from their midst, and to bring him into the barracks.

**23:11** Now on the following night, standing beside him, the Lord said, "Be steadfast; for just as you have testified about Me in Jerusalem, so you must testify in Rome also."

**23:12** And when day came, some of the Jews banded together and bound themselves under a curse, saying that they would neither eat nor drink until they killed Paul.

**23:13** Now there were more than forty men who had formed this conspiracy.

**23:14** They went to the chief priests and the elders and said, "With a curse we have bound ourselves to taste nothing until we kill Paul.

**23:15** Now therefore, you, together with the council, make it known to the tribune to bring him forth to you, as though you were about to ascertain something more certain concerning him. And we, before he comes near, are ready to kill him."

**23:16** Now when the son of Paul's sister heard of the ambush, he came and entered the barracks and reported it to Paul.

**23:17** Then Paul, calling one of the centurions to himself, said, "Bring this young man to the tribune, for he has something to report to him."

**23:18** So he took him and led him to the tribune and said, "The prisoner Paul asked me to bring this young man to you, who has something to speak to you."

**23:19** And the tribune, taking hold of his hand, withdrew with him privately and questioned him, "What is it that you have to report to me?"

**23:20** And he said, "The Jews have agreed to ask you to bring Paul forth tomorrow into the council, as though they were about to inquire into something more certain concerning him;

**23:21** but do not believe them; for more than forty men from among them are lying in ambush for him, who have bound themselves under a curse not to eat nor to drink until they kill him; and now they are ready, awaiting your promise."

**23:22** Therefore the tribune dismissed the young man, commanding him to tell no one that he had made these things known to him.

**23:23** And having called two centurions, he said to them, "Prepare two hundred soldiers to go as far as Caesarea, and seventy horsemen, and two hundred spearmen, from the third hour of the night,

**23:24** and prepare mounts so that, placing Paul on them, they may bring him safely to Felix the governor."

**23:25** (For he feared that perhaps the Jews might seize him and kill him, and that he himself might afterward suffer a false accusation, as though about to accept money.)

**23:26** He wrote a letter containing these words: "Claudius Lysias, to the most excellent governor Felix, greetings.

**23:27** This man was seized by the Jews and was about to be killed by them; coming upon them with the troops, I rescued him, having learned that he is a Roman.

**23:28** And wanting to know the charge that they were bringing against him, I brought him down into their council.

**23:29** I found him to be accused concerning questions of their law, but having no charge deserving of death or chains.

**23:30** And when it was reported to me about the ambush that they had prepared for him, I sent him to you, notifying the accusers also to speak before you. Farewell."

**23:31** Therefore the soldiers, taking Paul according to the command given to them, led him by night to Antipatris.

**23:32** And on the next day, having sent the horsemen on to go with him, they returned to the barracks.

**23:33** When they had come to Caesarea and delivered the letter to the governor, they presented Paul before him as well.

**23:34** And when he had read it and asked what province he was from, and learned that he was from Cilicia,

**23:35** "I will hear you," he said, "when your accusers have arrived." And he ordered him to be kept under guard in Herod's praetorium.

## Translator Notes

- **23:9** — The Clementine Vulgate reads simply "some of the Pharisees" (quidam pharisæorum) without mentioning scribes of the Pharisees' party, and ends the question at "or an angel?" without the longer Byzantine addition.
- **23:25** — This entire parenthetical verse explaining the tribune's fear of a false bribery charge is a distinctive Clementine Vulgate reading (reflecting the Western text tradition) absent from standard Greek editions, which instead place the opening clause of verse 26 here.
- **23:30** — The Vulgate reads "the ambush that they had prepared for him" (quas paraverant illi), omits "immediately," and concludes the letter with the concise instruction "to speak before you. Farewell."
