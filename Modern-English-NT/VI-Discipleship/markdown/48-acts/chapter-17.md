## Text

**17:1** Now when they had traveled through Amphipolis and Apollonia, they came to Thessalonica, where there was a synagogue of the Jews.

**17:2** And according to custom, Paul went in to them, and for three Sabbaths he reasoned with them from the Scriptures,

**17:3** opening up and setting forth that it was necessary for the Christ to suffer and to rise again from the dead, and that "This is Jesus Christ, whom I proclaim to you."

**17:4** And some of them believed and were joined to Paul and Silas, as well as a great multitude of the worshipers and Gentiles, and not a few noble women.

**17:5** But the Jews, being jealous, took some wicked men from the common crowd, and having formed a mob, they stirred up the city; and taking a stand at the house of Jason, they sought to bring them out to the people.

**17:6** And when they did not find them, they dragged Jason and some brothers to the rulers of the city, shouting, "These men who stir up the city have come here too,

**17:7** whom Jason has received; and all of these act against the decrees of Caesar, saying that there is another king, Jesus."

**17:8** And they stirred up the populace and the rulers of the city as they heard these things.

**17:9** And having received security from Jason and from the rest, they released them.

**17:10** But the brothers immediately sent Paul and Silas away by night to Berea. And when they arrived, they went into the synagogue of the Jews.

**17:11** Now these were more noble than those who are in Thessalonica; they received the word with complete eagerness, searching the Scriptures every day to see if these things were so.

**17:12** And indeed many of them believed, as well as distinguished Gentile women, and not a few men.

**17:13** But when the Jews in Thessalonica learned that the word of God had been preached by Paul in Berea as well, they came there also, agitating and troubling the multitude.

**17:14** And immediately then the brothers sent Paul away to go as far as the sea; but Silas and Timothy remained there.

**17:15** Now those who were escorting Paul brought him all the way to Athens; and having received a command from him for Silas and Timothy that they should come to him as quickly as possible, they departed.

**17:16** Now while Paul was waiting for them in Athens, his spirit was provoked within him, seeing the city given over to idolatry.

**17:17** Therefore he debated in the synagogue with the Jews and the worshipers, and in the marketplace every day with those who were present.

**17:18** Now some Epicurean and Stoic philosophers were conversing with him, and some were saying, "What does this sower of words want to say?" Others, however, said, "He seems to be a proclaimer of new divinities," because he was proclaiming Jesus and the resurrection to them.

**17:19** And taking hold of him, they led him to the Areopagus, saying, "May we know what this new teaching is that is spoken by you?

**17:20** For you are bringing certain new things to our ears; we therefore want to know what these things mean."

**17:21** (Now all the Athenians and the foreign visitors devoted their time to nothing else except either to tell or to hear something new.)

**17:22** Then Paul, standing in the middle of the Areopagus, said, "Men of Athens, I see that in all respects you are, as it were, quite superstitious.

**17:23** For as I passed by and looked at your idols, I also found an altar on which was written, 'To an Unknown God.' What you therefore worship without knowing, this I proclaim to you.

**17:24** The God who made the world and all things that are in it, since He is the Lord of heaven and earth, does not dwell in temples made by hand,

**17:25** nor is He worshiped by human hands as needing anything, since He Himself gives to all life, and breath, and all things;

**17:26** and He made from one every race of mankind to dwell upon the entire face of the earth, determining the appointed times and the boundaries of their dwelling,

**17:27** to seek God, if perhaps they might grope for Him or find Him, although He is not far from each one of us.

**17:28** For in Him we live, and move, and exist, as also some of your poets have said, 'For we too are His offspring.'

**17:29** Therefore, since we are the offspring of God, we should not suppose that the Divine is like gold, or silver, or stone, a carving of the skill and thought of man.

**17:30** And indeed, overlooking the times of this ignorance, God now proclaims to mankind that everyone everywhere should repent,

**17:31** because He has appointed a day on which He is going to judge the world in equity through a man whom He has appointed, giving assurance to all by raising Him from the dead."

**17:32** Now when they heard of the resurrection of the dead, some indeed mocked, while others said, "We will hear you about this again."

**17:33** So Paul went out from their midst.

**17:34** Certain men, however, attaching themselves to him, believed; among whom were also Dionysius the Areopagite, and a woman named Damaris, and others with them.

## Translator Notes

- **17:4** — The Vulgate reads de colentibus gentilibusque ("of the worshipers and Gentiles"), coordinating two groups with -que and reading "Gentiles" rather than the Greek "devout Greeks."
- **17:6** — The Clementine Vulgate reads urbem ("the city") rather than orbem ("the world," corresponding to Greek oikoumenen).
- **17:18** — Latin seminiverbius (literally "word-sower") renders Greek spermologos ("seed-picker" or "babbler"); the Vulgate also reads novorum daemoniorum ("new divinities") where Greek has "foreign divinities."
- **17:22** — Latin superstitiosiores ("quite/more superstitious"), softened by quasi ("as it were"), renders Greek deisidaimonesterous, which can be understood either positively ("very religious") or critically ("very superstitious").
- **17:30** — The Vulgate reads hujus ignorantiae ("of this ignorance") and annuntiat ("proclaims/declares"), where Greek lacks "this" and reads "commands."
