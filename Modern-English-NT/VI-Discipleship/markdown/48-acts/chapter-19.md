## Text

**19:1** Now it happened, while Apollos was at Corinth, that Paul, having traveled through the upper regions, came to Ephesus and found certain disciples.

**19:2** And he said to them, "Have you received the Holy Spirit upon believing?" But they said to him, "No, we have not even heard if there is a Holy Spirit."

**19:3** He, however, said, "In what, then, were you baptized?" And they said, "In John's baptism."

**19:4** Now Paul said, "John baptized the people with a baptism of repentance, saying that they should believe in Him who was to come after him, that is, in Jesus."

**19:5** Having heard these things, they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.

**19:6** And when Paul had laid hands on them, the Holy Spirit came upon them, and they were speaking in tongues and prophesying.

**19:7** Now the men were about twelve in all.

**19:8** And having entered the synagogue, he spoke with boldness for three months, reasoning and persuading concerning the kingdom of God.

**19:9** But when some became hardened and did not believe, speaking evil of the way of the Lord before the multitude, he departed from them and separated the disciples, reasoning daily in the school of a certain tyrant.

**19:10** Now this took place for two years, so that all who lived in Asia heard the word of the Lord, Jews as well as Gentiles.

**19:11** And God was working no ordinary miracles through the hand of Paul,

**19:12** so that even handkerchiefs and aprons were carried from his body onto the sick, and the sicknesses departed from them, and the wicked spirits went out.

**19:13** Now some of the wandering Jewish exorcists also tried to invoke the name of the Lord Jesus over those who had evil spirits, saying, "I charge you under oath by Jesus, whom Paul preaches."

**19:14** Now there were certain Jews, seven sons of Sceva, a chief priest, who were doing this.

**19:15** But the wicked spirit answered and said to them, "Jesus I know, and Paul I know of; but who are you?"

**19:16** And the man in whom there was a most evil demon, leaping upon them and overpowering both of them, prevailed against them, so that they fled out of that house naked and wounded.

**19:17** Now this became known to all the Jews and Gentiles who lived in Ephesus, and fear fell upon all of them, and the name of the Lord Jesus was being magnified.

**19:18** And many of the believers were coming, confessing and declaring their deeds.

**19:19** And many of those who had pursued magical arts brought books together and burned them before everyone; and having calculated their prices, they found the sum of money to be fifty thousand denarii.

**19:20** So powerfully was the word of God growing and being strengthened.

**19:21** Now when these things were completed, Paul resolved in the Spirit, after passing through Macedonia and Achaia, to go to Jerusalem, saying, "After I have been there, I must see Rome also."

**19:22** And sending to Macedonia two of those who ministered to him, Timothy and Erastus, he himself remained in Asia for a time.

**19:23** Now at that time no small disturbance occurred concerning the way of the Lord.

**19:24** For a certain man named Demetrius, a silversmith making silver shrines of Diana, was bringing no small profit to the craftsmen.

**19:25** Summoning them, together with those who were workers of this sort, he said, "Men, you know that from this craft we have our income,

**19:26** and you see and hear that not only at Ephesus, but throughout almost all of Asia, this Paul has persuaded and turned away a large crowd, saying, 'Those that are made by hands are not gods.'

**19:27** And not only will this business of ours be in danger of coming into discredit, but also the temple of the great Diana will be reckoned as nothing, and even the majesty of her whom all Asia and the world worship will begin to be destroyed."

**19:28** Having heard these things, they were filled with anger and cried out, saying, "Great is Diana of the Ephesians!"

**19:29** And the city was filled with confusion, and with one mind they rushed into the theater, having seized Gaius and Aristarchus, Macedonians, companions of Paul.

**19:30** Now when Paul wanted to go in among the people, the disciples did not permit it.

**19:31** And some also of the rulers of Asia, who were his friends, sent to him, asking him not to venture into the theater.

**19:32** Meanwhile, some were shouting one thing and some another. For the assembly was in confusion, and the majority did not know for what reason they had come together.

**19:33** Now they drew Alexander out of the crowd, with the Jews pushing him forward. And Alexander, having requested silence with his hand, wanted to give an account to the people.

**19:34** When they recognized that he was a Jew, one voice arose from them all, shouting for about two hours, "Great is Diana of the Ephesians!"

**19:35** And when the city clerk had calmed the crowds, he said, "Men of Ephesus, who indeed among people does not know that the city of the Ephesians is the worshiper of the great Diana and of the offspring of Jupiter?

**19:36** Since, therefore, these things cannot be contradicted, you ought to be calm and do nothing rashly.

**19:37** For you have brought these men here, who are neither sacrilegious nor blaspheming your goddess.

**19:38** So if Demetrius and the craftsmen who are with him have a case against anyone, court sessions are held and there are proconsuls; let them accuse one another.

**19:39** But if you seek anything regarding another matter, it can be settled in the lawful assembly.

**19:40** For we are even in danger of being accused of today's sedition, since there is no one guilty concerning whom we can give an account of this gathering." And when he had said these things, he dismissed the assembly.

## Translator Notes

- **19:9** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "the way of the Lord" (viam Domini, also in 19:23, where Greek has simply "the Way") and prints lowercase tyranni cujusdam ("a certain tyrant"), rendering the Greek personal name Tyrannus as a common noun.
- **19:16** — Latin dominatus amborum ("overpowering both of them") literally refers to "both" despite the seven sons mentioned in 19:14, reflecting a reading shared with early Greek witnesses where the term for "both" was sometimes used of more than two.
- **19:24** — Throughout this episode (vv. 24, 27, 28, 34, 35), the Vulgate uses the Roman goddess name Diana rather than the Greek name Artemis.
- **19:35** — Vulgate-distinct reading: cultricem ("worshiper") and Jovisque prolis ("and of the offspring of Jupiter," using the Roman name Jupiter for Zeus) differ from the Greek terms "temple keeper" and "fallen from Zeus/heaven."
- **19:40** — The Vulgate renders the clause personally as nullus obnoxius ("no one guilty") rather than neuter ("no cause"), and follows the Clementine versification that combines Greek verses 40 and 41 into a single verse 40.
