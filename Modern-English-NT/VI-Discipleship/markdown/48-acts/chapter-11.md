## Text

**11:1** Now the apostles and brothers who were in Judea heard that the Gentiles also received the word of God.

**11:2** And when Peter had gone up to Jerusalem, those who were of the circumcision were disputing against him,

**11:3** saying, "Why did you go in to men who are uncircumcised and eat with them?"

**11:4** But Peter began explaining the sequence to them, saying:

**11:5** "I was in the city of Joppa praying, and I saw a vision in a trance: a certain vessel descending, like a large linen sheet being lowered from heaven by four corners, and it came all the way to me.

**11:6** Gazing intently into it, I looked closely, and I saw four-footed animals of the earth, and wild beasts, and crawling creatures, and birds of the sky.

**11:7** And I also heard a voice saying to me, 'Get up, Peter; kill and eat.'

**11:8** But I said, 'Certainly not, Lord, because nothing common or unclean has ever gone into my mouth.'

**11:9** But the voice answered a second time from heaven, 'What God has cleansed, you must not call common.'

**11:10** And this took place three times, and all things were taken back again into heaven.

**11:11** And look, immediately three men stood at the house in which I was, sent from Caesarea to me.

**11:12** And the Spirit told me to go with them, not hesitating at all. These six brothers also came with me, and we entered the man's house.

**11:13** And he recounted to us how he had seen an angel in his house, standing and saying to him, 'Send to Joppa and summon Simon, who is called Peter,

**11:14** who will speak to you words by which you will be saved, you and your entire household.'

**11:15** And when I had begun to speak, the Holy Spirit fell upon them, just as upon us too at the beginning.

**11:16** And I remembered the word of the Lord, as He used to say, 'John indeed baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit.'

**11:17** Therefore, if God gave to them the same grace just as to us also who believed in the Lord Jesus Christ, who was I that I could hinder God?"

**11:18** Having heard these things, they fell silent and glorified God, saying, "So then, to the Gentiles too God has given repentance to life."

**11:19** Now those who had been scattered because of the tribulation that had occurred at the time of Stephen traveled as far as Phoenicia, and Cyprus, and Antioch, speaking the word to no one except the Jews alone.

**11:20** But there were some of them, men from Cyprus and Cyrene, who, when they had entered Antioch, were speaking to the Greeks also, proclaiming the Lord Jesus.

**11:21** And the hand of the Lord was with them, and a large number of those who believed turned to the Lord.

**11:22** Now the report reached the ears of the church that was in Jerusalem concerning these things, and they sent Barnabas as far as Antioch.

**11:23** When he had arrived and seen the grace of God, he rejoiced, and he was exhorting all of them to remain in the Lord with resolve of heart,

**11:24** because he was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith. And a large crowd was added to the Lord.

**11:25** Then Barnabas departed for Tarsus to search for Saul; and when he had found him, he brought him to Antioch.

**11:26** And for an entire year they associated together there in the church and taught a large crowd, so that in Antioch the disciples were first called Christians.

**11:27** Now in these days prophets came from Jerusalem to Antioch,

**11:28** and one of them, named Agabus, stood up and indicated through the Spirit that there was going to be a great famine throughout the entire world, which took place under Claudius.

**11:29** So each of the disciples, according to what he had, resolved to send relief to the brothers living in Judea,

**11:30** which they also did, sending it to the elders by the hands of Barnabas and Saul.

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — Latin Quare introisti ("Why did you go in") frames the charge as a question, whereas the critical Greek text has a statement ("You went in"). "Men who are uncircumcised" translates the blunt Latin phrase viros praeputium habentes (literally, "men having a foreskin").
- **11:11** — The Clementine Vulgate reads in qua eram ("in which I was"), following the first-person singular Greek variant where the critical Greek text reads "in which we were."
- **11:17** — Latin eamdem gratiam ("the same grace") renders the Greek phrase meaning "the same gift."
- **11:19** — Latin sub Stephano (literally "under Stephen," meaning "at the time of Stephen") renders the Greek circumstantial phrase epi Stephano ("over" or "because of Stephen").
- **11:20** — The Vulgate reads ad Graecos ("to the Greeks"), aligning with Greek manuscripts reading Hellenas rather than Hellenistas ("Hellenists").
