## Text

**21:1** Now when it came about that we set sail, after being torn away from them, we came on a straight course to Cos, and on the following day to Rhodes, and from there to Patara.

**21:2** And when we had found a ship crossing over to Phoenicia, going aboard, we set sail.

**21:3** Now when we had come into view of Cyprus, leaving it on the left, we sailed to Syria and came to Tyre, for there the ship was going to unload the cargo.

**21:4** And having found the disciples, we stayed there seven days; and they were telling Paul through the Spirit not to go up to Jerusalem.

**21:5** And when the days were completed, we set out and went on, while all of them, together with their wives and children, escorted us all the way outside the city; and kneeling down on the shore, we prayed.

**21:6** And when we had said farewell to one another, we boarded the ship, while they returned to their own homes.

**21:7** And we, having completed the voyage from Tyre, came down to Ptolemais; and having greeted the brothers, we stayed one day with them.

**21:8** And setting out on the next day, we came to Caesarea. And entering the house of Philip the evangelist, who was one of the seven, we stayed with him.

**21:9** Now this man had four virgin daughters who prophesied.

**21:10** And while we were staying for several days, a certain prophet named Agabus arrived from Judea.

**21:11** When he had come to us, he took Paul's belt, and binding his own feet and hands, he said, "This is what the Holy Spirit says: 'The man whose belt this is, the Jews in Jerusalem will bind in this way and hand over into the hands of the Gentiles.'"

**21:12** When we had heard this, both we and those who were of that place kept pleading with him not to go up to Jerusalem.

**21:13** Then Paul answered and said, "What are you doing, weeping and afflicting my heart? For I am ready not only to be bound, but also to die in Jerusalem for the sake of the name of the Lord Jesus."

**21:14** And when we could not persuade him, we fell silent, saying, "May the Lord's will be done."

**21:15** Now after these days, having made preparations, we were going up to Jerusalem.

**21:16** And some of the disciples from Caesarea also came with us, bringing with them a certain Mnason of Cyprus, an early disciple, with whom we were to lodge.

**21:17** And when we had come to Jerusalem, the brothers received us gladly.

**21:18** Now on the following day Paul went in with us to James, and all the elders were assembled.

**21:19** When he had greeted them, he related one by one the things that God had done among the Gentiles through his ministry.

**21:20** And when they had heard it, they were magnifying God, and they said to him, "You see, brother, how many thousands there are among the Jews who have believed, and they are all zealous for the law.

**21:21** Now they have heard about you that you teach the Jews who are among the Gentiles a departure from Moses, saying that they ought not to circumcise their sons, nor to walk according to custom.

**21:22** What is it, then? Certainly the multitude must assemble, for they will hear that you have arrived.

**21:23** Therefore do this that we tell you. We have four men who have a vow upon themselves.

**21:24** Taking these men, sanctify yourself with them and pay expenses for them so that they may shave their heads; and all will know that the things they have heard about you are false, but that you yourself also walk keeping the law.

**21:25** Now concerning those from among the Gentiles who have believed, we have written, judging that they should abstain from what has been sacrificed to idols, and from blood, and from what has been strangled, and from fornication."

**21:26** Then Paul, taking the men, on the next day was purified with them and entered the temple, announcing the completion of the days of purification, until an offering should be offered for each one of them.

**21:27** Now while the seven days were coming to completion, the Jews who were from Asia, when they had seen him in the temple, stirred up all the people and laid hands on him, shouting,

**21:28** "Men of Israel, help! This is the man who, teaching everyone everywhere against the people and the law and this place, has furthermore also brought Gentiles into the temple and defiled this holy place."

**21:29** For they had seen Trophimus the Ephesian in the city with him, whom they supposed Paul had brought into the temple.

**21:30** And the whole city was thrown into commotion, and a rushing together of the people took place. And seizing Paul, they were dragging him outside the temple, and immediately the doors were shut.

**21:31** Now while they were seeking to kill him, it was reported to the tribune of the cohort that all Jerusalem was in confusion.

**21:32** He at once, taking soldiers and centurions, ran down to them. And when they had seen the tribune and the soldiers, they stopped beating Paul.

**21:33** Then the tribune approached, seized him, and ordered him to be bound with two chains; and he was inquiring who he was and what he had done.

**21:34** But some in the crowd were shouting one thing and some another. And when he could not learn anything certain because of the uproar, he ordered him to be led into the barracks.

**21:35** And when he had come to the steps, it happened that he was carried by the soldiers because of the violence of the people.

**21:36** For the multitude of the people was following, shouting, "Away with him!"

**21:37** And when Paul had begun to be led into the barracks, he said to the tribune, "Is it permitted for me to say something to you?" And he said, "Do you know Greek?

**21:38** Are you not the Egyptian who before these days stirred up an uproar and led out into the desert four thousand men of the assassins?"

**21:39** And Paul said to him, "I am indeed a Jewish man from Tarsus of Cilicia, a citizen of no obscure city. Now I ask you, permit me to speak to the people."

## Translator Notes

- **21:3** — Vulgate 'Cum apparuissemus autem Cypro' literally reads 'when we had appeared to Cyprus,' a nautical idiom for coming into view of land, corresponding to the Greek variant 'anaphanentes' ('having been shown') rather than 'anaphanantes' ('having sighted').
- **21:16** — Vulgate 'adducentes secum ... Mnasonem' resolves a debated Greek dative construction by making Mnason the direct object accompanying the travelers ('bringing with them a certain Mnason') rather than a host awaiting them in Jerusalem.
- **21:22** — Vulgate 'utique oportet convenire multitudinem' ('Certainly the multitude must assemble') preserves the longer clause shared with the Byzantine tradition, which most modern critical Greek editions omit.
- **21:28** — Vulgate reads 'gentiles' ('Gentiles') where the Greek text has 'Hellenas' ('Greeks').
- **21:38** — Vulgate 'nonne tu es Aegyptius' frames the question positively ('Are you not the Egyptian...?') rather than as the negative inference of the Greek ('ouk ara,' 'So you are not the Egyptian...?').
