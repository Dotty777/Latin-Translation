## Text

**5:1** But a certain man named Ananias, with his wife Sapphira, sold a field,

**5:2** and fraudulently withheld some of the price of the field, his wife also being aware of it; and bringing a certain part, he placed it at the feet of the apostles.

**5:3** Then Peter said, "Ananias, why has Satan tempted your heart, that you should lie to the Holy Spirit and fraudulently withhold some of the price of the field?

**5:4** While it remained, did it not remain yours, and once sold, was it not in your power? Why have you placed this matter in your heart? You have not lied to men, but to God."

**5:5** And hearing these words, Ananias fell down and breathed his last. And great fear came upon all who heard.

**5:6** Then the young men rose up and removed him, and carrying him out, buried him.

**5:7** Now there was an interval of about three hours, and his wife, not knowing what had happened, came in.

**5:8** And Peter said to her, "Tell me, woman, whether you sold the field for so much?" And she said, "Yes, for so much."

**5:9** Then Peter said to her, "Why indeed have you agreed together to test the Spirit of the Lord? Look, the feet of those who buried your husband are at the door, and they will carry you out."

**5:10** Immediately she fell down before his feet and breathed her last. And the young men, coming in, found her dead, and they carried her out and buried her beside her husband.

**5:11** And great fear came upon the whole church and upon all who heard these things.

**5:12** Now through the hands of the apostles many signs and wonders were being done among the people. And they were all of one mind in the portico of Solomon.

**5:13** But none of the rest dared to join themselves to them, yet the people magnified them.

**5:14** And a multitude of men and women believing in the Lord was increasing all the more,

**5:15** so that they brought out the infirm into the streets and placed them on cots and pallets, in order that, as Peter came, at least his shadow might overshadow any of them and they might be freed from their infirmities.

**5:16** And a multitude from the cities neighboring Jerusalem also gathered together, bringing the sick and those tormented by unclean spirits, all of whom were healed.

**5:17** Then the high priest rose up, and all who were with him (which is the sect of the Sadducees), and they were filled with jealousy,

**5:18** and they laid hands on the apostles and placed them in public custody.

**5:19** But during the night an angel of the Lord opened the doors of the prison, and leading them out, said,

**5:20** "Go, and standing in the temple, speak to the people all the words of this life."

**5:21** When they had heard this, they entered the temple at daybreak and were teaching. Now when the high priest and those who were with him arrived, they summoned the council and all the elders of the sons of Israel, and sent to the prison for them to be brought.

**5:22** But when the officers came and, after the prison was opened, did not find them, they returned and reported,

**5:23** saying, "Indeed, we found the prison shut with all diligence and the guards standing before the doors, yet on opening it we found no one inside."

**5:24** Now when the magistrate of the temple and the chief priests heard these words, they were perplexed about them as to what might happen.

**5:25** Then someone arrived and reported to them, "Look, the men whom you placed in prison are in the temple, standing and teaching the people."

**5:26** Then the magistrate went with the officers and brought them without force, for they feared the people, afraid that they might be stoned.

**5:27** And when they had brought them, they set them in the council; and the high priest questioned them,

**5:28** saying, "We strictly commanded you not to teach in this name, and look, you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching, and you want to bring the blood of this man upon us."

**5:29** But Peter and the apostles answered and said, "It is necessary to obey God rather than men.

**5:30** The God of our fathers raised up Jesus, whom you put to death by hanging Him on a tree.

**5:31** God exalted Him by His right hand as Prince and Savior, to give repentance to Israel and forgiveness of sins;

**5:32** and we are witnesses of these words, as is the Holy Spirit, whom God has given to all who obey Him."

**5:33** When they had heard these things, they were cut to the core and were planning to kill them.

**5:34** But a certain Pharisee in the council, named Gamaliel, a teacher of the law honored by all the people, stood up and ordered the men to be put outside for a short while,

**5:35** and said to them, "Men of Israel, take care for yourselves concerning these men as to what you are about to do.

**5:36** For before these days Theudas rose up, claiming himself to be somebody, with whom a number of men, about four hundred, agreed. He was killed, and all who believed him were scattered and reduced to nothing.

**5:37** After him Judas the Galilean rose up in the days of the registration and drew the people away after him; he also perished, and all, as many as agreed with him, were dispersed.

**5:38** And so now I say to you, withdraw from these men and leave them alone, because if this plan or work is from men, it will be undone;

**5:39** but if it is from God, you will not be able to undo it, or else you may even be found fighting against God." And they agreed with him.

**5:40** And summoning the apostles, after beating them, they ordered them not to speak at all in the name of Jesus, and released them.

**5:41** So they went away rejoicing from the presence of the council, because they were counted worthy to suffer dishonor for the name of Jesus.

**5:42** And every day they did not cease in the temple and throughout the houses, teaching and proclaiming the good news of Christ Jesus.

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — Vulgate reads "tentavit" ("tempted"), where the Greek text has "eplērōsen" ("filled").
- **5:6** — Vulgate reads "amoverunt eum" ("removed him"), differing from the Greek "synesteilan auton" ("wrapped him up").
- **5:15** — The final clause "et liberarentur ab infirmitatibus suis" ("and they might be freed from their infirmities") is a distinctive Western and Clementine Vulgate reading not found in the standard Greek text.
- **5:22** — Vulgate includes the ablative absolute "aperto carcere" ("after the prison was opened"), where the Greek reads simply "did not find them in the prison."
- **5:39** — Vulgate reads neuter singular "illud" ("it," referring to the plan or work), whereas the Greek has masculine plural "autous" ("them," referring to the apostles).
