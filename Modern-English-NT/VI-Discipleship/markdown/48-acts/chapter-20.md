## Text

**20:1** Now after the uproar ceased, Paul, having summoned the disciples and exhorted them, said farewell and departed to go into Macedonia.

**20:2** And when he had traveled through those regions and had exhorted them with many words, he came to Greece,

**20:3** where, when he had spent three months, a plot was laid against him by the Jews as he was about to sail to Syria; and he formed a plan to return through Macedonia.

**20:4** Now Sopater, son of Pyrrhus, a Berean, accompanied him; and from the Thessalonians, Aristarchus and Secundus, and Gaius of Derbe, and Timothy; and from Asia, Tychicus and Trophimus.

**20:5** These men, having gone on ahead, waited for us at Troas;

**20:6** but we sailed from Philippi after the days of Unleavened Bread and came to them at Troas in five days, where we stayed for seven days.

**20:7** Now on the first day of the week, when we had come together to break bread, Paul was discoursing with them, about to depart on the next day, and he prolonged his speech until midnight.

**20:8** And there were numerous lamps in the upper room where we were assembled.

**20:9** Now a certain young man named Eutychus, sitting on the windowsill, was sinking into a heavy sleep as Paul discoursed at length; carried away by sleep, he fell down from the third story and was picked up dead.

**20:10** When Paul had gone down to him, he lay down upon him, and embracing him, he said, "Do not be disturbed, for his life is in him."

**20:11** Then going up, and breaking bread and eating, and having spoken at length until daylight, he thus departed.

**20:12** And they brought the boy alive, and they were comforted in no small measure.

**20:13** Now we, embarking on the ship, sailed to Assos, intending to take Paul aboard from there; for he had so arranged, as he himself was going to travel by land.

**20:14** And when he had met us at Assos, having taken him aboard, we came to Mitylene.

**20:15** And sailing from there, on the following day we came opposite Chios, and on the next day we put in at Samos, and on the following day we came to Miletus.

**20:16** For Paul had resolved to sail past Ephesus, so that no delay might occur for him in Asia. For he was hurrying, if it were possible for him, to spend the day of Pentecost in Jerusalem.

**20:17** Now from Miletus, sending to Ephesus, he summoned the elders of the church.

**20:18** When they had come to him and were together, he said to them, "You know, from the first day on which I entered Asia, in what manner I was with you throughout the whole time,

**20:19** serving the Lord with all humility and tears and trials that came upon me from the plots of the Jews;

**20:20** how I withheld nothing of what was beneficial, proclaiming it to you and teaching you publicly and house by house,

**20:21** testifying to Jews as well as Gentiles repentance toward God and faith in our Lord Jesus Christ.

**20:22** And now, look, bound in spirit, I am going to Jerusalem, not knowing what things will happen to me in it,

**20:23** except that the Holy Spirit solemnly testifies to me throughout all the cities, saying that chains and afflictions await me in Jerusalem.

**20:24** Yet I fear none of these things, nor do I regard my life as more precious than myself, provided only that I finish my course and the ministry of the word that I received from the Lord Jesus, to testify to the gospel of the grace of God.

**20:25** And now, look, I know that you will see my face no longer, all of you among whom I passed preaching the kingdom of God.

**20:26** Therefore I solemnly testify to you on this day that I am clean from the blood of all.

**20:27** For I did not shrink back from proclaiming the entire counsel of God to you.

**20:28** Pay attention to yourselves and to the entire flock, in which the Holy Spirit has placed you as overseers to govern the church of God, which He acquired with His own blood.

**20:29** I know that ravenous wolves will enter among you after my departure, not sparing the flock.

**20:30** And from among yourselves men will rise up speaking perverse things, in order to lead the disciples away after themselves.

**20:31** For this reason, stay alert, keeping in mind that for three years, night and day, I did not cease admonishing each one of you with tears.

**20:32** And now I entrust you to God and to the word of His grace, who is able to edify and to give an inheritance among all who have been sanctified.

**20:33** I have coveted no one's silver and gold, or clothing, just as

**20:34** you yourselves know; for these hands provided for the things that I needed and for those who are with me.

**20:35** I have shown you all things: that by laboring in this way it is necessary to support the weak and to remember the word of the Lord Jesus, for He Himself said, 'It is more blessed to give rather than to receive.'"

**20:36** And after he had said these things, having knelt down, he prayed with all of them.

**20:37** Now a great weeping arose among all of them, and falling upon Paul's neck, they kept kissing him,

**20:38** grieving most of all at the word he had spoken, that they would no longer see his face. And they escorted him to the ship.

## Translator Notes

- **20:22** — The Latin ablative spiritu ("in spirit," lowercase in the Vulgate) is variously understood as referring either to Paul's own spirit or to the Holy Spirit compelling him (compare Spiritus Sanctus in 20:23).
- **20:24** — The Clementine Vulgate preserves three distinctive readings here: nihil horum vereor ("I fear none of these things"), the comparative pretiosiorem quam me ("more precious than myself," where Greek reads "dear to myself"), and ministerium verbi ("ministry of the word," where Greek reads simply "the ministry").
- **20:28** — Latin episcopos literally means "overseers" or "bishops." The Vulgate renders the following verb as regere ("to govern" or "to rule"), whereas the Greek has "to shepherd," and reads ecclesiam Dei ("the church of God").
- **20:32** — Because Latin qui ("who") is masculine, it refers unambiguously to God (Deo) rather than to the neuter word (verbo), whereas the corresponding Greek participle is grammatically ambiguous.
- **20:33** — The Clementine Vulgate includes sicut ("just as") at the end of verse 33, linking it directly to ipsi scitis ("you yourselves know") at the start of verse 34, whereas Greek editions lack "just as" and begin a new sentence at verse 34.
