## Text

**26:1** Now Agrippa said to Paul, "You are permitted to speak for yourself." Then Paul, stretching out his hand, began to give his account:

**26:2** "Concerning all the things of which I am accused by the Jews, King Agrippa, I consider myself fortunate that I am going to defend myself before you today,

**26:3** especially since you know all things, both the customs and the disputes that exist among the Jews; for this reason I implore you to listen to me patiently.

**26:4** Indeed, my life from youth, which from the beginning was among my nation in Jerusalem, all the Jews know,

**26:5** having known me from the beginning (if they are willing to bear witness) that according to the most certain sect of our religion I lived as a Pharisee.

**26:6** And now, in the hope of the promise that was made by God to our fathers, I stand subject to judgment,

**26:7** to which our twelve tribes, serving night and day, hope to attain. Concerning this hope I am accused by the Jews, king.

**26:8** Why is it judged incredible among you if God raises the dead?

**26:9** Indeed, I myself had supposed that I ought to do many opposing things against the name of Jesus the Nazarene,

**26:10** which I also did in Jerusalem; and many of the saints I myself shut up in prisons, having received authority from the chief priests; and when they were being put to death, I delivered sentence.

**26:11** And throughout all the synagogues, frequently punishing them, I kept compelling them to blaspheme; and raging even more madly against them, I persecuted them even into foreign cities.

**26:12** In these matters, while I was going to Damascus with the authority and permission of the chief priests,

**26:13** at midday on the road I saw, king, a light from heaven, surpassing the splendor of the sun, shine around me and those who were together with me.

**26:14** And when all of us had fallen down to the ground, I heard a voice speaking to me in the Hebrew language, 'Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me? It is hard for you to kick against the goad.'

**26:15** And I said, 'Who are You, Lord?' And the Lord said, 'I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.

**26:16** But rise up and stand on your feet; for to this purpose I have appeared to you, to appoint you as a servant and a witness of the things that you have seen and of those things in which I will appear to you,

**26:17** rescuing you from the people and from the Gentiles, to whom I am now sending you,

**26:18** to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light and from the power of Satan to God, that they may receive forgiveness of sins and a portion among the saints, through the faith that is in Me.'

**26:19** Therefore, King Agrippa, I was not unbelieving toward the heavenly vision,

**26:20** but to those who are in Damascus first, and in Jerusalem, and throughout the entire region of Judea, and to the Gentiles, I kept proclaiming that they should repent and turn to God, doing works worthy of repentance.

**26:21** For this reason the Jews seized me when I was in the temple and tried to kill me.

**26:22** Yet, assisted by the help of God up to this very day, I stand bearing witness to both small and great, saying nothing beyond those things which the prophets and Moses spoke would take place:

**26:23** whether the Christ is subject to suffering, whether, as the first from the resurrection of the dead, He is about to proclaim light to the people and to the Gentiles."

**26:24** While he was speaking these things and giving his account, Festus said with a loud voice, "You are mad, Paul! Much learning is turning you to madness."

**26:25** And Paul said, "I am not mad, most excellent Festus, but I am speaking words of truth and sobriety.

**26:26** For the king knows about these things, to whom I also speak with confidence; for I think that none of these things is hidden from him. Indeed, none of these things took place in a corner.

**26:27** Do you believe the prophets, King Agrippa? I know that you believe."

**26:28** Then Agrippa said to Paul, "In a small measure you are persuading me to become a Christian."

**26:29** And Paul said, "I pray to God that, both in a small measure and in a great one, not only you, but also all who are listening today, may become such as I also am, except for these chains."

**26:30** And the king rose up, and the governor, and Bernice, and those who were sitting with them.

**26:31** And when they had withdrawn, they were speaking to one another, saying, "This man has done nothing deserving of death or chains."

**26:32** And Agrippa said to Festus, "This man could have been released if he had not appealed to Caesar."

## Translator Notes

- **26:5** — Latin certissimam (literally "most certain" or "surest") renders Greek akribestaten ("strictest" or "most exact").
- **26:19** — Latin incredulus ("unbelieving" or "incredulous") translates Greek apeithes ("disobedient" or "unpersuaded"), preserving the Vulgate's focus on belief.
- **26:23** — Latin si ... si ("whether ... whether," mirroring Greek ei ... ei) introduces the two propositions, and passibilis literally means "capable of" or "subject to suffering."
- **26:28** — Latin In modico ("in a small measure" or "in a little") is famously debated—capable of meaning "in a short time," "with little effort," or "almost"—and is echoed in Paul's reply et in modico et in magno ("both in a small measure and in a great one") in verse 29.
