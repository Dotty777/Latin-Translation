## Text

**24:1** Now after five days, the chief priest Ananias came down with certain elders and a certain orator, Tertullus, and they appeared before the governor against Paul.

**24:2** And when Paul had been summoned, Tertullus began to accuse him, saying, "Since we live in great peace through you, and many things are set right through your foresight,

**24:3** we accept it always and everywhere, most excellent Felix, with all gratitude.

**24:4** Yet so that I may not detain you any longer, I beg that you hear us briefly in your clemency.

**24:5** We have found this man to be pestilent, stirring up seditions among all the Jews throughout the whole world, and an instigator of the sedition of the sect of the Nazarenes,

**24:6** who even tried to violate the temple; and having seized him, we wanted to judge him according to our law,

**24:8** ordering his accusers to come to you; from whom you yourself, in judging, will be able to learn about all these things of which we accuse him."

**24:9** And the Jews also joined in, saying that these things were so.

**24:10** Then Paul answered (the governor nodding to him to speak), "Knowing that for many years you have been a judge over this nation, I will with good courage make my defense.

**24:11** For you are able to ascertain that it has been no more than twelve days since I went up to worship in Jerusalem;

**24:12** and neither in the temple did they find me disputing with anyone or causing a crowd to gather, nor in the synagogues, nor in the city;

**24:13** nor can they prove to you the things concerning which they now accuse me.

**24:14** Yet I confess this to you, that according to the sect that they call a heresy, so I serve the Father and my God, believing all things that are written in the law and the prophets,

**24:15** having a hope in God, which these men themselves also await, that there will be a resurrection of the just and the unjust.

**24:16** In this I myself also strive to have a conscience without offense toward God and toward people always.

**24:17** Now after several years, I came to bring alms to my nation, and offerings, and vows,

**24:18** in which they found me purified in the temple, not with a crowd, nor with a tumult.

**24:19** Yet certain Jews from Asia, who ought to be present before you and accuse me if they had anything against me;

**24:20** or let these men themselves say if they found any wrongdoing in me when I stood in the council,

**24:21** except concerning this one utterance alone that I cried out while standing among them, 'Concerning the resurrection of the dead I am being judged today by you.'"

**24:22** Then Felix put them off, knowing most accurately about this Way, saying, "When the tribune Lysias comes down, I will hear you."

**24:23** And he ordered the centurion to keep him under guard, and to allow him respite, and not to forbid any of his own people to attend to him.

**24:24** Now after some days, Felix came with his wife Drusilla, who was Jewish, and called for Paul, and heard from him of the faith that is in Christ Jesus.

**24:25** And as he discoursed on righteousness, and chastity, and the judgment to come, Felix was terrified and answered, "For the present, go; at an opportune time I will summon you."

**24:26** At the same time he was also hoping that money would be given to him by Paul; for this reason he also summoned him frequently and spoke with him.

**24:27** Now when two years were completed, Felix received Porcius Festus as his successor. And wanting to grant a favor to the Jews, Felix left Paul a prisoner.

## Translator Notes

- **24:5** — The Clementine Vulgate distinctively adds "of the sedition" (auctorem seditionis) before "of the sect of the Nazarenes," intensifying Tertullus's accusation of insurrection.
- **24:8** — The Clementine Vulgate omits verse 7 (which in the longer text tradition recounts the tribune Lysias taking Paul away by force), leaving the participle "ordering" (jubens) at the start of verse 8 syntactically attached to verse 6.
- **24:14** — Where the Greek reads "according to the Way which they call a sect" and "the ancestral God," the Clementine Vulgate distinctively reads "according to the sect that they call a heresy" (secundum sectam quam dicunt hæresim) and "the Father and my God" (Patri et Deo meo).
- **24:17** — The Clementine Vulgate adds "and vows" (et vota) alongside alms and offerings, a phrase absent from standard Greek texts.
- **24:25** — The Vulgate renders the Greek term for self-control specifically as castitate ("chastity").
