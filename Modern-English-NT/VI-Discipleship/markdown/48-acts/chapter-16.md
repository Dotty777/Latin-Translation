## Text

**16:1** Now he arrived at Derbe and Lystra. And look, a certain disciple was there named Timothy, the son of a believing Jewish woman, his father being a Gentile.

**16:2** The brothers who were in Lystra and Iconium gave a good testimony about him.

**16:3** Paul wanted him to set out with him, and taking him, he circumcised him because of the Jews who were in those places. For everyone knew that his father was a Gentile.

**16:4** Now as they passed through the cities, they delivered to them the ordinances to be observed, which had been decreed by the apostles and elders who were in Jerusalem.

**16:5** And indeed the churches were being strengthened in faith, and will abound in number daily.

**16:6** Now passing through Phrygia and the region of Galatia, they were forbidden by the Holy Spirit to speak the word of God in Asia.

**16:7** And when they had come into Mysia, they were trying to go into Bithynia, and the Spirit of Jesus did not permit them.

**16:8** Now when they had passed through Mysia, they went down to Troas.

**16:9** And a vision was shown to Paul during the night: a certain Macedonian man was standing and pleading with him, and saying, "Crossing over into Macedonia, help us."

**16:10** Now when he saw the vision, we immediately sought to set out for Macedonia, having become certain that God had called us to proclaim the gospel to them.

**16:11** Now sailing from Troas, we came on a straight course to Samothrace, and on the following day to Neapolis,

**16:12** and from there to Philippi, which is the first city of a part of Macedonia, a colony. Now we were in this city for several days, conferring.

**16:13** And on the Sabbath day we went outside the gate beside a river, where there seemed to be prayer, and sitting down, we were speaking to the women who had gathered.

**16:14** And a certain woman named Lydia, a seller of purple of the city of the Thyatirans, worshiping God, listened; and the Lord opened her heart to pay attention to the things that were being spoken by Paul.

**16:15** And when she had been baptized, and her household, she pleaded, saying, "If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, enter my house and stay." And she compelled us.

**16:16** Now it happened, as we were going to prayer, that a certain girl having a spirit of Python met us, who provided great gain to her masters by divining.

**16:17** Following after Paul and us, she kept crying out, saying, "These men are servants of God Most High, who announce to you the way of salvation."

**16:18** Now she kept doing this for many days. But Paul, being grieved and turning around, said to the spirit, "I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to go out from her." And it went out at that same hour.

**16:19** Now when her masters saw that the hope of their gain had gone out, seizing Paul and Silas, they led them into the marketplace to the rulers.

**16:20** And presenting them to the magistrates, they said, "These men are throwing our city into confusion, since they are Jews,

**16:21** and they announce a custom which it is not lawful for us to accept or to practice, since we are Romans."

**16:22** And the crowd rushed against them, and the magistrates, having torn off their tunics, ordered them to be beaten with rods.

**16:23** And when they had inflicted many blows upon them, they put them into prison, commanding the keeper to guard them diligently.

**16:24** When he had received such a command, he put them into the inner prison and clamped their feet in the stocks.

**16:25** Now at midnight Paul and Silas, praying, were praising God, and those who were in custody were listening to them.

**16:26** Then suddenly a great earthquake occurred, so that the foundations of the prison were shaken. And at once all the doors were opened, and the chains of everyone were loosened.

**16:27** Now the keeper of the prison, waking up and seeing the doors of the prison open, with his sword drawn, wanted to kill himself, supposing that the prisoners had fled.

**16:28** But Paul cried out with a loud voice, saying, "Do no harm to yourself, for all of us are here."

**16:29** And having asked for a light, he went inside, and trembling with fear, he fell down at the feet of Paul and Silas.

**16:30** And leading them outside, he said, "Sirs, what must I do so that I may be saved?"

**16:31** And they said, "Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, and your household."

**16:32** And they spoke the word of the Lord to him, together with all who were in his house.

**16:33** And taking them at that hour of the night, he washed their wounds; and he himself was baptized, and all his household at once.

**16:34** And when he had led them into his house, he set a table before them and rejoiced with all his household, believing in God.

**16:35** And when day had come, the magistrates sent the lictors, saying, "Release those men."

**16:36** Now the keeper of the prison reported these words to Paul: "The magistrates have sent word that you be released; now therefore, come out and go in peace."

**16:37** But Paul said to them, "After beating us publicly, uncondemned Roman men, they put us into prison, and now are they casting us out secretly? Not so; rather, let them come

**16:38** and cast us out themselves." Now the lictors reported these words to the magistrates. And they were afraid when they heard that they were Romans.

**16:39** And coming, they pleaded with them, and leading them out, they asked them to depart from the city.

**16:40** Now going out of the prison, they went into Lydia's house; and having seen the brothers, they comforted them and set out.

## Translator Notes

- **16:1** — Here and in verse 3 the Vulgate renders the Greek term for "Greek" as "gentilis" ("Gentile").
- **16:5** — The Clementine Vulgate reads the future tense "abundabunt" ("will abound"), whereas the Greek text and some Latin witnesses read the imperfect ("were increasing/abounding").
- **16:12** — The Vulgate renders the Greek participle for "staying" as "conferentes" ("conferring" or "conversing"), highlighting discussion during their stay in Philippi.
- **16:16** — Latin "spiritum pythonem" preserves the Greek reference to a divining spirit associated with the Pythian oracle of Apollo at Delphi.
- **16:38** — In the Clementine Vulgate, Paul's closing clause begins verse 38 rather than ending verse 37, and repeats the verb "ejicio" ("cast out") from verse 37 rather than shifting to "bring out" as in the Greek.
