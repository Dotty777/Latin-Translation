## Text

**8:1** Now on that day a great persecution arose in the church that was in Jerusalem, and all of them were scattered through the regions of Judea and Samaria, except the apostles.

**8:2** And God-fearing men attended to Stephen and made a great lamentation over him.

**8:3** But Saul was laying waste to the church; entering house by house and dragging away men and women, he was handing them over into custody.

**8:4** So those who had been scattered traveled about, preaching the good news of the word of God.

**8:5** Now Philip, going down into the city of Samaria, proclaimed Christ to them.

**8:6** And the crowds were paying attention to the things that were being spoken by Philip, hearing with one accord and seeing the signs that he was doing.

**8:7** For many of those who had unclean spirits, crying out with a loud voice, came out. Moreover, many who were paralyzed and lame were cured.

**8:8** Therefore, there was great joy in that city.

**8:9** Now there was a certain man named Simon, who previously had been a sorcerer in the city, leading astray the nation of Samaria, saying that he was someone great,

**8:10** to whom all were listening, from the smallest to the greatest, saying, "This man is the power of God, which is called Great."

**8:11** And they were giving attention to him, because for a long time he had bewitched them with his sorceries.

**8:12** But when they had believed Philip preaching the good news about the kingdom of God, men and women were being baptized in the name of Jesus Christ.

**8:13** Then Simon himself also believed; and when he had been baptized, he stayed close to Philip. Seeing also signs and very great acts of power taking place, he was astonished and kept marveling.

**8:14** Now when the apostles who were in Jerusalem had heard that Samaria had accepted the word of God, they sent Peter and John to them.

**8:15** When they had come, they prayed for them that they might receive the Holy Spirit.

**8:16** For He had not yet come upon any one of them, but they had merely been baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.

**8:17** Then they were laying hands upon them, and they were receiving the Holy Spirit.

**8:18** Now when Simon had seen that the Holy Spirit was being given through the laying on of the hand of the apostles, he offered them money,

**8:19** saying, "Give me this authority also, so that anyone on whom I lay hands may receive the Holy Spirit." But Peter said to him:

**8:20** "May your money go to destruction with you, because you supposed that the gift of God could be possessed with money.

**8:21** You have no share or lot in this matter, for your heart is not upright before God.

**8:22** Therefore repent from this wickedness of yours, and pray to God, if perhaps this thought of your heart may be forgiven you.

**8:23** For I see that you are in the bile of bitterness and the bond of iniquity."

**8:24** And Simon answered and said, "Pray yourselves for me to the Lord, so that nothing of these things that you have spoken may come upon me."

**8:25** And they indeed, having testified and spoken the word of the Lord, were returning to Jerusalem, and they were preaching the good news to many regions of the Samaritans.

**8:26** Now an angel of the Lord spoke to Philip, saying, "Rise and go toward the south, to the road that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza." This is deserted.

**8:27** And rising, he departed. And look, an Ethiopian man, a eunuch, a high official of Candace, queen of the Ethiopians, who was over all her treasures, had come to worship in Jerusalem.

**8:28** And he was returning, sitting on his chariot and reading the prophet Isaiah.

**8:29** Then the Spirit said to Philip, "Approach and join yourself to this chariot."

**8:30** And Philip, running up, heard him reading the prophet Isaiah and said, "Do you think you understand what you are reading?"

**8:31** And he said, "And how can I, unless someone shows me?" And he asked Philip to come up and sit with him.

**8:32** Now the passage of Scripture that he was reading was this: "Like a sheep He was led to slaughter, and as a lamb before the one shearing it is without a voice, so He did not open His mouth.

**8:33** In humiliation His justice was taken away. Who will recount His generation? For His life will be taken away from the earth."

**8:34** And the eunuch, answering Philip, said, "I ask you, about whom does the prophet say this? About himself, or about some other person?"

**8:35** Then Philip, opening his mouth and beginning from this Scripture, preached the good news of Jesus to him.

**8:36** And while they were going along the road, they came to some water; and the eunuch said, "Here is water! What hinders me from being baptized?"

**8:37** Then Philip said, "If you believe with your whole heart, it is permitted." And in response he said, "I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God."

**8:38** And he ordered the chariot to stand still, and both of them went down into the water, Philip and the eunuch, and he baptized him.

**8:39** Now when they had come up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord caught Philip away, and the eunuch did not see him any longer. And he went along his way rejoicing.

**8:40** But Philip was found in Azotus, and as he traveled through he kept preaching the good news to all the cities until he came to Caesarea.

## Translator Notes

- **8:5** — The Clementine Vulgate prints the third-person plural verb praedicabant ("were proclaiming") with the singular subject Philippus ("Philip"), an unusual textual reading likely arising as a scribal or printing slip for singular praedicabat (or understood loosely of Philip and those with him).
- **8:7** — The Latin literally makes "many of those who had unclean spirits" (Multi eorum...) the grammatical subject of "came out" (exibant), reproducing a grammatical shift (anacoluthon) also found in some Greek manuscripts; in meaning, it is the unclean spirits that cried out and came out of those afflicted.
- **8:12** — Unlike the Greek text, which links "and the name of Jesus Christ" to Philip's preaching, the Clementine Vulgate attaches in nomine Jesu Christi ("in the name of Jesus Christ") directly to baptizabantur ("were being baptized").
- **8:22** — Where the Greek reads "the Lord," the Clementine Vulgate reads Deum ("God"); poenitentiam ... age is the standard Latin idiom for "repent."
- **8:33** — Where the Greek uses present-tense verbs in this Isaiah quotation, the Vulgate renders non aperuit in verse 32 in the past tense ("did not open") and tolletur in verse 33 in the future passive ("will be taken away").
