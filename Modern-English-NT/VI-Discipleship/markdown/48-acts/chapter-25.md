## Text

**25:1** Therefore, when Festus had come into the province, after three days he went up to Jerusalem from Caesarea.

**25:2** And the chief priests and the leading men of the Jews approached him against Paul, and they kept asking him,

**25:3** requesting a favor against him, that he would order him to be brought to Jerusalem, laying an ambush to kill him on the road.

**25:4** But Festus answered that Paul was being kept in Caesarea, and that he himself was about to depart very soon.

**25:5** "Therefore," he said, "let those among you who hold authority go down together; if there is any offense in the man, let them accuse him."

**25:6** Now having stayed among them no more than eight or ten days, he went down to Caesarea, and on the next day he sat at the tribunal and ordered Paul to be brought forward.

**25:7** When he had been brought in, the Jews who had come down from Jerusalem stood around him, bringing many serious charges which they could not prove,

**25:8** while Paul gave his account: "Neither against the law of the Jews, nor against the temple, nor against Caesar have I committed any offense."

**25:9** But Festus, wishing to do a favor for the Jews, answered Paul and said, "Do you wish to go up to Jerusalem and be judged there concerning these things before me?"

**25:10** But Paul said, "I am standing at Caesar's tribunal; there I ought to be judged. To the Jews I have done no wrong, as you know full well.

**25:11** For if I have done wrong, or have committed anything deserving of death, I do not refuse to die; but if there is nothing in the things of which these men accuse me, no one can grant me to them. I appeal to Caesar."

**25:12** Then Festus, after speaking with the council, answered, "Have you appealed to Caesar? To Caesar you will go."

**25:13** And when several days had passed, King Agrippa and Bernice came down to Caesarea to greet Festus.

**25:14** And while they were staying there for many days, Festus reported to the king about Paul, saying, "There is a certain man left behind by Felix as a prisoner,

**25:15** about whom, when I was in Jerusalem, the chief priests and the elders of the Jews approached me, requesting condemnation against him.

**25:16** To them I answered that it is not the custom of the Romans to condemn any man before the one who is accused has his accusers present and receives an opportunity for defense to clear away the charges.

**25:17** Therefore, when they had assembled here without any delay, on the following day, sitting at the tribunal, I ordered the man to be brought forward.

**25:18** Concerning him, when the accusers stood up, they brought no charge of the things about which I was suspecting evil.

**25:19** Rather, they had certain disputes against him about their own superstition, and about a certain Jesus who had died, whom Paul affirmed to be alive.

**25:20** Now I, uncertain about a dispute of this kind, asked whether he wished to go to Jerusalem and be judged there concerning these things.

**25:21** But when Paul appealed to be kept for the hearing of Augustus, I ordered him to be kept until I send him to Caesar."

**25:22** Then Agrippa said to Festus, "I myself also wanted to hear the man." "Tomorrow," he said, "you will hear him."

**25:23** So on the next day, when Agrippa and Bernice had arrived with great pageantry and had entered the audience hall with the tribunes and the foremost men of the city, at Festus's command Paul was brought forward.

**25:24** And Festus said, "King Agrippa, and all the men who are present together with us, you see this man about whom the entire multitude of the Jews petitioned me in Jerusalem, demanding and shouting out that he ought not to live any longer.

**25:25** I indeed discovered that he had committed nothing deserving of death. However, since this man himself appealed to Augustus, I decided to send him.

**25:26** Concerning him I have nothing definite to write to my lord. For this reason I have brought him forth before you, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that once an examination has been conducted, I may have something to write.

**25:27** For it seems unreasonable to me to send a prisoner and not indicate the charges against him."

## Translator Notes

- **25:12** — The Clementine Vulgate punctuates Caesarem appellasti? as a question ("Have you appealed to Caesar?"), whereas many modern editions of the Greek take it as a declarative statement.
- **25:16** — Vulgate damnare aliquem hominem ("to condemn any man") and ad abluenda crimina (literally "for washing away the charges") are distinctly Latin formulations where the Greek reads "hand over any man" and "concerning the charge."
- **25:19** — Latin superstitione ("superstition") renders Greek deisidaimonia ("religion" or "superstition") in its more dismissive Roman sense, reflecting Festus's viewpoint.
- **25:24** — The Clementine Vulgate reads Jerosolymis ("in Jerusalem") alone—without the Greek phrase "and here"—and uses two participles, petentes et acclamantes ("demanding and shouting out").
