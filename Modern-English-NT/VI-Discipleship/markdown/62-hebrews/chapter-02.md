## Text

**2:1** Therefore we must all the more heed the things that we have heard, so that we may not perhaps slip away.

**2:2** For if the word spoken through angels became firm, and every transgression and disobedience received a just repayment of wages,

**2:3** how will we escape if we neglect so great a salvation? Having begun to be declared through the Lord, it was confirmed to us by those who heard,

**2:4** with God also bearing witness through signs and wonders, and various works of power, and distributions of the Holy Spirit according to His own will.

**2:5** For God did not subject to angels the world to come, about which we are speaking.

**2:6** But someone has testified in a certain place, saying, "What is man, that You are mindful of him, or a son of man, that You visit him?

**2:7** You made him a little less than the angels; You crowned him with glory and honor, and You appointed him over the works of Your hands.

**2:8** You subjected all things under his feet." For in that He subjected all things to him, He left nothing not subjected to him. But now we do not yet see all things subjected to him.

**2:9** But we see Him who was made a little lower than the angels, Jesus, crowned with glory and honor because of the suffering of death, so that, by the grace of God, He might taste death for everyone.

**2:10** For it was fitting for Him, for whom are all things and through whom are all things, who had brought many sons into glory, to perfect the author of their salvation through suffering.

**2:11** For He who sanctifies and those who are sanctified are all from one. For this reason He is not ashamed to call them brothers, saying,

**2:12** "I will announce Your name to My brothers; in the midst of the assembly I will praise You."

**2:13** And again, "I will be trusting in Him." And again, "Here I am, and My children, whom God has given Me."

**2:14** Therefore, since the children have shared in flesh and blood, He Himself likewise partook of the same, so that through death He might destroy the one who held the dominion of death, that is, the devil,

**2:15** and might set free those who through fear of death were bound to slavery throughout their whole life.

**2:16** For nowhere does He take hold of angels, but He takes hold of the seed of Abraham.

**2:17** Therefore He had to be made like the brothers in all things, so that He might become a merciful and faithful high priest before God, to make propitiation for the sins of the people.

**2:18** For in what He Himself suffered and was tested, He is able also to help those who are being tested.

## Translator Notes

- **2:5** — The Vulgate explicitly supplies "Deus" ("God") as the subject of "subjecit," whereas the Greek leaves the subject unexpressed.
- **2:7** — The Vulgate includes the third clause from Psalm 8:7, "et constituisti eum super opera manuum tuarum" ("and You appointed him over the works of Your hands"), which is omitted in many modern critical Greek editions.
- **2:10** — The Vulgate uses the pluperfect relative clause "qui ... adduxerat" ("who had brought") and singular "per passionem" ("through suffering"), where the Greek is plural.
- **2:16** — The Vulgate renders Greek "ou depou" ("surely not") as "Nusquam" ("nowhere"); "apprehendit" means "takes hold of."
