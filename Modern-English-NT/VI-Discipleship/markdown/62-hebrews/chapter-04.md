## Text

**4:1** Let us fear, therefore, that perhaps, while a promise of entering into His rest remains, anyone among you might be considered to fall short.

**4:2** For indeed it was announced to us also, just as to them; yet the word heard did not benefit them, not being mingled with faith from the things that they heard.

**4:3** For we who have believed will enter into rest, just as He said, "As I swore in My wrath, 'They will not enter into My rest,'" although indeed the works were completed from the founding of the world.

**4:4** For He spoke in a certain place concerning the seventh day in this manner: "And God rested on the seventh day from all His works."

**4:5** And in this place again: "They will not enter into My rest."

**4:6** Since, therefore, it remains for some to enter into it, and those to whom it was announced first did not enter because of unbelief,

**4:7** He again designates a certain day, "Today," saying in David after so long a time, just as was said above: "Today, if you hear His voice, do not harden your hearts."

**4:8** For if Joshua had granted them rest, He would never speak afterward about another day.

**4:9** Consequently, a Sabbath rest remains for the people of God.

**4:10** For he who has entered into His rest has himself rested from his works as well, just as God did from His own.

**4:11** Let us hasten, therefore, to enter into that rest, so that no one may fall into that very same example of unbelief.

**4:12** For the word of God is living and effective, more penetrating than any double-edged sword, extending all the way to the division of soul and spirit, as well as of joints and marrow, and a discerner of the thoughts and intentions of the heart.

**4:13** And no creature is invisible in His sight, but all things are bare and open to the eyes of Him to whom our account is due.

**4:14** Having, therefore, a great high priest who has penetrated the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast the confession.

**4:15** For we do not have a high priest who cannot sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who has been tempted in all respects in like manner, without sin.

**4:16** Therefore let us approach the throne of grace with confidence, so that we may obtain mercy and find grace in timely help.

## Translator Notes

- **4:2** — The Vulgate reads non admistus fidei ex iis quae audierunt ("not mingled with faith from the things that they heard"), taking the participle with sermo ("word") and using the neuter quae ("the things which") rather than the Greek masculine referring to "those who heard."
- **4:3** — The Vulgate uses the future tense Ingrediemur ("will enter") where Greek has a present tense. Both here and in 4:5, Latin Si introibunt literally means "If they will enter," preserving a Semitic elliptical oath idiom expressing emphatic negation ("They will not enter").
- **4:6** — Both here and in 4:11, the Vulgate reads incredulitatem ("unbelief"), rendering Greek apeitheia ("disobedience") specifically as lack of faith.
- **4:8** — The Latin literally reads Jesus, the Greco-Latin form of the Hebrew name Joshua, referring here to Joshua son of Nun who led Israel into Canaan.
- **4:13** — Latin ad quem nobis sermo literally means "to whom our word [is]," a concise idiom signifying either "to whom our account is due" or "of whom we are speaking."
