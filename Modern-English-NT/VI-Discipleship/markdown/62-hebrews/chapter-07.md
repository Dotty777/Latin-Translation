## Text

**7:1** For this Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of the Most High God, who met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him,

**7:2** to whom Abraham also apportioned tithes of everything, being translated first indeed as king of righteousness, and then also king of Salem, which is king of peace,

**7:3** without father, without mother, without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life, yet likened to the Son of God, remains a priest perpetually.

**7:4** Now consider how great this man is, to whom Abraham the patriarch even gave tithes from the choicest things.

**7:5** And indeed, those from the sons of Levi who receive the priesthood have a commandment to take tithes from the people according to the law, that is, from their own brothers, although they themselves also came out from the loins of Abraham.

**7:6** But the one whose lineage is not counted among them took tithes from Abraham and blessed him who had the promises.

**7:7** Now without any dispute, what is lesser is blessed by the superior.

**7:8** And here indeed, men who die receive tithes; but there it is testified that he lives.

**7:9** And, so to speak, through Abraham even Levi, who received tithes, was tithed;

**7:10** for he was still in the loins of his father when Melchizedek met him.

**7:11** If therefore perfection was through the Levitical priesthood (for under it the people received the law), what further need was there for another priest to arise according to the order of Melchizedek, and not to be named according to the order of Aaron?

**7:12** For with the priesthood changed, it is necessary that a change of the law also take place.

**7:13** For He concerning whom these things are spoken belongs to another tribe, from which no one has served at the altar.

**7:14** For it is evident that our Lord arose from Judah, regarding which tribe Moses spoke nothing about priests.

**7:15** And it is still more evident if another priest arises according to the likeness of Melchizedek,

**7:16** who has become so not according to the law of a fleshly commandment, but according to the power of an indissoluble life.

**7:17** For it is testified, "You are a priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek."

**7:18** Indeed, a setting aside of the preceding commandment takes place because of its weakness and uselessness;

**7:19** for the law brought nothing to perfection; on the other hand, there is the introduction of a better hope, through which we draw near to God.

**7:20** And to the extent that this is not without an oath (indeed, others were made priests without an oath,

**7:21** but He with an oath through the One who said to Him, "The Lord has sworn, and He will not repent: You are a priest forever"),

**7:22** to that same extent Jesus has become the guarantor of a better covenant.

**7:23** And indeed, those others were made priests in greater numbers, for the reason that they were prevented by death from continuing;

**7:24** but He, because He remains forever, holds an everlasting priesthood.

**7:25** Consequently, He is also able to save perpetually those who approach God through Himself, always living to intercede for us.

**7:26** For it was fitting that we should have such a high priest: holy, innocent, undefiled, separated from sinners, and made higher than the heavens;

**7:27** who does not have a daily necessity, like the priests, to offer sacrifices first for His own offenses, and then for those of the people; for this He did once, by offering Himself.

**7:28** For the law appoints men as priests who have weakness, but the word of the oath, which is after the law, appoints the Son, perfected forever.

## Translator Notes

- **7:4** — Latin "de praecipuis" literally means "from the choicest things" (or "principal things"), rendering Greek "akrothinion" ("choicest spoils") without explicitly specifying spoils.
- **7:24** — The Vulgate renders Greek "aparabaton" ("permanent" or "non-transferable") temporally as "sempiternum" ("everlasting").
- **7:25** — The Vulgate translates Greek "eis to panteles" ("completely" or "for all time") temporally as "in perpetuum" ("perpetually") and reads "pro nobis" ("for us") where most Greek manuscripts have "for them."
- **7:27** — Both here and in 7:28, the Vulgate uses "sacerdotes" ("priests") rather than "pontifices" ("high priests"), whereas the Greek text has "archiereis" ("high priests").
