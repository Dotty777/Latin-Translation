## Text

**6:1** For this reason, setting aside the introductory word about Christ, let us be carried forward to more mature things, not again laying a foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,

**6:2** of teaching concerning baptisms, also of the laying on of hands, and of the resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.

**6:3** And this we will do, if indeed God permits.

**6:4** For it is impossible for those who have once been enlightened, have also tasted the heavenly gift, and have been made partakers of the Holy Spirit,

**6:5** and have likewise tasted the good word of God and the powers of the coming age,

**6:6** and have fallen away, to be renewed again to repentance, crucifying again for themselves the Son of God and holding Him up to public spectacle.

**6:7** For ground that drinks in the rain often coming upon it, and produces vegetation suitable for those by whom it is cultivated, receives a blessing from God;

**6:8** but when it brings forth thorns and thistles, it is rejected and close to a curse, whose end is burning.

**6:9** Yet concerning you, dearly beloved, we are confident of better things, and things nearer to salvation, even though we speak in this way.

**6:10** For God is not unjust, that He would forget your work and the love you have shown in His name, you who have served the saints and continue to serve them.

**6:11** And we desire each one of you to display the same diligence toward the fulfillment of hope all the way to the end,

**6:12** so that you may not become sluggish, but rather imitators of those who through faith and patience will inherit the promises.

**6:13** For God, in making a promise to Abraham, because He had no one greater by whom He might swear, swore by Himself,

**6:14** saying, "Surely, blessing I will bless you, and multiplying I will multiply you."

**6:15** And in this way, enduring patiently, he attained the promise.

**6:16** For people swear by someone greater than themselves, and an oath, for confirmation, is the end of every dispute among them.

**6:17** In this matter, God, wishing more abundantly to show to the heirs of the promise the unchangeableness of His counsel, interposed an oath,

**6:18** so that through two unchangeable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we may have the strongest consolation, we who have fled for refuge to lay hold of the hope set before us,

**6:19** which we have as an anchor of the soul, secure and firm, and entering all the way into the interior behind the veil,

**6:20** where Jesus entered on our behalf as a forerunner, having become a high priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — The Vulgate uses the neuter plural comparative ad perfectiora ("to more mature things," literally "to more perfect things"), whereas the Greek reads the singular abstract noun teleioteta ("maturity/perfection").
- **6:6** — The Vulgate uses the passive infinitive renovari ("to be renewed") rather than the Greek active ("to renew"), and adds the explicit adverb rursum ("again") with crucifigentes.
- **6:7** — The Vulgate reads the agent phrase a quibus colitur ("by whom it is cultivated"), where the Greek reads di' hous ("for whose sake").
- **6:12** — The Vulgate uses the future tense haereditabunt ("will inherit"), where the Greek has a present participle ("who are inheriting"); in 6:11 the Vulgate also reads expletionem ("fulfillment") for Greek plerophorian ("full assurance").
- **6:14** — The Latin quotation literally opens with Nisi ("Unless / If not"), reflecting the Semitic positive oath formula (matching Greek ei men / e men) used to express emphatic certainty ("Surely").
