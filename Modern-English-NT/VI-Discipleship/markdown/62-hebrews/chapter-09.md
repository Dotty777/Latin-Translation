## Text

**9:1** Now indeed, the first one also had ordinances of worship and an earthly sanctuary.

**9:2** For a first tabernacle was made, in which were the lampstands, and the table, and the presentation of the loaves, which is called the Holy Place.

**9:3** And behind the second veil was the tabernacle that is called the Holy of Holies,

**9:4** having a golden censer, and the ark of the covenant covered on every side with gold, in which was a golden jar holding the manna, and the rod of Aaron that had budded, and the tablets of the covenant,

**9:5** and above it were the cherubim of glory overshadowing the mercy seat; concerning which we are not now to speak in detail.

**9:6** Now with these things arranged in this way, the priests continually entered into the first tabernacle, performing the duties of the sacrifices;

**9:7** but into the second, once a year, the high priest alone, not without blood, which he offers for his own ignorance and that of the people;

**9:8** the Holy Spirit signifying this, that the way into the holy places had not yet been disclosed while the first tabernacle still had standing,

**9:9** which is a parable of the present time, according to which gifts and sacrifices are offered that cannot make the one who serves perfect with respect to conscience, only in foods and in drinks,

**9:10** and various washings, and ordinances of the flesh imposed until the time of reformation.

**9:11** But Christ, being present as a high priest of the good things to come, through the greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made by hands, that is, not of this creation,

**9:12** and not through the blood of goats or calves, but through His own blood, entered once into the holy places, having obtained eternal redemption.

**9:13** For if the blood of goats and bulls, and the sprinkled ashes of a heifer, sanctify those who are defiled for the cleansing of the flesh,

**9:14** how much more will the blood of Christ, who through the Holy Spirit offered Himself unblemished to God, cleanse our conscience from dead works to serve the living God?

**9:15** And for this reason He is the mediator of a new covenant, so that, with a death intervening for the redemption of those transgressions that were under the first covenant, those who have been called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance.

**9:16** For where there is a testament, it is necessary that the death of the testator intervene.

**9:17** For a testament is confirmed in the case of the dead; otherwise it is not yet valid while the one who made the testament lives.

**9:18** Consequently, not even the first one was dedicated without blood.

**9:19** For when every commandment of the law had been read by Moses to all the people, taking the blood of calves and goats, with water and scarlet wool and hyssop, he sprinkled both the book itself and all the people,

**9:20** saying, "This is the blood of the covenant that God has commanded for you."

**9:21** Moreover, he likewise sprinkled with blood the tabernacle and all the vessels of the ministry.

**9:22** And almost all things are cleansed in blood according to the law, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness.

**9:23** Therefore it is necessary for the copies of the heavenly things to be cleansed with these, but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these.

**9:24** For Jesus did not enter into holy places made by hands, copies of the true ones, but into heaven itself, so that He may now appear before the face of God for us;

**9:25** nor that He should offer Himself often, just as the high priest enters into the holy places year by year with blood not his own;

**9:26** otherwise He would have had to suffer repeatedly from the beginning of the world; but now, once at the consummation of the ages, He has appeared for the abolition of sin through His sacrifice.

**9:27** And just as it is appointed for human beings to die once, and after this comes judgment,

**9:28** so Christ also was offered once to drain away the sins of many; a second time He will appear without sin to those who await Him, for salvation.

## Translator Notes

- **9:2** — The Vulgate reads plural "candelabra" ("lampstands"), whereas the Greek text has singular "lychnia" ("lampstand").
- **9:14** — The Vulgate reads "per Spiritum Sanctum" ("through the Holy Spirit"), whereas most Greek manuscripts read "through an eternal Spirit."
- **9:16** — Latin "testamentum" (like Greek "diatheke") carries both the sense of "covenant" and "last will/testament," underpinning the legal argument about a testator's death across 9:15-18.
- **9:19** — The Vulgate has "lecto" ("read") and "mandato legis" ("commandment of the law"), whereas the Greek text reads "spoken" and "according to the law."
- **9:24** — The Vulgate reads "Jesus," whereas the Greek text reads "Christos" ("Christ").
- **9:28** — The Vulgate translates Greek "anenenkein" ("to bear") with "exhaurienda" ("to drain away" or "to exhaust").
