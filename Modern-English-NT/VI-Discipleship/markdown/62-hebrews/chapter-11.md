## Text

**11:1** Now faith is the substance of things to be hoped for, the proof of things that do not appear.

**11:2** For in this the elders obtained testimony.

**11:3** By faith we understand that the ages were fashioned by the word of God, so that from invisible things visible things might come into being.

**11:4** By faith Abel offered to God a greater sacrifice than Cain, through which he obtained testimony that he was righteous, with God bearing testimony to his gifts; and through it, though dead, he still speaks.

**11:5** By faith Enoch was taken away so that he would not see death, and he was not found, because God took him away; for before his removal he had testimony that he had pleased God.

**11:6** Now without faith it is impossible to please God. For the one who approaches God must believe that He exists, and that He is a rewarder to those who seek Him.

**11:7** By faith Noah, having received a response concerning the things that were not yet seen, in fear fashioned an ark for the saving of his household, through which he condemned the world and was appointed heir of the righteousness that is through faith.

**11:8** By faith the one who is called Abraham obeyed to depart for a place that he was about to receive as an inheritance; and he departed, not knowing where he was going.

**11:9** By faith he resided in the land of promise as in a foreign land, living in small huts with Isaac and Jacob, fellow heirs of the same promise.

**11:10** For he was waiting for the city that has foundations, whose architect and founder is God.

**11:11** By faith Sarah herself also, though barren, received power for the conception of seed even past the time of life, since she believed Him who had promised to be faithful.

**11:12** For this reason also, there sprang from one man (and him already lifeless) as many as the stars of heaven in multitude, and like the sand that is by the shore of the sea, innumerable.

**11:13** All these died according to faith, not having received the promises, but seeing them from afar and greeting them, and confessing that they are foreigners and guests on the earth.

**11:14** For those who say these things indicate that they are seeking a homeland.

**11:15** And indeed, if they had remembered that one from which they went out, they would certainly have had time to return.

**11:16** But now they long for a better one, that is, a heavenly one. For this reason God is not ashamed to be called their God, for He prepared a city for them.

**11:17** By faith Abraham offered Isaac when he was tested, and the one who had received the promises was offering his only-begotten son,

**11:18** to whom it was said, "In Isaac your seed will be named,"

**11:19** considering that God is able to raise up even from the dead, from which he also received him as a parable.

**11:20** By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau even concerning things to come.

**11:21** By faith Jacob, as he was dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph, and worshiped the top of his staff.

**11:22** By faith Joseph, as he was dying, made mention of the departure of the sons of Israel and gave instructions concerning his bones.

**11:23** By faith Moses, when he was born, was hidden for three months by his parents, because they saw that the infant was handsome, and they did not fear the king's edict.

**11:24** By faith Moses, when he had grown up, denied that he was the son of Pharaoh's daughter,

**11:25** choosing rather to be afflicted with the people of God than to have the delight of temporary sin,

**11:26** esteeming the reproach of Christ greater wealth than the treasure of the Egyptians, for he was looking toward the reward.

**11:27** By faith he left Egypt, not fearing the fury of the king, for he endured as though seeing the Invisible One.

**11:28** By faith he celebrated the Passover and the pouring out of blood, so that the one who was destroying the firstborn would not touch them.

**11:29** By faith they crossed the Red Sea as through dry land; when the Egyptians attempted this, they were swallowed up.

**11:30** By faith the walls of Jericho collapsed after seven days of circling.

**11:31** By faith Rahab the prostitute did not perish with the unbelievers, welcoming the spies in peace.

**11:32** And what more should I say? For time will fail me as I tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, and the prophets,

**11:33** who through faith conquered kingdoms, practiced righteousness, attained promises, closed the mouths of lions,

**11:34** extinguished the force of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, recovered strength from weakness, became mighty in war, routed the armies of foreigners.

**11:35** Women received their dead from resurrection; but others were stretched on the rack, not accepting release, so that they might find a better resurrection.

**11:36** Still others experienced mockings and whippings, and furthermore chains and prisons.

**11:37** They were stoned, they were sawn apart, they were tested, they died by the slaughter of the sword; they went about in sheepskins, in goatskins, destitute, hard-pressed, afflicted,

**11:38** of whom the world was not worthy, wandering in deserts, in mountains, in caves, and in caverns of the earth.

**11:39** And all these, approved by the testimony of faith, did not receive the promise,

**11:40** with God providing something better for us, so that they would not be brought to completion without us.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — Latin sperandarum substantia rerum, argumentum non apparentium uses the gerundive sperandarum ("things to be hoped for"), substantia ("substance"), and argumentum ("proof"), where Greek-based versions often render hypostasis and elenchos as "assurance" and "conviction."
- **11:3** — The Vulgate states the clause positively as ut ex invisibilibus visibilia fierent ("so that from invisible things visible things might come into being"), whereas the Greek states negatively that what is seen was not made out of things that appear.
- **11:8** — The Vulgate reads qui vocatur Abraham ("the one who is called Abraham") rather than "Abraham, when he was called."
- **11:21** — The Vulgate takes fastigium virgae ejus as the direct object of adoravit ("worshiped the top of his staff"), omitting the Greek preposition epi ("upon" or "leaning upon").
- **11:28** — The Vulgate reads sanguinis effusionem ("pouring out of blood") rather than "sprinkling of blood" (Greek proschysin), contrasting with sanguinis aspersionem ("sprinkling of blood") in 12:24.
