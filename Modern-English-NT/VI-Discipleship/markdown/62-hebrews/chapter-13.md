## Text

**13:1** Let brotherly love remain in you,

**13:2** and do not forget hospitality; for through this some unknowingly received angels as guests.

**13:3** Remember those who are bound, as though bound together with them, and those who suffer hardship, as you yourselves also dwell in the body.

**13:4** Let marriage be honorable among all, and the marriage bed unstained. For God will judge fornicators and adulterers.

**13:5** Let your way of life be without greed, content with what is at hand; for He Himself has said, "I will not desert you, nor will I abandon you,"

**13:6** so that we say with confidence, "The Lord is a helper to me; I will not fear what man may do to me."

**13:7** Remember your leaders, who spoke the word of God to you; looking closely at the outcome of their conduct, imitate their faith.

**13:8** Jesus Christ, yesterday and today, is the same also forever.

**13:9** Do not be drawn away by various and foreign teachings. For it is best to establish the heart by grace, not by foods, which have not benefited those walking in them.

**13:10** We have an altar from which those who serve the tabernacle do not have authority to eat.

**13:11** For the bodies of those animals whose blood is brought for sin into the holy places through the high priest are burned outside the camp.

**13:12** For this reason Jesus also suffered outside the gate, so that He might sanctify the people through His own blood.

**13:13** Let us therefore go out to Him outside the camp, carrying His reproach.

**13:14** For we do not have an enduring city here, but we seek out the one to come.

**13:15** Through Him therefore let us always offer a sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of lips confessing His name.

**13:16** And do not forget beneficence and sharing; for by such sacrifices God's favor is won.

**13:17** Obey your leaders and be subject to them. For they themselves keep watch as those who will render an account for your souls, so that they may do this with joy and not groaning; for this is not beneficial for you.

**13:18** Pray for us, for we are confident that we have a good conscience, wishing to conduct ourselves well in all things.

**13:19** And I implore you all the more to do this, so that I may be restored to you more quickly.

**13:20** Now may the God of peace, who brought out from the dead the great shepherd of the sheep, in the blood of the eternal covenant, our Lord Jesus Christ,

**13:21** equip you in every good thing, so that you may do His will, working in you what is pleasing before Him through Jesus Christ, to whom belongs glory forever and ever. Amen.

**13:22** Now I ask you, brothers, that you bear with the word of consolation. For indeed I have written to you in very few words.

**13:23** Know that our brother Timothy has been released, with whom, if he comes soon, I will see you.

**13:24** Greet all your leaders, and all the saints. The brothers from Italy greet you.

**13:25** May grace be with all of you. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **13:2** — The Vulgate reads latuerunt quidam, angelis hospitio receptis (literally "some were hidden/unaware, angels having been received as guests"), a literal Latin calque of the Greek idiom for entertaining angels unknowingly.
- **13:6** — The Vulgate uses the subjunctive faciat (non timebo quid faciat mihi homo) to form an indirect question ("I will not fear what man may do to me"), rather than splitting the quotation into a separate direct question ("What can man do to me?").
- **13:9** — The Clementine Vulgate uses the superlative Optimum ("best") and the active infinitive stabilire cor ("to establish the heart"), whereas the Greek has the positive adjective ("good") with a passive infinitive ("for the heart to be strengthened").
- **13:16** — The Vulgate renders the Greek verb for being well pleased with the passive promeretur Deus ("God's favor is won" or "God is won over"), reflecting ecclesiastical Latin usage of promereri for gaining divine favor.
- **13:21** — The Clementine Vulgate reads in omni bono ("in every good thing," without "work"), in vobis ("in you," rather than "in us"), and the explicit indicative cui est gloria ("to whom belongs glory").
