## Text

**12:1** Therefore we also, having so great a cloud of witnesses set over us, laying aside every weight and the sin that surrounds us, let us run through endurance to the contest set before us,

**12:2** looking to the author and completer of faith, Jesus, who, having joy set before Him, endured the cross, despising the shame, and sits at the right hand of the throne of God.

**12:3** For reflect carefully on Him who endured such opposition from sinners against Himself, so that you may not grow weary, failing in your minds.

**12:4** For you have not yet resisted to the point of blood, fighting against sin,

**12:5** and you have forgotten the consolation that speaks to you as to sons, saying, "My son, do not neglect the discipline of the Lord, nor grow weary while you are reproved by Him.

**12:6** For the one whom the Lord loves, He chastises, and He scourges every son whom He receives."

**12:7** Persevere in discipline. God offers Himself to you as to sons; for what son is there whom a father does not correct?

**12:8** But if you are outside discipline, of which all have become partakers, then you are illegitimate, and not sons.

**12:9** Furthermore, we indeed had the fathers of our flesh as instructors, and we revered them; will we not much more obey the Father of spirits and live?

**12:10** And they indeed, for a time of a few days, instructed us according to their own will, but He for what is beneficial, in receiving His sanctification.

**12:11** Now all discipline for the present indeed seems not to be of joy, but of sorrow; afterward, however, it will yield the most peaceful fruit of righteousness to those trained through it.

**12:12** For this reason, lift up the drooping hands and the weakened knees,

**12:13** and make straight steps for your feet, so that no one who limps may go astray, but rather be healed.

**12:14** Follow peace with everyone, and holiness, without which no one will see God,

**12:15** watching closely so that no one falls short of the grace of God, so that no root of bitterness sprouting upward may hinder, and through it many be defiled;

**12:16** so that no one be a fornicator or a profane person like Esau, who for a single meal sold his firstborn rights.

**12:17** For know that afterward also, when he desired to inherit the blessing, he was rejected; for he found no place of repentance, although he had sought it with tears.

**12:18** For you have not approached a mountain that can be touched, and a blazing fire, and a whirlwind, and darkness, and a storm,

**12:19** and the sound of a trumpet, and the voice of words; those who heard it excused themselves, so that no word would be spoken to them.

**12:20** For they could not bear what was being said: "Even if a beast touches the mountain, it will be stoned."

**12:21** And what was seen was so terrifying; Moses said, "I am terrified and trembling."

**12:22** But you have approached Mount Zion, and the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and the throng of many thousands of angels,

**12:23** and the church of the firstborn, who are enrolled in the heavens, and God, the Judge of all, and the spirits of the righteous made perfect,

**12:24** and Jesus, the mediator of the new covenant, and the sprinkling of blood, speaking better than Abel.

**12:25** See that you do not refuse Him who speaks. For if they did not escape when they refused Him who was speaking on earth, much more will we not escape, who turn away from Him who speaks to us from the heavens.

**12:26** His voice shook the earth then, but now He promises, saying, "Yet once more, and I will shake not only the earth, but also heaven."

**12:27** Now His saying, "Yet once more," declares the removal of movable things, as of things that have been made, so that the things that are immovable may remain.

**12:28** Therefore, receiving an immovable kingdom, we have grace, through which let us serve, pleasing God with fear and reverence.

**12:29** For indeed our God is a consuming fire.

## Translator Notes

- **12:1** — The Vulgate reads impositam ("set over us") rather than "surrounding us" and circumstans nos peccatum ("the sin that surrounds us") rather than "sin that so easily entangles."
- **12:7** — The Vulgate renders In disciplina perseverate as a direct imperative ("Persevere in discipline") and translates Greek prospheretai ("deals with you") literally as offert se ("offers Himself").
- **12:8** — Latin adulteri is used here in the sense of spurious or illegitimate offspring, rendering Greek nothoi ("illegitimate children").
- **12:14** — The Vulgate reads Deum ("God") where the Greek text reads ton kyrion ("the Lord").
- **12:28** — The Vulgate reads the indicative habemus gratiam ("we have grace") rather than the Greek hortatory subjunctive echomen charin ("let us be thankful"), making "grace" the means by which believers serve God in the following clause.
