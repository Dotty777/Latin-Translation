## Text

**1:1** In various ways and in many manners, God, speaking long ago to the fathers in the prophets,

**1:2** has spoken to us last of all in these days in the Son, whom He appointed heir of all things, through whom He also made the ages;

**1:3** who, being the splendor of glory and the representation of His substance, and bearing all things by the word of His power, making purification of sins, sits at the right hand of the Majesty on high,

**1:4** having become so much better than the angels, by as much as He has inherited a more distinguished name above them.

**1:5** For to which of the angels did He ever say, "You are My Son; today I have fathered You"? And again, "I will be a Father to Him, and He will be a Son to Me"?

**1:6** And when He again brings the firstborn into the world, He says, "And let all God's angels worship Him."

**1:7** And to the angels indeed He says, "He who makes His angels spirits, and His servants a flame of fire."

**1:8** But to the Son, "Your throne, God, is forever and ever; a scepter of equity is the scepter of Your kingdom.

**1:9** You loved righteousness and hated iniquity; therefore God, Your God, has anointed You with the oil of exultation above Your companions."

**1:10** And, "You, Lord, in the beginning founded the earth, and the heavens are the works of Your hands.

**1:11** They will perish, but You will remain; and they will all grow old like a garment,

**1:12** and like a cloak You will change them, and they will be changed; but You are the very same, and Your years will not fail."

**1:13** But to which of the angels did He ever say, "Sit at My right hand until I set Your enemies as a footstool for Your feet"?

**1:14** Are they not all serving spirits, sent for service on account of those who will obtain the inheritance of salvation?

## Translator Notes

- **1:3** — The Vulgate uses the present tense "sedet" ("sits") rather than the Greek aorist ("sat down"), and renders Greek "charakter tes hypostaseos autou" as "figura substantiae ejus" ("the representation of His substance").
- **1:7** — Latin "spiritus" (rendering Greek "pneumata") can mean either "spirits" or "winds"; translating "spirits" preserves the direct verbal link with "serving spirits" ("spiritus") in 1:14.
- **1:8** — The Vulgate reads "regni tui" ("of Your kingdom"), whereas some modern critical Greek editions read "of his kingdom."
- **1:12** — The Vulgate reads "mutabis eos" ("You will change them," matching Greek witnesses with "allaxeis"), whereas most modern critical Greek editions read "roll them up" ("helixeis").
