## Text

**1:1** The elder to dearest Gaius, whom I love in truth.

**1:2** Dearest, concerning all things I offer prayer that you may go forward prosperously and be well, just as your soul fares prosperously.

**1:3** I rejoiced greatly when brothers came and bore testimony to your truth, just as you are walking in truth.

**1:4** I have no grace greater than these things: to hear that my children are walking in truth.

**1:5** Dearest, you act faithfully in whatever you perform for the brothers, and this for strangers,

**1:6** who gave testimony to your love in the sight of the church; doing well, you will send them forward in a way worthy of God.

**1:7** For they set out for the sake of His name, accepting nothing from the Gentiles.

**1:8** We therefore ought to welcome such people, so that we may be fellow workers of the truth.

**1:9** I would perhaps have written to the church, but he who loves to hold first place among them, Diotrephes, does not receive us;

**1:10** because of this, if I come, I will bring to remembrance his works, which he does, babbling against us with malicious words; and as though these things were not enough for him, he himself does not even welcome the brothers, and those who do welcome them he forbids and casts out of the church.

**1:11** Dearest, do not imitate evil, but what is good. The one who does good is from God; the one who does evil has not seen God.

**1:12** Testimony is given for Demetrius by all, and by the truth itself; and we also bear testimony, and you know that our testimony is true.

**1:13** I had many things to write to you, but I did not wish to write to you with ink and pen.

**1:14** But I hope to see you very soon, and we will speak mouth to mouth. Peace to you. The friends greet you. Greet the friends by name.

## Translator Notes

- **1:4** — Vulgate 'gratiam' ("grace") reflects the textual variant 'charin' rather than 'charan' ("joy"), and 'horum' is plural ("these things").
- **1:6** — Vulgate 'benefaciens, deduces' pairs a present participle ("doing well") with a future main verb ("you will send forward"), reversing the Greek syntax ("you will do well, having sent them forward").
- **1:7** — Vulgate explicitly includes the pronoun 'ejus' ("His") in 'Pro nomine enim ejus', whereas the Greek text reads simply "the Name."
- **1:9** — Vulgate 'Scripsissem forsitan' ("I would perhaps have written") uses a counterfactual pluperfect subjunctive with "perhaps," contrasting with the Greek aorist indicative ("I wrote something").
- **1:10** — Vulgate 'eos qui suscipiunt' ("those who do welcome them") lacks the Greek participle for wishing or desiring ("those who want to").
