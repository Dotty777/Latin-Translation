## Text

**4:1** I solemnly testify before God and Jesus Christ, who is going to judge the living and the dead, by His coming and His kingdom:

**4:2** preach the word, be persistent whether opportune or inopportune; reprove, implore, rebuke in all patience and teaching.

**4:3** For there will be a time when they will not tolerate sound teaching, but according to their own desires they will heap up teachers for themselves, itching in their ears,

**4:4** and they will indeed turn their hearing away from the truth, but will be turned to fables.

**4:5** As for you, keep watch, toil in all things, do the work of an evangelist, complete your ministry. Be sober.

**4:6** For I am already being poured out as a libation, and the time of my departure is imminent.

**4:7** I have contended in the good contest, I have completed the course, I have guarded the faith.

**4:8** For what remains, the crown of righteousness is stored up for me, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will render to me on that day, and not only to me, but also to those who love His coming.

**4:9** Hurry to come to me soon.

**4:10** For Demas left me, loving this age, and went away to Thessalonica; Crescens to Galatia, Titus to Dalmatia.

**4:11** Luke alone is with me. Take Mark and bring him with you, for he is useful to me for ministry.

**4:12** But I sent Tychicus to Ephesus.

**4:13** When you come, bring with you the cloak that I left at Troas with Carpus, and the books, but especially the parchments.

**4:14** Alexander the coppersmith did many evil things to me; the Lord will render to him according to his works.

**4:15** Avoid him yourself as well, for he strongly resisted our words.

**4:16** In my first defense no one stood with me, but all deserted me; may it not be charged to them.

**4:17** But the Lord stood beside me and strengthened me, so that through me the preaching might be completed and all the nations might hear; and I was rescued from the mouth of the lion.

**4:18** The Lord has rescued me from every evil work and will save me into His heavenly kingdom, to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen.

**4:19** Greet Prisca and Aquila, and the house of Onesiphorus.

**4:20** Erastus remained in Corinth. But I left Trophimus ill at Miletus.

**4:21** Hurry to come before winter. Eubulus, Pudens, Linus, Claudia, and all the brothers greet you.

**4:22** The Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Grace be with you. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **4:2** — The Vulgate orders the three imperatives argue, obsecra, increpa ("reprove, implore, rebuke"), placing "implore" before "rebuke," whereas standard Greek editions order them "reprove, rebuke, exhort."
- **4:5** — The Clementine Vulgate adds the closing imperative Sobrius esto ("Be sober."), a Vulgate-distinct doublet reflecting the Greek verb already rendered by vigila ("keep watch") at the beginning of the verse.
- **4:8** — Vulgate diligunt is present tense ("who love") rather than the Greek perfect ("who have loved").
- **4:9** — In the Latin text this sentence (Festina ad me venire cito) ends verse 8, and Latin verse 9 begins "For Demas left me."
- **4:18** — The Vulgate reads the perfect tense Liberavit ("has rescued") rather than the Greek future ("will rescue"), pairing the Lord's past rescue with His future saving into His heavenly kingdom (salvum faciet).
- **4:22** — The Vulgate preserves the fuller title Dominus Jesus Christus ("The Lord Jesus Christ") and the closing Amen, whereas critical Greek texts read simply "The Lord" and omit "Amen."
