## Text

**2:1** You therefore, my son, be strengthened in the grace that is in Christ Jesus,

**2:2** and the things that you heard from me through many witnesses, entrust these to faithful men who were qualified to teach others as well.

**2:3** Labor like a good soldier of Christ Jesus.

**2:4** No one serving as a soldier for God entangles himself in worldly affairs, so that he may please Him to whom he has proved himself.

**2:5** For indeed, anyone who competes in a contest is not crowned unless he has competed lawfully.

**2:6** The laboring farmer ought first to partake of the fruits.

**2:7** Understand the things I am saying, for the Lord will give you understanding in all things.

**2:8** Be mindful that the Lord Jesus Christ has risen from the dead, from the seed of David, according to my gospel,

**2:9** in which I labor even to bonds, like one doing evil; yet the word of God is not bound.

**2:10** For this reason I endure all things on account of the elect, so that they too may obtain the salvation that is in Christ Jesus, with heavenly glory.

**2:11** A faithful saying: for if we have died with Him, we will also live with Him;

**2:12** if we endure, we will also reign with Him; if we deny Him, He also will deny us;

**2:13** if we do not believe, He remains faithful; He cannot deny Himself.

**2:14** Remind them of these things, solemnly testifying before the Lord. Do not quarrel over words, for it is useful for nothing except the subversion of those who hear.

**2:15** Take diligent care to present yourself approved to God, a worker not to be put to shame, handling the word of truth rightly.

**2:16** But avoid profane and empty talk, for they advance greatly toward ungodliness,

**2:17** and their speech creeps like cancer; among them are Hymenaeus and Philetus,

**2:18** who have fallen away from the truth, saying that the resurrection has already taken place, and have subverted the faith of some.

**2:19** Yet the firm foundation of God stands, having this seal: "The Lord knows who are His," and, "Everyone who names the name of the Lord must depart from iniquity."

**2:20** Now in a great house there are not only golden and silver vessels, but also wooden and earthen ones, and some indeed for honor, yet some for dishonor.

**2:21** If anyone therefore purifies himself from these things, he will be a vessel for honor, sanctified, and useful to the Lord, ready for every good work.

**2:22** Now flee from youthful desires, and pursue righteousness, faith, hope, love, and peace with those who call upon the Lord from a pure heart.

**2:23** But avoid foolish and undisciplined questions, knowing that they produce quarrels.

**2:24** And a servant of the Lord ought not to quarrel, but to be gentle toward all, ready to teach, patient,

**2:25** correcting with restraint those who resist the truth, if perhaps at some time God may grant them repentance to come to know the truth,

**2:26** and they may come to their senses from the snares of the devil, by whom they are held captive at his will.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — The Clementine Vulgate reads the imperfect "were" (erant), whereas Greek manuscripts and some Vulgate editions read the future "will be" (erunt).
- **2:4** — Vulgate adds "for God" (Deo), absent from the Greek, and renders Greek "the one who enlisted him" as "Him to whom he has proved himself" (cui se probavit).
- **2:10** — Vulgate reads "heavenly glory" (gloria cælesti) where the Greek text has "eternal glory."
- **2:14** — Vulgate turns the Greek indirect command ("not to quarrel") into a direct second-person imperative addressed to Timothy ("Do not quarrel," Noli contendere).
- **2:22** — Vulgate includes "hope" (spem) in the list of virtues, absent from the Greek text.
