## Text

**4:1** Masters, render to your slaves what is just and fair, knowing that you have a Master in heaven as well.

**4:2** Persevere in prayer, keeping watch in it with thanksgiving,

**4:3** praying at the same time for us as well, that God may open to us a door for the word to speak the mystery of Christ (on account of which I am also bound),

**4:4** so that I may make it manifest in the way I ought to speak.

**4:5** Walk in wisdom toward those who are outside, redeeming the time.

**4:6** Let your speech always be in grace, seasoned with salt, so that you may know how you ought to respond to each one.

**4:7** Tychicus, the dearly loved brother, faithful minister, and fellow servant in the Lord, will make known to you all things concerning me,

**4:8** whom I have sent to you for this very purpose, that he may know the things concerning you and comfort your hearts,

**4:9** with Onesimus, the dearly loved and faithful brother, who is one of you. They will make known to you all things that are taking place here.

**4:10** Aristarchus, my fellow prisoner, greets you, and Mark, the cousin of Barnabas (concerning whom you have received instructions: if he comes to you, welcome him),

**4:11** and Jesus, who is called Justus. They are of the circumcision; these alone are my helpers in the kingdom of God, who have been a consolation to me.

**4:12** Epaphras, who is one of you, a servant of Christ Jesus, greets you, always earnestly concerned for you in prayers, so that you may stand perfect and full in all the will of God.

**4:13** For I testify for him that he has great labor for you, and for those who are in Laodicea and those in Hierapolis.

**4:14** Luke, the dearly loved physician, and Demas greet you.

**4:15** Greet the brothers who are in Laodicea, and Nympha, and the church that is in her house.

**4:16** And when this letter has been read among you, see to it that it is also read in the church of the Laodiceans, and that you yourselves read the one that belongs to the Laodiceans.

**4:17** And say to Archippus, "See to the ministry that you received in the Lord, so that you fulfill it."

**4:18** The greeting is in my own hand, Paul's. Be mindful of my chains. Grace be with you. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **4:8** — The Vulgate reads "that he may know the things concerning you" (ut cognoscat quæ circa vos sunt), agreeing with one Greek manuscript tradition against another that reads "that you may know how we are."
- **4:12** — The Vulgate renders the Greek participle meaning "struggling" as "earnestly concerned" (sollicitus) and reads "full" (pleni), reflecting a Greek variant meaning "filled" rather than "fully assured."
- **4:15** — Latin Nympham and the pronoun ejus are grammatically ambiguous in gender and may refer either to a woman ("Nympha... her house") or to a man ("Nymphas... his house").
- **4:16** — The Vulgate reads "the one that belongs to the Laodiceans" (eam, quæ Laodicensium est), a possessive genitive differing from the Greek phrasing "the letter from Laodicea."
- **4:18** — The Vulgate concludes with "Amen," matching the traditional Greek text, though it is omitted in many early Greek manuscripts.
