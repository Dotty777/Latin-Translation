## Text

**2:1** For I want you to know what concern I have for you, and for those who are at Laodicea, and for all who have not seen my face in the flesh,

**2:2** so that their hearts may be comforted, being instructed in love, and to all the riches of the fullness of understanding, to the knowledge of the mystery of God the Father and of Christ Jesus,

**2:3** in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden.

**2:4** Now I say this so that no one may deceive you by loftiness of words.

**2:5** For even though I am absent in body, yet I am with you in spirit, rejoicing and seeing your order and the steadfastness of your faith that is in Christ.

**2:6** Therefore, just as you received Jesus Christ the Lord, walk in Him,

**2:7** rooted and built up in Him, and established in faith, just as you also learned, abounding in Him in thanksgiving.

**2:8** See to it that no one deceives you through philosophy and empty deceit, according to the tradition of men, according to the elements of the world, and not according to Christ,

**2:9** because in Him all the fullness of divinity dwells bodily,

**2:10** and you have been filled in Him, who is the head of every principality and authority,

**2:11** in whom you were also circumcised with a circumcision not made by hand, in the stripping off of the body of the flesh, but in the circumcision of Christ,

**2:12** buried together with Him in baptism, in which you also rose again through faith in the working of God, who raised Him from the dead.

**2:13** And when you were dead in transgressions and the uncircumcision of your flesh, He made you alive together with Him, forgiving you all transgressions,

**2:14** blotting out the handwritten record of the decree that was against us, which was contrary to us; and He took it out of the way, fastening it to the cross,

**2:15** and stripping the principalities and authorities, He exposed them boldly, openly triumphing over them in Himself.

**2:16** Therefore let no one judge you in food or in drink, or in respect to a feast day, or a new moon, or Sabbaths,

**2:17** which are a shadow of things to come, but the body is Christ's.

**2:18** Let no one lead you astray, delighting in humility and the worship of angels, walking in things that he has not seen, puffed up in vain by the mind of his flesh,

**2:19** and not holding fast to the head, from whom the whole body, supplied and built together through ligaments and joints, grows into the increase of God.

**2:20** If therefore you died with Christ from the elements of this world, why do you still make decrees as though living in the world?

**2:21** "Do not touch, nor taste, nor handle,"

**2:22** which are all for destruction by their very use, according to the precepts and teachings of men,

**2:23** which indeed have a semblance of wisdom in superstition and humility and in not sparing the body, not in any honor for the filling of the flesh.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — The Vulgate reads 'instructi in caritate' ('instructed in love') rather than Greek 'knit together in love,' and reads 'Dei Patris et Christi Jesu' ('of God the Father and of Christ Jesus') where critical Greek editions read 'of God, Christ.'
- **2:8** — The Vulgate translates Greek 'sylagogon' ('taking captive') as 'decipiat' ('deceives'), using the same verb as in verse 4.
- **2:18** — The Vulgate renders Greek 'katabrabeueto' ('disqualify') as 'seducat' ('lead astray'), and reads 'quae non vidit ambulans' ('walking in things that he has not seen'), preserving the negative 'non' absent from critical Greek editions.
- **2:20** — The Vulgate uses the active verb 'decernitis' ('you make decrees') rather than the Greek middle/passive ('you submit to decrees'), and adds 'hujus' ('of this world').
- **2:23** — The Vulgate renders Greek 'ethelothreskia' ('self-made religion') as 'superstitione' ('superstition'), and translates the notoriously difficult closing clause literally as 'non in honore aliquo ad saturitatem carnis' ('not in any honor for the filling of the flesh').
