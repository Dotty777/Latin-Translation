## Text

**1:1** Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ through the will of God, and Timothy the brother,

**1:2** to those who are at Colossae, the saints and faithful brothers in Christ Jesus.

**1:3** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. We give thanks to God and the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, always praying for you,

**1:4** hearing of your faith in Christ Jesus and the love that you have toward all the saints,

**1:5** because of the hope that is stored up for you in the heavens, which you heard of in the word of the truth of the gospel,

**1:6** which has come to you, just as it is also in the entire world, and is bearing fruit and growing, just as in you, from that day on which you heard and came to know the grace of God in truth,

**1:7** just as you learned from Epaphras, our most beloved fellow servant, who is a faithful minister of Christ Jesus on your behalf,

**1:8** who also revealed to us your love in the Spirit.

**1:9** For this reason we also, from the day we heard, do not cease praying for you and asking that you may be filled with the knowledge of His will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding,

**1:10** so that you may walk worthily of God, pleasing Him in all things, bearing fruit in every good work and growing in the knowledge of God,

**1:11** strengthened with all power according to the might of His splendor, in all patience and forbearance with joy,

**1:12** giving thanks to God the Father, who has made us worthy for a share of the inheritance of the saints in the light,

**1:13** who rescued us from the authority of darkness and transferred us into the kingdom of the Son of His love,

**1:14** in whom we have redemption through His blood, the forgiveness of sins,

**1:15** who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation,

**1:16** because in Him all things were created in the heavens and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or principalities or authorities; all things have been created through Him and in Him,

**1:17** and He is before all, and all things hold together in Him.

**1:18** And He is the head of the body, the church, who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, so that in all things He Himself may hold first place,

**1:19** because in Him it was well pleasing for all fullness to dwell,

**1:20** and through Him to reconcile all things to Himself, making peace through the blood of His cross, whether the things on earth or the things in the heavens.

**1:21** And although you were once alienated and enemies in mind in evil works,

**1:22** yet now He has reconciled you in the body of His flesh through death, to present you holy and unblemished and blameless before Him,

**1:23** if indeed you remain in the faith, grounded and steadfast and unmoved from the hope of the gospel that you heard, which was proclaimed in all creation that is under heaven, of which I, Paul, was made a minister.

**1:24** And I now rejoice in sufferings for you, and fill up the things that are lacking of the sufferings of Christ in my flesh for His body, which is the church,

**1:25** of which I was made a minister according to the stewardship of God that was given to me for you, so that I may fulfill the word of God,

**1:26** the mystery that was hidden from ages and generations, but has now been revealed to His saints,

**1:27** to whom God willed to make known the riches of the glory of this mystery among the Gentiles, which is Christ, in you the hope of glory,

**1:28** whom we proclaim, correcting every person and teaching every person in all wisdom, so that we may present every person perfect in Christ Jesus;

**1:29** in which I also labor, striving according to His working, which He works in me with power.

## Translator Notes

- **1:3** — In the Clementine Vulgate, the greeting 'Grace to you and peace...' opens verse 3 rather than closing verse 2, and includes 'and the Lord Jesus Christ,' which is absent from critical Greek editions.
- **1:10** — The Vulgate reads 'digne Deo' ('worthily of God'), whereas the Greek manuscripts read 'worthy of the Lord.'
- **1:14** — The Vulgate includes 'per sanguinem ejus' ('through His blood'), matching the Byzantine Greek tradition (cf. Eph 1:7) rather than critical Greek editions.
- **1:16** — The Vulgate ends the verse with ablative 'in ipso' ('in Him'), differing from Greek 'eis auton' ('for/toward Him').
- **1:27** — Where verse 26 uses 'mysterium' ('mystery'), the Vulgate switches here to 'sacramentum' ('sacrament,' in the patristic sense of a sacred mystery or hidden divine plan), both rendering the same Greek word.
