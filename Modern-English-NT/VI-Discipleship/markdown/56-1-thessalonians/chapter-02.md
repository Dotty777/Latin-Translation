## Text

**2:1** For you yourselves know, brothers, our entrance to you, that it was not in vain;

**2:2** but having suffered beforehand and been subjected to mistreatment, as you know, in Philippi, we had confidence in our God to speak to you the gospel of God in much anxiety.

**2:3** For our exhortation is not from error, nor from impurity, nor in deceit,

**2:4** but just as we were approved by God so that the gospel might be entrusted to us, so we speak, not as pleasing people, but God, who tests our hearts.

**2:5** For neither at any time were we found in speech of flattery, as you know, nor in a pretext for greed (God is witness),

**2:6** nor seeking glory from people, either from you or from others.

**2:7** Although we could have been a burden to you as apostles of Christ, yet we became little children in your midst, just as a nursing mother cherishes her own children.

**2:8** Longing for you in this way, we eagerly wanted to impart to you not only the gospel of God, but also our souls, because you became most dear to us.

**2:9** For you are mindful, brothers, of our labor and weariness: working night and day, so that we would not burden any of you, we proclaimed the gospel of God among you.

**2:10** You are witnesses, and so is God, how holy, and just, and without blame we were toward you who have believed,

**2:11** just as you know how, with each one of you, like a father with his own children,

**2:12** imploring you and comforting you, we testified that you should walk worthy of God, who called you into His own kingdom and glory.

**2:13** For this reason we also give thanks to God without interruption, because when you received from us the word of the hearing of God, you received it not as a word of human beings, but, as it truly is, the word of God, who works in you who have believed.

**2:14** For you became imitators, brothers, of the churches of God that are in Judea in Christ Jesus, because you also suffered the same things from your fellow countrymen, just as they also did from the Jews,

**2:15** who killed both the Lord Jesus and the prophets, and persecuted us, and do not please God, and oppose all people,

**2:16** forbidding us to speak to the Gentiles so that they may be saved, so that they always fill up their sins; for the wrath of God has come upon them to the very end.

**2:17** But we, brothers, having been bereaved of you for the space of an hour, in sight, not in heart, hastened all the more to see your face with much longing,

**2:18** because we wanted to come to you, indeed I, Paul, both once and again; yet Satan hindered us.

**2:19** For what is our hope, or joy, or crown of glory? Is it not you, before our Lord Jesus Christ at His coming?

**2:20** For you are our glory and joy.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — Vulgate 'in multa sollicitudine' literally means 'in much anxiety' (or 'anxious concern'), differing in nuance from the Greek 'in much conflict/struggle.'
- **2:7** — Vulgate 'parvuli' ('little children') follows the Greek textual variant 'nepioi' ('infants/little children') rather than 'epioi' ('gentle').
- **2:13** — Vulgate 'verbum auditus Dei' ('the word of the hearing of God') reproduces the Greek genitive idiom meaning 'the message of God that you heard.' In addition, the masculine relative pronoun 'qui' ('who works') agrees grammatically with 'Dei' ('God') rather than with neuter 'verbum' ('word').
- **2:19** — Vulgate 'corona gloriae' reads 'crown of glory,' rendering the Greek phrase 'crown of boasting/exultation.'
