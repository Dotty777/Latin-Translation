## Text

**3:1** Therefore, when we could endure it no longer, it seemed good to us to remain in Athens alone,

**3:2** and we sent Timothy, our brother and minister of God in the gospel of Christ, to strengthen you and to exhort you concerning your faith,

**3:3** so that no one may be shaken in these afflictions; for you yourselves know that we have been appointed for this.

**3:4** For even when we were with you, we kept telling you beforehand that we would suffer afflictions, just as indeed happened, and you know.

**3:5** For this reason I also, enduring it no longer, sent to learn about your faith, for fear that perhaps the one who tempts might have tempted you, and our labor might become in vain.

**3:6** But now that Timothy has come to us from you and reported to us your faith and love, and that you always have a good memory of us, longing to see us just as we also long to see you,

**3:7** for this reason we have been comforted, brothers, in you, in all our distress and affliction, through your faith,

**3:8** because now we live, if you stand in the Lord.

**3:9** For what thanksgiving can we render back to God for you in all the joy with which we rejoice because of you before our God,

**3:10** praying all the more night and day that we may see your face and complete those things that are lacking in your faith?

**3:11** Now may our God and Father Himself, and our Lord Jesus Christ, direct our way to you.

**3:12** And may the Lord multiply you and make your love abound toward one another and toward all, just as we also do toward you,

**3:13** to strengthen your hearts without blame in holiness before our God and Father, at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ with all His saints. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — Vulgate 'ministrum Dei' ('minister of God') follows the Greek textual variant 'diakonon tou theou' rather than 'synergon tou theou' ('co-worker of God').
- **3:12** — Vulgate 'Vos ... multiplicet, et abundare faciat caritatem vestram' ('multiply you and make your love abound') places 'your love' in the accusative as the object of 'make abound,' whereas the Greek uses a dative ('make you increase and abound in love').
- **3:13** — The Clementine Vulgate concludes verse 13 with 'Amen,' agreeing with the majority Greek textual tradition against the earliest Greek manuscripts.
