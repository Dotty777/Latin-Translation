## Text

**2:1** Yet there were also false prophets among the people, just as there will also be lying teachers among you, who will introduce sects of destruction; and they deny the Lord who bought them, bringing swift destruction upon themselves.

**2:2** And many will follow their debaucheries, through whom the way of truth will be blasphemed;

**2:3** and in greed they will traffic in you with fabricated words; for them judgment from long ago is not idle, and their destruction does not slumber.

**2:4** For if God did not spare the angels when they sinned, but handed them over, dragged down with ropes of the underworld into Tartarus to be tormented, to be reserved for judgment;

**2:5** and did not spare the original world, but guarded Noah, the eighth person, a herald of righteousness, bringing a flood upon the world of the ungodly;

**2:6** and reducing the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah to ashes, condemned them to overthrow, setting them as an example of those who are about to act impiously;

**2:7** and rescued righteous Lot, oppressed by the outrage and debauched conduct of abominable men

**2:8** (for in sight and hearing he was righteous, dwelling among those who from day to day tormented his righteous soul with unjust deeds),

**2:9** then the Lord knows how to rescue the godly from trial, yet to reserve the unjust for the day of judgment to be tormented,

**2:10** and especially those who walk after the flesh in the lust of uncleanness and despise dominion. Bold, pleasing themselves, they do not fear to introduce sects, blaspheming,

**2:11** whereas angels, though they are greater in strength and power, do not bring a reviling judgment against themselves.

**2:12** But these, like irrational beasts, by nature for capture and for ruin, blaspheming in the things they do not know, will perish in their own corruption,

**2:13** receiving the wages of unrighteousness, counting daytime luxuries as pleasure; defilements and blemishes, overflowing with luxuries, reveling with you in their banquets,

**2:14** having eyes full of adultery and of unceasing sin. Enticing unstable souls, having a heart trained in greed, children of a curse,

**2:15** abandoning the straight way, they have gone astray, having followed the way of Balaam from Bosor, who loved the wages of iniquity;

**2:16** yet he received a rebuke for his own madness: a mute beast of burden, speaking with a man's voice, restrained the folly of the prophet.

**2:17** These are springs without water and mists driven by whirlwinds, for whom the gloom of darkness is reserved.

**2:18** For speaking proud words of emptiness, they entice through desires of fleshly debauchery those who are barely escaping those who live in error,

**2:19** promising them freedom, while they themselves are slaves of corruption; for by whomever someone is overcome, of him he is also a slave.

**2:20** For if, fleeing the defilements of the world in the knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, they are entangled in these again and overcome, their latter state has become worse for them than the former.

**2:21** For it would have been better for them not to have known the way of righteousness than, after acknowledging it, to turn back from that holy commandment which was handed down to them.

**2:22** For that saying of the true proverb has happened to them: "A dog returned to its own vomit," and, "A washed sow in a wallow of mud."

## Translator Notes

- **2:4** — The Vulgate reads "with ropes of hell" (rudentibus inferni) and adds "to be tormented" (cruciandos), whereas modern critical Greek editions read "chains" or "pits of gloom" without the added phrase.
- **2:8** — Unlike the Greek, in which Lot torments his own righteous soul, the Vulgate uses a plural verb (cruciabant) so that the wicked inhabitants of Sodom are the ones who tormented Lot's righteous soul from day to day.
- **2:10** — The Vulgate reads "they do not fear to introduce sects" (sectas non metuunt introducere), whereas the Greek speaks of not trembling to blaspheme "glorious ones" (angelic majesties).
- **2:11** — The Vulgate uses the reflexive "against themselves" (adversum se) and omits "before the Lord," whereas Greek manuscripts read "against them before the Lord."
- **2:15** — The Vulgate preserves "from Bosor" (ex Bosor, matching the Greek New Testament form Bosor), whereas the Old Testament Hebrew names Balaam's father "Beor" (Num 22:5).
