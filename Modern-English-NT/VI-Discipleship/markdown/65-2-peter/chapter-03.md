## Text

**3:1** Look, beloved, I am writing this second letter to you, in which I stir up your sincere mind by way of reminder,

**3:2** so that you may be mindful of those words which I spoke beforehand from the holy prophets, and of your apostles' precepts of the Lord and Savior.

**3:3** Knowing this first: that in the last days mockers will come in deception, walking according to their own lusts

**3:4** and saying, "Where is the promise, or His coming? For since the fathers fell asleep, all things continue in this way from the beginning of creation."

**3:5** For this willfully escapes their notice: that the heavens existed before, and the earth standing together out of water and through water by the word of God,

**3:6** through which the world of that time, flooded with water, perished.

**3:7** But the heavens that now exist, and the earth, have been stored up by the same word, reserved for fire for the day of judgment and destruction of ungodly men.

**3:8** Yet let this one thing not escape your notice, beloved, that with the Lord one day is like a thousand years, and a thousand years like one day.

**3:9** The Lord does not delay His promise, as some suppose, but acts patiently for your sake, not wishing any to perish, but all to return to repentance.

**3:10** Yet the day of the Lord will come like a thief, in which the heavens will pass away with great force, and the elements will be dissolved by heat, and the earth and the works that are in it will be burned up.

**3:11** Since, therefore, all these things are to be dissolved, what sort of people ought you to be in holy ways of life and acts of godliness,

**3:12** awaiting and hastening toward the coming of the day of the Lord, through which the heavens, burning, will be dissolved, and the elements will melt in the blaze of fire?

**3:13** Yet we await new heavens and a new earth according to His promises, in which righteousness dwells.

**3:14** For this reason, beloved, awaiting these things, be diligent to be found by Him spotless and unblemished in peace,

**3:15** and consider the forbearance of our Lord to be salvation, just as our beloved brother Paul also wrote to you according to the wisdom given to him,

**3:16** as also in all his letters, speaking in them about these matters, in which there are certain things difficult to understand, which the unlearned and unstable distort, as they also do the rest of the Scriptures, to their own destruction.

**3:17** You, therefore, brothers, knowing this beforehand, be on your guard so that, led away by the error of the foolish, you do not fall from your own steadfastness;

**3:18** but grow in grace and in the knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. To Him be glory both now and to the day of eternity. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — The Vulgate uniquely reads a first-person active verb, "which I spoke beforehand" (quae praedixi), and plural "precepts" (praeceptorum), whereas the Greek has a passive participle ("spoken beforehand by the holy prophets") and singular "commandment."
- **3:4** — The Vulgate reads "the promise, or His coming" (promissio, aut adventus ejus), joining the two nouns with "or" where the Greek reads "the promise of His coming."
- **3:10** — The Vulgate reads "will be burned up" (exurentur), matching the majority Greek tradition rather than the reading "will be found" (or "exposed") in modern critical Greek editions.
- **3:17** — The Vulgate reads "of the foolish" (insipientium), whereas the Greek reads "of the lawless."
