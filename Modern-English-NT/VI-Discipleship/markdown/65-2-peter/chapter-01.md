## Text

**1:1** Simon Peter, a servant and apostle of Jesus Christ, to those who have obtained a faith equal with ours in the righteousness of our God and Savior Jesus Christ.

**1:2** May grace and peace be brought to fullness for you in the knowledge of God and of Christ Jesus our Lord,

**1:3** as all things of His divine power that are for life and godliness have been granted to us through the knowledge of Him who called us by His own glory and virtue,

**1:4** through whom He has granted us the greatest and precious promises, so that through these you may become sharers of the divine nature, fleeing the corruption of that lust which is in the world.

**1:5** Now you, applying all diligence, supply in your faith virtue, and in virtue knowledge,

**1:6** and in knowledge self-restraint, and in self-restraint patience, and in patience godliness,

**1:7** and in godliness affection for the brotherhood, and in affection for the brotherhood love.

**1:8** For if these things are present with you and abound, they will make you neither idle nor without fruit in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:9** For the one to whom these things are not at hand is blind and groping by hand, falling into forgetfulness of the cleansing of his former sins.

**1:10** Therefore, brothers, be all the more diligent to make your calling and election sure through good works; for in doing these things, you will never sin.

**1:11** For in this way an entrance will be abundantly supplied to you into the eternal kingdom of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.

**1:12** For this reason I will begin always to remind you of these things, even though you know them and are established in the present truth.

**1:13** And I consider it right, as long as I am in this tent, to rouse you by way of reminder,

**1:14** being certain that the laying aside of my tent is swift, just as our Lord Jesus Christ also made clear to me.

**1:15** And I will make every effort so that after my departure you may also frequently be able to call these things to remembrance.

**1:16** For we made known to you the power and presence of our Lord Jesus Christ not by following cleverly crafted fables, but having become observers of His majesty.

**1:17** For He received honor and glory from God the Father when a voice of this kind came down to Him from the magnificent Glory: "This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; listen to Him."

**1:18** And we ourselves heard this voice brought from heaven when we were with Him on the holy mountain.

**1:19** And we have a firmer prophetic word, to which you do well to pay attention, as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts,

**1:20** understanding this first: that no prophecy of Scripture comes about by one's own interpretation.

**1:21** For prophecy was never brought by human will, but inspired by the Holy Spirit, holy men of God spoke.

## Translator Notes

- **1:4** — The Vulgate reads "through whom" (per quem, masculine singular, referring back to the one who called us in 1:3), whereas the Greek reads "through which" (plural, referring to glory and virtue).
- **1:9** — Latin "groping by hand" (manu tentans) is a Vulgate-distinct rendering where the Greek means "nearsighted" or "closing the eyes."
- **1:10** — The Vulgate includes "through good works" (per bona opera), reflecting a variant Greek manuscript tradition absent from modern critical Greek editions.
- **1:17** — The Vulgate adds "listen to Him" (ipsum audite) at the end of the Transfiguration voice (compare Matt 17:5), a clause absent from modern critical Greek editions of 2 Peter.
- **1:21** — The Vulgate reads "inspired by the Holy Spirit" (Spiritu Sancto inspirati) and "holy men of God" (sancti Dei homines), whereas critical Greek editions read "carried along by the Holy Spirit" and "men from God."
