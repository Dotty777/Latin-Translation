## Text

**1:1** Jude, a servant of Jesus Christ, and brother of James, to those who are loved in God the Father, and preserved for Christ Jesus, and called.

**1:2** May mercy, and peace, and love be fulfilled for you.

**1:3** Dearest ones, applying all diligence to write to you concerning your common salvation, I found it necessary to write to you, imploring you to strive earnestly for the faith delivered once for all to the saints.

**1:4** For certain men have slipped in secretly (who long ago were written down beforehand for this judgment), ungodly men, turning the grace of our God into debauchery, and denying the only Sovereign and our Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:5** Now I want to remind you, though you know all things once for all, that Jesus, having saved the people from the land of Egypt, afterward destroyed those who did not believe;

**1:6** and the angels who did not keep their own rulership, but abandoned their own dwelling, He has reserved in eternal bonds under gloom for the judgment of the great day.

**1:7** Just as Sodom and Gomorrah and the neighboring cities, having given themselves over to sexual immorality in a similar way and going away after different flesh, were made an example, enduring the penalty of eternal fire.

**1:8** In the same way, these men also defile the flesh, scorn dominion, and blaspheme majesty.

**1:9** When Michael the archangel, disputing with the devil, argued about the body of Moses, he did not dare to bring a judgment of blasphemy, but said, "May the Lord command you."

**1:10** But these men blaspheme whatever things they do not know; and whatever things they know by nature, like mute animals, in these things they are corrupted.

**1:11** Woe to them, for they have gone away in the way of Cain, and have poured themselves out in the error of Balaam for wages, and have perished in the defiance of Korah!

**1:12** These are stains in their feasts, banqueting together without fear, feeding themselves, clouds without water that will be carried around by winds, autumn trees, fruitless, twice dead, uprooted,

**1:13** fierce waves of the sea, foaming up their own disgraces, wandering stars, for whom the storm of darkness has been kept forever.

**1:14** Now Enoch, the seventh from Adam, also prophesied about these men, saying, "Look, the Lord comes with His holy thousands

**1:15** to carry out judgment against all, and to convict all the ungodly regarding all the works of their ungodliness by which they have acted in an ungodly manner, and regarding all the harsh things that ungodly sinners have spoken against God."

**1:16** These are complaining grumblers, walking according to their own desires, and their mouth speaks arrogant things, admiring people for the sake of gain.

**1:17** But you, dearest ones, be mindful of the words that were spoken beforehand by the apostles of our Lord Jesus Christ,

**1:18** who kept telling you that in the last time mockers will come, walking according to their own desires in acts of ungodliness.

**1:19** These are the ones who separate themselves, natural, not having the Spirit.

**1:20** But you, dearest ones, building yourselves up upon your most holy faith, praying in the Holy Spirit,

**1:21** keep yourselves in the love of God, awaiting the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ for eternal life.

**1:22** And indeed reprove some who have been judged;

**1:23** but save others, snatching them from the fire. And on still others have mercy in fear, hating even that stained tunic which is of the flesh.

**1:24** Now to Him who is able to preserve you without sin and to set you before the sight of His glory without stain, in exultation, at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ,

**1:25** to the only God our Savior, through Jesus Christ our Lord, be glory and magnificence, sovereignty and power, before every age, and now, and for all ages of ages. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **1:5** — The Vulgate explicitly reads "Jesus" as the one who saved the people from Egypt, agreeing with major early Greek manuscripts, whereas other Greek witnesses, including the Textus Receptus, read "the Lord."
- **1:8** — The Clementine Vulgate omits the Greek participle meaning "in their dreaming" and uses the singular "majesty" (majestatem) where the Greek has the plural "glorious ones" (doxas).
- **1:12** — The Vulgate reads "stains in their feasts" (in epulis suis maculae) rather than the Greek "hidden reefs in your love-feasts," and uses the future verb "will be carried around" (circumferentur).
- **1:22** — The Vulgate reads "reprove some who have been judged" (arguite judicatos), introducing a three-group instruction across verses 22-23, whereas critical Greek editions read "have mercy on those who doubt."
- **1:24** — The Vulgate reads "without sin" (sine peccato) rather than the Greek "from stumbling," and includes the closing phrase "at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ" (in adventu Domini nostri Jesu Christi), which is absent from standard Greek editions.
