## Text

**3:1** Remind them to be subject to rulers and authorities, to obey commands, to be ready for every good work,

**3:2** to slander no one, not to be quarrelsome, but gentle, showing all meekness toward all people.

**3:3** For we also were once foolish, unbelieving, going astray, enslaved to desires and various pleasures, living in malice and envy, hateful, hating one another.

**3:4** But when the kindness and benevolence of God our Savior appeared,

**3:5** not from works of righteousness which we ourselves did, but according to His mercy He saved us, through the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit,

**3:6** whom He poured out upon us abundantly through Jesus Christ our Savior,

**3:7** so that, justified by His grace, we may be heirs according to the hope of eternal life.

**3:8** The word is faithful, and I want you to insist on these things, so that those who believe in God may take care to excel in good works. These things are good and useful for people.

**3:9** But avoid foolish questions, and genealogies, and disputes, and quarrels about the law; for they are useless and futile.

**3:10** Avoid a heretical person after a first and second correction,

**3:11** knowing that such a person is subverted and sins, being condemned by his own judgment.

**3:12** When I send Artemas or Tychicus to you, hurry to come to me at Nicopolis, for I have decided to spend the winter there.

**3:13** Send Zenas the lawyer and Apollos on their way with care, so that nothing may be lacking to them.

**3:14** And let our people also learn to excel in good works for necessary uses, so that they may not be unfruitful.

**3:15** All who are with me greet you. Greet those who love us in the faith. The grace of God be with all of you. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **3:5** — Both genitives, regenerationis and renovationis, depend on lavacrum ("washing"), the accusative object of per: "the washing of regeneration and of renewal of the Holy Spirit."
- **3:8** — Latin bonis operibus praeesse (also in 3:14) literally means "to stand at the head of" or "lead in good works," rendering a Greek verb meaning "to devote oneself to" or "take the lead in."
- **3:10** — The Vulgate renders the Greek term for "factious" or "divisive" with the Christian Latin word haereticum ("heretical").
- **3:15** — The Clementine Vulgate includes Dei ("of God") in "The grace of God" and concludes with "Amen," whereas most critical Greek texts read simply "Grace be with all of you."
