## Text

**1:1** Paul, a servant of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ, according to the faith of the elect of God and the knowledge of the truth which is according to godliness,

**1:2** in hope of eternal life, which God, who does not lie, promised before the times of the ages;

**1:3** but at His own times He manifested His word in the preaching which was entrusted to me according to the command of God our Savior.

**1:4** To Titus, a beloved son according to the common faith: grace and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Savior.

**1:5** For this reason I left you in Crete, so that you may set right the things that are lacking and appoint elders throughout the cities, just as I also directed you:

**1:6** if anyone is blameless, the husband of one wife, having faithful children, not under accusation of debauchery nor insubordinate.

**1:7** For an overseer must be blameless, as God's steward: not arrogant, not hot-tempered, not addicted to wine, not violent, not greedy for shameful gain,

**1:8** but hospitable, kind, sober, righteous, holy, self-controlled,

**1:9** embracing the faithful word which is according to the teaching, so that he may be able to exhort in sound teaching and to reprove those who contradict.

**1:10** For there are also many who are disobedient, empty talkers, and deceivers, especially those who are of the circumcision,

**1:11** who must be refuted, who subvert entire households, teaching what they should not for the sake of shameful gain.

**1:12** One of them, a prophet of their own, said, "Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy bellies."

**1:13** This testimony is true. Because of this, rebuke them severely, so that they may be sound in the faith,

**1:14** not paying attention to Jewish fables and commands of people who turn themselves away from the truth.

**1:15** All things are clean to those who are clean, but to the defiled and unbelieving nothing is clean; rather, both their mind and conscience are defiled.

**1:16** They profess that they know God, but by their deeds they deny Him, since they are detestable, unbelieving, and disqualified for every good work.

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — Latin ante tempora saecularia, literally "before the times of the ages," renders the Greek expression for "before eternal times."
- **1:4** — The Vulgate reads dilecto filio ("beloved son"), whereas the Greek reads "true/genuine child."
- **1:11** — The Vulgate uses redargui ("be refuted" or "reproved"), where the Greek uses a verb meaning "to silence" or "muzzle."
- **1:16** — In Christian Latin of the Vulgate, incredibiles is used actively for "unbelieving" or "refusing obedience," rendering the Greek term for "disobedient."
