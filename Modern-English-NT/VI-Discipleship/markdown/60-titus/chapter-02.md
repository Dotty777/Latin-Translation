## Text

**2:1** But as for you, speak the things that are fitting for sound teaching:

**2:2** that older men be sober, modest, prudent, sound in faith, in love, in patience;

**2:3** that older women likewise be in holy demeanor, not slanderers, not enslaved to much wine, teaching well,

**2:4** so that they may teach prudence to the younger women, to love their husbands, to love their children,

**2:5** to be prudent, chaste, sober, taking care of the home, kind, subject to their own husbands, so that the word of God may not be blasphemed.

**2:6** Likewise, exhort the younger men to be sober.

**2:7** In all things present yourself as an example of good works, in teaching, in integrity, in dignity,

**2:8** sound, irreproachable speech, so that the one who is on the opposing side may be ashamed, having nothing evil to say about us.

**2:9** Servants are to be subject to their own masters, pleasing in all things, not contradicting,

**2:10** not defrauding, but showing good faith in all things, so that they may adorn the teaching of God our Savior in all things.

**2:11** For the grace of God our Savior has appeared to all people,

**2:12** training us so that, renouncing impiety and worldly desires, we may live soberly, righteously, and piously in this age,

**2:13** awaiting the blessed hope and the coming of the glory of the great God and our Savior Jesus Christ,

**2:14** who gave Himself for us, so that He might redeem us from all iniquity and cleanse for Himself an acceptable people, a pursuer of good works.

**2:15** Speak these things, and exhort, and reprove with all authority. Let no one despise you.

## Translator Notes

- **2:5** — The Clementine Vulgate lists six qualities here, adding sobrias ("sober") alongside prudentes ("prudent"), whereas the Greek lists five.
- **2:8** — The Vulgate reads de nobis ("about us"), where many Greek manuscripts read "about you."
- **2:11** — The Vulgate reads gratia Dei Salvatoris nostri ("the grace of God our Savior"), rendering the Greek adjective for "bringing salvation" as the genitive title "of our Savior."
- **2:13** — The Vulgate uses adventum ("coming") for Greek "appearing"; the Latin genitive magni Dei, et Salvatoris nostri Jesu Christi can refer either to Jesus Christ alone as "the great God and our Savior" or to God the Father and Jesus Christ.
- **2:14** — The Vulgate renders Greek "a people for His own possession" with populum acceptabilem ("an acceptable people").
