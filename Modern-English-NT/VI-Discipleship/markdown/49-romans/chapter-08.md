## Text

**8:1** So then, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh.

**8:2** For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has freed me from the law of sin and death.

**8:3** For what was impossible for the Law, in that it was weakened through the flesh—God, by sending His own Son in the likeness of the flesh of sin and concerning sin, condemned sin in the flesh,

**8:4** so that the righteous requirement of the Law might be fulfilled in us, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.

**8:5** For those who are according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh, while those who are according to the Spirit set their thoughts on the things of the Spirit.

**8:6** For the mindset of the flesh is death, but the mindset of the Spirit is life and peace;

**8:7** because the wisdom of the flesh is hostile to God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can it be.

**8:8** Now those who are in the flesh are not able to please God.

**8:9** You, however, are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if indeed the Spirit of God dwells in you. Yet if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, this person does not belong to Him.

**8:10** Now if Christ is in you, the body indeed is dead because of sin, while the spirit is alive because of justification.

**8:11** And if the Spirit of Him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you, He who raised Jesus Christ from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies because of His Spirit who dwells in you.

**8:12** Therefore, brothers, we are debtors not to the flesh, to live according to the flesh.

**8:13** For if you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the flesh, you will live.

**8:14** For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.

**8:15** For you have not received a spirit of slavery again in fear, but you have received a Spirit of adoption as sons, in whom we cry out, "Abba (Father)."

**8:16** For the Spirit Himself bears witness to our spirit that we are sons of God.

**8:17** And if sons, also heirs: heirs indeed of God, and fellow heirs with Christ, if indeed we suffer with Him so that we may also be glorified with Him.

**8:18** For I reckon that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the coming glory that will be revealed in us.

**8:19** For the eager expectation of creation awaits the revelation of the sons of God.

**8:20** For creation was subjected to futility, not willingly, but because of Him who subjected it in hope,

**8:21** because creation itself will also be freed from the slavery of corruption into the freedom of the glory of the sons of God.

**8:22** For we know that all creation groans and suffers the pains of childbirth even until now.

**8:23** And not only creation, but we ourselves also, having the firstfruits of the Spirit, even we groan within ourselves, awaiting adoption as sons of God, the redemption of our body.

**8:24** For in hope we were saved. Now hope that is seen is not hope; for what someone sees, why does he hope for it?

**8:25** Yet if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience.

**8:26** And likewise the Spirit also helps our weakness; for we do not know what we should pray for as we ought, but the Spirit Himself pleads for us with inexpressible groanings.

**8:27** And the One who searches hearts knows what the Spirit desires, because He pleads for the saints according to God.

**8:28** And we know that for those who love God, all things work together toward good, for those who according to His purpose have been called as saints.

**8:29** For those whom He foreknew, He also predestined to become conformed to the image of His Son, so that He Himself might be the firstborn among many brothers.

**8:30** And those whom He predestined, these He also called; and those whom He called, these He also justified; and those whom He justified, those He also glorified.

**8:31** What then will we say to these things? If God is for us, who is against us?

**8:32** He who did not spare even His own Son, but handed Him over for us all, how has He not also freely given us all things with Him?

**8:33** Who will bring a charge against God's chosen ones? It is God who justifies;

**8:34** who is there to condemn? It is Christ Jesus, who died, or rather who also rose again, who is at the right hand of God, who also intercedes for us.

**8:35** Who then will separate us from the love of Christ? Tribulation, or distress, or famine, or nakedness, or danger, or persecution, or sword?

**8:36** (Just as it is written: "For Your sake we are put to death all day long; we have been reckoned like sheep for slaughter.")

**8:37** Yet in all these things we overcome because of Him who loved us.

**8:38** For I am convinced that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, nor might,

**8:39** nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — The Vulgate includes the clause qui non secundum carnem ambulant ("who do not walk according to the flesh"), absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts.
- **8:2** — Latin reads first-person singular liberavit me ("has freed me") rather than "you."
- **8:3** — Latin de peccato literally reads "concerning sin" (often understood in context as "for a sin offering").
- **8:4** — Latin justificatio legis literally means "the justification/righteous requirement of the Law."
- **8:7** — Latin shifts from prudentia ("mindset/prudence") in verse 6 to sapientia ("wisdom") here.
- **8:11** — Latin reads Jesum Christum ("Jesus Christ") in the second clause, and propter inhabitantem Spiritum ejus ("because of His Spirit who dwells") uses causal propter rather than instrumental "through."
- **8:13** — The Vulgate reads facta carnis ("the deeds of the flesh") rather than Greek "the deeds of the body."
- **8:15** — Latin preserves the Aramaic address Abba followed by parenthetical (Pater).
- **8:16** — Latin testimonium reddit spiritui nostro uses the dative ("bears witness to our spirit") rather than Greek "with our spirit."
- **8:18** — Latin in nobis reads "in us" rather than "to us."
- **8:23** — The Vulgate explicitly reads adoptionem filiorum Dei ("adoption as sons of God").
- **8:28** — Two distinctive Vulgate features: omnia ("all things") is the subject of cooperantur ("work together"), and vocati sunt sancti adds sancti ("called as saints"). "His" is implied by secundum propositum ("according to the purpose").
- **8:32** — Latin donavit is past/perfect tense ("has He not also freely given") rather than future tense.
- **8:37** — Latin propter eum ("because of Him") is causal rather than instrumental "through Him."
- **8:38** — The Vulgate includes both virtutes ("powers") and fortitudo ("might") in the list.
