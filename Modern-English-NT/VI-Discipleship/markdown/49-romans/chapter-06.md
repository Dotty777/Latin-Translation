## Text

**6:1** What, then, will we say? Will we remain in sin so that grace may abound?

**6:2** Far from it. For we who died to sin, how will we still live in it?

**6:3** Or are you unaware that all of us who were baptized in Christ Jesus were baptized in His death?

**6:4** For we were buried together with Him through baptism into death, so that just as Christ rose from the dead through the glory of the Father, so we also might walk in newness of life.

**6:5** For if we have become planted together in the likeness of His death, we will likewise be so in that of His resurrection,

**6:6** knowing this, that our old man was crucified together with Him, so that the body of sin might be destroyed, and we might no longer serve sin.

**6:7** For the one who has died has been justified from sin.

**6:8** Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we will also live together with Christ,

**6:9** knowing that Christ, rising from the dead, no longer dies; death will no longer have mastery over Him.

**6:10** For in dying to sin, He died once; yet in living, He lives to God.

**6:11** In the same way, consider yourselves also to be dead indeed to sin, yet living to God, in Christ Jesus our Lord.

**6:12** Therefore, let sin not reign in your mortal body so that you obey its cravings.

**6:13** And do not present your members as weapons of iniquity to sin, but present yourselves to God as those living from the dead, and your members as weapons of righteousness to God.

**6:14** For sin will not have mastery over you, for you are not under law, but under grace.

**6:15** What then? Will we sin because we are not under law, but under grace? Far from it.

**6:16** Do you not know that to whomever you present yourselves as slaves for obedience, you are slaves of the one whom you obey, whether of sin to death, or of obedience to righteousness?

**6:17** Now thanks be to God that you were slaves of sin, yet you obeyed from the heart that pattern of teaching into which you were delivered.

**6:18** And freed from sin, you were made slaves of righteousness.

**6:19** I speak in a human way because of the weakness of your flesh: for just as you presented your members to serve impurity and iniquity leading to iniquity, so now present your members to serve righteousness leading to sanctification.

**6:20** For when you were slaves of sin, you were free from righteousness.

**6:21** What fruit, then, did you have at that time in those things over which you are now ashamed? For their end is death.

**6:22** Now, however, freed from sin and made slaves to God, you have your fruit leading to sanctification, and as the end, eternal life.

**6:23** For the pay of sin is death. Yet the grace of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **6:3** — The Vulgate uses the ablative 'in morte ipsius' ('in His death') here, contrasting with the accusative 'in mortem' ('into death') in 6:4.
- **6:5** — Latin 'complantati' ('planted together') uses an explicitly botanical metaphor where the Greek term means more broadly 'grown together' or 'united with'.
- **6:7** — Latin 'justificatus est' literally reads 'has been justified' from sin, retaining the same legal verb used in 5:1 and 5:9 rather than 'freed'.
- **6:23** — Latin 'Gratia' translates Greek 'charisma' ('free gift') with the standard term 'grace', contrasting unmerited grace with 'stipendia' ('pay' or military wages).
