## Text

**2:1** For this reason you are without excuse, man, everyone who judges. For in what you judge another, you condemn yourself, for you practice the same things that you judge.

**2:2** For we know that the judgment of God is according to truth against those who practice such things.

**2:3** Now do you suppose this, man, you who judge those who practice such things and do them, that you will escape the judgment of God?

**2:4** Or do you despise the riches of His goodness, and patience, and longsuffering? Are you unaware that the kindness of God leads you to repentance?

**2:5** But according to your hardness and unrepentant heart, you are storing up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath and of the revelation of the righteous judgment of God,

**2:6** who will repay each one according to his works:

**2:7** to those indeed who according to patience in good work seek glory, and honor, and incorruption, eternal life;

**2:8** but to those who act from contention and who do not assent to the truth, but trust in iniquity, wrath and indignation.

**2:9** Tribulation and distress upon every soul of man who works evil, of the Jew first and of the Greek;

**2:10** but glory, and honor, and peace to everyone who works good, to the Jew first and to the Greek;

**2:11** for there is no favoritism with God.

**2:12** For all who have sinned without the law will perish without the law, and all who have sinned in the law will be judged by the law.

**2:13** For the hearers of the law are not righteous before God, but the doers of the law will be justified.

**2:14** Now when Gentiles, who do not have the law, naturally do the things of the law, such people, not having the law, are a law to themselves;

**2:15** who show the work of the law written in their hearts, their own conscience bearing witness to them, and their thoughts between themselves accusing or even defending them,

**2:16** on the day when God will judge the hidden things of men, according to my Gospel, through Jesus Christ.

**2:17** But if you bear the name of a Jew, and rest in the law, and boast in God,

**2:18** and know His will, and approve the things that are more profitable, being instructed through the law,

**2:19** you are confident that you yourself are a guide of the blind, a light to those who are in darkness,

**2:20** an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of infants, having the form of knowledge and of truth in the law.

**2:21** You, therefore, who teach another, do not teach yourself; you who preach that one must not steal, you steal;

**2:22** you who say that one must not commit adultery, you commit adultery; you who abhor idols, you commit sacrilege;

**2:23** you who boast in the law, through transgression of the law you dishonor God.

**2:24** (For the name of God is blasphemed through you among the nations, as it is written.)

**2:25** Circumcision indeed is beneficial if you observe the law; but if you are a transgressor of the law, your circumcision has become uncircumcision.

**2:26** If, therefore, the uncircumcised one keeps the righteous ordinances of the law, will not his uncircumcision be counted as circumcision?

**2:27** And will that which is uncircumcision by nature, fulfilling the law, judge you, who through the letter and circumcision are a transgressor of the law?

**2:28** For he is not a Jew who is one in what is manifest, nor is that circumcision which is manifest in the flesh;

**2:29** but he is a Jew who is one in secret, and circumcision is of the heart, in spirit, not in letter; whose praise is not from men, but from God.

## Translator Notes

- **2:17** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "Si autem" ("But if"), matching the Byzantine Greek tradition, whereas modern critical Greek editions read "Ide" ("See"); the Vulgate treats verses 19-20 as the concluding clause of the sentence.
- **2:21** — In the Clementine Vulgate, verses 21-23 are punctuated as direct declarative indictments rather than rhetorical questions.
- **2:22** — The Vulgate renders the Greek "hierosyleis" ("rob temples") more broadly as "sacrilegium facis" ("commit sacrilege").
- **2:29** — Latin "in spiritu, non littera" lacks articles and may refer either to the Holy Spirit or to inward spiritual reality contrasted with the written letter of the law.
