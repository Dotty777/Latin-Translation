## Text

**11:1** I say then: Has God rejected His people? Far from it. For I also am an Israelite, from the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin.

**11:2** God has not rejected His people, whom He foreknew. Or do you not know what the Scripture says in Elijah, how he appeals to God against Israel?

**11:3** "Lord, they have killed Your prophets, they have undermined Your altars, and I have been left alone, and they are seeking my life."

**11:4** But what does the divine response say to him? "I have left for Myself seven thousand men who have not bent their knees before Baal."

**11:5** So then, at this time also, a remnant has been saved according to the election of grace.

**11:6** Now if it is by grace, it is no longer from works; otherwise grace is no longer grace.

**11:7** What then? What Israel was seeking, this it did not obtain, but the election obtained it, while the rest were blinded,

**11:8** just as it is written: "God has given them a spirit of compunction, eyes so that they may not see, and ears so that they may not hear, down to this day."

**11:9** And David says: "Let their table become a snare, and a trap, and a stumbling block, and a repayment to them.

**11:10** Let their eyes be darkened so that they may not see, and bend their back always."

**11:11** I say then: Did they stumble so that they would fall? Far from it. Rather, by their offense there is salvation for the Gentiles, so that they may emulate them.

**11:12** Now if their offense is the riches of the world, and their diminishment is the riches of the Gentiles, how much more their fullness?

**11:13** For to you Gentiles I speak: as long as I am indeed an apostle of the Gentiles, I will honor my ministry,

**11:14** if somehow I may provoke my flesh to emulation and save some of them.

**11:15** For if their loss is the reconciliation of the world, what will their reception be, except life from the dead?

**11:16** Now if the first portion is holy, so also is the lump; and if the root is holy, so also are the branches.

**11:17** Now if some of the branches were broken, and you, though you were a wild olive tree, were grafted in among them and became a sharer of the root and richness of the olive tree,

**11:18** do not boast against the branches. Now if you boast, you do not carry the root, but the root carries you.

**11:19** You will say then, "Branches were broken so that I might be grafted in."

**11:20** Well said: because of unbelief they were broken, while you stand by faith. Do not be haughty in mind, but fear.

**11:21** For if God did not spare the natural branches, perhaps He will not spare you either.

**11:22** See then the goodness and severity of God: toward those who fell, severity; but toward you, the goodness of God, if you remain in that goodness; otherwise you also will be cut off.

**11:23** And they also, if they do not remain in unbelief, will be grafted in, for God has the power to graft them in again.

**11:24** For if you were cut off from a natural wild olive tree and contrary to nature were grafted into a good olive tree, how much more will these, who are according to nature, be grafted into their own olive tree?

**11:25** For I do not want you to be ignorant of this mystery, brothers (so that you may not be wise to yourselves), that blindness in part has occurred in Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles should enter,

**11:26** and thus all Israel would be saved, just as it is written: "There will come out of Zion one who will deliver and turn away ungodliness from Jacob.

**11:27** And this is the covenant for them from Me, when I take away their sins."

**11:28** According to the gospel, indeed, they are enemies on your account; but according to election, they are most beloved on account of the fathers.

**11:29** For the gifts and calling of God are without regret.

**11:30** For just as you also formerly did not believe God, yet now have obtained mercy because of their unbelief,

**11:31** so now these also have not believed, for your mercy, so that they themselves may also obtain mercy.

**11:32** For God has confined all things in unbelief, so that He may have mercy on all.

**11:33** Oh, the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God: how incomprehensible are His judgments, and untraceable His ways!

**11:34** For who has come to know the mind of the Lord? Or who was His counselor?

**11:35** Or who first gave to Him, and it will be repaid to him?

**11:36** Because from Him, and through Him, and in Him are all things; to Him be glory forever. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **11:5** — The Vulgate reads 'salvæ factæ sunt' ('has been saved'), whereas the Greek text has 'gegonen' ('has come to be' or 'exists').
- **11:7** — The Vulgate renders Greek 'eporothesan' ('were hardened') as 'excæcati sunt' ('were blinded'), and similarly translates 'porosis' ('hardening') as 'cæcitas' ('blindness') in 11:25.
- **11:8** — Latin 'spiritum compunctionis' literally means 'a spirit of compunction' (or 'pricking'), a formal rendering of Greek 'pneuma katanyxeos' ('a spirit of stupor' or 'numbness') via the shared root sense of pricking or piercing.
- **11:32** — In 11:30-32 the Vulgate renders Greek 'apeitheia' ('disobedience') with 'incredulitas' ('unbelief') and 'non credere' ('not believe'), and here uses the neuter plural 'omnia' ('all things') where the Greek has masculine 'tous pantas' ('all people').
- **11:36** — The Vulgate reads 'in ipso' ('in Him') in the third prepositional phrase, where the Greek text reads 'eis auton' ('to Him').
