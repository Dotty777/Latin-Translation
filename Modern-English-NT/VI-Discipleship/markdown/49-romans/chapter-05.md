## Text

**5:1** Justified by faith, therefore, let us have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ,

**5:2** through whom we also have access through faith into this grace in which we stand, and we boast in the hope of the glory of the sons of God.

**5:3** And not only that, but we also boast in afflictions, knowing that affliction produces endurance;

**5:4** and endurance, proven worth; and proven worth, hope;

**5:5** and hope does not put to shame, because the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us.

**5:6** For why did Christ, while we were still weak, die at the appointed time for the ungodly?

**5:7** For scarcely does anyone die for a righteous person; for someone might perhaps dare to die for a good person.

**5:8** But God commends His own love in us, because while we were still sinners, at the appointed time,

**5:9** Christ died for us. Much more, therefore, now justified in His blood, we will be saved from wrath through Him.

**5:10** For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son, much more, having been reconciled, will we be saved in His life.

**5:11** And not only that, but we also boast in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received reconciliation.

**5:12** For this reason, just as through one man sin entered into this world, and through sin death, and so death passed through to all people, in whom all sinned.

**5:13** For until the law, sin was in the world, yet sin was not charged when there was no law.

**5:14** Yet death reigned from Adam until Moses, even over those who did not sin in the likeness of the transgression of Adam, who is a pattern of the One to come.

**5:15** But the gift is not like the offense: for if by the offense of one many died, much more have the grace of God and the gift in the grace of the one man Jesus Christ abounded toward many.

**5:16** And the gift is not as it was through one sin. For judgment indeed is from one to condemnation, whereas grace is from many offenses to justification.

**5:17** For if by the offense of one, death reigned through the one, much more will those who receive the abundance of grace, and of the gift, and of righteousness reign in life through the one, Jesus Christ.

**5:18** Therefore, just as through the offense of one it came upon all people to condemnation, so also through the righteousness of one it comes upon all people to justification of life.

**5:19** For just as through the disobedience of one man many were constituted sinners, so also through the obedience of one many will be constituted righteous.

**5:20** Now the law came in alongside so that the offense might abound. Yet where the offense abounded, grace superabounded,

**5:21** so that just as sin reigned to death, so also grace might reign through righteousness to eternal life, through Jesus Christ our Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — Latin 'habeamus' is a hortatory subjunctive ('let us have'); the standard Greek text reads the indicative ('we have'), though many Greek manuscripts have the subjunctive.
- **5:2** — The Vulgate reads 'gloriae filiorum Dei' ('of the glory of the sons of God'), including 'of the sons' where the standard Greek text reads simply 'the glory of God'.
- **5:6** — The Vulgate renders this verse as a question ('Ut quid enim... mortuus est?'), whereas the standard Greek text is a declarative statement. In 5:8 the Clementine Vulgate also repeats 'secundum tempus' ('at the appointed time') and places 'Christ died for us' at the beginning of 5:9.
- **5:12** — Latin 'in quo' may be masculine ('in whom', referring back to the one man) or neuter ('in which'); the underlying Greek expression is often taken as 'because'. The Vulgate also reads 'in hunc mundum' ('into this world').
- **5:16** — Latin 'per unum peccatum' ('through one sin') differs from the standard Greek ('through one who sinned'). In 5:17 the Vulgate likewise lists three coordinated genitives ('of grace, and of the gift, and of righteousness').
