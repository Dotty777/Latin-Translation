## Text

**13:1** Let every soul be subject to higher authorities, for there is no authority except from God, and those that exist have been ordained by God.

**13:2** Therefore whoever resists authority resists God's ordinance; and those who resist acquire condemnation for themselves.

**13:3** For rulers are not a cause of fear for a good work, but for an evil one. Now do you want not to fear authority? Do what is good, and you will have praise from it,

**13:4** for it is God's servant to you for good. But if you do evil, be afraid, for it does not carry the sword without cause; for it is God's servant, an avenger for wrath upon the one who does evil.

**13:5** Therefore be subject out of necessity, not only because of wrath, but also because of conscience.

**13:6** For on this account you also pay tribute, for they are God's servants, serving in this very matter.

**13:7** Render therefore to all what is owed: to whom tribute is owed, tribute; to whom revenue, revenue; to whom fear, fear; to whom honor, honor.

**13:8** Do not owe anyone anything, except to love one another; for whoever loves his neighbor has fulfilled the law.

**13:9** For, "You shall not commit adultery; you shall not kill; you shall not steal; you shall not give false testimony; you shall not covet," and if there is any other commandment, it is summed up in this word: "You shall love your neighbor as yourself."

**13:10** Love of one's neighbor works no evil; therefore love is the fullness of the law.

**13:11** And this, knowing the time: that it is already the hour for us to rise from sleep. For our salvation is closer now than when we believed.

**13:12** The night has passed on, and the day has drawn near. Let us therefore cast aside the works of darkness and clothe ourselves with the armor of light.

**13:13** Let us walk honorably, as in the daytime: not in revelry and drunkenness, not in promiscuity and indecency, not in contention and rivalry,

**13:14** but clothe yourselves with the Lord Jesus Christ, and do not make provision for the flesh in its desires.

## Translator Notes

- **13:8** — The Vulgate reads 'proximum' ('neighbor') here, aligning with 13:9-10, where the Greek text has 'ton heteron' ('the other').
- **13:9** — The Clementine Vulgate includes 'non falsum testimonium dices' ('you shall not give false testimony'), a clause attested in some Greek manuscripts and the Byzantine tradition but omitted in modern critical Greek editions.
- **13:10** — Latin 'Dilectio proximi' uses the genitive ('love of one's neighbor'), whereas the Greek text uses the dative ('to plesion', 'does no wrong to a neighbor').
