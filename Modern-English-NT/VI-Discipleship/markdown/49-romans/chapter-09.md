## Text

**9:1** I speak the truth in Christ; I do not lie, with my conscience bearing witness to me in the Holy Spirit,

**9:2** that I have great sorrow and continual pain in my heart.

**9:3** For I myself could wish to be accursed from Christ for my brothers, who are my relatives according to the flesh,

**9:4** who are Israelites, whose is the adoption of sons, and the glory, and the covenant, and the lawgiving, and the worship, and the promises;

**9:5** whose are the fathers, and from whom is Christ according to the flesh, who is God above all things, blessed forever. Amen.

**9:6** It is not, however, that the word of God has failed. For not all who are from Israel are Israelites;

**9:7** nor are those who are the offspring of Abraham all sons; rather, "In Isaac your offspring will be called."

**9:8** That is, it is not those who are sons of the flesh who are sons of God, but those who are sons of the promise are reckoned as offspring.

**9:9** For the word of promise is this: "At this time I will come, and Sarah will have a son."

**9:10** And not only she, but Rebekah also, having conceived from one union with our father Isaac.

**9:11** For when they had not yet been born, or done anything good or bad (so that God's purpose according to election might remain),

**9:12** not from works, but from Him who calls, it was said to her, "The older will serve the younger,"

**9:13** just as it is written: "I have loved Jacob, but I have hated Esau."

**9:14** What then will we say? Is there injustice with God? Far from it.

**9:15** For He says to Moses, "I will have mercy on whom I have mercy, and I will grant mercy to whom I will be merciful."

**9:16** Therefore it does not depend on the one who wills, nor on the one who runs, but on God who shows mercy.

**9:17** For Scripture says to Pharaoh, "For this very reason I have raised you up, so that I may show My power in you, and so that My name may be declared throughout all the earth."

**9:18** So then He has mercy on whom He wills, and whom He wills He hardens.

**9:19** You say to me then, "Why does He still find fault? For who resists His will?"

**9:20** Man, who are you to reply to God? Does the thing formed say to the one who formed it, "Why did you make me like this?"

**9:21** Or does the potter not have authority over the clay to make from the same lump one vessel for honor and another for dishonor?

**9:22** What if God, desiring to show wrath and to make known His power, endured with great patience vessels of wrath fitted for destruction,

**9:23** so that He might show the riches of His glory upon vessels of mercy, which He prepared in advance for glory,

**9:24** even us, whom He also called, not only from the Jews, but also among the Gentiles?

**9:25** Just as He says in Hosea, "I will call those who are not My people, 'My people,' and her who is not beloved, 'Beloved,' and her who has not obtained mercy, 'One who has obtained mercy.'"

**9:26** "And it will be: in the place where it was said to them, 'You are not My people,' there they will be called sons of the living God."

**9:27** And Isaiah cries out concerning Israel: "If the number of the sons of Israel is like the sand of the sea, a remnant will be saved.

**9:28** For He is completing the word and cutting it short in equity, because the Lord will carry out a shortened word upon the earth."

**9:29** And as Isaiah foretold: "If the Lord Sabaoth had not left us offspring, we would have become like Sodom, and we would have been similar to Gomorrah."

**9:30** What then will we say? That Gentiles, who were not pursuing righteousness, laid hold of righteousness, namely, the righteousness that is from faith;

**9:31** whereas Israel, in pursuing the law of righteousness, did not reach the law of righteousness.

**9:32** Why? Because it was not from faith, but as if from works; for they stumbled against the stone of stumbling,

**9:33** just as it is written: "Look, I place in Zion a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense, and everyone who believes in Him will not be brought to shame."

## Translator Notes

- **9:4** — The Vulgate reads singular 'testamentum' ("the covenant"), whereas the Greek text reads plural "the covenants."
- **9:5** — The Vulgate's relative clause ('qui est super omnia Deus benedictus') directly identifies Christ as God rather than treating the phrase as an independent doxology.
- **9:25** — The Vulgate includes a third clause from Hosea 2:23 ('et non misericordiam consecutam, misericordiam consecutam') that is absent from most Greek manuscripts of Romans.
- **9:28** — The Vulgate preserves the longer wording from Isaiah 10:23 ('et abbrevians in aequitate: quia verbum breviatum...'), which critical Greek editions shorten.
- **9:29** — The Vulgate retains the Hebrew transliteration 'Sabaoth,' meaning "hosts" or "armies."
