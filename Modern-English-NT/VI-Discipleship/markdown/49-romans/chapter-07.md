## Text

**7:1** Or are you unaware, brothers (for I am speaking to those who know the law), that the law has mastery over a person for as long as he lives?

**7:2** For a woman who is under a husband is bound to the law while her husband lives; yet if her husband dies, she is released from the law of the husband.

**7:3** Therefore, while her husband lives, she will be called an adulteress if she is with another man; yet if her husband dies, she is freed from the law of the husband, so that she is not an adulteress if she is with another man.

**7:4** So then, my brothers, you also have been put to death to the law through the body of Christ, so that you may belong to another, to Him who rose from the dead, in order that we may bear fruit for God.

**7:5** For while we were in the flesh, the passions of sins, which were through the law, were at work in our members so that they might bear fruit for death.

**7:6** Now, however, we have been released from the law of death, in which we were held, so that we may serve in newness of spirit, and not in oldness of the letter.

**7:7** What, then, will we say? Is the law sin? Far from it. Yet I did not know sin except through the law; for I would not have known coveting if the law had not said, "You shall not covet."

**7:8** Yet seizing an opportunity, sin produced all coveting in me through the commandment. For without the law, sin was dead.

**7:9** Now I was once alive without the law, but when the commandment came, sin lived again.

**7:10** I, however, died; and the commandment that was for life—this very one was found by me to be for death.

**7:11** For sin, seizing an opportunity through the commandment, led me astray, and through it killed me.

**7:12** So then, the law indeed is holy, and the commandment is holy, and righteous, and good.

**7:13** Did what is good, then, become death to me? Far from it. Rather, sin, so that it might appear to be sin, produced death for me through what is good, so that sin might become sinful beyond measure through the commandment.

**7:14** For we know that the law is spiritual, whereas I am fleshly, sold under sin.

**7:15** For what I carry out, I do not understand: for the good that I want, this I do not practice; rather, the evil that I hate, that I do.

**7:16** Now if I do that which I do not want, I concur with the law that it is good.

**7:17** Now, however, it is no longer I who carry it out, but the sin that dwells in me.

**7:18** For I know that good does not dwell in me, that is, in my flesh. For to will is at hand for me, yet how to accomplish the good I do not find.

**7:19** For the good that I want, this I do not do; rather, the evil that I do not want, this I practice.

**7:20** Now if I do that which I do not want, it is no longer I who carry it out, but the sin that dwells in me.

**7:21** I find, therefore, a law, that for me, wanting to do good, evil is at hand;

**7:22** for I take delight in the law of God according to the inner man,

**7:23** yet I see another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind and taking me captive in the law of sin that is in my members.

**7:24** Miserable man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death?

**7:25** The grace of God, through Jesus Christ our Lord. Therefore, I myself with the mind serve the law of God, yet with the flesh, the law of sin.

## Translator Notes

- **7:6** — The Vulgate reads 'a lege mortis' ('from the law of death'), a notable divergence from the standard Greek text ('from the law, having died to that in which we were held').
- **7:10** — Following the Clementine Vulgate verse division, 'Ego autem mortuus sum' ('I, however, died') opens 7:10 rather than closing 7:9.
- **7:15** — The Vulgate explicitly supplies 'bonum' ('good') and 'malum' ('evil') here, mirroring 7:19, whereas these two nouns are absent from the standard Greek text of 7:15.
- **7:18** — Latin 'non invenio' ('I do not find') reflects the Latin and Byzantine textual tradition, whereas critical Greek editions omit the verb at the end of the clause.
- **7:25** — The Vulgate reads the nominative 'gratia Dei' ('The grace of God') as a direct answer to the question in 7:24, whereas standard Greek editions read 'Thanks be to God'.
