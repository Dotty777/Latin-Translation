## Text

**16:1** Now I commend to you Phoebe, our sister, who is in the service of the church that is in Cenchreae,

**16:2** that you may welcome her in the Lord as is worthy of the saints, and assist her in whatever matter she may have need of you; for indeed she herself has also assisted many, and me myself.

**16:3** Greet Prisca and Aquila, my helpers in Christ Jesus

**16:4** (who laid down their own necks for my life, to whom not I alone give thanks, but also all the churches of the Gentiles),

**16:5** and the church in their house. Greet Epaenetus, beloved to me, who is the firstfruit of Asia in Christ.

**16:6** Greet Mary, who labored much among you.

**16:7** Greet Andronicus and Junia, my relatives and fellow prisoners, who are distinguished among the apostles, who also were in Christ before me.

**16:8** Greet Ampliatus, most beloved to me in the Lord.

**16:9** Greet Urbanus, our helper in Christ Jesus, and Stachys, my beloved.

**16:10** Greet Apelles, proven in Christ.

**16:11** Greet those who are from the household of Aristobulus. Greet Herodion, my relative. Greet those who are from the household of Narcissus, who are in the Lord.

**16:12** Greet Tryphaena and Tryphosa, who labor in the Lord. Greet Persis, dearly loved, who labored much in the Lord.

**16:13** Greet Rufus, chosen in the Lord, and his mother, and mine.

**16:14** Greet Asyncritus, Phlegon, Hermas, Patrobas, Hermes, and the brothers who are with them.

**16:15** Greet Philologus and Julia, Nereus and his sister, and Olympias, and all the saints who are with them.

**16:16** Greet one another with a holy kiss. All the churches of Christ greet you.

**16:17** Now I ask you, brothers, to keep watch on those who create dissensions and stumbling blocks contrary to the teaching that you learned, and turn away from them.

**16:18** For people of this kind do not serve Christ our Lord, but their own belly, and through sweet words and blessings they lead astray the hearts of the innocent.

**16:19** For your obedience has been made known in every place. Therefore I rejoice in you. But I want you to be wise in what is good, and simple in what is evil.

**16:20** Now may the God of peace crush Satan under your feet quickly. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you.

**16:21** Timothy, my helper, greets you, as do Lucius, and Jason, and Sosipater, my relatives.

**16:22** I, Tertius, who wrote the letter, greet you in the Lord.

**16:23** Gaius, my host, and the whole church greet you. Erastus, the treasurer of the city, and Quartus, the brother, greet you.

**16:24** The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with all of you. Amen.

**16:25** Now to Him who is able to strengthen you in accordance with my gospel and the proclamation of Jesus Christ, according to the revelation of the mystery kept silent for eternal ages

**16:26** (which has now been disclosed through the Scriptures of the prophets according to the command of the eternal God, for the obedience of faith), known among all the nations,

**16:27** to the only wise God, through Jesus Christ, to whom be honor and glory forever and ever. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **16:15** — The Clementine Vulgate reads the feminine name Olympiadem ("Olympias"), whereas the Greek text has the masculine name Olympan ("Olympas").
- **16:18** — Latin benedictiones ("blessings," literally "fair words") translates Greek eulogia, which in this context carries the sense of flattery or smooth praise.
- **16:20** — The Vulgate uses the optative subjunctive conterat ("may [He] crush"), expressing a prayerful wish where the Greek text has the future indicative ("will crush").
- **16:23** — The Vulgate places universa ecclesia ("the whole church") in the nominative as a co-sender of the greeting, whereas the Greek has the genitive ("host to me and to the whole church").
- **16:27** — The Vulgate reads honor et gloria ("honor and glory"), where the Greek text has only "glory," and retains the relative pronoun cui ("to whom").
