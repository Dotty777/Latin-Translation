## Text

**3:1** What more, then, does the Jew have? Or what is the benefit of circumcision?

**3:2** Much in every way. First indeed, because the utterances of God were entrusted to them.

**3:3** For what if some of them did not believe? Will their unbelief nullify the faithfulness of God? Far from it!

**3:4** But God is true, while every person is a liar, just as it is written: "So that You may be justified in Your words, and prevail when You are judged."

**3:5** But if our unrighteousness commends the righteousness of God, what shall we say? Is God unrighteous, who inflicts wrath?

**3:6** I speak in human terms. Far from it! Otherwise, how will God judge this world?

**3:7** For if the truth of God has abounded through my lie to His glory, why am I also still judged as a sinner?

**3:8** And why not (as we are slandered, and as some claim we say), "Let us do evil so that good may come"? Their condemnation is just.

**3:9** What then? Do we surpass them? Not at all. For we have charged that Jews and Greeks are all under sin,

**3:10** just as it is written: "There is no righteous person;

**3:11** there is no one who understands; there is no one who seeks God.

**3:12** All have turned aside; together they have become useless. There is no one who does good; there is not even one.

**3:13** An open tomb is their throat; with their tongues they acted deceitfully; the venom of asps is under their lips,

**3:14** whose mouth is full of cursing and bitterness;

**3:15** their feet are swift to shed blood;

**3:16** ruin and misery are in their ways,

**3:17** and the way of peace they have not known;

**3:18** there is no fear of God before their eyes."

**3:19** Now we know that whatever the law speaks, it speaks to those who are in the law, so that every mouth may be stopped and the whole world may become subject to God,

**3:20** because by works of the law no flesh will be justified before Him. For through the law comes the knowledge of sin.

**3:21** But now, apart from the law, the righteousness of God has been manifested, attested by the law and the prophets,

**3:22** namely, the righteousness of God through the faith of Jesus Christ, to all and upon all who believe in Him; for there is no distinction,

**3:23** for all have sinned and lack the glory of God,

**3:24** being justified freely through His grace, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus,

**3:25** whom God set forth as a propitiation through faith in His blood, for the demonstration of His righteousness because of the remission of previous offenses,

**3:26** in the forbearance of God, for the demonstration of His righteousness at this time, so that He Himself may be righteous and may justify him who is of the faith of Jesus Christ.

**3:27** Where then is your boasting? It is excluded. Through what law? Of deeds? No, but through the law of faith.

**3:28** For we consider that a person is justified through faith apart from works of the law.

**3:29** Or is God the God of the Jews only? Is He not of the Gentiles also? Yes, of the Gentiles also,

**3:30** since indeed God is one, who justifies the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through faith.

**3:31** Do we then destroy the law through faith? Far from it! Rather, we establish the law.

## Translator Notes

- **3:4** — The Vulgate uses the indicative "Est" ("God is true") where the Greek has an imperative ("let God be proved true"), and renders the quotation's final verb passively ("when You are judged"), following the Septuagint.
- **3:9** — Where the debated Greek middle/passive verb can mean "are we better off?" or "are we worse off?", the Vulgate renders it actively with an explicit object, "præcellimus eos" ("do we surpass them?").
- **3:22** — The Vulgate reads "in omnes et super omnes qui credunt in eum" ("to all and upon all who believe in Him"), including "upon all" and "in Him" which are absent from modern critical Greek texts. The genitive "fidem Jesu Christi" can be understood either objectively ("faith in Jesus Christ") or subjectively ("the faithfulness of Jesus Christ").
- **3:25** — The Vulgate renders the rare Greek word "paresis" ("passing over") as "remissionem" ("remission" or "forgiveness").
- **3:26** — The Latin genitive 'ex fide Jesu Christi' literally reads 'of the faith of Jesus Christ' and can be understood either objectively ('faith in Jesus Christ') or subjectively ('the faithfulness of Jesus Christ').
- **3:30** — The Vulgate uses the present tense "justificat" ("justifies") where the Greek has a future ("will justify"), and preserves the prepositional shift from "ex fide" ("by faith") to "per fidem" ("through faith").
