## Text

**1:1** James, a servant of God and of our Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes that are in the dispersion: greetings.

**1:2** Regard it as all joy, my brothers, when you fall into various trials,

**1:3** knowing that the testing of your faith produces patience.

**1:4** Now patience has a perfect work, so that you may be perfect and whole, falling short in nothing.

**1:5** But if anyone among you lacks wisdom, let him ask from God, who gives to all abundantly and does not reproach, and it will be given to him.

**1:6** Yet let him ask in faith, not hesitating at all; for the one who hesitates is like a wave of the sea that is stirred by the wind and tossed about.

**1:7** Therefore let that man not suppose that he will receive anything from the Lord.

**1:8** A man double in mind is unstable in all his ways.

**1:9** Now let the humble brother boast in his exaltation,

**1:10** and the rich man in his lowliness, because like a flower of grass he will pass away;

**1:11** for the sun has risen with scorching heat and dried up the grass, and its flower has fallen off, and the beauty of its appearance has perished; so too will the rich man fade away in his journeys.

**1:12** Blessed is the man who endures trial, because when he has been proved he will receive the crown of life, which God promised to those who love Him.

**1:13** Let no one, when tempted, say that he is tempted by God; for God is no tempter of evils, and He Himself tempts no one.

**1:14** But each one is tempted, drawn away and lured by his own desire.

**1:15** Then desire, when it has conceived, gives birth to sin; and sin, when it has been completed, brings forth death.

**1:16** Therefore do not go astray, my most beloved brothers.

**1:17** Every best bestowal and every perfect gift is from above, descending from the Father of lights, with whom there is no shifting nor shadow of alternation.

**1:18** For willingly He brought us forth by the word of truth, so that we might be a kind of beginning of His creation.

**1:19** You know, my most beloved brothers. Now let every person be swift to listen, yet slow to speak and slow to anger.

**1:20** For a man's anger does not produce God's righteousness.

**1:21** For this reason, casting off all uncleanness and abundance of malice, receive in gentleness the implanted word, which is able to save your souls.

**1:22** And be doers of the word, and not merely listeners, deceiving your very selves.

**1:23** Because if anyone is a listener of the word and not a doer, this one will be compared to a man examining the face of his birth in a mirror;

**1:24** for he examined himself and went away, and at once forgot what sort of person he was.

**1:25** But the one who looks deeply into the perfect law of liberty and remains in it, not having become a forgetful listener but a doer of the work, this one will be blessed in his deed.

**1:26** Now if anyone supposes himself to be religious, not bridling his tongue but misleading his own heart, this one's religion is empty.

**1:27** Religion clean and unstained before God and the Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their tribulation, and to keep oneself unstained from this world.

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — Latin tentatio (like Greek peirasmos) covers both 'trial/testing' and 'temptation,' linking the trials of vv. 2 and 12 with the temptation to evil in vv. 13-14.
- **1:4** — The Clementine Vulgate reads the indicative habet ('has'), whereas the Greek reads the imperative ('let ... have').
- **1:12** — The Vulgate explicitly supplies Deus ('God') as the subject of repromisit ('promised'), whereas the earliest Greek witnesses leave the subject unexpressed.
- **1:13** — The Vulgate converts the quotation to third-person indirect speech (tentatur, 'he is tempted') rather than first-person direct speech, and renders Greek apeirastos kakon ('cannot be tempted by evil') with the active Latin coinage intentator malorum ('no tempter of evils').
- **1:18** — The Vulgate translates Greek aparche ('firstfruits') as initium ('beginning') and adds enim ('For').
