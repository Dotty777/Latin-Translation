## Text

**2:1** My brothers, do not hold with favoritism the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ of glory.

**2:2** For if a man wearing a gold ring, in bright clothing, enters your assembly, and a poor man in filthy attire also enters,

**2:3** and you pay attention to the one who is dressed in splendid clothing and say to him, "You sit comfortably here," while to the poor man you say, "You stand over there, or sit under my footstool,"

**2:4** do you not pass judgment among yourselves, and have you not become judges with wicked thoughts?

**2:5** Listen, my most beloved brothers: did God not choose the poor in this world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom that God promised to those who love Him?

**2:6** But you have dishonored the poor man. Do the rich not oppress you through power and themselves drag you into the courts?

**2:7** Do they themselves not blaspheme the good name that has been invoked over you?

**2:8** If, however, you fulfill the royal law according to the Scriptures, "You shall love your neighbor as yourself," you do well;

**2:9** but if you show favoritism, you commit sin, convicted by the law as transgressors.

**2:10** Now whoever keeps the entire law, yet stumbles in one thing, has become guilty of all.

**2:11** For He who said, "You shall not commit adultery," also said, "You shall not kill." Now if you do not commit adultery, yet you kill, you have become a transgressor of the law.

**2:12** Speak in this way and act in this way, as those about to be judged through the law of liberty.

**2:13** For judgment without mercy is for the one who has not shown mercy; yet mercy rises high above judgment.

**2:14** What will it profit, my brothers, if someone says he has faith, yet does not have works? Will faith be able to save him?

**2:15** Now if a brother and a sister are naked and lack daily food,

**2:16** and one of you says to them, "Go in peace, be warmed and satisfied," yet you do not give them the things necessary for the body, what will it profit?

**2:17** In the same way faith too, if it does not have works, is dead in itself.

**2:18** But someone will say, "You have faith, and I have works." Show me your faith without works, and I will show you my faith by works.

**2:19** You believe that there is one God; you do well. The demons also believe, and they shudder.

**2:20** Now do you want to know, you empty man, that faith without works is dead?

**2:21** Was our father Abraham not justified by works, offering his son Isaac on the altar?

**2:22** Do you see that faith was working together with his works, and by works faith was completed?

**2:23** And the Scripture was fulfilled, saying, "Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him for righteousness," and he was called a friend of God.

**2:24** Do you see that a person is justified by works and not by faith only?

**2:25** Likewise, was Rahab the prostitute not also justified by works, receiving the messengers and sending them out by another way?

**2:26** For just as a body without spirit is dead, so too faith without works is dead.

## Translator Notes

- **2:4** — The Vulgate translates Greek diekrithete ('made distinctions') with judicatis ('pass judgment'), creating a wordplay with judices ('judges').
- **2:15** — The Clementine Vulgate reads frater et soror ('a brother and a sister'), whereas the Greek reads 'a brother or a sister.'
- **2:18** — The extent of the speaker's quotation is debated and may continue to the end of the verse. The Vulgate's sine operibus ('without works') agrees with the critical Greek text, whereas the Byzantine Greek tradition reads 'by your works.'
- **2:20** — The Vulgate reads mortua est ('is dead'), following the Byzantine Greek text rather than the Alexandrian reading 'useless/idle.'
