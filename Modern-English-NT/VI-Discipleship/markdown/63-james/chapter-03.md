## Text

**3:1** Do not let many of you become teachers, my brothers, knowing that you receive a greater judgment.

**3:2** For in many things we all stumble. If anyone does not stumble in word, this one is a perfect man; he is also able to lead the whole body around with a bridle.

**3:3** Now if we put bridles into the mouths of horses so that they comply with us, we also turn about their whole body.

**3:4** Look at ships too: although they are large and are driven by strong winds, they are turned about by a small rudder wherever the impulse of the one steering wills.

**3:5** So also the tongue is indeed a small member, and it exalts great things. Look how small a fire sets ablaze how large a forest!

**3:6** And the tongue is a fire, a universe of iniquity. The tongue is established among our members, which stains the whole body and inflames the wheel of our birth, itself inflamed by hell.

**3:7** For every nature of beasts and of birds and of reptiles and of the rest is tamed and has been tamed by human nature,

**3:8** but no human being is able to tame the tongue: a restless evil, full of lethal poison.

**3:9** With it we bless God and the Father, and with it we curse people who have been made after the likeness of God.

**3:10** From the same mouth proceed blessing and cursing. My brothers, these things ought not to happen in this way.

**3:11** Does a spring pour forth sweet and bitter water from the same opening?

**3:12** Can a fig tree, my brothers, produce grapes, or a vine figs? So neither can salt water produce sweet water.

**3:13** Who is wise and disciplined among you? Let him show his work from good conduct in the gentleness of wisdom.

**3:14** Now if you have bitter jealousy, and contentions are in your hearts, do not boast and be liars against the truth;

**3:15** for this wisdom is not one descending from above, but is earthly, natural, diabolical.

**3:16** For where there is jealousy and contention, there is instability and every wicked work.

**3:17** But the wisdom that is from above is first indeed chaste, then peaceable, modest, open to persuasion, consenting to what is good, full of mercy and good fruits, not judging, without pretense.

**3:18** And the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace for those who make peace.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The Vulgate reads the second-person present sumitis ('you receive'), whereas the Greek reads the first-person future ('we will receive').
- **3:7** — The Vulgate reads et ceterorum ('and of the rest'), whereas the Greek reads 'and of sea creatures.'
- **3:9** — The Vulgate reads Deum et Patrem ('God and the Father'), agreeing with the Byzantine Greek text rather than the Alexandrian reading 'the Lord and Father.'
- **3:12** — The Vulgate reads uvas ('grapes'), whereas the Greek reads 'olives.'
- **3:17** — The Clementine text reads bonus consentiens, a transmitted variant of bonis consentiens ('consenting to what is good'), a phrase not present in Greek; the Vulgate also renders Greek adiakritos ('impartial') literally as non judicans ('not judging').
