## Text

**4:1** Where do wars and quarrels among you come from? Is it not from here: from your cravings, which do battle in your members?

**4:2** You crave and do not have; you kill and are jealous, and you cannot obtain; you quarrel and wage war, and you do not have, because you do not ask.

**4:3** You ask and do not receive, because you ask wrongly, so that you may spend it on your cravings.

**4:4** Adulterers, do you not know that friendship with this world is an enemy of God? Therefore whoever wishes to be a friend of this age is made an enemy of God.

**4:5** Or do you think that Scripture says in vain, "The spirit that dwells in you craves to the point of envy"?

**4:6** But He gives greater grace. For this reason it says, "God resists the proud, but to the humble He gives grace."

**4:7** Therefore be subject to God, but resist the devil, and he will flee from you.

**4:8** Draw close to God, and He will draw close to you. Cleanse your hands, sinners, and purify your hearts, you who are double-minded.

**4:9** Be wretched, and mourn, and weep; let your laughter be changed into mourning, and joy into grief.

**4:10** Humble yourselves in the Lord's sight, and He will exalt you.

**4:11** Do not speak ill of one another, brothers. The one who speaks ill of a brother, or who judges his brother, speaks ill of the law and judges the law. But if you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law, but a judge.

**4:12** There is one lawgiver and judge, who is able to destroy and to deliver.

**4:13** But who are you, who judge your neighbor? Look now, you who say, "Today or tomorrow we will go into that city, and indeed spend a year there, and trade, and make a profit,"

**4:14** you who do not know what will be tomorrow.

**4:15** For what is your life? It is a vapor appearing for a little while, and afterward it will be done away with; instead of your saying, "If the Lord wills," and, "If we live, we will do this or that."

**4:16** But now you exult in your pride. All such exultation is evil.

**4:17** Therefore, for the one who knows to do good and does not do it, it is sin for him.

## Translator Notes

- **4:4** — The Vulgate reads masculine "Adulteri" ("Adulterers", whereas the critical Greek text has feminine "adulteresses"), uses "inimica" ("an enemy") rather than "enmity", and varies "hujus mundi" ("this world") with "sæculi hujus" ("this age").
- **4:5** — A famously debated verse: the Latin "spiritus qui habitat in vobis" ("the spirit that dwells in you", differing from the critical Greek "which he made to dwell in us") is understood by some interpreters as the human spirit inclined toward envy and by others as God's Spirit jealously longing for believers' devotion.
- **4:12** — The Clementine Vulgate places "But who are you, who judge your neighbor?" at the start of 4:13 (with 4:14 containing only "you who do not know what will be tomorrow" and 4:15 beginning with "For what is your life?"), and reads "to destroy and to deliver" ("perdere et liberare") in reverse of the Greek order.
- **4:15** — Unlike the Greek single conditional, the Clementine Vulgate divides the saying into two conditions ("Si Dominus voluerit. Et: Si vixerimus, faciemus...") and uses the future passive "exterminabitur" ("will be done away with").
