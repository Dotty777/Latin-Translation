## Text

**5:1** Come now, you rich, weep, howling over your miseries that will come upon you.

**5:2** Your riches have rotted, and your garments have been eaten by moths.

**5:3** Your gold and silver have rusted, and their rust will be a testimony against you, and it will eat your flesh like fire. You have stored up wrath for yourselves in the last days.

**5:4** Look, the pay of the workers who reaped your fields, which was fraudulently withheld by you, cries out, and their cry has entered into the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth.

**5:5** You have feasted on the earth, and in luxury you have nourished your hearts in the day of slaughter.

**5:6** You have condemned and killed the righteous one, and he does not resist you.

**5:7** Be patient, therefore, brothers, until the coming of the Lord. Look, the farmer waits for the precious fruit of the earth, enduring patiently until he receives the early and the late.

**5:8** Therefore, you also be patient, and strengthen your hearts, for the coming of the Lord has drawn near.

**5:9** Do not groan against one another, brothers, so that you may not be judged. Look, the judge stands before the door.

**5:10** Take, brothers, the prophets who spoke in the name of the Lord as an example of an evil outcome, of hardship, and of patience.

**5:11** Look, we call blessed those who have endured. You have heard of the endurance of Job, and you have seen the end of the Lord, for the Lord is merciful and compassionate.

**5:12** But above all, my brothers, swear neither by heaven, nor by earth, nor any other oath whatever. Rather, let your speech be, "Yes, yes; No, no," so that you may not fall under judgment.

**5:13** Is anyone among you sorrowful? Let him pray. Is he of a contented mind? Let him sing psalms.

**5:14** Is anyone among you sick? Let him bring in the elders of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord.

**5:15** And the prayer of faith will save the sick one, and the Lord will lift him up; and if he is in sins, they will be forgiven him.

**5:16** Therefore confess your sins to one another, and pray for one another, so that you may be saved; for the continual petition of a righteous person has great power.

**5:17** Elijah was a man like us, subject to suffering, and with prayer he prayed that it would not rain on the earth, and it did not rain for three years and six months.

**5:18** And he prayed again, and the sky gave rain, and the earth gave its fruit.

**5:19** My brothers, if anyone among you strays from the truth and someone turns him back,

**5:20** he ought to know that the one who causes a sinner to turn back from the error of his way will save his soul from death and will cover a multitude of sins.

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — The Clementine Vulgate explicitly includes "vobis iram" ("wrath for yourselves", echoing Romans 2:5), whereas the Greek text reads simply "you have stored up treasure in the last days."
- **5:4** — "Sabaoth" (Latin "sabbaoth") preserves the transliterated Hebrew title meaning "hosts" or "armies." The Vulgate also reads "clamor eorum" ("their cry") rather than the Greek "the cries of the harvesters."
- **5:7** — Latin "temporaneum et serotinum" ("the early and the late") omits an explicit noun; it is traditionally understood to refer to the early (autumn) and late (spring) rains needed for the crop.
- **5:10** — The Clementine Vulgate has a distinctive three-part list—"exitus mali, laboris, et patientiæ" ("of an evil outcome, of hardship, and of patience")—where the Greek text has two terms ("of suffering and of patience").
- **5:16** — The Vulgate reads "ut salvemini" ("so that you may be saved") rather than the Greek "so that you may be healed," and "assidua" ("continual") for the Greek participle meaning "working" or "effective."
