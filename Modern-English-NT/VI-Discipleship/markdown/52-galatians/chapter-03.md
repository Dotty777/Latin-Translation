## Text

**3:1** Unthinking Galatians, who has bewitched you not to obey the truth, before whose eyes Jesus Christ was portrayed, crucified among you?

**3:2** This alone I want to learn from you: did you receive the Spirit from works of the law, or from the hearing of faith?

**3:3** Are you so foolish that, although you began by the Spirit, you are now being brought to completion by the flesh?

**3:4** Have you suffered such great things for nothing? If indeed for nothing.

**3:5** Therefore He who grants the Spirit to you and works mighty deeds among you: is it from works of the law, or from the hearing of faith?

**3:6** Just as it is written: "Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him for righteousness."

**3:7** Know, therefore, that those who are of faith, they are sons of Abraham.

**3:8** Now Scripture, foreseeing that God justifies the nations by faith, announced beforehand to Abraham: "All the nations will be blessed in you."

**3:9** Therefore those who are of faith will be blessed with faithful Abraham.

**3:10** For all who are of works of the law are under a curse. For it is written: "Cursed is everyone who does not remain in all the things that are written in the book of the law, to do them."

**3:11** Now it is clear that no one is justified in the law before God, because the righteous one lives by faith.

**3:12** Yet the law is not of faith, but: "The one who does them will live in them."

**3:13** Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us, because it is written: "Cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree";

**3:14** so that among the nations the blessing of Abraham might come in Christ Jesus, so that we may receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.

**3:15** Brothers (I speak in human terms), yet no one rejects or adds to a man's confirmed covenant.

**3:16** The promises were spoken to Abraham and to his seed. It does not say, "And to seeds," as of many, but as of one, "And to your seed," who is Christ.

**3:17** Now this I say: the law, which came into being four hundred and thirty years later, does not make void a covenant confirmed by God, so as to abolish the promise.

**3:18** For if the inheritance is from the law, it is no longer from promise. Yet God granted it to Abraham through promise.

**3:19** Why then the law? It was set in place because of transgressions until the seed should come to whom He had promised, ordained through angels in the hand of a mediator.

**3:20** Now a mediator is not of one, yet God is one.

**3:21** Is the law therefore against the promises of God? Far from it! For if a law had been given that could give life, truly righteousness would be from the law.

**3:22** But Scripture confined all things under sin, so that the promise from the faith of Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe.

**3:23** Now before faith came, we were guarded under the law, confined for that faith which was to be revealed.

**3:24** Therefore the law was our custodian in Christ, so that we may be justified by faith.

**3:25** But when faith has come, we are no longer under a custodian.

**3:26** For you are all sons of God through the faith that is in Christ Jesus.

**3:27** For all of you who were baptized in Christ have put on Christ.

**3:28** There is no Jew or Greek; there is no slave or free; there is no male or female. For all of you are one in Christ Jesus.

**3:29** Now if you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's seed, heirs according to promise.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The Clementine Vulgate includes "not to obey the truth" (non obedire veritati) and "among you" (in vobis), phrases absent from many modern Greek critical editions.
- **3:11** — The Vulgate uses the present tense "lives" (vivit) where the Greek text uses a future ("will live"); similarly, in 3:9 the Vulgate reads future "will be blessed" (benedicentur) against Greek present "are blessed."
- **3:19** — The Vulgate reads "was set in place" (posita est) rather than Greek "was added," and uses an active verb "to whom He had promised" (cui promiserat) rather than a passive.
- **3:22** — The Latin genitive in "the faith of Jesus Christ" (fide Jesu Christi) is widely debated and can be understood either as "faith in Jesus Christ" or as "the faithfulness of Jesus Christ."
- **3:24** — Latin paedagogus denotes a household custodian who supervised a minor child. Notably, the Vulgate reads the ablative "in Christ" (in Christo, as also in 3:27) rather than the Greek accusative meaning "until" or "into Christ."
