## Text

**2:1** Then, after fourteen years, I again went up to Jerusalem with Barnabas, having taken Titus along as well.

**2:2** Now I went up according to a revelation, and I laid before them the gospel that I preach among the Gentiles, yet privately to those who seemed to be something, for fear that I was somehow running, or had run, in vain.

**2:3** Yet not even Titus, who was with me, although he was a Gentile, was compelled to be circumcised;

**2:4** but because of the false brothers secretly brought in, who slipped in to spy out our freedom which we have in Christ Jesus, so that they might reduce us to slavery.

**2:5** Not even for an hour did we give way to them in submission, so that the truth of the gospel may remain with you.

**2:6** Now from those who seemed to be something (whatever sort they once were makes no difference to me; God shows no partiality toward any man)—for those who seemed to be something contributed nothing to me.

**2:7** But on the contrary, when they saw that the gospel of the uncircumcision had been entrusted to me, just as that of the circumcision was to Peter

**2:8** (for He who worked for Peter toward the apostleship of the circumcision also worked for me among the Gentiles),

**2:9** and when they recognized the grace that had been given to me, James and Cephas and John, who seemed to be pillars, gave to me and Barnabas the right hands of fellowship, that we should go to the Gentiles, and they to the circumcision;

**2:10** only that we should remember the poor, which is the very thing I too was eager to do.

**2:11** But when Cephas came to Antioch, I withstood him to his face, because he was blameworthy.

**2:12** For before some men came from James, he used to eat with the Gentiles; but when they had come, he began to draw back and separate himself, fearing those who were of the circumcision.

**2:13** And the rest of the Jews consented to his hypocrisy, so that even Barnabas was led by them into that hypocrisy.

**2:14** But when I saw that they were not walking rightly in line with the truth of the gospel, I said to Cephas in front of everyone, "If you, though you are a Jew, live in the Gentile way and not in the Jewish way, how do you compel the Gentiles to live as Jews?"

**2:15** We by nature are Jews, and not sinners from among the Gentiles.

**2:16** Yet knowing that a man is not justified by works of the law, except through faith in Jesus Christ, we also believe in Christ Jesus, so that we may be justified by faith in Christ and not by works of the law, because by works of the law no flesh will be justified.

**2:17** But if, seeking to be justified in Christ, we ourselves also were found to be sinners, is Christ a minister of sin? Far from it!

**2:18** For if I build up again those very things that I tore down, I establish myself as a transgressor.

**2:19** For I, through the law, died to the law, so that I might live to God. I have been nailed to the cross with Christ.

**2:20** And I live—no longer I, but Christ lives in me. And what I now live in the flesh, I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and handed Himself over for me.

**2:21** I do not cast aside the grace of God. For if righteousness is through the law, then Christ died for nothing.

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — The Vulgate reads gentilis ("a Gentile") where the Greek text has Hellen ("a Greek").
- **2:13** — The Vulgate reads duceretur ab eis in illam simulationem ("was led by them into that hypocrisy"), whereas the Greek has "was carried away by their hypocrisy."
- **2:14** — Commentators differ on whether Paul's spoken address to Cephas ends at verse 14 or continues through verse 21.
- **2:16** — Latin fidem Jesu Christi and fide Christi literally read "faith of [Jesus] Christ" (traditionally taken as objective genitive, "faith in Christ," though also debated as subjective, "faithfulness of Christ"); the Vulgate also uses present-tense credimus ("we believe") rather than the Greek aorist ("we believed").
- **2:19** — Following the Clementine Vulgate verse division, Christo confixus sum cruci ("I have been nailed to the cross with Christ") concludes verse 19 rather than beginning verse 20 as in most modern English editions.
