## Text

**6:1** Brothers, even if a person is overtaken in some offense, you who are spiritual, instruct such a one in a spirit of gentleness, examining yourself, so that you too may not be tempted.

**6:2** Bear one another's burdens, and thus you will fulfill the law of Christ.

**6:3** For if anyone considers himself to be something when he is nothing, he deludes himself.

**6:4** But let each one test his own work, and then he will have glory in himself alone, and not in another.

**6:5** For each one will bear his own burden.

**6:6** Now let the one who is taught in the word share with the one who teaches him in all good things.

**6:7** Do not be mistaken: God is not mocked.

**6:8** For whatever things a man sows, these he will also reap. For the one who sows in his own flesh will also reap corruption from the flesh, but the one who sows in the Spirit will reap eternal life from the Spirit.

**6:9** And in doing good, let us not grow faint; for in its own time we will reap, if we do not grow faint.

**6:10** Therefore, while we have time, let us do good to all, but especially to those of the household of faith.

**6:11** See with what kind of letters I have written to you by my own hand.

**6:12** For all who want to please in the flesh, these compel you to be circumcised, only so that they may not suffer persecution for the cross of Christ.

**6:13** For not even those who are circumcised keep the law, but they want you to be circumcised so that they may boast in your flesh.

**6:14** But may it be far from me to boast, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.

**6:15** For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision has any value, but a new creation.

**6:16** And as for all who follow this rule, peace be upon them, and mercy, and upon the Israel of God.

**6:17** From now on, let no one trouble me, for I bear the brand marks of the Lord Jesus in my body.

**6:18** May the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit, brothers. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — Vulgate instruite literally means "instruct" or "set in order," rendering the Greek verb katartizo ("restore," "mend"). The Latin also shifts directly from plural ("you who are spiritual") to singular ("examining yourself, so that you too may not be tempted").
- **6:5** — The Vulgate uses the same noun onus ("burden") and verb portare ("bear") in both 6:2 and 6:5, whereas the Greek distinguishes two different nouns for burden and load.
- **6:8** — In the Clementine Vulgate, "For whatever things a man sows, these he will also reap" begins 6:8 rather than ending 6:7, and uses neuter plurals (quae ... haec) along with in carne sua ("in his own flesh") and in spiritu ("in the Spirit").
- **6:11** — Vulgate qualibus litteris literally means "with what kind of letters" (rather than Greek "with what large letters"), and can also denote "what sort of letter."
- **6:14** — Vulgate per quem ("through whom") is unambiguously masculine and therefore refers to Christ (since Latin crux, "cross," is feminine), whereas the Greek relative pronoun could refer to either Christ or the cross.
