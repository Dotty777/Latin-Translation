## Text

**4:1** Now I say: as long as the heir is a young child, he differs in nothing from a slave, though he is lord of all,

**4:2** but is under guardians and stewards until the time appointed beforehand by the father;

**4:3** so we also, when we were young children, were serving as slaves under the elements of the world.

**4:4** But when the fullness of time arrived, God sent His Son, made from a woman, made under the law,

**4:5** so that He might redeem those who were under the law, so that we might receive the adoption of sons.

**4:6** Now because you are sons, God sent the Spirit of His Son into your hearts, crying out: "Abba, Father."

**4:7** Therefore he is no longer a slave, but a son; and if a son, also an heir through God.

**4:8** Yet at that time indeed, not knowing God, you were slaves to those who by nature are not gods.

**4:9** But now, since you have come to know God, or rather have been known by God, how are you turning back again to the weak and needy elements, to which you want to be slaves once more?

**4:10** You observe days, and months, and seasons, and years.

**4:11** I fear for you, that perhaps I have labored among you for nothing.

**4:12** Be as I am, because I also am as you are; brothers, I implore you. You have not wronged me at all.

**4:13** Now you know that through weakness of the flesh I proclaimed the gospel to you previously, and your trial in my flesh

**4:14** you did not despise or reject, but you welcomed me as an angel of God, as Christ Jesus.

**4:15** Where then is your blessedness? For I bear witness to you that, if it were possible, you would have torn out your eyes and given them to me.

**4:16** Have I therefore become an enemy to you by speaking the truth to you?

**4:17** They are zealous for you, not honorably; rather, they want to exclude you, so that you may be zealous for them.

**4:18** Yet be zealous for what is good in a good thing always, and not only when I am present with you.

**4:19** My little children, for whom I am in labor again until Christ is formed in you,

**4:20** I would indeed wish to be with you right now and to change my voice, because I am confounded about you.

**4:21** Tell me, you who want to be under the law: have you not read the law?

**4:22** For it is written that Abraham had two sons: one from the slave woman, and one from the free woman.

**4:23** Yet the one from the slave woman was born according to the flesh, while the one from the free woman was through promise,

**4:24** which things are spoken through allegory. For these are two covenants: one indeed on Mount Sinai, giving birth into slavery, which is Hagar.

**4:25** For Sinai is a mountain in Arabia, which is joined to the Jerusalem that is now, and is in slavery with her children.

**4:26** But that Jerusalem which is above is free, who is our mother.

**4:27** For it is written: "Rejoice, barren one, you who do not bear; break forth and cry out, you who are not in labor, because many are the children of the desolate woman, more than of her who has a husband."

**4:28** Now we, brothers, like Isaac, are children of promise.

**4:29** Yet just as at that time the one who had been born according to the flesh persecuted the one who was according to the Spirit, so also now.

**4:30** But what does Scripture say? "Drive out the slave woman and her son, for the son of the slave woman will not be an heir with the son of the free woman."

**4:31** Therefore, brothers, we are not children of the slave woman, but of the free woman, by the freedom with which Christ freed us.

## Translator Notes

- **4:7** — The Clementine Vulgate reads third-person singular "he is no longer" (jam non est), continuing the image of the heir from 4:1, whereas the Greek text has second-person singular "you are no longer." In 4:6 the Vulgate also reads "into your hearts" (in corda vestra) against critical Greek "our hearts."
- **4:18** — The Vulgate renders this clause as a second-person imperative, "be zealous for what is good" (Bonum autem aemulamini), whereas the Greek text has a passive infinitive ("it is good to be zealously sought").
- **4:21** — The Vulgate reads "have you not read" (non legistis) rather than the Greek verb meaning "do you not hear/listen to."
- **4:25** — The Clementine Vulgate omits the name Hagar here and translates Greek systoichei ("corresponds to") with "is joined to" (conjunctus est).
- **4:31** — The Clementine Vulgate attaches the final relative clause, "by the freedom with which Christ freed us" (qua libertate Christus nos liberavit), to 4:31 rather than beginning 5:1 with it as in most modern editions.
