## Text

**9:1** Am I not free? Am I not an apostle? Have I not seen Christ Jesus our Lord? Are you not my work in the Lord?

**9:2** And if I am not an apostle to others, yet still I am to you; for you are the seal of my apostleship in the Lord.

**9:3** My defense before those who question me is this:

**9:4** Do we not have the right to eat and to drink?

**9:5** Do we not have the right to take around with us a woman who is a sister, just as the rest of the apostles also do, and the brothers of the Lord, and Cephas?

**9:6** Or do only I and Barnabas not have the right to do this?

**9:7** Who ever serves as a soldier at his own expense? Who plants a vineyard and does not eat from its fruit? Who shepherds a flock and does not eat from the milk of the flock?

**9:8** Am I saying these things according to man? Or does the law not also say these things?

**9:9** For it is written in the law of Moses, "You shall not bind the mouth of a threshing ox." Is God concerned about oxen?

**9:10** Or does He certainly say this for our sake? For these things were written for our sake, because the one who plows ought to plow in hope, and the one who threshes, in hope of receiving the fruit.

**9:11** If we have sown spiritual things for you, is it a great thing if we reap your material things?

**9:12** If others share in the right over you, why not we all the more? Yet we have not used this right, but we endure all things, so that we may not give any hindrance to the gospel of Christ.

**9:13** Do you not know that those who work in the sanctuary eat the things that are from the sanctuary, and those who serve at the altar share with the altar?

**9:14** In this way the Lord also ordained for those who proclaim the gospel to live from the gospel.

**9:15** But I have used none of these things. And I have not written these things so that they should be done this way in my case, for it is better for me to die than for anyone to make my glory void.

**9:16** For if I preach the gospel, there is no glory for me, because a necessity presses upon me; for woe to me if I do not preach the gospel.

**9:17** For if I do this willingly, I have a reward; but if unwillingly, a stewardship has been entrusted to me.

**9:18** What then is my reward? That in preaching the gospel I may present the gospel free of charge, so that I do not misuse my right in the gospel.

**9:19** For though I was free from all, I made myself the slave of all, so that I might gain more.

**9:20** And to the Jews I became like a Jew, so that I might gain the Jews;

**9:21** to those who are under the law, as though I were under the law (though I myself was not under the law), so that I might gain those who were under the law; to those who were without the law, as though I were without the law (though I was not without the law of God, but was in the law of Christ), so that I might gain those who were without the law.

**9:22** To the weak I became weak, so that I might gain the weak. To all I have become all things, so that I might save all.

**9:23** And I do all things on account of the gospel, so that I may become a sharer in it.

**9:24** Do you not know that those who run in a stadium all indeed run, yet one receives the prize? Run in such a way that you may lay hold of it.

**9:25** Now everyone who competes in a contest abstains from all things; and they indeed do so that they may receive a perishable crown, but we an imperishable one.

**9:26** I therefore run in this way, not as though toward something uncertain; I fight in this way, not as though beating the air;

**9:27** but I chastise my body and bring it into servitude, so that after I have preached to others, I myself may not somehow become disqualified.

## Translator Notes

- **9:5** — Latin mulierem sororem literally means "a woman, a sister" (since mulier can denote either "woman" or "wife"); interpreters debate whether Paul refers to a Christian wife or to a devout Christian woman assisting the apostles in ministry.
- **9:6** — The Vulgate distinctively reads hoc operandi ("to do this"), referring back to the rights just listed, whereas Greek manuscripts read me ergazesthai ("to refrain from working").
- **9:15** — Here and in 9:16, Latin gloria ("glory") renders Greek kauchema ("boast" or "ground for boasting"); the Vulgate also smooths the abrupt Greek break in 9:15 into a complete comparative clause ("than for anyone to make my glory void").
- **9:22** — The Vulgate distinctively reads ut omnes facerem salvos ("so that I might save all"), whereas Greek manuscripts read hina pantos tinas soso ("that by all means I might save some").
- **9:27** — Latin reprobus literally means "disapproved" or "rejected" after testing, here rendered "disqualified" in keeping with the athletic contest imagery of 9:24-27.
