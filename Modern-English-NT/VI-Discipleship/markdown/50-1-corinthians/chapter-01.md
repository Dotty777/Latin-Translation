## Text

**1:1** Paul, called to be an apostle of Jesus Christ through the will of God, and Sosthenes the brother,

**1:2** to the church of God that is at Corinth, to those sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints, along with all who call upon the name of our Lord Jesus Christ in every place, theirs and ours.

**1:3** Grace be to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:4** I always give thanks to my God on your behalf for the grace of God that was given to you in Christ Jesus,

**1:5** that in all things you were made rich in Him, in every word and in all knowledge.

**1:6** Just as the testimony of Christ was confirmed in you,

**1:7** so that you lack nothing in any grace, as you await the revelation of our Lord Jesus Christ,

**1:8** who will also confirm you all the way to the end, without blame in the day of the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:9** God is faithful, through whom you were called into the fellowship of His Son, Jesus Christ our Lord.

**1:10** Now I plead with you, brothers, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that all of you say the very same thing, and that there be no divisions among you, but that you be perfected in the same mind and in the same judgment.

**1:11** For it has been reported to me about you, my brothers, by those who belong to Chloe, that there are contentions among you.

**1:12** Now I say this, that each one of you says, "I indeed belong to Paul," "and I to Apollos," "and I to Cephas," "and I to Christ."

**1:13** Has Christ been divided? Was Paul crucified on your behalf? Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?

**1:14** I give thanks to God that I baptized none of you except Crispus and Gaius,

**1:15** so that no one may say that you were baptized in my name.

**1:16** Now I also baptized the household of Stephanas; beyond that, I do not know whether I baptized anyone else.

**1:17** For Christ did not send me to baptize, but to proclaim the gospel, not in wisdom of word, so that the cross of Christ may not be made void.

**1:18** For the word of the cross is indeed foolishness to those who are perishing, but to those who are being saved, that is, to us, it is the power of God.

**1:19** For it is written, "I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, and I will reject the discernment of the discerning."

**1:20** Where is the wise man? Where is the scribe? Where is the inquirer of this age? Has God not made the wisdom of this world foolish?

**1:21** For since in the wisdom of God the world did not know God through wisdom, it pleased God through the foolishness of preaching to save those who believe.

**1:22** For both Jews ask for signs and Greeks seek wisdom,

**1:23** but we preach Christ crucified, to Jews indeed a stumbling block, and to the Gentiles foolishness,

**1:24** but to those themselves who are called, Jews as well as Greeks, Christ, the power of God and the wisdom of God;

**1:25** because what is foolish of God is wiser than men, and what is weak of God is stronger than men.

**1:26** For look at your calling, brothers, that not many are wise according to the flesh, not many are powerful, not many are noble;

**1:27** but God chose the foolish things of the world so that He might put the wise to shame, and God chose the weak things of the world so that He might put the strong things to shame,

**1:28** and God chose the lowly things of the world and the despised things, and the things that are not, so that He might destroy the things that are,

**1:29** so that no flesh may boast in His sight.

**1:30** Now from Him you are in Christ Jesus, who became for us wisdom from God, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption,

**1:31** so that, just as it is written, "He who boasts, let him boast in the Lord."

## Translator Notes

- **1:7** — The Vulgate renders Greek charisma ("spiritual gift") as gratia ("grace"), using the same Latin word that appears in verses 3 and 4.
- **1:8** — The Clementine Vulgate adds adventus ("of the coming"), whereas Greek manuscripts read simply "in the day of our Lord Jesus Christ."
- **1:18** — The Vulgate inserts the explanatory gloss id est nobis ("that is, to us") to clarify the Greek phrasing "to us who are being saved."
- **1:20** — The Vulgate includes hujus ("this") before mundi ("world"), matching the majority Greek text against critical editions, and distinguishes hujus saeculi ("of this age") from hujus mundi ("of this world").
