## Text

**12:1** Now concerning spiritual things, I do not want you to be unaware, brothers.

**12:2** You know that when you were Gentiles, you went to mute idols just as you were being led.

**12:3** Therefore I make known to you that no one speaking in the Spirit of God says, "Anathema to Jesus," and no one can say, "Jesus is Lord," except in the Holy Spirit.

**12:4** Now there are distributions of graces, but the same Spirit;

**12:5** and there are distributions of ministries, but the same Lord;

**12:6** and there are distributions of workings, yet the same God, who works all things in all.

**12:7** Now to each one is given the manifestation of the Spirit for benefit.

**12:8** To one indeed through the Spirit is given an utterance of wisdom, and to another an utterance of knowledge according to the same Spirit;

**12:9** to another faith in the same Spirit; to another the grace of healings in the one Spirit;

**12:10** to another the working of miracles, to another prophecy, to another the discernment of spirits, to another kinds of tongues, to another the interpretation of utterances.

**12:11** Now one and the same Spirit works all these things, distributing to each individually just as He wills.

**12:12** For just as the body is one and has many members, and all the members of the body, though they are many, are yet one body, so also is Christ.

**12:13** For indeed, in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body, whether Jews or Gentiles, whether slaves or free, and we were all made to drink in one Spirit.

**12:14** For the body also is not one member, but many.

**12:15** If the foot says, "Because I am not a hand, I am not of the body," is it therefore not of the body?

**12:16** And if the ear says, "Because I am not an eye, I am not of the body," is it therefore of the body?

**12:17** If the whole body were an eye, where would hearing be? If the whole were hearing, where would smelling be?

**12:18** But now God has placed the members, each one of them in the body, just as He willed.

**12:19** And if all were one member, where would the body be?

**12:20** But now there are indeed many members, yet one body.

**12:21** And the eye cannot say to the hand, "I have no need of your service," or again the head to the feet, "You are not necessary to me."

**12:22** But much more, those members of the body that seem to be weaker are more necessary;

**12:23** and those members of the body that we consider less noble, around these we place more abundant honor; and our unseemly parts have more abundant seemliness.

**12:24** But our seemly parts have need of nothing; instead, God has tempered the body, by granting more abundant honor to that which lacked it,

**12:25** so that there may be no schism in the body, but that the members may have the very same care for one another.

**12:26** And if one member suffers anything, all the members suffer with it; or if one member is glorified, all the members rejoice with it.

**12:27** Now you are the body of Christ, and members of a member.

**12:28** And God has indeed placed some in the church: first, apostles; second, prophets; next, teachers; then, miracles; after that, graces of healings, helps, administrations, kinds of tongues, interpretations of utterances.

**12:29** Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers?

**12:30** Do all perform miracles? Do all have the grace of healings? Do all speak in tongues? Do all interpret?

**12:31** Now eagerly seek the better gifts. And I point out to you a still more excellent way.

## Translator Notes

- **12:3** — The Vulgate phrase anathema Jesu uses the dative ("anathema / a curse to Jesus"), and the verbless Dominus Jesus functions as the confession "Jesus is Lord" (literally "Lord Jesus").
- **12:13** — The Vulgate reads in uno Spiritu potati sumus ("we were all made to drink in one Spirit"), using the ablative with in, whereas the Greek text has "made to drink one Spirit" in the accusative.
- **12:16** — The Clementine Vulgate omits non before est (literally "is it therefore of the body?"), unlike v. 15 (num ideo non est de corpore?), the Greek text, and critical Vulgate editions, which include the negation.
- **12:27** — The Vulgate reads membra de membro ("members of a member," reflecting a Greek variant ek melous), whereas most Greek manuscripts read ek merous ("individually" or "in part").
- **12:28** — The Vulgate reads exinde ("next") rather than "third" before "teachers," and adds interpretationes sermonum ("interpretations of utterances") at the end of the verse, an addition absent from the Greek text.
