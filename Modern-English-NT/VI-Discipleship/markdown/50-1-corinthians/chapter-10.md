## Text

**10:1** For I do not want you to be ignorant, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud, and all crossed through the sea,

**10:2** and all were baptized in Moses in the cloud and in the sea,

**10:3** and all ate the same spiritual food,

**10:4** and all drank the same spiritual drink (for they were drinking from a spiritual rock that followed them, and the rock was Christ);

**10:5** yet God was not well pleased with most of them, for they were struck down in the desert.

**10:6** Now these things happened as a figure for us, so that we might not be desiring evil things, just as they also desired.

**10:7** And do not become idolaters, as some of them were, just as it is written, "The people sat down to eat and to drink, and they rose up to play."

**10:8** And let us not practice sexual immorality, as some of them practiced it, and twenty-three thousand fell in one day.

**10:9** And let us not put Christ to the test, as some of them put Him to the test, and perished by serpents.

**10:10** And do not grumble, as some of them grumbled, and perished by the destroyer.

**10:11** Now all these things happened to them as a figure, and they were written for our correction, upon whom the ends of the ages have arrived.

**10:12** Therefore let the one who thinks he is standing watch out that he does not fall.

**10:13** Let no temptation seize you except what is human; yet God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but with the temptation will also make a way out, so that you may be able to endure it.

**10:14** For this reason, my dearly beloved, flee from the worship of idols.

**10:15** I speak as to prudent people; you yourselves judge what I say.

**10:16** The cup of blessing that we bless, is it not a communion in the blood of Christ? And the bread that we break, is it not a participation in the body of the Lord?

**10:17** Because there is one bread, we who are many are one body, all of us who partake of the one bread.

**10:18** Look at Israel according to the flesh: are not those who eat the sacrifices partakers of the altar?

**10:19** What then? Am I saying that what has been sacrificed to idols is anything, or that an idol is anything?

**10:20** Rather, the things that the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to demons and not to God. And I do not want you to become partners with demons.

**10:21** You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons; you cannot be partakers of the table of the Lord and the table of demons.

**10:22** Or do we provoke the Lord to jealousy? Are we stronger than He? All things are permitted for me, yet not all things are beneficial.

**10:23** All things are permitted for me, yet not all things build up.

**10:24** Let no one seek what is his own, but what belongs to another.

**10:25** Eat everything that is sold in the meat market, asking nothing on account of conscience.

**10:26** The earth is the Lord's, and its fullness.

**10:27** If anyone among the unbelievers invites you and you wish to go, eat everything that is set before you, asking nothing on account of conscience.

**10:28** But if someone says, "This has been sacrificed to idols," do not eat, on account of the one who pointed it out, and on account of conscience.

**10:29** By conscience, however, I mean not your own, but another's. For why is my freedom judged by someone else's conscience?

**10:30** If I partake with gratitude, why am I reviled over that for which I give thanks?

**10:31** Therefore, whether you eat, or drink, or do anything else, do all things for the glory of God.

**10:32** Be without offense to Jews, and to Gentiles, and to the church of God,

**10:33** just as I also please everyone in all things, not seeking what is advantageous to myself, but what is for many, so that they may be saved.

## Translator Notes

- **10:13** — The Vulgate uniquely uses the subjunctive apprehendat ("Let no temptation seize you"), expressing a wish or warning, whereas Greek manuscripts use the perfect indicative eilephen ("No temptation has overtaken you").
- **10:16** — The Vulgate distinctively reads corporis Domini ("the body of the Lord") rather than the Greek "body of Christ," and renders Greek koinonia with two distinct nouns: communicatio ("communion") for the cup and participatio ("participation") for the bread.
- **10:17** — Latin Quoniam unus panis, unum corpus multi sumus can also be read without an implied verb as "For we who are many are one bread, one body"; the Vulgate also omits "for" (Greek gar) in the closing clause, forming a relative clause ("all of us who partake").
- **10:22** — In the Clementine Vulgate verse division, the first maxim ("All things are permitted for me, yet not all things are beneficial") belongs to 10:22 rather than 10:23, and both verses include mihi ("for me"), absent from the oldest Greek manuscripts.
- **10:26** — The Clementine Vulgate omits an introductory "For" (Greek gar) at the start of this quotation from Psalm 24:1 (Vulgate 23:1).
