## Text

**16:1** Now regarding the collections that are being made for the saints, just as I instructed the churches of Galatia, you also do so.

**16:2** On each first day of the week, let each one of you put aside at home, storing up whatever pleases him well, so that when I come, collections will not be made then.

**16:3** And when I am present, those whom you approve by letters, these I will send to deliver your gift to Jerusalem.

**16:4** And if it is fitting that I also go, they will go with me.

**16:5** Now I will come to you when I have passed through Macedonia, for I will pass through Macedonia.

**16:6** And with you I will perhaps stay, or even spend the winter, so that you may see me on my way wherever I go.

**16:7** For I do not want to see you now in passing, for I hope to stay with you for a little while, if the Lord permits.

**16:8** I will remain in Ephesus, however, until Pentecost.

**16:9** For a great and evident door has been opened to me, and there are many adversaries.

**16:10** Now if Timothy comes, see to it that he is without fear among you, for he does the work of the Lord, just as I also do.

**16:11** Therefore let no one despise him, but see him on his way in peace so that he may come to me, for I am waiting for him with the brothers.

**16:12** Now regarding Apollos the brother, I make known to you that I strongly urged him to come to you with the brothers, and there was certainly no will that he should come now; but he will come when he has free time.

**16:13** Keep watch, stand in the faith, act like men, and be strengthened.

**16:14** Let everything you do be done in love.

**16:15** Now I implore you, brothers (you know the household of Stephanas, and of Fortunatus, and of Achaicus, that they are the firstfruits of Achaia and have committed themselves to the service of the saints),

**16:16** that you also be subject to people of this kind, and to everyone who works together and labors.

**16:17** Now I rejoice at the presence of Stephanas, Fortunatus, and Achaicus, because they themselves have supplied what was lacking from you.

**16:18** For they refreshed both my spirit and yours. Recognize, therefore, those who are of this kind.

**16:19** The churches of Asia greet you. Aquila and Priscilla, together with their household church, greet you warmly in the Lord; with them I am also lodging.

**16:20** All the brothers greet you. Greet one another with a holy kiss.

**16:21** The greeting is by my own hand, Paul's.

**16:22** If anyone does not love our Lord Jesus Christ, let him be accursed. Maran Atha.

**16:23** May the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you.

**16:24** My love is with all of you in Christ Jesus. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **16:2** — The Vulgate reads quod ei bene placuerit ("whatever pleases him well"), whereas the Greek text has "as he may prosper."
- **16:9** — The Vulgate reads evidens ("evident"), reflecting a reading of the Greek as "clear/manifest" rather than the Greek text's "effective."
- **16:12** — The Vulgate includes vobis notum facio ("I make known to you"), absent from most Greek manuscripts, and leaves voluntas ("will") without a possessive pronoun, leaving open whether Apollos's own will or God's will is meant.
- **16:15** — The Vulgate names Fortunatus and Achaicus here alongside Stephanas (domum Stephanae, et Fortunati, et Achaici), whereas the Greek text mentions only the household of Stephanas in this verse and introduces the other two in verse 17.
- **16:19** — The Vulgate includes the closing clause apud quos et hospitor ("with them I am also lodging"), a Western reading absent from standard Greek texts, and uses the form Priscilla rather than Prisca.
- **16:22** — The Vulgate expands "the Lord" of the critical Greek text to Dominum nostrum Jesum Christum ("our Lord Jesus Christ") and retains the Aramaic expression Maran Atha ("Our Lord has come" or "Our Lord, come!"); "accursed" translates Latin anathema.
