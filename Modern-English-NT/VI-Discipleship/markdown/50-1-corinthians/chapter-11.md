## Text

**11:1** Be imitators of me, just as I too am of Christ.

**11:2** Now I praise you, brothers, because in all things you are mindful of me and hold to my precepts just as I handed them down to you.

**11:3** Yet I want you to know that every man's head is Christ, while woman's head is man, and Christ's head is God.

**11:4** Every man praying or prophesying with a veiled head disgraces his head.

**11:5** Yet every woman praying or prophesying with an unveiled head disgraces her head, for it is one and the same as if she were shaved bald.

**11:6** For if a woman is not veiled, let her be shorn. But if it is disgraceful for a woman to be shorn or shaved bald, let her veil her head.

**11:7** A man indeed ought not to veil his head, since he is the image and glory of God; but woman is the glory of man.

**11:8** For man is not from woman, but woman from man.

**11:9** For indeed, man was not created for the sake of woman, but woman for the sake of man.

**11:10** Therefore a woman ought to have authority on her head because of the angels.

**11:11** Nevertheless, neither is man without woman, nor woman without man, in the Lord.

**11:12** For just as woman is from man, so also is man through woman; yet all things are from God.

**11:13** You yourselves judge: is it fitting for an unveiled woman to pray to God?

**11:14** Does not even nature itself teach you that if a man indeed grows long hair, it is a dishonor to him,

**11:15** whereas if a woman grows long hair, it is a glory to her? For her hair has been given to her in place of a veil.

**11:16** But if anyone seems to be contentious, we have no such custom, nor does the church of God.

**11:17** Now this I command, not giving praise, because you come together not for the better, but for the worse.

**11:18** First indeed, when you come together in the church, I hear that there are divisions among you, and in part I believe it.

**11:19** For there must also be heresies, so that those too who are approved may become manifest among you.

**11:20** Therefore, when you come together into one place, it is no longer to eat the Lord's supper.

**11:21** For each one takes his own supper beforehand to eat, and one indeed is hungry, while another is drunk.

**11:22** Do you not have houses for eating and drinking? Or do you despise the church of God and put to shame those who have nothing? What shall I say to you? Do I praise you? In this I do not praise you.

**11:23** For I received from the Lord what I also handed down to you, that the Lord Jesus, on the night in which He was being handed over, took bread,

**11:24** and giving thanks, He broke it and said, "Take and eat: this is My body, which will be handed over for you. Do this in remembrance of Me."

**11:25** Similarly He took the cup also, after He had eaten supper, saying, "This cup is the new covenant in My blood. Do this, as often as you drink, in remembrance of Me."

**11:26** For as often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you will proclaim the death of the Lord until He comes.

**11:27** Therefore, whoever eats this bread or drinks the cup of the Lord unworthily will be guilty of the body and blood of the Lord.

**11:28** But let a person examine himself, and in this way let him eat from that bread and drink from the cup.

**11:29** For the one who eats and drinks unworthily eats and drinks judgment for himself, not discerning the body of the Lord.

**11:30** For this reason many among you are weak and sickly, and many sleep.

**11:31** But if we judged ourselves rightly, we would certainly not be judged.

**11:32** Yet while we are judged, we are disciplined by the Lord, so that we may not be condemned with this world.

**11:33** So then, my brothers, when you come together to eat, wait for one another.

**11:34** If anyone is hungry, let him eat at home, so that you may not come together for judgment. And the rest I will set in order when I come.

## Translator Notes

- **11:2** — The Vulgate reads "my precepts" (praecepta mea) and includes "brothers" (fratres), whereas the critical Greek text has simply "the traditions" without "my" or "brothers."
- **11:10** — Latin potestatem supra caput is literally "authority over the head"; "her" is supplied in English.
- **11:16** — The Vulgate reads the singular "church of God" (ecclesia Dei), whereas the Greek text has the plural ("churches of God").
- **11:24** — The Vulgate includes "Take and eat" (Accipite, et manducate) and reads the future passive "which will be handed over for you" (quod pro vobis tradetur), differing from both the critical Greek text ("which is for you") and the Textus Receptus ("which is broken for you").
- **11:29** — The Vulgate retains "unworthily" (indigne) and "of the Lord" (Domini) in "the body of the Lord," readings absent from the critical Greek text.
