## Text

**4:1** So let a person consider us as servants of Christ and stewards of the mysteries of God.

**4:2** Now here it is required among stewards that someone be found faithful.

**4:3** To me, however, it counts for very little that I should be judged by you or by a human day; yet I do not even judge myself.

**4:4** For I am aware of nothing against myself, yet I am not justified by this; rather, the One who judges me is the Lord.

**4:5** Therefore do not judge before the time, until the Lord comes, who will both illuminate the hidden things of darkness and reveal the counsels of the hearts; and then there will be praise for each one from God.

**4:6** Now these things, brothers, I have applied in a figure to myself and Apollos for your sake, so that in us you may learn that, beyond what is written, one should not be puffed up against another on behalf of another.

**4:7** For who distinguishes you? And what do you have that you did not receive? Yet if you received it, why do you boast as though you had not received it?

**4:8** Already you are satisfied, already you have become rich; without us you reign. And I wish that you did reign, so that we also might reign with you.

**4:9** For I think that God has displayed us apostles as last of all, as ones destined for death, because we have been made a spectacle to the world, both to angels and to people.

**4:10** We are fools on account of Christ, yet you are prudent in Christ; we are weak, yet you are strong; you are noble, yet we are ignoble.

**4:11** Up to this hour we both hunger and thirst, and are naked, and are struck with fists, and are unsettled,

**4:12** and we toil, working with our own hands; we are cursed, and we bless; we suffer persecution, and we endure;

**4:13** we are slandered, and we plead; we have been made like the sweepings of this world, the dregs of all things until now.

**4:14** I am not writing these things to put you to shame, but as my dearest children I admonish you.

**4:15** For even if you have ten thousand guardians in Christ, still you do not have many fathers. For in Christ Jesus, through the gospel, I fathered you.

**4:16** Therefore I appeal to you: be imitators of me, just as I also am of Christ.

**4:17** For this reason I have sent Timothy to you, who is my dearest son and faithful in the Lord, who will remind you of my ways that are in Christ Jesus, just as I teach everywhere in every church.

**4:18** As though I were not coming to you, so some have become puffed up.

**4:19** However, I will come to you soon, if the Lord wills, and I will find out not the talk of those who are puffed up, but their power.

**4:20** For the kingdom of God is not in talk, but in power.

**4:21** What do you want? Should I come to you with a rod, or in love and a spirit of gentleness?

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — Literally "by a human day" (ab humano die), an idiom for a human tribunal or day of human judgment, contrasted with the Lord's judgment in vv. 4-5.
- **4:6** — Unlike the Greek, which separates the maxim "not beyond what is written" from a second-person plural clause, the Vulgate combines them into a single third-person singular clause; transfiguravi literally means "applied in a figure."
- **4:13** — The Vulgate retains the Greek loanword peripsema (literally scrapings, offscouring, or dregs) and specifies "this world" (hujus mundi) where the Greek has simply "the world."
- **4:16** — The closing clause "just as I also am of Christ" (sicut et ego Christi) is a distinctly Vulgate reading (cf. 1 Cor 11:1) absent from the standard critical Greek text.
