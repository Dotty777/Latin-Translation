## Text

**14:1** Pursue love, be eager for spiritual things, yet even more that you may prophesy.

**14:2** For the one who speaks in a tongue does not speak to men, but to God, for no one hears; yet in spirit he speaks mysteries.

**14:3** For the one who prophesies speaks to men for edification, and encouragement, and comfort.

**14:4** The one who speaks in a tongue edifies himself, but the one who prophesies edifies the church of God.

**14:5** Now I want you all to speak in tongues, yet even more to prophesy. For the one who prophesies is greater than the one who speaks in tongues, unless perhaps he interprets so that the church may receive edification.

**14:6** But now, brothers, if I come to you speaking in tongues, what benefit will I be to you unless I speak to you either in revelation, or in knowledge, or in prophecy, or in teaching?

**14:7** Yet things without life that give sound, whether flute or lyre, unless they give a distinction of tones, how will it be known what is played on the flute or what is played on the lyre?

**14:8** For indeed, if the trumpet gives an uncertain sound, who will prepare himself for war?

**14:9** So also you, unless by the tongue you give clear speech, how will it be known what is said? For you will be speaking into the air.

**14:10** There are, for example, so many kinds of languages in this world, and nothing is without a voice.

**14:11** If therefore I do not know the meaning of the voice, I will be a foreigner to the one to whom I speak, and the one who speaks will be a foreigner to me.

**14:12** So also you, since you are zealous for spirits, seek to abound for the edification of the church.

**14:13** And therefore let the one who speaks in a tongue pray that he may interpret.

**14:14** For if I pray in a tongue, my spirit prays, but my mind is without fruit.

**14:15** What is it then? I will pray with the spirit, and I will also pray with the mind; I will sing psalms with the spirit, and I will also sing psalms with the mind.

**14:16** Otherwise, if you bless with the spirit, how will the one who fills the place of an uninstructed person say "Amen" upon your blessing, since he does not know what you are saying?

**14:17** For you indeed give thanks well, but the other person is not edified.

**14:18** I give thanks to my God that I speak in the tongue of all of you.

**14:19** Yet in the church I would rather speak five words with my understanding, so that I may instruct others also, than ten thousand words in a tongue.

**14:20** Brothers, do not become children in your understanding, but in malice be little children; in your understanding, however, be mature.

**14:21** In the law it is written, "In other tongues and with other lips I will speak to this people, and not even so will they listen to Me," says the Lord.

**14:22** Therefore tongues are for a sign, not for believers, but for unbelievers; prophecies, however, are not for unbelievers, but for believers.

**14:23** If therefore the whole church comes together in one place, and all speak in tongues, and uninstructed persons or unbelievers enter, will they not say that you are insane?

**14:24** But if all prophesy, and some unbeliever or uninstructed person enters, he is convicted by all, he is judged by all.

**14:25** The hidden things of his heart become manifest, and so, falling on his face, he will worship God, declaring that God is truly among you.

**14:26** What is it then, brothers? When you come together, each one of you has a psalm, has a teaching, has a revelation, has a tongue, has an interpretation. Let all things be done for edification.

**14:27** If anyone speaks in a tongue, let it be by two, or at the most three, and in turn, and one interprets.

**14:28** But if there is no interpreter, let him keep silent in the church; instead, let him speak to himself and to God.

**14:29** And let two or three prophets speak, and let the rest judge.

**14:30** But if something is revealed to another who is seated, let the first keep silent.

**14:31** For you can all prophesy one by one, so that all may learn and all may be encouraged.

**14:32** And the spirits of prophets are subject to prophets.

**14:33** For God is not a God of dissension, but of peace, just as I also teach in all the churches of the saints.

**14:34** Let women keep silent in the churches, for it is not permitted for them to speak, but to be submissive, just as the law also says.

**14:35** But if they want to learn anything, let them ask their own husbands at home. For it is shameful for a woman to speak in the church.

**14:36** Or did the word of God come forth from you? Or did it reach you alone?

**14:37** If anyone thinks himself to be a prophet or spiritual, let him recognize that the things I write to you are the Lord's commands.

**14:38** But if anyone does not know, he will not be known.

**14:39** Therefore, brothers, be eager to prophesy, and do not forbid speaking in tongues.

**14:40** Yet let all things be done properly and according to order.

## Translator Notes

- **14:4** — The Vulgate explicitly reads 'the church of God' (ecclesiam Dei), whereas most Greek manuscripts read simply 'the church.'
- **14:10** — The Vulgate reads 'kinds of languages' (genera linguarum) and 'in this world' (in hoc mundo), where the Greek has 'kinds of sounds' (gene phonon) and 'in the world.'
- **14:16** — Latin idiota (from Greek idiotes, also in vv. 23-24) denotes an uninstructed person or outsider lacking specialized knowledge; 'upon your blessing' (super tuam benedictionem) is a Vulgate reading where the Greek has 'at your thanksgiving' (eucharistia).
- **14:18** — The Clementine Vulgate reads 'my God' (Deo meo) and 'that I speak in the tongue of all of you' (omnium vestrum lingua loquor), omitting the comparative 'more than' (mallon) found in the Greek.
- **14:33** — The Vulgate translates Greek akatastasias ('disorder/confusion') as 'dissension' (dissensionis) and explicitly adds the verb 'I teach' (doceo), joining 'as in all the churches of the saints' to v. 33 rather than v. 34.
