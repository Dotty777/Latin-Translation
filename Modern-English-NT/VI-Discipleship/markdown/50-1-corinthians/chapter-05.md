## Text

**5:1** Actually, sexual immorality is heard of among you, and such sexual immorality as is not even among the Gentiles, so that someone has his father's wife.

**5:2** And you are puffed up, and have not rather mourned, so that the one who has done this deed may be removed from your midst.

**5:3** I indeed, absent in body yet present in spirit, have already judged, as though present, the one who has acted in this way,

**5:4** in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, when you and my spirit are gathered together, with the power of our Lord Jesus,

**5:5** to hand such a person over to Satan for the destruction of the flesh, so that the spirit may be saved on the day of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**5:6** Your boasting is not good. Do you not know that a little leaven corrupts the whole lump?

**5:7** Purge out the old leaven, so that you may be a new batch, just as you are unleavened. For indeed, Christ our Passover has been sacrificed.

**5:8** So let us feast, not with old leaven, nor with the leaven of malice and depravity, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.

**5:9** I wrote in the letter, "Do not associate with sexually immoral people,"

**5:10** not at all with the sexually immoral people of this world, or the greedy, or extortioners, or those who serve idols; otherwise you would have had to go out of this world.

**5:11** But now I have written to you not to associate: if the one who is named a brother is sexually immoral, or greedy, or one who serves idols, or a reviler, or a drunkard, or an extortioner, with such a person not even to take food.

**5:12** For what is it to me to judge those who are outside? Do you not judge those who are inside?

**5:13** For those who are outside, God will judge. Remove the evil from among yourselves.

## Translator Notes

- **5:4** — The Vulgate has fuller titles in v. 4a ("our Lord Jesus Christ") and v. 5 ("the day of our Lord Jesus Christ") where the critical Greek text has shorter forms (in v. 5 simply "the day of the Lord").
- **5:6** — The Vulgate reads corrumpit ("corrupts" or "spoils"), a distinctive Western reading where the standard Greek text has "leavens" (zymoi).
- **5:9** — The Clementine Vulgate omits "to you" (vobis) here, reading simply "I wrote in the letter," unlike v. 11 and the Greek text.
- **5:13** — The Vulgate reads the future tense judicabit ("will judge"); malum can grammatically be masculine ("the evil person") or neuter ("the evil").
