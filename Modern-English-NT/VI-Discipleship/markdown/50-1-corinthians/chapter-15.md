## Text

**15:1** Now, brothers, I make known to you the gospel that I preached to you, which you also received, in which you also stand,

**15:2** through which you are also being saved, if you hold fast to the manner in which I preached to you, unless you believed in vain.

**15:3** For I handed down to you first of all what I also received: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures,

**15:4** and that He was buried, and that He rose on the third day according to the Scriptures,

**15:5** and that He was seen by Cephas, and after this by the eleven;

**15:6** then He was seen by more than five hundred brothers together, of whom many remain until now, though some have fallen asleep;

**15:7** then He was seen by James, then by all the apostles;

**15:8** and last of all, as by one born prematurely, He was seen by me also.

**15:9** For I myself am the least of the apostles, one who is not worthy to be called an apostle, since I persecuted the church of God.

**15:10** Yet by the grace of God I am what I am, and His grace in me was not empty, but I labored more abundantly than all of them; not I, however, but the grace of God with me.

**15:11** For whether it is I or they, thus we preach, and thus you believed.

**15:12** But if Christ is preached, that He has risen from the dead, how do some among you say that there is no resurrection of the dead?

**15:13** But if there is no resurrection of the dead, neither has Christ risen.

**15:14** And if Christ has not risen, then our preaching is empty, and your faith is empty as well.

**15:15** And we are also found to be false witnesses of God, since we bore witness against God that He raised Christ, whom He did not raise, if the dead do not rise.

**15:16** For if the dead do not rise, neither has Christ risen.

**15:17** And if Christ has not risen, your faith is futile; for you are still in your sins.

**15:18** Therefore those who have fallen asleep in Christ have also perished.

**15:19** If in this life only we are hoping in Christ, we are more pitiable than all people.

**15:20** But now Christ has risen from the dead, the firstfruits of those who sleep,

**15:21** since indeed through a man came death, and through a man the resurrection of the dead.

**15:22** And just as in Adam all die, so in Christ all will also be made alive.

**15:23** But each one in his own order: Christ the firstfruits; then those who belong to Christ, who have believed in His coming.

**15:24** Then the end, when He delivers the kingdom to God and the Father, when He has nullified every rule and authority and power.

**15:25** Now He must reign until He places all enemies under His feet.

**15:26** And as the last enemy, death will be destroyed; for He has subjected all things under His feet. But when He says,

**15:27** "All things have been subjected to Him," without doubt this excludes Him who subjected all things to Him.

**15:28** And when all things have been subjected to Him, then even the Son Himself will be subjected to Him who subjected all things to Him, so that God may be all in all.

**15:29** Otherwise, what will those do who are baptized for the dead, if the dead do not rise at all? Why are they even baptized for them?

**15:30** Why do we also face danger every hour?

**15:31** I die daily, by your glory, brothers, which I have in Christ Jesus our Lord.

**15:32** If in human terms I fought against wild beasts at Ephesus, what does it benefit me, if the dead do not rise? "Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we will die."

**15:33** Do not be led astray: bad conversations corrupt good morals.

**15:34** Wake up, you righteous, and do not sin; for some have ignorance of God. To your shame I speak to you.

**15:35** But someone will say, "How do the dead rise? Or with what kind of body will they come?"

**15:36** Foolish one, what you yourself sow is not made alive unless it first dies;

**15:37** and what you sow, you do not sow the body that is to be, but a bare grain, for instance of wheat or of one of the others.

**15:38** But God gives it a body as He wills, just as to each of the seeds its own body.

**15:39** Not all flesh is the same flesh, but one kind is of humans, another of livestock, another of birds, and another of fish.

**15:40** There are both heavenly bodies and earthly bodies, yet one is the glory of the heavenly, and another is that of the earthly.

**15:41** One is the brightness of the sun, another the brightness of the moon, and another the brightness of the stars; for star differs from star in brightness.

**15:42** So also is the resurrection of the dead. It is sown in corruption; it will rise in incorruption.

**15:43** It is sown in dishonor; it will rise in glory. It is sown in weakness; it will rise in power.

**15:44** It is sown a natural body; it will rise a spiritual body. If there is a natural body, there is also a spiritual one, as it is written:

**15:45** "The first man Adam was made into a living soul"; the last Adam, into a life-giving spirit.

**15:46** Yet what is spiritual is not first, but what is natural; then what is spiritual.

**15:47** The first man is from the earth, earthly; the second man is from heaven, heavenly.

**15:48** As is the earthly one, such also are the earthly; and as is the heavenly one, such also are the heavenly.

**15:49** Therefore, just as we have borne the image of the earthly one, let us also bear the image of the heavenly one.

**15:50** Now I say this, brothers, that flesh and blood cannot possess the kingdom of God, nor will corruption possess incorruptibility.

**15:51** Look, I tell you a mystery: we will indeed all rise, but we will not all be changed.

**15:52** In a moment, in the blink of an eye, at the last trumpet; for the trumpet will sound, and the dead will rise incorruptible, and we will be changed.

**15:53** For it is necessary for this corruptible to put on incorruption, and for this mortal to put on immortality.

**15:54** And when this mortal has put on immortality, then the word that is written will come about: "Death has been swallowed up in victory."

**15:55** "Where, death, is your victory? Where, death, is your sting?"

**15:56** And the sting of death is sin, while the power of sin is the law.

**15:57** But thanks be to God, who gave us victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.

**15:58** Therefore, my beloved brothers, be firm and immovable, abounding in the work of the Lord always, knowing that your labor is not empty in the Lord.

## Translator Notes

- **15:5** — The Clementine Vulgate reads undecim ("eleven") rather than "twelve" as in Greek manuscripts, reflecting Judas's absence after the resurrection (similarly, 15:6 reads multi, "many," rather than "most").
- **15:23** — The Clementine Vulgate distinctively includes qui ... crediderunt ("who have believed in His coming"), whereas the Greek text simply reads "those who belong to Christ at His coming."
- **15:34** — The Vulgate reads the plural adjective/vocative justi ("you righteous") rather than the Greek adverb "righteously," and renders Greek entrope ("shame") with Ecclesiastical Latin reverentiam (humbling shame or respect).
- **15:51** — A famous Vulgate-distinct reading: omnes quidem resurgemus, sed non omnes immutabimur ("we will indeed all rise, but we will not all be changed"), contrasting with the Greek text's "we will not all sleep, but we will all be changed."
- **15:54** — Following its textual tradition, the Clementine Vulgate includes only the clause about this mortal putting on immortality, omitting the parallel clause about this corruptible putting on incorruption found in most Greek manuscripts.
