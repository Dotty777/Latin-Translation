## Text

**7:1** Now regarding the matters you wrote to me about: it is good for a man not to touch a woman.

**7:2** Yet because of fornication, let each man have his own wife, and let each woman have her own husband.

**7:3** Let the husband pay what is due to his wife, and likewise the wife also to her husband.

**7:4** The wife holds no power over her own body, but the husband does. And likewise the husband also holds no power over his own body, but the wife does.

**7:5** Do not deprive each other, except perhaps by consent for a time, so that you may be free for prayer; and return together again, so that Satan may not tempt you on account of your lack of self-restraint.

**7:6** Now I say this as a concession, not as a command.

**7:7** For I wish all of you to be as I myself am; but each one has a gift of his own from God, one in this way, and another in that.

**7:8** Now I say to the unmarried and to widows: it is good for them if they remain this way, just as I also do.

**7:9** But if they do not practice self-restraint, let them marry. For it is better to marry than to burn.

**7:10** Now to those who are joined in marriage, I command, not I but the Lord, that a wife must not separate from her husband.

**7:11** Yet if she does separate, she is to remain unmarried, or be reconciled to her husband. And a husband must not divorce his wife.

**7:12** For to the rest I say, not the Lord: if any brother has an unbelieving wife, and she consents to live with him, let him not divorce her.

**7:13** And if any believing woman has an unbelieving husband, and he consents to live with her, let her not divorce her husband.

**7:14** For the unbelieving husband has been sanctified through the believing wife, and the unbelieving wife has been sanctified through the believing husband; otherwise your children would be unclean, but now they are holy.

**7:15** But if the unbeliever separates, let him separate; for a brother or sister is not subjected to bondage in such cases, yet God has called us in peace.

**7:16** For how do you know, wife, if you will save your husband? Or how do you know, husband, if you will save your wife?

**7:17** Only, as the Lord has apportioned to each one, as God has called each one, so let him walk, and just as I teach in all the churches.

**7:18** Was anyone called circumcised? Let him not draw forward the foreskin. Was anyone called in uncircumcision? Let him not be circumcised.

**7:19** Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but rather the keeping of God's commandments.

**7:20** Let each person remain in that calling in which he was called.

**7:21** Were you called a slave? Do not let it be a concern to you; yet even if you are able to become free, rather make use of it.

**7:22** For the one who was called a slave in the Lord is a freedman of the Lord; likewise, the one who was called free is a slave of Christ.

**7:23** You were purchased at a price; do not become slaves of human beings.

**7:24** Brothers, let each one remain with God in that in which he was called.

**7:25** Now regarding virgins, I do not have a command of the Lord, yet I give counsel as one who has received mercy from the Lord to be trustworthy.

**7:26** Therefore I consider this to be good because of the pressing necessity, that it is good for a man to be this way.

**7:27** Are you bound to a wife? Do not look for release. Are you released from a wife? Do not look for a wife.

**7:28** Yet if you take a wife, you have not sinned. And if a virgin marries, she has not sinned; still, such people will have affliction of the flesh. And I am sparing you.

**7:29** Therefore I say this, brothers: the time is short. What remains is that even those who have wives should be as though having none;

**7:30** and those who weep, as though not weeping; and those who rejoice, as though not rejoicing; and those who buy, as though not possessing;

**7:31** and those who use this world, as though they were not using it; for the shape of this world is passing away.

**7:32** Now I want you to be free from concern. The one who is without a wife is concerned with the things of the Lord, how he may please God.

**7:33** Yet the one who is with a wife is concerned with the things of the world, how he may please his wife, and he is divided.

**7:34** And the unmarried woman, as well as the virgin, gives thought to the things of the Lord, so that she may be holy in body and in spirit. But the one who is married gives thought to the things of the world, how she may please her husband.

**7:35** Furthermore, I say this for your own benefit, not to throw a snare over you, but for what is honorable and for what affords the opportunity to implore the Lord without hindrance.

**7:36** Now if anyone thinks he appears dishonorable regarding his virgin because she is past her prime, and so it ought to be done, let him do what he wishes; he does not sin if she marries.

**7:37** For the one who has resolved steadfastly in his heart, having no necessity, yet having power over his own will, and has decided this in his heart, to keep his virgin, does well.

**7:38** Therefore, both the one who joins his virgin in marriage does well, and the one who does not join her in marriage does better.

**7:39** A wife is bound to the law for as long as her husband lives; yet if her husband falls asleep, she is set free: let her marry whomever she wishes, only in the Lord.

**7:40** Yet she will be more blessed if she remains this way, according to my counsel; and I suppose that I also have the Spirit of God.

## Translator Notes

- **7:7** — The Clementine Vulgate reads omnes vos ("all of you"), whereas the Greek text reads pantas anthropous ("all people/men").
- **7:18** — Latin adducat praeputium literally means "draw forward the foreskin," referring to the ancient practice of epispasm (attempting to conceal or reverse circumcision); in the next clause and v. 19, praeputium denotes the state of uncircumcision.
- **7:21** — Latin magis utere ("rather make use of it") leaves the object unstated, preserving the debated ambiguity between taking advantage of the opportunity for freedom and making use of one's present calling.
- **7:35** — Vulgate facultatem praebeat sine impedimento Dominum obsecrandi ("affords the opportunity to implore the Lord without hindrance") differs from the Greek text, which speaks of undistracted attendance or devotion to the Lord.
- **7:36** — The Vulgate reads the singular conditional si nubat ("if she marries") rather than the Greek plural imperative gameitosan ("let them marry"), and keeps "his virgin" open to either an unmarried daughter in a father's care or a betrothed virgin.
