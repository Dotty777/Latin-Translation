## Text

**8:1** Now regarding the things that are sacrificed to idols, we know that all of us have knowledge. Knowledge puffs up, whereas love builds up.

**8:2** Yet if anyone considers himself to know something, he has not yet known in what way he ought to know.

**8:3** Yet if anyone loves God, this one is known by Him.

**8:4** Now regarding the foods that are offered in sacrifice to idols, we know that an idol is nothing in the world, and that there is no God except one.

**8:5** For even if there are those who are called gods, whether in heaven or on earth (since indeed there are many gods and many lords),

**8:6** yet for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things, and we for Him; and one Lord Jesus Christ, through whom are all things, and we through Him.

**8:7** Yet knowledge is not in everyone. Some, however, with conscience of the idol up to now, eat it as something sacrificed to an idol; and their conscience, since it is weak, is defiled.

**8:8** Now food does not commend us to God. For neither if we eat will we abound, nor if we do not eat will we fall short.

**8:9** Be careful, however, that this freedom of yours does not perhaps become a stumbling block for the weak.

**8:10** For if someone sees the one who has knowledge reclining at table in an idol's temple, will not his conscience, since it is weak, be built up to eat things sacrificed to idols?

**8:11** And will the weak one perish by your knowledge, the brother for whom Christ died?

**8:12** Yet by sinning in this way against the brothers, and striking their weak conscience, you sin against Christ.

**8:13** For this reason, if food causes my brother to stumble, I will not eat meat ever again, so that I may not cause my brother to stumble.

## Translator Notes

- **8:4** — The Vulgate reads De escis ("regarding the foods"), whereas the Greek text reads peri tes broseos ("concerning the eating").
- **8:7** — The Vulgate reads cum conscientia ... idoli ("with conscience of the idol"), reflecting Greek manuscripts with syneidesei ("conscience/awareness") rather than the critical Greek text's synetheia ("habit/being accustomed").
- **8:8** — The Vulgate uses the present tense commendat ("does not commend") and reverses the order of the conditional clauses compared to the critical Greek text, placing eating before not eating.
- **8:10** — The Vulgate reads third-person eum, qui habet scientiam ("the one who has knowledge") instead of Greek second-person "you who have knowledge," and uses aedificabitur ("be built up") in ironic echo of aedificat ("builds up") in 8:1.
