## Text

**3:1** Likewise also, let wives be subject to their husbands, so that even if any do not believe the word, they may be won without a word through the conduct of the wives,

**3:2** observing your chaste conduct in fear.

**3:3** Let theirs not be the outward styling of hair, or the putting on of gold, or the adornment of wearing garments,

**3:4** but the hidden person of the heart, in the incorruptibility of a quiet and gentle spirit, which is rich in the sight of God.

**3:5** For in this way formerly the holy women also, hoping in God, adorned themselves, being subject to their own husbands,

**3:6** just as Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him lord, whose daughters you are, doing good and not fearing any alarm.

**3:7** Husbands, likewise, live together according to knowledge, imparting honor to the female vessel as the weaker one, as also to fellow heirs of the grace of life, so that your prayers may not be hindered.

**3:8** Finally, all of you be of one mind, sympathetic, lovers of the brotherhood, merciful, gentle, humble,

**3:9** not returning evil for evil, nor reviling for reviling, but on the contrary blessing, because to this you were called, so that you may possess a blessing by inheritance.

**3:10** For the one who wants to love life and to see good days, let him restrain his tongue from evil, and let his lips not speak deceit.

**3:11** Let him turn aside from evil and do good; let him seek peace and pursue it,

**3:12** because the eyes of the Lord are upon the righteous, and His ears are toward their prayers; yet the face of the Lord is upon those who do evil things.

**3:13** And who is there who would harm you, if you are eager followers of what is good?

**3:14** But even if you suffer anything for the sake of righteousness, you are blessed. And do not fear their fear, and do not be troubled.

**3:15** But sanctify the Lord Christ in your hearts, ready at all times to give a satisfactory answer to everyone asking you for a reason about that hope which is in you;

**3:16** yet with gentleness and fear, having a good conscience, so that in the matter in which they slander you, those who falsely accuse your good conduct in Christ may be put to shame.

**3:17** For it is better (if the will of God should will it) to suffer doing good than doing evil.

**3:18** Because Christ also died once for our sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, so that He might offer us to God, put to death indeed in the flesh, yet made alive in the spirit,

**3:19** in which He also came and preached to those spirits who were in prison,

**3:20** who had once been unbelieving, when they waited for the patience of God in the days of Noah, while the ark was being built, in which a few, that is, eight souls, were saved through water.

**3:21** And of like form to this, baptism has now saved you also, not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the inquiry of a good conscience toward God, through the resurrection of Jesus Christ,

**3:22** who is at the right hand of God, swallowing up death so that we might be made heirs of eternal life, having gone into heaven with angels and authorities and powers made subject to Him.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The Clementine text prints "per mulierem conversationem" (accusative singular, a traditional textual variant for genitive plural "mulierum", "of the wives").
- **3:8** — The Vulgate lists six qualities, including both "modesti" ("gentle") and "humiles" ("humble"), where the Greek text lists five.
- **3:18** — The Vulgate uses the sacrificial verb "offerret" ("offer") for Greek "prosagage" ("bring"); lowercase "spiritu" (parallel to "carne") may refer either to Christ's spirit or to the Holy Spirit.
- **3:20** — The Vulgate reads "quando exspectabant Dei patientiam" ("when they waited for the patience of God") with a plural verb, differing from the Greek "when the patience of God waited".
- **3:22** — The Vulgate adds "deglutiens mortem ut vitae aeternae haeredes efficeremur" ("swallowing up death so that we might be made heirs of eternal life"), a distinctive Latin clause absent from the Greek text.
