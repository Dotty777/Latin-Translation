## Text

**2:1** Laying aside therefore all malice, and all deceit, and pretenses, and envies, and all slanders,

**2:2** like newborn infants, long for the reasonable milk without deceit, so that by it you may grow into salvation,

**2:3** if indeed you have tasted that the Lord is sweet.

**2:4** Coming to Him, a living stone, rejected indeed by men, yet chosen and honored by God,

**2:5** you yourselves also, like living stones, are being built up, a spiritual house, a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.

**2:6** For this reason Scripture contains this: "Look, I am laying in Zion a chief cornerstone, chosen, precious; and the one who believes in Him will not be put to shame."

**2:7** To you therefore who believe belongs the honor; but to those who do not believe, the stone that the builders rejected, this has become the head of the corner,

**2:8** and a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense to those who stumble at the word and do not believe, for which they were also appointed.

**2:9** But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people of possession, so that you may announce the virtues of Him who called you out of darkness into His wonderful light.

**2:10** You who once were not a people, yet now are the people of God; who had not obtained mercy, yet now have obtained mercy.

**2:11** Beloved, I implore you as sojourners and foreigners to abstain from fleshly desires, which wage war against the soul,

**2:12** keeping your conduct good among the Gentiles, so that in the matter in which they slander you as evildoers, observing you from your good works, they may glorify God on the day of visitation.

**2:13** Be subject therefore to every human creature for God's sake, whether to the king as preeminent,

**2:14** or to governors as sent by him for the punishment of evildoers, yet for the praise of the good;

**2:15** for such is the will of God, that by doing good you may silence the ignorance of foolish men,

**2:16** as free, and not having freedom as a covering for malice, but as servants of God.

**2:17** Honor all people; love the brotherhood; fear God; hold the king in honor.

**2:18** Servants, be subject in all fear to your masters, not only to the good and gentle, but also to the harsh.

**2:19** For this is grace, if because of conscience toward God someone endures sorrows, suffering unjustly.

**2:20** For what glory is it if, sinning and being beaten, you endure it? But if, doing good, you endure patiently, this is grace before God.

**2:21** For to this you were called, because Christ also suffered for us, leaving you an example so that you may follow His footsteps,

**2:22** who committed no sin, nor was deceit found in His mouth;

**2:23** who, when He was reviled, did not revile; when He suffered, He did not threaten, but handed Himself over to the one judging Him unjustly;

**2:24** who Himself bore our sins in His body upon the tree, so that, dead to sins, we might live to righteousness; by whose bruise you were healed.

**2:25** For you were like straying sheep, yet now you have turned back to the shepherd and overseer of your souls.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — The Vulgate renders Greek "logikon" ("rational," "spiritual," or "of the word") as "rationabile" ("reasonable"); in 2:3, "dulcis" ("sweet") corresponds to Greek "chrestos" ("good/kind").
- **2:13** — The Vulgate reads "omni humanae creaturae" ("every human creature") and "propter Deum" ("for God's sake"), whereas the Greek has "every human institution/creation" and "for the Lord's sake".
- **2:20** — The Vulgate reads the adverb "patienter" ("patiently"), differing from the Greek participle "paschontes" ("suffering").
- **2:21** — The Vulgate shifts pronouns from first-person "pro nobis" ("for us") to second-person "vobis" ("leaving you").
- **2:23** — The Vulgate reading "judicanti se injuste" ("to the one judging Him unjustly") is a notable divergence from the Greek "to krinonti dikaios" ("to Him who judges justly").
