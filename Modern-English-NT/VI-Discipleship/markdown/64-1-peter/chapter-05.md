## Text

**5:1** Therefore I urge the elders who are among you, as a fellow elder and witness of the sufferings of Christ, and also a sharer in that glory which is to be revealed in the future:

**5:2** shepherd the flock of God that is among you, exercising oversight not under compulsion, but of your own accord according to God; not for the sake of shameful gain, but willingly;

**5:3** and not as domineering over the allotted portions, but becoming a model for the flock from the heart.

**5:4** And when the chief of shepherds appears, you will obtain the unfading crown of glory.

**5:5** Likewise, young men, be subject to the elders. And all of you, instill humility in one another, because God resists the proud, but to the humble He gives grace.

**5:6** Humble yourselves, therefore, under the powerful hand of God, so that He may exalt you in the time of visitation,

**5:7** casting all your worry upon Him, because He Himself cares about you.

**5:8** Be sober, and keep watch, because your adversary the devil goes around like a roaring lion, looking for someone to devour.

**5:9** Resist him, strong in faith, knowing that the same suffering is happening to your brotherhood that is in the world.

**5:10** Now the God of all grace, who called us into His eternal glory in Christ Jesus, will Himself perfect, confirm, and establish us after we have suffered a little.

**5:11** To Him be glory and sovereignty forever and ever. Amen.

**5:12** Through Silvanus, a faithful brother to you, as I judge, I have written briefly, urging and testifying that this is the true grace of God, in which you stand.

**5:13** The church that is in Babylon, chosen together with you, greets you, and so does Mark, my son.

**5:14** Greet one another with a holy kiss. Grace to all of you who are in Christ Jesus. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — Latin in cleris ("over the allotted portions") borrows Greek kleron ("allotments" or "heritages," the origin of English "clergy"); the closing phrase ex animo ("from the heart") is a Vulgate addition absent from the Greek text.
- **5:5** — Latin humilitatem insinuate ("instill humility," literally "bring into the bosom") translates Greek egkombosasthe ("clothe yourselves with").
- **5:6** — The Vulgate adds visitationis ("of visitation") to in tempore ("in the time"), whereas the standard Greek text reads simply "in due time."
- **5:10** — The Vulgate reads vocavit nos ("called us") rather than Greek "called you," and has three verbs (perficiet, confirmabit, solidabitque) where the critical Greek text has four.
- **5:14** — The Vulgate reads in osculo sancto ("with a holy kiss") instead of Greek "with a kiss of love," and Gratia ("Grace") instead of Greek "Peace."
