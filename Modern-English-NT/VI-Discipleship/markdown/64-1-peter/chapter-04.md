## Text

**4:1** Since Christ therefore suffered in the flesh, arm yourselves also with the same mindset, because the one who has suffered in the flesh has ceased from sins,

**4:2** so that he may live the remaining time in the flesh no longer for the desires of men, but for the will of God.

**4:3** For the time that has passed is sufficient to accomplish the will of the Gentiles, for those who have walked in debaucheries, desires, bouts of drunkenness, revelries, drinking parties, and unlawful worship of idols.

**4:4** At this they are astonished when you do not run along with them into the same confusion of debauchery, and they blaspheme.

**4:5** They will render an account to Him who is prepared to judge the living and the dead.

**4:6** For because of this the gospel has been proclaimed even to the dead, so that they may indeed be judged according to men in the flesh, yet live according to God in the spirit.

**4:7** Now the end of all things has drawn near. Be prudent, therefore, and keep watch in prayers.

**4:8** Now above all, maintain constant mutual love among yourselves, because love covers over a multitude of sins.

**4:9** Be hospitable toward one another without grumbling.

**4:10** Just as each one has received grace, administer it to one another as good stewards of the multifaceted grace of God.

**4:11** If anyone speaks, let it be as the utterances of God; if anyone serves, let it be as from the power that God supplies, so that in all things God may be honored through Jesus Christ, to whom belong glory and sovereignty forever and ever. Amen.

**4:12** Beloved, do not feel like strangers in the burning heat that comes upon you for testing, as though something new were happening to you,

**4:13** but sharing in the sufferings of Christ, rejoice, so that in the revelation of His glory also you may rejoice exultantly.

**4:14** If you are reproached in the name of Christ, you will be blessed, because what is of the honor, glory, and power of God, and He who is His Spirit, rests upon you.

**4:15** But let none of you suffer as a murderer, or a thief, or a slanderer, or one who covets what belongs to others.

**4:16** Yet if as a Christian, let him not be ashamed, but let him glorify God in this name,

**4:17** because it is time for judgment to begin with the house of God. Now if it begins first with us, what will be the end of those who do not believe the gospel of God?

**4:18** And if the righteous one will barely be saved, where will the ungodly and the sinner appear?

**4:19** Therefore let those also who suffer according to the will of God commit their souls to the faithful Creator in good deeds.

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — The Vulgate uses a third-person dative construction, his qui ambulaverunt ("for those who have walked"), whereas the Greek text uses a participle referring to the readers ("you").
- **4:4** — The Vulgate noun confusionem ("confusion") renders Greek anachysin ("outpouring" or "flood").
- **4:12** — Latin nolite peregrinari in fervore literally means "do not be strangers in the burning heat," calquing the Greek verb xenizesthe ("be surprised" or "think strange"); similarly, novi ("new") renders Greek xenou ("strange").
- **4:14** — The Vulgate expands the subject to quod est honoris, gloriae, et virtutis Dei, et qui est ejus Spiritus ("what is of the honor, glory, and power of God, and He who is His Spirit"), whereas the Greek reads simply "the Spirit of glory and of God."
- **4:15** — Latin maledicus ("slanderer") and alienorum appetitor ("one who covets what belongs to others") are distinctly Vulgate renderings of Greek kakopoios ("evildoer") and allotriepiskopos ("meddler" or "busybody").
