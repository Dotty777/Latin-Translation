## Text

**1:1** Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, to the chosen sojourners of the dispersion in Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia,

**1:2** according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, for the sanctification of the Spirit, for obedience and the sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ. Grace and peace be multiplied to you.

**1:3** Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who according to His great mercy regenerated us into a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead,

**1:4** into an inheritance incorruptible, undefiled, and unfading, preserved in the heavens in you,

**1:5** who are guarded in the power of God through faith for salvation, ready to be revealed in the last time.

**1:6** In this you will exult, if for a little while now it is necessary to be grieved in various trials,

**1:7** so that the testing of your faith, much more precious than gold (which is tested through fire), may be found for praise and glory and honor at the revelation of Jesus Christ,

**1:8** whom, though you have not seen Him, you love; in whom, not seeing Him even now, you believe; and believing, you will exult with inexpressible and glorified joy,

**1:9** obtaining the goal of your faith, the salvation of souls.

**1:10** Concerning this salvation, the prophets who prophesied about the grace to come in you inquired carefully and searched,

**1:11** searching into what or what kind of time the Spirit of Christ in them was indicating, foretelling those sufferings that are in Christ and the subsequent glories,

**1:12** to whom it was revealed that it was not to themselves, but to you, that they were ministering those things which have now been announced to you through those who proclaimed the gospel to you, with the Holy Spirit sent from heaven, upon whom angels desire to look.

**1:13** For this reason, with the loins of your mind girded, being sober, hope completely in the grace that is offered to you at the revelation of Jesus Christ,

**1:14** as children of obedience, not conformed to the former desires of your ignorance,

**1:15** but according to Him who called you, the Holy One, you yourselves also be holy in all conduct,

**1:16** because it is written, "You will be holy, because I am holy."

**1:17** And if you call upon Him as Father who judges without partiality according to each one's work, conduct yourselves in fear during the time of your sojourning,

**1:18** knowing that you were redeemed not with corruptible things, gold or silver, from your futile conduct of ancestral tradition,

**1:19** but with the precious blood of Christ, as of an unblemished and undefiled lamb,

**1:20** foreknown indeed before the foundation of the world, yet manifested in the last times for your sake,

**1:21** who through Him are believers in God, who raised Him from the dead and gave Him glory, so that your faith and hope might be in God.

**1:22** Purifying your souls in the obedience of love, in love of the brotherhood, love one another more earnestly from a sincere heart,

**1:23** having been born again not from corruptible seed, but incorruptible, through the word of the living God, who endures forever;

**1:24** for all flesh is like grass, and all its glory is like the flower of grass; the grass has withered, and its flower has fallen.

**1:25** But the word of the Lord remains forever. And this is the word that was proclaimed as the gospel to you.

## Translator Notes

- **1:4** — The Vulgate reads "in vobis" (ablative, "in you"; cf. 1:10), reflecting a Latin rendering of Greek "eis hymas" ("for you").
- **1:6** — The Vulgate "exsultabis" is second-person singular future ("you [sg.] will exult"), shifting briefly from the plural verbs around it (cf. "exsultabitis" in 1:8).
- **1:11** — The Vulgate reads "in Christo" ("in Christ"), differing from the Greek "eis Christon" ("for/destined for Christ").
- **1:12** — The Vulgate reads "in quem" (masculine singular, referring to the Holy Spirit), differing from the Greek neuter plural "eis ha" ("into which things").
- **1:22** — The Vulgate reads "in obedientia caritatis" ("in the obedience of love"), differing from the Greek "en te hypakoe tes aletheias" ("in obedience to the truth").
