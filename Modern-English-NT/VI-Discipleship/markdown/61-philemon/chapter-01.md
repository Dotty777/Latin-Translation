## Text

**1:1** Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus, and Timothy the brother, to Philemon, beloved and our helper,

**1:2** and to Apphia, a very dear sister, and to Archippus, our fellow soldier, and to the church that is in your house.

**1:3** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**1:4** I give thanks to my God, always making mention of you in my prayers,

**1:5** hearing of your love and the faith that you have in the Lord Jesus and toward all the saints,

**1:6** so that the sharing of your faith may become evident in the knowledge of every good work that is in you in Christ Jesus.

**1:7** For I have had great joy and comfort in your love, because the hearts of the saints have found rest through you, brother.

**1:8** For this reason, though I have great confidence in Christ Jesus to command you in what pertains to the matter,

**1:9** for the sake of love I appeal instead, since you are such a one, as Paul, an old man, and now also a prisoner of Jesus Christ:

**1:10** I appeal to you for my son, whom I fathered in chains, Onesimus,

**1:11** who was once useless to you, but now is useful both to me and to you,

**1:12** whom I have sent back to you. But you, welcome him as my own heart.

**1:13** I had wanted to keep him with me, so that he might serve me on your behalf in the chains of the gospel;

**1:14** but without your consent I did not want to do anything, so that your good deed might not be as if out of necessity, but voluntary.

**1:15** For perhaps he departed from you for a short while for this reason, so that you might receive him back forever,

**1:16** no longer as a slave, but instead of a slave, a very dear brother, especially to me; and how much more to you, both in the flesh and in the Lord?

**1:17** If therefore you consider me a partner, welcome him as you would me;

**1:18** but if he has harmed you in anything or owes anything, charge this to me.

**1:19** I, Paul, have written this with my own hand: I myself will repay it, not to say to you that you owe even your very self to me.

**1:20** Yes, brother. May I have joy from you in the Lord; refresh my heart in the Lord.

**1:21** Confident in your obedience, I have written to you, knowing that you will do even beyond what I say.

**1:22** At the same time, also prepare lodging for me; for I hope that through your prayers I will be granted to you.

**1:23** Epaphras, my fellow captive in Christ Jesus, greets you,

**1:24** and so do Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke, my helpers.

**1:25** The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **1:6** — The Vulgate reads 'may become evident' (evidens fiat, vs. Greek 'effective'), adds 'work' in 'every good work' (omnis operis boni), uses plural 'in you' (in vobis, vs. critical Greek 'in us'), and ends with 'in Christ Jesus' (vs. 'for Christ').
- **1:9** — The Clementine Vulgate reads second-person cum sis talis ('since you are such a one'), whereas the Greek participle refers to Paul himself ('being such a one').
- **1:12** — The Vulgate includes the imperative 'But you, welcome him' (matching the Byzantine Greek tradition) and renders Greek 'that is' with ut ('as'). Latin viscera (also in vv. 7 and 20) literally means 'inward parts,' used idiomatically for the heart as the seat of deepest affection.
- **1:16** — Latin pro servo literally means 'instead of a slave' (rendering Greek hyper doulon, 'more than a slave'). Earlier in v. 15, Vulgate discessit ('he departed') uses an active verb where the Greek has a passive ('was separated').
- **1:20** — At the close of the verse the Vulgate repeats in Domino ('in the Lord'), where the critical Greek text reads 'in Christ.' Latin te fruar ('May I have joy from you') reflects a verb of enjoyment or benefit, echoing the wordplay on the name Onesimus ('useful').
