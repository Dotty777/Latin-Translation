## Text

**3:1** See what sort of love the Father has given us, that we may be called and be children of God. Because of this, the world does not know us, for it does not know Him.

**3:2** Beloved, we are now children of God, and it has not yet appeared what we will be. We know that once He appears, we will be like Him, since we will see Him as He is.

**3:3** And everyone who has this hope in Him sanctifies himself, just as He also is holy.

**3:4** Everyone who commits sin also commits iniquity, and sin is iniquity.

**3:5** And you know that He appeared in order to take away our sins, and there is no sin in Him.

**3:6** Everyone who remains in Him does not sin; and everyone who sins has not seen Him, nor come to know Him.

**3:7** Little children, let no one deceive you. The one who does righteousness is righteous, just as He also is righteous.

**3:8** The one who commits sin is of the devil, because the devil sins from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God appeared: to undo the works of the devil.

**3:9** Everyone who has been born of God does not commit sin, because His seed remains in him; and he cannot sin, because he has been born of God.

**3:10** By this the children of God and the children of the devil are clearly revealed: everyone who is not righteous is not of God, nor is the one who does not love his brother.

**3:11** For this is the proclamation that you have heard from the beginning: that you should love one another.

**3:12** Not like Cain, who was of the evil one and killed his brother. And for what reason did he kill him? Because his works were evil, while his brother's were righteous.

**3:13** Do not be amazed, brothers, if the world hates you.

**3:14** We know that we have been transferred from death to life, because we love the brothers. The one who does not love remains in death.

**3:15** Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer. And you know that no murderer has eternal life remaining in himself.

**3:16** By this we have come to know the love of God, that He laid down His life for us; and we ought to lay down our lives for the brothers.

**3:17** Whoever has the goods of this world and sees his brother in need, and shuts his compassion against him, how does the love of God remain in him?

**3:18** My little children, let us not love in word or in tongue, but in deed and in truth.

**3:19** By this we know that we are of the truth, and in His sight we will persuade our hearts.

**3:20** For if our heart reproaches us, God is greater than our heart, and knows all things.

**3:21** Beloved, if our heart does not reproach us, we have confidence toward God.

**3:22** And whatever we ask, we will receive from Him, because we keep His commandments and do the things that are pleasing before Him.

**3:23** And this is His commandment: that we should believe in the name of His Son Jesus Christ and love one another, just as He gave us a commandment.

**3:24** And the one who keeps His commandments remains in Him, and He Himself in him; and by this we know that He remains in us, from the Spirit whom He has given to us.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The Vulgate uses the subjunctive simus ('and should be'), linking it to ut nominemur, whereas the Greek uses the indicative esmen ('and we are').
- **3:3** — The Vulgate reads sanctificat ... sanctus ('sanctifies ... holy'), where the Greek uses hagnizei ... hagnos ('purifies ... pure').
- **3:4** — The Vulgate renders Greek anomia ('lawlessness') as iniquitas ('iniquity'), matching iniquitate in 1:9.
- **3:10** — The Vulgate reads qui non est justus ('who is not righteous'), whereas the Greek reads 'who does not practice righteousness'; similarly, 3:11 uses second-person plural diligatis ('that you should love') rather than Greek first-person plural ('that we should love').
- **3:16** — The Vulgate explicitly reads caritatem Dei ('the love of God'), adding Dei where Greek manuscripts have simply 'love.'
