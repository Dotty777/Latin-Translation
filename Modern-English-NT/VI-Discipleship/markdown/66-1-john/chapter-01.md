## Text

**1:1** What was from the beginning, what we have heard, what we have seen with our eyes, what we have observed closely and our hands have touched, concerning the word of life;

**1:2** and the life was revealed, and we have seen, and we testify, and we proclaim to you eternal life, which was with the Father and appeared to us;

**1:3** what we have seen and heard, we proclaim to you, so that you also may have fellowship with us, and that our fellowship may be with the Father and with His Son Jesus Christ.

**1:4** And we are writing these things to you so that you may rejoice, and that your joy may be full.

**1:5** And this is the proclamation that we have heard from Him and proclaim to you: that God is light, and there is not any darkness in Him.

**1:6** If we say that we have fellowship with Him and walk in darkness, we are lying and do not do the truth.

**1:7** But if we walk in the light, just as He Himself also is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus Christ, His Son, cleanses us from all sin.

**1:8** If we say that we do not have sin, we are deceiving ourselves, and the truth is not in us.

**1:9** If we confess our sins, He is faithful and righteous, so that He will forgive us our sins and cleanse us from all iniquity.

**1:10** If we say that we have not sinned, we are making Him a liar, and His word is not in us.

## Translator Notes

- **1:3** — The Vulgate uses the subjunctive sit ('may be'), coordinating this clause with habeatis under ut, whereas the Greek has no verb here and is usually read as indicative ('is').
- **1:4** — The Vulgate includes the clause ut gaudeatis ('so that you may rejoice'), which the Greek text lacks, and reads gaudium vestrum ('your joy') with part of the Greek tradition, whereas other Greek manuscripts read 'our joy.'
- **1:7** — The Vulgate reads Jesu Christi ('Jesus Christ'), whereas many critical Greek editions omit 'Christ.'
