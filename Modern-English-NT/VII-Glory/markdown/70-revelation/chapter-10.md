## Text

**10:1** And I saw another strong angel descending from heaven, clothed in a cloud, and a rainbow was on his head, and his face was like the sun, and his feet were like pillars of fire.

**10:2** And he had in his hand a small open book, and he placed his right foot on the sea, and his left on the earth.

**10:3** And he cried out with a loud voice, just as when a lion roars. And when he had cried out, seven thunders spoke their voices.

**10:4** And when the seven thunders had spoken their voices, I was about to write; and I heard a voice from heaven saying to me, "Seal up the things that the seven thunders have spoken, and do not write them down."

**10:5** And the angel whom I saw standing on the sea and on the earth raised his hand to heaven.

**10:6** And he swore by Him who lives forever and ever, who created heaven and the things that are in it, and the earth and the things that are in it, and the sea and the things that are in it, that time will be no longer,

**10:7** but in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he begins to sound the trumpet, the mystery of God will be completed, just as He proclaimed the good news through His servants the prophets.

**10:8** And I heard the voice from heaven speaking with me again and saying, "Go and take the open book from the hand of the angel standing on the sea and on the earth."

**10:9** And I went to the angel, telling him to give me the book. And he said to me, "Take the book and devour it; and it will make your stomach bitter, but in your mouth it will be sweet like honey."

**10:10** And I took the book from the hand of the angel and devoured it, and in my mouth it was sweet like honey; and when I had devoured it, my stomach turned bitter.

**10:11** And he said to me, "You must prophesy again to nations and peoples and languages and many kings."

## Translator Notes

- **10:2** — The Clementine Vulgate uses the diminutive libellum ("small book") only in 10:2, whereas 10:8, 9, and 10 all use librum ("book").
- **10:5** — The Clementine Vulgate reads simply manum suam ("his hand"), omitting the word "right" found in Greek manuscripts.
- **10:6** — Literally "time will be no longer" (tempus non erit amplius); in context, the oath is commonly understood to mean that there will be no further delay before the seventh trumpet.
- **10:7** — The Vulgate reads per servos suos prophetas ("through His servants the prophets"), whereas the Greek has an accusative object ("announced the good news to his servants the prophets").
- **10:11** — The Clementine Vulgate has singular dixit ("he said") and orders gentibus, et populis ("nations and peoples"), whereas critical Greek texts read plural "they said" and place "peoples" before "nations."
