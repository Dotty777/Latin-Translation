## Text

**14:1** And I saw, and look: the Lamb was standing on Mount Zion, and with Him one hundred forty-four thousand, having His name and the name of His Father written on their foreheads.

**14:2** And I heard a sound from heaven, like the sound of many waters and like the sound of loud thunder, and the sound that I heard was like that of harpists playing on their harps.

**14:3** And they were singing, as it were, a new song before the throne and before the four living beings and the elders; and no one was able to recite the song except those one hundred forty-four thousand who were bought from the earth.

**14:4** These are the ones who have not been defiled with women, for they are virgins. These follow the Lamb wherever He goes. These were bought from among mankind as firstfruits to God and to the Lamb,

**14:5** and in their mouth no lie was found, for they are without blemish before the throne of God.

**14:6** And I saw another angel flying through the middle of heaven, having an eternal gospel to proclaim to those who sit on the earth, and to every nation and tribe and language and people,

**14:7** saying with a loud voice, "Fear the Lord and give Him honor, because the hour of His judgment has come, and worship Him who made heaven and earth, the sea, and the springs of waters."

**14:8** And another angel followed, saying, "Fallen, fallen is that great Babylon, who made all the nations drink from the wine of the wrath of her fornication."

**14:9** And a third angel followed them, saying with a loud voice, "If anyone worships the beast and its image, and receives a mark on his forehead or on his hand,

**14:10** he also will drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is mixed undiluted in the cup of His own wrath, and he will be tormented with fire and sulfur in the sight of the holy angels and before the sight of the Lamb;

**14:11** and the smoke of their torments will ascend forever and ever; and they have no rest day and night, those who worshiped the beast and its image, and if anyone receives the mark of its name."

**14:12** Here is the endurance of the saints, those who keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus.

**14:13** And I heard a voice from heaven saying to me, "Write: 'Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord.'" "From now on, indeed," says the Spirit, "that they may rest from their labors, for their works follow them."

**14:14** And I saw, and look: a white cloud, and seated on the cloud one like a Son of Man, having on His head a golden crown, and in His hand a sharp sickle.

**14:15** And another angel came out from the temple, crying out with a loud voice to Him who was seated on the cloud, "Send out Your sickle and reap, because the hour has come to reap, since the harvest of the earth has dried."

**14:16** And He who was seated on the cloud sent His sickle onto the earth, and the earth was reaped.

**14:17** And another angel came out from the temple that is in heaven, also having a sharp sickle himself.

**14:18** And another angel came out from the altar, who had authority over fire, and he cried out with a loud voice to the one who had the sharp sickle, saying, "Send out your sharp sickle and harvest the clusters of the vineyard of the earth, since its grapes are ripe."

**14:19** And the angel sent his sharp sickle onto the earth, and harvested the vineyard of the earth, and threw it into the great winepress of the wrath of God,

**14:20** and the winepress was trodden outside the city, and blood came out from the winepress up to the bridles of the horses for one thousand six hundred stadia.

## Translator Notes

- **14:3** — The Vulgate reads dicere canticum ('recite [or say] the song') where the Greek text has 'learn the song.'
- **14:5** — The Vulgate includes ante thronum Dei ('before the throne of God'), a phrase omitted in most modern critical Greek editions.
- **14:7** — The Vulgate distinctively reads Dominum ('the Lord') and honorem ('honor'), where the Greek text has 'God' and 'glory.'
- **14:10** — The Latin phrase mistum est mero (literally 'mixed with pure/undiluted wine') preserves the Greek oxymoron for wine poured out at full strength without water.
- **14:13** — The Clementine Vulgate places Amodo ('from now on') at the opening of the Spirit's response (Amodo jam dicit Spiritus, omitting 'Yes') rather than at the end of the beatitude as in most Greek editions.
