## Text

**16:1** And I heard a loud voice from the temple saying to the seven angels, "Go, and pour out the seven bowls of the wrath of God onto the earth."

**16:2** And the first departed and poured out his bowl onto the earth, and a fierce and malignant wound came upon the people who had the mark of the beast and upon those who worshiped its image.

**16:3** And the second angel poured out his bowl into the sea, and it became blood like that of a dead person, and every living soul died in the sea.

**16:4** And the third poured out his bowl upon the rivers and upon the springs of waters, and they became blood.

**16:5** And I heard the angel of the waters saying, "You are just, Lord, who are and who were holy, who have judged these things,

**16:6** because they poured out the blood of saints and prophets, and You have given them blood to drink; for they are deserving."

**16:7** And I heard another from the altar saying, "Yes, Lord God Almighty, true and just are Your judgments."

**16:8** And the fourth angel poured out his bowl upon the sun, and it was given to it to afflict people with heat and with fire;

**16:9** and people were scorched with great heat, and they blasphemed the name of God, who has authority over these plagues, nor did they repent so as to give Him glory.

**16:10** And the fifth angel poured out his bowl upon the seat of the beast, and its kingdom became dark, and they gnawed their tongues because of pain,

**16:11** and they blasphemed the God of heaven because of their pains and their wounds, and they did not repent from their deeds.

**16:12** And the sixth angel poured out his bowl into that great river, the Euphrates, and dried up its water, so that the way might be prepared for the kings from the rising of the sun.

**16:13** And I saw, out of the mouth of the dragon, and out of the mouth of the beast, and out of the mouth of the false prophet, three unclean spirits in the likeness of frogs.

**16:14** For they are spirits of demons performing signs, and they go out to the kings of the whole earth to gather them into battle for the great day of Almighty God.

**16:15** "Look, I am coming like a thief. Blessed is the one who keeps watch and guards his garments, so that he does not walk naked and they see his shame."

**16:16** And he will gather them into the place that is called in Hebrew Armageddon.

**16:17** And the seventh angel poured out his bowl into the air, and a loud voice came out from the temple, from the throne, saying, "It is done."

**16:18** And there were flashes of lightning, and voices, and peals of thunder, and there was a great earthquake, such as never was since people were on the earth, such an earthquake, so great.

**16:19** And the great city was turned into three parts, and the cities of the nations fell. And Babylon the great came into remembrance before God, to give her the cup of the wine of the indignation of His wrath.

**16:20** And every island fled, and mountains were not found.

**16:21** And great hail like a talent came down from heaven upon people, and people blasphemed God because of the plague of the hail, because it became extremely great.

## Translator Notes

- **16:5** — The Clementine Vulgate reads 'qui es, et qui eras sanctus' ('who are and who were holy', without an article corresponding to the Greek title 'the Holy One') and uses the relative pronoun 'qui' ('who have judged') rather than the Greek conjunction 'because.'
- **16:7** — The Vulgate reads 'alterum ab altari' ('another from the altar'), whereas Greek manuscripts present the altar itself as speaking.
- **16:8** — The Vulgate reads both 'æstu' ('heat') and 'et igni' ('and with fire'), expanding the Greek expression 'to scorch people with fire.'
- **16:12** — The Vulgate uses the active verb 'siccavit' ('dried up'), making the sixth angel the subject, whereas the Greek uses a passive verb ('its water was dried up').
- **16:16** — The Clementine Vulgate reads the future tense 'congregabit' ('he will gather'), contrasting with the past tense in the Greek text and some Latin manuscripts ('congregavit').
