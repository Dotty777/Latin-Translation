## Text

**8:1** And when He had opened the seventh seal, there came to be silence in heaven for about half an hour.

**8:2** And I saw seven angels standing in the sight of God, and seven trumpets were given to them.

**8:3** And another angel came and stood before the altar, having a golden censer; and much incense was given to him, so that he might offer from the prayers of all the saints on the golden altar that is before the throne of God.

**8:4** And the smoke of the incense from the prayers of the saints ascended from the hand of the angel before God.

**8:5** And the angel took the censer, and filled it from the fire of the altar, and threw it onto the earth; and there came thunders, and voices, and flashes of lightning, and a great earthquake.

**8:6** And the seven angels who had the seven trumpets prepared themselves to sound the trumpet.

**8:7** And the first angel sounded the trumpet, and there came hail and fire, mixed in blood, and it was thrown onto the earth; and a third of the earth was burned up, and a third of the trees were burned completely, and all green grass was burned up.

**8:8** And the second angel sounded the trumpet, and something like a great mountain burning with fire was thrown into the sea, and a third of the sea became blood,

**8:9** and a third of the creatures, those in the sea that had life, died, and a third of the ships perished.

**8:10** And the third angel sounded the trumpet, and a great star fell from heaven, burning like a torch, and it fell on a third of the rivers and on the springs of waters;

**8:11** and the name of the star is called Wormwood; and a third of the waters turned into wormwood, and many of the people died from the waters, because they had become bitter.

**8:12** And the fourth angel sounded the trumpet, and a third of the sun was struck, and a third of the moon, and a third of the stars, so that a third of them was darkened, and a third of the day did not shine, and likewise of the night.

**8:13** And I looked, and I heard the voice of one eagle flying through the middle of heaven, saying with a loud voice, "Woe, woe, woe to those who dwell on the earth, because of the remaining voices of the three angels who were about to sound the trumpet."

## Translator Notes

- **8:3** — Vulgate de orationibus ('from/of the prayers') in 8:3-4 uses a preposition of source/partitive sense rather than the Greek dative ('with/to the prayers'), and 8:3 uniquely adds Dei ('of God') after 'throne.'
- **8:5** — The Clementine Vulgate orders the phenomena as 'thunders, and voices, and flashes of lightning' and adds magnus ('a great earthquake'), where Greek manuscripts generally read simply 'an earthquake.'
- **8:9** — Latin creaturae eorum, quae habebant animas literally reads 'of the creation of those that had living souls/life,' with animas denoting vital life or living souls.
- **8:13** — Vulgate de ceteris vocibus trium angelorum reads 'because of the remaining voices of the three angels' (omitting 'of the trumpet' found in Greek) and retains the past-sequence imperfect erant tuba canituri ('were about to sound the trumpet') within the eagle's cry.
