## Text

**11:1** And a reed like a rod was given to me, and it was said to me, "Rise and measure the temple of God, and the altar, and those who worship in it.

**11:2** But cast out the court that is outside the temple, and do not measure it, because it has been given to the nations, and they will trample the holy city for forty-two months.

**11:3** And I will give to My two witnesses, and they will prophesy for one thousand two hundred sixty days, clothed in sackcloth."

**11:4** These are the two olive trees and the two lampstands standing in the presence of the Lord of the earth.

**11:5** And if anyone wants to harm them, fire will come out from their mouth and devour their enemies; and if anyone wants to injure them, in this way he must be killed.

**11:6** These have authority to shut the sky so that it may not rain in the days of their prophecy, and they have authority over the waters to turn them into blood and to strike the earth with every plague as often as they wish.

**11:7** And when they have finished their testimony, the beast that ascends from the abyss will wage war against them, and will conquer them, and will kill them.

**11:8** And their bodies will lie in the streets of the great city, which is spiritually called Sodom and Egypt, where their Lord also was crucified.

**11:9** And some from the tribes and peoples and languages and nations will look at their bodies for three and a half days, and they will not allow their bodies to be placed in tombs.

**11:10** And the inhabitants of the earth will rejoice over them and celebrate, and they will send gifts to one another, because these two prophets tormented those who lived on the earth.

**11:11** And after three and a half days, the spirit of life from God entered into them. And they stood on their feet, and great fear fell upon those who saw them.

**11:12** And they heard a loud voice from heaven saying to them, "Come up here." And they ascended into heaven in a cloud, and their enemies saw them.

**11:13** And in that hour a great earthquake took place, and a tenth part of the city fell, and seven thousand persons were killed in the earthquake; and the rest were thrown into fear and gave glory to the God of heaven.

**11:14** The second woe has passed; and look, the third woe will come soon.

**11:15** And the seventh angel sounded the trumpet, and loud voices arose in heaven, saying, "The kingdom of this world has become that of our Lord and of His Christ, and He will reign forever and ever. Amen."

**11:16** And the twenty-four elders, who sit on their seats in the presence of God, fell on their faces and worshiped God, saying,

**11:17** "We give thanks to You, Lord God Almighty, who are, and who were, and who are to come, because You have taken Your great power and reigned.

**11:18** And the nations grew angry, and Your wrath has come, and the time for the dead to be judged, and to give the reward to Your servants the prophets, and to the saints, and to those who fear Your name, small and great, and to destroy those who have corrupted the earth."

**11:19** And the temple of God in heaven was opened, and the ark of His covenant was seen in His temple; and there came flashes of lightning, and voices, and an earthquake, and great hail.

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — Latin dabo duobus testibus meis ("I will give to My two witnesses") preserves the elliptic biblical construction without an explicit direct object, denoting God's granting of a prophetic commission.
- **11:8** — The Clementine Vulgate uses plural corpora ("bodies") and plateis ("streets") and adds the verb jacebunt ("will lie"), whereas critical Greek texts read singular "corpse" and "street" without a verb; likewise 11:9 has plural corpora and monumentis ("tombs") and places tribubus ("tribes") before populis ("peoples").
- **11:13** — Literally nomina hominum septem millia ("seven thousand names of people/men"), a biblical idiom referring to counted individuals.
- **11:15** — The Clementine Vulgate distinctly specifies hujus mundi ("of this world") and closes the acclamation with Amen, both absent from standard Greek editions.
- **11:19** — The Clementine Vulgate lists four phenomena (flashes of lightning, voices, an earthquake, and great hail), omitting the "thunders" present in Greek manuscripts.
