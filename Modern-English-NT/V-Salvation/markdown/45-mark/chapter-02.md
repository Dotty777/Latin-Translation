## Text

**2:1** And He entered Capernaum again after some days,

**2:2** and it was heard that He was in the house, and many gathered together, so that there was no room, not even at the door, and He was speaking the word to them.

**2:3** And they came to Him, bringing a paralyzed man who was being carried by four.

**2:4** And when they could not present him to Him because of the crowd, they uncovered the roof where He was; and making an opening, they lowered the cot on which the paralyzed man was lying.

**2:5** Now when Jesus saw their faith, He said to the paralyzed man, "Son, your sins are forgiven you."

**2:6** Now some of the scribes were sitting there, thinking in their hearts:

**2:7** "Why does this man speak like this? He is blaspheming. Who can forgive sins except God alone?"

**2:8** Immediately knowing in His spirit that they were thinking this way within themselves, Jesus said to them, "Why are you thinking these things in your hearts?

**2:9** Which is easier, to say to the paralyzed man, 'Your sins are forgiven,' or to say, 'Get up, take your cot, and walk'?

**2:10** But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins" (He said to the paralyzed man),

**2:11** "I tell you, get up, take your cot, and go to your house."

**2:12** And immediately he got up, and taking up the cot, he went away in front of everyone, so that all were amazed and honored God, saying, "We have never seen anything like this."

**2:13** And He went out again to the sea, and all the crowd was coming to Him, and He was teaching them.

**2:14** And as He was passing by, He saw Levi the son of Alphaeus sitting at the tax booth, and He said to him, "Follow Me." And getting up, he followed Him.

**2:15** And it happened, as He was reclining at table in his house, that many tax collectors and sinners were reclining together with Jesus and His disciples; for there were many who were also following Him.

**2:16** And the scribes and the Pharisees, seeing that He was eating with tax collectors and sinners, were saying to His disciples, "Why does your Teacher eat and drink with tax collectors and sinners?"

**2:17** Hearing this, Jesus said to them, "The healthy do not need a physician, but those who are unwell; for I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners."

**2:18** And the disciples of John and the Pharisees were fasting; and they came and said to Him, "Why do the disciples of John and of the Pharisees fast, but Your disciples do not fast?"

**2:19** And Jesus said to them, "Can the sons of the wedding fast as long as the bridegroom is with them? For as long as they have the bridegroom with them, they cannot fast.

**2:20** But the days will come when the bridegroom will be taken away from them, and then they will fast in those days.

**2:21** No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth onto an old garment; otherwise the new piece pulls away from the old, and a greater tear results.

**2:22** And no one puts new wine into old wineskins; otherwise the wine will burst the wineskins, and the wine will be poured out, and the wineskins will be ruined; but new wine must be put into new wineskins."

**2:23** And it happened again that the Lord was walking through the grainfields on the Sabbath, and His disciples began to go forward and pluck heads of grain.

**2:24** Now the Pharisees were saying to Him, "Look, why are they doing on the Sabbath what is not lawful?"

**2:25** And He said to them, "Have you never read what David did when he had need and was hungry, he himself and those who were with him,

**2:26** how he entered the house of God under Abiathar the high priest, and ate the loaves of presentation, which it was not lawful to eat except for the priests, and gave them to those who were with him?"

**2:27** And He was saying to them, "The Sabbath was made for the sake of man, and not man for the sake of the Sabbath.

**2:28** Therefore the Son of Man is Lord even of the Sabbath."

## Translator Notes

- **2:16** — The Clementine Vulgate reads 'scribæ et pharisæi' ("the scribes and the Pharisees") rather than "the scribes of the Pharisees" found in many Greek manuscripts, and also includes "and drinks" and "your Teacher."
- **2:19** — Literally 'filii nuptiarum' ("sons of the wedding"), a Semitic idiom referring to the wedding guests or attendants of the bridegroom.
- **2:20** — The Vulgate reads plural 'in illis diebus' ("in those days"), whereas most critical Greek texts read the singular "on that day."
- **2:23** — The Clementine Vulgate distinctively reads 'iterum Dominus' ("again ... the Lord"), adding "again" and naming the Lord explicitly where the Greek text uses only a pronoun.
- **2:26** — The reference to Abiathar as high priest is a well-known historical crux, since 1 Samuel 21 names his father Ahimelech during David's flight; 'panes propositionis' literally denotes the "loaves of presentation" set before God.
