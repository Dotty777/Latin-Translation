## Text

**6:1** And departing from there, He went to His native place, and His disciples were following Him.

**6:2** And when the Sabbath came, He began to teach in the synagogue; and many who heard were astonished at His teaching, saying, "Where does this man get all these things? And what is the wisdom that has been given to Him, and such deeds of power that are performed through His hands?

**6:3** Is this not the craftsman, the son of Mary, the brother of James, and Joseph, and Judas, and Simon? Are not His sisters also here with us?" And they were taking offense at Him.

**6:4** And Jesus said to them, "A prophet is not without honor except in his native place, and in his house, and among his relatives."

**6:5** And He could not perform any deed of power there, except that He healed a few sick people by laying His hands on them.

**6:6** And He marveled because of their unbelief, and He was going around the surrounding villages, teaching.

**6:7** And He called the Twelve, and began to send them out two by two, and He was giving them authority over unclean spirits.

**6:8** And He commanded them to take nothing on the way except a staff only: no bag, no bread, and no copper money in their belt,

**6:9** but to wear sandals, and not to put on two tunics.

**6:10** And He said to them, "Wherever you enter a house, remain there until you depart from there.

**6:11** And whoever does not receive you or listen to you, as you go out from there, shake off the dust from your feet as a testimony to them."

**6:12** And going out, they preached that people should repent.

**6:13** And they were casting out many demons, and anointing many sick people with oil, and healing them.

**6:14** And King Herod heard (for His name had become well known), and he was saying, "John the Baptist has risen from the dead, and for this reason powers are at work in him."

**6:15** Others, however, were saying, "He is Elijah"; while others were saying, "He is a prophet, like one of the prophets."

**6:16** When he heard this, Herod said, "John, whom I beheaded, he has risen from the dead."

**6:17** For Herod himself had sent and seized John, and bound him in prison on account of Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip, because he had married her.

**6:18** For John had been saying to Herod, "It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife."

**6:19** Now Herodias was plotting against him and wanted to kill him, and could not.

**6:20** For Herod feared John, knowing him to be a righteous and holy man, and he kept him safe; and when he heard him, he did many things, and listened to him gladly.

**6:21** And when an opportune day arrived, Herod gave a birthday banquet for the nobles, and the tribunes, and the leading men of Galilee.

**6:22** And when the daughter of Herodias herself had come in and danced and pleased Herod and those reclining with him, the king said to the girl, "Ask me for what you wish, and I will give it to you."

**6:23** And he swore to her, "Whatever you ask, I will give to you, even half of my kingdom."

**6:24** And when she had gone out, she said to her mother, "What should I ask for?" And she said, "The head of John the Baptist."

**6:25** And when she had come in immediately with haste to the king, she made her request, saying, "I want you to give me at once on a platter the head of John the Baptist."

**6:26** And the king was grieved; because of the oath and because of those reclining with him, he did not want to grieve her,

**6:27** but having sent an executioner, he commanded his head to be brought on a platter. And he beheaded him in the prison,

**6:28** and brought his head on a platter, and gave it to the girl, and the girl gave it to her mother.

**6:29** When they heard this, his disciples came and took his body, and placed it in a tomb.

**6:30** And gathering together to Jesus, the apostles reported to Him everything that they had done and had taught.

**6:31** And He said to them, "Come away by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a little." For there were many who were coming and going, and they did not even have time to eat.

**6:32** And boarding a boat, they went away to a deserted place by themselves.

**6:33** And people saw them departing, and many recognized them; and on foot from all the cities they ran together there and arrived ahead of them.

**6:34** And coming out, Jesus saw a large crowd, and He had compassion on them, because they were like sheep not having a shepherd; and He began to teach many things.

**6:35** And when the hour was already late, His disciples approached, saying, "This place is deserted, and the hour has already passed;

**6:36** send them away, so that going into the nearest hamlets and villages, they may buy themselves food to eat."

**6:37** And answering, He said to them, "You give them something to eat." And they said to Him, "Should we go and buy loaves for two hundred denarii and give them something to eat?"

**6:38** And He said to them, "How many loaves do you have? Go and see." And when they had found out, they said, "Five, and two fish."

**6:39** And He commanded them to have everyone recline in companies on the green grass.

**6:40** And they reclined in groups, by hundreds and by fifties.

**6:41** And having taken the five loaves and the two fish, looking up to heaven, He blessed and broke the loaves, and gave them to His disciples to set before them; and He divided the two fish among them all.

**6:42** And they all ate and were satisfied.

**6:43** And they picked up the leftovers, twelve baskets full of fragments, and of the fish.

**6:44** Now those who ate were five thousand men.

**6:45** And immediately He compelled His disciples to board the boat, so that they would go ahead of Him across the water to Bethsaida, while He Himself dismissed the people.

**6:46** And when He had dismissed them, He went away to the mountain to pray.

**6:47** And when evening came, the boat was in the middle of the sea, and He Himself was alone on the land.

**6:48** And He saw them straining at rowing (for the wind was against them), and around the fourth watch of the night He came to them, walking on the sea; and He wanted to pass by them.

**6:49** But when they saw Him walking on the sea, they thought it was a ghost, and they cried out.

**6:50** For they all saw Him and were deeply troubled. And immediately He spoke with them and said to them, "Have courage, it is I; do not be afraid."

**6:51** And He climbed into the boat with them, and the wind ceased. And they were all the more astonished within themselves.

**6:52** For they had not understood about the loaves, for their heart was blinded.

**6:53** And when they had crossed over, they came to the land of Gennesaret and landed.

**6:54** And when they had come out of the boat, people immediately recognized Him,

**6:55** and running through that entire region, they began to carry around on mats those who were ill, wherever they heard He was.

**6:56** And wherever He entered, into villages, or into hamlets, or cities, they would place the sick in the public squares and beg Him that they might touch even the fringe of His garment; and as many as touched Him were made well.

## Translator Notes

- **6:3** — Latin faber denotes a craftsman or artisan (traditionally rendered "carpenter"). The Vulgate reads Joseph (matching certain ancient manuscripts), whereas many Greek texts read "Joses."
- **6:4** — The Vulgate places "in his house" (in domo sua) before "among his relatives" (in cognatione sua), reversing the order found in most Greek manuscripts.
- **6:20** — Following the majority Greek tradition (polla epoiei), the Vulgate reads multa faciebat ("he did many things"), whereas critical Greek editions read polla eporei ("he was greatly perplexed").
- **6:26** — The Vulgate reads singular "the oath" (jusjurandum) and echoes contristatus est ("was grieved") with contristare ("to grieve her"), where the Greek has plural "oaths" and athetesai ("to refuse her").
- **6:52** — The Vulgate reads obcaecatum ("blinded"), whereas the Greek reads peporomene ("hardened").
