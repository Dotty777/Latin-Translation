## Text

**5:1** And they came across the strait of the sea into the region of the Gerasenes.

**5:2** And as He came out of the boat, immediately a man with an unclean spirit met Him out of the tombs,

**5:3** who had his dwelling in the tombs, and not even with chains could anyone bind him any longer;

**5:4** because, having often been bound with shackles and chains, he had torn the chains apart and shattered the shackles, and no one was able to tame him;

**5:5** and always, day and night, he was in the tombs and in the mountains, crying out and cutting himself with stones.

**5:6** Now seeing Jesus from a distance, he ran and worshiped Him.

**5:7** And crying out with a loud voice, he said, "What is there between me and You, Jesus, Son of God Most High? I adjure You by God not to torture me."

**5:8** For He was saying to him, "Come out, unclean spirit, from the man."

**5:9** And He asked him, "What is your name?" And he said to Him, "Legion is my name, because we are many."

**5:10** And he pleaded with Him earnestly not to drive him outside the region.

**5:11** Now a large herd of pigs was feeding there near the mountain.

**5:12** And the spirits pleaded with Him, saying, "Send us into the pigs, so that we may enter into them."

**5:13** And Jesus immediately granted it to them. And coming out, the unclean spirits entered into the pigs; and with a great rush the herd plunged headlong into the sea, about two thousand, and they were drowned in the sea.

**5:14** Now those who were tending them fled and reported it in the city and in the fields. And they went out to see what had happened.

**5:15** And they came to Jesus, and saw the one who had been tormented by the demon sitting, clothed, and of sound mind; and they were afraid.

**5:16** And those who had seen it recounted to them how it had happened to the one who had had the demon, and about the pigs.

**5:17** And they began to ask Him to depart from their territory.

**5:18** And as He was getting into the boat, the one who had been tormented by the demon began to plead with Him that he might be with Him.

**5:19** And He did not permit him, but said to him, "Go to your house, to your own people, and announce to them how much the Lord has done for you, and how He has had mercy on you."

**5:20** And he went away and began to proclaim in the Decapolis how much Jesus had done for him; and all were amazed.

**5:21** And when Jesus had crossed over again in the boat across the strait, a large crowd gathered to Him, and He was near the sea.

**5:22** And one of the synagogue leaders, named Jairus, came, and seeing Him, he fell down at His feet.

**5:23** And he pleaded with Him earnestly, saying, "My daughter is at the point of death; come, lay Your hand on her, so that she may be saved and live."

**5:24** And He went with him, and a large crowd was following Him, and they were pressing against Him.

**5:25** And a woman who had had a flow of blood for twelve years,

**5:26** and had endured many things from numerous physicians, and had spent all that was hers, and had gained nothing, but rather was getting worse,

**5:27** having heard about Jesus, came from behind in the crowd and touched His garment.

**5:28** For she was saying, "If I touch even His garment, I will be saved."

**5:29** And at once the source of her blood dried up, and she felt in her body that she had been healed from the affliction.

**5:30** And immediately Jesus, perceiving in Himself the power that had gone out from Him, turned to the crowd and said, "Who touched My garments?"

**5:31** And His disciples said to Him, "You see the crowd pressing against You, and You say, 'Who touched Me?'"

**5:32** And He was looking around to see her who had done this.

**5:33** But the woman, fearing and trembling, knowing what had happened in her, came and fell down before Him, and told Him the whole truth.

**5:34** But He said to her, "Daughter, your faith has saved you; go in peace, and be healed from your affliction."

**5:35** While He was still speaking, some came from the synagogue leader's house, saying, "Your daughter is dead; why do you trouble the Teacher any further?"

**5:36** But Jesus, having heard the word that was being spoken, said to the synagogue leader, "Do not be afraid; only believe."

**5:37** And He did not permit anyone to follow Him except Peter, and James, and John the brother of James.

**5:38** And they came to the house of the synagogue leader, and He saw a commotion, and people weeping and wailing loudly.

**5:39** And entering, He said to them, "Why are you in turmoil and weeping? The girl is not dead, but is sleeping."

**5:40** And they were laughing at Him. But He, having put everyone out, took the father and the mother of the girl, and those who were with Him, and entered where the girl was lying.

**5:41** And holding the girl's hand, He said to her, "Talitha cumi," which is translated, "Girl, I say to you, rise."

**5:42** And at once the girl rose and began walking; now she was twelve years old. And they were stunned with great amazement.

**5:43** And He strictly commanded them that no one should know this, and He said that something should be given to her to eat.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — The Clementine Vulgate renders this phrase as "across the strait of the sea" (trans fretum maris; likewise "across the strait" in 5:21), where the Greek reads simply "to the other side of the sea."
- **5:13** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "with a great rush" (magno impetu) without the Greek reference to a "steep bank," and explicitly includes "immediately Jesus" (statim Jesus) in the opening clause.
- **5:15** — The Clementine Vulgate describes him simply as "the one who had been tormented by the demon" (illum qui a daemonio vexabatur), omitting the Greek appositive phrase "the one who had had the legion."
- **5:34** — Latin salvus/salva (rendered consistently as "saved" in 5:23, 5:28, and 5:34) spans both physical deliverance/restoration and spiritual salvation, contrasted here with sana ("healed" / "sound").
- **5:36** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "having heard the word" (audito verbo), following the Greek variant akousas rather than the critical Greek reading parakousas ("overhearing" or "ignoring").
