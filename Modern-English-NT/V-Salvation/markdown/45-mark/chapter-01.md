## Text

**1:1** The beginning of the Gospel of Jesus Christ, Son of God.

**1:2** Just as it is written in the prophet Isaiah: "Look, I am sending My messenger before Your face, who will prepare Your way before You.

**1:3** A voice of one calling out in the desert: 'Prepare the way of the Lord; make straight His paths.'"

**1:4** John was in the desert baptizing and preaching a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins.

**1:5** And the whole region of Judea and all the inhabitants of Jerusalem were going out to him, and they were being baptized by him in the river Jordan, confessing their sins.

**1:6** And John was dressed in camel hair, with a leather belt around his waist, and he ate locusts and wild honey.

**1:7** And he was preaching, saying, "One stronger than I is coming after me; I am not worthy to stoop down and untie the strap of His sandals.

**1:8** I have baptized you with water, yet He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit."

**1:9** And it happened in those days that Jesus came from Nazareth of Galilee, and He was baptized by John in the Jordan.

**1:10** And immediately, coming up out of the water, He saw the heavens opened, and the Spirit descending like a dove and remaining in Him.

**1:11** And a voice came from the heavens: "You are My beloved Son; in You I am greatly pleased."

**1:12** And immediately the Spirit drove Him into the desert.

**1:13** And He was in the desert for forty days and forty nights, and was being tempted by Satan; and He was with wild animals, and angels were serving Him.

**1:14** Now after John was handed over, Jesus came into Galilee, preaching the Gospel of the kingdom of God,

**1:15** and saying, "The time has been fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has drawn near; repent and believe the Gospel."

**1:16** And passing along beside the Sea of Galilee, He saw Simon and his brother Andrew casting nets into the sea (for they were fishermen),

**1:17** and Jesus said to them, "Come after Me, and I will make you become fishers of men."

**1:18** And at once, leaving the nets behind, they followed Him.

**1:19** And moving on a little way from there, He saw James son of Zebedee and his brother John, who were likewise mending nets in the boat;

**1:20** and immediately He called them. And leaving their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired workers, they followed Him.

**1:21** And they entered Capernaum; and immediately on the Sabbath, having entered the synagogue, He was teaching them.

**1:22** And they were astounded at His teaching, for He was teaching them as one possessing authority, and not like the scribes.

**1:23** And in their synagogue there was a man with an unclean spirit, and he cried out,

**1:24** saying, "What is there between us and You, Jesus the Nazarene? Have You come to destroy us? I know who You are, the Holy One of God."

**1:25** And Jesus sternly rebuked it, saying, "Be silent, and come out of the man."

**1:26** And convulsing him and crying out in a loud voice, the unclean spirit went out from him.

**1:27** And everyone was amazed, so that they questioned one another, saying, "What is this? What new teaching is this? For with authority He commands even the unclean spirits, and they obey Him."

**1:28** And immediately the report about Him went out into the whole region of Galilee.

**1:29** And at once, going out from the synagogue, they came into the house of Simon and Andrew with James and John.

**1:30** Now Simon's mother-in-law was lying down with a fever, and immediately they spoke to Him about her.

**1:31** And approaching, He raised her up, taking hold of her hand; and instantly the fever left her, and she was serving them.

**1:32** Now when evening came, after the sun had set, they were bringing to Him all who were unwell and those who had demons;

**1:33** and the entire city was assembled at the door.

**1:34** And He healed many who were afflicted with various illnesses, and He was casting out many demons; and He would not allow them to speak, because they knew Him.

**1:35** And rising very early at dawn, He went out and departed to a deserted place, and there He was praying.

**1:36** And Simon and those who were with him went after Him.

**1:37** And when they had found Him, they said to Him, "Everyone is looking for You."

**1:38** And He said to them, "Let us go into the nearby villages and cities, so that I may preach there as well; for I came for this purpose."

**1:39** And He was preaching in their synagogues and in all of Galilee, and casting out demons.

**1:40** And a leper came to Him, pleading with Him; and kneeling down, he said to Him, "If You are willing, You are able to cleanse me."

**1:41** Now Jesus, having compassion on him, reached out His hand, and touching him, He said to him, "I am willing. Be cleansed."

**1:42** And when He had spoken, immediately the leprosy departed from him, and he was cleansed.

**1:43** And He sternly warned him and immediately sent him out,

**1:44** and said to him, "See that you tell no one; but go, show yourself to the high priest, and offer for your cleansing the things that Moses commanded, as a testimony to them."

**1:45** Yet having gone out, he began to proclaim and spread the word widely, so that He could no longer openly enter a city, but stayed outside in deserted places; and people were gathering to Him from everywhere.

## Translator Notes

- **1:10** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "cælos apertos" ("the heavens opened") rather than "torn open," and includes "et manentem in ipso" ("and remaining in Him," or "on Him"), an addition echoing John 1:32 absent from standard Greek manuscripts of Mark 1:10.
- **1:13** — The Vulgate includes "et quadraginta noctibus" ("and forty nights"), paralleling Matthew 4:2, whereas most Greek manuscripts of Mark 1:13 mention only forty days.
- **1:27** — Following the Vulgate wording and clause division ("quænam doctrina hæc nova? quia in potestate..."), "with authority" modifies "He commands" rather than "new teaching."
- **1:38** — The Vulgate splits the Greek compound for "market-towns" into two nouns, "vicos, et civitates" ("villages and cities"), and reads "veni" ("I came") rather than "I came out."
- **1:44** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "principi sacerdotum" ("to the high priest"), whereas Greek manuscripts read simply "to the priest."
