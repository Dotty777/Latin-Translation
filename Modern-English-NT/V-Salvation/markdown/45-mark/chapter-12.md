## Text

**12:1** And He began to speak to them in parables: "A man planted a vineyard, and put a hedge around it, and dug a vat, and built a tower, and leased it to farmers, and went abroad.

**12:2** And in season he sent a servant to the farmers, so that he might receive from the farmers some of the fruit of the vineyard.

**12:3** They seized him, beat him, and sent him away empty-handed.

**12:4** And again he sent another servant to them; and they wounded him in the head and treated him with insults.

**12:5** And once more he sent another, and they killed him; and many others: some they beat, while others they killed.

**12:6** Therefore, still having one dearest son, he sent him also to them last, saying, 'They will respect my son.'

**12:7** But the tenants said to one another, 'This is the heir; come, let us kill him, and ours will be the inheritance.'

**12:8** And seizing him, they killed him and threw him outside the vineyard.

**12:9** What then will the master of the vineyard do? He will come and destroy the tenants, and give the vineyard to others.

**12:10** Have you not even read this Scripture: 'The stone that the builders rejected, this has become the head of the corner;

**12:11** by the Lord this has been done, and it is marvelous in our eyes'?"

**12:12** And they were seeking to lay hold of Him, and they feared the crowd; for they realized that He had spoken this parable against them. And leaving Him, they went away.

**12:13** And they sent to Him some of the Pharisees and the Herodians, so that they might catch Him in speech.

**12:14** And coming, they said to Him, "Teacher, we know that You are truthful and are not concerned about anyone; for You do not look at the face of men, but in truth You teach the way of God. Is it lawful for tribute to be given to Caesar, or shall we not give it?"

**12:15** But He, knowing their craftiness, said to them, "Why do you test Me? Bring Me a denarius so that I may see it."

**12:16** And they brought one to Him. And He said to them, "Whose is this image and inscription?" They said to Him, "Caesar's."

**12:17** And answering, Jesus said to them, "Give back, therefore, the things that are Caesar's to Caesar, and the things that are God's to God." And they were marveling at Him.

**12:18** And Sadducees came to Him, who say that there is no resurrection, and they were questioning Him, saying,

**12:19** "Teacher, Moses wrote for us that if anyone's brother dies and leaves behind a wife, and leaves no children, his brother should take his wife and raise up offspring for his brother.

**12:20** There were, then, seven brothers; and the first took a wife, and died leaving no offspring.

**12:21** And the second took her and died, and neither did he leave offspring. And the third likewise.

**12:22** And the seven took her likewise, and left no offspring. Last of all, the woman also died.

**12:23** In the resurrection, therefore, when they rise, whose wife among these will she be? For the seven had her as wife."

**12:24** And answering, Jesus said to them, "Are you not in error for this reason, not knowing the Scriptures nor the power of God?

**12:25** For when they rise from the dead, they will neither marry nor be married, but are like angels in heaven.

**12:26** But concerning the dead, that they rise, have you not read in the book of Moses, at the bush, how God spoke to him, saying, 'I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob'?

**12:27** He is not the God of the dead, but of the living. You, therefore, are greatly in error."

**12:28** And one of the scribes approached, who had heard them disputing, and seeing that He had answered them well, he asked Him which was the first commandment of all.

**12:29** And Jesus answered him, "The first commandment of all is: 'Hear, Israel: the Lord your God is one God,

**12:30** and you shall love the Lord your God with your entire heart, and with your entire soul, and with your entire mind, and with your entire strength.' This is the first commandment.

**12:31** And the second is like it: 'You shall love your neighbor just as yourself.' No other commandment is greater than these."

**12:32** And the scribe said to Him, "Rightly, Teacher, You have spoken in truth, that God is one, and there is no other besides Him.

**12:33** And for Him to be loved with the entire heart, and with the entire understanding, and with the entire soul, and with the entire strength, and to love one's neighbor just as oneself, is greater than all burnt offerings and sacrifices."

**12:34** And Jesus, seeing that he had answered wisely, said to him, "You are not far from the kingdom of God." And no one dared to question Him any longer.

**12:35** And answering, Jesus was saying while teaching in the temple, "How do the scribes say that the Christ is the son of David?

**12:36** For David himself says in the Holy Spirit: 'The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at My right hand, until I make Your enemies a footstool for Your feet.'

**12:37** David himself, therefore, calls Him Lord, and how is He his son?" And a large crowd listened to Him gladly.

**12:38** And He was saying to them in His teaching, "Beware of the scribes, who desire to walk in long robes and to be greeted in the marketplace,

**12:39** and to sit in the first seats in the synagogues, and the first places at banquets;

**12:40** who devour the houses of widows under the pretext of lengthy prayer: these will receive a heavier judgment."

**12:41** And Jesus, sitting opposite the treasury, was watching how the crowd was dropping money into the treasury, and many rich people were dropping in much.

**12:42** And when one poor widow came, she put in two small coins, which is a quadrans.

**12:43** And calling His disciples together, He said to them, "Amen, I tell you that this poor widow has put in more than all who put into the treasury.

**12:44** For all of them put in out of what was abundant to them; she, however, out of her poverty put in everything that she had, her entire livelihood."

## Translator Notes

- **12:14** — The Clementine Vulgate compresses the closing question into a single clause ("or shall we not give it?"), whereas Greek manuscripts read "or not? Shall we give, or shall we not give?"
- **12:15** — Latin "versutiam" ("craftiness") is a distinctly Vulgate lexical choice here, where Greek manuscripts read "hypocrisy."
- **12:29** — The Vulgate reads "the Lord your God is one God" ("Dominus Deus tuus, Deus unus est"), echoing Deuteronomy 6:4, whereas Greek manuscripts read "the Lord our God, the Lord is one."
- **12:33** — The Vulgate lists all four faculties, including "and with the entire soul" ("et ex tota anima"), where critical Greek editions omit "soul," and moves from passive ("ut diligatur," "for Him to be loved") to active ("diligere," "to love").
- **12:42** — Latin "minuta" refers to tiny copper coins (Greek "lepta"), and a "quadrans" was the smallest Roman bronze coin, valued at one quarter of an as.
