## Text

**7:1** And the Pharisees and some of the scribes, coming from Jerusalem, gathered to Him.

**7:2** And when they saw some of His disciples eating bread with common hands, that is, unwashed, they found fault.

**7:3** For the Pharisees and all the Jews do not eat unless they wash their hands frequently, holding to the tradition of the elders;

**7:4** and coming from the marketplace, they do not eat unless they wash themselves; and there are many other things that have been handed down to them to keep: washings of cups, and pitchers, and bronze vessels, and couches.

**7:5** And the Pharisees and the scribes asked Him, "Why do Your disciples not walk according to the tradition of the elders, but eat bread with common hands?"

**7:6** But answering, He said to them, "Isaiah prophesied well about you hypocrites, as it is written: 'This people honors Me with their lips, but their heart is far from Me;

**7:7** and in vain they worship Me, teaching doctrines and precepts of men.'

**7:8** For abandoning the commandment of God, you hold to the tradition of men, washings of pitchers and cups; and you do many other things like these."

**7:9** And He was saying to them, "How well you nullify the precept of God, so that you may keep your tradition!

**7:10** For Moses said, 'Honor your father and your mother,' and, 'Whoever curses father or mother, let him surely die.'

**7:11** But you say, 'If a man says to father or mother, Corban (which is a gift) is whatever from me would benefit you,'

**7:12** and you no longer allow him to do anything for his father or mother,

**7:13** invalidating the word of God through your tradition that you have handed down; and you do many similar things of this kind."

**7:14** And calling the crowd again, He was saying to them, "Listen to Me, all of you, and understand.

**7:15** There is nothing outside a man, entering into him, that can pollute him; but the things that proceed from a man, those are what defile a man."

**7:16** If anyone has ears for hearing, let him hear.

**7:17** And when He had entered the house away from the crowd, His disciples were asking Him about the parable.

**7:18** And He said to them, "Are you also so lacking in understanding? Do you not understand that everything entering into a man from outside cannot defile him,

**7:19** because it does not enter into his heart, but goes into the stomach and goes out into the latrine, cleansing all foods?"

**7:20** And He was saying, "The things that go out of a man, those defile a man.

**7:21** For from within, out of the heart of men, evil thoughts proceed: adulteries, fornications, murders,

**7:22** thefts, greed, wickedness, deceit, lewdness, an evil eye, blasphemy, pride, foolishness.

**7:23** All these evils proceed from within, and they defile a man."

**7:24** And rising from there, He went away into the territory of Tyre and Sidon; and entering a house, He wanted no one to know, and He could not remain hidden.

**7:25** For a woman whose daughter had an unclean spirit, as soon as she heard about Him, entered and fell down at His feet.

**7:26** For the woman was a Gentile, a Syrophoenician by birth. And she kept asking Him to cast the demon out of her daughter.

**7:27** And He said to her, "Allow the children to be satisfied first; for it is not good to take the bread of the children and throw it to the dogs."

**7:28** But she answered and said to Him, "Yes, Lord; for even the little dogs eat under the table from the crumbs of the children."

**7:29** And He said to her, "On account of this statement, go; the demon has gone out from your daughter."

**7:30** And when she had gone away to her house, she found the girl lying on the bed, and that the demon had gone out.

**7:31** And going out again from the territory of Tyre, He came through Sidon to the Sea of Galilee, through the middle of the territory of the Decapolis.

**7:32** And they brought to Him a deaf and mute man, and they were imploring Him to lay His hand on him.

**7:33** And taking him aside from the crowd, He put His fingers into his ears; and spitting, He touched his tongue.

**7:34** And looking up to heaven, He sighed deeply and said to him, "Ephphatha," which is, "Be opened."

**7:35** And immediately his ears were opened, and the bond of his tongue was released, and he was speaking properly.

**7:36** And He commanded them not to tell anyone; yet the more He kept commanding them, so much the more abundantly they kept proclaiming it,

**7:37** and even more they marveled, saying, "He has done everything well; He has made both the deaf hear and the mute speak."

## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — Latin crebro ("frequently") reflects the Greek variant pykna ("often"), whereas many Greek manuscripts read pygme ("with the fist" or "thoroughly").
- **7:7** — The Clementine Vulgate joins "doctrines and precepts of men" with et ("and"), whereas the Greek lacks "and" and reads "teaching as doctrines the commandments of men."
- **7:15** — Latin communicant (also in vv. 18, 20, 23) literally means "make common," a biblical Latin calque for ritual defilement corresponding to "common hands" in vv. 2 and 5.
- **7:19** — In the Clementine Vulgate, "cleansing all foods?" (purgans omnes escas?) stands within Jesus's question rather than as a parenthetical narrator comment.
- **7:34** — The Clementine Vulgate prints suscipiens in caelum, a well-known historical Vulgate variant for suspiciens ("looking up") to heaven; similarly, in v. 32 the Vulgate reads mutum ("mute") where the Greek has mogilalon ("having difficulty speaking").
