## Text

**14:1** Now the Passover and Unleavened Bread were in two days, and the chief priests and scribes were seeking how they might lay hold of Him by deceit and kill Him.

**14:2** But they were saying, "Not on the feast day, for fear that an uproar might arise among the people."

**14:3** And while He was in Bethany in the house of Simon the leper and was reclining at table, a woman came holding an alabaster flask of costly spikenard ointment; and having broken the alabaster flask, she poured it over His head.

**14:4** However, there were some who were indignant within themselves and saying, "For what purpose has this waste of ointment occurred?

**14:5** For this ointment could have been sold for more than three hundred denarii and given to the poor." And they were grumbling angrily against her.

**14:6** But Jesus said, "Let her be; why are you troubling her? She has performed a good work toward Me.

**14:7** For you have the poor with you always, and whenever you wish, you are able to do them good; yet Me you do not always have.

**14:8** This woman did what she was able; she came beforehand to anoint My body for burial.

**14:9** Amen, I tell you, wherever this Gospel is proclaimed in the entire world, what this woman did will also be recounted in remembrance of her."

**14:10** And Judas Iscariot, one of the Twelve, went off to the chief priests so that he might betray Him to them.

**14:11** Hearing this, they rejoiced and promised that they would give him money. And he kept seeking how he might hand Him over at a favorable moment.

**14:12** And on the first day of Unleavened Bread, when they sacrificed the Passover, the disciples said to Him, "Where do You wish us to go and prepare for You so that You may eat the Passover?"

**14:13** And He sent two of His disciples and said to them, "Go into the city, and a man carrying a jar of water will meet you; follow him,

**14:14** and wherever he enters, say to the master of the house that the Teacher says, 'Where is My dining room, where I may eat the Passover with My disciples?'

**14:15** And he himself will show you a large upper room, furnished; and prepare for us there."

**14:16** And His disciples went off and came into the city, and they found it just as He had told them, and they prepared the Passover.

**14:17** Now when evening had arrived, He came with the Twelve.

**14:18** And while they were reclining and eating, Jesus said, "Amen, I tell you that one of you will hand Me over, one who is eating with Me."

**14:19** But they began to be saddened and to say to Him one by one, "Surely not I?"

**14:20** He said to them, "One of the Twelve, the one who dips his hand with Me in the dish.

**14:21** And the Son of Man indeed goes as it is written about Him, but woe to that man through whom the Son of Man will be handed over! It would have been good for him if that man had not been born."

**14:22** And while they were eating, Jesus took bread, and pronouncing a blessing, He broke it and gave it to them, and said, "Take; this is My body."

**14:23** And having taken a cup, giving thanks, He gave it to them, and all of them drank from it.

**14:24** And He said to them, "This is My blood of the new covenant, which will be poured out for many.

**14:25** Amen, I tell you that I will no longer drink from this fruit of the vine until that day when I drink it new in the kingdom of God."

**14:26** And after a hymn had been recited, they went out to the Mount of Olives.

**14:27** And Jesus said to them, "All of you will be caused to stumble because of Me on this night, because it is written, 'I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered.'

**14:28** But after I have risen, I will go ahead of you into Galilee."

**14:29** But Peter said to Him, "Even if all are caused to stumble because of You, yet not I."

**14:30** And Jesus said to him, "Amen, I tell you that you, today, on this night, before a rooster crows twice, are going to deny Me three times."

**14:31** But he kept speaking even more, "Even if I must die together with You, I will not deny You." And all of them were also saying the same.

**14:32** And they came to an estate whose name was Gethsemane. And He said to His disciples, "Sit here while I pray."

**14:33** And He took Peter and James and John along with Him, and began to feel dread and deep distress.

**14:34** And He said to them, "My soul is sorrowful even to death; wait here and stay awake."

**14:35** And when He had gone forward a short distance, He fell down upon the ground and was praying that, if it were possible, the hour might pass from Him.

**14:36** And He said, "Abba, Father, all things are possible to You; take this cup away from Me; yet not what I wish, but what You wish."

**14:37** And He came and found them sleeping. And He said to Peter, "Simon, are you sleeping? Were you not able to stay awake for one hour?

**14:38** Stay awake and pray so that you may not enter into temptation. The spirit indeed is ready, yet the flesh is weak."

**14:39** And going away again, He prayed, saying the same words.

**14:40** And having returned, He once more found them sleeping, for their eyes were weighed down, and they did not know what they should answer Him.

**14:41** And He came a third time and said to them, "Sleep now and rest. It is enough; the hour has come. Look, the Son of Man will be handed over into the hands of sinners.

**14:42** Rise, let us go; look, the one who will hand Me over is near."

**14:43** And while He was still speaking, Judas Iscariot, one of the Twelve, arrived, and with him a large crowd with swords and clubs from the chief priests and the scribes and the elders.

**14:44** Now His betrayer had given them a sign, saying, "Whomever I kiss, He is the one; seize Him and lead Him away carefully."

**14:45** And when he had arrived, immediately approaching Him, he said, "Greetings, Rabbi," and kissed Him.

**14:46** Then they laid hands on Him and seized Him.

**14:47** But a certain one of those standing by, drawing a sword, struck the servant of the high priest and cut off his ear.

**14:48** And answering, Jesus said to them, "Have you come out as against a bandit with swords and clubs to apprehend Me?

**14:49** Every day I was with you in the temple teaching, and you did not seize Me; yet this is so that the Scriptures may be fulfilled."

**14:50** Then His disciples, abandoning Him, all fled.

**14:51** Now a certain young man was following Him, wrapped in a linen cloth over his bare body; and they seized him.

**14:52** But having cast off the linen cloth, he fled naked from them.

**14:53** And they led Jesus to the high priest, and all the priests and the scribes and the elders gathered together.

**14:54** Now Peter followed Him from a distance, all the way inside the courtyard of the high priest; and he was sitting with the attendants by the fire and warming himself.

**14:55** Now the chief priests and the whole council were seeking testimony against Jesus so that they might hand Him over to death, and they were finding none.

**14:56** For many were speaking false testimony against Him, yet the testimonies were not in agreement.

**14:57** And some, standing up, were bearing false testimony against Him, saying,

**14:58** "We heard Him saying, 'I will tear down this temple made by hand, and within three days I will build another not made by hand.'"

**14:59** And their testimony was not in agreement.

**14:60** And the high priest, rising up into the center, questioned Jesus, saying, "Do You answer nothing to the things that are charged against You by these men?"

**14:61** But He kept silent and answered nothing. Again the high priest was questioning Him and said to Him, "Are You the Christ, the Son of the blessed God?"

**14:62** But Jesus said to him, "I am; and you will see the Son of Man sitting at the right hand of the power of God and coming with the clouds of heaven."

**14:63** Then the high priest, tearing his garments, said, "Why do we still require witnesses?

**14:64** You have heard the blasphemy; how does it seem to you?" And all of them condemned Him as deserving of death.

**14:65** And some began to spit on Him, and to cover His face, and to strike Him with fists, and to say to Him, "Prophesy!" And the attendants were striking Him with slaps.

**14:66** And while Peter was below in the courtyard, one of the servant girls of the high priest came.

**14:67** And when she saw Peter warming himself, looking closely at him, she said, "You too were with Jesus the Nazarene."

**14:68** But he denied it, saying, "I neither know nor understand what you are saying." And he went outside in front of the courtyard, and a rooster crowed.

**14:69** And when the servant girl saw him again, she began to say to those standing by, "This man is one of them."

**14:70** But he denied it again. And after a little while, those who were standing nearby were again saying to Peter, "Truly you are one of them, for you are also a Galilean."

**14:71** But he began to invoke curses and to swear, "I do not know this man whom you are speaking of."

**14:72** And immediately a rooster crowed a second time. And Peter recalled the word that Jesus had spoken to him, "Before a rooster crows twice, you will deny Me three times." And he began to weep.

## Translator Notes

- **14:3** — The Vulgate reads nardi spicati ("spikenard," literally "spiked nard"), reflecting the traditional Latin rendering of the rare Greek adjective pistikes (often taken in modern Greek scholarship as "pure" or "genuine").
- **14:14** — Latin refectio literally means "refreshment" or "dining room/refectory," translating Greek katalyma ("guest room"). In 14:15 the Clementine Vulgate likewise describes the upper room solely as stratum ("furnished"), without a second word for "ready."
- **14:24** — Unlike modern critical Greek texts, the Clementine Vulgate includes novi ("new") with testamenti ("covenant/testament") and puts the verb in the future passive (effundetur, "will be poured out").
- **14:27** — The Clementine Vulgate includes in me in nocte ista ("because of Me on this night") here and in te ("because of You") in 14:29, preserving readings omitted in shorter critical Greek editions.
- **14:45** — The Clementine Vulgate includes the greeting Ave ("Greetings" or "Hail") before Rabbi, whereas Greek manuscripts read Rabbi once or twice without a preceding greeting.
- **14:61** — Here (Dei benedicti, "of the blessed God") and in 14:62 (virtutis Dei, "of the power of God"), the Clementine Vulgate explicitly includes Dei ("of God") where the Greek uses reverential circumlocutions ("the Blessed One" and "the Power") alone.
- **14:72** — The Vulgate renders the debated Greek participle epibalōn ("breaking down" or "thinking upon it") simply as cœpit ("he began to weep").
