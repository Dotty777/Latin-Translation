## Text

**8:1** In those days, when a large crowd was there again and they did not have anything to eat, having called the disciples together, He said to them,

**8:2** "I have compassion on the crowd, because, look, they have already stayed with Me for three days and do not have anything to eat;

**8:3** and if I send them away fasting to their home, they will grow faint on the way, for some of them have come from a distance."

**8:4** And His disciples answered Him, "From where will anyone be able to satisfy them with bread in the wilderness?"

**8:5** And He asked them, "How many loaves do you have?" And they said, "Seven."

**8:6** And He instructed the crowd to recline on the ground. And taking the seven loaves, giving thanks, He broke them and kept giving them to His disciples to serve, and they served them to the crowd.

**8:7** And they had a few small fish; and He blessed these as well and ordered them to be served.

**8:8** And they ate and were satisfied; and they picked up what was left over of the broken pieces, seven large baskets.

**8:9** Now those who ate were about four thousand, and He sent them away.

**8:10** And immediately, boarding the boat with His disciples, He came into the region of Dalmanutha.

**8:11** And the Pharisees came out and began to dispute with Him, seeking from Him a sign from heaven, testing Him.

**8:12** And groaning in spirit, He said, "Why is this generation seeking a sign? Amen, I tell you, no sign will be given to this generation."

**8:13** And leaving them, He boarded the boat again and departed across the sea.

**8:14** And they forgot to take bread, and except for one loaf they had none with them in the boat.

**8:15** And He was instructing them, saying, "Watch out and guard against the leaven of the Pharisees and the leaven of Herod."

**8:16** And they were reasoning with one another, saying, "Because we have no bread."

**8:17** Perceiving this, Jesus said to them, "Why are you reasoning because you have no bread? Will you not yet perceive, nor do you understand? Do you still have your heart blinded?

**8:18** Having eyes, do you not see? And having ears, do you not hear? Nor do you recall,

**8:19** when I broke the five loaves for the five thousand, how many baskets full of broken pieces did you pick up?" They said to Him, "Twelve."

**8:20** "When I also broke the seven loaves for the four thousand, how many large baskets of broken pieces did you pick up?" And they said to Him, "Seven."

**8:21** And He was saying to them, "How is it that you do not yet understand?"

**8:22** And they came to Bethsaida, and they brought a blind man to Him, and were pleading with Him to touch him.

**8:23** And taking hold of the blind man's hand, He led him outside the village; and spitting on his eyes, having laid His hands on him, He asked him if he saw anything.

**8:24** And looking, he said, "I see men like trees walking."

**8:25** Then He laid His hands on his eyes again, and he began to see, and he was restored so that he saw all things clearly.

**8:26** And He sent him to his home, saying, "Go to your home, and if you enter the village, tell no one."

**8:27** And Jesus and His disciples went out into the towns of Caesarea Philippi; and on the way He was asking His disciples, saying to them, "Who do men say that I am?"

**8:28** They answered Him, saying, "John the Baptist; others, Elijah; and others, as one of the prophets."

**8:29** Then He said to them, "But you, who do you say that I am?" Peter, answering, said to Him, "You are the Christ."

**8:30** And He sternly warned them not to tell anyone about Him.

**8:31** And He began to teach them that it is necessary for the Son of Man to suffer many things, and to be rejected by the elders and by the chief priests and the scribes, and to be put to death, and after three days to rise again.

**8:32** And He was speaking the word openly. And taking hold of Him, Peter began to rebuke Him.

**8:33** But He, turning around and seeing His disciples, sternly rebuked Peter, saying, "Go behind Me, Satan, because you do not have a mind for the things of God, but for the things of men."

**8:34** And having called the crowd together with His disciples, He said to them, "If anyone wants to follow Me, let him deny himself and take up his cross and follow Me.

**8:35** For whoever wants to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life on account of Me and the Gospel will save it.

**8:36** For what will it profit a man if he gains the entire world and incurs the loss of his soul?

**8:37** Or what will a man give in exchange for his soul?

**8:38** For the one who is ashamed of Me and of My words in this adulterous and sinful generation, the Son of Man will also be ashamed of him when He comes in His Father's glory with the holy angels."

## Translator Notes

- **8:12** — Latin literally reads "if a sign will be given to this generation" (si dabitur generationi isti signum), preserving a Semitic oath idiom that expresses an emphatic negative.
- **8:17** — The Clementine Vulgate has future cognoscetis ("Will you not yet perceive") alongside present intelligitis, and reads caecatum ("blinded") where the Greek text reads "hardened."
- **8:25** — The Vulgate reads et coepit videre ("and he began to see"), where critical Greek editions read "and he looked intently."
- **8:26** — The Vulgate preserves the fuller command "Go to your home, and if you enter the village, tell no one," whereas critical Greek editions read simply "Do not even enter the village."
- **8:35** — Latin anima, rendered "life" here and "soul" in verses 36-37, encompasses both earthly life and the enduring soul.
