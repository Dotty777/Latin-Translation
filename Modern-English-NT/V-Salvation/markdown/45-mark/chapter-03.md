## Text

**3:1** And He entered the synagogue again, and a man with a withered hand was there.

**3:2** And they were watching Him closely to see whether He would heal on the Sabbath, so that they might accuse Him.

**3:3** And He said to the man with the withered hand, "Stand up in the middle."

**3:4** And He said to them, "Is it lawful on the Sabbath to do good or to do evil, to save a life or to destroy it?" But they kept silent.

**3:5** And looking around at them with anger, grieved over the blindness of their heart, He said to the man, "Stretch out your hand." And he stretched it out, and his hand was restored to him.

**3:6** And going out, the Pharisees immediately began taking counsel with the Herodians against Him, as to how they might destroy Him.

**3:7** Now Jesus withdrew with His disciples to the sea, and a large crowd from Galilee and Judea followed Him,

**3:8** and from Jerusalem, and from Idumea, and beyond the Jordan; and those around Tyre and Sidon, a great multitude, hearing what He was doing, came to Him.

**3:9** And He told His disciples that a small boat should be kept ready for Him because of the crowd, so that they would not crush Him;

**3:10** for He was healing many, so that all who had afflictions were pressing in upon Him in order to touch Him.

**3:11** And whenever the unclean spirits saw Him, they would fall down before Him and cry out, saying,

**3:12** "You are the Son of God." And He strictly warned them not to make Him known.

**3:13** And going up onto the mountain, He called to Himself those whom He Himself wanted, and they came to Him.

**3:14** And He appointed twelve so that they would be with Him, and so that He might send them to preach.

**3:15** And He gave them authority to heal illnesses and to drive out demons.

**3:16** And He gave Simon the name Peter;

**3:17** and James the son of Zebedee, and John the brother of James, and He gave them the name Boanerges, which is, Sons of Thunder;

**3:18** and Andrew, and Philip, and Bartholomew, and Matthew, and Thomas, and James the son of Alphaeus, and Thaddaeus, and Simon the Cananaean,

**3:19** and Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed Him.

**3:20** And they came to a house, and the crowd gathered again, so that they could not even eat bread.

**3:21** And when His own people heard this, they went out to take hold of Him; for they were saying, "He has gone mad."

**3:22** And the scribes who had come down from Jerusalem were saying, "He has Beelzebub," and, "By the prince of demons He drives out demons."

**3:23** And having called them together, He was speaking to them in parables: "How can Satan drive out Satan?

**3:24** And if a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand.

**3:25** And if a house is divided against itself, that house cannot stand.

**3:26** And if Satan has risen up against himself, he is divided, and he will not be able to stand, but has an end.

**3:27** No one can enter the house of a strong man and plunder his belongings unless he first binds the strong man, and then he will plunder his house.

**3:28** Amen, I tell you, all sins will be forgiven the sons of men, and the blasphemies with which they blaspheme;

**3:29** but whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit will never have forgiveness, but will be guilty of an eternal offense."

**3:30** Because they were saying, "He has an unclean spirit."

**3:31** And His mother and brothers came, and standing outside, they sent to Him, calling Him,

**3:32** and a crowd was sitting around Him; and they said to Him, "Look, Your mother and Your brothers are outside seeking You."

**3:33** And answering them, He said, "Who are My mother and My brothers?"

**3:34** And looking around at those who were sitting in a circle around Him, He said, "Here are My mother and My brothers.

**3:35** For whoever does the will of God, this one is My brother, and My sister, and mother."

## Translator Notes

- **3:5** — The Vulgate distinctively reads 'cæcitate cordis eorum' ("the blindness of their heart") rather than "hardness of heart" as in most Greek-based translations.
- **3:12** — In the Clementine Vulgate, 'Tu es Filius Dei' ("You are the Son of God") begins verse 12 rather than closing verse 11 as in standard Greek verse numbering.
- **3:15** — The Vulgate includes 'curandi infirmitates' ("to heal illnesses") alongside authority to drive out demons, a longer reading omitted in many critical Greek editions.
- **3:18** — 'Cananæum' ("Cananaean") transliterates the Aramaic epithet meaning "zealous one" (cf. "Zealot" in Luke 6:15) rather than indicating a native of Canaan.
- **3:22** — The Vulgate preserves the name in its Latin form 'Beelzebub' (reflecting the Old Testament name in 2 Kings 1:2) rather than the Greek form Beelzeboul.
