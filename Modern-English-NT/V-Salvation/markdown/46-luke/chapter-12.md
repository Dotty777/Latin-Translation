## Text

**12:1** Now as many crowds were standing around, so that they were trampling on one another, He began to say to His disciples, "Watch out for the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy.

**12:2** Yet nothing is covered over that will not be revealed, nor concealed that will not be known.

**12:3** For the things you have said in the darkness will be said in the light, and what you have spoken into the ear in the inner rooms will be proclaimed on the rooftops.

**12:4** Now I say to you, My friends, do not be terrified by those who kill the body and after these things have nothing further they can do.

**12:5** But I will show you whom you should fear: fear Him who, after He has killed, has authority to throw into Gehenna; yes, I say to you, fear Him.

**12:6** Are not five sparrows sold for a dupondius, and not one of them is forgotten before God?

**12:7** But even the hairs of your head have all been numbered. Therefore do not fear; you are worth more than many sparrows.

**12:8** Now I say to you, everyone who confesses Me before people, the Son of Man will also confess him before the angels of God;

**12:9** but the one who denies Me before people will be denied before the angels of God.

**12:10** And everyone who says a word against the Son of Man, it will be forgiven him; but to the one who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven.

**12:11** Now when they bring you into the synagogues and before magistrates and authorities, do not be anxious about how or what you should answer, or what you should say.

**12:12** For the Holy Spirit will teach you at that very hour what you must say."

**12:13** Then someone from the crowd said to Him, "Teacher, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me."

**12:14** But He said to him, "Man, who appointed Me a judge or a divider over you?"

**12:15** And He said to them, "Watch out and guard against all greed, for when anyone has an abundance, his life does not come from the things he possesses."

**12:16** Then He told a parable to them, saying, "The field of a certain rich man yielded abundant crops,

**12:17** and he was thinking within himself, saying, 'What should I do, because I have nowhere to gather my crops?'

**12:18** And he said, 'I will do this: I will tear down my barns and build larger ones, and there I will gather all the things that have grown for me, and my goods,

**12:19** and I will say to my soul, "Soul, you have many goods stored up for very many years; rest, eat, drink, feast."'

**12:20** But God said to him, 'Fool, this night they are demanding your soul back from you; now the things you prepared, whose will they be?'

**12:21** So it is with the one who stores up treasure for himself and is not rich toward God."

**12:22** And He said to His disciples, "For this reason I say to you, do not be anxious for your life, what you will eat, nor for the body, what you will put on.

**12:23** Life is more than food, and the body is more than clothing.

**12:24** Consider the ravens, for they do not sow or reap; they have neither storeroom nor barn, and God feeds them. How much more are you worth than they?

**12:25** And which of you by thinking can add one cubit to his stature?

**12:26** If then you cannot do even the smallest thing, why are you anxious about the rest?

**12:27** Consider the lilies, how they grow: they do not labor, nor do they spin; yet I say to you, not even Solomon in all his glory was dressed like one of these.

**12:28** Now if God thus clothes the grass, which today is in the field and tomorrow is thrown into the oven, how much more you, of little faith?

**12:29** And you, do not seek what you will eat or what you will drink, and do not be lifted up on high;

**12:30** for the nations of the world seek all these things, yet your Father knows that you are in need of these things.

**12:31** Nevertheless, seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things will be added to you.

**12:32** Do not fear, little flock, because it has pleased your Father to give you the kingdom.

**12:33** Sell the things you possess and give alms. Make for yourselves purses that do not grow old, an unfailing treasure in the heavens, where no thief approaches, nor does a moth destroy.

**12:34** For where your treasure is, there your heart will also be.

**12:35** Let your loins be girded and lamps burning in your hands,

**12:36** and you yourselves be like people waiting for their master when he returns from the wedding, so that when he comes and knocks, they may immediately open to him.

**12:37** Blessed are those servants whom the master, when he comes, will find watching. Amen, I tell you that he will gird himself and make them recline at table, and passing along he will serve them.

**12:38** And if he comes in the second watch, and if he comes in the third watch, and finds them so, blessed are those servants.

**12:39** Now know this, that if the head of the household knew at what hour the thief was coming, he would surely keep watch and would not permit his house to be broken through.

**12:40** And you also, be ready, because at an hour you do not expect, the Son of Man will come."

**12:41** Then Peter said to Him, "Lord, are You speaking this parable to us, or also to everyone?"

**12:42** And the Lord said, "Who, do you think, is the faithful and prudent steward whom the master has appointed over his household, to give them the measure of wheat at the proper time?

**12:43** Blessed is that servant whom the master, when he comes, will find doing so.

**12:44** Truly I say to you that he will appoint him over all the things he possesses.

**12:45** But if that servant says in his heart, 'My master is delaying in coming,' and begins to beat the servants and maidservants, and to eat and to drink and to become drunk,

**12:46** the master of that servant will come on a day he does not expect and at an hour he does not know, and will sever him and place his portion with the unfaithful.

**12:47** Now that servant who knew the will of his master, and did not prepare, and does not act according to his will, will be beaten with many blows;

**12:48** but the one who did not know and did things deserving blows will be beaten with few. Now from everyone to whom much has been given, much will be required, and from the one to whom they have entrusted much, they will ask more.

**12:49** I have come to cast fire onto the earth, and what do I want except that it be kindled?

**12:50** But I have a baptism with which to be baptized, and how constrained am I until it is completed?

**12:51** Do you think that I have come to give peace on the earth? No, I say to you, but separation;

**12:52** for from now on five in one house will be divided; three against two and two against three

**12:53** will be divided: father against son and son against his father, mother against daughter and daughter against mother, mother-in-law against her daughter-in-law and daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law."

**12:54** Now He was also saying to the crowds, "When you see a cloud rising from the west, at once you say, 'A rainstorm is coming,' and so it happens.

**12:55** And when the south wind is blowing, you say, 'There will be intense heat,' and it happens.

**12:56** Hypocrites! You know how to discern the face of the sky and the earth, yet how do you not discern this time?

**12:57** And why even on your own do you not judge what is right?

**12:58** Now as you go with your adversary to the ruler, on the way make an effort to be freed from him, so that he might not drag you to the judge, and the judge hand you over to the bailiff, and the bailiff throw you into prison.

**12:59** I say to you, you will not come out from there until you pay back even the very last mite."

## Translator Notes

- **12:1** — Vulgate reads multis turbis circumstantibus ("many crowds standing around") and omits "first" before "to His disciples," where the Greek text has "myriads of the crowd gathered together" and "to His disciples first."
- **12:3** — Vulgate reads Quoniam ... dicentur ("For ... will be said"), whereas the Greek text reads "Therefore ... will be heard."
- **12:6** — Latin dipondio (dupondius) is a Roman bronze coin worth two asses, rendering the Greek "two assaria."
- **12:25** — Vulgate reads cogitando ("by thinking," distinct from solliciti in 12:22 and 12:26) and staturam ("stature," physical height), whereas the Greek wording is often taken as "by worrying" and "lifespan."
- **12:29** — Vulgate reads nolite in sublime tolli ("do not be lifted up on high"), rendering the Greek verb for anxious suspense literally by its spatial image of being lifted aloft, which Latin commentators also understood as a warning against pride or ambitious restlessness.
- **12:31** — Vulgate reads quærite primum regnum Dei, et justitiam ejus ("seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness") and hæc omnia ("all these things"), echoing Matthew 6:33, whereas the critical Greek text reads simply "seek His kingdom, and these things."
- **12:35** — Vulgate includes in manibus vestris ("in your hands"), a phrase not present in the standard Greek text.
- **12:46** — Latin dividet eum literally means "will divide him" (here "will sever him"), translating the Greek verb meaning "cut in two" while also linking to "his portion" (partem ejus).
- **12:49** — Vulgate reads et quid volo nisi ut accendatur? ("and what do I want except that it be kindled?"), whereas the Greek text is usually rendered "and how I wish it were already kindled!"
- **12:56** — Vulgate places "the sky and the earth" (cæli et terræ) in that order and omits "know how to" in the second clause (quomodo non probatis?), differing from the Greek order ("earth and sky") and repeated verb.
