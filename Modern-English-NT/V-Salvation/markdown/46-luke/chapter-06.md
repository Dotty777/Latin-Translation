## Text

**6:1** Now it happened on the second-first Sabbath, as He was passing through the grainfields, that His disciples were plucking heads of grain and eating them, rubbing them with their hands.

**6:2** But some of the Pharisees were saying to them, "Why are you doing what is not lawful on the Sabbaths?"

**6:3** And answering them, Jesus said, "Have you not even read this, what David did when he himself was hungry, and those who were with him,

**6:4** how he entered the house of God, and took the loaves of presentation, and ate, and gave to those who were with him, which it is not lawful to eat except for the priests alone?"

**6:5** And He was saying to them, "The Son of Man is Lord even of the Sabbath."

**6:6** Now it happened on another Sabbath that He entered the synagogue and taught. And a man was there, and his right hand was withered.

**6:7** Now the scribes and Pharisees were watching whether He would cure on the Sabbath, so that they might find grounds to accuse Him.

**6:8** But He Himself knew their thoughts, and He said to the man who had the withered hand, "Rise, and stand in the middle." And rising, he stood.

**6:9** Then Jesus said to them, "I ask you whether it is lawful on the Sabbaths to do good or to do evil, to save a life or to destroy it?"

**6:10** And having looked around at them all, He said to the man, "Stretch out your hand." And he stretched it out, and his hand was restored.

**6:11** But they themselves were filled with folly, and they were discussing with one another what they might do to Jesus.

**6:12** Now it happened in those days that He went out onto the mountain to pray, and He was spending the whole night in prayer to God.

**6:13** And when day came, He called His disciples, and He chose twelve from among them (whom He also named apostles):

**6:14** Simon, whom He surnamed Peter, and Andrew his brother, James and John, Philip and Bartholomew,

**6:15** Matthew and Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, and Simon, who is called the Zealot,

**6:16** and Judas son of James, and Judas Iscariot, who was a traitor.

**6:17** And coming down with them, He stood on a level place, along with a crowd of His disciples and a very large multitude of the people from all Judea, and Jerusalem, and the coastal region of both Tyre and Sidon,

**6:18** who had come to hear Him and to be healed from their ailments. And those who were troubled by unclean spirits were being cured.

**6:19** And the whole crowd was seeking to touch Him, because power was going out from Him and healing all of them.

**6:20** And He, having lifted up His eyes upon His disciples, was saying, "Blessed are the poor, because yours is the kingdom of God.

**6:21** Blessed are you who are hungry now, because you will be filled. Blessed are you who weep now, because you will laugh.

**6:22** Blessed will you be when people hate you, and when they exclude you, and reproach you, and cast out your name as evil on account of the Son of Man.

**6:23** Rejoice on that day and exult, for look, your reward is great in heaven; for in this same way their fathers acted toward the prophets.

**6:24** However, woe to you who are rich, because you have your consolation.

**6:25** Woe to you who have been filled, because you will be hungry. Woe to you who laugh now, because you will mourn and weep.

**6:26** Woe when people speak well of you, for in this same way their fathers acted toward the false prophets.

**6:27** But to you who hear, I say: love your enemies; do good to those who hate you.

**6:28** Bless those who curse you, and pray for those who slander you.

**6:29** And to the one who strikes you on the cheek, offer the other as well. And from the one who takes away your cloak, do not withhold even the tunic.

**6:30** Now to everyone who asks you, give; and from the one who takes away the things that are yours, do not ask for them back.

**6:31** And just as you want people to do to you, you also do likewise to them.

**6:32** And if you love those who love you, what thanks do you have? For even sinners love those who love them.

**6:33** And if you do good to those who do good to you, what thanks do you have? For indeed, even sinners do this.

**6:34** And if you lend to those from whom you hope to receive back, what thanks do you have? For even sinners lend to sinners so that they may receive back an equal amount.

**6:35** However, love your enemies, do good, and lend, hoping for nothing from it; and your reward will be great, and you will be sons of the Most High, because He Himself is kind toward the ungrateful and the evil.

**6:36** Be merciful, therefore, just as your Father also is merciful.

**6:37** Do not judge, and you will not be judged; do not condemn, and you will not be condemned. Forgive, and you will be forgiven.

**6:38** Give, and it will be given to you: a good measure, packed down and shaken together and overflowing, they will put into your lap. For with the same measure by which you measure, it will be measured back to you."

**6:39** Now He was also speaking a parable to them: "Can a blind man lead a blind man? Do they not both fall into a pit?

**6:40** A disciple is not above the teacher; but everyone will be perfect if he is like his teacher.

**6:41** Now why do you see the splinter in your brother's eye, yet do not consider the beam that is in your eye?

**6:42** Or how can you say to your brother, 'Brother, let me pull out the splinter from your eye,' when you yourself do not see the beam in your eye? Hypocrite, first pull out the beam from your eye, and then you will see clearly to draw out the splinter from your brother's eye.

**6:43** For there is no good tree that produces bad fruits, nor a bad tree producing good fruit.

**6:44** For each tree is known by its own fruit. For people do not gather figs from thorns, nor do they harvest grapes from a bramble bush.

**6:45** A good man brings out good from the good treasury of his heart, and an evil man brings out evil from the evil treasury. For from the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.

**6:46** Now why do you call Me 'Lord, Lord,' and not do the things that I say?

**6:47** Everyone who comes to Me, and hears My words, and does them, I will show you whom he is like:

**6:48** he is like a man building a house, who dug deep and laid a foundation upon the rock; and when a flood came, the river dashed against that house and could not move it, for it had been founded upon the rock.

**6:49** But the one who hears and does not do so is like a man building his house upon the ground without a foundation, against which the stream dashed, and immediately it fell, and the ruin of that house was great."

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "in sabbato secundo, primo" ("on the second-first Sabbath"), reflecting the rare and debated Greek term deuteroproto omitted by many modern critical Greek editions. It has been variously interpreted as the first Sabbath after the second day of Passover or a Sabbath tied to a festival cycle.
- **6:28** — Vulgate "calumniantibus" specifically means "those who slander" or "falsely accuse," a distinctive Latin rendering where the Greek reads "those who mistreat."
- **6:35** — Vulgate "nihil inde sperantes" ("hoping for nothing from it") unambiguously resolves a debated Greek phrase (meden apelpizontes) that has also been interpreted as "despairing of no one."
- **6:40** — The Vulgate reads "perfectus autem omnis erit, si sit sicut magister ejus" ("everyone will be perfect if he is like his teacher"), making likeness to the teacher the condition for perfection rather than the Greek wording ("everyone, when fully trained, will be like his teacher").
- **6:48** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "fundata enim erat super petram" ("for it had been founded upon the rock"), whereas many modern critical Greek editions read "because it had been well built."
