## Text

**20:1** And it happened on one of the days, while He was teaching the people in the temple and proclaiming the good news, that the chief priests and the scribes assembled with the elders,

**20:2** and they spoke, saying to Him, "Tell us: by what authority do You do these things? Or who is it who gave You this authority?"

**20:3** But Jesus, answering, said to them, "I too will ask you one question. Answer Me:

**20:4** Was John's baptism from heaven, or from men?"

**20:5** But they were reasoning within themselves, saying, "If we say, 'From heaven,' He will say, 'Why then did you not believe him?'

**20:6** But if we say, 'From men,' all the people will stone us; for they are certain that John is a prophet."

**20:7** And they answered that they did not know where it was from.

**20:8** And Jesus said to them, "Neither do I tell you by what authority I do these things."

**20:9** Then He began to tell this parable to the people: "A man planted a vineyard and leased it to tenant farmers, and he himself was abroad for a long time.

**20:10** And at the proper time he sent a servant to the growers so that they would give him some of the fruit of the vineyard; yet after beating him, they sent him away empty-handed.

**20:11** And he went on to send another servant. Yet beating this one as well and treating him with contempt, they sent him away empty-handed.

**20:12** And he went on to send a third; and wounding him also, they threw him out.

**20:13** Then the master of the vineyard said, 'What should I do? I will send my beloved son; perhaps, when they see him, they will respect him.'

**20:14** When the tenant farmers saw him, they reasoned within themselves, saying, 'This is the heir; let us kill him so that the inheritance may become ours.'

**20:15** And after throwing him out of the vineyard, they killed him. What then will the master of the vineyard do to them?

**20:16** He will come and destroy those tenant farmers, and give the vineyard to others." Upon hearing this, they said to Him, "May that never happen!"

**20:17** But He, looking at them, said, "What then is this that is written: 'The stone that the builders rejected, this one has become the head of the corner'?

**20:18** Everyone who falls on that stone will be shattered; yet on whomever it falls, it will crush him to pieces."

**20:19** And the chief priests and the scribes were seeking to lay hands on Him at that hour, and they feared the people; for they perceived that He had spoken this parable against them.

**20:20** And watching closely, they sent spies who pretended to be righteous, so that they might catch Him in His speech in order to hand Him over to the rule and authority of the governor.

**20:21** And they questioned Him, saying, "Teacher, we know that You speak and teach rightly, and You do not show partiality, but teach the way of God in truth.

**20:22** Is it permitted for us to pay tribute to Caesar, or not?"

**20:23** But discerning their deceit, He said to them, "Why do you test Me?

**20:24** Show Me a denarius. Whose image and inscription does it have?" Answering, they said to Him, "Caesar's."

**20:25** And He said to them, "Therefore give back the things that are Caesar's to Caesar, and the things that are God's to God."

**20:26** And they were unable to find fault with His word in the presence of the people; and marveling at His answer, they fell silent.

**20:27** Now some of the Sadducees approached, who deny that there is a resurrection, and they questioned Him,

**20:28** saying, "Teacher, Moses wrote for us that if anyone's brother dies having a wife, and this man is without children, his brother should take her as his wife and raise up offspring for his brother.

**20:29** So there were seven brothers; and the first took a wife, and died without children.

**20:30** And the next took her, and he also died without a son.

**20:31** And the third took her, and likewise all seven as well, and they left no offspring and died.

**20:32** Last of all, the woman also died.

**20:33** In the resurrection, therefore, whose wife among them will she be? For indeed the seven had her as a wife."

**20:34** And Jesus said to them, "The sons of this age marry and are given away in marriage;

**20:35** yet those who will be considered worthy of that age and of the resurrection from the dead will neither marry nor take wives;

**20:36** for neither could they die any longer; for they are equal to the angels, and they are sons of God, since they are sons of the resurrection.

**20:37** Now that the dead rise, even Moses showed beside the bush, just as he calls the Lord the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.

**20:38** Yet God is not the God of the dead, but of the living; for all live to Him."

**20:39** Then some of the scribes, answering, said to Him, "Teacher, You have spoken well."

**20:40** And they no longer dared to question Him about anything.

**20:41** Then He said to them, "How do they say that the Christ is the son of David?

**20:42** And David himself says in the book of Psalms, 'The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at My right hand,

**20:43** until I set Your enemies as a footstool for Your feet.'

**20:44** David therefore calls Him Lord; and how is He his son?"

**20:45** Now while all the people were listening, He said to His disciples,

**20:46** "Watch out for the scribes, who desire to walk in long robes, and love greetings in the marketplace, and the foremost seats in the synagogues, and the foremost places at banquets,

**20:47** who devour the houses of widows, making a pretense of a long prayer; these will receive a greater condemnation."

## Translator Notes

- **20:23** — The Clementine Vulgate includes "Why do you test Me?" (Quid me tentatis?), a clause absent from several early Greek manuscripts of Luke 20:23 though present in Matthew 22:18 and Mark 12:15.
- **20:30** — The Vulgate reads "And the next took her, and he also died without a son" (Et sequens accepit illam, et ipse mortuus est sine filio), using "the next" (sequens) rather than "the second" and preserving the longer clause with singular "without a son," which several early Greek manuscripts omit.
- **20:35** — Where the Greek repeats "neither marry nor are given in marriage," the Vulgate uses Roman marital idiom, "will neither marry nor take wives" (neque nubent, neque ducent uxores).
- **20:36** — The Clementine Vulgate prints the perfect-tense form potuerunt ("could"), whereas the Greek has the present "are able" and critical Vulgate editions read the future poterunt ("will be able").
- **20:37** — The Vulgate renders the Greek phrase meaning "in the passage about the bush" literally as a spatial preposition, "beside the bush" (secus rubum).
