## Text

**4:1** Now Jesus, full of the Holy Spirit, returned from the Jordan, and was led by the Spirit into the wilderness

**4:2** for forty days, and was tempted by the devil. And He ate nothing in those days; and when they were completed, He grew hungry.

**4:3** Then the devil said to Him, "If You are the Son of God, say to this stone that it become bread."

**4:4** And Jesus replied to him, "It is written, 'Man does not live by bread alone, but by every word of God.'"

**4:5** And the devil brought Him onto a high mountain and showed Him all the kingdoms of the world in a moment of time,

**4:6** and said to Him, "To You I will give all this authority and their glory, because they have been handed over to me, and I give them to whomever I wish.

**4:7** Therefore, if You worship before me, all of them will be Yours."

**4:8** And answering, Jesus said to him, "It is written, 'You shall worship the Lord your God, and you shall serve Him alone.'"

**4:9** And he brought Him into Jerusalem, and placed Him on the pinnacle of the temple, and said to Him, "If You are the Son of God, cast Yourself down from here.

**4:10** For it is written, 'He has commanded His angels concerning You, to protect You,'

**4:11** and, 'In their hands they will lift You up, so that You may not by chance strike Your foot against a stone.'"

**4:12** And answering, Jesus said to him, "It has been said, 'You shall not tempt the Lord your God.'"

**4:13** And when every temptation had been completed, the devil withdrew from Him for a time.

**4:14** And Jesus returned in the power of the Spirit into Galilee, and a report went out throughout the entire region concerning Him.

**4:15** And He Himself was teaching in their synagogues and was magnified by everyone.

**4:16** And He came to Nazareth, where He had been raised, and entered the synagogue on the Sabbath day according to His custom, and rose to read.

**4:17** And the scroll of Isaiah the prophet was handed to Him. And when He unrolled the scroll, He found the place where it was written:

**4:18** "The Spirit of the Lord is upon Me, because He has anointed Me; He has sent Me to announce good news to the poor, to heal the brokenhearted, to proclaim freedom to the captives and sight to the blind, to let the crushed go in freedom, to proclaim the acceptable year of the Lord and the day of retribution."

**4:20** And when He had rolled up the scroll, He returned it to the attendant and sat down. And the eyes of everyone in the synagogue were gazing intently at Him.

**4:21** Then He began to say to them, "Today this scripture has been fulfilled in your ears."

**4:22** And all were bearing witness to Him, and marveling at the words of grace that were proceeding from His mouth, and they were saying, "Is this not the son of Joseph?"

**4:23** And He said to them, "Certainly you will say this proverb to Me: 'Physician, heal yourself! All the things we have heard were done in Capernaum, do here also in Your homeland.'"

**4:24** Then He said, "Amen, I tell you that no prophet is accepted in his own homeland.

**4:25** In truth I say to you, there were many widows in the days of Elijah in Israel, when the sky was closed for three years and six months, when a great famine arose in all the land,

**4:26** and to none of them was Elijah sent, except to Zarephath of Sidon, to a woman who was a widow.

**4:27** And there were many lepers in Israel under Elisha the prophet, and not one of them was cleansed except Naaman the Syrian."

**4:28** And all in the synagogue were filled with anger as they heard these things.

**4:29** And they rose up and drove Him outside the city, and brought Him as far as the brow of the mountain on which their city had been built, so that they might hurl Him headlong.

**4:30** But He Himself, passing through the midst of them, went on.

**4:31** And He went down into Capernaum, a city of Galilee, and there He was teaching them on the Sabbaths.

**4:32** And they were astounded at His teaching, because His speech was with authority.

**4:33** And in the synagogue there was a man having an unclean demon, and he cried out in a loud voice,

**4:34** saying, "Leave us alone! What do we have to do with You, Jesus the Nazarene? Have You come to destroy us? I know You, who You are: the Holy One of God."

**4:35** And Jesus rebuked it, saying, "Be silent, and come out of him." And when the demon had thrown him into the middle, it came out of him and did not harm him at all.

**4:36** And dread came upon all, and they were conversing with one another, saying, "What is this word, that with authority and power He commands unclean spirits, and they come out?"

**4:37** And the report concerning Him was spreading into every place of the region.

**4:38** Now Jesus, rising from the synagogue, entered Simon's house. And Simon's mother-in-law was gripped by great fevers, and they asked Him on her behalf.

**4:39** And He stood over her and commanded the fever, and it left her. And rising at once, she served them.

**4:40** Now when the sun had set, all who had people sick with various ailments were bringing them to Him. And laying His hands on each one, He was healing them.

**4:41** And demons were coming out from many, shouting and saying, "You are the Son of God." And rebuking them, He would not permit them to speak, because they knew that He was the Christ.

**4:42** Now when day came, He left and went into a deserted place, and the crowds were seeking Him; and they came up to Him and were holding Him back so that He would not depart from them.

**4:43** To them He said, "To other cities also I must announce the good news of the kingdom of God, because for this reason I was sent."

**4:44** And He kept preaching in the synagogues of Galilee.

## Translator Notes

- **4:4** — The Vulgate includes the closing clause 'sed in omni verbo Dei' ("but by every word of God"), aligning with the Byzantine Greek tradition and Matthew 4:4, whereas most modern critical Greek editions end the quotation after "bread alone."
- **4:5** — The Vulgate explicitly includes 'diabolus' ("the devil") and 'in montem excelsum' ("onto a high mountain"), matching the Byzantine Greek tradition, whereas many critical Greek manuscripts omit both details in Luke.
- **4:10** — Following the Latin Psalter (Psalm 90:11 [91:11]), the Vulgate uses the perfect tense 'mandavit' ("He has commanded") rather than the future tense found in the Greek text.
- **4:18** — The Clementine Vulgate combines verses 18 and 19 into a single verse (with no separate 4:19), includes 'sanare contritos corde' ("to heal the brokenhearted"), and uniquely adds 'et diem retributionis' ("and the day of retribution") from Isaiah 61:2.
- **4:44** — The Vulgate reads 'Galilææ' ("of Galilee"), matching the Byzantine Greek tradition, whereas many early Greek manuscripts and modern critical editions read "of Judea."
