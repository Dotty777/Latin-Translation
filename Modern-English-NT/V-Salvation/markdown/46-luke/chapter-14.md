## Text

**14:1** And it happened, when Jesus entered the house of a certain leader of the Pharisees on the Sabbath to eat bread, that they themselves were watching Him.

**14:2** And look, a certain man with dropsy was before Him.

**14:3** And answering, Jesus spoke to the experts in the law and the Pharisees, saying, "Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath?"

**14:4** But they remained silent. Then He took hold of him, healed him, and sent him away.

**14:5** And answering them, He said, "If any one of you has a donkey or an ox fall into a well, will he not immediately pull it out on the Sabbath day?"

**14:6** And they were not able to reply to Him about these things.

**14:7** Now He was also telling a parable to those who were invited, noticing how they were choosing the first places at table, saying to them,

**14:8** "When you are invited to a wedding feast, do not recline in the first place, in case someone more honored than you has been invited by him,

**14:9** and the one who called you and him comes and says to you, 'Give place to this man,' and then with shame you begin to take the last place.

**14:10** But when you are called, go and recline in the last place, so that when the one who invited you comes, he may say to you, 'Friend, go up higher.' Then you will have glory in the presence of those reclining together,

**14:11** because everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and the one who humbles himself will be exalted."

**14:12** Now He was also saying to the one who had invited Him, "When you give a lunch or a dinner, do not call your friends, nor your brothers, nor your relatives, nor rich neighbors, in case they too invite you back and repayment is made to you;

**14:13** but when you give a banquet, call the poor, the disabled, the lame, and the blind,

**14:14** and you will be blessed, because they do not have the means to repay you; for you will be repaid at the resurrection of the righteous."

**14:15** When one of those reclining together heard these things, he said to Him, "Blessed is the one who will eat bread in the kingdom of God."

**14:16** But He said to him, "A certain man prepared a great dinner and called many.

**14:17** And at the hour of dinner he sent his servant to tell those who had been invited to come, because everything is now ready.

**14:18** And all of them together began to make excuses. The first said to him, 'I have bought a farm, and I need to go out and see it; I ask you, consider me excused.'

**14:19** And another said, 'I have bought five yoke of oxen, and I am going to test them; I ask you, consider me excused.'

**14:20** And another said, 'I have married a wife, and therefore I cannot come.'

**14:21** And returning, the servant reported these things to his master. Then the master of the house, angry, said to his servant, 'Go out quickly into the streets and lanes of the city, and bring in here the poor, the disabled, the blind, and the lame.'

**14:22** And the servant said, 'Master, it has been done as you commanded, and there is still room.'

**14:23** And the master said to the servant, 'Go out into the roads and hedges and compel them to enter, so that my house may be filled.

**14:24** And I tell you that none of those men who were called will taste my dinner.'"

**14:25** Now many crowds were going with Him, and turning around, He said to them,

**14:26** "If anyone comes to Me and does not hate his father and mother, and wife and children, and brothers and sisters, and even his own life as well, he cannot be My disciple.

**14:27** And whoever does not carry his cross and come after Me cannot be My disciple.

**14:28** For which one of you, wanting to build a tower, does not first sit down and calculate the expenses that are necessary, to see whether he has enough to finish it,

**14:29** so that, after he has laid the foundation and is not able to finish, all who see it may not begin to mock him,

**14:30** saying, 'This man began to build and was not able to complete it'?

**14:31** Or what king, about to go wage war against another king, does not first sit down and consider whether he is able with ten thousand to meet the one who comes against him with twenty thousand?

**14:32** Otherwise, while the other is still far away, sending a delegation, he asks for the things that pertain to peace.

**14:33** In the same way, therefore, every one of you who does not renounce all that he possesses cannot be My disciple.

**14:34** Salt is good; but if salt has lost its flavor, with what will it be seasoned?

**14:35** It is useful neither for the soil nor for the manure pile; instead, it will be thrown outside. The one who has ears to hear, let him hear."

## Translator Notes

- **14:5** — The Vulgate reads asinus, aut bos ("a donkey or an ox"), whereas critical Greek editions generally read "a son or an ox."
- **14:10** — The Vulgate omits "all" before "those reclining together," whereas most Greek manuscripts read "all who sit at table with you."
- **14:24** — The Vulgate reads Dico autem vobis ("And I tell you," plural) rather than the Greek explanatory "For I tell you"; while continuing the host's declaration about "my dinner," the plural pronoun also reaches Jesus's own hearers.
- **14:28** — The Vulgate expands the Greek "the cost" into sumptus, qui necessarii sunt ("the expenses that are necessary").
