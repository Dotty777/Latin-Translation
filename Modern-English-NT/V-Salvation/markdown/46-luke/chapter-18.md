## Text

**18:1** Now He was also speaking a parable to them, that it is necessary to pray always and not grow faint,

**18:2** saying, "A certain judge was in a certain city who did not fear God and did not respect man.

**18:3** And a certain widow was in that city, and she kept coming to him, saying, 'Vindicate me against my adversary.'

**18:4** And he was unwilling for a long time. After these things, however, he said within himself, 'Even though I do not fear God, nor do I respect man,

**18:5** yet because this widow is bothersome to me, I will vindicate her, so that in the end she does not come and bruise me.'"

**18:6** And the Lord said, "Hear what the judge of iniquity says.

**18:7** Now will God not carry out the vindication of His chosen ones who cry out to Him day and night, and will He have patience with them?

**18:8** I tell you that He will quickly carry out their vindication. Nevertheless, when the Son of Man comes, do you think He will find faith on the earth?"

**18:9** Now He also spoke this parable to certain ones who trusted in themselves as righteous and scorned the rest:

**18:10** "Two men ascended into the temple to pray: one was a Pharisee and the other a tax collector.

**18:11** Standing, the Pharisee was praying these things to himself, 'God, I give thanks to You because I am not like the rest of men: robbers, the unjust, adulterers, or even like this tax collector.

**18:12** I fast twice in the week; I give tithes of all things that I possess.'

**18:13** And the tax collector, standing far off, was unwilling even to lift his eyes toward heaven, but kept striking his chest, saying, 'God, be gracious to me, a sinner.'

**18:14** I tell you, this man went down into his house justified rather than that one; because everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and the one who humbles himself will be exalted."

**18:15** Now they were also bringing infants to Him so that He might touch them. When the disciples saw this, they were rebuking them.

**18:16** But Jesus, calling them together, said, "Allow the children to come to Me, and do not forbid them, for the kingdom of God belongs to such ones.

**18:17** Amen, I tell you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God like a child will not enter into it."

**18:18** And a certain ruler questioned Him, saying, "Good Teacher, by doing what will I possess eternal life?"

**18:19** But Jesus said to him, "Why do you call Me good? There is no one good except God alone.

**18:20** You know the commandments: you shall not kill; you shall not commit adultery; you shall not commit theft; you shall not speak false testimony; honor your father and mother."

**18:21** He said, "I have observed all these things from my youth."

**18:22** Upon hearing this, Jesus said to him, "Still one thing is lacking to you: sell everything that you have and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow Me."

**18:23** When he had heard these things, he was saddened, because he was exceedingly rich.

**18:24** Now Jesus, seeing that he had become sad, said, "With what difficulty will those who have wealth enter into the kingdom of God!

**18:25** For it is easier for a camel to pass through a needle's eye than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God."

**18:26** And those who were listening said, "And who can be saved?"

**18:27** He said to them, "Things that are impossible with human beings are possible with God."

**18:28** Now Peter said, "Look, we have left all things and have followed You."

**18:29** And He said to them, "Amen, I tell you, there is no one who has left house, or parents, or brothers, or wife, or children on account of the kingdom of God,

**18:30** and will not receive much more in this time, and in the coming age eternal life."

**18:31** Now Jesus took the Twelve and said to them, "Look, we are going up to Jerusalem, and all things that have been written through the prophets concerning the Son of Man will be accomplished.

**18:32** For He will be handed over to the Gentiles, and mocked, and flogged, and spat upon;

**18:33** and after they have flogged Him, they will kill Him, and on the third day He will rise again."

**18:34** And they themselves understood none of these things, and this word was hidden from them, and they did not understand what was being said.

**18:35** Now it happened, as He was approaching Jericho, that a certain blind man was sitting beside the road, begging.

**18:36** And when he heard a crowd passing by, he was asking what this was.

**18:37** And they told him that Jesus the Nazarene was passing by.

**18:38** And he cried out, saying, "Jesus, Son of David, show mercy to me."

**18:39** And those who were going ahead were rebuking him so that he would be silent. Yet he himself kept crying out much more, "Son of David, show mercy to me."

**18:40** Now Jesus, standing still, ordered him to be brought to Himself. And when he had approached, He questioned him,

**18:41** saying, "What do you want Me to do for you?" And he said, "Lord, that I may see."

**18:42** And Jesus said to him, "See again; your faith has saved you."

**18:43** And immediately he saw, and he was following Him, magnifying God. And as soon as all the people saw this, they gave praise to God.

## Translator Notes

- **18:5** — Latin sugillet literally means "bruise" or "beat black and blue," and figuratively "harass" or "wear down."
- **18:7** — The Vulgate has the future tense "and will He have patience with them?" (et patientiam habebit in illis?), which can refer either to God's forbearance toward His chosen ones or to His apparent delay in their case.
- **18:8** — The Vulgate includes the parenthetical verb putas ("do you think"), rendering the Greek emphatic interrogative particle explicitly.
- **18:20** — The Clementine Vulgate places "you shall not kill" (non occides) before "you shall not commit adultery" (non moechaberis) and uses future indicatives, whereas the Greek text of Luke reverses those two commandments.
- **18:32** — The Clementine Vulgate reads flagellabitur ("flogged") where the Greek text has "mistreated" or "insulted," anticipating the repeated verb flagellaverint in verse 33.
