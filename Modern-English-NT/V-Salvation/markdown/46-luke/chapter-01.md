## Text

**1:1** Since indeed many have attempted to put in order an account of the things that have been completed among us,

**1:2** just as those who from the beginning saw for themselves and were servants of the word handed them down to us,

**1:3** it has seemed right to me also, having carefully traced all things from the first, to write to you in order, most excellent Theophilus,

**1:4** so that you may know the truth of those words about which you have been instructed.

**1:5** In the days of Herod, king of Judea, there was a certain priest by the name of Zechariah, from the division of Abijah, and his wife was from the daughters of Aaron, and her name was Elizabeth.

**1:6** Now both of them were righteous before God, walking in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord without reproach.

**1:7** And they had no son, because Elizabeth was barren, and both of them were advanced in their days.

**1:8** Now it happened that while he was performing the priestly office in the order of his division before God,

**1:9** according to the custom of the priesthood, it fell to him by lot, upon entering the temple of the Lord, to offer incense.

**1:10** And the entire multitude of the people was praying outside at the hour of incense.

**1:11** Now an angel of the Lord appeared to him, standing at the right side of the altar of incense.

**1:12** And Zechariah was troubled at the sight, and fear rushed upon him.

**1:13** But the angel spoke to him, "Do not be afraid, Zechariah, because your supplication has been heard, and your wife Elizabeth will bear a son for you, and you will call his name John.

**1:14** And there will be joy and exultation for you, and many will rejoice at his birth.

**1:15** For he will be great in the presence of the Lord, and he will not drink wine or strong drink, and he will be filled with the Holy Spirit even from his mother's womb.

**1:16** And he will turn many of the sons of Israel to the Lord their God,

**1:17** and he himself will go ahead before Him in the spirit and power of Elijah, so that he may turn the hearts of fathers to their children, and the unbelieving to the prudence of the righteous, to prepare for the Lord a perfected people."

**1:18** And Zechariah said to the angel, "How am I to know this? For I am an old man, and my wife has advanced in her days."

**1:19** And answering, the angel said to him, "I am Gabriel, who stand before God, and I was sent to speak to you and to proclaim this good news to you.

**1:20** And look, you will be silent and will not be able to speak until the day on which these things happen, because you did not believe my words, which will be fulfilled in their own time."

**1:21** And the people were waiting for Zechariah, and they were marveling that he was delaying in the temple.

**1:22** But upon coming out, he was not able to speak to them, and they recognized that he had seen a vision in the temple. And he himself was signaling to them, and he remained mute.

**1:23** And it happened that when the days of his service were completed, he went away to his house.

**1:24** Now after these days, Elizabeth his wife conceived, and she kept herself hidden for five months, saying,

**1:25** "So the Lord has done for me in the days in which He looked with favor to remove my disgrace among people."

**1:26** Now in the sixth month, the angel Gabriel was sent by God into a city of Galilee whose name was Nazareth,

**1:27** to a virgin betrothed to a man whose name was Joseph, from the house of David; and the name of the virgin was Mary.

**1:28** And entering in to her, the angel said, "Greetings, full of grace; the Lord is with you. Blessed are you among women."

**1:29** And when she had heard this, she was troubled at his word, and she was reflecting on what kind of greeting this was.

**1:30** And the angel spoke to her, "Do not be afraid, Mary, for you have found grace with God.

**1:31** Look, you will conceive in your womb and bear a son, and you will call His name Jesus.

**1:32** He will be great and will be called Son of the Most High, and the Lord God will give to Him the throne of David His father, and He will reign in the house of Jacob forever,

**1:33** and there will be no end to His kingdom."

**1:34** Now Mary said to the angel, "How will this happen, since I do not know a man?"

**1:35** And answering, the angel said to her, "The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. Therefore also the Holy One who will be born from you will be called the Son of God.

**1:36** And look, Elizabeth your relative, she also has conceived a son in her old age, and this is the sixth month for her who is called barren,

**1:37** for no word will be impossible with God."

**1:38** Then Mary said, "Here is the maidservant of the Lord; may it happen to me according to your word." And the angel withdrew from her.

**1:39** Now arising in those days, Mary went away with haste into the hill country, to a city of Judah,

**1:40** and she entered into Zechariah's house and greeted Elizabeth.

**1:41** And it happened that when Elizabeth heard the greeting of Mary, the infant leaped in her womb, and Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit,

**1:42** and she exclaimed in a loud voice and said, "Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb.

**1:43** And how does this fall to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?

**1:44** For look, as soon as the voice of your greeting came to my ears, the infant in my womb leaped in joy.

**1:45** And blessed are you who have believed, because the things that were spoken to you by the Lord will be brought to completion."

**1:46** And Mary spoke, "My soul magnifies the Lord,

**1:47** and my spirit has exulted in God my Savior.

**1:48** For He has looked upon the lowliness of His maidservant; for look, from this time on all generations will call me blessed,

**1:49** because the One who is mighty has done great things for me, and His name is holy,

**1:50** and His mercy is from generation to generations for those who fear Him.

**1:51** He has displayed might with His arm; He has scattered the proud in the thought of their heart.

**1:52** He has brought down the mighty from the throne and has exalted the lowly.

**1:53** He has filled those who hunger with good things, and He has sent the rich away empty.

**1:54** He has upheld Israel His servant, remembering His mercy,

**1:55** just as He spoke to our fathers, to Abraham and to his seed forever."

**1:56** Now Mary stayed with her for about three months, and she returned to her own house.

**1:57** Now for Elizabeth the time of giving birth was fulfilled, and she bore a son.

**1:58** And her neighbors and relatives heard that the Lord had magnified His mercy with her, and they were rejoicing with her.

**1:59** And it happened on the eighth day that they came to circumcise the child, and they were calling him by the name of his father, Zechariah.

**1:60** And answering, his mother said, "Not at all, but he will be called John."

**1:61** And they said to her, "There is no one among your kin who is called by this name."

**1:62** Now they were signaling to his father what he wanted him to be called.

**1:63** And asking for a writing tablet, he wrote, saying, "John is his name." And all of them marveled.

**1:64** Now at once his mouth was opened and his tongue released, and he was speaking, blessing God.

**1:65** And fear came upon all their neighbors, and throughout all the hill country of Judea all these words were being spread abroad;

**1:66** and all who had heard them placed them in their heart, saying, "Who, do you think, will this child be?" For indeed the hand of the Lord was with him.

**1:67** And Zechariah his father was filled with the Holy Spirit, and he prophesied, saying,

**1:68** "Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, because He has visited and accomplished the redemption of His people,

**1:69** and He has lifted up for us a horn of salvation in the house of David His servant,

**1:70** just as He spoke through the mouth of His holy prophets who have been from ancient times:

**1:71** salvation from our enemies and out of the hand of all those who hate us,

**1:72** to show mercy with our fathers and to be mindful of His holy covenant,

**1:73** the oath that He swore to Abraham our father, that He would give to us,

**1:74** that without fear, freed from the hand of our enemies, we may serve Him

**1:75** in holiness and righteousness in His presence, all our days.

**1:76** And you, child, will be called a prophet of the Most High, for you will go ahead before the face of the Lord to prepare His ways,

**1:77** to give His people knowledge of salvation, for the forgiveness of their sins,

**1:78** through the depths of the mercy of our God, in which the Dawn from on high has visited us,

**1:79** to illuminate those who sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, to direct our feet into the way of peace."

**1:80** Now the child was growing and being strengthened in spirit, and he was in the deserts until the day of his appearing to Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **1:17** — The Vulgate renders Greek 'disobedient' as incredulos ('the unbelieving') and 'a prepared people' as plebem perfectam ('a perfected people').
- **1:28** — The Clementine Vulgate translates Greek kecharitomene as gratia plena ('full of grace,' echoing gratiam in 1:30) and includes angelus ('the angel') and benedicta tu in mulieribus ('Blessed are you among women'), phrases omitted in critical Greek editions.
- **1:35** — The Clementine Vulgate includes ex te ('from you'), absent in critical Greek editions, and places the neuter subject quod nascetur ex te sanctum (lit. 'what will be born holy from you') before the predicate 'will be called the Son of God.'
- **1:37** — Literally 'every word will not be impossible with God,' a biblical universal negation meaning 'no word'; Latin verbum ('word,' reflecting Greek rhema) links directly to Mary's 'your word' (verbum tuum) in 1:38.
- **1:45** — Unlike Greek manuscripts that use the third person ('she who believed... spoken to her'), the Clementine Vulgate uses the second person (credidisti... tibi, 'you who have believed... spoken to you').
- **1:64** — The Latin uses a zeugma with a single verb (Apertum est... os ejus, et lingua ejus, lit. 'his mouth was opened, and his tongue'), smoothed here with 'released.'
- **1:78** — Latin viscera misericordiæ literally means 'the inward parts/compassion of the mercy,' and the Clementine Vulgate reads the perfect tense visitavit ('has visited') where critical Greek editions read the future ('will visit'). Latin oriens ('rising,' 'dawn,' 'east') translates Greek anatole.
