## Text

**10:1** After these things, the Lord also designated seventy-two others and sent them in pairs ahead of Him into every city and place where He Himself was about to come.

**10:2** And He was saying to them, "The harvest indeed is abundant, but the workers are few. Ask the Lord of the harvest, therefore, to send workers into His harvest.

**10:3** Go; look, I am sending you like lambs among wolves.

**10:4** Do not carry a purse, nor a knapsack, nor sandals, and greet no one along the road.

**10:5** Into whatever house you enter, first say, 'Peace to this house.'

**10:6** And if a son of peace is there, your peace will rest on him; if not, it will return to you.

**10:7** Remain in that same house, eating and drinking what they have, for the worker is worthy of his wage. Do not move from house to house.

**10:8** And into whatever city you enter, and they receive you, eat what is placed before you,

**10:9** and cure the sick who are in it, and say to them, 'The kingdom of God has drawn near to you.'

**10:10** But into whatever city you enter, and they do not receive you, going out into its streets, say,

**10:11** 'Even the dust that clung to us from your city we wipe off against you; yet know this, that the kingdom of God has drawn near.'

**10:12** I tell you that for Sodom on that day it will be more bearable than for that city.

**10:13** Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! For if the deeds of power that were done in you had been done in Tyre and Sidon, long ago they would have repented, sitting in sackcloth and ashes.

**10:14** Nevertheless, for Tyre and Sidon it will be more bearable in the judgment than for you.

**10:15** And you, Capernaum, exalted up to heaven, will be plunged down to the underworld.

**10:16** The one who hears you hears Me, and the one who despises you despises Me; and the one who despises Me despises Him who sent Me."

**10:17** Now the seventy-two returned with joy, saying, "Lord, even demons submit to us in Your name."

**10:18** And He said to them, "I was watching Satan falling like lightning from heaven.

**10:19** Look, I have given you authority to trample upon serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, and nothing will harm you.

**10:20** Nevertheless, do not rejoice in this, that the spirits submit to you; rejoice instead that your names are written in heaven."

**10:21** At that very hour He exulted in the Holy Spirit and said, "I praise You, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because You have hidden these things from the wise and discerning and revealed them to little ones. Yes, Father, for so it was pleasing before You.

**10:22** All things have been delivered to Me by My Father. And no one knows who the Son is except the Father, and who the Father is except the Son and the one to whom the Son desires to reveal Him."

**10:23** And turning to His disciples, He said, "Blessed are the eyes that see the things you see.

**10:24** For I tell you that many prophets and kings desired to see the things you see, and did not see them, and to hear the things you hear, and did not hear them."

**10:25** And look, a certain expert in the law stood up, testing Him and saying, "Teacher, by doing what will I possess eternal life?"

**10:26** But He said to him, "In the law, what is written? How do you read it?"

**10:27** He answered and said, "You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with all your powers, and with all your mind, and your neighbor as yourself."

**10:28** And He said to him, "You have answered rightly; do this, and you will live."

**10:29** But wanting to justify himself, he said to Jesus, "And who is my neighbor?"

**10:30** Replying, Jesus said, "A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho and fell among robbers, who also stripped him and, after inflicting blows, went away, leaving him half dead.

**10:31** Now it happened that a certain priest was going down that same road, and upon seeing him, he passed by.

**10:32** Likewise a Levite also, when he was beside the place and saw him, passed on by.

**10:33** But a certain Samaritan who was traveling came beside him, and seeing him, he was moved with compassion.

**10:34** And approaching, he bandaged his wounds, pouring in oil and wine; and setting him on his own mount, he brought him to an inn and took care of him.

**10:35** And on the next day he brought out two denarii and gave them to the innkeeper, and said, 'Take care of him, and whatever more you spend, I myself will repay you when I return.'

**10:36** Which of these three seems to you to have been a neighbor to the one who fell among the robbers?"

**10:37** But he said, "The one who showed mercy to him." And Jesus said to him, "Go, and you do likewise."

**10:38** Now it happened, while they were going, that He Himself entered a certain village; and a certain woman named Martha welcomed Him into her house.

**10:39** And she had a sister named Mary, who also, sitting beside the Lord's feet, was listening to His word.

**10:40** But Martha was busying herself about continual serving; and she stood and said, "Lord, is it of no concern to You that my sister has left me to serve alone? Tell her, therefore, to help me."

**10:41** And the Lord answered and said to her, "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about very many things,

**10:42** yet one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the best part, which will not be taken away from her."

## Translator Notes

- **10:11** — The Clementine Vulgate reads simply 'that clung to us' (qui adhaesit nobis), without the phrase 'to our feet' found in Greek manuscripts.
- **10:15** — Where critical Greek editions read a question ('will you be exalted to heaven?'), the Vulgate has the participle exaltata ('exalted up to heaven'), followed by 'plunged down to the underworld' (usque ad infernum demergeris).
- **10:27** — Where Greek manuscripts use the singular 'strength,' the Vulgate renders the term in the plural as 'with all your powers' (ex omnibus virtutibus tuis).
- **10:31** — Both here (praeterivit) and in 10:32 (pertransiit), the Vulgate states simply that the priest and the Levite 'passed by' without adding 'on the other side.'
- **10:42** — The Vulgate uses the superlative 'the best part' (optimam partem), whereas the Greek text has the positive adjective 'the good part.'
