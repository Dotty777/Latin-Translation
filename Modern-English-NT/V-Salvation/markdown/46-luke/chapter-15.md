## Text

**15:1** Now tax collectors and sinners were drawing near to Him in order to hear Him.

**15:2** And the Pharisees and scribes were murmuring, saying, "This man welcomes sinners and eats with them."

**15:3** And He spoke this parable to them, saying,

**15:4** "What man among you, who has one hundred sheep, and if he loses one of them, does not leave the ninety-nine in the wilderness and go after the one that was lost until he finds it?

**15:5** And when he finds it, he places it on his shoulders, rejoicing,

**15:6** and coming home, he calls together his friends and neighbors, saying to them, 'Rejoice with me, because I have found my sheep that was lost.'

**15:7** I tell you that in this way there will be joy in heaven over one sinner who repents, rather than over ninety-nine righteous ones who do not need repentance.

**15:8** Or what woman, having ten drachmas, if she loses one drachma, does not light a lamp and sweep the house and search diligently until she finds it?

**15:9** And when she finds it, she calls together her friends and neighbors, saying, 'Rejoice with me, because I have found the drachma that I had lost.'

**15:10** In this way, I tell you, there will be joy before the angels of God over one sinner who repents."

**15:11** Then He said, "A certain man had two sons,

**15:12** and the younger of them said to his father, 'Father, give me the portion of the property that falls to me.' And he divided the property between them.

**15:13** And not many days later, having gathered everything together, the younger son traveled abroad into a distant region, and there he squandered his property by living dissolutely.

**15:14** And after he had spent everything, a severe famine arose in that region, and he himself began to be in need.

**15:15** And he went and attached himself to one of the citizens of that region, and he sent him to his farm to feed pigs.

**15:16** And he longed to fill his belly with the pods that the pigs were eating, and no one gave him any.

**15:17** Then coming back to himself, he said, 'How many hired workers in my father's house have an abundance of bread, while I am perishing here from hunger!

**15:18** I will rise and go to my father, and say to him, "Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you.

**15:19** I am no longer worthy to be called your son; make me like one of your hired workers."'

**15:20** And rising, he came to his father. Now while he was still far off, his father saw him and was moved with compassion, and running up, he fell on his neck and kissed him.

**15:21** And the son said to him, 'Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son.'

**15:22** But the father said to his servants, 'Quickly bring out the finest robe and clothe him in it, and put a ring on his hand and sandals on his feet;

**15:23** and bring the fattened calf and slaughter it, and let us eat and feast,

**15:24** because this son of mine was dead and has come back to life; he was lost and has been found.' And they began to feast.

**15:25** Now his older son was in the field, and as he was coming and drawing near to the house, he heard music and dancing.

**15:26** And he called one of the servants and asked what these things were.

**15:27** And he said to him, 'Your brother has come, and your father has slaughtered the fattened calf, because he has received him back safe.'

**15:28** But he became indignant and was unwilling to go in. Therefore his father came out and began to plead with him.

**15:29** But answering, he said to his father, 'Look, for so many years I have been serving you, and I have never disregarded your command, and yet you have never given me a young goat so that I might feast with my friends.

**15:30** But after this son of yours came, who devoured his property with prostitutes, you slaughtered the fattened calf for him.'

**15:31** And he said to him, 'Son, you are always with me, and everything that is mine is yours;

**15:32** yet it was fitting to feast and rejoice, because this brother of yours was dead and has come back to life; he was lost and has been found.'"

## Translator Notes

- **15:1** — The Clementine Vulgate lacks "all" (Greek pantes) before "tax collectors and sinners."
- **15:7** — Latin ita ... quam preserves an elliptical comparative without an explicit "more." Poenitentiam agente (literally "doing penance," also in 15:10 and 16:30) is the Vulgate idiom for repentance and is rendered "repents" in keeping with the other Gospel occurrences.
- **15:10** — The Vulgate uses the future tense "erit" ("there will be joy," matching 15:7) rather than the Greek present tense ("there is joy").
- **15:30** — The Vulgate reads "substantiam suam" ("his property," referring to the younger son's divided share), whereas Greek manuscripts read "your livelihood/property."
