## Text

**13:1** Now some people were present at that very time, reporting to Him about the Galileans whose blood Pilate mixed with their sacrifices.

**13:2** And answering, He said to them, "Do you think that these Galileans were sinners beyond all Galileans because they suffered such things?

**13:3** No, I tell you; but unless you have repentance, you will all perish in the same way.

**13:4** Like those eighteen on whom the tower in Siloam fell and killed them, do you think that they too were debtors beyond all the people living in Jerusalem?

**13:5** No, I tell you; but if you do not practice repentance, you will all perish in the same way."

**13:6** Now He was also telling this parable: "A certain man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard, and he came looking for fruit on it and found none.

**13:7** So he said to the keeper of the vineyard, 'Look, it is three years now that I have been coming looking for fruit on this fig tree, and I find none. Cut it down, therefore; why does it even occupy the ground?'

**13:8** But answering, he says to him, 'Sir, leave it alone this year as well, until I dig around it and put down manure,

**13:9** and if indeed it bears fruit—but if not, in the future you will cut it down.'"

**13:10** Now He was teaching in their synagogue on the Sabbaths.

**13:11** And look, there was a woman who had a spirit of infirmity for eighteen years, and she was bent over and could not look upward at all.

**13:12** When Jesus saw her, He called her to Himself and said to her, "Woman, you are released from your infirmity."

**13:13** And He placed His hands on her, and immediately she was straightened up and was glorifying God.

**13:14** But answering, the synagogue leader, indignant because Jesus had healed on the Sabbath, was saying to the crowd, "There are six days in which one ought to work; therefore come on those days and be healed, and not on the Sabbath day."

**13:15** But the Lord, answering him, said, "Hypocrites! Does not each one of you untie his ox or donkey from the manger on the Sabbath and lead it to water?

**13:16** And ought not this daughter of Abraham, whom Satan bound—look, for eighteen years—to be untied from this bond on the Sabbath day?"

**13:17** And as He was saying these things, all His adversaries were ashamed, and all the people were rejoicing over all the things that were gloriously being done by Him.

**13:18** Therefore He was saying, "What is the kingdom of God like, and what will I reckon it to be like?

**13:19** It is like a grain of mustard seed that a man took and put into his garden, and it grew and turned into a large tree, and the birds of the sky rested in its branches."

**13:20** And again He said, "What will I reckon the kingdom of God to be like?

**13:21** It is like leaven that a woman took and hid in three measures of flour, until all of it was leavened."

**13:22** And He was going through cities and villages, teaching and making His journey to Jerusalem.

**13:23** Now someone said to Him, "Lord, are there few who are being saved?" And He said to them,

**13:24** "Strive to enter through the narrow gate, because many, I tell you, will seek to enter and will not be able.

**13:25** Once the master of the house has entered and closed the door, you will begin to stand outside and knock on the door, saying, 'Lord, open for us,' and answering, He will say to you, 'I do not know you, where you are from.'

**13:26** Then you will begin to say, 'We ate and drank in Your presence, and You taught in our streets.'

**13:27** And He will say to you, 'I do not know you, where you are from; depart from Me, all you workers of unrighteousness.'

**13:28** In that place there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth, when you see Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and all the prophets in the kingdom of God, and yourselves being driven outside.

**13:29** And they will come from the east and the west, and the north and the south, and will recline at table in the kingdom of God.

**13:30** And look, there are last ones who will be first, and there are first ones who will be last."

**13:31** On that very day, some of the Pharisees came up, saying to Him, "Leave and go away from here, because Herod wants to kill You."

**13:32** And He said to them, "Go and tell that fox, 'Look, I drive out demons and perform healings today and tomorrow, and on the third day I am brought to completion.'

**13:33** Nevertheless, I must journey today and tomorrow and the following day, because it is not possible for a prophet to perish outside Jerusalem.

**13:34** Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets and stones those who are sent to you, how often did I want to gather your children, just as a bird gathers her brood under her wings, and you were unwilling?

**13:35** Look, your house will be left desolate to you. And I tell you that you will not see Me until the time comes when you will say, 'Blessed is the One who comes in the name of the Lord.'"

## Translator Notes

- **13:3** — The Vulgate shifts from poenitentiam habueritis ("have repentance") here to poenitentiam non egeritis ("do not practice repentance," traditionally "do penance") in verse 5, varying its rendering of the Greek verb for repenting.
- **13:9** — The Latin preserves an abrupt break in syntax (aposiopesis) after "if indeed it bears fruit" without supplying an implied "well," and places in futurum ("in the future") clearly with the second clause ("you will cut it down").
- **13:10** — The Vulgate reads in synagoga eorum ("in their synagogue"), whereas the Greek text reads "in one of the synagogues."
- **13:25** — The Vulgate reads intraverit ("has entered") rather than the Greek "has risen up."
- **13:31** — The Vulgate reads In ipsa die ("On that very day"), whereas the Greek text reads "In that very hour."
