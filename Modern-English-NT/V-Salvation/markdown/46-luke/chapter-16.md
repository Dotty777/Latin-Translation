## Text

**16:1** Now He was also saying to His disciples, "There was a certain rich man who had a steward, and this man was accused before him as though he had squandered his possessions.

**16:2** And he called him and said to him, 'What is this that I hear about you? Render an account of your stewardship, for you can no longer act as steward.'

**16:3** Then the steward said within himself, 'What am I to do, since my master is taking the stewardship away from me? I am not strong enough to dig; I am ashamed to beg.

**16:4** I know what I will do, so that when I am removed from the stewardship, they may receive me into their houses.'

**16:5** So having called together his master's debtors one by one, he said to the first, 'How much do you owe my master?'

**16:6** And he said, 'One hundred jars of oil.' And he said to him, 'Take your bond, and sit down quickly; write fifty.'

**16:7** Then he said to another, 'And you, how much do you owe?' And he said, 'One hundred cors of wheat.' He said to him, 'Take your document and write eighty.'

**16:8** And the master praised the unjust steward because he had acted prudently; for the sons of this age are more prudent than the sons of light in their own generation.

**16:9** And I say to you, make friends for yourselves from the mammon of injustice, so that when you fail, they may receive you into the eternal dwellings.

**16:10** The one who is faithful in what is least is also faithful in what is greater, and the one who is unjust in what is small is also unjust in what is greater.

**16:11** If therefore you have not been faithful in unjust mammon, who will entrust to you what is true?

**16:12** And if you have not been faithful in what belongs to another, who will give you what is yours?

**16:13** No servant can serve two masters; for either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will cling to the one and scorn the other. You cannot serve God and mammon."

**16:14** Now the Pharisees, who were greedy, were hearing all these things, and they were mocking Him.

**16:15** And He said to them, "You are the ones who justify yourselves before people, yet God knows your hearts; for what is lofty among people is an abomination before God.

**16:16** The Law and the Prophets were until John; from that time the kingdom of God is proclaimed as good news, and everyone presses forcefully into it.

**16:17** Yet it is easier for heaven and earth to pass away than for one pen-stroke from the Law to fall.

**16:18** Everyone who divorces his wife and marries another commits adultery, and the one who marries a woman divorced by her husband commits adultery.

**16:19** There was a certain rich man who was clothed in purple and fine linen and feasted splendidly every day.

**16:20** And there was a certain beggar named Lazarus, who lay at his gate, full of sores,

**16:21** longing to be satisfied with the crumbs that fell from the rich man's table, and no one gave him any; moreover, even the dogs came and licked his sores.

**16:22** Now it happened that the beggar died and was carried by the angels into Abraham's bosom. And the rich man also died and was buried in hell.

**16:23** And lifting up his eyes while he was in torments, he saw Abraham from a distance and Lazarus in his bosom.

**16:24** And crying out, he said, 'Father Abraham, have mercy on me and send Lazarus to dip the tip of his finger in water so that he may cool my tongue, because I am tormented in this flame.'

**16:25** And Abraham said to him, 'Son, remember that you received good things in your lifetime, and Lazarus likewise bad things; yet now he is comforted, whereas you are tormented;

**16:26** and besides all these things, a great chasm has been fixed between us and you, so that those who wish to pass from here to you cannot, nor cross over from there to here.'

**16:27** And he said, 'Then I beg you, father, to send him to my father's house,

**16:28** for I have five brothers, to testify to them so that they too may not come into this place of torments.'

**16:29** And Abraham said to him, 'They have Moses and the Prophets; let them listen to them.'

**16:30** But he said, 'No, father Abraham, yet if someone from the dead goes to them, they will repent.'

**16:31** Then he said to him, 'If they do not listen to Moses and the Prophets, neither will they believe if someone rises from the dead.'"

## Translator Notes

- **16:6** — Latin "cados" ("jars," rendering the Hebrew liquid measure bath, roughly eight to nine gallons) and "coros" in 16:7 ("cors," a dry measure of roughly ten to twelve bushels) represent substantial commercial debts.
- **16:9** — The Vulgate reads the second-person plural "cum defeceritis" ("when you fail," meaning when your life comes to an end), whereas modern critical Greek editions read third-person singular ("when it [wealth] fails"). Latin "mammona" (also in 16:11, 13) transliterates the Aramaic term for worldly wealth.
- **16:21** — The clause "et nemo illi dabat" ("and no one gave him any") is a distinctive Vulgate reading echoing 15:16 and is absent from Greek manuscripts.
- **16:22** — The Clementine Vulgate attaches "in inferno" ("in hell," rendering Greek hades, the underworld or realm of the dead) to "was buried" at the end of verse 22, whereas the Greek text places it at the beginning of verse 23.
- **16:25** — Latin "hic" can be read either as the pronoun "he/this man" (contrasting directly with "tu," "you") or as the adverb "here" (matching Greek hode); in 16:31, Vulgate "credent" ("will they believe") differs from Greek "will they be persuaded."
