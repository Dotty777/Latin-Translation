## Text

**5:1** Now it happened, when the crowds pressed upon Him to hear the word of God, that He Himself was standing beside the Lake of Gennesaret.

**5:2** And He saw two boats standing beside the lake, but the fishermen had disembarked and were washing the nets.

**5:3** Now boarding one boat, which was Simon's, He asked him to push out a little from the land. And sitting down, He was teaching the crowds from the boat.

**5:4** Now when He stopped speaking, He said to Simon, "Steer into the deep, and lower your nets for a catch."

**5:5** And answering, Simon said to Him, "Master, laboring through the whole night we caught nothing; yet at Your word I will lower the net."

**5:6** And when they had done this, they enclosed an abundant multitude of fish, and their net was tearing.

**5:7** And they signaled to the partners who were in the other boat to come and help them. And they came and filled both boats, so that they were nearly sinking.

**5:8** When Simon Peter saw this, he fell down at the knees of Jesus, saying, "Go away from me, because I am a sinful man, Lord."

**5:9** For astonishment had enveloped him, and all who were with him, at the catch of fish that they had caught;

**5:10** and likewise James and John, the sons of Zebedee, who were partners of Simon. And Jesus said to Simon, "Do not be afraid; from this time on you will be catching people."

**5:11** And when they had brought the boats to land, having left everything, they followed Him.

**5:12** And it happened, while He was in one of the cities, look, there was a man full of leprosy; and seeing Jesus and falling down on his face, he begged Him, saying, "Lord, if You are willing, You can cleanse me."

**5:13** And extending His hand, He touched him, saying, "I am willing; be cleansed." And at once the leprosy departed from him.

**5:14** And He Himself commanded him not to tell anyone, "But go, show yourself to the priest, and present an offering for your cleansing, just as Moses commanded, for a testimony to them."

**5:15** Yet the report about Him was circulating all the more, and large crowds were coming together to hear and to be cured of their illnesses.

**5:16** But He Himself would withdraw into the wilderness and pray.

**5:17** And it happened on one of the days that He Himself was sitting, teaching. And Pharisees were sitting there, and teachers of the law, who had come from every village of Galilee and Judea, and from Jerusalem; and the power of the Lord was present to heal them.

**5:18** And look, men were carrying on a bed a man who was paralyzed, and they were seeking to bring him in and place him before Him.

**5:19** And not finding by what way they might bring him in because of the crowd, they went up onto the roof, and through the tiles they lowered him with the bed into the middle before Jesus.

**5:20** When He saw their faith, He said, "Man, your sins are forgiven you."

**5:21** And the scribes and Pharisees began to think, saying, "Who is this who speaks blasphemies? Who is able to forgive sins, except God alone?"

**5:22** Now when Jesus knew their thoughts, answering, He said to them, "What are you thinking in your hearts?

**5:23** What is easier to say, 'Sins are forgiven you,' or to say, 'Rise and walk'?

**5:24** But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins," He said to the paralyzed man, "I say to you, rise, take up your bed, and go to your house."

**5:25** And at once rising up before them, he took up the bed on which he was lying, and went away to his house, magnifying God.

**5:26** And astonishment seized everyone, and they were magnifying God. And they were filled with fear, saying, "We have seen marvelous things today."

**5:27** And after these things He went out and saw a tax collector by the name of Levi, sitting at the tax office, and He said to him, "Follow Me."

**5:28** And having left everything, he rose up and followed Him.

**5:29** And Levi made a great banquet for Him in his house; and there was a large crowd of tax collectors and others who were reclining at table with them.

**5:30** And the Pharisees and their scribes were grumbling, saying to His disciples, "Why are you eating and drinking with tax collectors and sinners?"

**5:31** And answering, Jesus said to them, "Those who are healthy do not need a physician, but those who are unwell.

**5:32** I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance."

**5:33** But they said to Him, "Why do the disciples of John fast frequently and make supplications, and likewise those of the Pharisees, yet Yours eat and drink?"

**5:34** To them He Himself said, "Can you make the sons of the bridegroom fast while the bridegroom is with them?

**5:35** Yet the days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them; then they will fast in those days."

**5:36** Now He was also telling a parable to them: "No one puts a patch from a new garment onto an old garment; otherwise he tears the new one as well, and the patch from the new does not match the old.

**5:37** And no one puts new wine into old wineskins; otherwise the new wine will burst the wineskins, and it will be poured out, and the wineskins will be destroyed;

**5:38** but new wine must be put into new wineskins, and both are preserved.

**5:39** And no one drinking the old immediately wants the new, for he says, 'The old is better.'"

## Translator Notes

- **5:5** — The Clementine Vulgate reads singular rete ("the net") here and rete eorum ("their net") in 5:6, contrasting with plural retia vestra ("your nets") in 5:4 and differing from the plural Greek text.
- **5:17** — The Clementine Vulgate reads ad sanandum eos ("to heal them"), reflecting the majority Greek tradition rather than critical Greek editions that read "for him to heal."
- **5:23** — Unlike 5:20 (peccata tua, "your sins"), the Clementine Vulgate here omits tua and reads simply Dimittuntur tibi peccata ("Sins are forgiven you").
- **5:34** — Latin filios sponsi ("the sons of the bridegroom") preserves a literal Semitic idiom for wedding attendants and differs slightly from the Greek wording "sons of the bridal chamber."
- **5:38** — The Clementine Vulgate retains et utraque conservantur ("and both are preserved"), which modern critical Greek editions omit here; likewise, 5:39 preserves statim ("immediately") and comparative melius ("better").
