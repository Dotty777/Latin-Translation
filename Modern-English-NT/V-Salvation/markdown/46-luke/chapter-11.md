## Text

**11:1** And it happened that while He was praying in a certain place, when He ceased, one of His disciples said to Him, "Lord, teach us to pray, just as John also taught his disciples."

**11:2** And He said to them, "When you pray, say, 'Father, may Your name be sanctified. May Your kingdom come.

**11:3** Give us today our daily bread.

**11:4** And forgive us our sins, since we ourselves also forgive everyone who is in debt to us. And do not bring us into temptation.'"

**11:5** And He said to them, "Which of you will have a friend, and will go to him at midnight, and will say to him, 'Friend, lend me three loaves,

**11:6** because a friend of mine has come to me from a journey, and I have nothing to set before him,'

**11:7** and he, answering from inside, should say, 'Do not bother me; the door is already closed, and my children are with me in bed; I cannot rise and give to you'?

**11:8** And if he persists in knocking, I tell you, even though he will not rise and give to him because he is his friend, yet because of his shamelessness he will rise and give him as many as he needs.

**11:9** And I tell you: Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened for you.

**11:10** For everyone who asks receives, and the one who seeks finds, and for the one knocking it will be opened.

**11:11** Now which of you asks a father for bread: will he give him a stone? Or for a fish: will he give him a snake instead of a fish?

**11:12** Or if he asks for an egg, will he hand him a scorpion?

**11:13** If then you, though you are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father from heaven give the good Spirit to those who ask Him?"

**11:14** And He was driving out a demon, and it was mute. And when He had driven out the demon, the mute man spoke, and the crowds marveled.

**11:15** But some of them said, "By Beelzebub, the ruler of the demons, He drives out demons."

**11:16** And others, testing Him, were seeking from Him a sign from heaven.

**11:17** But He, when He saw their thoughts, said to them, "Every kingdom divided against itself will be laid waste, and house will fall upon house.

**11:18** Now if Satan also is divided against himself, how will his kingdom stand? For you say that I drive out demons by Beelzebub.

**11:19** And if I drive out demons by Beelzebub, by whom do your sons drive them out? Therefore they themselves will be your judges.

**11:20** But if by the finger of God I drive out demons, surely the kingdom of God has come upon you.

**11:21** When an armed strong man guards his courtyard, the things that he possesses are in peace.

**11:22** But if one stronger than he comes upon him and conquers him, he will take away all his armor in which he was trusting, and will distribute his spoils.

**11:23** The one who is not with Me is against Me, and the one who does not gather with Me disperses.

**11:24** When an unclean spirit has gone out from a man, it walks through waterless places, seeking rest; and finding none, it says, 'I will return to my house from which I came out.'

**11:25** And when it arrives, it finds it swept clean and adorned.

**11:26** Then it goes and takes along with itself seven other spirits more wicked than itself, and having entered, they dwell there. And the final condition of that man becomes worse than the former."

**11:27** Now it happened, as He was saying these things, that a certain woman from the crowd, raising her voice, said to Him, "Blessed is the womb that carried You, and the breasts that You nursed!"

**11:28** But He said, "Rather, blessed are those who hear the word of God and guard it."

**11:29** Now as the crowds were gathering together, He began to say, "This generation is a wicked generation; it seeks a sign, and no sign will be given to it except the sign of Jonah the prophet.

**11:30** For just as Jonah was a sign to the Ninevites, so also will the Son of Man be to this generation.

**11:31** The queen of the South will rise in the judgment with the men of this generation and will condemn them, because she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon; and look, more than Solomon is here.

**11:32** The men of Nineveh will rise in the judgment with this generation and will condemn it, because they repented at the preaching of Jonah; and look, more than Jonah is here.

**11:33** No one lights a lamp and puts it in a hidden place, nor under a measuring basket, but on a lampstand, so that those who enter may see the light.

**11:34** The lamp of your body is your eye. If your eye is single, your whole body will be bright; but if it is wicked, your body also will be dark.

**11:35** See to it, then, that the light which is in you is not darkness.

**11:36** If therefore your whole body is bright, having no part of darkness, it will be bright as a whole, and like a lamp of brilliance it will illuminate you."

**11:37** And while He was speaking, a certain Pharisee asked Him to dine with him. And having entered, He reclined.

**11:38** Now the Pharisee, reflecting within himself, began to say why He had not washed before the meal.

**11:39** And the Lord said to him, "Now you Pharisees clean what is on the outside of the cup and the dish, but what is inside you is full of robbery and iniquity.

**11:40** Fools! Did not He who made what is on the outside also make that which is on the inside?

**11:41** Nevertheless, give what remains over as alms, and look, all things are clean for you.

**11:42** But woe to you, Pharisees, because you tithe mint and rue and every herb, and pass over justice and the love of God; yet these things you ought to have done, and not omitted those.

**11:43** Woe to you, Pharisees, because you love the foremost seats in the synagogues and greetings in the marketplace.

**11:44** Woe to you, because you are like tombs that are not visible, and people walking over them do not know."

**11:45** Then one of the experts in the law answered and said to Him, "Teacher, in saying these things You also insult us."

**11:46** But He said, "Woe also to you, experts in the law, because you burden people with burdens that they cannot carry, and you yourselves do not touch the loads with one of your fingers.

**11:47** Woe to you who build the tombs of the prophets, yet your fathers killed them.

**11:48** Surely you testify that you consent to the deeds of your fathers, for they indeed killed them, while you build their graves.

**11:49** For this reason the wisdom of God also said, 'I will send prophets and apostles to them, and some of them they will kill and persecute,

**11:50** so that the blood of all the prophets that was shed from the foundation of the world may be demanded from this generation,

**11:51** from the blood of Abel down to the blood of Zechariah, who perished between the altar and the sanctuary.' Yes, I tell you, it will be required from this generation.

**11:52** Woe to you, experts in the law, because you have taken away the key of knowledge; you yourselves did not enter, and you prevented those who were entering."

**11:53** Now while He was saying these things to them, the Pharisees and the experts in the law began to bear down heavily upon Him and to press upon His mouth about many things,

**11:54** lying in wait for Him, and seeking to catch something from His mouth, so that they might accuse Him.

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — The Clementine Vulgate reads hodie ("today"), matching Matthew 6:11, whereas the Greek text of Luke reads "each day" or "day by day."
- **11:8** — The opening conditional clause "And if he persists in knocking" (Et si ille perseveraverit pulsans) is a distinctive Vulgate reading absent from the standard Greek text.
- **11:13** — The Clementine Vulgate reads spiritum bonum ("the good Spirit," or "a good spirit"), paralleling "good gifts," whereas the standard Greek text reads "the Holy Spirit."
- **11:15** — The Vulgate retains the Latin and Old Testament spelling Beelzebub here and in 11:18-19, whereas critical Greek editions read Beelzebul.
- **11:41** — Latin quod superest literally means "what remains over" or "your surplus," whereas the Greek text (ta enonta) means "the things that are inside."
- **11:53** — The Clementine Vulgate begins with "while He was saying these things to them" (vs. critical Greek "when He went out from there"), names "the Pharisees and the experts in the law" (vs. Greek "scribes and Pharisees"), and renders Greek apostomatizein ("question closely") literally as os ejus opprimere ("press upon His mouth").
