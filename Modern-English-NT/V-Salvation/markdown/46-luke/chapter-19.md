## Text

**19:1** And having entered, He was walking through Jericho.

**19:2** And look, there was a man named Zacchaeus; and this man was a chief of the tax collectors, and he himself was rich.

**19:3** And he was seeking to see Jesus, who He was, and he could not on account of the crowd, because he was short in stature.

**19:4** And running ahead, he climbed into a sycamore tree so that he might see Him, because He was going to pass that way.

**19:5** And when He had come to the place, looking up, Jesus saw him and said to him, "Zacchaeus, hurry down, because today I must remain in your house."

**19:6** And he hurried down and welcomed Him, rejoicing.

**19:7** And when they all saw it, they were grumbling, saying that He had turned aside to lodge with a sinful man.

**19:8** Now Zacchaeus, standing, said to the Lord, "Look, half of my goods, Lord, I give to the poor, and if I have cheated anyone of anything, I repay fourfold."

**19:9** Jesus said to him, "Today salvation has come about for this house, because he himself is also a son of Abraham.

**19:10** For the Son of Man came to seek and to save what had been lost."

**19:11** While they were listening to these things, adding further, He spoke a parable, because He was close to Jerusalem and because they supposed that the kingdom of God would immediately be manifested.

**19:12** Therefore He said, "A certain man of noble birth went away into a distant region to receive a kingdom for himself and to return.

**19:13** And having summoned ten of his servants, he gave them ten minas and said to them, 'Conduct business until I come.'

**19:14** Yet his citizens hated him, and they sent an embassy after him, saying, 'We do not wish this man to reign over us.'

**19:15** And it happened that he returned, having received the kingdom, and he ordered the servants to whom he had given the money to be summoned, so that he might know how much each one had gained in business.

**19:16** Now the first came, saying, 'Master, your mina has acquired ten minas.'

**19:17** And he said to him, 'Well done, good servant! Because you were faithful in a small matter, you will have authority over ten cities.'

**19:18** And another came, saying, 'Master, your mina has made five minas.'

**19:19** And to this one he said, 'You also be over five cities.'

**19:20** And another came, saying, 'Master, here is your mina, which I had stored away in a handkerchief;

**19:21** for I feared you, because you are a stern man; you take up what you did not set down, and you reap what you did not sow.'

**19:22** He says to him, 'From your own mouth I judge you, wicked servant. You knew that I am a stern man, taking up what I did not set down and reaping what I did not sow;

**19:23** and why did you not give my money to the bank, so that when I came I would certainly have collected it with interest?'

**19:24** And to those standing nearby he said, 'Take the mina away from him, and give it to the one who has ten minas.'

**19:25** And they said to him, 'Master, he has ten minas.'

**19:26** 'Now I tell you that to everyone who has, it will be given, and he will have an abundance; but from the one who does not have, even what he has will be taken away from him.

**19:27** However, those enemies of mine who did not wish me to reign over them, bring them here and put them to death before me.'"

**19:28** And when these things had been spoken, He was proceeding ahead, going up to Jerusalem.

**19:29** And it happened, when He had drawn near to Bethphage and Bethany, to the mountain called Olivet, He sent two of His disciples,

**19:30** saying, "Go into the village that is opposite; upon entering it, you will find a donkey's colt tied, on which no human being has ever sat. Untie it and bring it.

**19:31** And if anyone asks you, 'Why are you untying it?' this is what you will say to him: 'Because the Lord desires its service.'"

**19:32** Now those who had been sent went away and found the colt standing, just as He told them.

**19:33** Now while they were untying the colt, its owners said to them, "Why are you untying the colt?"

**19:34** But they said, "Because the Lord needs it."

**19:35** And they led it to Jesus. And throwing their garments over the colt, they placed Jesus on it.

**19:36** And while He was going along, they were spreading their garments down on the road.

**19:37** And when He was already drawing near to the descent of Mount Olivet, all the crowds of disciples, rejoicing, began to praise God with a loud voice for all the deeds of power that they had seen,

**19:38** saying, "Blessed is He who comes as King in the name of the Lord! Peace in heaven, and glory in the highest!"

**19:39** And some of the Pharisees from the crowds said to Him, "Teacher, rebuke Your disciples."

**19:40** To them He Himself said, "I tell you that if these remain silent, the stones will cry out."

**19:41** And as soon as He drew near, seeing the city, He wept over it, saying,

**19:42** "If you also had known, and indeed on this day of yours, the things that are for your peace! But now they are hidden from your eyes.

**19:43** For days will come upon you, and your enemies will surround you with a rampart, and they will surround you and press you in on every side,

**19:44** and they will cast you down to the ground, and your children who are in you, and they will not leave in you stone upon stone, because you did not know the time of your visitation."

**19:45** And having entered into the temple, He began to drive out those selling in it and those buying,

**19:46** saying to them, "It is written, 'My house is a house of prayer,' but you have made it a cave of robbers."

**19:47** And He was teaching every day in the temple. Now the chief priests and the scribes and the leader of the people were seeking to destroy Him,

**19:48** and they did not find what they might do to Him. For all the people were held in rapt attention, listening to Him.

## Translator Notes

- **19:26** — The Clementine Vulgate adds et abundabit ('and he will have an abundance') and the final ab eo ('from him'), whereas standard Greek editions of Luke omit both phrases.
- **19:30** — The Vulgate specifies pullum asinae ('a donkey's colt', literally 'the colt of a female donkey'), while the Greek text reads simply 'a colt.'
- **19:31** — The Vulgate renders the response with operam ejus desiderat ('desires its service') here but uses eum necessarium habet ('needs it') in verse 34, whereas the Greek uses the same phrase in both verses.
- **19:32** — The Vulgate explicitly adds stantem pullum ('the colt standing'), which is not present in the Greek text.
- **19:47** — The Clementine Vulgate reads the singular princeps plebis ('the leader of the people'), whereas the Greek text has the plural ('the leading men of the people').
