## Text

**2:1** Therefore, when Jesus had been born in Bethlehem of Judah in the days of King Herod, look, magi from the east came to Jerusalem,

**2:2** saying, "Where is the One born King of the Jews? For we saw His star in the east, and we came to worship Him."

**2:3** Now on hearing this, King Herod was disturbed, and all Jerusalem with him.

**2:4** And assembling all the chief priests and scribes of the people, he inquired of them where the Christ was to be born.

**2:5** And they said, "In Bethlehem of Judah, for thus it is written through the prophet:

**2:6** 'And you, Bethlehem, land of Judah, are by no means least among the rulers of Judah; for from you will come forth a leader who will rule My people Israel.'"

**2:7** Then Herod, having secretly summoned the magi, carefully learned from them the time of the star that appeared to them.

**2:8** And sending them to Bethlehem, he said, "Go and inquire carefully concerning the child; and when you have found Him, report back to me, so that I too may come and worship Him."

**2:9** After they had heard the king, they departed; and look, the star that they had seen in the east was going ahead of them, until it came and stood above where the child was.

**2:10** And when they saw the star, they rejoiced with very great joy.

**2:11** And entering the house, they found the child with Mary His mother, and falling down, they worshiped Him; and opening their treasures, they offered Him gifts: gold, frankincense, and myrrh.

**2:12** And having received an answer in a dream not to return to Herod, they went back to their own region by another way.

**2:13** Now when they had departed, look, an angel of the Lord appeared to Joseph in a dream, saying, "Get up, take the child and His mother, and flee into Egypt, and remain there until I tell you. For Herod is about to search for the child to destroy Him."

**2:14** So rising up, he took the child and His mother by night and withdrew into Egypt,

**2:15** and he was there until the death of Herod, so that what was spoken by the Lord through the prophet might be fulfilled, saying, "From Egypt I called My Son."

**2:16** Then Herod, seeing that he had been tricked by the magi, became very angry; and he sent and killed all the boys who were in Bethlehem and in all its districts, from two years of age and under, according to the time that he had ascertained from the magi.

**2:17** Then what was spoken through Jeremiah the prophet was fulfilled, saying,

**2:18** "A voice was heard in Ramah, weeping and much wailing: Rachel weeping for her children, and she was unwilling to be comforted, because they are no more."

**2:19** Now when Herod had died, look, an angel of the Lord appeared in a dream to Joseph in Egypt,

**2:20** saying, "Get up, take the child and His mother, and go into the land of Israel; for those who were seeking the child's life have died."

**2:21** So rising up, he took the child and His mother and came into the land of Israel.

**2:22** But hearing that Archelaus was reigning in Judea in place of his father Herod, he was afraid to go there; and being warned in a dream, he withdrew into the districts of Galilee.

**2:23** And he came and lived in a city that is called Nazareth, so that what was spoken through the prophets might be fulfilled: "He will be called a Nazarene."

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "in Bethlehem Juda" ("in Bethlehem of Judah"; likewise "Judæ" in v. 5) rather than "Judea" (contrast "Judæa" in 2:22, 3:1, and 3:5).
- **2:6** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "qui regat" ("who will rule/govern") rather than the Greek verb meaning "will shepherd."
- **2:11** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "invenerunt" ("they found") rather than "they saw," a distinct reading established in the Latin textual tradition.
- **2:12** — Latin "responso accepto" literally means "having received an answer," a common Vulgate idiom for a divine reply or directive (contrast "admonitus," "warned," in v. 22).
