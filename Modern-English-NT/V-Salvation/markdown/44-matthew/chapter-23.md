## Text

**23:1** Then Jesus spoke to the crowds, and to His disciples,

**23:2** saying, "The scribes and the Pharisees have sat upon the seat of Moses.

**23:3** Therefore, observe and do all things, whatever they tell you; yet do not act according to their works, for they speak and do not act.

**23:4** For they bind up heavy and unbearable burdens and lay them upon the shoulders of men, yet with their own finger they are unwilling to move them.

**23:5** But they do all their works so that they may be seen by men; for they broaden their phylacteries and enlarge the fringes.

**23:6** And they love the first reclining places at banquets, and the first seats in the synagogues,

**23:7** and greetings in the marketplace, and to be called Rabbi by men.

**23:8** But you, do not be called Rabbi; for one is your Teacher, and all of you are brothers.

**23:9** And do not call anyone upon the earth your father; for one is your Father, who is in heaven.

**23:10** Nor be called teachers, because your Teacher is one, Christ.

**23:11** The one who is greatest among you will be your servant.

**23:12** Now the one who exalts himself will be humbled, and the one who humbles himself will be exalted.

**23:13** But woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, because you shut the kingdom of heaven before men! For you yourselves do not enter, nor do you permit those who are entering to enter.

**23:15** Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, because you go around sea and dry land so that you may make one proselyte, and when he has become one, you make him a son of Gehenna twice as much as yourselves.

**23:16** Woe to you, blind guides, who say, 'Whoever swears by the temple, it is nothing; but whoever swears by the gold of the temple is bound.'

**23:17** Fools and blind ones! For which is greater? The gold, or the temple that sanctifies the gold?

**23:18** And, 'Whoever swears by the altar, it is nothing; but whoever swears by the gift that is upon it is bound.'

**23:19** Blind ones! For which is greater, the gift, or the altar that sanctifies the gift?

**23:20** Therefore, the one who swears by the altar swears by it and by all things that are upon it.

**23:21** And whoever swears by the temple swears by it and by Him who dwells in it;

**23:22** and the one who swears by heaven swears by the throne of God and by Him who sits upon it.

**23:23** Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, who tithe mint, and dill, and cumin, and have left aside the weightier things of the law: judgment, and mercy, and faith! These things you ought to have done, and not to have omitted those.

**23:24** Blind guides, straining out a gnat, yet swallowing a camel.

**23:25** Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, because you clean what is on the outside of the cup and the dish, yet inside you are full of robbery and uncleanness!

**23:26** Blind Pharisee, clean first what is on the inside of the cup and the dish, so that what is on the outside may become clean.

**23:27** Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, because you are like whitewashed tombs, which on the outside appear beautiful to men, yet inside they are full of bones of the dead and all filth!

**23:28** So you also indeed appear righteous to men on the outside, yet inside you are full of hypocrisy and iniquity.

**23:29** Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, who build the tombs of the prophets and adorn the monuments of the righteous,

**23:30** and say, 'If we had been in the days of our fathers, we would not have been their partners in the blood of the prophets!'

**23:31** Therefore, you are a witness against yourselves that you are sons of those who killed the prophets.

**23:32** And you, fill up the measure of your fathers.

**23:33** Serpents, offspring of vipers, how will you flee from the judgment of Gehenna?

**23:34** For this reason, look, I send to you prophets, and wise men, and scribes; and some of them you will kill and crucify, and some of them you will scourge in your synagogues and persecute from city to city,

**23:35** so that all the righteous blood that has been shed upon the earth may come upon you, from the blood of Abel the righteous up to the blood of Zechariah, son of Barachiah, whom you killed between the temple and the altar.

**23:36** Amen, I tell you, all these things will come upon this generation.

**23:37** Jerusalem, Jerusalem, you who kill the prophets and stone those who have been sent to you, how many times have I wanted to gather your children, just as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, and you were unwilling?

**23:38** Look, your house will be left to you desolate.

**23:39** For I tell you, you will not see Me from now on until you say, 'Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord.'"

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**23:14** *(after `23:13`)* Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, because you devour the houses of widows while praying long prayers! For this reason you will receive a greater judgment.

## Translator Notes

- **23:10** — The Clementine Vulgate uses the same Latin noun magister ("teacher" or "master") in both verses 8 and 10, whereas the Greek distinguishes didaskalos (v. 8) and kathegetes (v. 10).
- **23:16** — Latin debet (also in v. 18) literally means "owes" or "is indebted," here signifying being bound to fulfill the oath.
- **23:25** — Unlike Greek manuscripts that read third-person plural ("they are full," referring to the cup and dish) with "self-indulgence," the Vulgate reads second-person plural pleni estis ("you are full") and immunditia ("uncleanness"), directly contrasting with mundatis ("you clean") and mundum ("clean," v. 26).
- **23:37** — The Vulgate maintains second-person singular address throughout (occidis, lapidas, ad te, noluisti) and punctuates the verse as a rhetorical question, whereas the Greek shifts to third-person "to her" and ends with second-person plural.
- **23:38** — The Vulgate uses the future passive relinquetur ("will be left") and includes deserta ("desolate"), whereas many Greek manuscripts use the present passive ("is left").
- **23:14 *(additional)*** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "orationes longas orantes" ("praying long prayers") without any phrase equivalent to "for a pretense."
