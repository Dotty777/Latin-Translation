## Text

**25:1** "Then the kingdom of heaven will be like ten virgins who, taking their lamps, went out to meet the bridegroom and the bride.

**25:2** Now five of them were foolish, and five prudent.

**25:3** But the five foolish ones, after taking their lamps, took no oil with them.

**25:4** The prudent ones, however, took oil in their vessels along with the lamps.

**25:5** While the bridegroom delayed, however, all of them grew drowsy and slept.

**25:6** Now at midnight a cry arose, 'Look, the bridegroom is coming; go out to meet him.'

**25:7** Then all of those virgins got up and trimmed their lamps.

**25:8** And the foolish ones said to the wise ones, 'Give us some of your oil, because our lamps are going out.'

**25:9** The prudent ones answered, saying, 'Perhaps there will not be enough for us and for you; go instead to those who sell, and buy for yourselves.'

**25:10** Now while they were going to buy, the bridegroom arrived; and the ones who were ready entered with him to the wedding, and the door was shut.

**25:11** Last of all the remaining virgins also came, saying, 'Lord, lord, open for us.'

**25:12** But answering, he said, 'Amen, I tell you, I do not know you.'

**25:13** Keep watch, therefore, because you do not know the day nor the hour.

**25:14** For it is like a man setting out on a journey abroad, who called his servants and handed over his possessions to them.

**25:15** And to one he gave five talents, to another two, and to another one, to each according to his own ability; and he set out immediately.

**25:16** Now the one who had received five talents went away and worked with them, and gained another five.

**25:17** Likewise, the one who had received two also gained another two.

**25:18** But the one who had received one went away, dug into the ground, and hid his master's money.

**25:19** After a long time, however, the master of those servants came and settled accounts with them.

**25:20** And the one who had received five talents came forward and presented another five talents, saying, 'Master, you handed over five talents to me; look, I have gained another five in addition.'

**25:21** His master said to him, 'Well done, good and faithful servant! Because you were faithful over a few things, I will appoint you over many things; enter into the joy of your master.'

**25:22** Then the one who had received two talents also came forward and said, 'Master, you handed over two talents to me; look, I have gained another two.'

**25:23** His master said to him, 'Well done, good and faithful servant! Because you were faithful over a few things, I will appoint you over many things; enter into the joy of your master.'

**25:24** Then the one who had received one talent also came forward and said, 'Master, I know that you are a harsh man: you reap where you did not sow, and you gather where you did not scatter;

**25:25** and being afraid, I went away and hid your talent in the ground; look, you have what is yours.'

**25:26** But in reply his master said to him, 'Wicked and lazy servant, you knew that I reap where I do not sow and gather where I did not scatter;

**25:27** therefore you ought to have entrusted my money to the bankers, and when I came, I would certainly have received back what is mine with interest.

**25:28** Take the talent away from him, therefore, and give it to the one who has ten talents.

**25:29** For to everyone who has, it will be given, and he will abound; but as for the one who does not have, even what he seems to have will be taken away from him.

**25:30** And cast out the useless servant into the outer darkness; in that place there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.'

**25:31** Now when the Son of Man comes in His majesty, and all the angels with Him, then He will sit on the throne of His majesty.

**25:32** And all the nations will be gathered before Him, and He will separate them from one another, just as a shepherd divides the sheep from the goats;

**25:33** and He will set the sheep on His right, and the goats on the left.

**25:34** Then the King will say to those who will be on His right, 'Come, you blessed of My Father, take possession of the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world;

**25:35** for I was hungry, and you gave Me something to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave Me something to drink; I was a stranger, and you took Me in;

**25:36** naked, and you covered Me; ill, and you visited Me; I was in prison, and you came to Me.'

**25:37** Then the righteous will answer Him, saying, 'Lord, when did we see You hungry and feed You, thirsty and give You a drink?

**25:38** And when did we see You a stranger and take You in, or naked and cover You?

**25:39** Or when did we see You ill, or in prison, and come to You?'

**25:40** And answering, the King will say to them, 'Amen, I tell you, as long as you did it for one of these least brothers of Mine, you did it for Me.'

**25:41** Then He will also say to those who will be on the left, 'Depart from Me, you cursed ones, into the eternal fire that has been prepared for the devil and his angels;

**25:42** for I was hungry, and you did not give Me anything to eat; I was thirsty, and you did not give Me a drink;

**25:43** I was a stranger, and you did not take Me in; naked, and you did not cover Me; ill and in prison, and you did not visit Me.'

**25:44** Then they themselves will also answer Him, saying, 'Lord, when did we see You hungry, or thirsty, or a stranger, or naked, or ill, or in prison, and not serve You?'

**25:45** Then He will answer them, saying, 'Amen, I tell you, as long as you did not do it for one of these lesser ones, neither did you do it for Me.'

**25:46** And these will go into eternal punishment, but the righteous into eternal life."

## Translator Notes

- **25:1** — The Clementine Vulgate adds 'and the bride' (et sponsae), a reading supported by several Old Latin and Syriac witnesses but absent from standard Greek editions.
- **25:15** — The Vulgate places 'immediately' (statim) at the end of v. 15 modifying the master's departure, whereas many modern Greek editions and translations attach the adverb to the first servant's departure at the start of v. 16.
- **25:29** — The Clementine Vulgate reads 'even what he seems to have' (et quod videtur habere, paralleling Luke 8:18), whereas standard Greek manuscripts of Matthew read simply 'even what he has.'
- **25:31** — The Vulgate translates Greek doxa ('glory') as majestas ('majesty') in both occurrences here, and Greek thronos as sedes ('seat' or 'throne').
- **25:40** — Here and in v. 45, the Vulgate translates Greek eph' hoson ('to the extent that') temporally as quamdiu ('as long as'); note also the Vulgate shift from superlative 'least brothers of Mine' (fratribus meis minimis) in v. 40 to comparative 'these lesser ones' (minoribus his) in v. 45.
