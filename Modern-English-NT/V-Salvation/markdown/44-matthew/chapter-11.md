## Text

**11:1** And it happened that when Jesus had finished instructing His twelve disciples, He departed from there to teach and to proclaim in their cities.

**11:2** Now when John heard in chains about the works of Christ, sending two of his disciples,

**11:3** he said to Him, "Are You the One who is to come, or are we waiting for another?"

**11:4** And answering, Jesus said to them, "Go and report to John what you have heard and seen.

**11:5** The blind see, the lame walk, lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead rise, the poor have the good news proclaimed to them;

**11:6** and blessed is the one who is not caused to stumble over Me."

**11:7** Now as they were departing, Jesus began to speak to the crowds about John: "What did you go out into the desert to see? A reed swayed by the wind?

**11:8** But what did you go out to see? A man clothed in soft garments? Look, those who wear soft garments are in the houses of kings.

**11:9** But what did you go out to see? A prophet? Yes, I tell you, and more than a prophet.

**11:10** For this is the one about whom it is written, 'Look, I am sending My messenger before Your face, who will prepare Your way before You.'

**11:11** Amen, I tell you, among those born of women no one greater than John the Baptist has arisen; yet whoever is lesser in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he is.

**11:12** Now from the days of John the Baptist until now, the kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent seize it.

**11:13** For all the prophets and the Law prophesied until John;

**11:14** and if you are willing to receive it, he himself is Elijah, who is to come.

**11:15** The one who has ears to hear, let him hear.

**11:16** Now what will I judge this generation to be like? It is like children sitting in the marketplace, who, calling out to their peers,

**11:17** say, 'We played music for you, and you did not dance; we lamented, and you did not mourn.'

**11:18** For John came neither eating nor drinking, and they say, 'He has a demon.'

**11:19** The Son of Man came eating and drinking, and they say, 'Look, a gluttonous man and a drinker of wine, a friend of tax collectors and sinners.' And wisdom has been justified by her children."

**11:20** Then He began to reproach the cities in which most of His mighty deeds had been done, because they had not repented:

**11:21** "Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! For if the mighty deeds that were done in you had been done in Tyre and Sidon, they would long ago have repented in sackcloth and ashes.

**11:22** Nevertheless, I tell you: It will be more lenient for Tyre and Sidon on the day of judgment than for you.

**11:23** And you, Capernaum, will you really be exalted all the way to heaven? You will descend all the way to the underworld. For if the mighty deeds that were done in you had been done in Sodom, perhaps it would have remained until this day.

**11:24** Nevertheless, I tell you that it will be more lenient for the land of Sodom on the day of judgment than for you."

**11:25** At that time Jesus answered and said, "I give praise to You, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because You have hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and have revealed them to little ones.

**11:26** Yes, Father, because thus it was pleasing before You.

**11:27** Everything has been handed over to Me by My Father. And no one knows the Son except the Father; neither does anyone know the Father except the Son and the one to whom the Son wishes to reveal Him.

**11:28** Come to Me, all of you who toil and are weighed down, and I will refresh you.

**11:29** Take My yoke upon yourselves and learn from Me, because I am gentle and humble of heart; and you will find rest for your souls.

**11:30** For My yoke is pleasant, and My burden is light."

## Translator Notes

- **11:2** — The Vulgate reads "in vinculis" ("in chains") rather than "in prison," and "duos de discipulis suis" ("two of his disciples") rather than critical Greek "by his disciples."
- **11:19** — The Vulgate reads "a filiis suis" ("by her children"), whereas modern critical Greek editions read "by her deeds."
- **11:20** — The Vulgate phrase "pœnitentiam agere" (here and in 11:21) literally means "to perform repentance" or "do penance," and serves as the regular Latin equivalent for "repent."
- **11:23** — The Vulgate adds "forte" ("perhaps it would have remained") in rendering the Greek conditional particle, and uses "infernum" ("the underworld," distinct from "gehennam" in 10:28) for Greek "Hades."
- **11:28** — The Vulgate uses "reficiam" ("refresh" or "restore") here rather than repeating "rest" ("requiem," 11:29), and in 11:30 describes the yoke as "suave" ("sweet" or "pleasant").
