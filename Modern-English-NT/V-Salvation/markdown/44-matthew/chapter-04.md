## Text

**4:1** Then Jesus was led into the desert by the Spirit, so that He might be tempted by the devil.

**4:2** And when He had fasted for forty days and forty nights, afterward He became hungry.

**4:3** And approaching, the tempter said to Him, "If You are the Son of God, command that these stones become loaves of bread."

**4:4** Responding, He said, "It is written: 'Not on bread alone does man live, but on every word that goes forth from the mouth of God.'"

**4:5** Then the devil took Him into the holy city, and placed Him on the pinnacle of the temple,

**4:6** and said to Him, "If You are the Son of God, throw Yourself down; for it is written that 'He has commanded His angels concerning You, and in their hands they will lift You up, so that You may not by chance strike Your foot against a stone.'"

**4:7** Jesus said to him, "Again, it is written: 'You shall not test the Lord your God.'"

**4:8** Again the devil took Him onto an exceedingly high mountain, and showed Him all the kingdoms of the world and their glory,

**4:9** and said to Him, "I will give all these things to You if, falling down, You worship me."

**4:10** Then Jesus said to him, "Go away, Satan! For it is written: 'The Lord your God you shall worship, and Him alone you shall serve.'"

**4:11** Then the devil left Him, and look, angels approached and were ministering to Him.

**4:12** Now when Jesus had heard that John had been handed over, He withdrew into Galilee;

**4:13** and having left the city of Nazareth, He came and lived in Capernaum by the sea, within the territory of Zebulun and Naphtali,

**4:14** in order that what was spoken through Isaiah the prophet might be fulfilled:

**4:15** "Land of Zebulun and land of Naphtali, the way of the sea, across the Jordan, Galilee of the nations:

**4:16** the people who were sitting in darkness have seen a great light, and for those sitting in the region of the shadow of death, light has dawned on them."

**4:17** From then on Jesus began to preach and to say, "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near."

**4:18** Now, walking beside the Sea of Galilee, Jesus saw two brothers, Simon, who is called Peter, and Andrew his brother, casting a net into the sea (for they were fishermen),

**4:19** and said to them, "Come after Me, and I will make you become fishers of men."

**4:20** And at once, having left their nets, they followed Him.

**4:21** And proceeding from there, He saw two other brothers, James, son of Zebedee, and John his brother, in a boat with Zebedee their father, repairing their nets; and He called them.

**4:22** And immediately, having left their nets and their father, they followed Him.

**4:23** And Jesus was going around all of Galilee, teaching in their synagogues, and preaching the gospel of the kingdom, and healing every illness and every infirmity among the people.

**4:24** And His reputation went out into all of Syria, and they brought to Him all who were sick, gripped by various illnesses and torments, and those who had demons, and lunatics, and paralytics, and He cured them;

**4:25** and many crowds followed Him from Galilee and the Decapolis, and from Jerusalem, and from Judea, and from across the Jordan.

## Translator Notes

- **4:6** — Vulgate reads the perfect tense "mandavit" ("He has commanded"), matching the Vulgate Psalter (Psalm 90:11 [91:11]), whereas the Greek text of Matthew has the future tense ("He will command").
- **4:16** — Vulgate reads "in regione umbrae mortis" ("in the region of the shadow of death"), whereas the Greek text coordinates both nouns ("in the region and shadow of death").
- **4:17** — Latin poenitentiam agite literally means "do penance" or "practice repentance," the Vulgate idiom for Greek metanoeite; it is rendered "Repent" here, as in 3:2, where the Latin is identical.
- **4:22** — Vulgate reads "relictis retibus" ("having left their nets"), whereas the Greek text has "leaving the boat."
- **4:24** — Latin "lunaticos" literally means "moonstruck," a term anciently used for people suffering from epilepsy or periodic seizures.
