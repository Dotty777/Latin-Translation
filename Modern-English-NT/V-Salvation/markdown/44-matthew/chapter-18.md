## Text

**18:1** In that hour the disciples approached Jesus, saying, "Who, do you think, is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven?"

**18:2** And calling a little child to Himself, Jesus set him in their midst,

**18:3** and said, "Amen, I tell you, unless you turn around and become like little children, you will not enter into the kingdom of heaven.

**18:4** Therefore, whoever humbles himself like this little child, this one is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven.

**18:5** And whoever receives one such little child in My name receives Me;

**18:6** but whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in Me to stumble, it is better for him that a donkey's millstone be hung around his neck and that he be sunk into the depth of the sea.

**18:7** Woe to the world because of stumbling blocks! For it is necessary that stumbling blocks come; nevertheless, woe to that man through whom the stumbling block comes.

**18:8** Now if your hand or your foot causes you to stumble, cut it off and throw it away from you. It is better for you to enter into life crippled or lame than, having two hands or two feet, to be thrown into eternal fire.

**18:9** And if your eye causes you to stumble, tear it out and throw it away from you. It is better for you to enter into life with one eye than, having two eyes, to be thrown into the Gehenna of fire.

**18:10** See that you do not despise one of these little ones; for I say to you that their angels in heaven always see the face of My Father who is in heaven.

**18:12** What do you think? If someone has one hundred sheep, and one of them goes astray, does he not leave the ninety-nine on the mountains and go to search for the one that went astray?

**18:13** And if it happens that he finds it, amen, I tell you that he rejoices over it more than over the ninety-nine that did not go astray.

**18:14** So it is not the will before your Father who is in heaven that one of these little ones should perish.

**18:15** Now if your brother sins against you, go and reprove him between you and him alone. If he listens to you, you will have gained your brother.

**18:16** But if he does not listen to you, take with you one or two more, so that in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may stand.

**18:17** And if he does not listen to them, tell the church. Yet if he does not listen to the church, let him be to you like a Gentile and a tax collector.

**18:18** Amen, I tell you, whatever you bind upon the earth will also be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose upon the earth will also be loosed in heaven.

**18:19** Again I say to you that if two of you agree upon the earth concerning any matter whatever they ask, it will be done for them by My Father who is in heaven.

**18:20** For where two or three are gathered together in My name, there I am in their midst."

**18:21** Then Peter, approaching Him, said, "Lord, how many times will my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? Up to seven times?"

**18:22** Jesus said to him, "I do not say to you up to seven times, but up to seventy times seven.

**18:23** For this reason, the kingdom of heaven has been likened to a human king who wished to settle accounts with his servants.

**18:24** And when he had begun to settle accounts, one was brought to him who owed him ten thousand talents.

**18:25** But since he had nothing with which to repay, his master ordered him to be sold, and his wife, and children, and all that he had, and repayment to be made.

**18:26** Then that servant, falling down, implored him, saying, 'Have patience with me, and I will repay you everything.'

**18:27** And the master of that servant, taking pity, released him and forgave him the debt.

**18:28** But going out, that servant found one of his fellow servants who owed him one hundred denarii; and seizing him, he choked him, saying, 'Repay what you owe.'

**18:29** And falling down, his fellow servant pleaded with him, saying, 'Have patience with me, and I will repay you everything.'

**18:30** But he was unwilling; instead he went away and threw him into prison until he repaid the debt.

**18:31** Now when his fellow servants saw what was happening, they were greatly grieved, and they came and reported to their master everything that had happened.

**18:32** Then his master summoned him and said to him, 'Wicked servant, I forgave you the entire debt because you pleaded with me;

**18:33** should you not therefore also have had pity on your fellow servant, just as I also had pity on you?'

**18:34** And his master, being angry, handed him over to the torturers until he repaid the whole debt.

**18:35** So also will My heavenly Father do to you, if each of you does not forgive his brother from your hearts."

## Translator Notes

- **18:1** — The Vulgate adds parenthetical "putas" ("do you think") and uses comparative "major" ("greater") with superlative force ("the greatest"), as also in 18:4.
- **18:6** — Literally "mola asinaria," a large heavy millstone turned by a donkey rather than a small hand mill.
- **18:9** — The Vulgate retains the loanword "gehennam" ("Gehenna," the Valley of Hinnom, symbolizing the place of final fiery punishment), and uses positive "bonum ... quam" ("good ... than") with comparative force ("better ... than"), as in 18:8.
- **18:22** — Latin "septuagies septies" literally means "seventy times seven" (490), though the underlying idiom echoing Genesis 4:24 is also understood by some as "seventy-seven times."
- **18:27** — The Vulgate repeats the same verb "dimisit" ("released him and forgave him the debt"), linking the servant's physical release with the cancellation of his debt.
