## Text

**16:1** And the Pharisees and Sadducees approached Him, testing Him, and asked Him to show them a sign from heaven.

**16:2** But He answered and said to them, "When evening comes, you say, 'It will be fair weather, for the sky is red.'

**16:3** And in the morning, 'A storm today, for the gloomy sky glows red.'

**16:4** So you know how to discern the face of the sky, yet can you not know the signs of the times? An evil and adulterous generation seeks a sign, and no sign will be given to it except the sign of Jonah the prophet." And having left them, He departed.

**16:5** And when His disciples had come across the water, they forgot to take loaves.

**16:6** And He said to them, "Watch closely and beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and Sadducees."

**16:7** But they were reasoning among themselves, saying, "Because we did not take loaves."

**16:8** But Jesus, knowing this, said, "Why are you reasoning among yourselves, you of little faith, because you have no loaves?

**16:9** Do you not yet understand, nor remember the five loaves for the five thousand men, and how many wicker baskets you took up?

**16:10** Nor the seven loaves for the four thousand men, and how many large baskets you took up?

**16:11** Why do you not understand that it was not about bread that I said to you, 'Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and Sadducees'?"

**16:12** Then they understood that He had not said to beware of the leaven of loaves, but of the doctrine of the Pharisees and Sadducees.

**16:13** Now Jesus came into the districts of Caesarea Philippi, and He was asking His disciples, saying, "Who do people say the Son of Man is?"

**16:14** And they said, "Some, John the Baptist; others, Elijah; and still others, Jeremiah, or one of the prophets."

**16:15** Jesus said to them, "But you, who do you say that I am?"

**16:16** Answering, Simon Peter said, "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God."

**16:17** And Jesus answered and said to him, "Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, because flesh and blood did not reveal this to you, but My Father who is in heaven.

**16:18** And I say to you that you are Peter, and upon this rock I will build My church, and the gates of the underworld will not prevail against it.

**16:19** And to you I will give the keys of the kingdom of heaven. And whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven also, and whatever you unbind on earth will be unbound in heaven also."

**16:20** Then He commanded His disciples to tell no one that He was Jesus Christ.

**16:21** From that time on, Jesus began to show His disciples that He must go to Jerusalem, and suffer many things from the elders and scribes and chief priests, and be killed, and rise again on the third day.

**16:22** And Peter, taking Him aside, began to rebuke Him, saying, "Far be it from You, Lord! This will not happen to You."

**16:23** But He turned around and said to Peter, "Get behind Me, Satan! You are a stumbling block to Me, because you do not have a mind for the things that are of God, but for those that are of men."

**16:24** Then Jesus said to His disciples, "If anyone desires to come after Me, let him renounce himself, and take up his cross, and follow Me.

**16:25** For whoever desires to save his life will lose it; yet whoever loses his life on account of Me will find it.

**16:26** For what does it benefit a man if he gains the entire world, yet suffers the loss of his own soul? Or what exchange will a man give for his soul?

**16:27** For the Son of Man is going to come in the glory of His Father with His angels, and then He will repay each one according to his works.

**16:28** Amen, I tell you, there are some among those standing here who will not taste death until they see the Son of Man coming in His kingdom."

## Translator Notes

- **16:4** — In the Clementine Vulgate verse division, the sentence about discerning the sky and the signs of the times opens verse 4 rather than concluding verse 3. The Vulgate also retains the weather saying in 16:2-3 and includes "the prophet" after Jonah.
- **16:18** — The Latin preserves the wordplay between Petrus ("Peter") and petram ("rock"). Inferi (genitive singular of inferus) literally denotes the underworld or realm of the dead (rendering Greek Hades), distinct from gehenna, though traditionally often translated "hell."
- **16:20** — The Clementine Vulgate reads Jesus Christus ("Jesus Christ"), whereas most critical Greek texts omit "Jesus" and read simply "the Christ."
- **16:25** — Across 16:25-26, the Latin uses the single noun anima four times, spanning both earthly "life" (16:25) and one's eternal "soul" (16:26).
- **16:27** — The Vulgate reads plural opera ("works"), whereas the Greek uses the singular praxin ("deed" or "conduct").
