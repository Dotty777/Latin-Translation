## Text

**7:1** "Do not pass judgment, so that you may not be judged.

**7:2** For with the judgment by which you judge, you will be judged; and with the measure by which you measure, it will be measured back to you.

**7:3** And why do you see the splinter in your brother's eye, yet do not see the beam in your own eye?

**7:4** Or how do you say to your brother, 'Let me remove the splinter from your eye,' when look, a beam is in your own eye?

**7:5** Hypocrite, first remove the beam from your own eye, and then you will see to remove the splinter from your brother's eye.

**7:6** Do not give what is holy to dogs, nor throw your pearls in front of pigs; otherwise they might trample them under their feet and, turning around, tear you to pieces.

**7:7** Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened for you.

**7:8** For everyone who asks receives, and the one who seeks finds, and for the one knocking it will be opened.

**7:9** Or what man is there among you who, if his son asks him for bread, would hand him a stone?

**7:10** Or if he asks for a fish, would he hand him a snake?

**7:11** If therefore you, though you are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father, who is in heaven, give good things to those who ask Him?

**7:12** Therefore, everything that you want people to do for you, you also do for them; for this is the Law and the Prophets.

**7:13** Enter through the narrow gate, because wide is the gate and spacious is the road that leads to destruction, and many are those who enter through it.

**7:14** How narrow is the gate and constricted is the road that leads to life, and few are those who find it!

**7:15** Watch out for false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing, yet inwardly they are ravenous wolves.

**7:16** By their fruits you will recognize them. Do people gather grapes from thorns, or figs from thistles?

**7:17** In this way every good tree produces good fruits, while a bad tree produces bad fruits.

**7:18** A good tree cannot produce bad fruits, nor can a bad tree produce good fruits.

**7:19** Every tree that does not produce good fruit will be cut down and thrown into the fire.

**7:20** So then, from their fruits you will recognize them.

**7:21** Not everyone who says to Me, 'Lord, Lord,' will enter the kingdom of heaven, but the one who does the will of My Father, who is in heaven, he himself will enter the kingdom of heaven.

**7:22** Many will say to Me on that day, 'Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in Your name, and in Your name cast out demons, and in Your name perform many mighty works?'

**7:23** And then I will declare to them, 'I never knew you; depart from Me, you who practice iniquity.'

**7:24** Therefore everyone who hears these words of Mine and does them will be likened to a wise man who built his house upon the rock.

**7:25** And the rain came down, and the floods came, and the winds blew and rushed against that house, and it did not fall, for it had been founded upon the rock.

**7:26** And everyone who hears these words of Mine and does not do them will be like a foolish man who built his house upon the sand.

**7:27** And the rain came down, and the floods came, and the winds blew and rushed against that house, and it fell, and its ruin was great."

**7:28** And it happened, when Jesus had finished these words, that the crowds were amazed at His teaching.

**7:29** For He was teaching them as one having authority, and not like their scribes and the Pharisees.

## Translator Notes

- **7:14** — The Vulgate reads the exclamation Quam ("How narrow...!"), corresponding to the Greek variant ti, where many Greek manuscripts read causal hoti ("For/Because the gate is narrow").
- **7:17** — The Vulgate uses the same adjective malus ("bad") for both the tree and its fruits, whereas the Greek distinguishes sapron ("diseased/rotten") for the tree from ponerous ("bad/evil") for the fruit.
- **7:21** — Vulgate-distinct addition: the Clementine Vulgate includes the concluding clause "he himself will enter the kingdom of heaven" (ipse intrabit in regnum caelorum), which is absent from most standard Greek editions.
- **7:29** — Vulgate-distinct addition: the Clementine Vulgate adds "and the Pharisees" (et pharisaei) after "their scribes," a reading attested in Latin tradition and some Greek manuscripts but absent from most critical Greek texts.
