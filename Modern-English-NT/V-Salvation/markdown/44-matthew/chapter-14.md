## Text

**14:1** At that time Herod the tetrarch heard the report about Jesus,

**14:2** and said to his servants, "This is John the Baptist; he himself has risen from the dead, and for this reason powers are working in him."

**14:3** For Herod had seized John and bound him and put him into prison on account of Herodias, his brother's wife.

**14:4** For John was saying to him, "It is not permitted for you to have her."

**14:5** And wanting to kill him, he feared the people, because they regarded him as a prophet.

**14:6** Now on Herod's birthday, the daughter of Herodias danced in the center and pleased Herod;

**14:7** therefore with an oath he promised to give her whatever she might ask from him.

**14:8** But she, having been instructed beforehand by her mother, said, "Give to me here on a platter the head of John the Baptist."

**14:9** And the king was saddened; yet on account of the oath and those who were reclining together, he commanded that it be given.

**14:10** And he sent and beheaded John in prison.

**14:11** And his head was brought on a platter, and it was given to the young girl, and she brought it to her mother.

**14:12** And his disciples approached, took his body, and buried it, and came and reported it to Jesus.

**14:13** When Jesus had heard this, He withdrew from there in a boat to a deserted place by Himself; and when the crowds had heard, they followed Him on foot from the cities.

**14:14** And going out, He saw a large crowd, and had compassion on them, and healed their sick.

**14:15** Now when evening had come, His disciples approached Him, saying, "The place is deserted, and the hour has already passed; send the crowds away, so that going into the villages they may buy food for themselves."

**14:16** But Jesus said to them, "They have no need to go; you yourselves give them something to eat."

**14:17** They answered Him, "We have nothing here except five loaves and two fish."

**14:18** And He said to them, "Bring them to Me here."

**14:19** And when He had commanded the crowd to recline on the grass, having taken the five loaves and the two fish and looking up to heaven, He gave a blessing, and broke and gave the loaves to the disciples, and the disciples to the crowds.

**14:20** And they all ate and were satisfied. And they took up the leftovers, twelve baskets full of broken pieces.

**14:21** Now the number of those who ate was five thousand men, excluding women and little children.

**14:22** And immediately Jesus compelled the disciples to board the boat and to go ahead of Him across the water while He sent the crowds away.

**14:23** And having sent the crowd away, He went up on the mountain alone to pray. Now when evening had come, He was there alone,

**14:24** but the boat was being tossed by the waves in the middle of the sea, for the wind was contrary.

**14:25** For in the fourth watch of the night, He came to them walking on the sea.

**14:26** And seeing Him walking on the sea, they were troubled, saying, "It is a phantom!" And because of fear they cried out.

**14:27** And immediately Jesus spoke to them, saying, "Have confidence; it is I, do not be afraid."

**14:28** And answering, Peter said, "Lord, if it is You, command me to come to You on the waters."

**14:29** And He said, "Come." And Peter, stepping down from the boat, was walking on the water in order to come to Jesus.

**14:30** But seeing the strong wind, he became afraid; and when he had begun to sink, he cried out, saying, "Lord, save me!"

**14:31** And at once Jesus, stretching out His hand, caught hold of him and said to him, "You of little faith, why did you doubt?"

**14:32** And when they had boarded the boat, the wind ceased.

**14:33** And those who were in the boat came and worshiped Him, saying, "Truly You are the Son of God."

**14:34** And when they had crossed over, they came into the land of Gennesar.

**14:35** And when the men of that place had recognized Him, they sent word into that entire region and brought to Him all who were ill,

**14:36** and they kept begging Him that they might touch even the fringe of His garment. And all who touched it were made well.

## Translator Notes

- **14:3** — The Clementine Vulgate reads simply "his brother's wife" (uxorem fratris sui), omitting the name "Philip" found in most Greek manuscripts.
- **14:21** — The Vulgate states "five thousand men" (quinque millia virorum) without the qualifying "about" present in the Greek text.
- **14:24** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "in the middle of the sea" (in medio mari), agreeing with the Byzantine Greek tradition, whereas critical Greek editions read "already many stadia from the land."
- **14:29** — The Vulgate has a purpose clause ("in order to come to Jesus," ut veniret ad Jesum) and singular "water" (aquam, contrasting with plural aquas in 14:28), whereas critical Greek editions read "and came to Jesus" with plural "waters" in both verses.
- **14:34** — The Clementine Vulgate reads the shorter place name "Genesar" (rendered "Gennesar," as in 1 Maccabees 11:67) and "into the land of Gennesar," whereas the Greek text reads "Gennesaret."
