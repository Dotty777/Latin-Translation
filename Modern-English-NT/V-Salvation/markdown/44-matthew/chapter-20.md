## Text

**20:1** "The kingdom of heaven is like a man, the master of a household, who went out early in the morning to hire laborers for his vineyard.

**20:2** And when an agreement had been made with the laborers for a denarius a day, he sent them into his vineyard.

**20:3** And going out about the third hour, he saw others standing idle in the marketplace,

**20:4** and he said to them, 'You also go into my vineyard, and I will give you what is just.'

**20:5** And they went away. Again he went out about the sixth and the ninth hour, and did likewise.

**20:6** Then about the eleventh hour he went out and found others standing, and said to them, 'Why are you standing here idle all day?'

**20:7** They said to him, 'Because no one has hired us.' He said to them, 'You also go into my vineyard.'

**20:8** Now when evening had come, the owner of the vineyard said to his steward, 'Call the laborers and pay them their wages, beginning from the last up to the first.'

**20:9** So when those who had come about the eleventh hour arrived, they each received a denarius.

**20:10** And when the first ones also came, they supposed that they were going to receive more; yet they themselves also each received a denarius.

**20:11** And upon receiving it, they were grumbling against the master of the household,

**20:12** saying, 'These last ones worked one hour, and you have made them equal to us, who have borne the weight of the day and the heat.'

**20:13** But answering one of them, he said, 'Friend, I am doing you no wrong; did you not agree with me for a denarius?

**20:14** Take what is yours and go; yet I wish to give to this last one also, just as to you.

**20:15** Or is it not lawful for me to do what I wish? Or is your eye evil because I am good?'

**20:16** In this way, the last will be first, and the first, last. For many are called, yet few are chosen."

**20:17** And as Jesus was going up to Jerusalem, He took the twelve disciples privately and said to them,

**20:18** "Look, we are going up to Jerusalem, and the Son of Man will be handed over to the chief priests and scribes, and they will condemn Him to death,

**20:19** and will hand Him over to the Gentiles to be mocked, and flogged, and crucified, and on the third day He will rise again."

**20:20** Then the mother of the sons of Zebedee approached Him with her sons, worshiping and asking something from Him.

**20:21** He said to her, "What do you want?" She said to Him, "Say that these two sons of mine may sit, one at Your right and one at Your left, in Your kingdom."

**20:22** But Jesus answered and said, "You do not know what you are asking. Are you able to drink the cup that I am about to drink?" They said to Him, "We are able."

**20:23** He said to them, "My cup indeed you will drink; but to sit at My right or left is not Mine to give to you; rather, it is for those for whom it has been prepared by My Father."

**20:24** And when the ten heard this, they were indignant about the two brothers.

**20:25** But Jesus called them to Himself and said, "You know that the rulers of the Gentiles exercise dominion over them, and those who are greater exercise authority over them.

**20:26** It will not be like this among you; rather, whoever wants to become greater among you, let him be your servant,

**20:27** and whoever wants to be first among you will be your slave,

**20:28** just as the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give His life as a redemption for many."

**20:29** And as they were going out from Jericho, a large crowd followed Him,

**20:30** and look, two blind men sitting beside the road heard that Jesus was passing by, and they cried out, saying, "Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David."

**20:31** Now the crowd was rebuking them so that they would keep silent. Yet they kept crying out all the more, saying, "Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David."

**20:32** And Jesus stopped and called them, and said, "What do you want Me to do for you?"

**20:33** They said to Him, "Lord, that our eyes may be opened."

**20:34** And moved with compassion for them, Jesus touched their eyes. And immediately they saw, and followed Him.

## Translator Notes

- **20:15** — The Clementine Vulgate reads Aut non licet mihi quod volo, facere? ("Or is it not lawful for me to do what I wish?"), omitting the Greek phrase "with what belongs to me." The idiom "your eye evil" (oculus tuus nequam) denotes envy or stinginess.
- **20:16** — The Vulgate includes the concluding sentence Multi enim sunt vocati, pauci vero electi ("For many are called, yet few are chosen"), which is absent from many early Greek manuscripts here.
- **20:23** — The Vulgate explicitly reads dare vobis ("to give to you"), whereas critical Greek texts omit "to you."
- **20:34** — The Vulgate reads simply viderunt ("they saw"), whereas the underlying Greek verb means "regained their sight" or "looked up."
