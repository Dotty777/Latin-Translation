## Text

**26:1** And it happened that when Jesus had completed all these discourses, He said to His disciples:

**26:2** "You know that after two days the Passover will take place, and the Son of Man will be handed over to be crucified."

**26:3** Then the chief priests and the elders of the people were gathered together in the courtyard of the high priest, who was called Caiaphas,

**26:4** and they formed a plan to seize Jesus by deceit and kill Him.

**26:5** But they were saying, "Not on the festival day, in case an uproar should arise among the people."

**26:6** Now while Jesus was in Bethany, in the house of Simon the leper,

**26:7** a woman approached Him holding an alabaster jar of costly ointment, and she poured it out over His head as He reclined.

**26:8** But seeing this, the disciples grew indignant, saying, "For what purpose is this waste?

**26:9** For this could have been sold for a great deal and given to the poor."

**26:10** But Jesus, knowing this, said to them, "Why are you causing trouble for this woman? For she has performed a good work toward Me.

**26:11** For you always have the poor with you; Me, however, you do not always have.

**26:12** For in putting this ointment on My body, this woman did it for My burial.

**26:13** Amen, I tell you, wherever this Gospel is preached in the entire world, what this woman did will also be told in remembrance of her."

**26:14** Then one of the twelve, who was called Judas Iscariot, went off to the chief priests,

**26:15** and said to them, "What do you want to give me, and I will hand Him over to you?" And they assigned him thirty silver coins.

**26:16** And from then on he was seeking an opportunity to hand Him over.

**26:17** Now on the first day of Unleavened Bread, the disciples approached Jesus, saying, "Where do You wish us to prepare for You to eat the Passover?"

**26:18** And Jesus said, "Go into the city to a certain man and tell him, 'The Teacher says, "My time is near; at your house I am celebrating the Passover with My disciples."'"

**26:19** And the disciples did just as Jesus had instructed them, and they prepared the Passover.

**26:20** Now when evening had come, He was reclining at table with His twelve disciples.

**26:21** And as they were eating, He said, "Amen, I tell you that one of you is about to hand Me over."

**26:22** And deeply grieved, they began one by one to say, "It is not I, is it, Lord?"

**26:23** And He answered and said, "The one who dips his hand with Me in the dish, this one will hand Me over.

**26:24** The Son of Man indeed goes just as it is written about Him, yet woe to that man through whom the Son of Man will be handed over! It would have been good for him if that man had not been born."

**26:25** Then Judas, who handed Him over, answered and said, "It is not I, is it, Rabbi?" He said to him, "You have said it."

**26:26** Now while they were eating supper, Jesus took bread, blessed it, broke it, and gave it to His disciples, and said, "Take and eat: this is My body."

**26:27** And taking a cup, He gave thanks and gave it to them, saying, "Drink from this, all of you.

**26:28** For this is My blood of the new covenant, which will be poured out on behalf of many for the forgiveness of sins.

**26:29** But I tell you, from now on I will not drink of this produce of the vine until that day when I drink it new with you in the kingdom of My Father."

**26:30** And when a hymn had been recited, they went out to the Mount of Olives.

**26:31** Then Jesus said to them, "All of you will suffer stumbling because of Me on this very night. For it is written, 'I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock will be dispersed.'

**26:32** But after I have risen again, I will go ahead of you into Galilee."

**26:33** But Peter answered and said to Him, "Even if all are made to stumble because of You, I will never be made to stumble."

**26:34** Jesus said to him, "Amen, I tell you that on this night, before a rooster crows, you will deny Me three times."

**26:35** Peter said to Him, "Even if it is necessary for me to die with You, I will not deny You." All the disciples also spoke in the same way.

**26:36** Then Jesus came with them to an estate that is called Gethsemane, and He said to His disciples, "Remain seated here while I go over there and pray."

**26:37** And taking along Peter and the two sons of Zebedee, He began to grow sorrowful and heavy-hearted.

**26:38** Then He said to them, "My soul is sorrowful to the point of death; wait here and stay awake with Me."

**26:39** And advancing a little farther, He fell onto His face, praying and saying, "My Father, if it is possible, may this cup pass from Me; yet not as I will, but as You do."

**26:40** And He came to His disciples and found them sleeping, and He said to Peter, "So were you not able to stay awake with Me for one hour?

**26:41** Stay awake and pray so that you do not enter into temptation. The spirit indeed is ready, yet the flesh is weak."

**26:42** Again, a second time, He went away and prayed, saying, "My Father, if this cup cannot pass unless I drink it, may Your will be done."

**26:43** And He came again and found them sleeping, for their eyes were weighed down.

**26:44** And having left them, He went away again and prayed a third time, saying the same words.

**26:45** Then He came to His disciples and said to them, "Sleep now and rest; look, the hour has drawn near, and the Son of Man will be handed over into the hands of sinners.

**26:46** Get up, let us go; look, the one who will hand Me over has drawn near."

**26:47** While He was still speaking, look, Judas, one of the twelve, came, and with him a large crowd with swords and clubs, sent by the chief priests and the elders of the people.

**26:48** Now the one who handed Him over gave them a sign, saying, "Whoever I kiss, He is the one; seize Him."

**26:49** And immediately approaching Jesus, he said, "Greetings, Rabbi." And he kissed Him.

**26:50** And Jesus said to him, "Friend, for what purpose have you come?" Then they approached, laid hands on Jesus, and seized Him.

**26:51** And look, one of those who were with Jesus, stretching out his hand, drew out his sword, and striking the servant of the high priest, cut off his ear.

**26:52** Then Jesus said to him, "Return your sword to its place, for all those who take the sword will perish by the sword.

**26:53** Or do you suppose that I cannot ask My Father, and He will provide for Me right now more than twelve legions of angels?

**26:54** How then will the Scriptures be fulfilled, that it must come about in this way?"

**26:55** In that hour Jesus said to the crowds, "You have come out as against a robber with swords and clubs to capture Me; every day I sat with you teaching in the temple, and you did not seize Me."

**26:56** Now all of this happened so that the Scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled. Then all the disciples, having left Him, fled.

**26:57** And those who had seized Jesus led Him to Caiaphas the high priest, where the scribes and the elders had assembled.

**26:58** But Peter was following Him from a distance, all the way into the courtyard of the high priest. And entering inside, he was sitting with the attendants to see the outcome.

**26:59** Now the chief priests and the whole council were seeking false testimony against Jesus so that they might hand Him over to death,

**26:60** and they found none, even though many false witnesses had come forward. Last of all, however, two false witnesses came,

**26:61** and said, "This man said, 'I am able to destroy the temple of God and after three days rebuild it.'"

**26:62** And standing up, the high priest said to Him, "Do You answer nothing to the things that these men are testifying against You?"

**26:63** But Jesus remained silent. And the high priest said to Him, "I put You under oath by the living God to tell us whether You are the Christ, the Son of God."

**26:64** Jesus said to him, "You have said it. Nevertheless, I tell you, from now on you will see the Son of Man sitting at the right hand of the power of God and coming in the clouds of heaven."

**26:65** Then the high priest tore his garments, saying, "He has blasphemed! What further need do we have of witnesses? Look, now you have heard the blasphemy;

**26:66** what is your opinion?" And answering, they said, "He is deserving of death."

**26:67** Then they spat in His face and beat Him with their fists, while others struck His face with the palms of their hands,

**26:68** saying, "Prophesy to us, Christ: who is the one who struck You?"

**26:69** Now Peter was sitting outside in the courtyard, and one servant girl approached him, saying, "You too were with Jesus the Galilean."

**26:70** But he denied it in front of everyone, saying, "I do not know what you are saying."

**26:71** Now as he was going out the gateway, another servant girl saw him and said to those who were there, "This man too was with Jesus the Nazarene."

**26:72** And again he denied it with an oath: "I do not know the man."

**26:73** And after a little while, those who were standing there approached and said to Peter, "Truly you too are one of them, for even your speech reveals you."

**26:74** Then he began to invoke curses and swear that he did not know the man. And at once a rooster crowed.

**26:75** And Peter remembered the word of Jesus that He had spoken: "Before a rooster crows, you will deny Me three times." And going outside, he wept bitterly.

## Translator Notes

- **26:15** — The Vulgate uses constituerunt ("assigned," "appointed," or "set"), emphasizing the fixing of an agreed payment rather than physically weighing out the silver.
- **26:20** — The Clementine Vulgate explicitly reads cum duodecim discipulis suis ("with His twelve disciples"), where critical Greek texts read simply "with the twelve."
- **26:28** — The Clementine Vulgate retains novi ("new") in novi testamenti ("of the new covenant") and uses the future passive effundetur ("will be poured out"), whereas critical Greek editions omit "new" and use a present participle.
- **26:31** — The Vulgate translates the Greek passive verb periphrastically as scandalum patiemini ("will suffer stumbling" or "scandal"), distinguishing it from the passive verb forms of scandalizo in 26:33.
- **26:50** — The Vulgate translates the ambiguous Greek clause ep' ho parei clearly as a direct question, ad quid venisti? ("for what purpose have you come?"), whereas many translations from the Greek render it as a command ("do what you have come for").
- **26:61** — The Clementine Vulgate reads post triduum ("after three days") and reaedificare ("rebuild"), whereas the Greek reads dia trion hemeron ("within three days") and oikodomesai ("build").
- **26:62** — The Vulgate combines the high priest's words into a single question (Nihil respondes ad ea, quae isti adversum te testificantur?), whereas the Greek divides them into two separate questions.
- **26:64** — The Clementine Vulgate explicitly includes Dei ("of God") in virtutis Dei ("of the power of God") and uses in ("in the clouds of heaven"), whereas critical Greek texts read simply "of Power" and "on the clouds of heaven."
