## Text

**1:1** The book of the lineage of Jesus Christ, son of David, son of Abraham.

**1:2** Abraham fathered Isaac, and Isaac fathered Jacob, and Jacob fathered Judah and his brothers.

**1:3** Judah fathered Perez and Zerah by Tamar, and Perez fathered Hezron, and Hezron fathered Ram.

**1:4** Ram fathered Amminadab, and Amminadab fathered Nahshon, and Nahshon fathered Salmon.

**1:5** Salmon fathered Boaz by Rahab, and Boaz fathered Obed by Ruth, and Obed fathered Jesse, and Jesse fathered David the king.

**1:6** David the king fathered Solomon by her who had been Uriah's.

**1:7** Solomon fathered Rehoboam, and Rehoboam fathered Abijah, and Abijah fathered Asa.

**1:8** Asa fathered Jehoshaphat, and Jehoshaphat fathered Joram, and Joram fathered Uzziah.

**1:9** Uzziah fathered Jotham, and Jotham fathered Ahaz, and Ahaz fathered Hezekiah.

**1:10** Hezekiah fathered Manasseh, and Manasseh fathered Amon, and Amon fathered Josiah.

**1:11** Josiah fathered Jeconiah and his brothers during the deportation to Babylon.

**1:12** And after the deportation to Babylon, Jeconiah fathered Shealtiel, and Shealtiel fathered Zerubbabel.

**1:13** Zerubbabel fathered Abiud, and Abiud fathered Eliakim, and Eliakim fathered Azor.

**1:14** Azor fathered Zadok, and Zadok fathered Achim, and Achim fathered Eliud.

**1:15** Eliud fathered Eleazar, and Eleazar fathered Matthan, and Matthan fathered Jacob.

**1:16** Jacob fathered Joseph, the husband of Mary, from whom Jesus was born, who is called Christ.

**1:17** Therefore all the generations from Abraham up to David are fourteen generations; and from David up to the deportation to Babylon, fourteen generations; and from the deportation to Babylon up to Christ, fourteen generations.

**1:18** Now the birth of Christ was in this manner: when His mother Mary had been betrothed to Joseph, before they came together, she was found pregnant from the Holy Spirit.

**1:19** Now Joseph, her husband, since he was righteous and did not wish to expose her publicly, intended to send her away secretly.

**1:20** While he was pondering these things, look, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream, saying, "Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife, for what has been conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit.

**1:21** And she will bear a son, and you will call His name Jesus, for He Himself will save His people from their sins."

**1:22** Now all this happened so that what was spoken by the Lord through the prophet might be fulfilled, saying,

**1:23** "Look, the virgin will be pregnant and will bear a son, and they will call His name Emmanuel," which is translated, "God with us."

**1:24** Now Joseph, rising from sleep, did as the angel of the Lord commanded him, and took his wife.

**1:25** And he did not know her until she bore her firstborn son, and he called His name Jesus.

## Translator Notes

- **1:6** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "David autem rex" ("David the king"), retaining the royal title here, and literally refers to Bathsheba as "ex ea quæ fuit Uriæ" ("her who had been Uriah's") without supplying the word "wife."
- **1:18** — The Clementine Vulgate reads simply "Christi" ("of Christ") rather than "of Jesus Christ," and uses "in utero habens" (literally "having in the womb," i.e., pregnant), matching "in utero habebit" in v. 23.
- **1:20** — Latin "natum est" (literally "has been born" or "has come into being") renders Greek "gennēthen" ("begotten/conceived"), translated here as "has been conceived" to fit the prenatal context.
- **1:25** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "filium suum primogenitum" ("her firstborn son"), preserving the fuller reading alongside the imperfect tense "non cognoscebat" ("did not know").
