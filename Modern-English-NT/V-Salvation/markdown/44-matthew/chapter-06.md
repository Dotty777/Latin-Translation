## Text

**6:1** "Watch out that you do not practice your righteousness in front of people in order to be seen by them; otherwise you will have no reward with your Father who is in heaven.

**6:2** Therefore, when you give alms, do not blow a trumpet ahead of yourself, just as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets, so that they may be honored by people. Amen, I tell you, they have received their reward.

**6:3** But while you are giving alms, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing,

**6:4** so that your almsgiving may be in secret, and your Father, who sees in secret, will repay you.

**6:5** And when you pray, you shall not be like the hypocrites, who love standing in the synagogues and at the corners of the broad streets to pray, so that they may be seen by people. Amen, I tell you, they have received their reward.

**6:6** But as for you, when you pray, enter your inner room, and with the door shut, pray to your Father in secret; and your Father, who sees in secret, will repay you.

**6:7** And while praying, do not speak much, like the Gentiles, for they think that through their much speaking they will be heard.

**6:8** Therefore do not become like them, for your Father knows what you need before you ask Him.

**6:9** Therefore you shall pray like this: 'Our Father, who are in heaven, may Your name be sanctified.

**6:10** May Your kingdom come; may Your will be done, just as in heaven, also on earth.

**6:11** Give us today our supersubstantial bread,

**6:12** and forgive us our debts, just as we also forgive our debtors.

**6:13** And do not lead us into temptation, but rescue us from evil. Amen.'

**6:14** For if you forgive people their sins, your heavenly Father will also forgive you your offenses.

**6:15** But if you do not forgive people, neither will your Father forgive you your sins.

**6:16** And when you fast, do not become gloomy like the hypocrites. For they disfigure their faces so that they may appear to people to be fasting. Amen, I tell you that they have received their reward.

**6:17** But as for you, when you fast, anoint your head and wash your face,

**6:18** so that you may not appear to people to be fasting, but to your Father who is in secret; and your Father, who sees in secret, will repay you.

**6:19** Do not store up treasures for yourselves on earth, where rust and moth destroy, and where thieves dig through and steal.

**6:20** But store up treasures for yourselves in heaven, where neither rust nor moth destroys, and where thieves do not dig through or steal.

**6:21** For where your treasure is, there your heart is also.

**6:22** The lamp of your body is your eye. If your eye is sound, your whole body will be filled with light.

**6:23** But if your eye is evil, your whole body will be filled with darkness. If therefore the light that is in you is darkness, how great will that darkness itself be?

**6:24** No one can serve two masters; for either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will uphold the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and mammon.

**6:25** For this reason I tell you: do not be anxious for your life, what you will eat, nor for your body, what you will put on. Is not life more than food, and the body more than clothing?

**6:26** Look at the birds of the sky, for they neither sow nor reap nor gather into barns, and your heavenly Father feeds them. Are you not worth much more than they?

**6:27** And which of you, by thinking, can add one cubit to his stature?

**6:28** And why are you anxious about clothing? Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow: they neither toil nor spin.

**6:29** Yet I tell you that not even Solomon in all his glory was clothed like one of these.

**6:30** But if God so clothes the grass of the field, which is here today and tomorrow is thrown into the oven, how much more you, you of little faith?

**6:31** Therefore do not be anxious, saying, 'What will we eat, or what will we drink, or with what will we be clothed?'

**6:32** For the nations seek after all these things. For your Father knows that you need all these things.

**6:33** So then, seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things will be added to you.

**6:34** Therefore do not be anxious for tomorrow. For tomorrow's day will be anxious for itself; its own trouble is enough for the day."

## Translator Notes

- **6:11** — Vulgate-distinct reading: Jerome coined supersubstantialem ("supersubstantial" or "above-essential") to render Greek epiousion here, whereas in Luke 11:3 he translated the same Greek term as quotidianum ("daily").
- **6:13** — Latin a malo can mean either "from evil" (neuter) or "from the evil one" (masculine). The Clementine Vulgate ends the prayer with "Amen" without the liturgical doxology found in later Greek manuscripts.
- **6:19** — The Vulgate places "rust" (aerugo) before "moth" (tinea), reversing the Greek order ("moth and rust"), and uses effodiunt (literally "dig through") for thieves breaking through mud-brick walls.
- **6:24** — Latin sustinebit literally means "will uphold" or "will bear with," and mammonae ("mammon") preserves an Aramaic loanword referring to wealth or money.
- **6:27** — The Vulgate uses cogitans ("by thinking"), differing from sollicitus ("anxious") in nearby verses, and staturam, which specifically means physical height ("stature") rather than lifespan.
