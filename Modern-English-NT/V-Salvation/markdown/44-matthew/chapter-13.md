## Text

**13:1** On that day, going out from the house, Jesus was sitting beside the sea.

**13:2** And many crowds were gathered to Him, so that He stepped into a boat and sat down, and the whole crowd was standing on the shore.

**13:3** And He spoke many things to them in parables, saying, "Look, the one who sows went out to sow.

**13:4** And while he was sowing, some fell beside the path, and the birds of the sky came and ate them.

**13:5** Others, however, fell onto rocky places, where they did not have much soil, and they immediately sprang up because they did not have depth of soil.

**13:6** But when the sun had risen, they were scorched, and because they did not have a root, they dried up.

**13:7** Still others fell among thorns, and the thorns grew up and choked them.

**13:8** And others fell onto good soil and were yielding fruit: one a hundredfold, another sixtyfold, another thirtyfold.

**13:9** The one who has ears to hear, let him hear."

**13:10** And the disciples approached and said to Him, "Why do You speak to them in parables?"

**13:11** Answering, He said to them, "Because to you it has been given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, yet to them it has not been given.

**13:12** For whoever has, to him it will be given, and he will have an abundance; yet whoever does not have, even what he has will be taken away from him.

**13:13** For this reason I speak to them in parables: because seeing they do not see, and hearing they do not hear, nor do they understand.

**13:14** And in them the prophecy of Isaiah is fulfilled, who says, 'With hearing you will hear and will not understand, and seeing you will see and will not see.

**13:15** For the heart of this people has grown dull, and with their ears they have heard with difficulty, and they have closed their eyes, so that they would never see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and turn back, and I would heal them.'

**13:16** Yet blessed are your eyes, because they see, and your ears, because they hear.

**13:17** For amen, I tell you that many prophets and righteous ones longed to see what you see, and did not see it, and to hear what you hear, and did not hear it.

**13:18** You, therefore, hear the parable of the one who sows.

**13:19** Everyone who hears the word of the kingdom and does not understand: the evil one comes and snatches away what was sown in his heart. This is the one who was sown beside the path.

**13:20** And the one who was sown on rocky places, this is the one who hears the word and immediately receives it with joy.

**13:21** Yet he has no root in himself, but is temporary; and when tribulation and persecution occur because of the word, he immediately stumbles.

**13:22** And the one who was sown among thorns, this is the one who hears the word, and the anxiety of this age and the deceitfulness of wealth choke the word, and it becomes unfruitful.

**13:23** But the one who was sown onto good soil, this is the one who hears the word and understands, and bears fruit and yields: one a hundredfold, another sixtyfold, and another thirtyfold."

**13:24** He set another parable before them, saying, "The kingdom of heaven has become like a man who sowed good seed in his field.

**13:25** But while people were sleeping, his enemy came and sowed weeds in the midst of the wheat, and went away.

**13:26** And when the blade had grown and had produced fruit, then the weeds also appeared.

**13:27** Then the servants of the master of the household came up and said to him, 'Master, did you not sow good seed in your field? From where, then, does it have weeds?'

**13:28** And he said to them, 'An enemy has done this.' Then the servants said to him, 'Do you want us to go and gather them?'

**13:29** And he said, 'No, for fear that while gathering the weeds you might uproot the wheat along with them as well.

**13:30** Let both grow until the harvest, and at the time of harvest I will say to the harvesters, "Gather the weeds first and bind them into bundles for burning, yet gather the wheat together into my granary."'"

**13:31** He set another parable before them, saying, "The kingdom of heaven is like a mustard seed, which a man took and sowed in his field.

**13:32** It is indeed the smallest of all seeds, yet when it has grown, it is larger than all garden plants and becomes a tree, so that the birds of the sky come and dwell in its branches."

**13:33** He spoke another parable to them: "The kingdom of heaven is like leaven, which a woman took and hid in three measures of flour until all of it was leavened."

**13:34** Jesus spoke all these things in parables to the crowds, and without parables He would not speak to them,

**13:35** so that what had been spoken through the prophet might be fulfilled, saying, "I will open My mouth in parables; I will pour forth things hidden since the foundation of the world."

**13:36** Then, having dismissed the crowds, He came into the house, and His disciples came up to Him, saying, "Explain to us the parable of the weeds of the field."

**13:37** Answering, He said to them, "The one who sows the good seed is the Son of Man.

**13:38** And the field is the world; the good seed, these are the sons of the kingdom; and the weeds are the sons of the wicked one.

**13:39** And the enemy who sowed them is the devil; the harvest is the end of the age; and the harvesters are angels.

**13:40** Therefore, just as the weeds are gathered and burned with fire, so it will be at the end of the age.

**13:41** The Son of Man will send His angels, and they will gather out of His kingdom all causes of stumbling and those who commit iniquity,

**13:42** and they will throw them into the furnace of fire. In that place will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.

**13:43** Then the righteous will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father. The one who has ears to hear, let him hear.

**13:44** The kingdom of heaven is like a treasure hidden in a field, which a man found and hid; and for joy over it he goes and sells everything that he has, and buys that field.

**13:45** Again, the kingdom of heaven is like a man who is a merchant, seeking good pearls.

**13:46** And having found one precious pearl, he went away and sold all that he had, and bought it.

**13:47** Again, the kingdom of heaven is like a dragnet thrown into the sea and gathering from every kind of fish.

**13:48** When it was full, pulling it out and sitting beside the shore, they picked out the good ones into containers, yet the bad ones they threw out.

**13:49** So it will be at the end of the age: the angels will go out and separate the evil from the midst of the righteous,

**13:50** and they will throw them into the furnace of fire; in that place will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.

**13:51** Have you understood all these things?" They say to Him, "Yes."

**13:52** He said to them, "Therefore every scribe instructed in the kingdom of heaven is like a man who is master of a household, who brings out from his treasury new things and old."

**13:53** And it happened that when Jesus had finished these parables, He departed from there.

**13:54** And coming into His own homeland, He was teaching them in their synagogues, so that they were astonished and said, "From where does this Man have this wisdom and mighty works?

**13:55** Is He not the carpenter's son? Is not His mother called Mary, and His brothers James, and Joseph, and Simon, and Judas?

**13:56** And His sisters, are they not all with us? From where, then, does this Man have all these things?"

**13:57** And they were taking offense at Him. But Jesus said to them, "A prophet is not without honor except in his own homeland and in his own house."

**13:58** And He did not do many mighty works there because of their unbelief.

## Translator Notes

- **13:9** — The Vulgate includes the gerund audiendi ("to hear") here and in 13:43, whereas the critical Greek text simply reads "ears" without "to hear."
- **13:25** — Latin zizania denotes darnel (Lolium temulentum), a noxious weed closely resembling wheat until maturity; it is rendered "weeds" to preserve the Latin plural grammar across the parable.
- **13:47** — The Vulgate explicitly adds piscium ("of fish") in ex omni genere piscium, where the Greek text reads simply "of every kind."
- **13:48** — The Clementine Vulgate reads the dative/ablative form bonis where accusative bonos ("the good ones," parallel to malos, "the bad ones") is grammatically expected and found in older Vulgate manuscripts.
- **13:54** — The Vulgate reads plural in synagogis eorum ("in their synagogues"), whereas the Greek text has singular "in their synagogue."
