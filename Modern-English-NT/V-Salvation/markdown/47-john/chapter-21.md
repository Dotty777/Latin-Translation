## Text

**21:1** Afterward Jesus manifested Himself again to the disciples by the Sea of Tiberias. Now He manifested Himself in this manner:

**21:2** Simon Peter, and Thomas, who is called Didymus, and Nathanael, who was from Cana of Galilee, and the sons of Zebedee, and two others of His disciples were together.

**21:3** Simon Peter said to them, "I am going to fish." They said to him, "We are also coming with you." And they went out and boarded the boat, and that night they caught nothing.

**21:4** Now when morning had come, Jesus stood on the shore; yet the disciples did not recognize that it was Jesus.

**21:5** Therefore Jesus said to them, "Children, you do not have anything to eat, do you?" They replied to Him, "No."

**21:6** He said to them, "Throw the net on the right side of the boat, and you will find some." Therefore they threw it, and they were no longer able to pull it in because of the multitude of fish.

**21:7** Therefore that disciple whom Jesus loved said to Peter, "It is the Lord." When Simon Peter heard that it was the Lord, he fastened his tunic around himself (for he was naked) and threw himself into the sea.

**21:8** But the other disciples came in the boat (for they were not far from land, but about two hundred cubits), pulling the net of fish.

**21:9** So when they stepped down onto the land, they saw glowing coals placed there, and fish placed upon them, and bread.

**21:10** Jesus said to them, "Bring some of the fish that you have caught just now."

**21:11** Simon Peter went up and pulled the net onto the land, full of large fish, one hundred fifty-three; and even though there were so many, the net was not torn.

**21:12** Jesus said to them, "Come, have breakfast." And none of those dining dared to question Him, "Who are You?" knowing that it was the Lord.

**21:13** And Jesus came, took the bread, and gave it to them, and the fish in the same way.

**21:14** This was now the third time that Jesus was manifested to His disciples after He had risen from the dead.

**21:15** So when they had eaten breakfast, Jesus said to Simon Peter, "Simon, son of John, do you love Me more than these?" He said to Him, "Yes, Lord, You know that I love You." He said to him, "Feed My lambs."

**21:16** He said to him again, "Simon, son of John, do you love Me?" He said to Him, "Yes, Lord, You know that I love You." He said to him, "Feed My lambs."

**21:17** He said to him the third time, "Simon, son of John, do you love Me?" Peter was saddened because He said to him the third time, "Do you love Me?" and he said to Him, "Lord, You know all things; You know that I love You." He said to him, "Feed My sheep.

**21:18** Amen, amen, I tell you, when you were younger, you used to fasten your own belt and walk where you wished; but when you grow old, you will stretch out your hands, and another will fasten your belt and lead you where you do not wish to go."

**21:19** Now He said this, signifying by what death he would glorify God. And when He had said this, He said to him, "Follow Me."

**21:20** Turning around, Peter saw that disciple whom Jesus loved following, the one who had also leaned back upon His chest at the supper and said, "Lord, who is the one who will betray You?"

**21:21** Therefore when Peter saw him, he said to Jesus, "Lord, but what about this man?"

**21:22** Jesus said to him, "So I want him to remain until I come; what is that to you? You follow Me."

**21:23** Therefore this word went out among the brothers that that disciple does not die. And Jesus did not say to him, "He does not die," but, "So I want him to remain until I come; what is that to you?"

**21:24** This is that disciple who bears testimony about these things and wrote these things, and we know that his testimony is true.

**21:25** Now there are also many other things that Jesus did, and if these were written one by one, I think that not even the world itself could hold the books that would be written.

## Translator Notes

- **21:5** — Latin "pulmentarium" refers to food or relish eaten with bread (often fish), rather than naming fish directly.
- **21:12** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "of those dining" (discumbentium, literally "of those reclining at meal"), whereas the Greek text reads "of the disciples."
- **21:15** — Here and in v. 16 Jesus uses "diligere" (devoted or esteeming love), while Peter replies with "amare" (warm, affectionate love); in v. 17 Jesus switches to "amare" as well.
- **21:16** — The Clementine Vulgate reads "Feed My lambs" (Pasce agnos meos) in both vv. 15 and 16 and uses "pasce" ("feed") in all three commands, whereas the Greek text reads "Shepherd My sheep" in v. 16.
- **21:22** — Here and in v. 23, the Clementine Vulgate reads "Sic" ("So" / "Thus"), expressing a statement ("So I want him to remain..."), whereas the Greek text reads "If" (ean).
