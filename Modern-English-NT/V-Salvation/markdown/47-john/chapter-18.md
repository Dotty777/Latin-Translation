## Text

**18:1** When Jesus had said these things, He went out with His disciples across the Kidron stream, where there was a garden, into which He Himself and His disciples entered.

**18:2** Now Judas also, who was betraying Him, knew the place, because Jesus had frequently met there with His disciples.

**18:3** Judas, therefore, having taken a cohort and officers from the chief priests and Pharisees, came there with lanterns and torches and weapons.

**18:4** Jesus, therefore, knowing all things that were about to come upon Him, stepped forward and said to them, "Whom are you seeking?"

**18:5** They answered Him, "Jesus the Nazarene." Jesus said to them, "I am." Now Judas also, who was betraying Him, was standing with them.

**18:6** So when He said to them, "I am," they went backward and fell to the ground.

**18:7** Therefore He questioned them again, "Whom are you seeking?" And they said, "Jesus the Nazarene."

**18:8** Jesus answered, "I told you that I am. Therefore, if you are seeking Me, let these go away."

**18:9** This was so that the word He spoke might be fulfilled: "Of those whom You have given Me, I have not lost a single one."

**18:10** Simon Peter, therefore, having a sword, drew it and struck the high priest's servant, and cut off his right ear. Now the servant's name was Malchus.

**18:11** Jesus therefore said to Peter, "Put your sword into the sheath. The cup that the Father has given Me, will I not drink it?"

**18:12** So the cohort, and the tribune, and the officers of the Jews seized Jesus and bound Him.

**18:13** And they led Him to Annas first, for he was the father-in-law of Caiaphas, who was the high priest of that year.

**18:14** Now Caiaphas was the one who had given counsel to the Jews that it is expedient for one man to die for the people.

**18:15** Now Simon Peter was following Jesus, and so was another disciple. That disciple was known to the high priest, and he entered with Jesus into the courtyard of the high priest.

**18:16** But Peter was standing at the door outside. Therefore the other disciple, who was known to the high priest, went out and spoke to the doorkeeper, and brought Peter in.

**18:17** Then the servant girl who kept the door said to Peter, "You are not also one of this man's disciples, are you?" He said, "I am not."

**18:18** Now the servants and the officers were standing by the burning coals, because it was cold, and were warming themselves; and Peter also was with them, standing and warming himself.

**18:19** The high priest therefore questioned Jesus about His disciples and about His teaching.

**18:20** Jesus answered him, "I have spoken openly to the world. I always taught in the synagogue and in the temple, where all the Jews come together, and in secret I have spoken nothing.

**18:21** Why do you question Me? Question those who heard what I spoke to them; see, these people know what I said."

**18:22** Now when He had said these things, one of the officers standing nearby gave Jesus a slap, saying, "Do You answer the high priest like this?"

**18:23** Jesus answered him, "If I have spoken wrongly, bear witness about the wrong; but if well, why do you strike Me?"

**18:24** And Annas sent Him bound to Caiaphas the high priest.

**18:25** Now Simon Peter was standing and warming himself. So they said to him, "You are not also one of His disciples, are you?" He denied it and said, "I am not."

**18:26** One of the servants of the high priest, a relative of the man whose ear Peter cut off, said to him, "Did I not see you in the garden with Him?"

**18:27** Peter therefore denied it again, and immediately a rooster crowed.

**18:28** Then they led Jesus from Caiaphas into the Praetorium. Now it was early morning, and they themselves did not enter the Praetorium, so that they would not be defiled, but might eat the Passover.

**18:29** Pilate therefore went outside to them and said, "What accusation do you bring against this man?"

**18:30** They answered and said to him, "If this man were not a wrongdoer, we would not have handed Him over to you."

**18:31** So Pilate said to them, "Take Him yourselves, and judge Him according to your law." The Jews therefore said to him, "It is not lawful for us to put anyone to death."

**18:32** This was so that the word of Jesus might be fulfilled, which He spoke, indicating by what death He was about to die.

**18:33** Pilate therefore entered the Praetorium again, and summoned Jesus, and said to Him, "Are You the King of the Jews?"

**18:34** Jesus answered, "Are you saying this on your own, or did others tell you about Me?"

**18:35** Pilate answered, "I am not a Jew, am I? Your nation and the chief priests handed You over to me. What have You done?"

**18:36** Jesus answered, "My kingdom is not of this world. If My kingdom were of this world, My attendants would certainly be fighting so that I would not be handed over to the Jews; now, however, My kingdom is not from here."

**18:37** Pilate therefore said to Him, "So You are a king?" Jesus answered, "You say that I am a king. For this I was born, and for this I came into the world, that I may bear witness to the truth. Everyone who is of the truth hears My voice."

**18:38** Pilate said to Him, "What is truth?" And when he had said this, he went out again to the Jews and said to them, "I find no grounds for a charge in Him.

**18:39** Now you have a custom that I should release one man for you at the Passover. Do you want me, therefore, to release for you the King of the Jews?"

**18:40** Therefore they all cried out again, saying, "Not this man, but Barabbas!" Now Barabbas was a robber.

## Translator Notes

- **18:5** — Latin Ego sum literally means "I am" without an expressed predicate "he" (likewise in 18:6 and 18:8).
- **18:18** — The Vulgate reads simply ad prunas ("by the burning coals"), omitting the Greek participial clause "having made a charcoal fire."
- **18:30** — The Vulgate uses the noun malefactor ("a wrongdoer" or "evildoer"), corresponding to the Byzantine/Textus Receptus reading rather than the critical Greek participial phrase "doing evil."
- **18:38** — Latin causam carries a legal sense ("ground for a charge," "case," or "fault"); in 18:40 the Vulgate also includes omnes ("all"), which is absent in critical Greek editions.
