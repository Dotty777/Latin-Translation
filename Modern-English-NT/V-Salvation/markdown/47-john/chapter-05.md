## Text

**5:1** After these things, there was a feast day of the Jews, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.

**5:2** Now there is in Jerusalem a sheep pool, which in Hebrew is called Bethsaida, having five porticoes.

**5:3** In these lay a great multitude of the sick, the blind, the lame, and the withered, waiting for the movement of the water.

**5:5** Now a certain man was there who had been in his infirmity for thirty-eight years.

**5:6** Now when Jesus saw this man lying there, and realized that he had already been that way for a long time, He said to him, "Do you want to become well?"

**5:7** The sick man answered Him, "Sir, I do not have a man to put me into the pool when the water is disturbed; for while I am coming, another goes down before me."

**5:8** Jesus said to him, "Rise, pick up your cot, and walk."

**5:9** And immediately that man became well, and he picked up his cot and began walking. Now on that day it was the Sabbath.

**5:10** Therefore the Jews were saying to the one who had been healed, "It is the Sabbath; it is not lawful for you to carry your cot."

**5:11** He answered them, "The One who made me well, He said to me, 'Pick up your cot and walk.'"

**5:12** Therefore they questioned him, "Who is that man who said to you, 'Pick up your cot and walk'?"

**5:13** Now the one who had been made well did not know who He was, for Jesus had withdrawn from the crowd present in that place.

**5:14** Later Jesus found him in the temple and said to him, "Look, you have been made well; do not sin any longer, so that something worse does not happen to you."

**5:15** That man went away and reported to the Jews that it was Jesus who made him well.

**5:16** On this account, the Jews were persecuting Jesus, because He was doing these things on the Sabbath.

**5:17** But Jesus replied to them, "My Father is working up to now, and I am working."

**5:18** On this account, therefore, the Jews were seeking even more to kill Him, because He was not only breaking the Sabbath, but also calling God His own Father, making Himself equal to God. So Jesus answered and said to them:

**5:19** "Amen, amen, I tell you, the Son is not able to do anything on His own, except what He sees the Father doing; for whatever He does, these things the Son likewise does.

**5:20** For the Father loves the Son and shows Him everything that He Himself does; and He will show Him works greater than these, so that you may be amazed.

**5:21** For just as the Father raises up the dead and gives life, so too the Son gives life to those whom He wills.

**5:22** For neither does the Father judge anyone, but He gave all judgment to the Son,

**5:23** in order that all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father; the one who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent Him.

**5:24** Amen, amen, I tell you that the one who hears My word and believes the One who sent Me has eternal life, and does not come into judgment, but has crossed over from death into life.

**5:25** Amen, amen, I tell you that the hour is coming, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who have heard will live.

**5:26** For as the Father has life within Himself, so He also gave the Son to have life within Himself;

**5:27** and He gave Him authority to pass judgment, because He is the Son of Man.

**5:28** Do not be amazed at this, because an hour is coming in which all who are in the tombs will hear the voice of the Son of God,

**5:29** and they will come forth: those who have done good to a resurrection of life, but those who have committed evil to a resurrection of judgment.

**5:30** I am not able to do anything on My own. Just as I hear, so I judge; and My judgment is righteous, because I do not seek My will, but the will of the One who sent Me.

**5:31** If I bear testimony about Myself, My testimony is not true.

**5:32** There is Another who bears testimony about Me, and I know that the testimony He bears about Me is true.

**5:33** You sent to John, and he bore testimony to the truth.

**5:34** Yet I do not receive testimony from a human being; rather, I say these things in order that you may be saved.

**5:35** He was a burning and shining lamp, yet you were willing for an hour to exult in his light.

**5:36** But I have a testimony greater than John's. For the works that the Father gave Me so that I might complete them, the very works themselves that I do, bear testimony about Me that the Father sent Me.

**5:37** And the Father who sent Me, He Himself bore testimony about Me. Neither have you ever heard His voice, nor have you seen His appearance,

**5:38** and you do not have His word remaining in you, because you do not believe the One whom He sent.

**5:39** You search the Scriptures, because you suppose that you have eternal life in them; and those are the ones that bear testimony about Me,

**5:40** and yet you do not want to come to Me so that you may have life.

**5:41** I do not receive renown from people.

**5:42** But I have come to know you, that you do not have the love of God within you.

**5:43** I have come in the name of My Father, and you do not receive Me; if someone else comes in his own name, that one you will receive.

**5:44** How are you able to believe, you who receive glory from one another and do not seek the glory that is from God alone?

**5:45** Do not suppose that I am going to accuse you before the Father; there is one who accuses you, Moses, in whom you hope.

**5:46** For if you believed Moses, you would perhaps believe Me as well; for he wrote about Me.

**5:47** If, however, you do not believe his writings, how will you believe My words?"

## Translator Notes

- **5:2** — The Clementine Vulgate reads probatica piscina ("a sheep pool," treating probatica as an adjective modifying "pool" rather than "by the Sheep Gate") and names the pool Bethsaida rather than Bethesda or Bethzatha.
- **5:18** — In the Clementine Vulgate verse division, the clause introducing Jesus's discourse (Respondit itaque Jesus, et dixit eis) belongs to the end of verse 18 rather than the beginning of verse 19.
- **5:28** — The Vulgate distinctly reads vocem Filii Dei ("the voice of the Son of God"), echoing verse 25, whereas the Greek text reads "his voice."
- **5:39** — Latin Scrutamini (like the underlying Greek) is morphologically ambiguous and can be translated either as an indicative ("You search") or as an imperative ("Search").
- **5:46** — The Vulgate translates the Greek conditional particle with forsitan ("perhaps"), a distinctive Latin nuance preserved here.
