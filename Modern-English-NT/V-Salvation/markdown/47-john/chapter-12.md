## Text

**12:1** Therefore, six days before the Passover, Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus had been dead, whom Jesus raised.

**12:2** Now they made a dinner for Him there, and Martha was serving, while Lazarus was one of those reclining with Him.

**12:3** Then Mary took a pound of costly ointment of genuine nard, and anointed the feet of Jesus, and wiped His feet with her hair; and the house was filled with the fragrance of the ointment.

**12:4** Then one of His disciples, Judas Iscariot, who was about to betray Him, said,

**12:5** "Why was this ointment not sold for three hundred denarii and given to the needy?"

**12:6** Now he said this, not because he cared about the needy, but because he was a thief, and having the money box, he carried the things that were put in.

**12:7** Therefore Jesus said, "Leave her alone, so that she may keep it for the day of My burial.

**12:8** For the poor you always have with you, but Me you do not always have."

**12:9** Then a large crowd of the Jews learned that He was there, and they came, not only on account of Jesus, but so that they might see Lazarus, whom He raised from the dead.

**12:10** Now the chief priests planned to kill Lazarus as well,

**12:11** because on account of him many of the Jews were going away and believing in Jesus.

**12:12** Now on the next day, a large crowd that had come to the feast day, when they heard that Jesus was coming to Jerusalem,

**12:13** took palm branches and went out to meet Him, and were shouting, "Hosanna! Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord, King of Israel!"

**12:14** And Jesus found a young donkey and sat upon it, just as it is written:

**12:15** "Do not be afraid, daughter of Zion; look, your King is coming, sitting upon the colt of a female donkey."

**12:16** His disciples did not understand these things at first, but when Jesus was glorified, then they remembered that these things had been written about Him and that they had done these things to Him.

**12:17** Therefore the crowd that was with Him when He called Lazarus out of the tomb and raised him from the dead was bearing witness.

**12:18** For this reason also the crowd came to meet Him, because they heard that He had done this sign.

**12:19** Therefore the Pharisees said to themselves, "Do you see that we are accomplishing nothing? Look, the whole world has gone after Him!"

**12:20** Now there were certain Gentiles among those who had gone up to worship on the feast day.

**12:21** So these approached Philip, who was from Bethsaida of Galilee, and were asking him, saying, "Sir, we want to see Jesus."

**12:22** Philip came and told Andrew; and in turn Andrew and Philip told Jesus.

**12:23** But Jesus answered them, saying, "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified.

**12:24** Amen, amen, I tell you, unless a grain of wheat, falling into the ground, dies,

**12:25** it remains by itself alone; but if it dies, it yields much fruit. The one who loves his life will lose it, and the one who hates his life in this world keeps it for eternal life.

**12:26** If anyone serves Me, let him follow Me; and where I am, there My servant will also be. If anyone serves Me, My Father will honor him.

**12:27** Now My soul is troubled. And what shall I say? Father, save Me from this hour. Yet for this reason I came to this hour:

**12:28** Father, glorify Your name." Then a voice came from heaven: "I have both glorified it, and I will glorify it again."

**12:29** Therefore the crowd that was standing by and had heard was saying that there had been thunder. Others were saying, "An angel has spoken to Him."

**12:30** Jesus answered and said, "This voice did not come on account of Me, but on account of you.

**12:31** Now is the judgment of the world; now the ruler of this world will be cast out.

**12:32** And I, if I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all things to Myself."

**12:33** (Now He was saying this, signifying by what death He was going to die.)

**12:34** The crowd answered Him, "We have heard from the law that the Christ remains forever; and how do You say, 'The Son of Man must be lifted up'? Who is this Son of Man?"

**12:35** Therefore Jesus said to them, "For a little while longer, the light is among you. Walk while you have the light, so that darkness may not overtake you; and the one who walks in darkness does not know where he is going.

**12:36** While you have the light, believe in the light, so that you may be sons of light." Jesus spoke these things, and He went away and hid Himself from them.

**12:37** Yet although He had performed so many signs before them, they were not believing in Him,

**12:38** so that the word of Isaiah the prophet might be fulfilled, which he spoke: "Lord, who believed our report? And to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?"

**12:39** For this reason they were not able to believe, because Isaiah said again:

**12:40** "He blinded their eyes and hardened their heart, so that they might not see with their eyes, and might not understand with their heart, and turn back, and I would heal them."

**12:41** Isaiah said these things when he saw His glory and spoke about Him.

**12:42** Nevertheless, even among the rulers many believed in Him, but because of the Pharisees they were not confessing it, so that they would not be cast out of the synagogue.

**12:43** For they loved the glory of men more than the glory of God.

**12:44** Now Jesus cried out and said, "The one who believes in Me does not believe in Me, but in Him who sent Me.

**12:45** And the one who sees Me sees Him who sent Me.

**12:46** I have come as light into the world, so that everyone who believes in Me may not remain in darkness.

**12:47** And if anyone hears My words and does not keep them, I do not judge him; for I came not to judge the world, but to save the world.

**12:48** The one who spurns Me and does not receive My words has one who judges him. The word that I have spoken, it will judge him on the last day.

**12:49** Because I have not spoken from Myself, but He who sent Me, the Father, Himself gave Me a commandment: what I should say and what I should speak.

**12:50** And I know that His commandment is eternal life; therefore, the things that I speak, just as the Father told Me, so I speak."

## Translator Notes

- **12:3** — Latin pistici (borrowing Greek pistikos) is a rare term whose exact meaning is debated; it is usually understood as "genuine," "pure," or "unadulterated."
- **12:19** — The Clementine Vulgate reads first-person plural proficimus ("we are accomplishing nothing") and punctuates the clause as a question, whereas the Greek reads second-person plural ("you are gaining nothing").
- **12:20** — The Clementine Vulgate reads gentiles ("Gentiles"), whereas the Greek text reads Hellenes ("Greeks").
- **12:25** — The Clementine Vulgate places the conclusion of the grain-of-wheat saying ("it remains by itself alone...") at the start of verse 25 rather than verse 24, and pairs future perdet ("will lose") with present custodit ("keeps").
- **12:32** — The Clementine Vulgate reads neuter plural omnia ("all things"), reflecting an ancient textual tradition, where the majority Greek text reads masculine plural pantas ("all people").
