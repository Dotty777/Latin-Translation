## Text

**6:1** After these things, Jesus departed across the Sea of Galilee, which is that of Tiberias.

**6:2** And a great multitude was following Him, because they were seeing the signs that He was performing upon those who were ill.

**6:3** So Jesus went up onto the mountain, and there He was sitting with His disciples.

**6:4** Now the Passover, the feast day of the Jews, was near.

**6:5** Therefore, when Jesus had raised His eyes and saw that a very great multitude was coming to Him, He said to Philip, "Where will we buy bread so that these people may eat?"

**6:6** Now He was saying this, testing him, for He Himself knew what He was going to do.

**6:7** Philip answered Him, "Two hundred denarii worth of bread is not enough for them, so that each one may receive a small amount."

**6:8** One of His disciples, Andrew, the brother of Simon Peter, said to Him,

**6:9** "There is one boy here who has five barley loaves and two fish; but what are these among so many?"

**6:10** Jesus therefore said, "Have the people recline." Now there was abundant grass in the place. So the men reclined, numbering about five thousand.

**6:11** So Jesus took the loaves, and when He had given thanks, He distributed them to those who were reclining, and in the same way also from the fish, as much as they wished.

**6:12** Now when they were filled, He said to His disciples, "Gather the fragments that were left over, so that they may not be lost."

**6:13** Therefore they gathered them and filled twelve baskets with the fragments from the five barley loaves that remained over from those who had eaten.

**6:14** So when those people had seen the sign that Jesus had performed, they were saying, "This One is truly the Prophet who is to come into the world."

**6:15** Jesus therefore, when He had realized that they were about to come to seize Him and make Him king, fled again to the mountain Himself alone.

**6:16** Now when it became evening, His disciples went down to the sea.

**6:17** And after they had boarded a boat, they came across the sea to Capernaum; and darkness had already fallen, and Jesus had not come to them.

**6:18** Now the sea, with a great wind blowing, was rising up.

**6:19** Therefore, when they had rowed about twenty-five or thirty stadia, they saw Jesus walking upon the sea and coming close to the boat, and they became afraid.

**6:20** But He said to them, "It is I; do not fear."

**6:21** Therefore they wanted to take Him into the boat, and at once the boat was at the land to which they were going.

**6:22** On the next day, the crowd that was standing on the other side of the sea saw that no other small boat was there except one, and that Jesus had not entered the boat with His disciples, but His disciples alone had departed.

**6:23** However, other boats arrived from Tiberias near the place where they ate the bread after the Lord gave thanks.

**6:24** Therefore, when the crowd saw that Jesus was not there, nor His disciples, they boarded the small boats and came to Capernaum, seeking Jesus.

**6:25** And when they had found Him on the other side of the sea, they said to Him, "Rabbi, when did You come here?"

**6:26** Jesus answered them and said, "Amen, amen, I tell you, you are seeking Me not because you saw signs, but because you ate from the loaves and were satisfied.

**6:27** Do not work for the food that perishes, but for that which endures to eternal life, which the Son of Man will give you. For God the Father has sealed Him."

**6:28** So they said to Him, "What shall we do to work the works of God?"

**6:29** Jesus replied and said to them, "This is the work of God: that you believe in Him whom He sent."

**6:30** Therefore they said to Him, "What sign, then, do You perform, so that we may see and believe You? What work do You do?

**6:31** Our fathers ate manna in the desert, just as it is written, 'Bread from heaven He gave them to eat.'"

**6:32** Jesus therefore said to them, "Amen, amen, I tell you, Moses did not give you the bread from heaven, but My Father is giving you the true bread from heaven.

**6:33** For the bread of God is that which descends from heaven and gives life to the world."

**6:34** So they said to Him, "Lord, give us this bread always."

**6:35** Now Jesus said to them, "I am the bread of life; the one who comes to Me will not hunger, and the one who believes in Me will never thirst.

**6:36** But I have told you that you have even seen Me, and you do not believe.

**6:37** Everything that the Father gives Me will come to Me, and the one who comes to Me I will not cast out.

**6:38** For I descended from heaven, not to do My own will, but the will of Him who sent Me.

**6:39** Now this is the will of Him who sent Me, the Father: that from everything He has given Me I lose nothing, but raise it up on the last day.

**6:40** And this is the will of My Father, who sent Me: that everyone who sees the Son and believes in Him may have eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day."

**6:41** Therefore the Jews were murmuring about Him, because He had said, "I am the living bread who descended from heaven."

**6:42** And they were saying, "Is this not Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know? How, then, does He say, 'I descended from heaven'?"

**6:43** Jesus therefore answered and said to them, "Do not murmur among yourselves.

**6:44** No one is able to come to Me unless the Father, who sent Me, draws him, and I will raise him up on the last day.

**6:45** It is written in the prophets, 'And all will be taught by God.' Everyone who has heard from the Father and learned comes to Me.

**6:46** Not that anyone has seen the Father, except Him who is from God; this One has seen the Father.

**6:47** Amen, amen, I tell you, the one who believes in Me has eternal life.

**6:48** I am the bread of life.

**6:49** Your fathers ate manna in the desert, and they died.

**6:50** This is the bread descending from heaven, so that if anyone eats from it, he may not die.

**6:51** I am the living bread who descended from heaven.

**6:52** If anyone eats from this bread, he will live forever; and the bread that I will give is My flesh for the life of the world."

**6:53** Therefore the Jews were disputing with one another, saying, "How is this man able to give us His flesh to eat?"

**6:54** Jesus therefore said to them, "Amen, amen, I tell you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink His blood, you will not have life in yourselves.

**6:55** The one who eats My flesh and drinks My blood has eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day.

**6:56** For My flesh truly is food, and My blood truly is drink.

**6:57** The one who eats My flesh and drinks My blood remains in Me, and I in him.

**6:58** Just as the living Father sent Me, and I live on account of the Father, so the one who eats Me, he too will live on account of Me.

**6:59** This is the bread that descended from heaven, not as your fathers ate manna and died. The one who eats this bread will live forever."

**6:60** He said these things while teaching in the synagogue in Capernaum.

**6:61** Many of His disciples, therefore, on hearing it, said, "This saying is hard, and who is able to listen to it?"

**6:62** Now Jesus, knowing within Himself that His disciples were murmuring about this, said to them, "Does this offend you?

**6:63** What, then, if you see the Son of Man ascending to where He was before?

**6:64** The Spirit is the One who gives life; the flesh is of no benefit at all. The words that I Myself have spoken to you are spirit and life.

**6:65** But there are some among you who do not believe." For Jesus knew from the beginning which ones did not believe, and who was going to betray Him.

**6:66** And He was saying, "For this reason I have told you that no one is able to come to Me unless it has been given to him by My Father."

**6:67** From this time many of His disciples went back and were no longer walking with Him.

**6:68** Jesus therefore said to the twelve, "You do not also want to depart, do you?"

**6:69** Therefore Simon Peter answered Him, "Lord, to whom will we go? You have words of eternal life,

**6:70** and we have believed and come to know that You are the Christ, the Son of God."

**6:71** Jesus replied to them, "Did I not choose you twelve, and yet one of you is a devil?"

**6:72** Now He was speaking of Judas Iscariot, the son of Simon, for he was going to betray Him, although he was one of the twelve.

## Translator Notes

- **6:12** — The Vulgate reads plural ne pereant ("so that they may not be lost"), referring directly back to fragmenta ("fragments"), whereas the Greek has singular me ti apoletai ("so that nothing may be lost").
- **6:15** — The Vulgate reads fugit ("fled"), aligning with Codex Sinaiticus and Old Latin witnesses, where most Greek manuscripts have anechoresen ("withdrew").
- **6:17** — The Vulgate uses the perfect venerunt ("came") rather than the Greek imperfect erchonto ("were going"), and omits "yet" in non venerat ("had not come", vs. Greek oupo).
- **6:33** — Because Latin panis ("bread") is masculine, the relative pronoun qui is grammatically ambiguous and may be read either impersonally ("that which") or personally ("He who" / "the One who"), anticipating Jesus's declaration in v. 35.
- **6:41** — The Vulgate explicitly reads panis vivus ("the living bread"), whereas the Greek text has simply ho artos ("the bread", reserving "living" for v. 51).
- **6:51** — Native Vulgate John 6 divides Saints.Bible 6:51 across native 6:51 ('I am the living bread...') and 6:52 ('If anyone eats from this bread...'), shifting native 6:53-72 one number higher than Saints.Bible 6:52-71.
- **6:56** — The Vulgate translates with the adverb vere ("truly", matching Byzantine/TR alethos), whereas critical Greek editions read the adjective alethes ("true food ... true drink").
- **6:70** — The Vulgate reads Christus Filius Dei ("the Christ, the Son of God"), differing both from critical Greek manuscripts ("the Holy One of God") and the Byzantine/TR addition ("of the living God").
- **6:72** — Because Iscariotem is accusative agreeing with Judam rather than genitive agreeing with Simonis, the Vulgate attaches the name Iscariot to Judas himself rather than to his father Simon.
