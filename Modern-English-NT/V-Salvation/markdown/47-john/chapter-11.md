## Text

**11:1** Now there was a certain ailing man, Lazarus from Bethany, from the village of Mary and Martha her sister.

**11:2** (Now Mary was the one who anointed the Lord with ointment and wiped His feet with her hair, whose brother Lazarus was ill.)

**11:3** Therefore his sisters sent to Him, saying, "Lord, look, the one You love is ill."

**11:4** Now upon hearing this, Jesus said to them, "This illness is not for death, but for the glory of God, so that through it the Son of God may be glorified."

**11:5** And Jesus loved Martha, her sister Mary, and Lazarus.

**11:6** Therefore, when He heard that he was ill, He then indeed remained in the same place for two days;

**11:7** then after these things He said to His disciples, "Let us go into Judea again."

**11:8** The disciples said to Him, "Rabbi, just now the Jews were seeking to stone You, and You are going there again?"

**11:9** Jesus replied, "Are there not twelve hours of the day? If someone walks during the day, he does not stumble, because he sees the light of this world;

**11:10** but if he walks during the night, he stumbles, because light is not in him."

**11:11** He spoke these things, and after this He said to them, "Lazarus, our friend, is sleeping; but I am going so that I may rouse him from sleep."

**11:12** Therefore His disciples said, "Lord, if he is sleeping, he will be well."

**11:13** Now Jesus had spoken about his death, but they supposed that He was speaking about the slumber of sleep.

**11:14** So then Jesus said to them plainly, "Lazarus has died,

**11:15** and I rejoice on your account, so that you may believe, that I was not there; but let us go to him."

**11:16** So Thomas, who is called Didymus, said to his fellow disciples, "Let us go as well, so that we may die with Him."

**11:17** And so Jesus came and found him already four days in the tomb.

**11:18** (Now Bethany was near Jerusalem, about fifteen stadia away.)

**11:19** Now many from among the Jews had come to Martha and Mary so that they might comfort them about their brother.

**11:20** Therefore, when Martha heard that Jesus was coming, she went to meet Him; Mary, however, was sitting at home.

**11:21** Then Martha said to Jesus, "Lord, if You had been here, my brother would not have died;

**11:22** yet even now I know that whatever You ask from God, God will give to You."

**11:23** Jesus told her, "Your brother will rise again."

**11:24** Martha told Him, "I know that he will rise again in the resurrection on the last day."

**11:25** Jesus said to her, "I am the resurrection and the life; the one who believes in Me, even if he dies, will live;

**11:26** and every person who lives and believes in Me will never die. Do you believe this?"

**11:27** She said to Him, "Yes, Lord, I have believed that You are the Christ, the Son of the living God, who have come into this world."

**11:28** And when she had said these things, she went away and quietly called Mary her sister, saying, "The Teacher is here and is calling you."

**11:29** As soon as she heard it, she rose quickly and came to Him;

**11:30** for Jesus had not yet come into the village, but was still in that place where Martha had met Him.

**11:31** Therefore the Jews who were with her in the house and were comforting her, when they saw that Mary rose quickly and went out, followed her, saying, "She is going to the tomb to weep there."

**11:32** So when Mary had come where Jesus was, seeing Him, she fell at His feet and said to Him, "Lord, if You had been here, my brother would not have died."

**11:33** Therefore, when Jesus saw her weeping, and the Jews who had come with her weeping, He groaned in spirit and troubled Himself,

**11:34** and said, "Where have you placed him?" They said to Him, "Lord, come and see."

**11:35** And Jesus shed tears.

**11:36** Therefore the Jews said, "Look how He loved him!"

**11:37** Some of them, however, said, "Could not this man, who opened the eyes of the one born blind, have brought it about that this man would not die?"

**11:38** Therefore Jesus, groaning within Himself again, came to the tomb. Now it was a cave, and a stone had been placed over it.

**11:39** Jesus said, "Take away the stone." Martha, the sister of the one who had died, said to Him, "Lord, he already gives off a stench, for he is four days dead."

**11:40** Jesus told her, "Did I not tell you that if you believe, you will see the glory of God?"

**11:41** Therefore they took away the stone; and Jesus, having lifted His eyes upward, said, "Father, I give thanks to You that You have heard Me.

**11:42** And I knew that You always hear Me, yet on account of the people who are standing around I spoke, so that they may believe that You have sent Me."

**11:43** After He had said these things, He called out in a loud voice, "Lazarus, come out!"

**11:44** And immediately the one who had died came out, his feet and hands bound with wrappings, and his face was bound with a face cloth. Jesus said to them, "Untie him and let him go."

**11:45** Therefore many from among the Jews who had come to Mary and Martha, and had seen the things Jesus did, believed in Him.

**11:46** Some of them, however, went away to the Pharisees and told them the things Jesus did.

**11:47** So the chief priests and the Pharisees assembled a council and were saying, "What are we doing, since this man performs many signs?

**11:48** If we leave Him alone like this, all will believe in Him, and the Romans will come and take away our place and nation."

**11:49** One of them, however, Caiaphas by name, since he was high priest of that year, said to them, "You do not know anything,

**11:50** nor do you consider that it is advantageous for you that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation should not perish."

**11:51** Now he did not say this on his own, but since he was high priest of that year, he prophesied that Jesus was about to die for the nation,

**11:52** and not only for the nation, but so that He might gather into one the children of God who were scattered.

**11:53** Therefore from that day they plotted to kill Him.

**11:54** Therefore Jesus no longer walked openly among the Jews, but went away into the region near the desert, into a city that is called Ephraim, and there He stayed with His disciples.

**11:55** Now the Passover of the Jews was near, and many went up to Jerusalem from the countryside before the Passover, so that they might sanctify themselves.

**11:56** Therefore they were seeking Jesus, and standing in the temple they were talking with one another, "What do you think, that He is not coming to the feast day?" Now the chief priests and the Pharisees had given a command that if anyone found out where He was, he should disclose it, so that they might seize Him.

## Translator Notes

- **11:27** — The Clementine Vulgate distinctly reads "of the living God" (Dei vivi), "this world" (hunc mundum), and the second-person perfect "who have come" (venisti), where critical Greek texts read "of God, the one coming into the world."
- **11:31** — The Vulgate reads dicentes ("saying"), reflecting a Greek variant (legontes), whereas most modern critical Greek editions read doxantes ("supposing").
- **11:33** — Latin infremuit spiritu ("groaned in spirit," echoed by fremens in v. 38) conveys intense inner agitation, indignation, or audible groaning, and turbavit seipsum ("troubled Himself") is explicitly active and reflexive.
- **11:35** — Unlike the Greek text, the Vulgate begins with Et ("And"); its verb lacrimatus est ("shed tears") also distinguishes quiet weeping from plorare ("weep" aloud) in vv. 31 and 33.
- **11:45** — The Vulgate explicitly includes "and Martha" (et Martham) alongside Mary, whereas the Greek text names only Mary (compare also the Vulgate additions of "Mary" in v. 5, "born" in v. 37, and "And immediately" in v. 44).
