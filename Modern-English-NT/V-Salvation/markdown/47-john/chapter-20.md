## Text

**20:1** Now on the first day of the week, Mary Magdalene came in the morning, while it was still dark, to the tomb, and saw the stone removed from the tomb.

**20:2** Therefore she ran and came to Simon Peter and to the other disciple whom Jesus loved, and said to them, "They have taken the Lord from the tomb, and we do not know where they have placed Him."

**20:3** So Peter went out, and that other disciple, and they came to the tomb.

**20:4** Now the two were running together, and that other disciple ran ahead faster than Peter, and came first to the tomb.

**20:5** And when he had bent down, he saw the linen cloths placed there; yet he did not go in.

**20:6** Therefore Simon Peter came following him, and went into the tomb, and saw the linen cloths placed there,

**20:7** and the facecloth that had been upon His head, not placed with the linen cloths, but rolled up separately in one place.

**20:8** So then that disciple who had come first to the tomb also went in, and he saw and believed.

**20:9** For they did not yet know the Scripture, that it was necessary for Him to rise from the dead.

**20:10** So the disciples went away again to their own place.

**20:11** But Mary was standing outside by the tomb, weeping. So while she was weeping, she bent down and looked into the tomb,

**20:12** and she saw two angels sitting in white, one at the head and one at the feet, where the body of Jesus had been placed.

**20:13** They said to her, "Woman, why are you weeping?" She said to them, "Because they have taken my Lord, and I do not know where they have placed Him."

**20:14** When she had said these things, she turned back around and saw Jesus standing, and she did not know that it was Jesus.

**20:15** Jesus said to her, "Woman, why are you weeping? Whom are you seeking?" Supposing that He was the gardener, she said to Him, "Sir, if you have taken Him away, tell me where you have placed Him, and I will take Him away."

**20:16** Jesus said to her, "Mary." Turning around, she said to Him, "Rabboni" (which means "Teacher").

**20:17** Jesus said to her, "Do not touch Me, for I have not yet ascended to My Father; but go to My brothers and say to them, 'I am ascending to My Father and your Father, My God and your God.'"

**20:18** Mary Magdalene came, announcing to the disciples, "I have seen the Lord, and He said these things to me."

**20:19** Therefore, when it was evening on that day, the first day of the week, and the doors were shut where the disciples were assembled because of fear of the Jews, Jesus came and stood in the midst and said to them, "Peace be to you."

**20:20** And when He had said this, He showed them His hands and His side. Therefore the disciples rejoiced upon seeing the Lord.

**20:21** Therefore He said to them again, "Peace be to you. Just as the Father sent Me, I also send you."

**20:22** When He had said these things, He breathed upon them and said to them, "Receive the Holy Spirit.

**20:23** If you forgive anyone's sins, they are forgiven them; and if you retain anyone's, they have been retained."

**20:24** However, Thomas, one of the Twelve, who is called Didymus, was not with them when Jesus came.

**20:25** Therefore the other disciples said to him, "We have seen the Lord." He, however, said to them, "Unless I see in His hands the mark of the nails, and put my finger into the place of the nails, and put my hand into His side, I will not believe."

**20:26** And after eight days, His disciples were inside again, and Thomas with them. Jesus came with the doors shut, and stood in the midst and said, "Peace be to you."

**20:27** Then He said to Thomas, "Bring your finger here and see My hands, and bring your hand and put it into My side; and do not be unbelieving, but faithful."

**20:28** Thomas replied and said to Him, "My Lord and my God."

**20:29** Jesus said to him, "Because you have seen Me, Thomas, you have believed; blessed are those who have not seen and have believed."

**20:30** Indeed, Jesus also performed many other signs in the sight of His disciples, which are not written in this book.

**20:31** Now these things have been written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and so that, believing, you may have life in His name.

## Translator Notes

- **20:1** — Latin literally reads "on one of the Sabbath" (una sabbati; similarly "una sabbatorum" in v. 19), a Semitic idiom for the first day of the week.
- **20:10** — Latin literally reads "to themselves" (ad semetipsos), an idiom for returning to their own lodging or quarters.
- **20:17** — Latin "Noli me tangere" literally means "Do not touch Me" (whereas the Greek present imperative is often rendered "Do not cling to Me"). The Clementine Vulgate also reads "to My Father" (ad Patrem meum) in the first clause, where the critical Greek text has "to the Father."
- **20:18** — The Clementine Vulgate places the second clause in the first person ("to me," mihi), whereas the Greek text uses the third person ("to her").
- **20:25** — Following an ancient variant, the Latin distinguishes "the mark of the nails" (fixuram clavorum) from "the place of the nails" (locum clavorum), where most Greek manuscripts repeat "mark" (typon).
