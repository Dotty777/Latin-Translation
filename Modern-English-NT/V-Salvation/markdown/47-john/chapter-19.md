## Text

**19:1** So then Pilate took hold of Jesus and flogged Him.

**19:2** And the soldiers, weaving a crown from thorns, placed it on His head and wrapped Him in a purple robe.

**19:3** And they were coming to Him and saying, "Hail, King of the Jews!" and they were slapping Him.

**19:4** Then Pilate went outside again and said to them, "Look, I am bringing Him outside to you, so that you may know that I find no grounds for a charge in Him."

**19:5** (So Jesus went outside, wearing the crown of thorns and the purple garment.) And he said to them, "Here is the man."

**19:6** So when the chief priests and the attendants saw Him, they were shouting, saying, "Crucify, crucify Him!" Pilate said to them, "Take Him yourselves and crucify Him, for I myself find no grounds for a charge in Him."

**19:7** The Jews replied to him, "We have a law, and according to the law He must die, because He made Himself the Son of God."

**19:8** So when Pilate heard this statement, he feared all the more.

**19:9** And he entered the praetorium again and said to Jesus, "Where are You from?" But Jesus did not give him a reply.

**19:10** So Pilate said to Him, "Do You not speak to me? Do You not know that I have authority to crucify You, and I have authority to release You?"

**19:11** Jesus replied, "You would not have any authority against Me unless it had been given to you from above. For this reason, the one who handed Me over to you has a greater sin."

**19:12** And from then on Pilate was seeking to release Him. But the Jews were shouting, saying, "If you release this man, you are not a friend of Caesar. For everyone who makes himself a king speaks against Caesar."

**19:13** Now when Pilate heard these statements, he brought Jesus outside and sat on the judgment seat, at the place that is called Lithostrotos, and in Hebrew, Gabbatha.

**19:14** Now it was the Preparation of the Passover, about the sixth hour, and he said to the Jews, "Here is your king."

**19:15** But they were shouting, "Take Him away, take Him away, crucify Him!" Pilate said to them, "Should I crucify your king?" The chief priests replied, "We have no king except Caesar."

**19:16** So then he handed Him over to them to be crucified. And they took Jesus and led Him out.

**19:17** And carrying the cross for Himself, He went out to what is called the Place of Calvary, and in Hebrew, Golgotha,

**19:18** where they crucified Him, and with Him two others, on this side and on that side, and Jesus in the middle.

**19:19** Now Pilate also wrote an inscription and placed it on the cross. And it was written: "Jesus the Nazarene, King of the Jews."

**19:20** Therefore many of the Jews read this inscription, because the place where Jesus was crucified was near the city, and it was written in Hebrew, Greek, and Latin.

**19:21** So the chief priests of the Jews were saying to Pilate, "Do not write, 'King of the Jews,' but that He Himself said, 'I am King of the Jews.'"

**19:22** Pilate replied, "What I have written, I have written."

**19:23** Then when the soldiers had crucified Him, they took His garments (and made four parts, a part for each soldier) and the tunic. Now the tunic was seamless, woven from the top throughout.

**19:24** So they said to one another, "Let us not tear it, but let us cast lots over it to decide whose it will be," so that the Scripture might be fulfilled, saying, "They divided My garments among themselves, and for My clothing they cast a lot." And indeed the soldiers did these things.

**19:25** Now standing beside the cross of Jesus were His mother, and His mother's sister, Mary of Cleopas, and Mary Magdalene.

**19:26** So when Jesus saw His mother, and the disciple whom He loved standing there, He said to His mother, "Woman, here is your son."

**19:27** Then He said to the disciple, "Here is your mother." And from that hour the disciple received her into his own household.

**19:28** Afterward, Jesus, knowing that all things had been completed, so that the Scripture might be completed, said, "I am thirsty."

**19:29** So a vessel full of vinegar was set there. And they, putting a sponge full of vinegar around hyssop, brought it to His mouth.

**19:30** So when Jesus had received the vinegar, He said, "It is completed." And bowing His head, He handed over His spirit.

**19:31** Then the Jews (since it was the Preparation), so that the bodies would not remain on the cross on the Sabbath (for that Sabbath day was a great one), asked Pilate that their legs might be broken and that they might be taken away.

**19:32** So the soldiers came and broke the legs of the first one, and of the other who was crucified with Him.

**19:33** But after they came to Jesus, when they saw that He was already dead, they did not break His legs,

**19:34** but one of the soldiers opened His side with a spear, and immediately blood and water came out.

**19:35** And the one who saw has given testimony, and his testimony is true. And he knows that he speaks the truth, so that you also may believe.

**19:36** For these things took place so that the Scripture might be fulfilled: "You will not break a bone of Him."

**19:37** And again another Scripture says, "They will look upon Him whom they pierced."

**19:38** Now after these things, Joseph from Arimathea (because he was a disciple of Jesus, though a secret one out of fear of the Jews) asked Pilate that he might take away the body of Jesus. And Pilate gave permission. So he came and took away the body of Jesus.

**19:39** And Nicodemus also came, who had first come to Jesus at night, bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about a hundred pounds.

**19:40** So they took the body of Jesus and bound it in linen cloths with spices, as it is the custom for the Jews to bury.

**19:41** Now in the place where He was crucified there was a garden, and in the garden a new tomb, in which no one had yet been laid.

**19:42** So there, because of the Preparation of the Jews, since the tomb was nearby, they laid Jesus.

## Translator Notes

- **19:10** — The Clementine Vulgate places "to crucify You" before "to release You" (crucifigere te ... dimittere te), reversing the order in modern Greek critical editions; similarly, v. 20 orders the languages "Hebrew, Greek, and Latin."
- **19:17** — Latin Calvariae (the origin of English "Calvary") literally means "of a skull," corresponding to Greek Kraniou Topos; similarly, in v. 13 the Vulgate keeps the transliterated Greek place name Lithostrotos ("Stone Pavement").
- **19:25** — The Vulgate reads Cleophae, the same name it gives the disciple Cleopas in Luke 24:18, so it is rendered "Cleopas" here (the Greek text has Clopas). The genitive leaves the relationship implicit (traditionally "wife of Cleopas"), and the syntax permits counting either three or four women.
- **19:34** — The Clementine Vulgate distinctly reads aperuit ("opened") rather than the Greek verb for "pierced" or "stabbed," a wording noted by Latin patristic interpreters.
- **19:36** — The Vulgate renders the Scripture quotation with a second-person plural active verb (comminuetis, "you will not break"), echoing Exodus 12:46, whereas the Greek text uses a passive ("will not be broken").
