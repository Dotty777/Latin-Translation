## Text

**17:1** Jesus spoke these things, and raising His eyes to heaven, He said, "Father, the hour has come; glorify Your Son, so that Your Son may glorify You,

**17:2** just as You gave Him authority over all flesh, so that to all that You have given Him He may give eternal life.

**17:3** Now this is eternal life: that they may know You, the only true God, and Him whom You sent, Jesus Christ.

**17:4** I glorified You on the earth; I have completed the work that You gave Me to do.

**17:5** And now, You, Father, glorify Me with Yourself, with the glory that I had with You before the world was.

**17:6** I have manifested Your name to the people whom You gave Me out of the world. They were Yours, and You gave them to Me, and they have kept Your word.

**17:7** Now they have come to know that all the things You have given Me are from You,

**17:8** because the words that You gave Me I have given to them, and they themselves received them, and truly came to know that I came forth from You, and they believed that You sent Me.

**17:9** I pray for them; I do not pray for the world, but for these whom You have given Me, because they are Yours,

**17:10** and everything of Mine is Yours, and what is Yours is Mine; and I have been glorified in them.

**17:11** And I am no longer in the world, and these are in the world, and I am coming to You. Holy Father, keep them in Your name, those whom You have given Me, so that they may be one, just as We also are.

**17:12** While I was with them, I was keeping them in Your name. Those whom You gave Me I have guarded, and not one of them perished except the son of destruction, so that the Scripture may be fulfilled.

**17:13** But now I am coming to You, and I speak these things in the world, so that they may have My joy fulfilled within themselves.

**17:14** I have given them Your word, and the world has hated them, because they are not of the world, just as I also am not of the world.

**17:15** I am not asking that You take them out of the world, but that You keep them from evil.

**17:16** They are not of the world, just as I also am not of the world.

**17:17** Sanctify them in truth. Your word is truth.

**17:18** Just as You sent Me into the world, I also sent them into the world;

**17:19** and on their behalf I sanctify Myself, so that they themselves also may be sanctified in truth.

**17:20** Now I do not pray for them alone, but also for those who will believe in Me through their word,

**17:21** that they all may be one, just as You, Father, are in Me and I in You, that they themselves also may be one in Us, so that the world may believe that You sent Me.

**17:22** And the glory that You gave Me I have given to them, so that they may be one, just as We also are one:

**17:23** I in them and You in Me, so that they may be made complete as one, and that the world may know that You sent Me and loved them, just as You also loved Me.

**17:24** Father, I desire that where I am, those whom You have given Me may also be with Me, so that they may see My glory, which You gave Me, because You loved Me before the founding of the world.

**17:25** Righteous Father, the world has not known You, yet I have known You, and these have come to know that You sent Me.

**17:26** And I have made Your name known to them, and will make it known, so that the love with which You have loved Me may be in them, and I in them."

## Translator Notes

- **17:1** — The Vulgate renders the glory vocabulary throughout chapter 17 with clarifico and claritas (radiant splendor/glory), and includes tuus ("Your") in the second clause ("so that Your Son may glorify You"), where critical Greek editions read simply "the Son."
- **17:11** — The Clementine Vulgate reads masculine plural quos dedisti mihi ("those whom You have given Me"), referring to the disciples, whereas critical Greek editions read a neuter singular relative referring to the Father's "name, which You have given Me"; the same masculine plural begins the second sentence of 17:12 (Quos dedisti mihi, custodivi).
- **17:15** — Latin a malo is grammatically ambiguous and can be understood either as neuter ("from evil") or as masculine ("from the evil one").
- **17:20** — The Vulgate uses the future periphrastic qui credituri sunt ("who will believe"), whereas critical Greek editions have the present participle ("who believe").
- **17:21** — The Vulgate includes unum in the clause in nobis unum sint ("may be one in Us"), whereas critical Greek editions omit "one" there; likewise in 17:24, the Vulgate reads masculine plural quos ("those whom") rather than neuter singular ("that which").
