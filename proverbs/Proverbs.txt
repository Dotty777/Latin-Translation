PROVERBS 1

1:1  LA: Parabolæ Salomonis, filii David, regis Israël,
     EN: The proverbs of Solomon, son of David, king of Israel,

1:2  LA: ad sciendam sapientiam et disciplinam ;
     EN: for knowing wisdom and discipline,

1:3  LA: ad intelligenda verba prudentiæ, et suscipiendam eruditionem doctrinæ, justitiam, et judicium, et æquitatem :
     EN: for understanding words of prudence and receiving instruction in learning, justice, judgment, and fairness,

1:4  LA: ut detur parvulis astutia, adolescenti scientia et intellectus.
     EN: so that shrewdness may be given to the inexperienced, knowledge and understanding to the young.

1:5  LA: Audiens sapiens, sapientior erit, et intelligens gubernacula possidebit.
     EN: A wise person who listens will become wiser, and an understanding person will gain the means to steer,

1:6  LA: Animadvertet parabolam et interpretationem, verba sapientum et ænigmata eorum.
     EN: and will grasp a proverb and its interpretation, the words of the wise and their riddles.

1:7  LA: Timor Domini principium sapientiæ ; sapientiam atque doctrinam stulti despiciunt.]
     EN: Fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom; fools despise wisdom and teaching.

1:8  LA: [Audi, fili mi, disciplinam patris tui, et ne dimittas legem matris tuæ :
     EN: Listen, my son, to your father's discipline, and do not abandon your mother's law,

1:9  LA: ut addatur gratia capiti tuo, et torques collo tuo.
     EN: so that grace may be added to your head and a necklace to your neck.

1:10  LA: Fili mi, si te lactaverint peccatores, ne acquiescas eis.
      EN: My son, if sinners entice you, do not give in to them.

1:11  LA: Si dixerint : Veni nobiscum, insidiemur sanguini ; abscondamus tendiculas contra insontem frustra ;
      EN: If they say, “Come with us; let us lie in wait for blood; let us hide snares for the innocent without cause.

1:12  LA: deglutiamus eum sicut infernus viventem, et integrum quasi descendentem in lacum ;
      EN: Let us swallow him alive like the underworld, whole like those who go down into the pit.

1:13  LA: omnem pretiosam substantiam reperiemus ; implebimus domos nostras spoliis :
      EN: We will find every kind of precious wealth; we will fill our houses with plunder.

1:14  LA: sortem mitte nobiscum, marsupium unum sit omnium nostrum :
      EN: Cast your lot with us; let us all have one purse,”

1:15  LA: fili mi, ne ambules cum eis ; prohibe pedem tuum a semitis eorum :
      EN: my son, do not walk with them; keep your foot from their paths.

1:16  LA: pedes enim illorum ad malum currunt, et festinant ut effundant sanguinem.
      EN: For their feet run toward evil, and they hurry to shed blood.

1:17  LA: Frustra autem jacitur rete ante oculos pennatorum.
      EN: Yet a net is spread in vain before the eyes of winged creatures.

1:18  LA: Ipsi quoque contra sanguinem suum insidiantur, et moliuntur fraudes contra animas suas.
      EN: They themselves lie in wait for their own blood and devise traps for their own lives.

1:19  LA: Sic semitæ omnis avari : animas possidentium rapiunt.]
      EN: Such are the paths of everyone greedy for gain: they snatch away the lives of those who possess it.

1:20  LA: [Sapientia foris prædicat ; in plateis dat vocem suam :
      EN: Wisdom proclaims aloud outside; in the squares she raises her voice.

1:21  LA: in capite turbarum clamitat ; in foribus portarum urbis profert verba sua, dicens :
      EN: At the head of the crowds she cries out; at the entrances of the city gates she speaks her words, saying:

1:22  LA: Usquequo, parvuli, diligitis infantiam, et stulti ea quæ sibi sunt noxia cupient, et imprudentes odibunt scientiam ?
      EN: “How long, you inexperienced people, will you love childishness? How long will fools desire what harms them and the imprudent hate knowledge?

1:23  LA: convertimini ad correptionem meam. En proferam vobis spiritum meum, et ostendam vobis verba mea.
      EN: Turn at my correction. Look, I will bring forth my spirit for you and make my words known to you.

1:24  LA: Quia vocavi, et renuistis ; extendi manum meam, et non fuit qui aspiceret :
      EN: Because I called and you refused, I stretched out my hand and no one looked,

1:25  LA: despexistis omne consilium meum, et increpationes meas neglexistis.
      EN: because you despised all my counsel and neglected my rebukes,

1:26  LA: Ego quoque in interitu vestro ridebo, et subsannabo cum vobis id quod timebatis advenerit.
      EN: I too will laugh at your destruction and mock when what you feared comes upon you,

1:27  LA: Cum irruerit repentina calamitas, et interitus quasi tempestas ingruerit ; quando venerit super vos tribulatio et angustia :
      EN: when sudden calamity breaks in and destruction rushes upon you like a storm, when trouble and distress come upon you.

1:28  LA: tunc invocabunt me, et non exaudiam ; mane consurgent, et non invenient me :
      EN: Then they will call upon me, and I will not answer; they will rise early and will not find me,

1:29  LA: eo quod exosam habuerint disciplinam, et timorem Domini non susceperint,
      EN: because they hated discipline and did not accept fear of the Lord,

1:30  LA: nec acquieverint consilio meo, et detraxerint universæ correptioni meæ.
      EN: and did not give in to my counsel, but disparaged all my correction.

1:31  LA: Comedent igitur fructus viæ suæ, suisque consiliis saturabuntur.
      EN: They will therefore eat the fruits of their own way and have their fill of their own counsels.

1:32  LA: Aversio parvulorum interficiet eos, et prosperitas stultorum perdet illos.
      EN: The turning away of the inexperienced will kill them, and the prosperity of fools will destroy them.

1:33  LA: Qui autem me audierit, absque terrore requiescet, et abundantia perfruetur, timore malorum sublato.]
      EN: But whoever listens to me will rest without terror and enjoy abundance, with fear of evils removed.”

PROVERBS 2

2:1  LA: Fili mi, si susceperis sermones meos, et mandata mea absconderis penes te :
     EN: My son, if you receive my words and store up my commands within you,

2:2  LA: ut audiat sapientiam auris tua, inclina cor tuum ad cognoscendam prudentiam.
     EN: so that your ear may hear wisdom, incline your heart to know prudence.

2:3  LA: Si enim sapientiam invocaveris, et inclinaveris cor tuum prudentiæ ;
     EN: For if you call upon wisdom and incline your heart to prudence,

2:4  LA: si quæsieris eam quasi pecuniam, et sicut thesauros effoderis illam :
     EN: if you seek her like money and dig for her as for treasures,

2:5  LA: tunc intelliges timorem Domini, et scientiam Dei invenies,
     EN: then you will understand fear of the Lord and find the knowledge of God,

2:6  LA: quia Dominus dat sapientiam, et ex ore ejus prudentia et scientia.
     EN: because the Lord gives wisdom, and from His mouth come prudence and knowledge.

2:7  LA: Custodiet rectorum salutem, et proteget gradientes simpliciter,
     EN: He will safeguard the well-being of the upright and protect those who walk in integrity,

2:8  LA: servans semitas justitiæ, et vias sanctorum custodiens.
     EN: preserving the paths of justice and guarding the ways of the holy.

2:9  LA: Tunc intelliges justitiam, et judicium, et æquitatem, et omnem semitam bonam.]
     EN: Then you will understand justice, judgment, fairness, and every good path.

2:10  LA: [Si intraverit sapientia cor tuum, et scientia animæ tuæ placuerit,
      EN: If wisdom enters your heart and knowledge delights your soul,

2:11  LA: consilium custodiet te, et prudentia servabit te :
      EN: counsel will guard you and prudence preserve you,

2:12  LA: ut eruaris a via mala, et ab homine qui perversa loquitur ;
      EN: to rescue you from the evil way and from the man who speaks perversely,

2:13  LA: qui relinquunt iter rectum, et ambulant per vias tenebrosas ;
      EN: from those who leave the straight road and walk along dark ways,

2:14  LA: qui lætantur cum malefecerint, et exsultant in rebus pessimis ;
      EN: who rejoice when they have done evil and delight in the worst things,

2:15  LA: quorum viæ perversæ sunt, et infames gressus eorum.
      EN: whose ways are perverse and whose steps are disgraceful;

2:16  LA: Ut eruaris a muliere aliena, et ab extranea quæ mollit sermones suos,
      EN: to rescue you from another's wife, from the strange woman who smooths her words,

2:17  LA: et relinquit ducem pubertatis suæ,
      EN: who leaves the guide of her youth

2:18  LA: et pacti Dei sui oblita est. Inclinata est enim ad mortem domus ejus, et ad inferos semitæ ipsius.
      EN: and has forgotten the covenant of her God. For her house slopes down toward death and her paths toward the underworld.

2:19  LA: Omnes qui ingrediuntur ad eam non revertentur, nec apprehendent semitas vitæ.
      EN: None who go in to her will return or take hold of the paths of life.

2:20  LA: Ut ambules in via bona, et calles justorum custodias :
      EN: Thus you may walk in the good way and keep to the tracks of the just.

2:21  LA: qui enim recti sunt habitabunt in terra, et simplices permanebunt in ea ;
      EN: For the upright will dwell in the land, and those of integrity will remain in it;

2:22  LA: impii vero de terra perdentur, et qui inique agunt auferentur ex ea.]
      EN: but the wicked will be destroyed from the land, and those who act unjustly will be taken away from it.

PROVERBS 3

3:1  LA: Fili mi, ne obliviscaris legis meæ, et præcepta mea cor tuum custodiat :
     EN: My son, do not forget my law, and let your heart keep my commands,

3:2  LA: longitudinem enim dierum, et annos vitæ, et pacem, apponent tibi.
     EN: for they will add length of days, years of life, and peace to you.

3:3  LA: Misericordia et veritas te non deserant ; circumda eas gutturi tuo, et describe in tabulis cordis tui :
     EN: Let mercy and truth never leave you; bind them around your neck and inscribe them on the tablets of your heart,

3:4  LA: et invenies gratiam, et disciplinam bonam, coram Deo et hominibus.
     EN: and you will find favor and good discipline before God and people.

3:5  LA: Habe fiduciam in Domino ex toto corde tuo, et ne innitaris prudentiæ tuæ.
     EN: Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and do not lean on your own prudence.

3:6  LA: In omnibus viis tuis cogita illum, et ipse diriget gressus tuos.
     EN: Think of Him in all your ways, and He will direct your steps.

3:7  LA: Ne sis sapiens apud temetipsum ; time Deum, et recede a malo :
     EN: Do not be wise in your own eyes; fear God and turn away from evil,

3:8  LA: sanitas quippe erit umbilico tuo, et irrigatio ossium tuorum.
     EN: for this will be health for your navel and refreshment for your bones.

3:9  LA: Honora Dominum de tua substantia, et de primitiis omnium frugum tuarum da ei :
     EN: Honor the Lord from your wealth, and give Him the firstfruits of all your crops;

3:10  LA: et implebuntur horrea tua saturitate, et vino torcularia tua redundabunt.]
      EN: then your barns will be filled to abundance, and your presses will overflow with wine.

3:11  LA: [Disciplinam Domini, fili mi, ne abjicias, nec deficias cum ab eo corriperis :
      EN: My son, do not reject the Lord's discipline or lose heart when He corrects you,

3:12  LA: quem enim diligit Dominus, corripit, et quasi pater in filio complacet sibi.
      EN: for the Lord corrects the one He loves and takes delight in him as a father in his son.

3:13  LA: Beatus homo qui invenit sapientiam, et qui affluit prudentia.
      EN: Blessed is the person who finds wisdom and abounds in prudence.

3:14  LA: Melior est acquisitio ejus negotiatione argenti, et auri primi et purissimi fructus ejus.
      EN: Acquiring her is better than trading in silver, and her fruit is better than the finest, purest gold.

3:15  LA: Pretiosior est cunctis opibus, et omnia quæ desiderantur huic non valent comparari.
      EN: She is more precious than all riches, and nothing that people desire can compare with her.

3:16  LA: Longitudo dierum in dextera ejus, et in sinistra illius divitiæ et gloria.
      EN: Length of days is in her right hand, and riches and glory in her left.

3:24  LA: Si dormieris, non timebis ; quiesces, et suavis erit somnus tuus.

PROVERBS 4

4:1  LA: Audite , filii, disciplinam patris, et attendite ut sciatis prudentiam.
     EN: Listen, children, to a father's discipline, and attend, so that you may know prudence.

4:2  LA: Donum bonum tribuam vobis : legem meam ne derelinquatis.
     EN: I will give you a good gift; do not forsake my law.

4:3  LA: Nam et ego filius fui patris mei, tenellus et unigenitus coram matre mea.
     EN: For I too was my father's son, tender and an only child in my mother's sight.

4:4  LA: Et docebat me, atque dicebat : Suscipiat verba mea cor tuum ; custodi præcepta mea, et vives.
     EN: He taught me and said, “Let your heart receive my words; keep my commands and you will live.

4:5  LA: Posside sapientiam, posside prudentiam : ne obliviscaris, neque declines a verbis oris mei.
     EN: Acquire wisdom, acquire prudence; do not forget or turn aside from the words of my mouth.

4:6  LA: Ne dimittas eam, et custodiet te : dilige eam, et conservabit te.
     EN: Do not abandon her, and she will guard you; love her, and she will preserve you.

4:8  LA: Arripe illam, et exaltabit te ; glorificaberis ab ea cum eam fueris amplexatus.
     EN: Seize hold of her, and she will exalt you; you will be glorified by her when you embrace her.

4:9  LA: Dabit capiti tuo augmenta gratiarum, et corona inclyta proteget te.]
     EN: She will give your head an abundance of graces and shelter you with a splendid crown.”

4:10  LA: [Audi, fili mi, et suscipe verba mea, ut multiplicentur tibi anni vitæ.
      EN: Listen, my son, and receive my words, so that your years of life may be multiplied.

4:11  LA: Viam sapientiæ monstrabo tibi ; ducam te per semitas æquitatis :
      EN: I will show you the way of wisdom; I will lead you along paths of fairness.

4:12  LA: quas cum ingressus fueris, non arctabuntur gressus tui, et currens non habebis offendiculum.
      EN: When you enter them, your steps will not be cramped, and when you run, you will have no obstacle.

4:13  LA: Tene disciplinam, ne dimittas eam ; custodi illam, quia ipsa est vita tua.
      EN: Hold fast to discipline; do not let her go. Guard her, for she is your life.

4:14  LA: Ne delecteris in semitis impiorum, nec tibi placeat malorum via.
      EN: Do not delight in the paths of the wicked, and do not take pleasure in the way of evil people.

4:15  LA: Fuge ab ea, nec transeas per illam ; declina, et desere eam.
      EN: Flee from it; do not pass along it. Turn aside and leave it behind.

4:16  LA: Non enim dormiunt nisi malefecerint, et rapitur somnus ab eis nisi supplantaverint.
      EN: For they cannot sleep unless they have done evil, and sleep is snatched from them unless they have brought someone down.

4:17  LA: Comedunt panem impietatis, et vinum iniquitatis bibunt.
      EN: They eat the bread of wickedness and drink the wine of injustice.

4:18  LA: Justorum autem semita quasi lux splendens procedit, et crescit usque ad perfectam diem.
      EN: But the path of the just advances like a shining light and grows until full day.

4:19  LA: Via impiorum tenebrosa ; nesciunt ubi corruant.
      EN: The way of the wicked is dark; they do not know where they may fall.

4:20  LA: Fili mi, ausculta sermones meos, et ad eloquia mea inclina aurem tuam.
      EN: My son, listen to my words and incline your ear to what I say.

4:21  LA: Ne recedant ab oculis tuis : custodi ea in medio cordis tui :
      EN: Do not let them leave your sight; guard them in the midst of your heart,

4:22  LA: vita enim sunt invenientibus ea, et universæ carni sanitas.
      EN: for they are life to those who find them and health to all flesh.

4:23  LA: Omni custodia serva cor tuum, quia ex ipso vita procedit.
      EN: Guard your heart with every care, for life proceeds from it.

4:24  LA: Remove a te os pravum, et detrahentia labia sint procul a te.
      EN: Put a crooked mouth away from you, and let disparaging lips be far from you.

4:25  LA: Oculi tui recta videant, et palpebræ tuæ præcedant gressus tuos.
      EN: Let your eyes look straight ahead, and let your eyelids lead your steps.

4:26  LA: Dirige semitam pedibus tuis, et omnes viæ tuæ stabilientur.
      EN: Direct the path of your feet, and all your ways will be established.

4:27  LA: Ne declines ad dexteram neque ad sinistram ; averte pedem tuum a malo : vias enim quæ a dextris sunt novit Dominus : perversæ vero sunt quæ a sinistris sunt. Ipse autem rectos faciet cursus tuos, itinera autem tua in pace producet.]
      EN: Do not turn aside to the right or to the left; turn your foot away from evil. For the Lord knows the ways on the right, but those on the left are perverse. He Himself will make your courses straight and bring your journeys onward in peace.

PROVERBS 5

5:1  LA: Fili mi, attende ad sapientiam meam, et prudentiæ meæ inclina aurem tuam :
     EN: My son, attend to my wisdom and incline your ear to my prudence,

5:2  LA: ut custodias cogitationes, et disciplinam labia tua conservent. Ne attendas fallaciæ mulieris ;
     EN: so that you may guard your thoughts and your lips may preserve discipline. Do not attend to a woman's deceit,

5:3  LA: favus enim distillans labia meretricis, et nitidius oleo guttur ejus :
     EN: for a prostitute's lips are a dripping honeycomb, and her throat is smoother than oil;

5:4  LA: novissima autem illius amara quasi absinthium, et acuta quasi gladius biceps.
     EN: but her end is bitter as wormwood and sharp as a two-edged sword.

5:5  LA: Pedes ejus descendunt in mortem, et ad inferos gressus illius penetrant.
     EN: Her feet go down to death, and her steps penetrate the underworld.

5:6  LA: Per semitam vitæ non ambulant ; vagi sunt gressus ejus et investigabiles.
     EN: They do not walk along the path of life; her steps wander and cannot be traced.

5:7  LA: Nunc ergo fili mi, audi me, et ne recedas a verbis oris mei.
     EN: Now therefore, my son, listen to me and do not turn away from the words of my mouth.

5:8  LA: Longe fac ab ea viam tuam, et ne appropinques foribus domus ejus.
     EN: Keep your way far from her, and do not approach the doors of her house,

5:9  LA: Ne des alienis honorem tuum, et annos tuos crudeli :
     EN: lest you give your honor to strangers and your years to someone cruel,

5:10  LA: ne forte implentur extranei viribus tuis, et labores tui sint in domo aliena,
      EN: lest outsiders have their fill of your strength and your labors end up in another's house,

5:11  LA: et gemas in novissimis, quando consumpseris carnes tuas et corpus tuum, et dicas :
      EN: and you groan at the end, when you have wasted your flesh and your body, and say,

5:12  LA: Cur detestatus sum disciplinam, et increpationibus non acquievit cor meum,
      EN: “Why did I hate discipline, and why did my heart refuse to yield to rebukes?

5:13  LA: nec audivi vocem docentium me, et magistris non inclinavi aurem meam ?
      EN: Why did I not listen to the voice of those who taught me or incline my ear to my instructors?

5:14  LA: pene fui in omni malo, in medio ecclesiæ et synagogæ.
      EN: I nearly came to every kind of evil in the midst of the assembly and congregation.”

5:15  LA: Bibe aquam de cisterna tua, et fluenta putei tui ;
      EN: Drink water from your own cistern and the streams of your own well.

5:16  LA: deriventur fontes tui foras, et in plateis aquas tuas divide.
      EN: Let your springs flow outside, and distribute your waters in the squares.

5:17  LA: Habeto eas solus, nec sint alieni participes tui.
      EN: Possess them for yourself alone, and let no strangers share them with you.

5:18  LA: Sit vena tua benedicta, et lætare cum muliere adolescentiæ tuæ.
      EN: Let your spring be blessed, and rejoice with the wife of your youth.

5:19  LA: Cerva carissima, et gratissimus hinnulus : ubera ejus inebrient te in omni tempore ; in amore ejus delectare jugiter.
      EN: A dearly loved doe, a most delightful fawn: let her breasts intoxicate you at all times; delight continually in her love.

5:20  LA: Quare seduceris, fili mi, ab aliena, et foveris in sinu alterius ?
      EN: Why, my son, are you seduced by another's wife and cherished in another woman's embrace?

5:21  LA: Respicit Dominus vias hominis, et omnes gressus ejus considerat.
      EN: The Lord looks upon a person's ways and considers all his steps.

5:22  LA: Iniquitates suas capiunt impium, et funibus peccatorum suorum constringitur.
      EN: His own iniquities seize the wicked man, and he is bound with the cords of his own sins.

5:23  LA: Ipse morietur, quia non habuit disciplinam, et in multitudine stultitiæ suæ decipietur.]
      EN: He will die because he had no discipline, and in the abundance of his folly he will be led astray.

PROVERBS 6

6:1  LA: Fili mi, si spoponderis pro amico tuo, defixisti apud extraneum manum tuam :
     EN: My son, if you have stood surety for your friend, you have pledged your hand to a stranger.

6:2  LA: illaqueatus es verbis oris tui, et captus propriis sermonibus.
     EN: You are ensnared by the words of your mouth and caught by your own speech.
