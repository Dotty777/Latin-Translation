2 MACCABEES 1

1:1  LA: Fratribus qui sunt per Ægyptum Judæis, salutem dicunt fratres qui sunt in Jerosolymis Judæi, et qui in regione Judææ, et pacem bonam.
     EN: To the Jewish brothers throughout Egypt, the Jewish brothers in Jerusalem and in the region of Judea send greetings and good peace.

1:2  LA: Benefaciat vobis Deus, et meminerit testamenti sui, quod locutus est ad Abraham, et Isaac, et Jacob servorum suorum fidelium :
     EN: May God do good to you and remember His covenant, which He declared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, His faithful servants.

1:3  LA: et det vobis cor omnibus ut colatis eum, et faciatis ejus voluntatem, corde magno et animo volenti.
     EN: May He give all of you a heart to worship Him and do His will with a generous heart and a willing spirit.

1:4  LA: Adaperiat cor vestrum in lege sua, et in præceptis suis, et faciat pacem.
     EN: May He open your heart to His law and His commandments, and make peace.

1:5  LA: Exaudiat orationes vestras, et reconcilietur vobis, nec vos deserat in tempore malo.
     EN: May He hear your prayers and be reconciled to you, and not abandon you in an evil time.

1:6  LA: Et nunc hic sumus orantes pro vobis.
     EN: And now we are here praying for you.

1:7  LA: Regnante Demetrio, anno centesimo sexagesimo nono, nos Judæi scripsimus vobis in tribulatione et impetu qui supervenit nobis in istis annis, ex quo recessit Jason a sancta terra, et a regno.
     EN: While Demetrius was reigning, in the one hundred sixty-ninth year, we Jews wrote to you amid the distress and assault that came upon us in those years, from the time Jason withdrew from the holy land and the kingdom.

1:8  LA: Portam succenderunt, et effuderunt sanguinem innocentem : et oravimus ad Dominum, et exauditi sumus, et obtulimus sacrificium et similaginem, et accendimus lucernas, et proposuimus panes.
     EN: They set the gate on fire and shed innocent blood. We prayed to the Lord and were heard; we offered sacrifice and fine flour, lit the lamps, and set out the loaves.

1:9  LA: Et nunc frequentate dies scenopegiæ mensis Casleu. · Translation note. Scenopegia is rendered “Feast of Booths”; the reference to Casleu is retained. The Latin does not make this the ordinary autumn calendar date of that feast.
     EN: And now keep the days of the Feast of Booths in the month of Casleu.

1:10  LA: Anno centesimo octogesimo octavo, populus qui est Jerosolymis et in Judæa, senatusque et Judas, Aristobolo magistro Ptolemæi regis, qui est de genere christorum sacerdotum, et his qui in Ægypto sunt Judæis, salutem et sanitatem. · Translation note. Christorum sacerdotum means “anointed priests” here; christorum is not inserted as a reference to Jesus Christ.
      EN: In the one hundred eighty-eighth year, the people in Jerusalem and Judea, the council, and Judas send greetings and health to Aristobulus, teacher of King Ptolemy, who is of the family of the anointed priests, and to the Jews in Egypt.

1:11  LA: De magnis periculis a Deo liberati, magnifice gratias agimus ipsi, utpote qui adversus talem regem dimicavimus.
      EN: Delivered by God from great dangers, we give Him great thanks, since we fought against such a king.

1:12  LA: Ipse enim ebullire fecit de Perside eos qui pugnaverunt contra nos et sanctam civitatem. · Translation note. Ebullire literally evokes boiling or surging forth. “Surge forth from Persia” preserves the unusual Latin expression rather than replacing its direction or agency with an inferred historical explanation.
      EN: For He caused those who fought against us and the holy city to surge forth from Persia.

1:13  LA: Nam cum in Perside esset dux ipse, et cum ipso immensus exercitus, cecidit in templo Naneæ, consilio deceptus sacerdotum Naneæ. · Translation note. Nanea is the name actually read in the Latin. The account is translated as this letter presents it; no attempt is made to harmonize its death narrative with chapter 9.
      EN: For when the commander himself was in Persia, with an immense army beside him, he fell in the temple of Nanea, deceived by the plan of Nanea's priests.

1:14  LA: Etenim cum ea habitaturus venit ad locum Antiochus et amici ejus, et ut acciperet pecunias multas dotis nomine.
      EN: For Antiochus and his friends came to the place, intending that he should live with her and receive large sums of money in the name of a dowry.

1:15  LA: Cumque proposuissent eas sacerdotes Naneæ, et ipse cum paucis ingressus esset intra ambitum fani, clauserunt templum,
      EN: When Nanea's priests had displayed the money, and he had entered the enclosure of the shrine with a few men, they closed the temple

1:16  LA: cum intrasset Antiochus : apertoque occulto aditu templi, mittentes lapides percusserunt ducem et eos qui cum eo erant : et diviserunt membratim, et capitibus amputatis foras projecerunt.
      EN: after Antiochus had entered. Opening a concealed entrance to the temple, they hurled stones and struck down the commander and those with him. They cut them limb from limb, severed their heads, and threw them outside.

1:17  LA: Per omnia benedictus Deus, qui tradidit impios.
      EN: Blessed in all things is God, who handed over the ungodly.

1:18  LA: Facturi igitur quinta et vigesima die mensis Casleu purificationem templi, necessarium duximus significare vobis : ut et vos quoque agatis diem scenopegiæ, et diem ignis, qui datus est quando Nehemias ædificato templo et altari obtulit sacrificia.
      EN: Since we are going to celebrate the purification of the temple on the twenty-fifth day of the month of Casleu, we thought it necessary to inform you, so that you too may observe the day of the Feast of Booths and the day of the fire that was given when Nehemiah, after the temple and altar were built, offered sacrifices.

1:19  LA: Nam cum in Persidem ducerentur patres nostri, sacerdotes qui tunc cultores Dei erant, acceptum ignem de altari occulte absconderunt in valle, ubi erat puteus altus et siccus, et in eo contutati sunt eum, ita ut omnibus ignotus esset locus.
      EN: For when our fathers were being led away to Persia, the priests who then worshiped God secretly took fire from the altar and hid it in a valley where there was a deep, dry well. They kept it safe there, so that the place was unknown to everyone.

1:20  LA: Cum autem præterissent anni multi, et placuit Deo ut mitteretur Nehemias a rege Persidis, nepotes sacerdotum illorum qui absconderant, misit ad requirendum ignem : et sicut narraverunt nobis, non invenerunt ignem, sed aquam crassam.
      EN: After many years had passed, when it pleased God that Nehemiah should be sent by the king of Persia, he sent the descendants of those priests who had hidden it to search for the fire. As they reported to us, they found no fire, but thick water.

1:21  LA: Et jussit eos haurire, et afferre sibi : et sacrificia quæ imposita erant, jussit sacerdos Nehemias aspergi ipsa aqua : et ligna, et quæ erant superposita. · Translation note. The source explicitly calls Nehemiah sacerdos, “priest”; the wording is retained without resolving the historical identification.
      EN: He ordered them to draw it out and bring it to him. Nehemiah the priest ordered the sacrifices that had been laid out, the wood, and the things placed on it to be sprinkled with that water.

1:22  LA: Utque hoc factum est, et tempus affuit quo sol refulsit, qui prius erat in nubilo, accensus est ignis magnus, ita ut omnes mirarentur.
      EN: When this was done, and the time came for the sun to shine, which had previously been behind clouds, a great fire was kindled, so that everyone marveled.

1:23  LA: Orationem autem faciebant omnes sacerdotes, dum consummaretur sacrificium, Jonatha inchoante, ceteris autem respondentibus.
      EN: All the priests prayed while the sacrifice was being consumed, Jonathan beginning and the others responding.

1:24  LA: Et Nehemiæ erat oratio hunc habens modum : Domine Deus omnium creator, terribilis et fortis, justus et misericors, qui solus est bonus rex, · Translation note. The Latin moves between direct address and a third-person relative clause. English maintains the prayer as direct address without changing its titles.
      EN: Nehemiah's prayer took this form: “Lord God, Creator of all, fearsome and strong, just and merciful, You who alone are the good King,

1:25  LA: solus præstans, solus justus et omnipotens et æternus, qui liberas Israël de omni malo ; qui fecisti patres electos, et sanctificasti eos :
      EN: alone excellent, alone just and almighty and eternal, who deliver Israel from every evil, who chose the fathers and sanctified them:

1:26  LA: accipe sacrificium pro universo populo tuo Israël, et custodi partem tuam, et sanctifica.
      EN: accept the sacrifice for all Your people Israel; guard Your portion and sanctify it.

1:27  LA: Congrega dispersionem nostram, libera eos qui serviunt gentibus, et contemptos et abominatos respice, ut sciant gentes quia tu es Deus noster.
      EN: Gather our scattered people; free those who serve the nations; look upon those who are despised and abhorred, so that the nations may know that You are our God.

1:28  LA: Afflige opprimentes nos, et contumeliam facientes in superbia.
      EN: Afflict those who oppress us and insult us in their arrogance.

1:29  LA: Constitue populum tuum in loco sancto tuo, sicut dixit Moyses.
      EN: Establish Your people in Your holy place, as Moses said.”

1:30  LA: Sacerdotes autem psallebant hymnos usquequo consumptum esset sacrificium.
      EN: The priests sang hymns until the sacrifice had been consumed.

1:31  LA: Cum autem consumptum esset sacrificium, ex residua aqua Nehemias jussit lapides majores perfundi.
      EN: When the sacrifice was consumed, Nehemiah ordered the larger stones to be drenched with the remaining water.

1:32  LA: Quod ut factum est, ex eis flamma accensa est : sed ex lumine quod refulsit ab altari, consumpta est.
      EN: When this was done, a flame was kindled from them, but it was consumed by the light that shone from the altar.

1:33  LA: Ut vero manifestata est res, renuntiatum est regi Persarum quod in loco in quo ignem absconderent hi qui translati fuerant sacerdotes, aqua apparuit, de qua Nehemias, et qui cum eo erant, purificaverunt sacrificia.
      EN: When the matter became known, the king of the Persians was told that water had appeared in the place where the priests who had been deported had hidden the fire, and that Nehemiah and those with him had purified the sacrifices with it.

1:34  LA: Considerans autem rex, et rem diligenter examinans, fecit ei templum, ut probaret quod factum erat : · Translation note. Fecit ei templum is rendered “made a shrine for it.” The pronoun’s precise referent and the nature of the structure remain uncertain; no new historical identification is supplied.
      EN: The king considered it and examined the matter carefully, and made a shrine for it, to verify what had happened.

1:35  LA: et cum probasset, sacerdotibus donavit multa bona, et alia atque alia munera : et accipiens manu sua, tribuebat eis.
      EN: When he had verified it, he gave the priests many good things and gift after gift; taking them in his own hand, he distributed them to them.

1:36  LA: Appellavit autem Nehemias hunc locum Nephthar, quod interpretatur Purificatio : vocatur autem apud plures Nephi. · Translation note. The Latin calls the referent “this place,” not merely the liquid; that wording is preserved.
      EN: Nehemiah called this place Nephthar, which means “Purification,” but among many it is called Nephi.

2 MACCABEES 2

2:1  LA: Invenitur autem in descriptionibus Jeremiæ prophetæ, quod jussit eos ignem accipere qui transmigrabant, ut significatum est, et ut mandavit transmigratis.
     EN: In the records of Jeremiah the prophet it is found that he ordered those who were being deported to take fire, as has been related, and as he commanded the deportees.

2:2  LA: Et dedit illis legem, ne obliviscerentur præcepta Domini, et non exerrarent mentibus, videntes simulacra aurea et argentea, et ornamenta eorum.
     EN: He gave them the law, so that they would not forget the Lord's commandments or stray in their minds when they saw gold and silver images and their ornaments.

2:3  LA: Et alia hujusmodi dicens, hortabatur ne legem amoverent a corde suo.
     EN: Saying other things of this kind, he urged them not to remove the law from their hearts.

2:4  LA: Erat autem in ipsa scriptura, quomodo tabernaculum et arcam jussit propheta divino responso ad se facto comitari secum, usquequo exiit in montem in quo Moyses ascendit, et vidit Dei hæreditatem.
     EN: In that same writing it was recorded how the prophet, after receiving a divine response, ordered the tent and the ark to accompany him, until he went out to the mountain that Moses had climbed and from which he had seen God's inheritance.

2:5  LA: Et veniens ibi Jeremias, invenit locum speluncæ : et tabernaculum, et arcam, et altare incensi intulit illuc, et ostium obstruxit.
     EN: When Jeremiah arrived there, he found a place with a cave. He brought the tent, the ark, and the altar of incense into it, and blocked the entrance.

2:6  LA: Et accesserunt quidam simul, qui sequebantur, ut notarent sibi locum : et non potuerunt invenire.
     EN: Some who had followed came together to mark the place for themselves, but could not find it.

2:7  LA: Ut autem cognovit Jeremias, culpans illos dixit : Quod ignotus erit locus donec congreget Deus congregationem populi, et propitius fiat :
     EN: When Jeremiah learned of this, he reproved them and said, “The place will remain unknown until God gathers the assembly of the people and becomes merciful.

2:8  LA: et tunc Dominus ostendet hæc, et apparebit majestas Domini, et nubes erit, sicut et Moysi manifestabatur, et sicut cum Salomon petiit ut locus sanctificaretur magno Deo, manifestabat hæc. · Translation note. The Latin sentence intertwines the cloud, divine manifestation, and Solomon’s request. The rendering preserves the sequence without supplying a detailed reconstruction.
     EN: Then the Lord will reveal these things, and the Lord's majesty will appear, and there will be a cloud, just as it was revealed to Moses, and just as He revealed these things when Solomon asked that the place be sanctified for the great God.”

2:9  LA: Magnifice etenim sapientiam tractabat : et ut sapientiam habens, obtulit sacrificium dedicationis et consummationis templi.
     EN: For he displayed wisdom magnificently, and as one possessing wisdom, he offered the sacrifice for the dedication and completion of the temple.

2:10  LA: Sicut et Moyses orabat ad Dominum, et descendit ignis de cælo et consumpsit holocaustum, sic et Salomon oravit, et descendit ignis de cælo et consumpsit holocaustum.
      EN: Just as Moses prayed to the Lord and fire came down from heaven and consumed the whole burnt offering, so Solomon also prayed, and fire came down from heaven and consumed the whole burnt offering.

2:11  LA: Et dixit Moyses : Eo quod non sit comestum quod erat pro peccato, consumptum est.
      EN: And Moses said, “Because the offering for sin was not eaten, it was consumed.”

2:12  LA: Similiter et Salomon octo diebus celebravit dedicationem.
      EN: Likewise Solomon also celebrated the dedication for eight days.

2:13  LA: Inferebantur autem in descriptionibus et commentariis Nehemiæ hæc eadem : et ut construens bibliothecam congregavit de regionibus libros et prophetarum et David, et epistolas regum, et de donariis. · Translation note. The accessed Latin reads de regionibus, “from the regions”; the translation does not substitute “concerning kings.” The relation of et de donariis to the letters is expressed as “including those concerning offerings.”
      EN: These same things were included in Nehemiah's records and memoirs: how, in establishing a library, he gathered books from the regions, books of the prophets and of David, and letters of kings, including those concerning offerings.

2:14  LA: Similiter autem et Judas ea quæ deciderant per bellum quod nobis acciderat, congregavit omnia, et sunt apud nos.
      EN: Likewise Judas gathered together everything that had been lost through the war that came upon us, and they are in our possession.

2:15  LA: Si ergo desideratis hæc, mittite qui perferant vobis.
      EN: Therefore, if you desire these things, send people to carry them to you.

2:16  LA: Acturi itaque purificationem scripsimus vobis : bene ergo facietis, si egeritis hos dies.
      EN: Since we are about to celebrate the purification, we have written to you. You will therefore do well if you observe these days.

2:17  LA: Deus autem, qui liberavit populum suum, et reddidit hæreditatem omnibus, et regnum, et sacerdotium, et sanctificationem,
      EN: But God, who delivered His people and restored the inheritance to all, and the kingdom, priesthood, and sanctification,

2:18  LA: sicut promisit in lege, speramus quod cito nostri miserebitur, et congregavit de sub cælo in locum sanctum. · Translation note. The accessed Latin has congregavit, a perfect tense, “has gathered.” It is retained despite the surrounding expectation of future mercy; it is not silently changed to congregabit, “will gather.”
      EN: as He promised in the law—we hope that He will soon have mercy on us—has gathered us from under heaven into the holy place.

2:19  LA: De Juda vero Machabæo, et fratribus ejus, et de templi magni purificatione, et de aræ dedicatione,
      EN: Now concerning Judas Maccabeus and his brothers, the purification of the great temple, and the dedication of the altar,

2:20  LA: sed et de præliis quæ pertinent ad Antiochum Nobilem et filium ejus Eupatorem, · Translation note. Nobilis is translated as the title “the Illustrious.” This is a rendering of the Latin title rather than a substitution of a Greek form.
      EN: and also the battles involving Antiochus the Illustrious and his son Eupator,

2:21  LA: et de illuminationibus quæ de cælo factæ sunt ad eos qui pro Judæis fortiter fecerunt, ita ut universam regionem, cum pauci essent, vindicarent, et barbaram multitudinem fugarent,
      EN: and the manifestations of light from heaven to those who acted bravely for the Jews, so that, though few, they recovered the whole region and put the barbarian multitude to flight,

2:22  LA: et famosissimum in toto orbe templum recuperarent, et civitatem liberarent, et leges quæ abolitæ erant, restituerentur, Domino cum omni tranquillitate propitio facto illis.
      EN: and recovered the temple renowned throughout the world, freed the city, and restored the laws that had been abolished, the Lord having become gracious to them with complete tranquility—

2:23  LA: Itemque ab Jasone Cyrenæo quinque libris comprehensa tentavimus nos uno volumine breviare.
      EN: these things, comprised by Jason of Cyrene in five books, we have attempted to abridge into one volume.

2:24  LA: Considerantes enim multitudinem librorum, et difficultatem volentibus aggredi narrationes historiarum propter multitudinem rerum,
      EN: For, considering the multitude of books and the difficulty for those wishing to undertake the historical narratives because of the abundance of events,

2:25  LA: curavimus volentibus quidem legere, ut esset animi oblectatio : studiosis vero, ut facilius possint memoriæ commendare : omnibus autem legentibus utilitas conferatur.
      EN: we have taken care that those who wish to read may find enjoyment, that the studious may more easily commit things to memory, and that all readers may receive benefit.

2:26  LA: Et nobis quidem ipsis, qui hoc opus breviandi causa suscepimus, non facilem laborem, immo vero negotium plenum vigiliarum et sudoris assumpsimus.
      EN: For our own part, in undertaking this work of abridgment, we have assumed no easy labor, but rather a task full of sleeplessness and sweat.

2:27  LA: Sicut hi qui præparant convivium, et quærunt aliorum voluntati parere propter multorum gratiam, libenter laborem sustinemus.
      EN: Like those who prepare a banquet and seek to satisfy the wishes of others, we gladly endure the labor for the pleasure of many.

2:28  LA: Veritatem quidem de singulis auctoribus concedentes, ipsi autem secundum datam formam brevitati studentes.
      EN: We leave the truth of individual matters to the original authors, while we ourselves strive for brevity according to the form we have adopted.

2:29  LA: Sicut enim novæ domus architecto de universa structura curandum est ; ei vero qui pingere curat, quæ apta sunt ad ornatum exquirenda sunt : ita æstimandum est et in nobis.
      EN: For just as the architect of a new house must care for the entire structure, while the one who undertakes its painting must seek what is suitable for its decoration, so it must also be understood in our case.

2:30  LA: Etenim intellectum colligere, et ordinare sermonem, et curiosius partes singulas quasque disquirere, historiæ congruit auctori :
      EN: For gathering understanding, arranging the account, and investigating each individual part more carefully belong to the author of a history.

2:31  LA: brevitatem vero dictionis sectari, et executiones rerum vitare, brevianti concedendum est.
      EN: But the abridger must be allowed to pursue brevity of expression and avoid detailed treatment of events.

2:32  LA: Hinc ergo narrationem incipiemus : de præfatione tantum dixisse sufficiat. Stultum etenim est ante historiam effluere, in ipsa autem historia succingi.
      EN: Here, then, we will begin the narrative; let this much suffice for the preface. For it is foolish to overflow before the history but be restricted within the history itself.

2 MACCABEES 3

3:1  LA: Igitur cum sancta civitas habitaretur in omni pace, leges etiam adhuc optime custodirentur, propter Oniæ pontificis pietatem, et animos odio habentes mala,
     EN: While the holy city was inhabited in complete peace, and the laws were still kept very well because of the piety of Onias the high priest and his hatred of evil,

3:2  LA: fiebat ut et ipsi reges et principes locum summo honore dignum ducerent, et templum maximis muneribus illustrarent :
     EN: it came about that even kings and rulers regarded the place as worthy of the highest honor and adorned the temple with very great gifts.

3:3  LA: ita ut Seleucus Asiæ rex de redditibus suis præstaret omnes sumptus ad ministerium sacrificiorum pertinentes.
     EN: Thus Seleucus, king of Asia, supplied from his own revenues all the expenses belonging to the service of the sacrifices.

3:4  LA: Simon autem de tribu Benjamin, præpositus templi constitutus, contendebat, obsistente sibi principe sacerdotum, iniquum aliquid in civitate moliri.
     EN: But Simon, of the tribe of Benjamin, who had been appointed overseer of the temple, strove to carry out something lawless in the city, while the chief priest opposed him.

3:5  LA: Sed cum vincere Oniam non posset, venit ad Apollonium Tharsææ filium, qui eo tempore erat dux Cœlesyriæ et Phœnicis :
     EN: When he could not overcome Onias, he went to Apollonius son of Tharseas, who at that time was governor of Coelesyria and Phoenicia.

3:6  LA: et nuntiavit ei pecuniis innumerabilibus plenum esse ærarium Jerosolymis, et communes copias immensas esse, quæ non pertinent ad rationem sacrificiorum : esse autem possibile sub potestate regis cadere universa.
     EN: He informed him that the treasury in Jerusalem was full of uncountable riches, and that its common funds were immense and did not belong to the account for sacrifices; it was possible for all of them to fall under the king's control.

3:7  LA: Cumque retulisset ad regem Apollonius de pecuniis quæ delatæ erant, illæ accitum Heliodorum, qui erat super negotia ejus, misit, cum mandatis ut prædictam pecuniam transportaret.
     EN: When Apollonius reported to the king the money that had been disclosed, the king summoned Heliodorus, who was in charge of his affairs, and sent him with orders to carry away the aforesaid money.

3:8  LA: Statimque Heliodorus iter est agressus, specie quidem quasi per Cœlesyriam et Phœnicen civitates esset peragraturus, re vera autem regis propositum perfecturus.
     EN: Heliodorus immediately set out, ostensibly to visit the cities throughout Coelesyria and Phoenicia, but actually to accomplish the king's purpose.

3:9  LA: Sed cum venisset Jerosolymam, et benigne a summo sacerdote in civitate esset exceptus, narravit de dato indicio pecuniarum, et cujus rei gratia adesset, aperuit : interrogabat autem si vere hæc ita essent.
     EN: When he arrived in Jerusalem and was kindly received in the city by the high priest, he recounted the information given about the money and disclosed why he was there. He asked whether these things were really so.

3:10  LA: Tunc summus sacerdos ostendit deposita esse hæc, et victualia viduarum et pupillorum :
      EN: Then the high priest explained that these were deposits and provisions for widows and orphans.

3:11  LA: quædam vero esse Hircani Tobiæ viri valde eminentis, in his quæ detulerat impius Simon : universa autem argenti talenta esse quadringenta, et auri ducenta : · Translation note. Hircani Tobiæ is understood as the conventional patronymic “Hyrcanus son of Tobias”; “son” makes the implied kinship readable in English.
      EN: Some belonged to Hyrcanus son of Tobias, a very prominent man, among the funds that the ungodly Simon had reported. The total was four hundred talents of silver and two hundred of gold.

3:12  LA: decipi vero eos qui credidissent loco et templo quod per universum mundum honoratur pro sui veneratione et sanctitate, omnino impossibile esse.
      EN: It was altogether impossible to defraud those who had entrusted their possessions to the place and to the temple honored throughout the whole world for its dignity and holiness.

3:13  LA: At ille pro his quæ habebat in mandatis a rege, dicebat omni genere regi ea esse deferenda.
      EN: But Heliodorus, because of the orders he had from the king, said that by every means these funds must be brought to the king.

3:14  LA: Constituta autem die, intrabat de his Heliodorus ordinaturus. Non modica vero per universam civitatem erat trepidatio.
      EN: On the appointed day, Heliodorus went in to arrange these matters. No small alarm spread throughout the whole city.

3:15  LA: Sacerdotes autem ante altare cum stolis sacerdotalibus jactaverunt se, et invocabant de cælo eum qui de depositis legem posuit, ut his qui deposuerant ea salva custodiret.
      EN: The priests, in their priestly robes, threw themselves down before the altar and called upon Him in heaven who had established the law concerning deposits, asking Him to keep them safe for those who had deposited them.

3:16  LA: Jam vero qui videbat summi sacerdotis vultum, mente vulnerabatur : facies enim et color immutatus declarabat internum animi dolorem :
      EN: Anyone who saw the high priest's face was wounded in spirit, for his face and altered color revealed the inward anguish of his soul.

3:17  LA: circumfusa enim erat mœstitia quædam viro, et horror corporis, per quem manifestus aspicientibus dolor cordis ejus efficiebatur.
      EN: A certain grief and bodily trembling had enveloped the man, making the sorrow of his heart plain to those who looked upon him.

3:18  LA: Alii etiam gregatim de domibus confluebant, publica supplicatione obsecrantes, pro eo quod in contemptum locus esset venturus.
      EN: Others also streamed from their houses in groups, entreating in public supplication because the place was about to be brought into contempt.

3:19  LA: Accinctæque mulieres ciliciis pectus, per plateas confluebant : sed et virgines quæ conclusæ erant, procurrebant ad Oniam, aliæ autem ad muros, quædam vero per fenestras aspiciebant : · Translation note. The source reads ad Oniam, “to Onias.” This is retained rather than substituted with a different destination.
      EN: Women, with sackcloth bound around their breasts, streamed through the streets. Even the maidens who had been secluded ran out to Onias; others went to the walls, and some looked out through the windows.

3:20  LA: universæ autem protendentes manus in cælum, deprecabantur :
      EN: All stretched out their hands toward heaven and prayed.

3:21  LA: erat enim misera commistæ multitudinis, et magni sacerdotis in agone constituti exspectatio.
      EN: For the expectation of the mingled crowd and the high priest in his anguish was pitiable.

3:22  LA: Et hi quidem invocabant omnipotentem Deum, ut credita sibi his qui crediderant, cum omni integritate conservarentur.
      EN: They called upon almighty God, asking that what had been entrusted be preserved entirely intact for those who had entrusted it.

3:23  LA: Heliodorus autem, quod decreverat, perficiebat eodem loco ipse cum satellitibus circa ærarium præsens.
      EN: But Heliodorus proceeded with what he had decided, standing there by the treasury with his guards.

3:24  LA: Sed spiritus omnipotentis Dei magnam fecit suæ ostensionis evidentiam, ita ut omnes qui ausi fuerant parere ei, ruentes Dei virtute, in dissolutionem et formidinem converterentur. · Translation note. Spiritus omnipotentis Dei is rendered “the Spirit of almighty God.” The capitalization follows the project’s divine-reference convention without claiming a later doctrinal formulation.
      EN: Then the Spirit of almighty God made His manifestation unmistakably evident, so that all who had dared obey Heliodorus fell by God's power and were overcome with weakness and dread.

3:25  LA: Apparuit enim illis quidam equus terribilem habens sessorem, optimis operimentis adornatus : isque cum impetu Heliodoro priores calces elisit : qui autem ei sedebat, videbatur arma habere aurea.
      EN: For a horse appeared to them, adorned with splendid coverings and bearing a fearsome rider. Charging forward, it struck Heliodorus with its forehooves, while the one seated upon it appeared to have golden armor.

3:26  LA: Alii etiam apparuerunt duo juvenes virtute decori, optimi gloria, speciosique amictu : qui circumsteterunt eum, et ex utraque parte flagellabant, sine intermissione multis plagis verberantes.
      EN: Two other young men also appeared, beautiful in strength, splendid in glory, and magnificent in clothing. They stood beside him and flogged him on either side, beating him without interruption with many blows.

3:27  LA: Subito autem Heliodorus concidit in terram, eumque multa caligine circumfusum rapuerunt, atque in sella gestatoria positum ejecerunt.
      EN: Suddenly Heliodorus fell to the ground. Enveloped in deep darkness, he was seized, placed on a carrying chair, and taken out.

3:28  LA: Et is, qui cum multis cursoribus et satellitibus prædictum ingressus est ærarium, portabatur nullo sibi auxilium ferente, manifesta Dei cognita virtute :
      EN: The man who had entered the aforesaid treasury with many runners and guards was carried away with no one bringing him help, God's power having been plainly recognized.

3:29  LA: et ille quidem per divinam virtutem jacebat mutus, atque omni spe et salute privatus.
      EN: Through the divine power he lay speechless and deprived of all hope and deliverance.

3:30  LA: Hi autem Dominum benedicebant, quia magnificabat locum suum : et templum, quod paulo ante timore ac tumultu erat plenum, apparente omnipotente Domino, gaudio et lætitia impletum est.
      EN: But they blessed the Lord, because He had glorified His place. The temple, which shortly before had been full of fear and tumult, was filled with joy and gladness when the almighty Lord appeared.

3:31  LA: Tunc vero ex amicis Heliodori quidam rogabant confestim Oniam, ut invocaret Altissimum ut vitam donaret ei qui in supremo spiritu erat constitutus.
      EN: Then some of Heliodorus's friends immediately begged Onias to call upon the Most High to grant life to the man who was at his last breath.

3:32  LA: Considerans autem summus sacerdos ne forte rex suspicaretur malitiam aliquam ex Judæis circa Heliodorum consummatum, obtulit pro salute viri hostiam salutarem. · Translation note. Hostiam salutarem is rendered “a saving sacrifice”; pro salute viri specifies the man’s recovery. The text is not assigned a more precise sacrificial category than the Latin supplies.
      EN: The high priest, considering that the king might perhaps suspect that some malice against Heliodorus had been carried out by the Jews, offered a saving sacrifice for the man's recovery.

3:33  LA: Cumque summus sacerdos exoraret, iidem juvenes eisdem vestibus amicti astantes Heliodoro, dixerunt : Oniæ sacerdoti gratias age : nam propter eum Dominus tibi vitam donavit.
      EN: While the high priest prayed, those same young men, clothed in the same garments, stood beside Heliodorus and said, “Give thanks to Onias the priest, for on his account the Lord has granted you life.

3:34  LA: Tu autem a Deo flagellatus, nuntia omnibus magnalia Dei, et potestatem. Et his dictis, non comparuerunt.
      EN: But you, who have been scourged by God, proclaim to everyone God's mighty deeds and power.” Having said this, they disappeared.

3:35  LA: Heliodorus autem, hostia Deo oblata, et votis magnis promissis ei qui vivere illi concessit, et Oniæ gratias agens, recepto exercitu, repedabat ad regem.
      EN: Heliodorus offered a sacrifice to God and made great vows to Him who had allowed him to live. Giving thanks to Onias and taking his force with him, he returned to the king.

3:36  LA: Testabatur autem omnibus ea quæ sub oculis suis viderat opera magni Dei.
      EN: He testified to everyone about the works of the great God that he had seen with his own eyes.

3:37  LA: Cum autem rex interrogasset Heliodorum, quis esset aptus adhuc semel Jerosolymam mitti, ait :
      EN: When the king asked Heliodorus who would be suitable to send to Jerusalem once more, he said,

3:38  LA: Si quem habes hostem, aut regni tui insidiatorem, mitte illuc, et flagellatum eum recipies, si tamen evaserit : eo quod in loco sit vere Dei quædam virtus.
      EN: “If you have an enemy or someone plotting against your kingdom, send him there, and you will receive him back scourged—if he survives at all—because there is truly a divine power in that place.

3:39  LA: Nam ipse, qui habet in cælis habitationem, visitator et adjutor est loci illius, et venientes ad malefaciendum percutit ac perdit.
      EN: For He whose dwelling is in heaven watches over and assists that place, and strikes and destroys those who come to do evil.”

3:40  LA: Igitur de Heliodoro et ærarii custodia ita res se habet.
      EN: Thus matters stand concerning Heliodorus and the preservation of the treasury.

2 MACCABEES 4

4:1  LA: Simon autem prædictus, pecuniarum et patriæ delator, male loquebatur de Onia, tamquam ipse Heliodorum instigasset ad hæc, et ipse fuisset incentor malorum :
     EN: But the aforesaid Simon, who had informed against the treasury and his homeland, spoke maliciously about Onias, as though he himself had incited Heliodorus to these deeds and had stirred up the troubles.

4:2  LA: provisoremque civitatis, ac defensorem gentis suæ, et æmulatorem legis Dei, audebat insidiatorem regni dicere.
     EN: He dared to call the city's guardian, the defender of his nation, and the zealous champion of God's law a plotter against the kingdom.

4:3  LA: Sed cum inimicitiæ in tantum procederent ut etiam per quosdam Simonis necessarios homicidia fierent,
     EN: When their hostilities advanced so far that even murders were committed through certain associates of Simon,

4:4  LA: considerans Onias periculum contentionis, et Apollonium insanire, utpote ducem Cœlesyriæ et Phœnicis, ad augendam malitiam Simonis ad regem se contulit,
     EN: Onias considered the danger of the conflict and the frenzied efforts of Apollonius, as governor of Coelesyria and Phoenicia, to increase Simon's malice, and went to the king.

4:5  LA: non ut civium accusator, sed communem utilitatem apud semetipsum universæ multitudinis considerans.
     EN: He did not go as an accuser of his fellow citizens, but with the common good of the whole multitude in mind.

4:6  LA: Videbat enim sine regali providentia impossibile esse pacem rebus dari, nec Simonem posse cessare a stultitia sua.
     EN: For he saw that without royal intervention it was impossible to bring peace to affairs, and that Simon could not cease from his folly.

4:7  LA: Sed post Seleuci vitæ excessum, cum suscepisset regnum Antiochus, qui Nobilis appellabatur, ambiebat Jason frater Oniæ summum sacerdotium :
     EN: After Seleucus's death, when Antiochus, who was called the Illustrious, took the kingdom, Jason, Onias's brother, sought the high priesthood.

4:8  LA: adito rege, promittens ei argenti talenta trecenta sexaginta, et ex redditibus aliis talenta octoginta,
     EN: He approached the king, promising him three hundred sixty talents of silver and eighty talents from other revenues.

4:9  LA: super hæc promittebat et alia centum quinquaginta, si potestati ejus concederetur, gymnasium et ephebiam sibi constituere, et eos qui in Jerosolymis erant, Antiochenos scribere. · Translation note. Ephebia denotes an institution for young men; the translation gives a brief functional English rendering. Gymnasium retains its ancient institutional setting, not the narrower meaning of a modern exercise room.
     EN: Besides these, he promised another hundred fifty if he were granted authority to establish a gymnasium and a training institution for young men, and to enroll the people of Jerusalem as Antiochenes.

4:10  LA: Quod cum rex annuisset, et obtinuisset principatum, statim ad gentilem ritum contribules suos transferre cœpit,
      EN: When the king agreed and Jason obtained the office, he immediately began to turn his fellow tribesmen toward Gentile customs.

4:11  LA: et amotis his quæ humanitatis causa Judæis a regibus fuerant constituta per Joannem patrem Eupolemi, qui apud Romanos de amicitia et societate functus est legatione legitima, civium jura destituens, prava instituta sanciebat.
      EN: He removed the concessions that the kings had established out of kindness to the Jews through John, the father of Eupolemus, who had undertaken a lawful embassy to the Romans concerning friendship and alliance. Abolishing the rights of the citizens, he sanctioned corrupt institutions.

4:12  LA: Etenim ausus est sub ipsa arce gymnasium constituere, et optimos quosque epheborum in lupanaribus ponere. · Translation note. The Latin explicitly reads in lupanaribus, “in brothels.” This conspicuous wording is retained; it has not been harmonized to another textual tradition.
      EN: For he dared to establish a gymnasium directly below the citadel and to place the finest of the young men in brothels.

4:13  LA: Erat autem hoc non initium, sed incrementum quoddam, et profectus gentilis et alienigenæ conversationis, propter impii et non sacerdotis Jasonis nefarium, et inauditum scelus :
      EN: This was not a beginning but a certain increase and advance of the Gentile and foreign way of life, because of the wicked and unheard-of crime of the ungodly Jason, who was no priest.

4:14  LA: ita ut sacerdotes jam non circa altaris officia dediti essent, sed contempto templo et sacrificiis neglectis, festinarent participes fieri palæstræ et præbitionis ejus injustæ, et in exercitiis disci.
      EN: Thus the priests were no longer devoted to their duties at the altar. Despising the temple and neglecting the sacrifices, they hurried to take part in the wrestling ground, its unlawful provision, and the exercises of the discus.

4:15  LA: Et patrios quidem honores nihil habentes, græcas glorias optimas arbitrabantur :
      EN: They regarded the honors of their fathers as nothing and considered Greek glories the best.

4:16  LA: quarum gratia periculosa eos contentio habebat, et eorum instituta æmulabantur, ac per omnia his consimiles esse cupiebant, quos hostes et peremptores habuerant.
      EN: For their sake a dangerous rivalry gripped them, and they imitated the institutions of those whom they had as enemies and destroyers, desiring to resemble them in every respect.

4:17  LA: In leges enim divinas impie agere impune non cedit : sed hoc tempus sequens declarabit.
      EN: For acting impiously against the divine laws does not go unpunished; the time that follows will show this.

4:18  LA: Cum autem quinquennalis agon Tyri celebraretur, et rex præsens esset,
      EN: When the games held every fifth year were being celebrated at Tyre, with the king present,

4:19  LA: misit Jason facinorosus ab Jerosolymis viros peccatores, portantes argenti didrachmas trecentas in sacrificum Herculis : quas postulaverunt hi qui asportaverant ne in sacrificiis erogarentur, quia non oporteret, sed in alios sumptus eas deputari. · Translation note. Didrachms are retained as the named denomination; no modern currency or fixed wage equivalent is substituted.
      EN: the criminal Jason sent sinful men from Jerusalem carrying three hundred silver didrachms for a sacrifice to Hercules. Those who carried them requested that they not be spent on sacrifices, since that was not proper, but assigned to other expenses.

4:20  LA: Sed hæ oblatæ sunt quidem ab eo qui miserat in sacrificium Herculis : propter præsentes autem datæ sunt in fabricam navium triremium. · Translation note. Propter præsentes is literally “because of those present.” Its compressed expression is retained without identifying a motive absent from the Latin.
      EN: Though the money had been offered for a sacrifice to Hercules by the man who sent it, because of those present it was given for the construction of triremes.

4:21  LA: Misso autem in Ægyptum Apollonio Mnesthei filio propter primates Ptolemæi Philometoris regis, cum cognovisset Antiochus alienum se a negotiis regni effectum, propriis utilitatibus consulens, profectus inde venit Joppen, et inde Jerosolymam. · Translation note. Propter primates is rendered “on account of the leading men,” preserving the accessed wording rather than silently supplying a coronation or other event.
      EN: When Apollonius son of Menestheus was sent to Egypt on account of the leading men of King Ptolemy Philometor, Antiochus learned that he had been excluded from the affairs of the kingdom. Looking after his own interests, he departed from there, came to Joppa, and then to Jerusalem.

4:22  LA: Et magnifice ab Jasone et civitate susceptus, cum facularum luminibus et laudibus ingressus est : et inde in Phœnicen exercitum convertit.
      EN: Magnificently received by Jason and the city, he entered amid the light of torches and acclamations. From there he turned his army toward Phoenicia.

4:23  LA: Et post triennii tempus, misit Jason Menelaum supradicti Simonis fratrem portantem pecunias regi, et de negotiis necessariis responsa perlaturum.
      EN: After three years, Jason sent Menelaus, the brother of the aforementioned Simon, to carry money to the king and bring back responses concerning necessary affairs.

4:24  LA: At ille commendatus regi, cum magnificasset faciem potestatis ejus, in semetipsum retorsit summum sacerdotium, superponens Jasoni talenta argenti trecenta.
      EN: But Menelaus, after being presented to the king and magnifying the display of his power, diverted the high priesthood to himself, offering three hundred silver talents more than Jason.

4:25  LA: Acceptisque a rege mandatis, venit, nihil quidem habens dignum sacerdotio : animos vero crudelis tyranni, et feræ beluæ iram gerens.
      EN: Having received the king's orders, he returned with nothing worthy of the priesthood, but bearing the disposition of a cruel tyrant and the rage of a wild beast.

4:26  LA: Et Jason quidem, qui proprium fratrem captivaverat, ipse deceptus profugus in Ammanitem expulsus est regionem. · Translation note. Captivaverat is rendered “had taken captive.” Its stronger wording is preserved rather than reduced to a neutral change of office.
      EN: Jason, who had taken his own brother captive, was himself deceived and driven out as a fugitive into the country of the Ammonites.

4:27  LA: Menelaus autem principatum quidem obtinuit : de pecuniis vero regi promissis, nihil agebat, cum exactionem faceret Sostratus, qui arci erat præpositus,
      EN: Menelaus obtained the office but did nothing about the money promised to the king, although Sostratus, the commander of the citadel, demanded payment.

4:28  LA: nam ad hunc exactio vectigalium pertinebant : quam ob causam utrique ad regem sunt evocati.
      EN: For the collection of revenues belonged to him; for this reason both men were summoned to the king.

4:29  LA: Et Menelaus amotus est a sacerdotio, succedente Lysimacho fratre suo : Sostratus autem prælatus est Cypriis. · Translation note. The Latin states that Menelaus “was removed” and that Lysimachus succeeded him; the translation does not silently replace this with temporary deputizing.
      EN: Menelaus was removed from the priesthood, his brother Lysimachus succeeding him, while Sostratus was placed over the Cypriots.

4:30  LA: Et cum hæc agerentur, contigit Tharsenses et Mallotas seditionem movere, eo quod Antiochidi regis concubinæ dono essent dati.
      EN: While these things were taking place, the people of Tarsus and Mallus happened to rise in revolt, because they had been given as a gift to Antiochis, the king's concubine.

4:31  LA: Festinanter itaque rex venit sedare illos, relicto suffecto uno ex comitibus suis Andronico.
      EN: The king therefore came in haste to pacify them, leaving Andronicus, one of his companions, as his deputy.

4:32  LA: Ratus autem Menelaus accepisse se tempus opportunum, aurea quædam vasa e templo furatus donavit Andronico, et alia vendiderat Tyri, et per vicinas civitates.
      EN: Menelaus, thinking that he had obtained a favorable opportunity, stole certain gold vessels from the temple and gave them to Andronicus. Other vessels he had sold at Tyre and throughout the neighboring cities.

4:33  LA: Quod cum certissime cognovisset Onias, arguebat eum, ipse in loco tuto se continens Antiochiæ secus Daphnem.
      EN: When Onias learned this with certainty, he denounced him, while remaining in a safe place at Antioch near Daphne.

4:34  LA: Unde Menelaus accedens ad Andronicum, rogabat ut Oniam interficeret. Qui cum venisset ad Oniam, et datis dextris cum jurejurando (quamvis esset ei suspectus) suasisset de asylo procedere, statim eum peremit, non veritus justitiam.
      EN: Therefore Menelaus approached Andronicus and asked him to kill Onias. Andronicus came to Onias and, giving his right hand with an oath, persuaded him to leave the place of asylum, although Onias suspected him. He immediately killed him, without regard for justice.

4:35  LA: Ob quam causam non solum Judæi, sed aliæ quoque nationes indignabantur, et moleste ferebant de nece tanti viri injusta.
      EN: For this reason not only the Jews but other nations also were indignant and deeply distressed at the unjust killing of so great a man.

4:36  LA: Sed regressum regem de Ciliciæ locis adierunt Judæi apud Antiochiam, simul et Græci, conquerentes de iniqua nece Oniæ.
      EN: When the king returned from the districts of Cilicia, the Jews at Antioch, together with the Greeks, approached him and complained about the lawless killing of Onias.

4:37  LA: Contristatus itaque animo Antiochus propter Oniam, et flexus ad misericordiam, lacrimas fudit, recordatus defuncti sobrietatem et modestiam :
      EN: Antiochus was therefore grieved in spirit over Onias and moved to compassion. He shed tears, remembering the dead man's self-restraint and modesty.

4:38  LA: accensisque animis Andronicum purpura exutum, per totam civitatem jubet circumduci : et in eodem loco in quo in Oniam impietatem commiserat, sacrilegum vita privari, Domino illi condignam retribuente pœnam.
      EN: Enraged, he ordered Andronicus to be stripped of his purple robe and led around the whole city. He ordered that sacrilegious man to be deprived of life in the very place where he had committed his impiety against Onias, the Lord repaying him with the punishment he deserved.

4:39  LA: Multis autem sacrilegiis in templo a Lysimacho commissis Menelai consilio, et divulgata fama, congregata est multitudo adversum Lysimachum multo jam auro exportato.
      EN: When Lysimachus had committed many acts of sacrilege in the temple at Menelaus's suggestion, and the report had spread, the multitude assembled against Lysimachus, much gold having already been carried away.

4:40  LA: Turbis autem insurgentibus, et animis ira repletis, Lysimachus armatis fere tribus millibus iniquis manibus uti cœpit, duce quodam tyranno, ætate pariter et dementia provecto. · Translation note. The source has a descriptive tyrannus, rendered “tyrant”; no different personal name is imported.
      EN: As the crowds rose and their spirits filled with anger, Lysimachus armed nearly three thousand men and began to use violent hands, under a certain tyrant advanced alike in age and madness.

4:41  LA: Sed ut intellexerunt conatum Lysimachi, alii lapides, alii fustes validos arripuere : quidam vero cinerem in Lysimachum jecere.
      EN: When they understood Lysimachus's attempt, some seized stones and others stout clubs, while some threw ashes at Lysimachus.

4:42  LA: Et multi quidem vulnerati, quidam autem et prostrati, omnes vero in fugam conversi sunt : ipsum etiam sacrilegum secus ærarium interfecerunt.
      EN: Many were wounded, some were struck down, and all were put to flight. They also killed the sacrilegious man himself beside the treasury.

4:43  LA: De his ergo cœpit judicium adversus Menelaum agitari.
      EN: Proceedings concerning these matters were therefore begun against Menelaus.

4:44  LA: Et cum venisset rex Tyrum, ad ipsum negotium detulerunt missi tres viri a senioribus.
      EN: When the king came to Tyre, three men sent by the elders brought the case before him.

4:45  LA: Et cum superaretur Menelaus, promisit Ptolemæo multas pecunias dare ad suadendum regi.
      EN: When Menelaus was losing the case, he promised to give Ptolemy large sums of money to persuade the king.

4:46  LA: Itaque Ptolemæus in quodam atrio positum quasi refrigerandi gratia regem adiit, et deduxit a sententia :
      EN: Ptolemy therefore approached the king, who was in a certain courtyard as if to refresh himself, and drew him away from his decision.

4:47  LA: et Menelaum quidem universæ malitiæ reum criminibus absolvit : miseros autem qui, etiamsi apud Scythas causam dixissent, innocentes judicarentur, hos morte damnavit.
      EN: The king acquitted Menelaus of the charges, though he was guilty of all the wickedness, but condemned to death the unfortunate men who would have been judged innocent even if they had pleaded their case before the Scythians.

4:48  LA: Cito ergo injustam pœnam dederunt, qui pro civitate, et populo, et sacris vasis causam prosecuti sunt.
      EN: Thus those who had pursued the case on behalf of the city, the people, and the sacred vessels quickly suffered an unjust punishment.

4:49  LA: Quam ob rem Tyrii quoque indignati, erga sepulturam eorum liberalissimi extiterunt.
      EN: For this reason even the Tyrians were indignant and showed the greatest generosity toward their burial.

4:50  LA: Menelaus autem, propter eorum qui in potentia erant avaritiam, permanebat in potestate, crescens in malitia ad insidias civium.
      EN: But Menelaus, because of the greed of those in power, remained in authority, growing in wickedness as he plotted against the citizens.

2 MACCABEES 5

5:1  LA: Eodem tempore, Antiochus secundam profectionem paravit in Ægyptum.
     EN: At the same time, Antiochus prepared a second expedition into Egypt.

5:2  LA: Contigit autem per universam Jerosolymorum civitatem videri diebus quadraginta per aëra equites discurrentes, auratas stolas habentes et hastis, quasi cohortes armatos :
     EN: For forty days, throughout the whole city of Jerusalem, riders were seen rushing through the air, wearing robes adorned with gold and armed with spears like companies of soldiers.

5:3  LA: et cursus equorum per ordines digestos, et congressiones fieri cominus, et scutorum motus, et galeatorum multitudinem gladiis districtis, et telorum jactus, et aureorum armorum splendorem, omnisque generis loricarum.
     EN: There were charges of horses arranged in ranks, close encounters, movements of shields, a multitude wearing helmets with swords drawn, the casting of missiles, and the splendor of gold armor and breastplates of every kind.

5:4  LA: Quapropter omnes rogabant in bonum monstra converti.
     EN: Therefore everyone prayed that the portents might turn out for good.

5:5  LA: Sed cum falsus rumor exisset, tamquam vita excessisset Antiochus, assumptis Jason non minus mille viris, repente agressus est civitatem : et civibus ad murum convolantibus ad ultimum apprehensa civitate, Menelaus fugit in arcem :
     EN: When a false report spread that Antiochus had died, Jason took no fewer than a thousand men and suddenly attacked the city. As the citizens rushed to the wall, the city was finally taken, and Menelaus fled into the citadel.

5:6  LA: Jason vero non parcebat in cæde civibus suis, nec cogitabat prosperitatem adversum cognatos malum esse maximum, arbitrans hostium et non civium se trophæa capturum.
     EN: Jason showed no restraint in slaughtering his fellow citizens. He did not consider that success against his own kin was the greatest evil, imagining that he would take trophies from enemies, not from fellow citizens.

5:7  LA: Et principatum quidem non obtinuit, finem vero insidiarum suarum confusionem accepit, et profugus iterum abiit in Ammanitem.
     EN: Yet he did not obtain the office; instead he received disgrace as the outcome of his plots, and once more went away as a fugitive into Ammonite territory.

5:8  LA: Ad ultimum, in exitium sui conclusus ab Areta Arabum tyranno fugiens de civitate in civitatem, omnibus odiosus, ut refuga legum et execrabilis, ut patriæ et civium hostis, in Ægyptum extrusus est :
     EN: Finally, confined to his own ruin by Aretas, ruler of the Arabs, he fled from city to city. Hated by everyone as a deserter of the laws and abhorred as an enemy of his homeland and fellow citizens, he was driven into Egypt.

5:9  LA: et qui multos de patria sua expulerat, peregre periit, Lacedæmonas profectus, quasi pro cognatione ibi refugium habiturus :
     EN: He who had driven many from their homeland perished abroad, having gone to the Spartans as though he would find refuge there because of their kinship.

5:10  LA: et qui insepultos multos abjecerat, ipse et illamentatus et insepultus abjicitur, sepultura neque peregrina usus, neque patrio sepulchro participans.
      EN: He who had cast out many unburied was himself cast out unmourned and unburied, receiving neither burial in a foreign land nor a share in his ancestral tomb.

5:11  LA: His itaque gestis, suspicatus est rex societatem deserturos Judæos : et ob hoc profectus ex Ægypto efferatis animis, civitatem quidem armis cepit.
      EN: After these events, the king suspected that the Jews would abandon their alliance. For this reason he set out from Egypt with a savage spirit and took the city by force of arms.

5:12  LA: Jussit autem militibus interficere, nec parcere occursantibus, et per domos ascendentes trucidare.
      EN: He ordered the soldiers to kill without sparing those they met, and to climb into the houses and slaughter people.

5:13  LA: Fiebant ergo cædes juvenum ac seniorum, et mulierum et natorum exterminia, virginumque et parvulorum neces.
      EN: So there were slaughters of young and old, destruction of women and children, and killings of maidens and little ones.

5:14  LA: Erant autem toto triduo octoginta millia interfecti, quadraginta millia vincti, non minus autem venundati. · Translation note. The Latin specifies eighty thousand killed, forty thousand bound, and no fewer sold. These stated categories and quantities are preserved without reconciling them with another account.
      EN: Within three whole days, eighty thousand were killed, forty thousand were bound, and no fewer were sold.

5:15  LA: Sed nec ista sufficiunt : ausus est etiam intrare templum universa terra sanctius, Menelao ductore, qui legum et patriæ fuit proditor :
      EN: But these things were not enough: he even dared to enter the temple, holier than all the earth, with Menelaus as his guide, who had become a betrayer of the laws and his homeland.

5:16  LA: et scelestis manibus sumens sancta vasa, quæ ab aliis regibus et civitatibus erant posita ad ornatum loci, et gloriam, contrectabat indigne, et contaminabat.
      EN: Taking the sacred vessels in his criminal hands, he handled them unworthily and defiled them—the vessels that other kings and cities had placed there for the adornment and glory of the place.

5:17  LA: Ita alienatus mente Antiochus, non considerabat quod propter peccata habitantium civitatem, modicum Deus fuerat iratus : propter quod et accidit circa locum despectio :
      EN: So estranged in mind was Antiochus that he did not consider that God had been angry for a little while because of the sins of the city's inhabitants, and that for this reason the place had suffered contempt.

5:18  LA: alioquin nisi contigisset eos multis peccatis esse involutos, sicut Heliodorus, qui missus est a Seleuco rege ad expoliandum ærarium, etiam hic statim adveniens flagellatus, et repulsus utique fuisset ab audacia.
      EN: Otherwise, had they not been entangled in many sins, he too would certainly have been scourged immediately upon his arrival and driven back from his audacity, as happened to Heliodorus, who was sent by King Seleucus to plunder the treasury.

5:19  LA: Verum non propter locum, gentem : sed propter gentem, locum Deus elegit.
      EN: But God did not choose the nation for the sake of the place; He chose the place for the sake of the nation.

5:20  LA: Ideoque et ipse locus particeps factus est populi malorum : postea autem fiet socius bonorum, et qui derelictus in ira Dei omnipotentis est, iterum in magni Domini reconciliatione cum summa gloria exaltabitur.
      EN: Therefore the place itself also shared in the people's misfortunes. Afterward it will share in their good things, and the place abandoned in the wrath of almighty God will again be exalted with the greatest glory when the great Lord is reconciled.

5:21  LA: Igitur Antiochus mille et octingentis ablatis de templo talentis, velociter Antiochiam regressus est, existimans se præ superbia terram ad navigandum, pelagus vero ad iter agendum deducturum propter mentis elationem.
      EN: Antiochus therefore took eighteen hundred talents from the temple and quickly returned to Antioch, imagining in his arrogance that he could make the land navigable and the sea passable on foot, so exalted was his mind.

5:22  LA: Reliquit autem et præpositos ad affligendam gentem : Jerosolymis quidem Philippum genere Phrygem, moribus crudeliorem eo ipso a quo constitutus est :
      EN: He also left governors to oppress the nation: at Jerusalem, Philip, a Phrygian by birth, more cruel in character than the very man who appointed him;

5:23  LA: in Garizim autem Andronicum et Menelaum, qui gravius quam ceteri imminebant civibus.
      EN: and at Gerizim, Andronicus and Menelaus, who pressed more heavily upon the citizens than the others.

5:24  LA: Cumque appositus esset contra Judæos, misit odiosum principem Apollonium cum exercitu viginti et duobus millibus, præcipiens ei omnes perfectæ ætatis interficere, mulieres ac juvenes vendere.
      EN: Being set against the Jews, he sent the hateful commander Apollonius with an army of twenty-two thousand, ordering him to kill all those of mature age and sell the women and youths.

5:25  LA: Qui cum venisset Jerosolymam, pacem simulans, quievit usque ad diem sanctum sabbati : et tunc feriatis Judæis arma capere suis præcepit.
      EN: When Apollonius came to Jerusalem, he pretended peace and remained quiet until the holy Sabbath day. Then, while the Jews were resting, he ordered his men to take up arms.

5:26  LA: Omnesque qui ad spectaculum processerant, trucidavit : et civitatem cum armatis discurrens, ingentem multitudinem peremit.
      EN: He slaughtered everyone who had come out to watch, and running through the city with his armed men, killed an immense multitude.

5:27  LA: Judas autem Machabæus, qui decimus fuerat, secesserat in desertum locum, ibique inter feras vitam in montibus cum suis agebat : et fœni cibo vescentes, demorabantur, ne participes essent coinquinationis. · Translation note. Qui decimus fuerat is understood as “who was one of ten.” Fœni is rendered “grass” as food; no particular edible species is inferred.
      EN: But Judas Maccabeus, who was one of ten, had withdrawn to a deserted place. There he lived among the wild beasts in the mountains with his companions, and they remained there eating grass as food, so that they would not share in the defilement.

2 MACCABEES 6

6:1  LA: Sed non post multum temporis, misit rex senem quemdam Antiochenum, qui compelleret Judæos ut se transferrent a patriis et Dei legibus : · Translation note. Antiochenum is rendered “from Antioch,” preserving the source’s place designation.
     EN: Not long afterward, the king sent a certain old man from Antioch to compel the Jews to turn away from the laws of their fathers and of God,

6:2  LA: contaminare etiam quod in Jerosolymis erat templum, et cognominare Jovis Olympii : et in Garizim, prout erant hi qui locum inhabitabant, Jovis hospitalis.
     EN: and also to defile the temple in Jerusalem and name it after Olympian Jupiter, and the temple on Gerizim, in keeping with those who inhabited the place, after Jupiter the Hospitable.

6:3  LA: Pessima autem et universis gravis erat malorum incursio :
     EN: The onslaught of evils was most grievous and burdensome to everyone.

6:4  LA: nam templum luxuria et comessationibus gentium erat plenum, et scortantium cum meretricibus : sacratisque ædibus mulieres se ultro ingerebant, intro ferentes ea quæ non licebat.
     EN: For the temple was full of the debauchery and revelry of the nations, and of people engaging in sexual immorality with prostitutes. Women thrust themselves unbidden into the sacred buildings, bringing inside things that were unlawful.

6:5  LA: Altare etiam plenum erat illicitis, quæ legibus prohibebantur.
     EN: The altar too was full of unlawful things forbidden by the laws.

6:6  LA: Neque autem sabbata custodiebantur, neque dies solemnes patrii servabantur, nec simpliciter Judæum se esse quisquam confitebatur.
     EN: Neither were the Sabbaths kept nor the ancestral feast days observed, nor did anyone openly confess himself to be a Jew.

6:7  LA: Ducebantur autem cum amara necessitate in die natalis regis ad sacrificia : et cum Liberi sacra celebrarentur, cogebantur hedera coronati Libero circuire. · Translation note. Liber is the Latin divine name in the source, associated with Bacchus/Dionysus. It is retained here and at 14:33 rather than silently replaced by a Greek name.
     EN: On the king's birthday they were led under bitter compulsion to the sacrifices, and when the rites of Liber were celebrated, they were forced to march around in honor of Liber, crowned with ivy.

6:8  LA: Decretum autem exiit in proximas gentilium civitates, suggerentibus Ptolemæis, ut pari modo et ipsi adversus Judæos agerent, ut sacrificarent : · Translation note. The accessed text reads Ptolemæis, a plural form, translated “the Ptolemies.” The precise persons intended remain unresolved.
     EN: At the suggestion of the Ptolemies, a decree went out to the neighboring Gentile cities that they too should act in the same way against the Jews and make them sacrifice,

6:9  LA: eos autem qui nollent transire ad instituta gentium, interficerent : erat ergo videre miseriam.
     EN: and should kill those unwilling to adopt the institutions of the nations. Misery was therefore plain to see.

6:10  LA: Duæ enim mulieres delatæ sunt natos suos circumcidisse : quas, infantibus ad ubera suspensis, cum publice per civitatem circumduxissent, per muros præcipitaverunt.
      EN: For two women were reported to have circumcised their sons. With their infants suspended at their breasts, they were publicly led around the city and thrown headlong from the walls.

6:11  LA: Alii vero, ad proximas coëuntes speluncas, et latenter sabbati diem celebrantes, cum indicati essent Philippo, flammis succensi sunt, eo quod verebantur propter religionem et observantiam manu sibimet auxilium ferre.
      EN: Others had gathered in nearby caves and were secretly celebrating the Sabbath day. When they were reported to Philip, they were burned with fire, because religious devotion and observance made them afraid to defend themselves by force.

6:12  LA: Obsecro autem eos qui hunc librum lecturi sunt, ne abhorrescant propter adversos casus : sed reputent ea quæ acciderunt, non ad interitum, sed ad correptionem esse generis nostri.
      EN: I beg those who will read this book not to recoil because of these adverse events, but to consider that what happened was for the correction of our people, not their destruction.

6:13  LA: Etenim multo tempore non sinere peccatoribus ex sententia agere, sed statim ultiones adhibere, magni beneficii est indicium.
      EN: For not allowing sinners to act as they please for a long time, but applying punishments immediately, is a sign of great kindness.

6:14  LA: Non enim, sicut in aliis nationibus, Dominus patienter exspectat, ut eas cum judicii dies advenerit, in plenitudine peccatorum puniat : · Translation note. Verses 14–15 form one contrast. English makes their connected syntax explicit: God’s treatment of Israel is distinguished from the delayed punishment of other nations.
      EN: For our case is not like that of other nations, for whom the Lord waits patiently to punish them in the fullness of their sins when the day of judgment arrives;

6:15  LA: ita et in nobis statuit ut, peccatis nostris in finem devolutis, ita demum in nos vindicet.
      EN: He has not determined to take vengeance on us only when our sins have reached their end.

6:16  LA: Propter quod numquam quidem a nobis misericordiam suam amovet : corripiens vero in adversis, populum suum non dereliquit.
      EN: For this reason He never removes His mercy from us. Though He corrects us through adversity, He has not abandoned His people.

6:17  LA: Sed hæc nobis ad commonitionem legentium dicta sint paucis. Jam enim veniendum est ad narrationem.
      EN: Let these few things have been said by us as a reminder to our readers. Now we must come to the narrative.

6:18  LA: Igitur Eleazarus, unus de primoribus scribarum, vir ætate provectus, et vultu decorus, aperto ore hians compellebatur carnem porcinam manducare.
      EN: Eleazar, one of the foremost scribes, a man advanced in age and handsome in appearance, was being forced to eat pork with his mouth pried open.

6:19  LA: At ille gloriosissimam mortem magis quam odibilem vitam complectens, voluntarie præibat ad supplicium.
      EN: But embracing a most glorious death rather than a hateful life, he went forward willingly to the place of torment.

6:20  LA: Intuens autem quemadmodum oporteret accedere, patienter sustinens, destinavit non admittere illicita propter vitæ amorem.
      EN: Considering how he ought to approach it, and patiently enduring, he resolved not to admit anything unlawful for love of life.

6:21  LA: Hi autem qui astabant, iniqua miseratione commoti propter antiquam viri amicitiam, tollentes eum secreto rogabant afferri carnes quibus vesci ei licebat, ut simularetur manducasse sicut rex imperaverat de sacrificii carnibus,
      EN: Those standing nearby, moved by an unjust pity because of their long friendship with the man, took him aside and urged that meat lawful for him to eat be brought, so that he might seem to have eaten the sacrificial meat as the king had commanded.

6:22  LA: ut hoc facto, a morte liberaretur : et propter veterem viri amicitiam, hanc in eo faciebant humanitatem.
      EN: By doing this he would be delivered from death. Because of their long friendship with the man, they showed him this kindness.

6:23  LA: At ille cogitare cœpit ætatis ac senectutis suæ eminentiam dignam, et ingenitæ nobilitatis canitiem, atque a puero optimæ conversationis actus : et secundum sanctæ et a Deo conditæ legis constituta, respondit cito, dicens præmitti se velle in infernum. · Translation note. Infernum is rendered “the realm of the dead”; the term is not made to specify final punishment in this context.
      EN: But he began to consider the dignity befitting his age and old age, the white hair of his inborn nobility, and the deeds of his excellent way of life from childhood. In accordance with the ordinances of the holy law established by God, he quickly answered, saying that he wished to be sent ahead to the realm of the dead.

6:24  LA: Non enim ætati nostræ dignum est, inquit, fingere : ut multi adolescentium, arbitrantes Eleazarum nonaginta annorum transisse ad vitam alienigenarum,
      EN: “For it is not worthy of our age,” he said, “to pretend, so that many young people, supposing that Eleazar at ninety years had turned to a foreign way of life,

6:25  LA: et ipsi propter meam simulationem, et propter modicum corruptibilis vitæ tempus decipiantur, et per hoc maculam atque execrationem meæ senectuti conquiram.
      EN: might themselves be deceived through my pretense, for the sake of a little time of perishable life, and I might thereby bring stain and a curse upon my old age.

6:26  LA: Nam etsi in præsenti tempore suppliciis hominum eripiar, sed manum Omnipotentis nec vivus, nec defunctus, effugiam.
      EN: For even if in the present time I escape the torments of human beings, neither living nor dead will I escape the hand of the Almighty.

6:27  LA: Quam ob rem fortiter vita excedendo, senectute quidem dignus apparebo :
      EN: Therefore, by departing this life bravely, I shall show myself worthy of my old age,

6:28  LA: adolescentibus autem exemplum forte relinquam, si prompto animo ac fortiter pro gravissimis ac sanctissimis legibus honesta morte perfungar. His dictis, confestim ad supplicium trahebatur.
      EN: and leave the young a courageous example, if with willing spirit and courage I undergo an honorable death for the most venerable and holy laws.” After saying this, he was immediately dragged to the torment.

6:29  LA: Hi autem qui eum ducebant, et paulo ante fuerant mitiores, in iram conversi sunt propter sermones ab eo dictos, quos illi per arrogantiam prolatos arbitrabantur.
      EN: Those leading him, who a little before had been gentler, turned to anger because of the words he had spoken, which they thought had been uttered in arrogance.

6:30  LA: Sed cum plagis perimeretur, ingemuit, et dixit : Domine, qui habes sanctam scientiam, manifeste tu scis quia cum a morte possem liberari, duros corporis sustineo dolores : secundum animam vero propter timorem tuum libenter hæc patior.
      EN: As he was being beaten to death, he groaned and said, “Lord, who possess holy knowledge, You clearly know that although I could have been delivered from death, I endure harsh bodily pains; yet in my soul I suffer these things gladly because I fear You.”

6:31  LA: Et iste quidem hoc modo vita decessit, non solum juvenibus, sed et universæ genti memoriam mortis suæ ad exemplum virtutis et fortitudinis derelinquens.
      EN: In this way he departed this life, leaving the memory of his death as an example of virtue and courage not only to the young but to the whole nation.

2 MACCABEES 7

7:1  LA: Contigit autem et septem fratres una cum matre sua apprehensos compelli a rege edere contra fas carnes porcinas, flagris et taureis cruciatos.
     EN: It also happened that seven brothers were arrested with their mother and compelled by the king to eat pork contrary to divine law, while being tortured with whips and ox-hide thongs.

7:2  LA: Unus autem ex illis, qui erat primus, sic ait : Quid quæris, et quid vis discere a nobis ? parati sumus mori, magis quam patrias Dei leges prævaricari.
     EN: One of them, who was first, said, “What do you seek, and what do you wish to learn from us? We are ready to die rather than transgress the ancestral laws of God.”

7:3  LA: Iratus itaque rex, jussit sartagines et ollas æneas succendi : quibus statim succensis,
     EN: The king became angry and ordered frying pans and bronze cauldrons to be heated. When they had immediately been heated,

7:4  LA: jussit ei qui prior fuerat locutus amputari linguam, et cute capitis abstracta, summas quoque manus et pedes ei præscindi, ceteris ejus fratribus et matre inspicientibus.
     EN: he ordered the tongue of the one who had spoken first to be cut out, the skin of his head to be stripped away, and the extremities of his hands and feet to be cut off, while his other brothers and his mother watched.

7:5  LA: Et cum jam per omnia inutilis factus esset, jussit ignem admoveri, et adhuc spirantem torreri in sartagine : in qua cum diu cruciaretur, ceteri una cum matre invicem se hortabantur mori fortiter,
     EN: When he had been made helpless in every respect, the king ordered him to be brought to the fire and roasted in the pan while still breathing. While he was tormented there for a long time, the others and their mother encouraged one another to die bravely,

7:6  LA: dicentes : Dominus Deus aspiciet veritatem, et consolabitur in nobis, quemadmodum in protestatione cantici declaravit Moyses : Et in servis suis consolabitur. · Translation note. Consolabitur is rendered with its consolation imagery, “will take comfort.” The biblical allusion does not justify replacing the accessed Latin with the wording of another passage or tradition.
     EN: saying, “The Lord God will look upon the truth and take comfort in us, as Moses declared in the testimony of his song: ‘And He will take comfort in His servants.’”

7:7  LA: Mortuo itaque illo primo hoc modo, sequentem deducebant ad illudendum : et cute capitis ejus cum capillis abstracta, interrogabant si manducaret, priusquam toto corpore per membra singula puniretur.
     EN: After the first died in this manner, they brought forward the next to abuse him. Stripping the skin from his head along with his hair, they asked whether he would eat before his whole body was punished, limb by limb.

7:8  LA: At ille respondens patria voce, dixit : Non faciam. Propter quod et iste, sequenti loco, primi tormenta suscepit :
     EN: But he answered in his native language, “I will not do it.” Therefore he too, next in order, underwent the first one's torments.

7:9  LA: et in ultimo spiritu constitutus, sic ait : Tu quidem scelestissime in præsenti vita nos perdis : sed Rex mundi defunctos nos pro suis legibus in æternæ vitæ resurrectione suscitabit.
     EN: At his last breath, he said, “You, most wicked man, destroy us in the present life; but the King of the world will raise us, who die for His laws, in the resurrection of eternal life.”

7:10  LA: Post hunc tertius illuditur, et linguam postulatus cito protulit, et manus constanter extendit :
      EN: After him the third was abused. When his tongue was demanded, he quickly put it out and firmly extended his hands.

7:11  LA: et cum fiducia ait : E cælo ista possideo, sed propter Dei leges nunc hæc ipsa despicio, quoniam ab ipso me ea recepturum spero :
      EN: He said with confidence, “I possess these from heaven, but for God's laws I now regard these very things as nothing, because I hope to receive them back from Him.”

7:12  LA: ita ut rex, et qui cum ipso erant, mirarentur adolescentis animum, quod tamquam nihilum duceret cruciatus.
      EN: Thus the king and those with him marveled at the young man's spirit, because he counted the torments as nothing.

7:13  LA: Et hoc ita defuncto, quartum vexabant similiter torquentes.
      EN: When he had died in this way, they afflicted the fourth with similar tortures.

7:14  LA: Et cum jam esset ad mortem, sic ait : Potius est ab hominibus morti datos spem exspectare a Deo, iterum ab ipso resuscitandos : tibi enim resurrectio ad vitam non erit.
      EN: When he was near death, he said, “It is better, when put to death by human beings, to await hope from God that we will be raised again by Him. But for you there will be no resurrection to life.”

7:15  LA: Et cum admovissent quintum, vexabant eum. At ille respiciens in eum,
      EN: When they brought forward the fifth, they afflicted him. Looking at the king,

7:16  LA: dixit : Potestatem inter homines habens, cum sis corruptibilis, facis quod vis : noli autem putare genus nostrum a Deo esse derelictum :
      EN: he said, “Having authority among human beings, though you are perishable, you do what you wish. But do not think our people have been abandoned by God.

7:17  LA: tu autem patienter sustine, et videbis magnam potestatem ipsius, qualiter te et semen tuum torquebit.
      EN: Wait patiently, and you will see His great power, how He will torment you and your offspring.”

7:18  LA: Post hunc ducebant sextum, et is, mori incipiens, sic ait : Noli frustra errare : nos enim propter nosmetipsos hæc patimur, peccantes in Deum nostrum, et digna admiratione facta sunt in nobis :
      EN: After him they brought the sixth, and as he began to die he said, “Do not deceive yourself in vain. For we suffer these things because of ourselves, having sinned against our God, and astonishing things have happened among us.

7:19  LA: tu autem ne existimes tibi impune futurum, quod contra Deum pugnare tentaveris.
      EN: But do not suppose you will go unpunished for attempting to fight against God.”

7:20  LA: Supra modum autem mater mirabilis, et bonorum memoria digna, quæ pereuntes septem filios sub unius diei tempore conspiciens, bono animo ferebat propter spem quam in Deum habebat :
      EN: The mother was marvelous beyond measure and worthy to be remembered by the good. Seeing her seven sons perish within a single day, she bore it with good courage because of the hope she had in God.

7:21  LA: singulos illorum hortabatur voce patria fortiter, repleta sapientia : et, femineæ cogitationi masculinum animum inserens, · Translation note. The contrast between a woman’s thought and a man’s courage follows the Latin’s gendered rhetoric; it is not recast as an editorial claim about women or men generally.
      EN: Filled with wisdom, she bravely encouraged each of them in their native language, joining a man's courage to a woman's thought.

7:22  LA: dixit ad eos : Nescio qualiter in utero meo apparuistis, neque enim ego spiritum et animam donavi vobis et vitam, et singulorum membra non ego ipsa compegi :
      EN: She said to them, “I do not know how you appeared in my womb. For I did not give you spirit and soul and life, nor did I myself arrange the limbs of each of you.

7:23  LA: sed enim mundi Creator, qui formavit hominis nativitatem, quique omnium invenit originem, et spiritum vobis iterum cum misericordia reddet et vitam, sicut nunc vosmetipsos despicitis propter leges ejus.
      EN: But the Creator of the world, who formed the birth of humankind and devised the origin of all things, will mercifully restore spirit and life to you, just as you now count yourselves as nothing for His laws.”

7:24  LA: Antiochus autem, contemni se arbitratus, simul et exprobrantis voce despecta, cum adhuc adolescentior superesset, non solum verbis hortabatur, sed et cum juramento affirmabat se divitem et beatum facturum, et translatum a patriis legibus amicum habiturum, et res necessarias ei præbiturum.
      EN: Antiochus, thinking himself treated with contempt and despising her reproachful voice, began to encourage the youngest, who still remained. He not only spoke words but also affirmed with an oath that he would make him rich and happy, receive him as a friend if he turned from his ancestral laws, and provide him with what he needed.

7:25  LA: Sed ad hæc cum adolescens nequaquam inclinaretur, vocavit rex matrem, et suadebat ei ut adolescenti fieret in salutem.
      EN: When the young man was in no way inclined to these things, the king called his mother and urged her to act for the young man's preservation.

7:26  LA: Cum autem multis eam verbis esset hortatus, promisit suasurum se filio suo. · Translation note. Promisit suasurum has irregular agreement in the transcription. The mother is the contextual subject, and the English follows that subject without changing the reproduced Latin.
      EN: After he had encouraged her with many words, she promised to persuade her son.

7:27  LA: Itaque inclinata ad illum, irridens crudelem tyrannum, ait patria voce : Fili mi, miserere mei, quæ te in utero novem mensibus portavi, et lac triennio dedi et alui, et in ætatem istam perduxi.
      EN: Bending toward him and mocking the cruel tyrant, she said in their native language, “My son, have pity on me, who carried you in my womb for nine months, gave you milk for three years, nourished you, and brought you to this age.

7:28  LA: Peto, nate, ut aspicias ad cælum et terram, et ad omnia quæ in eis sunt, et intelligas quia ex nihilo fecit illa Deus, et hominum genus : · Translation note. Ex nihilo is explicit: “out of nothing.” This translation preserves the Latin assertion; discussion of its theological reception belongs outside the translation.
      EN: I ask you, my child, to look at heaven and earth and all that is in them, and understand that God made them out of nothing, and the human race as well.

7:29  LA: ita fiet, ut non timeas carnificem istum, sed dignus fratribus tuis effectus particeps, suscipe mortem, ut in illa miseratione cum fratribus tuis te recipiam.
      EN: In this way you will not fear this executioner. Become a worthy companion of your brothers and accept death, so that in that mercy I may receive you back with your brothers.”

7:30  LA: Cum hæc illa adhuc diceret, ait adolescens : Quem sustinetis ? non obedio præcepto regis, sed præcepto legis, quæ data est nobis per Moysen.
      EN: While she was still saying these things, the young man said, “For whom are you waiting? I do not obey the king's command, but the command of the law that was given to us through Moses.

7:31  LA: Tu vero, qui inventor omnis malitiæ factus es in Hebræos, non effugies manum Dei.
      EN: But you, who have become the inventor of every evil against the Hebrews, will not escape the hand of God.

7:32  LA: Nos enim pro peccatis nostris hæc patimur.
      EN: For we suffer these things for our sins.

7:33  LA: Et si nobis propter increpationem et correptionem Dominus Deus noster modicum iratus est : sed iterum reconciliabitur servis suis.
      EN: And if the Lord our God has been angry with us for a little while for rebuke and correction, yet He will be reconciled again to His servants.

7:34  LA: Tu autem, o sceleste, et omnium hominum flagitiosissime, noli frustra extolli vanis spebus in servos ejus inflammatus :
      EN: But you, wicked man and most shameful of all human beings, do not exalt yourself in vain with empty hopes, inflamed against His servants.

7:35  LA: nondum enim omnipotentis Dei, et omnia inspicientis, judicium effugisti.
      EN: For you have not yet escaped the judgment of almighty God, who sees all things.

7:36  LA: Nam fratres mei, modico nunc dolore sustentato, sub testamento æternæ vitæ effecti sunt : tu vero judicio Dei justas superbiæ tuæ pœnas exsolves.
      EN: For my brothers, having now endured a little pain, have come under the covenant of eternal life; but by God's judgment you will pay the just penalties for your arrogance.

     EN: Carried away with anger, he thought he could turn against the Jews the injury done by those who had put him to flight. 9:11  LA: Hinc igitur cœpit ex gravi superbia deductus ad agnitionem sui venire, divina admonitus plaga, per momenta singula doloribus suis augmenta capientibus.

9:12  LA: Et cum nec ipse jam fœtorem suum ferre posset, ita ait : Justum est subditum esse Deo, et mortalem non paria Deo sentire.

       EN: The Jews who held suitable fortifications received those driven from Jerusalem and attempted to wage war.

10:16  LA: Hi vero qui erant cum Machabæo, per orationes Dominum rogantes ut esset sibi adjutor, impetum fecerunt in munitiones Idumæorum :

10:17  LA: multaque vi insistentes, loca obtinuerunt, occurrentes interemerunt, et omnes simul non minus viginti millibus trucidaverunt.

10:18  LA: Quidam autem cum confugissent in duas turres valde munitas, omnem apparatum ad repugnandum habentes,

       EN: But the men with Simon, led by greed, were persuaded with money by some of those in the towers. Taking seventy thousand didrachms, they let some escape.

10:21  LA: Cum autem Machabæo nuntiatum esset quod factum est, principibus populi congregatis accusavit quod pecunia fratres vendidissent, adversariis eorum dimissis.

10:23  LA: Armis autem ac manibus omnia prospere agendo in duabus munitionibus plus quam viginti millia peremit.

10:24  LA: At Timotheus, qui prius a Judæis fuerat superatus, convocato exercitu peregrinæ multitudinis, et congregato equitatu Asiano, advenit quasi armis Judæam capturus.

10:25  LA: Machabæus autem et qui cum ipso erant, appropinquante illo, deprecabantur Dominum, caput terra aspergentes, lumbosque ciliciis præcincti,

10:27  LA: Et ita post orationem, sumptis armis, longius de civitate procedentes, et proximi hostibus effecti, resederunt.

10:28  LA: Primo autem solis ortu utrique commiserunt : isti quidem victoriæ et prosperitatis sponsorem cum virtute Dominum habentes : illi autem ducem belli animum habebant.

10:29  LA: Sed cum vehemens pugna esset, apparuerunt adversariis de cælo viri quinque in equis, frenis aureis decori, ducatum Judæis præstantes :

10:31  LA: Interfecti sunt autem viginti millia quingenti, et equites sexcenti. · Translation note. The Latin lists twenty thousand five hundred and six hundred horsemen. The English does not silently combine the figures or reassign the first group to a more specific category.
       EN: Twenty thousand five hundred were killed, and six hundred horsemen.

10:32  LA: Timotheus vero confugit in Gazaram præsidium munitum, cui præerat Chæreas.
       EN: Timothy fled into Gazara, a fortified stronghold commanded by Chaereas.

10:33  LA: Machabæus autem et qui cum eo erant, lætantes obsederunt præsidium diebus quatuor.
       EN: Maccabeus and those with him joyfully besieged the stronghold for four days.

10:34  LA: At hi qui intus erant, loci firmitate confisi, supra modum maledicebant, et sermones nefandos jactabant.

10:35  LA: Sed cum dies quinta illucesceret, viginti juvenes ex his qui cum Machabæo erant, accensi animis propter blasphemiam, viriliter accesserunt ad murum, et feroci animo incedentes ascendebant :
       EN: When the fifth day dawned, twenty young men from those with Maccabeus, inflamed in spirit by the blasphemy, bravely approached the wall and began climbing with fierce resolve.

10:36  LA: sed et alii similiter ascendentes, turres portasque succendere aggressi sunt, atque ipsos maledicos vivos concremare.
       EN: Others likewise climbed up and set about burning the towers and gates, and burning those very revilers alive.

10:37  LA: Per continuum autem biduum præsidio vastato, Timotheum occultantem se in quodam repertum loco peremerunt : et fratrem illius Chæream et Apollophanem occiderunt.
       EN: After devastating the stronghold for two successive days, they found Timothy hiding in a certain place and killed him. They also killed his brother Chaereas and Apollophanes.

10:38  LA: Quibus gestis, in hymnis et confessionibus benedicebant Dominum, qui magna fecit in Israël, et victoriam dedit illis.
       EN: When these things were done, they blessed the Lord with hymns and praises, for He had done great things in Israel and given them victory.

2 MACCABEES 11

11:1  LA: Sed parvo post tempore, Lysias procurator regis et propinquus, ac negotiorum præpositus, graviter ferens de his quæ acciderant,
      EN: A little while afterward, Lysias, the king's administrator and kinsman, who was in charge of his affairs, took what had happened very badly.

11:2  LA: congregatis octoginta millibus, et equitatu universo, veniebat adversus Judæos, existimans se civitatem quidem captam gentibus habitaculum facturum,
      EN: Gathering eighty thousand men and all the cavalry, he came against the Jews, thinking that he would capture the city and make it a dwelling for the nations,

11:3  LA: templum vero in pecuniæ quæstum, sicut cetera delubra gentium, habiturum, et per singulos annos venale sacerdotium :
      EN: turn the temple into a source of revenue like the other shrines of the nations, and put the priesthood up for sale every year.

11:4  LA: nusquam recogitans Dei potestatem, sed mente effrenatus in multitudine peditum, et in millibus equitum, et in octoginta elephantis confidebat.
      EN: He gave no thought to God's power, but, unrestrained in mind, trusted in the multitude of infantry, the thousands of cavalry, and the eighty elephants.

11:5  LA: Ingressus autem Judæam, et appropians Bethsuræ, quæ erat in angusto loco, ab Jerosolyma intervallo quinque stadiorum, illud præsidium expugnabat. · Translation note. The source gives five stadia from Jerusalem and describes a narrow place. These details are retained without correcting the geography.
      EN: He entered Judea and approached Beth-zur, which was in a narrow place five stadia from Jerusalem, and attacked that stronghold.

11:6  LA: Ut autem Machabæus et qui cum eo erant cognoverunt expugnari præsidia, cum fletu et lacrimis rogabant Dominum, et omnis turba simul, ut bonum angelum mitteret ad salutem Israël.
      EN: When Maccabeus and those with him learned that the strongholds were being attacked, they and the entire crowd together begged the Lord with weeping and tears to send a good angel for Israel's deliverance.

11:7  LA: Et ipse primus Machabæus, sumptis armis, ceteros adhortatus est simul secum periculum subire, et ferre auxilium fratribus suis.
      EN: Maccabeus himself was the first to take up arms and encouraged the others to face the danger together with him and bring help to their brothers.

11:8  LA: Cumque pariter prompto animo procederent, Jerosolymis apparuit præcedens eos eques in veste candida, armis aureis hastam vibrans.
      EN: As they advanced together with eager spirit, a horseman appeared at Jerusalem, going before them in white clothing and golden armor, brandishing a spear.

11:9  LA: Tunc omnes simul benedixerunt misericordem Dominum, et convaluerunt animis : non solum homines, sed et bestias ferocissimas, et muros ferreos parati penetrare.
      EN: Then all together they blessed the merciful Lord and grew strong in spirit, ready to break through not only human beings, but even the fiercest beasts and walls of iron.

11:10  LA: Ibant igitur prompti, de cælo habentes adjutorem et miserantem super eos Dominum.
       EN: They therefore advanced eagerly, having a helper from heaven and the Lord showing mercy upon them.

11:11  LA: Leonum autem more impetu irruentes in hostes, prostraverunt ex eis undecim millia peditum, et equitum mille sexcentos :
       EN: Rushing upon the enemies like lions, they struck down eleven thousand of their infantry and sixteen hundred horsemen.

11:12  LA: universos autem in fugam verterunt, plures autem ex eis vulnerati nudi evaserunt. Sed et ipse Lysias turpiter fugiens evasit.
       EN: They put all the rest to flight, and many of them escaped wounded and stripped. Lysias himself also escaped in shameful flight.

11:13  LA: Et quia non insensatus erat, secum ipse reputans factam erga se diminutionem, et intelligens invictos esse Hebræos, omnipotentis Dei auxilio innitentes, misit ad eos :
       EN: Since he was not without understanding, he reflected on the loss he had suffered and recognized that the Hebrews were invincible while relying on almighty God's help. He sent to them

11:14  LA: promisitque se consensurum omnibus quæ justa sunt, et regem compulsurum amicum fieri.
       EN: and promised to agree to everything that was just and to compel the king to become their friend.

11:15  LA: Annuit autem Machabæus precibus Lysiæ, in omnibus utilitati consulens : et quæcumque Machabæus scripsit Lysiæ de Judæis, ea rex concessit.
       EN: Maccabeus assented to Lysias's requests, considering the common benefit in everything. Whatever Maccabeus wrote to Lysias concerning the Jews, the king granted.

11:16  LA: Nam erant scriptæ Judæis epistolæ a Lysia quidem hunc modum continentes : Lysias populo Judæorum salutem.
       EN: Letters were written to the Jews; the one from Lysias contained the following: “Lysias to the Jewish people, greetings.

11:17  LA: Joannes et Abesalom, qui missi fuerant a vobis, tradentes scripta, postulabant ut ea quæ per illos significabantur, implerem.
       EN: John and Absalom, who were sent by you, delivered your writings and requested that I carry out the things made known through them.

11:18  LA: Quæcumque igitur regi potuerunt perferri, exposui : et quæ res permittebat, concessit.
       EN: I therefore presented whatever could be brought before the king, and he granted what circumstances permitted.

11:19  LA: Si igitur in negotiis fidem conservaveritis, et deinceps bonorum vobis causa esset, tentabo. · Translation note. The transcription has esset where the syntax is irregular.        EN: If, then, you remain faithful in these affairs, I will endeavor to be a cause of good for you in the future as well.

11:20  LA: De ceteris autem per singula verbo mandavi et istis, et his, qui a me missi sunt, colloqui vobiscum.
       EN: Concerning the remaining details, I have instructed these men and those sent by me to speak with you in person.

11:21  LA: Bene valete. Anno centesimo, quadragesimo octavo mensis Dioscori, die vigesima et quarta. · Translation note. Dioscori is retained in English as Dioscorus, the source’s month name. No modern-calendar conversion is attempted.

11:22  LA: Regis autem epistola ista continebat : Rex Antiochus Lysiæ fratri salutem.

11:23  LA: Patre nostro inter deos translato, nos volentes eos qui sunt in regno nostro sine tumultu agere, et rebus suis adhibere diligentiam,

       EN: We have heard that the Jews did not consent to my father that they should turn to the Greek way of life, but wish to maintain their own institutions, and therefore ask us to allow them their own laws.

11:25  LA: Volentes igitur hanc quoque gentem quietam esse, statuentes judicavimus templum restitui illis, ut agerent secundum suorum majorum consuetudinem.
       EN: Wishing this nation also to be at peace, we have therefore determined and judged that their temple should be restored to them, so that they may live according to the custom of their ancestors.

11:26  LA: Bene igitur feceris, si miseris ad eos et dexteram dederis : ut cognita nostra voluntate, bono animo sint, et utilitatibus propriis deserviant.

11:27  LA: Ad Judæos vero regis epistola talis erat : Rex Antiochus senatui Judæorum, et ceteris Judæis salutem.
       EN: The king's letter to the Jews was as follows: “King Antiochus to the council of the Jews and the rest of the Jews, greetings.

11:28  LA: Si valetis, sic estis ut volumus : sed et ipsi bene valemus.

11:29  LA: Adiit nos Menelaus, dicens velle vos descendere ad vestros, qui sunt apud nos.
       EN: Menelaus came to us, saying that you wish to come down to your people who are with us.

11:30  LA: His igitur qui commeant usque ad diem trigesimum mensis Xanthici, damus dextras securitatis,

11:31  LA: ut Judæi utantur cibis et legibus suis, sicut et prius : et nemo eorum ullo modo molestiam patiatur de his quæ per ignorantiam gesta sunt.

11:32  LA: Misimus autem et Menelaum, qui vos alloquatur.
       EN: We have also sent Menelaus to speak with you.

11:33  LA: Valete. Anno centesimo quadragesimo octavo, Xanthici mensis quintadecima die.
       EN: Farewell. In the one hundred forty-eighth year, on the fifteenth day of the month of Xanthicus.”

       EN: The Romans also sent a letter, which read as follows: “Quintus Memmius and Titus Manilius, envoys of the Romans, to the Jewish people, greetings.

11:35  LA: De his quæ Lysias cognatus regis concessit vobis, et nos concessimus.
       EN: Whatever Lysias, the king's kinsman, granted you, we also have granted.

       EN: As for the matters he judged should be referred to the king, discuss them carefully among yourselves and immediately send someone, so that we may decide what is suitable for you; for we are on our way to Antioch.

11:37  LA: Ideoque festinate rescribere, ut nos quoque sciamus cujus estis voluntatis.
       EN: Therefore hasten to write back, so that we too may know your wishes.

11:38  LA: Bene valete. Anno centesimo quadragesimo octavo, quintadecima die mensis Xanthici.
       EN: Farewell. In the one hundred forty-eighth year, on the fifteenth day of the month of Xanthicus.”

2 MACCABEES 12

12:1  LA: His factis pactionibus, Lysias pergebat ad regem, Judæi autem agriculturæ operam dabant.
      EN: After these agreements had been made, Lysias went to the king, while the Jews applied themselves to agriculture.

12:2  LA: Sed hi qui resederant, Timotheus, et Apollonius Gennæi filius, sed et Hieronymus, et Demophon super hos, et Nicanor Cypriarches, non sinebant eos in silentio agere et quiete. · Translation note. Cypriarches is rendered “governor of Cyprus,” a functional title. No additional office or precise administrative chronology is inferred.
      EN: But those who remained—Timothy, Apollonius son of Gennaeus, and also Hieronymus, and beyond these Demophon and Nicanor, the governor of Cyprus—would not allow them to live in quiet and peace.

12:3  LA: Joppitæ vero tale quoddam flagitium perpetrarunt : rogaverunt Judæos cum quibus habitabant, ascendere scaphas quas paraverant, cum uxoribus et filiis, quasi nullis inimicitiis inter eos subjacentibus.
      EN: The people of Joppa committed an outrage of this kind: they invited the Jews who lived among them to board boats they had prepared, with their wives and children, as though no hostility lay between them.

12:4  LA: Secundum commune itaque decretum civitatis, et ipsis acquiescentibus, pacisque causa nihil suspectum habentibus : cum in altum processissent, submerserunt non minus ducentos.
      EN: In accordance with the city's common decree, when the Jews agreed, suspecting nothing because of the peace, they went out into deep water and drowned no fewer than two hundred of them.

12:5  LA: Quam crudelitatem Judas in suæ gentis homines factam ut cognovit, præcepit viris qui erant cum ipso : et invocato justo judice Deo,
      EN: When Judas learned of this cruelty committed against people of his own nation, he gave orders to the men with him and called upon God, the just Judge.

12:6  LA: venit adversus interfectores fratrum, et portum quidem noctu succendit, scaphas exussit, eos autem qui ab igne refugerant, gladio peremit.
      EN: He came against the murderers of his brothers, set the harbor on fire by night, burned the boats, and killed with the sword those who had fled from the fire.

12:7  LA: Et cum hæc ita egisset, discessit quasi iterum reversurus, et universos Joppitas eradicaturus.
      EN: After doing this, he departed, intending to return again and uproot all the people of Joppa.

12:8  LA: Sed cum cognovisset et eos qui erant Jamniæ, velle pari modo facere habitantibus secum Judæis,
      EN: When he learned that the people of Jamnia also intended to do the same to the Jews living among them,

12:9  LA: Jamnitis quoque nocte supervenit, et portum cum navibus succendit : ita ut lumen ignis appareret Jerosolymis a stadiis ducentis quadraginta.
      EN: he came upon the people of Jamnia by night as well and set fire to the harbor and ships, so that the light of the fire was visible in Jerusalem, two hundred forty stadia away.

12:10  LA: Inde cum jam abiissent novem stadiis, et iter facerent ad Timotheum, commiserunt cum eo Arabes quinque millia viri, et equites quingenti.
       EN: When they had gone nine stadia from there and were making their way toward Timothy, five thousand Arab men and five hundred horsemen engaged him in battle.

12:11  LA: Cumque pugna valida fieret, et auxilio Dei prospere cessisset, residui Arabes victi petebant a Juda dextram sibi dari, promittentes se pascua daturos, et in ceteris profuturos. · Translation note. Pascua means “pastures” or grazing grounds. That wording is retained rather than replaced with livestock.
       EN: The battle was fierce, but with God's help it turned out successfully. The surviving Arabs, defeated, asked Judas to give them his right hand, promising to provide pastures and to be useful in other matters.

12:12  LA: Judas autem arbitratus vere in multis eos utiles, promisit pacem : dextrisque acceptis, discessere ad tabernacula sua.
       EN: Judas, considering that they would indeed be useful in many ways, promised peace. After receiving pledges by the right hand, they departed to their tents.

12:13  LA: Aggressus est autem et civitatem quamdam firmam pontibus murisque circumseptam, quæ a turbis habitabatur gentium promiscuarum : cui nomen Casphin. · Translation note. Pontibus is rendered “bridges”; the translation does not substitute a conjectural geographic feature.
       EN: He also attacked a certain strong city enclosed by bridges and walls, inhabited by crowds of mixed nations; its name was Casphin.

12:14  LA: Hi vero qui intus erant, confidentes in stabilitate murorum et apparatu alimoniarum, remissius agebant, maledictis lacessentes Judam et blasphemantes, ac loquentes quæ fas non est.
       EN: Those inside, trusting in the strength of the walls and their store of provisions, acted carelessly, provoking Judas with curses and blasphemies, and saying things that were unlawful to utter.

12:15  LA: Machabæus autem, invocato magno mundi Principe, qui sine arietibus et machinis temporibus Jesu præcipitavit Jericho, irruit ferociter muris :
       EN: But Maccabeus called upon the great Ruler of the world, who in Joshua's time had overthrown Jericho without battering rams or siege engines, and fiercely attacked the walls.

12:16  LA: et capta civitate per Domini voluntatem, innumerabiles cædes fecit, ita ut adjacens stagnum stadiorum duorum latitudinis sanguine interfectorum fluere videretur.
       EN: Having captured the city by the Lord's will, he inflicted innumerable deaths, so that the nearby lake, two stadia wide, appeared to flow with the blood of the slain.

12:17  LA: Inde discesserunt stadia septingenta quinquaginta, et venerunt in Characa ad eos, qui dicuntur Tubianæi, Judæos :
       EN: From there they traveled seven hundred fifty stadia and came to Characa, to the Jews called Tubianites.

12:18  LA: et Timotheum quidem in illis locis non comprehenderunt, nulloque negotio perfecto regressus est, relicto in quodam loco firmissimo præsidio. · Translation note.        EN: They did not find Timothy in those places, for he had returned without accomplishing anything, leaving a garrison in a certain very strong position.

12:19  LA: Dositheus autem et Sosipater, qui erant duces cum Machabæo, peremerunt a Timotheo relictos in præsidio, decem millia viros.
       EN: Dositheus and Sosipater, who were commanders with Maccabeus, killed the men Timothy had left in the stronghold, ten thousand men.

12:20  LA: At Machabæus, ordinatis circum se sex millibus, et constitutis per cohortes, adversus Timotheum processit, habentem secum centum viginti millia peditum, equitumque duo millia quingentos. · Translation note. The Latin gives six thousand for Judas, one hundred twenty thousand infantry for Timothy, and twenty-five hundred cavalry; the figures are preserved.
       EN: Maccabeus arranged six thousand men around himself and organized them into companies. He advanced against Timothy, who had with him one hundred twenty thousand infantry and twenty-five hundred horsemen.

12:21  LA: Cognito autem Judæ adventu, Timotheus præmisit mulieres et filios, et reliquum apparatum, in præsidium quod Carnion dicitur : erat enim inexpugnabile, et accessu difficile propter locorum angustias.
       EN: Learning of Judas's approach, Timothy sent the women, children, and remaining equipment ahead to the stronghold called Carnion, for it was impregnable and difficult to approach because of the narrowness of the terrain.

12:22  LA: Cumque cohors Judæ prima apparuisset, timor hostibus incussus est ex præsentia Dei, qui universa conspicit : et in fugam versi sunt alius ab alio, ita ut magis a suis dejicerentur, et gladiorum suorum ictibus debilitarentur.
       EN: When Judas's first company appeared, fear struck the enemies through the presence of God, who sees all things. They fled from one another, so that they were more often struck down by their own men and disabled by blows from their own swords.

12:23  LA: Judas autem vehementer instabat puniens profanos, et prostravit ex eis triginta millia virorum.
       EN: Judas pressed them fiercely, punishing the profane, and struck down thirty thousand of their men.

12:24  LA: Ipse vero Timotheus incidit in partes Dosithei et Sosipatris : et multis precibus postulabat ut vivus dimitteretur, eo quod multorum ex Judæis parentes haberet ac fratres, quos morte ejus decipi eveniret.
       EN: Timothy himself fell into the hands of Dositheus's and Sosipater's troops. With many entreaties he asked to be released alive, because he held the parents and brothers of many Jews, who would come to harm through his death.

12:25  LA: Et cum fidem dedisset restituturum se eos secundum constitutum, illæsum eum dimiserunt propter fratrum salutem.
       EN: When he had pledged to restore them according to the agreement, they released him unharmed for their brothers' preservation.

12:26  LA: Judas autem egressus est ad Carnion, interfectis viginti quinque millibus. · Translation note. The Latin uses the compressed perfect ablative absolute interfectis viginti quinque millibus. “Twenty-five thousand having been killed” retains its syntax and does not supply a separate temple episode.
       EN: Judas then went out to Carnion, twenty-five thousand having been killed.

12:27  LA: Post horum fugam et necem, movit exercitum ad Ephron civitatem munitam, in qua multitudo diversarum gentium habitabat : et robusti juvenes pro muris consistentes fortiter repugnabant : in hac autem machinæ multæ et telorum erat apparatus.
       EN: After their flight and slaughter, he moved his army to the fortified city of Ephron, where a multitude of various nations lived. Strong young men stood before the walls and resisted bravely; there were many siege engines and a supply of missiles in the city.

12:28  LA: Sed cum Omnipotentem invocassent, qui potestate sua vires hostium confringit, ceperunt civitatem : et ex eis qui intus erant, viginti quinque millia prostraverunt.
       EN: But after calling upon the Almighty, who breaks the strength of enemies by His power, they captured the city and struck down twenty-five thousand of those within it.

12:29  LA: Inde ad civitatem Scytharum abierunt, quæ ab Jerosolymis sexcentis stadiis aberat.
       EN: From there they went to the city of the Scythians, which was six hundred stadia from Jerusalem.

12:30  LA: Contestantibus autem his, qui apud Scythopolitas erant, Judæis, quod benigne ab eis haberentur, etiam temporibus infelicitatis quod modeste secum egerint :
       EN: The Jews living among the people of Scythopolis testified that they had been treated kindly by them and that even in times of misfortune they had dealt moderately with them.

12:31  LA: gratias agentes eis, et exhortati etiam de cetero erga genus suum benignos esse, venerunt Jerosolymam die solemni septimanarum instante.
       EN: Giving them thanks and urging them to remain kind toward their people in the future, they came to Jerusalem as the feast day of Weeks drew near.

12:32  LA: Et post Pentecosten abierunt contra Gorgiam præpositum Idumææ.
       EN: After Pentecost they marched against Gorgias, the governor of Idumea.

12:33  LA: Exivit autem cum peditibus tribus millibus, et equitibus quadringentis. · Translation note. The Latin leaves the subject of exivit unexpressed. English retains “he”; the preceding named figure is Gorgias, but the translation does not replace the pronoun with a certain identification.
       EN: He came out with three thousand infantry and four hundred horsemen.

12:34  LA: Quibus congressis, contigit paucos ruere Judæorum.
       EN: When they joined battle, a few of the Jews happened to fall.

12:35  LA: Dositheus vero quidam de Bacenoris eques, vir fortis, Gorgiam tenebat : et, cum vellet illum capere vivum, eques quidam de Thracibus irruit in eum, humerumque ejus amputavit : atque ita Gorgias effugit in Maresa. · Translation note. Humerum is rendered “shoulder,” preserving the source’s named body part rather than substituting “arm.”
       EN: A certain Dositheus, one of Bacenor's horsemen and a brave man, seized Gorgias. As he wished to capture him alive, a Thracian horseman rushed at him and cut off his shoulder; in this way Gorgias escaped to Maresa.

12:36  LA: At illis qui cum Esdrim erant diutius pugnantibus et fatigatis, invocavit Judas Dominum adjutorem et ducem belli fieri : · Translation note. The source name Esdrim is represented as Esdris, distinct in form from Esdra/Ezra at 8:23; no identity is assumed.
       EN: When those with Esdris had been fighting for a long time and were weary, Judas called upon the Lord to become their helper and leader in battle.

12:37  LA: incipiens voce patria, et cum hymnis clamorem extollens, fugam Gorgiæ militibus incussit.
       EN: Beginning in his native language and raising a shout with hymns, he put Gorgias's soldiers to flight.

12:38  LA: Judas autem collecto exercitu venit in civitatem Odollam : et cum septima dies superveniret, secundum consuetudinem purificati, in eodem loco sabbatum egerunt.
       EN: Judas gathered the army and came to the city of Adullam. As the seventh day arrived, they purified themselves according to custom and kept the Sabbath in that place.

12:39  LA: Et sequenti die venit cum suis Judas, ut corpora prostratorum tolleret, et cum parentibus poneret in sepulchris paternis.
       EN: On the following day Judas came with his men to take up the bodies of the fallen and place them with their relatives in their ancestral tombs.

12:40  LA: Invenerunt autem sub tunicis interfectorum de donariis idolorum quæ apud Jamniam fuerunt, a quibus lex prohibet Judæos : omnibus ergo manifestum factum est, ob hanc causam eos corruisse.
       EN: Under the tunics of the slain they found some of the offerings dedicated to idols at Jamnia, from which the law forbids the Jews. It therefore became clear to everyone that they had fallen for this reason.

12:41  LA: Omnes itaque benedixerunt justum judicium Domini, qui occulta fecerat manifesta :
       EN: All therefore blessed the just judgment of the Lord, who had made hidden things manifest.

12:42  LA: atque ita ad preces conversi, rogaverunt ut id quod factum erat delictum oblivioni traderetur. At vero fortissimus Judas hortabatur populum conservare se sine peccato, sub oculis videntes quæ facta sunt pro peccatis eorum qui prostrati sunt.
       EN: Turning to prayer, they asked that the offense committed might be consigned to oblivion. The most courageous Judas urged the people to keep themselves without sin, since they saw before their eyes what had happened because of the sins of those who had fallen.

12:43  LA: Et facta collatione, duodecim millia drachmas argenti misit Jerosolymam offerri pro peccatis mortuorum sacrificium, bene et religiose de resurrectione cogitans · Translation note. Duodecim millia gives twelve thousand silver drachmas. The source’s stated purpose, a sacrifice for the sins of the dead, is retained without importing later doctrinal explanations.
       EN: Having made a collection, he sent twelve thousand silver drachmas to Jerusalem for a sacrifice to be offered for the sins of the dead, thinking well and devoutly about the resurrection—

12:44  LA: (nisi enim eos qui ceciderant resurrecturos speraret, superfluum videretur et vanum orare pro mortuis),
       EN: for unless he hoped that those who had fallen would rise again, it would seem superfluous and vain to pray for the dead—

12:45  LA: et quia considerabat quod hi qui cum pietate dormitionem acceperant, optimam haberent repositam gratiam.
       EN: and because he considered that those who had fallen asleep in godliness had an excellent gift of grace laid up for them.

2 MACCABEES 13

13:1  LA: Anno centesimo quadragesimo nono, cognovit Judas Antiochum Eupatorem venire cum multitudine adversus Judæam,
      EN: In the one hundred forty-ninth year, Judas learned that Antiochus Eupator was coming against Judea with a multitude,

13:2  LA: et cum eo Lysiam procuratorem et præpositum negotiorum, secum habentem peditum centum decem millia, et equitum quinque millia, et elephantos viginti duos, currus cum falcibus trecentos. · Translation note. The Latin gives one hundred ten thousand infantry, five thousand cavalry, twenty-two elephants, and three hundred scythed chariots; all four figures and categories are preserved.
      EN: and with him Lysias, his administrator and director of affairs, bringing one hundred ten thousand infantry, five thousand horsemen, twenty-two elephants, and three hundred chariots equipped with scythes.

13:3  LA: Commiscuit autem se illis et Menelaus : et cum multa fallacia deprecabatur Antiochum, non pro patriæ salute, sed sperans se constitui in principatum.
      EN: Menelaus also joined them and pleaded with Antiochus with much deceit, not for the preservation of his homeland, but hoping to be appointed to office.

13:4  LA: Sed Rex regum suscitavit animos Antiochi in peccatorem : et suggerente Lysia hunc esse causam omnium malorum, jussit (ut eis est consuetudo) apprehensum in eodem loco necari.
      EN: But the King of kings stirred Antiochus's anger against the sinner. When Lysias suggested that this man was the cause of all the evils, the king ordered him arrested and killed in that very place, according to their custom.

13:5  LA: Erat autem in eodem loco turris quinquaginta cubitorum, aggestum undique habens cineris : hæc prospectum habebat in præceps. · Translation note. Prospectum … in præceps is expressed as overlooking a sheer drop. The exact architecture of the execution site remains unstated.
      EN: In that place there was a tower fifty cubits high, with a mound of ashes all around it; it overlooked a sheer drop.

13:6  LA: Inde in cinerem dejici jussit sacrilegum, omnibus eum propellentibus ad interitum.
      EN: From there he ordered the sacrilegious man to be thrown down into the ashes, everyone driving him toward destruction.

13:7  LA: Et tali lege prævaricatorem legis contigit mori, nec terræ dari Menelaum. · Translation note. The wordplay lege / legis is retained as “by such a law … a transgressor of the law.” “Not given to the earth” preserves the burial image.
      EN: By such a law it befell Menelaus, a transgressor of the law, to die without being given to the earth.

13:8  LA: Et quidem satis juste : nam quia multa erga aram Dei delicta commisit, cujus ignis et cinis erat sanctus : ipse in cineris morte damnatus est.
      EN: And this was entirely just, for he had committed many offenses against God's altar, whose fire and ashes were holy, and he himself was condemned to death in ashes.

13:9  LA: Sed rex mente effrenatus veniebat, nequiorem se patre suo Judæis ostensurus.
      EN: But the king came with an unrestrained mind, intending to show himself more wicked toward the Jews than his father.

13:10  LA: Quibus Judas cognitis, præcepit populo ut die ac nocte Dominum invocarent, quo, sicut semper, et nunc adjuvaret eos,
       EN: When Judas learned these things, he commanded the people to call upon the Lord day and night, so that, as always, He might now also help them,

13:11  LA: quippe qui lege, et patria, sanctoque templo privari vererentur : ac populum, qui nuper paululum respirasset, ne sineret blasphemis rursus nationibus subdi.
       EN: for they feared being deprived of the law, their homeland, and the holy temple; and that He might not allow the people, who had recently gained a little breathing space, to be subjected again to blasphemous nations.

13:12  LA: Omnibus itaque simul id facientibus, et petentibus a Domino misericordiam cum fletu et jejuniis, per triduum continuum prostratis, hortatus est eos Judas ut se præpararent.
       EN: All therefore did this together, asking the Lord for mercy with weeping and fasting, lying prostrate for three successive days. Judas then urged them to prepare themselves.

13:13  LA: Ipse vero cum senioribus cogitavit priusquam rex admoveret exercitum ad Judæam et obtineret civitatem, exire, et Domini judicio committere exitum rei.
       EN: He himself took counsel with the elders to go out before the king brought his army into Judea and captured the city, and to entrust the outcome to the Lord's judgment.

13:14  LA: Dans itaque potestatem omnium Deo mundi creatori, et exhortatus suos ut fortiter dimicarent, et usque ad mortem pro legibus, templo, civitate, patria, et civibus starent, circa Modin exercitum constituit.
       EN: Giving authority over everything to God, the Creator of the world, he exhorted his men to fight bravely and stand to the death for the laws, temple, city, homeland, and fellow citizens. He stationed the army near Modein.

13:15  LA: Et dato signo suis Dei victoriæ, juvenibus fortissimis electis nocte aggressus aulam regiam, in castris interfecit viros quatuor millia, et maximum elephantorum cum his qui superpositi fuerant :
       EN: Giving his men the watchword “God's victory,” he selected the bravest young men and attacked the royal quarters by night. In the camp he killed four thousand men and the largest of the elephants, together with those mounted upon it.

13:16  LA: summoque metu ac perturbatione hostium castra replentes, rebus prospere gestis, abierunt.
       EN: Filling the enemies' camp with the greatest fear and confusion, they departed after their successful action.

13:17  LA: Hoc autem factum est die illucescente, adjuvante eum Domini protectione.
       EN: This happened as day was dawning, the Lord's protection helping him.

13:18  LA: Sed rex, accepto gustu audaciæ Judæorum, arte difficultatem locorum tentabat :
       EN: But the king, having tasted the Jews' daring, attempted by strategy to overcome the difficulties of the terrain.

13:19  LA: et Bethsuræ, quæ erat Judæorum præsidium munitum, castra admovebat : sed fugabatur, impingebat, minorabatur.
       EN: He advanced his camp against Beth-zur, which was a fortified stronghold of the Jews, but he was driven back, repulsed, and diminished.

13:20  LA: His autem qui intus erant, Judas necessaria mittebat.
       EN: Judas sent necessities to those inside.

13:21  LA: Enuntiavit autem mysteria hostibus Rhodocus quidam de judaico exercitu, qui requisitus comprehensus est, et conclusus.
       EN: A certain Rhodocus from the Jewish army disclosed secrets to the enemies. He was sought out, arrested, and confined.

13:22  LA: Iterum rex sermonem habuit ad eos qui erant in Bethsuris : dextram dedit, accepit, abiit :
       EN: The king again held a discussion with those in Beth-zur. He gave his right hand, received theirs, and departed.

13:23  LA: commisit cum Juda, superatus est. Ut autem cognovit rebellasse Philippum Antiochiæ, qui relictus erat super negotia, mente consternatus, Judæos deprecans, subditusque eis, jurat de omnibus quibus justum visum est : et reconciliatus obtulit sacrificium, honoravit templum, et munera posuit.
       EN: He engaged Judas and was defeated. When he learned that Philip, whom he had left in charge of affairs, had rebelled at Antioch, he became dismayed. Appealing to the Jews and yielding to them, he swore to everything that seemed just. Reconciled with them, he offered sacrifice, honored the temple, and presented gifts.

13:24  LA: Machabæum amplexatus est, et fecit eum a Ptolemaide usque ad Gerrenos ducem et principem. · Translation note. The Latin identifies Maccabeus as the person made commander. No alternative officer’s name is supplied.
       EN: He embraced Maccabeus and made him commander and ruler from Ptolemais as far as the Gerrhenes.

13:25  LA: Ut autem venit Ptolemaidam, graviter ferebant Ptolemenses amicitiæ conventionem, indignantes ne forte fœdus irrumperent. · Translation note. The Latin has compressed syntax linking the inhabitants’ indignation with the danger of breaking the treaty. The English expresses the possible consequence rather than presenting a completed breach.
       EN: When he came to Ptolemais, its people took the agreement of friendship badly, becoming so indignant that they might break the treaty.

13:26  LA: Tunc ascendit Lysias tribunal, et exposuit rationem, et populum sedavit, regressusque est Antiochiam : et hoc modo regis profectio et reditus processit.
       EN: Then Lysias mounted the tribunal, explained the reasoning, and calmed the people. He returned to Antioch. In this manner the king's expedition and return came about.

2 MACCABEES 14

14:1  LA: Sed post triennii tempus, cognovit Judas et qui cum eo erant Demetrium Seleuci cum multitudine valida et navibus per portam Tripolis ascendisse ad loca opportuna,
      EN: After three years, Judas and those with him learned that Demetrius son of Seleucus had come up through the harbor of Tripolis with a strong force and ships, to suitable positions,

14:2  LA: et tenuisse regiones adversus Antiochum, et ducem ejus Lysiam.
      EN: and had taken possession of the regions against Antiochus and his commander Lysias.

14:3  LA: Alcimus autem quidam, qui summus sacerdos fuerat, sed voluntarie coinquinatus est temporibus commistionis, considerans nullo modo sibi esse salutem neque accessum ad altare, · Translation note. Temporibus commistionis is rendered “in the times of intermingling.” The Latin wording is not replaced by a fully reconstructed historical period name.
      EN: A certain Alcimus, who had been high priest but had voluntarily defiled himself in the times of intermingling, considered that there was no way for him to find safety or access to the altar.

14:4  LA: venit ad regem Demetrium centesimo quinquagesimo anno, offerens ei coronam auream et palmam, super hæc et thallos, qui templi esse videbantur. Et ipsa quidem die siluit. · Translation note. Thallos is rendered “boughs.” The source’s uncertainty, “appeared to belong to the temple,” is retained.
      EN: In the one hundred fiftieth year he came to King Demetrius, offering him a golden crown and a palm branch, and besides these, boughs that appeared to belong to the temple. That day he remained silent.

14:5  LA: Tempus autem opportunum dementiæ suæ nactus, convocatus a Demetrio ad consilium, et interrogatus quibus rebus et consiliis Judæi niterentur,
      EN: But finding a suitable opportunity for his madness, he was summoned to the council by Demetrius and asked upon what resources and plans the Jews relied.

14:6  LA: respondit : Ipsi qui dicuntur Assidæi Judæorum, quibus præest Judas Machabæus, bella nutriunt, et seditiones movent, nec patiuntur regnum esse quietum : · Translation note. Assidæi is represented by the familiar form Hasideans; the ancient group is not identified with later Jewish movements sharing a related name.
      EN: He answered, “Those among the Jews who are called Hasideans, whose leader is Judas Maccabeus, nourish wars and stir up rebellions, and do not allow the kingdom to be at peace.

14:7  LA: nam et ego defraudatus parentum gloria (dico autem summo sacerdotio) huc veni :
      EN: I too, having been deprived of the glory of my ancestors—I mean the high priesthood—have come here,

14:8  LA: primo quidem utilitatibus regis fidem servans, secundo autem etiam civibus consulens : nam illorum pravitate universum genus nostrum non minime vexatur.
      EN: first, maintaining loyalty to the king's interests, and second, also considering my fellow citizens. For through the wickedness of those men our entire people are afflicted in no small way.

14:9  LA: Sed oro his singulis, o rex, cognitis, et regioni et generi, secundum humanitatem tuam pervulgatam omnibus, prospice :
      EN: But I beg you, O king, having learned each of these things, to provide for the region and the nation according to your kindness, renowned among everyone.

14:10  LA: nam, quamdiu superest Judas, impossibile est pacem esse negotiis.
       EN: For as long as Judas survives, it is impossible for affairs to be at peace.”

14:11  LA: Talibus autem ab hoc dictis, et ceteri amici hostiliter se habentes adversus Judam, inflammaverunt Demetrium.
       EN: When he had said such things, the other friends who were hostile toward Judas also inflamed Demetrius.

14:12  LA: Qui statim Nicanorem præpositum elephantorum ducem misit in Judæam :
       EN: He immediately sent Nicanor, the commander of the elephants, as governor into Judea,

14:13  LA: datis mandatis ut ipsum quidem Judam caperet : eos vero qui cum illo erant, dispergeret, et constitueret Alcimum maximi templi summum sacerdotem.
       EN: with orders to capture Judas himself, scatter those with him, and establish Alcimus as high priest of the greatest temple.

14:14  LA: Tunc gentes quæ de Judæa fugerant Judam, gregatim se Nicanori miscebant, miserias et clades Judæorum prosperitates rerum suarum existimantes.
       EN: Then the nations who had fled from Judas out of Judea joined Nicanor in crowds, regarding the miseries and disasters of the Jews as prosperity for their own affairs.

14:15  LA: Audito itaque Judæi Nicanoris adventu, et conventu nationum, conspersi terra rogabant eum qui populum suum constituit, ut in æternum custodiret, quique suam portionem signis evidentibus protegit.
       EN: When the Jews heard of Nicanor's arrival and the gathering of the nations, they sprinkled themselves with earth and prayed to Him who had established His people to preserve them forever, and who protects His portion by manifest signs.

14:16  LA: Imperante autem duce, statim inde moverunt, conveneruntque ad castellum Dessau.
       EN: At the commander's order they immediately moved from there and assembled at the village of Dessau.

14:17  LA: Simon vero frater Judæ commiserat cum Nicanore : sed conterritus est repentino adventu adversariorum.
       EN: Simon, Judas's brother, had engaged Nicanor but was terrified by the sudden arrival of the adversaries.

14:18  LA: Nicanor tamen, audiens virtutem comitum Judæ, et animi magnitudinem quam pro patriæ certaminibus habebant, sanguine judicium facere metuebat.
       EN: Nicanor, however, hearing of the courage of Judas's companions and the greatness of spirit they displayed in struggles for their homeland, was afraid to settle the issue by bloodshed.

14:19  LA: Quam ob rem præmisit Posidonium, et Theodotium, et Matthiam, ut darent dextras atque acciperent.
       EN: Therefore he sent Posidonius, Theodotius, and Matthias ahead to give and receive pledges by the right hand.

14:20  LA: Et cum diu de his consilium ageretur, et ipse dux ad multitudinem retulisset, omnium una fuit sententia amicitiis annuere.
       EN: After lengthy deliberation about these matters, and after the commander himself had referred them to the multitude, all were of one mind in agreeing to friendship.

14:21  LA: Itaque diem constituerunt, qua secreto inter se agerent : et singulis sellæ prolatæ sunt, et positæ.
       EN: They therefore appointed a day to meet privately with one another, and seats were brought out and set in place for each.

14:22  LA: Præcepit autem Judas armatos esse locis opportunis, ne forte ab hostibus repente mali aliquid oriretur : et congruum colloquium fecerunt.
       EN: Judas ordered armed men to occupy suitable positions, lest some sudden harm arise from the enemies. They held an agreeable conference.

14:23  LA: Morabatur autem Nicanor Jerosolymis, nihilque inique agebat : gregesque turbarum quæ congregatæ fuerant, dimisit.
       EN: Nicanor stayed in Jerusalem and did nothing unjust; he dismissed the bands of crowds that had been assembled.

14:24  LA: Habebat autem Judam semper carum ex animo, et erat viro inclinatus.
       EN: He always held Judas dear from the heart and was well disposed toward the man.

14:25  LA: Rogavitque eum ducere uxorem, filiosque procreare. Nuptias fecit : quiete egit, communiterque vivebant. · Translation note. The subject of the marriage clauses is understood to be Judas, whom Nicanor has just encouraged to marry. Their subsequent shared or friendly life does not establish a particular household arrangement.
       EN: He urged him to take a wife and father children. Judas married and lived quietly, and they lived on friendly terms.

14:26  LA: Alcimus autem, videns caritatem illorum ad invicem et conventiones, venit ad Demetrium, et dicebat Nicanorem rebus alienis assentire, Judamque regni insidiatorem successorem sibi destinasse.
       EN: But Alcimus, seeing their affection for one another and their agreements, came to Demetrius and said that Nicanor was supporting interests foreign to the king and had designated Judas, a plotter against the kingdom, as his own successor.

14:27  LA: Itaque rex exasperatus, et pessimis hujus criminationibus irritatus, scripsit Nicanori, dicens graviter quidem se ferre de amicitiæ conventione, jubere tamen Machabæum citius vinctum mittere Antiochiam.
       EN: The king therefore became exasperated and, provoked by this man's most wicked accusations, wrote to Nicanor, saying that he took the agreement of friendship very badly and ordering him to send Maccabeus bound to Antioch without delay.

14:28  LA: Quibus cognitis, Nicanor consternabatur, et graviter ferebat, si ea quæ convenerant irrita faceret, nihil læsus a viro :
       EN: When Nicanor learned this, he was dismayed and deeply distressed at having to invalidate their agreements, since he had suffered no injury from the man.

14:29  LA: sed quia regi resistere non poterat, opportunitatem observabat qua præceptum perficeret.
       EN: But because he could not resist the king, he watched for an opportunity to carry out the command.

14:30  LA: At Machabæus, videns secum austerius agere Nicanorem, et consuetum occursum ferocius exhibentem, intelligens non ex bono esse austeritatem istam, paucis suorum congregatis, occultavit se a Nicanore.
       EN: Maccabeus saw that Nicanor was treating him more harshly and conducting their usual meetings with greater hostility. Understanding that this severity was not for good, he gathered a few of his men and hid himself from Nicanor.

14:31  LA: Quod cum ille cognovit, fortiter se a viro præventum, venit ad maximum et sanctissimum templum : et sacerdotibus solitas hostias offerentibus, jussit sibi tradi virum.
       EN: When Nicanor learned that he had been decisively forestalled by the man, he came to the greatest and most holy temple. While the priests were offering the customary sacrifices, he ordered them to hand the man over to him.

14:32  LA: Quibus cum juramento dicentibus nescire se ubi esset qui quærebatur, extendens manum ad templum,
       EN: When they declared with an oath that they did not know where the man being sought was, he stretched out his hand toward the temple

14:33  LA: juravit, dicens : Nisi Judam mihi vinctum tradideritis, istud Dei fanum in planitiem deducam, et altare effodiam, et templum hoc Libero patri consecrabo.
       EN: and swore, “Unless you hand Judas over to me bound, I will level this shrine of God to the ground, dig up the altar, and dedicate this temple to Father Liber.”

14:34  LA: Et his dictis abiit. Sacerdotes autem protendentes manus in cælum, invocabant eum qui semper propugnator esset gentis ipsorum, hæc dicentes :
       EN: After saying this he departed. The priests stretched out their hands toward heaven and called upon Him who had always been the defender of their nation, saying,

14:35  LA: Tu, Domine universorum, qui nullius indiges, voluisti templum habitationis tuæ fieri in nobis.
       EN: “You, Lord of all, who need nothing, willed that a temple for Your dwelling should be made among us.

14:36  LA: Et nunc, Sancte sanctorum, omnium Domine, conserva in æternum impollutam domum istam, quæ nuper mundata est. · Translation note. Sancte sanctorum is retained as “Holy One of holy ones,” rather than made into the name of a room in the temple.
       EN: And now, Holy One of holy ones, Lord of all, preserve forever undefiled this house that has recently been purified.”

14:37  LA: Razias autem quidam de senioribus ab Jerosolymis delatus est Nicanori, vir amator civitatis, et bene audiens : qui pro affectu pater Judæorum appellabatur.
       EN: A certain Razis, one of the elders of Jerusalem, was reported to Nicanor. He was a lover of the city and a man of good reputation, called the father of the Jews because of his affection for them.

14:38  LA: Hic multis temporibus continentiæ propositum tenuit in Judaismo, corpusque et animam tradere contentus pro perseverantia. · Translation note. Continentiæ is translated “self-restraint.” The word alone does not establish lifelong celibacy in this context.
       EN: For a long time he had maintained a resolve of self-restraint in Judaism, and was content to surrender body and soul for his perseverance.

14:39  LA: Volens autem Nicanor manifestare odium quod habebat in Judæos, misit milites quingentos ut eum comprehenderent.
       EN: Nicanor, wishing to display the hatred he had for the Jews, sent five hundred soldiers to arrest him.

14:40  LA: Putabat enim, si illum decepisset, se cladem Judæis maximam illaturum.
       EN: For he thought that if he ensnared him, he would inflict the greatest disaster on the Jews.

14:41  LA: Turbis autem irruere in domum ejus, et januam dirumpere : atque ignem admovere cupientibus, cum jam comprehenderetur, gladio se petiit,
       EN: As the crowds sought to break into his house, burst open the door, and set fire to it, and he was already about to be captured, he struck himself with his sword,

14:42  LA: eligens nobiliter mori potius quam subditus fieri peccatoribus, et contra natales suos indignis injuriis agi.
       EN: choosing to die nobly rather than become subject to sinners and suffer injuries unworthy of his birth.

14:43  LA: Sed cum per festinationem non certo ictu plagam dedisset, et turbæ intra ostia irrumperent, recurrens audacter ad murum præcipitavit semetipsum viriliter in turbas :
       EN: But in his haste he had not struck an accurate blow. As the crowds burst through the doors, he boldly ran back to the wall and bravely threw himself down into the crowds.

14:44  LA: quibus velociter locum dantibus casui ejus, venit per mediam cervicem. · Translation note. Per mediam cervicem is difficult. Cervix ordinarily means “neck”; the provisional rendering is “landed squarely on his neck.” It is not replaced by an empty space in a different textual tradition. This clause needs independent Latin and textual review.
       EN: They quickly made room for his fall, and he landed squarely on his neck.

14:45  LA: Et cum adhuc spiraret, accensus animo, surrexit, et cum sanguis ejus magno fluxu deflueret, et gravissimis vulneribus esset saucius, cursu turbam pertransiit :
       EN: Still breathing and fired with resolve, he rose. Though his blood poured out in a great stream and he was injured with the gravest wounds, he ran through the crowd.

14:46  LA: et stans supra quamdam petram præruptam, et jam exsanguis effectus, complexus intestina sua, utrisque manibus projecit super turbas, invocans dominatorem vitæ ac spiritus ut hæc illi iterum redderet : atque ita vita defunctus est.
       EN: Standing on a certain steep rock, now drained of blood, he grasped his intestines and hurled them upon the crowds with both hands, calling upon the Ruler of life and spirit to restore these to him again. In this way he died.

2 MACCABEES 15

15:1  LA: Nicanor autem, ut comperit Judam esse in locis Samariæ, cogitavit cum omni impetu die sabbati committere bellum.
      EN: When Nicanor learned that Judas was in the regions of Samaria, he planned to join battle with full force on the Sabbath day.

15:2  LA: Judæis vero qui illum per necessitatem sequebantur, dicentibus : Ne ita ferociter et barbare feceris, sed honorem tribue diei sanctificationis, et honora eum qui universa conspicit :
      EN: The Jews who followed him under compulsion said, “Do not act so savagely and barbarously, but give honor to the day of sanctification and honor Him who sees all things.”

15:3  LA: ille infelix interrogavit si est potens in cælo, qui imperavit agi diem sabbatorum.
      EN: That wretched man asked whether there was a ruler in heaven who had commanded the Sabbath day to be observed.

15:4  LA: Et respondentibus illis : Est Dominus vivus ipse in cælo potens, qui jussit agi septimam diem :
      EN: They answered, “There is the living Lord Himself, the Ruler in heaven, who commanded the seventh day to be observed.”

15:5  LA: at ille ait : Et ego potens sum super terram qui impero sumi arma, et negotia regis impleri. Tamen non obtinuit ut consilium perficeret.
      EN: But he said, “And I am a ruler upon earth, commanding that weapons be taken up and the king's business accomplished.” Yet he did not succeed in carrying out his plan.

15:6  LA: Et Nicanor quidem cum summa superbia erectus, cogitaverat commune trophæum statuere de Juda.
      EN: Nicanor, exalted with the greatest arrogance, had planned to erect a public trophy from Judas's defeat.

15:7  LA: Machabæus autem semper confidebat cum omni spe auxilium sibi a Deo affuturum :
      EN: But Maccabeus always trusted with full hope that help from God would be present for him.

15:8  LA: et hortabatur suos ne formidarent ad adventum nationum, sed in mente haberent adjutoria sibi facta de cælo, et nunc sperarent ab Omnipotente sibi affuturam victoriam.
      EN: He encouraged his men not to be frightened at the coming of the nations, but to remember the help they had received from heaven and now to hope that victory would come to them from the Almighty.

15:9  LA: Et allocutus eos de lege et prophetis, admonens etiam certamina quæ fecerant prius, promptiores constituit eos :
      EN: Speaking to them from the law and the prophets, and reminding them also of the struggles they had previously undertaken, he made them more eager.

15:10  LA: et ita animis eorum erectis simul ostendebat gentium fallaciam, et juramentorum prævaricationem.
       EN: Having thus raised their spirits, he also pointed out the deceit of the nations and their violation of oaths.

15:11  LA: Singulos autem illorum armavit, non clypei et hastæ munitione, sed sermonibus optimis et exhortationibus, exposito digno fide somnio, per quod universos lætificavit.
       EN: He armed each of them, not with the protection of shield and spear, but with excellent words and exhortations, relating a trustworthy dream through which he made them all glad.

15:12  LA: Erat autem hujuscemodi visus : Oniam, qui fuerat summus sacerdos, virum bonum et benignum, verecundum visu, modestum moribus, et eloquio decorum, et qui a puero in virtutibus exercitatus sit, manus protendentem orare pro omni populo Judæorum. · Translation note. The dream presents the deceased Onias praying for the people; this narrative content is preserved. Later doctrinal application is not inserted into the verse.
       EN: This was the vision: Onias, who had been high priest, a good and kindly man, dignified in appearance, modest in conduct, graceful in speech, and trained in virtues from childhood, was stretching out his hands and praying for the whole Jewish people.

15:13  LA: Post hoc apparuisse et alium virum ætate et gloria mirabilem, et magni decoris habitudine circa illum.
       EN: After this, another man appeared, marvelous in age and glory, with an appearance of great majesty about him.

15:14  LA: Respondentem vero Oniam dixisse : Hic est fratrum amator, et populi Israël : hic est qui multum orat pro populo et universa sancta civitate, Jeremias propheta Dei.
       EN: Onias spoke and said, “This is the lover of his brothers and of the people of Israel; this is the one who prays much for the people and the entire holy city: Jeremiah, the prophet of God.”

15:15  LA: Extendisse autem Jeremiam dextram, et dedisse Judæ gladium aureum, dicentem :
       EN: Jeremiah stretched out his right hand and gave Judas a golden sword, saying,

15:16  LA: Accipe sanctum gladium munus a Deo, in quo dejicies adversarios populi mei Israël. · Translation note. Jeremiah’s speech has “my people Israel”; the possessive is preserved as part of his reported words.
       EN: “Receive this holy sword, a gift from God, with which you will strike down the adversaries of my people Israel.”

15:17  LA: Exhortati itaque Judæ sermonibus bonis valde, de quibus extolli posset impetus, et animi juvenum confortari, statuerunt dimicare et confligere fortiter : ut virtus de negotiis judicaret, eo quod civitas sancta et templum periclitarentur.
       EN: Encouraged by Judas's very good words, which could heighten their eagerness and strengthen the spirits of the young men, they resolved to fight and engage bravely, so that courage might decide the matter, because the holy city and the temple were in danger.

15:18  LA: Erat enim pro uxoribus et filiis, itemque pro fratribus et cognatis, minor sollicitudo : maximus vero et primus pro sanctitate timor erat templi.
       EN: For their concern for wives and children, and likewise for brothers and relatives, was less; their greatest and foremost fear was for the holiness of the temple.

15:19  LA: Sed et eos qui in civitate erant, non minima sollicitudo habebat pro his qui congressuri erant.
       EN: Those in the city also felt no small anxiety for the men who were about to engage in battle.

15:20  LA: Et cum jam omnes sperarent judicium futurum, hostesque adessent atque exercitus esset ordinatus, bestiæ equitesque opportuno in loco compositi,
       EN: When everyone was now awaiting the decision, the enemies were present, the army was arranged, and the beasts and cavalry were positioned in suitable places,

15:21  LA: considerans Machabæus adventum multitudinis, et apparatum varium armorum, et ferocitatem bestiarum, extendens manus in cælum, prodigia facientem Dominum invocavit, qui non secundum armorum potentiam, sed prout ipsi placet, dat dignis victoriam.
       EN: Maccabeus considered the multitude's approach, their varied array of weapons, and the ferocity of the beasts. Stretching out his hands toward heaven, he called upon the wonder-working Lord, who gives victory to the worthy, not according to the power of weapons, but as He Himself pleases.

15:22  LA: Dixit autem invocans hoc modo : Tu Domine, qui misisti angelum tuum sub Ezechia rege Juda, et interfecisti de castris Sennacherib centum octoginta quinque millia :
       EN: He prayed in this manner: “You, Lord, who sent Your angel in the days of Hezekiah king of Judah and killed one hundred eighty-five thousand in Sennacherib's camp,

15:23  LA: et nunc, dominator cælorum, mitte angelum tuum bonum ante nos in timore et tremore magnitudinis brachii tui,
       EN: now also, Ruler of the heavens, send Your good angel before us with the fear and trembling brought by the greatness of Your arm,

15:24  LA: ut metuant qui cum blasphemia veniunt adversus sanctum populum tuum. Et hic quidem ita peroravit.
       EN: so that those who come with blasphemy against Your holy people may be afraid.” In this way he completed his prayer.

15:25  LA: Nicanor autem et qui cum ipso erant, cum tubis et canticis admovebant.
       EN: Nicanor and those with him advanced with trumpets and songs.

15:26  LA: Judas vero et qui cum eo erant, invocato Deo, per orationes congressi sunt :
       EN: But Judas and those with him called upon God and joined battle with prayers.

15:27  LA: manu quidem pugnantes, sed Dominum cordibus orantes, prostraverunt non minus triginta quinque millia, præsentia Dei magnifice delectati.
       EN: Fighting with their hands but praying to the Lord in their hearts, they struck down no fewer than thirty-five thousand, rejoicing greatly in God's presence.

15:28  LA: Cumque cessassent, et cum gaudio redirent, cognoverunt Nicanorem ruisse cum armis suis.
       EN: When they had ceased and were returning with joy, they learned that Nicanor had fallen in his armor.

15:29  LA: Facto itaque clamore, et perturbatione excitata, patria voce omnipotentem Dominum benedicebant.
       EN: They therefore raised a shout and stirred up a tumult, blessing the almighty Lord in their native language.

15:30  LA: Præcepit autem Judas, qui per omnia corpore et animo mori pro civibus paratus erat, caput Nicanoris, et manum cum humero abscissam, Jerosolymam perferri.
       EN: Judas, who was ready in every way, in body and spirit, to die for his fellow citizens, ordered Nicanor's head and his hand, cut off together with the shoulder, to be carried to Jerusalem.

15:31  LA: Quo cum pervenisset, convocatis contribulibus et sacerdotibus ad altare, accersiit et eos qui in arce erant.
       EN: When he arrived there, he summoned his fellow tribesmen and the priests to the altar, and called for those in the citadel as well.

15:32  LA: Et ostenso capite Nicanoris, et manu nefaria quam extendens contra domum sanctam omnipotentis Dei magnifice gloriatus est.
       EN: He displayed Nicanor's head and the wicked hand that he had stretched out against the holy house of almighty God while boasting magnificently.

15:33  LA: Linguam etiam impii Nicanoris præcisam jussit particulatim avibus dari : manum autem dementis contra templum suspendi.
       EN: He also ordered the ungodly Nicanor's tongue to be cut out and given to the birds piece by piece, and the madman's hand to be hung up facing the temple.

15:34  LA: Omnes igitur cæli benedixerunt Dominum, dicentes : Benedictus qui locum suum incontaminatum servavit. · Translation note. The source explicitly reads omnes cæli, “all the heavens”; this is not silently replaced by “all the people.”
       EN: Then all the heavens blessed the Lord, saying, “Blessed is He who has preserved His place undefiled.”

15:35  LA: Suspendit autem Nicanoris caput in summa arce, ut evidens esset, et manifestum signum auxilii Dei.
       EN: He hung Nicanor's head at the top of the citadel, so that it would be a visible and manifest sign of God's help.

15:36  LA: Itaque omnes communi consilio decreverunt nullo modo diem istum absque celebritate præterire :
       EN: All therefore decreed by common counsel that this day should by no means pass without celebration,

15:37  LA: Igitur his erga Nicanorem gestis, et ex illis temporibus ab Hebræis civitate possessa, ego quoque in his faciam finem sermonis.
       EN: Since these things happened concerning Nicanor, and from those times the city was held by the Hebrews, I too will bring my account to an end here.

15:38  LA: Et si quidem bene, et ut historiæ competit, hoc et ipse velim : sin autem minus digne, concedendum est mihi.
       EN: If it has been done well and as befits a history, that is what I myself would wish; but if less worthily, allowance must be made for me.

15:39  LA: Sicut enim vinum semper bibere, aut semper aquam, contrarium est ; alternis autem uti, delectabile : ita legentibus si semper exactus sit sermo, non erit gratus. Hic ergo erit consummatus. · Translation note. Alternis … uti is rendered “using them alternately,” not “mixing” the wine and water. The final comparison also explicitly mentions overly painstaking discourse; both features of this Latin ending are retained.
       EN: For just as always drinking wine or always water is disagreeable, while using them alternately is delightful, so an account will not please its readers if it is always painstaking. Here, then, it will be concluded.
